# Design AI learning assistants to make the implications of AI use visible rather than restricting assistance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article derives practical design recommendations for reducing deskilling risk without restricting LLM use: "designers should make the implications of AI use visible, make complete-answer requests more deliberate, and support learning-oriented help-seeking." The mechanism is to keep assistance unrestricted while supporting learners' own regulation of how much cognitive work to hand over, rather than constraining what the assistant may provide, which the article notes can demotivate lower-proficiency learners or be circumvented.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The assistant must leave offloading as an active, deliberate choice, providing complete answers only on explicit request, so feedback can target the learner's own decisions.
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is a single online experiment with adult participants practicing fraction arithmetic; the reward-style incentive approach showed no significant benefit for either preregistered outcome.

### Target Learners
- Adult learners using LLM assistants for practice-based skill development

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing answer offloading; preserving unaided performance after AI-assisted practice
