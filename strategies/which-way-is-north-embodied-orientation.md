# Embodied orientation lessons that mark compass directions in the room and align internal and external compasses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes curriculum such as "Which way is North?" and "Follow the Season; Follow the Stars, Four-Direction Star Gazing," in which the meeting room wall is marked with tape and signs showing the Ojibwe and Dakota words for north or south, and participants physically face that direction while discussing it. Marking the north compass direction in the room "connects the abstract with the 'here and now' and makes the idea tangible," and when participants face north while talking about the North, "our internal and external compasses are aligned and the learning is amplified." The lessons cover the north star, the north celestial pole, identifying Ojibwe, D(L)akota and Greek constellations, and seasonal star motion facing south.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A meeting room whose compass directions can be located and marked at the time of the discussion; tape and handwritten signs with the Ojibwe and Dakota direction words
#### Constraints
- Assumes a northern-hemisphere mid-latitude viewing location

### Target Learners
- workshop participants
- K-12 students via educators

### Target Learning Goals
- patterns of motion in the stars
- north star and north celestial pole
- Ojibwe, D(L)akota and Greek constellations
- cultural meaning of the four directions
