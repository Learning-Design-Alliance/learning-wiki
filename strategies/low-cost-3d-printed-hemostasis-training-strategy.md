# Use low-cost 3D-printed simulators to train beginners in hemostasis before treating real bleeding patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends 3D-printed hemostasis simulation as a low-cost, reusable route to pre-patient training: the simulator costs approximately $200 and works with conventional endoscopic equipment. The authors argue that "endoscopists in hospitals without sufficient experience can easily receive training, which should significantly improve patient safety", positioning simulator practice before live procedures on gastrointestinal bleeding patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to 3D printing facilities and basic materials (silicone molding, pump, artificial blood supply)
#### Constraints
- Does not reproduce gastric movement from heartbeat and respiration; elasticity differs from real stomach; not compared against other simulators

### Target Learners
- Beginner endoscopists
- First-year clinical fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- Endoscopic hemostasis technique before performing procedures on patients with gastrointestinal bleeding
