Prioritize rigorous VR training studies with larger samples, validated outcome measures, immersive designs, and LLM integration in mental health education research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's forward-looking recommendations for VR training research in mental health care education. It states that "Future research on the effectiveness of VR training for specific learning outcomes in assessing and treating mental health disorders should encompass more rigorous experimental studies with larger sample sizes", with verifiable intervention descriptions and validated measurement tools. It further recommends in-depth designs evaluating broader learning processes, immersive VR applications, and exploring integration of large language models to augment interactive learning, plus agreed terms and classifications for reporting.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should include verifiable descriptions of the VR interventions and employ validated tools to measure outcomes.

Constraints¶

  • Integrating interactive VSPs into fully immersive VR environments remains costly, potentially limiting widespread adoption; ethical and regulatory issues such as data privacy and LLM bias must be addressed.

Target Learners¶

  • health care professionals
  • health care professional students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mental health assessment and treatment knowledge and skills

Claims¶

  • All included VR training interventions used non-immersive desktop systems; no immersive VR studies met the review's inclusion criteria [+W]
  • An RCT using self-reported combined knowledge and skills found a significant within-group pretest-to-posttest increase with a large effect size, but reported no between-group comparison [+W]
  • VR training improved mental health knowledge in two non-randomized studies but showed no statistically significant effect on diagnostic recognition in a cohort study [~W]
  • VR training interventions can promote knowledge and skills acquisition in health care professionals and students for assessing and treating mental health disorders, though the evidence base is uncertain [+W]
  • A single-group pretest–posttest study found no significant effect of VSP training on attitudes toward individuals with substance abuse disorders but a significant increase in perceived competence [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design and implement VS teaching with simplified interfaces, multimodal feedback, multilingual diverse case libraries, pre-training, and controlled VR session lengths
  • Future HFS trials for practising nurses need larger samples, rigorous randomised design and standardised, fully reported measures
  • Use cluster randomized clinical trials and blinded simulation-based behavior assessment to strengthen NTS training evidence
  • Future research should include long-term RCTs with adequate sample sizes, standardized outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses
