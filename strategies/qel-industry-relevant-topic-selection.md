# Select course project topics for direct industry relevance in near-term application categories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A core design principle of the QEL is that "students work on quantum topics with direct industry relevance", defined as near-term applications addressing immediate market needs rather than long-term speculative innovations. The article identifies two overlapping topic categories: quantum-inspired techniques (e.g., tensor networks, machine learning algorithms, optimization methods) and solutions for the quantum industry itself (e.g., tailor-made simulators, compilers, error mitigation consulting). Topics should be feasible within the course timeline, suited to collaborative work, research-oriented, and open-ended yet structured.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Foresight, industry awareness, and understanding of emerging research directions in topic selection
#### Constraints
- Widely recognized areas may already be extensively explored, diminishing novelty and impact
- Topics are expected to fluctuate with the changing needs of the quantum landscape

### Target Learners
- Master's-level technical and business students in quantum-related fields

### Target Learning Goals
- Working on real-world quantum industry challenges
- Translating research into practical solutions
