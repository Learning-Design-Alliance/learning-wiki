# Leverage students' partial metacognitive self-awareness of overreliance through reflection prompts, acceptance-rate feedback, and required justifications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students showed partial self-awareness of their overreliance (r = 0.355), the authors propose interventions that make reliance patterns explicit: "By leveraging students' existing metacognitive awareness through reflection prompts, acceptance rate feedback, or required justifications for following AI suggestions, we can strengthen their ability to recognize and correct overreliant behaviors." This aligns the strategy with cognitive forcing functions research showing prompting users to think before accepting AI recommendations reduces overreliance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must possess some metacognitive awareness of their own overreliance, which the article reports students partially demonstrated
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a pilot study with computer science students in a single session; the authors note reliance patterns may develop differently over time and in other disciplines

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students using AI assistants in coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing and correcting overreliant behaviors
- calibrating trust in AI systems
