# Four district-level recommendations for improving school safety and student well-being regardless of position on the spectrum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the case study and similar research, Ed Trust recommends four steps for any school or district: "Provide clear messaging to principals and school staff on existing policies, including detailed guidance and support in implementing recent policy changes"; seek deeper engagement with students and families in decision-making, such as structured partnerships or a diversely-represented family and student advisory group; strengthen adults' capacity to build relationships and foster belonging through professional development on bias, asset-based lenses, and restorative practices; and hire staff who support well-being beyond physical security, such as counselors and restorative justice coordinators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained, intentional action by district and school leaders, since shifting mindsets and building skills 'take time to learn' and cannot happen overnight
#### Constraints
- The report notes reforms require thoughtful consideration to gain buy-in from all stakeholders and ensure fidelity of implementation

### Target Learners
- students in urban districts, particularly Black, Latino, and low-income students disproportionately affected by negative school climates

### Target Learning Goals
- student safety, well-being, and sense of belonging in school
