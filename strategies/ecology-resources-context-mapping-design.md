# Use the Ecology of Resources framework as a design tool for context mapping in underserved settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the framework during design to map potential resources, links and barriers before building technology. In VeSEL, "The Ecology of Resources framework is useful at this stage of the design for mapping the potential resources that could be included, the links between them and the potential barriers". Identifying the elements present in an existing context indicates where effective technology interventions might be made, and participatory design methods help surface features of the wider context that inform how tensions are tackled.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Iterative, interdisciplinary and participatory design processes involving local partners and communities
#### Constraints
- Illustrated at an early stage of the VeSEL project, during prototype design; the article notes the informal, fluid-context case still requires investigation

### Target Learners
- rural community learners, including school children, farmers and family members

### Target Learning Goals
- water resource management, appropriate crop knowledge and basic skills through ICT
