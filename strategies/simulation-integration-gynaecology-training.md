# Integrate simulation-based training into gynaecology training programs to compensate for declining hands-on experience in vaginal hysterectomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating simulation and other training models into gynaecology training programs because hands-on experience for complex procedures like vaginal hysterectomy is increasingly limited. It states that "Simulation‐based training not only enhances technical skills but also improves decision‐making and confidence in performing procedures", and that these training models offer a standardised assessment of competence before independent practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of simulation methods into gynaecology training programs, which the article says educators should do to ensure comprehensive education
#### Constraints
- The article frames simulation as compensation for limited hands-on experience with complex procedures like vaginal hysterectomy, not as a replacement for operative volume

### Target Learners
- obstetrics and gynaecology trainees
- urogynaecology subspecialty trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- vaginal hysterectomy technical skill
- intraoperative decision-making
- surgical confidence
- standardised assessment of competence
