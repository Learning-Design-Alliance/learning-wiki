# Invite local Elders into classrooms as co-teachers of Indigenous Knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study engaged two Kasena Elders, as holders of Indigenous knowledge, to visit the school weekly and teach children through outdoor learning activities tied to curriculum topics. Kim and Dionne's suggestion quoted in the article is that "it is essential to create a venue for true experts to share their knowledge directly with learners." The researchers met the Elders before each visit, but the Elders taught the concepts and topics their way. This made inviting Elders into classrooms easier and gave children a different view of who a teacher is.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Funds should be made available so that Elders can be provided honoraria to support teachers in teaching IKs
- Opportunities must exist to invite Elders into classrooms, which the authors note were lacking in the Ghanaian context
#### Constraints
- Teaching of some IKs is time-bound and seasonal; certain content (e.g. kenaf plants) could not be taught because the study took place in the dry season
- Navigating between the Elders' child-led pedagogy and the teacher's more structured pedagogy was a challenge

### Target Learners
- Early childhood pupils in rural communities with living Indigenous knowledge traditions

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning local Indigenous knowledge connected to environmental studies curriculum topics
