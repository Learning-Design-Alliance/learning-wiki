Use blended learning approaches for teachers' professional development in ICT integration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends adopting a blended approach to professional development to address the challenge of community building in online environments, since virtual communities struggle to develop the belonging, trust, and support that are prerequisites to learning. Blended programs give teachers opportunities for learning on the job and collaborating with other teachers, and can help teachers better understand and implement technology in their classrooms. The study's own blended model — combining face-to-face lab sessions with asynchronous and synchronous online learning — produced higher ICT knowledge gains than traditional training.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Combination of virtual and physical learning environments
  • Online platform plus face-to-face sessions

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base for blended learning in teacher education is limited, per the article's own literature discussion

Target Learners¶

  • in-service teachers
  • teacher trainees

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ICT integration knowledge
  • professional development in technology use

Claims¶

  • The authors attribute the blended group's advantage to enhanced interaction, flexibility, and opportunities for continuous improvement [+W]
  • Blended learning professional development produces significantly higher gain means in ICT knowledge (TK, TPK, TCK, TPACK) than traditional training for in-service science teachers [+W]
  • Both blended and traditionally trained teacher groups improved significantly on all four TPACK-based ICT knowledge dimensions from pretest to posttest [+W]
  • Experimental and control groups were statistically equivalent in ICT knowledge before the intervention [+W]
  • Teachers' ICT knowledge was above the general average in both groups after the intervention [+W]
