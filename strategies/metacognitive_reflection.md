# Metacognitive Reflection

## Description
This strategy focuses on learners' awareness of their thought processes as they complete tasks, emphasizing self-improvement rather than assessment. It involves reflecting on how to improve future work and developing resilience by viewing frustration as a signal to pause and think through the situation. The aim is to help learners monitor their progress, construct meaning from the content, and apply their learnings to new contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured time for reflection; prompts to guide thinking; documentation methods (e.g., logs, journals).
#### Constraints
- Danger of the reflection turning into a feedback loop. Learners need to focus on how the reflection will help them in the future.
