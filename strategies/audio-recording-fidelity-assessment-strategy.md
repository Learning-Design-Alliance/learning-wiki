# Assess fidelity of group courses via audio-recorded checklists plus speech-time didacticism scoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends evaluating a sample of delivered group sessions by audio-recording them and having independent raters score adherence checklists, group interaction, and overall impression, while computing didacticism from facilitator speech time using annotation software. Based on prior work "recommending that at least 25% of recordings should be evaluated", the study analysed 28% of courses, purposively selected so each facilitator was evaluated once. Independent raters were used because this "reduces the risk of bias".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Audio recordings of sessions, a facilitator's manual defining core items, independent raters, and annotation software for speech timing
#### Constraints
- Audio-only recording made facilitator techniques difficult to identify consistently, so that measure was omitted

### Target Learners
- adults with poorly controlled epilepsy attending self-management courses

### Target Learning Goals
- faithful delivery of self-management course content
