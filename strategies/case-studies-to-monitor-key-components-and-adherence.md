# Apply case study methods more widely to identify key intervention components and monitor adherence in surgical trials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend extending the tested case study approach beyond the pilot so that trial teams can agree on the components surgeons will deliver, monitor those components during the trial, and report adherence. They write that "Applying these methods more widely may help identify key components of interventions to be delivered by surgeons in trials, enabling monitoring of key components and adherence to the protocol." The methods are now being tested in other surgical RCTs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Case study work during an internal pilot or design phase, with surgeon agreement on key components before full trial evaluation
#### Constraints
- Investigating the entire intervention in this way may be time consuming and generate large volumes of data, per the article's discussion

### Target Learners
- trialists designing surgical RCTs
- surgeons delivering trial interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- standardise and describe interventions appropriately in trials
- monitor delivery of key intervention components

### Affordances
- Operating Theatre Case Study Methodology
