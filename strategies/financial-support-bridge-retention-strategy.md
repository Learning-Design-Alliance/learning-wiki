# Provide sufficient financial support — stipend, tuition, scholarships and health benefits — to remove financial barriers to PhD entry and retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues financial barriers disproportionately exclude URM talent: most scholars would work 20–30 hours per week in the absence of aid, impeding their academics, and even application costs can deter talented students. It concludes that a programme providing a sufficient stipend, tuition and health benefits is critical to student retention, particularly for vulnerable groups. The average Cal-Bridge scholarship of $8,000 fills the unmet needs of CSU students already receiving state and/or federal aid.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Funding for scholarships, stipends, tuition and health benefits across the bridging period
#### Constraints
- The article notes typical graduate stipends are at a subsistence level at best, and students with obligations beyond their own are often on the edge of financial insolvency

### Target Learners
- Low-income and first-generation undergraduates and bridge scholars in physics and astronomy

### Target Learning Goals
- Remaining on the PhD pathway without financial insolvency
