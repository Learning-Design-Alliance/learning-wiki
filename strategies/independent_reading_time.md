# Independent Reading Time

## Description
Independent Reading Time (also known as sustained silent reading, SSR, or DEAR time) allocates regular class time for students to read self-selected books silently. Unlike assigned reading, the defining feature is learner choice of text, paired with teacher conferencing, sharing routines, or other light accountability structures. It is carried out as a recurring block — typically 10–30 minutes — embedded within a broader literacy program rather than as the whole of reading instruction.

## Design Implications

Independent reading builds fluency, vocabulary, and background knowledge through volume of reading, and its effectiveness depends heavily on whether students experience autonomy and access to books they can and want to read [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. Unstructured "read and hope" implementations show weak or null effects; structured variants with teacher conferencing, goal-setting, and text access produce measurable comprehension and motivation gains [~M]. Time spent reading is necessary but not sufficient — the quality of engagement and fit between reader and text drive outcomes.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-stocked, diverse classroom library with texts at a range of readability levels and on topics students care about
- Self-selection of texts by students, with teacher guidance on choosing books they can read with reasonable fluency
- Protected, consistent time — not a filler activity to be cancelled when other demands arise
- Light accountability: reading conferences, reading logs, or peer book talks ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Teacher modeling of reading engagement ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) — e.g., the teacher reading alongside students)

#### Constraints
- Unstructured SSR with no interaction, goal-setting, or accountability shows negligible effects on comprehension in experimental comparisons [-M] — the National Reading Panel (2000) found insufficient evidence for SSR alone as an instructional approach
- Students reading texts far above their decoding level disengage or fake-read; without matching support, the time is lost [-M]
- Cannot substitute for explicit instruction in phonics, comprehension strategies, or [Disciplinary Literacy](../principles/disciplinary-literacy.md) — volume of reading amplifies instruction, it does not replace it [-S]
- When reading is tied to heavy accountability (points, grades per book), intrinsic motivation for reading can decline [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reading workshop** (Atwell, Calkins): mini-lesson → independent reading with conferring → share
- **Structured SSR**: silent reading plus brief written or oral response
- **Concept-Oriented Reading Instruction (CORI)**: self-selected reading organized around thematic science/social studies inquiry, with strategy instruction integrated
- **First Chapter Fridays / book tastings**: short exposure routines to build text-selection skills

### Target Learners
- Developing readers who need volume to consolidate fluency and automatic word recognition [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Reluctant or disengaged readers, for whom choice is often the decisive lever [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Advanced readers who need access to texts beyond grade-level instruction
- Least effective for students who cannot yet decode independently — these students need explicit foundational instruction first [-S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading fluency and stamina through sustained practice
- Vocabulary growth and background knowledge from wide reading
- Reading motivation and identity: becoming someone who chooses to read
- Independent application of comprehension strategies taught during instruction

### Instructions
1. Establish routines and expectations for the reading block (where books come from, what noise level, what to do when finished).
2. Ensure every student has a self-selected, appropriately matched book; use book talks or a [Choice Board](../elements/choice-boards.md) to support selection.
3. Open with a brief mini-lesson or goal-setting prompt (2–5 minutes) connecting to strategies taught in instruction.
4. Protect 10–30 minutes of silent reading; teacher confers with 2–4 students individually or reads alongside them.
5. Close with a brief share: partner book talks, one-sentence recommendations, or log entries ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
6. Track reading volume over time and adjust text access and scaffolds accordingly.

## Examples
- **[Reading Workshop](https://www.heinemann.com)** (Lucy Calkins, Teachers College Reading and Writing Project) — a published curriculum in which independent reading with conferring is the daily core.
- **CORI (Concept-Oriented Reading Instruction)** — Guthrie's research-based program integrating self-selected reading with inquiry themes; see [cori.umd.edu](http://www.cori.umd.edu).
- **DEAR (Drop Everything and Read)** — a widely adopted school-wide scheduled silent reading routine ([deread.org](https://deread.org)).
