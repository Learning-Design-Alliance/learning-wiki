# Differentiate cultivating perseverance from supporting intrinsic interest in exam-oriented EFL classrooms: effort-to-mastery pathways plus structured autonomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practitioner recommendations split into two strands matched to the two grit dimensions. For POE, "teachers can intentionally design effort-to-mastery pathways", breaking exam skills into incremental targets, giving feedback linking improvement to effort, and using progress-tracking tools to strengthen control appraisals. To buffer the COI–boredom mismatch, "teachers can introduce structured autonomy within structured curricula", offering limited topic choice, brief communicative activities after drills, and low-stakes student-selected sessions that preserve exam preparation demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires exam-oriented EFL classrooms where teachers retain some latitude to add choice and communicative activities within a test-aligned curriculum.
#### Constraints
- The article states that if evaluation remains predominantly high-stakes and rank-based, stable commitment may continue to be externally regulated and emotionally vulnerable.

### Target Learners
- Senior high school EFL students in exam-oriented systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Foreign language achievement
- sustained engagement and positive emotion in exam preparation
