# Use paired sheet writing, exchange, and mutual assessment with game-theoretic scoring to structure peer learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete classroom recipe: after the instructor teaches, students choose a teammate, briefly write about a given concept on a sheet, swap sheets, study the partner's sheet, return it, and fill in assessment of the teammate's sheet, self-assessment, and familiarity with the teammate. Scores based on both sheets are posted to an educational network. The article emphasizes "the written text must be understood by their teammate".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sheets include fields for teammate assessment, self-assessment, and familiarity with the teammate
- Scores are announced in the educational network after each execution
#### Constraints
- Mechanism is run at the end of some sessions after the instructor teaches the lesson

### Target Learners
- university students in computer engineering courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of session concepts such as programming instructions, discrete mathematics properties, and compiler concepts
