# Summer research internships at a nearby research institution as the substitute for in-house intermediate and advanced laboratory courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Physics majors take summer research internship course(s) after their sophomore and/or junior year 'as supplements to the theoretical courses', joining a research group at Columbia University's Nevis Research Laboratory in Irvington NY. The article presents this as the method for students to obtain laboratory experiences and training in lieu of conventional intermediate and advanced laboratory courses, which staffing and budget constraints make impractical at the college.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Close ties between home-institution faculty and faculty at the partner research institution, and geographic proximity to major research laboratories.
#### Constraints
- Full-fledged intermediate and/or advanced laboratory courses as part of the curriculum are impractical due to the status of the instructional laboratory, staffing and budgetary constraints at the college and departmental levels.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics majors, typically after sophomore and/or junior year

### Target Learning Goals
- Experimental training on modern instrumentation, techniques and methods
- Interaction with researchers and peer students in an active scientific environment
