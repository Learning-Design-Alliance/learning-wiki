Use the HeDiCom framework as a common reference and blueprint for ICT policy planning, with indicators to measure teachers' competencies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using the HeDiCom framework as a common reference providing an overview of digital competencies, thereby enhancing transparency of what is expected of Higher Education teachers and supporting the development of their expertise. In a best-case scenario the authors state "the HeDiCom Framework can serve as a blueprint for ICT policy planning in Higher Education institutions", used to provide insights on the strengths and weaknesses of HE teachers' digital competencies. This requires indicators describing what teachers should know and be able and willing to do, and future researchers might explore how the competencies can be measured.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Indicators that describe what exactly teachers should know and be able and willing to do in order to master the competencies underlying the HeDiCom sub-dimensions

Constraints¶

  • The article states the use of the framework should involve understanding of the historic, social, cultural, economic, and political contexts, and that the assumption that ICT can facilitate and improve teaching and learning processes is often value-laden and context specific

Target Learners¶

  • Higher Education teachers
  • Higher Education institutions and policy makers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Developing teachers' digital competencies
  • Guiding curriculum design and ICT policy planning

Claims¶

  • Experts argued teachers' digital literacy should be a separate dimension from professional development, and computational thinking was new to most participants [+W]
  • Validation sessions reframed fostering students' digital literacy as empowering students for a digital society, adding ethical competences [+W]
  • Compared to pre-Covid frameworks, HeDiCom foregrounds online and blended learning design and discipline-specific professional digital literacy [+W]
  • The HeDiCom framework was developed through a three-stage process of systematic review, framework comparison, and expert/practitioner validation [+W]
  • Educator-focused digital literacy frameworks prioritize pedagogy, empowering learners, and curriculum and assessment, with more evenly distributed categories [+W]
