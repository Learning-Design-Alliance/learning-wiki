# Implementing constructivist engagement elements within a traditional lecture format

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A set of implementable measures for fostering student communication and active engagement inside a curriculum-mandated lecture. The authors state they "implemented a variety of means fostering the students' communication among themselves and with us lecturers at best", including a well-structured lecture with short breaks, group-work phases on demanding calculations, professional learning groups via Moodle, weekly self-assessment questions, bonus tasks scored for the exam, a tutorial, time-coordinated exercises, weekly team meetings, and a Q&A session. The aim is to combine direct instruction with elements supporting active construction of knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination among lecturers, exercise instructors, and a tutor (weekly team meetings)
- A learning platform such as Moodle for PLGs, bonus tasks, and materials
#### Constraints
- The authors report access counts do not show whether cognitive processes were fostered, and SAQs were not used by the majority of students in the intended way

### Target Learners
- university students in large heterogeneous lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- active engagement and conceptual understanding
- peer exchange and self-organized learning
