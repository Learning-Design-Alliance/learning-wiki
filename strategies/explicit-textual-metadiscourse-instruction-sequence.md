# Explicitly teach four categories of textual metadiscourse connectives through modelling, repetition, elicitation, and controlled speaking practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's instructional treatment introduced one category of textual metadiscourse markers per session: additional connectives, contrastive connectives, sequencers, and summative connectives. For each category the teacher "introduced one category of metadiscourse markers to the class explicitly", modelled example sentences (e.g. a sequencer in a recipe), had students repeat and produce new sentences, then set controlled pair narration of an assigned story using only the newly learned markers, followed by learners talking about their own news. The control group only narrated the story and discussed news without instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who introduces, models, and elicits the markers session by session
- Classroom materials including a story book and students' own news pieces for speaking practice
#### Constraints
- Tested over four three-hour sessions with intermediate-level Iranian EFL university students; the article notes successful integration into EFL classrooms is dependent on teacher's method

### Target Learners
- Intermediate-level EFL university students (ages 22-35)

### Target Learning Goals
- Speaking ability, including use of textual metadiscourse markers in oral production
