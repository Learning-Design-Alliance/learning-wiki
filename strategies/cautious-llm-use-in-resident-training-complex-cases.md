# Use LLMs cautiously in ophthalmology resident training, particularly for complex clinical decision-making cases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that although LLMs may be useful educational tools in ophthalmology, "they should be used cautiously in resident training, particularly when addressing complex clinical decision-making cases", because LLM performance was relatively lower on problem-solving-type questions and none significantly outperformed trainees on that subtype. LLMs are suggested as supplementary tools for reinforcing foundational learning and as support in structured case-based reasoning, and may be more appropriately regarded as advanced clinical decision support systems rather than autonomous clinical agents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- LLM outputs should be checked for incorrect answers and false information, as LLMs sometimes generate incorrect answers to fit their responses
#### Constraints
- Relatively lower accuracy on problem-solving-type questions requiring nuanced prioritization, procedural judgment, or experience-based reasoning; multimodal image-interpretation tasks were not evaluated

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents
- cornea fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational ophthalmology knowledge
- structured case-based reasoning
- complex clinical decision-making
