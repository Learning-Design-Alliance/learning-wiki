# Add SDL components to nursing curricula and teach SDL skills through training courses, workshops, and problem-based, student-centered methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that professors and administrators in nursing schools actively cultivate self-directed learning to enhance clinical competence. The conclusion states that "Adding SDL components to the nursing curricula is essential also nursing professors need to teach SDL skills to students through training courses," and the abstract suggests educational workshops and changing instructors' educational methods. It also points to problem-based and student-centered curricula as ways to improve teaching. The recommendation rests on the observed weak but significant correlation between SDL and competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nursing professors and curriculum administrators must incorporate SDL components and deliver SDL skills training courses
#### Constraints
- The supporting correlation is weak and derived from self-report measures in northwestern Iran, limiting generalization

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed learning ability
- clinical competence in nursing
