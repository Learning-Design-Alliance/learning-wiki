# Design blended learning through eight sequential stages from learner readiness to cooperation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article prescribes an eight-stage structure for designing blended learning programs, spanning formal pedagogical events and informal blended activities. The stages run from assuring learner readiness through representing subject matter, showing the work process, exercise, evaluation, support, lectureship, and cooperation. The authors applied this structure in designing their blended critical-thinking course for medical sciences students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers must sequence all eight stages, beginning with assurance of learner readiness and ending with cooperative activities
#### Constraints
- The article notes presenting live classes is difficult in many cases because of limitations of electronic teaching bases

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical sciences students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- academic achievement
- active self-learning
