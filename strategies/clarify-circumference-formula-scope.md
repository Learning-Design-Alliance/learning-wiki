# Make explicit in the intervention that the traditional circumference calculation is correct only under specific circumstances

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After one follow-up participant reported that the intervention's curved-space circumference activity was contradicted in high school by the standard formula, the authors recommend reducing this confusion by "making it clear in the intervention that the 'traditional' circumferenc e calculation is correct, but only under speciﬁc circumstances". The strategy pairs curved-space activities with an explicit statement of when flat-space formulas apply.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An intervention activity, such as measuring circumference around a curved surface, that could otherwise appear to contradict later flat-space school formulas.
#### Constraints
- The contradiction arises when Newtonian physics and flat space-time are treated as the rule, not the exception, in later schooling.

### Target Learners
- Upper primary students who will later encounter standard flat-space formulas in secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding curved space-time without later perceived contradiction
