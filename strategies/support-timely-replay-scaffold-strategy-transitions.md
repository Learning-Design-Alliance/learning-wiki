# Design digital learning environments to support timely (immediate) replay and scaffold transitions toward efficient strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that learning environments should make replay available at the right moment rather than uniformly. It reports that "Immediate Replay was consistently and positively associated with students' proximal and distal learning outcomes, while Delayed Replay was either unrelated or negatively associated." The authors conclude that "Digital learning environments that support timely replay, encourage adaptive exploration, and scaffold transitions toward efficient strategies may be particularly effective in fostering flexibility." This positions replay timing as a designable feature of game-based practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The environment must offer replay as an optional feature students can choose, as FH2T does, and log attempt sequences so immediate versus delayed replay can be distinguished.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a secondary log analysis of one algebra game with seventh graders; associations are correlational, and the authors note motivational factors were not accounted for in these analyses.

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade algebra students in middle school mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- algebraic conceptual knowledge
- procedural knowledge
- procedural flexibility
