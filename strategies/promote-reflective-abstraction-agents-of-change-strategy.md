# Promote reflective abstraction through instructor, peer, and curricular agents of change in mathematics teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's closing recommendation is that mathematics teachers promote reflective abstraction deliberately by acting as an agent of change, structuring peer challenge, and designing curriculum activities that question students. As the article puts it, "Teachers should promote reflective abstraction using instructor, peer, and curricular as agents of change." It further recommends designing problem sets that let students initiate reflective abstraction independently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should design problem sets that enable students to initiate reflective abstraction independently
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study on the limit concept in college calculus
