# Teach foundational MI skills through didactic instruction plus a simulated interview that students transcribe, self-rate with the MITI tool, and reflect on in a written assignment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This interprofessional teaching sequence combines one MI lecture, two mixed-discipline tutorials practising in triads, then a recorded motivational interview with a family member or friend that students transcribe and self-code with the MITI, followed by a reflective assignment on what they would do differently. The article reports that "students were required to apply their learnings by conducting a motivational interview with a family member or friend involving physical activity and/or exercise". Reflective self-assessment against a validated tool is presented as a resource-efficient way to build competence and prepare students for reflective practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- instruction on MI principles, practice opportunities with tutorial support, the MITI tool with sample questions and a decisional balance list, and guided reflective assignment criteria
#### Constraints
- MI skills tend to decay over time and training needs supervised interviewing and feedback to maintain quality; using family or friends as interviewees may produce staged interviews

### Target Learners
- graduate-entry health professional students in interprofessional core topics

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational motivational interviewing skills including evocation, collaboration, autonomy support, direction and empathy

### Affordances
- Mezirow Transformative Learning Reflection Taxonomy
