State and federal policy recommendations: recruit and prepare more candidates, retain new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improve working conditions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends seven "evidence-based approaches for resolving teacher shortages, which focus on recruiting and preparing more candidates, retaining new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improving teacher working conditions." These include federal action on compensation (debt-free college, teacher tax credits of at least $5,000 annually and up to $12,500 in highest-poverty schools), a statewide recruitment navigation initiative, community-college-to-university "2+2" pathways, capacity-building grants for preparation programs in high-demand fields, stronger uptake of Golden State Teacher Grant scholarships, cultivating NBCT mentors for novice teachers, and a teacher working-conditions survey plus staff wellness programs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Ongoing state investments and complementary federal actions; adequate preparation-program capacity; high-quality mentoring and induction for new teachers

Constraints¶

  • The report notes the federal government has a limited role in addressing locally set salaries, and that preparation programs' admissions may be constrained by university funding rules

Target Learners¶

  • Prospective teacher candidates, novice teachers, and students in high-need schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • A stable, fully prepared teacher workforce; reduced turnover; uninterrupted student learning

Related Strategies¶

  • Four-part state policy agenda: coherent administration, universal affordability, a well-qualified workforce, and quality improvement for all programs
  • Seven-policy comprehensive approach to equalizing student access to certified and experienced teachers
  • A federal Marshall Plan for Teaching focused on seven areas: compensation, debt-free preparation, high-retention pathways, mentoring, expertise-sharing, school redesign, and accountability
  • Underwrite comprehensive preparation, improve teaching conditions, raise compensation, and expand induction to retain teachers of color
  • Develop high-retention pathways — Grow Your Own programs and teacher residencies — to build and diversify local teacher supply
  • State policy actions across the career continuum: attract, prepare, and develop/support/retain educators
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
