# Time microsurgical skills courses early in postgraduate education, before trainees begin operating on patients, to hasten learning and reduce patient complications.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that "an opportunity to hasten learning and reduce patient complications is missed" when the course is mandated only after selection onto the training programme, and suggests that "Mandating this course for junior doctors in their first year of being a pre-vocational ophthalmology registrar should be considered". It notes that by the time doctors are selected for RANZCO training, many have already had some operating time, and that the posterior capsular rupture rate of new surgeons can be as high as 14%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination with training programme selection timelines
- Careful scheduling so trainees have time to relocate before commencing training
#### Constraints
- Some RANZCO trainees with prior surgical experience found some stations too basic for their skill level

### Target Learners
- Pre-vocational junior doctors
- First-year ophthalmology trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Ophthalmic microsurgical skills
- Patient safety
