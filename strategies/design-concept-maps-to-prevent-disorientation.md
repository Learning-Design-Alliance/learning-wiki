# Design concept maps so learners can find a meaningful reading order and do not feel disoriented

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
For practitioners, the article recommends that instructional designers place primary emphasis on creating concept maps that do not cause feelings of disorientation, particularly by supporting learners in finding a meaningful reading order through the map. If this prerequisite is met, learners can construct a mental model of both the map's physical structure and its semantic representation, including learners with low prior knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The map must make an easily identifiable navigation path visible so learners can maintain a meaningful reading order
#### Constraints
- Results come from a single biology topic with university students; generalization to other school types, subjects, or ages requires replications

### Target Learners
- students with low prior knowledge learning complex interrelated content

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing disorientation and extraneous processing when learning with concept maps
