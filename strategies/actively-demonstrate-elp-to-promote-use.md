# Actively demonstrate and announce an e-learning platform within the curriculum to promote its broad adoption

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude from their observational data that passive promotion is insufficient: announcement by group mail, lecture announcements, billboards and flyers did not stimulate exploration of the platform, whereas a live in-curriculum demonstration produced a sharp activity peak. They write that "the active announcement of an e-learning platform is necessary to promote its broad use among students". The strategy is therefore to embed a live demonstration of the platform into regular teaching sessions at launch.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the target student cohort within an existing curricular session (e.g., a dissection course) where the platform can be demonstrated live
#### Constraints
- Evidence is observational, from one cohort and one demonstration event; the authors call for systematic studies to predict and explain e-learning platform use

### Target Learners
- first-year medical (human and dental medicine) students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed use of curated digital textbooks and e-learning resources in medical study
