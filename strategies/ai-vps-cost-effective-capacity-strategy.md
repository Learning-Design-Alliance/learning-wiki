# Use AI-driven virtual patient simulators as a cost-effective supplement to build communication skills training capacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend AI-driven virtual patient simulator training as a scalable, asynchronous complement to actor-based simulation for expanding communication skills training capacity. They conclude that "AI-CST may therefore provide a cost-effective solution to building capacity for teaching communication in health care professionals," given its substantially lower per-student cost and demonstrated, though smaller, self-reported skill gains. The strategy targets capacity constraints where clinical space and supervisor capacity limit trainee numbers, while retaining actor-based simulation where richer interpersonal feedback is needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the voice-recognition VPS platform and suitable devices with microphone and speaker
- Rubrics aligned to intended learning outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article reports AI-CST was slightly inferior to AB-CST in attainment gains and satisfaction
- Evidence comes from a single short session with relative novices; the authors caution against extrapolation to other training stages and call for research on sustained improvement

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students
- Health care professional trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication and consultation skills
- Training capacity expansion
