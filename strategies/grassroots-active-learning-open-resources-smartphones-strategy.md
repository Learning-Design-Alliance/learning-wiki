# Implement active learning grassroots-style in developing-country universities using open educational resources, local context-rich editing, and students' smartphones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A bottom-up implementation recipe in which instructors adopt open educational resources developed elsewhere, edit them "to generate context-rich activities with local context and practical relevance", run active learning across lectures, tutorials, and assignments all semester, and use the free Google Classroom app accessed on students' smartphones instead of clickers. The article reports this reduced costs, better engaged students inside and outside class, and required no dedicated resources, making the model applicable to other MENA and developing-country universities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors coordinate transformation across multisection courses and keep examinations at the same level as traditional offerings through existing departmental review practice
#### Constraints
- Policy makers should take into account the available resources and cultural contexts to achieve the desired outcomes, per the article

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and astronomy students in developing countries, including nonnative English speakers

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of physics
- critical thinking
- collaborative and technical skills
- student engagement

## Claims

- [Active learning reduced the failure rate by 66% overall, with students on average 2.98 times more likely to fail under traditional teaching](../claims/active-learning-mena-failure-rate-reduction.md) [+W]
- [MENA students showed a considerably larger failure-rate reduction (36% greater) than reported in developed-country meta-analyses under comparable active-learning approaches](../claims/active-learning-mena-greater-failure-reduction-than-developed-countries.md) [+W]
- [The lowest-performing score tail was eliminated in active-learning offerings, with students below ~25 points appearing exclusively in traditional offerings](../claims/active-learning-mena-low-tail-eliminated.md) [+W]
- [Active learning improved mean course performance by 9% (g = 0.48) across five physics and astronomy courses in a MENA university](../claims/active-learning-mena-mean-gain-g048.md) [+W]
- [Active learning narrowed the performance gap between top-75% and lowest-25% students by 16.56% (from 38.64 to 32.24 points)](../claims/active-learning-mena-performance-gap-narrowed.md) [+W]
- [Upper grades (A+B) increased by 8.09 to 73.60% across the five courses while the middle grade C remained nearly unchanged](../claims/active-learning-mena-upper-grades-increased.md) [+W]
