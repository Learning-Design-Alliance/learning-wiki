Collaborative Strategic Reading¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 14 studies (5 quant-synthesis, 4 causal, 4 review, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 5 of 14 report an effect size

Description¶

Collaborative Strategic Reading (CSR) is a research-validated instructional approach that combines reading comprehension strategy instruction with cooperative learning. Students work in small, heterogeneous groups to apply four strategies to a text: preview (activating prior knowledge and predicting), click and clunk (monitoring comprehension and fixing breakdowns in vocabulary or concepts), get the gist (identifying the main idea of a section), and wrap up (generating questions and summarizing). Each student takes a role (leader, clunk expert, gist expert, announcer, timekeeper), and the teacher first models the strategies through think-alouds before students assume responsibility in their groups.

Design Implications¶

CSR operationalizes the finding that explicit strategy instruction improves comprehension, and that strategy use transfers better when practiced in socially supported, structured peer settings Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement. [+S]. The fixed strategy sequence externalizes the monitoring that skilled readers do silently, reducing the working-memory and metacognitive demands of deciding what to do while reading Chunking reduces working memory load. [+M]. Roles and clunk cards function as scaffolding that is progressively faded as students internalize the strategies Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Expository or narrative text divided into sections, so strategies can be applied at natural stopping points
  • Teacher modeling of each strategy (especially "get the gist," which students find hardest) before group work
  • Role assignments and cue cards ("clunk cards") that specify fix-up steps: reread, read on, break the word apart, use context, ask a peer
  • Group-level and individual accountability structures, not just group participation

Constraints¶

  • Gist statements frequently degrade into copying sentences verbatim or listing details rather than synthesizing main ideas [-M] — this requires ongoing teacher feedback at the process level Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S]
  • Groups without trained roles or individual accountability drift into social talk with little strategy use [-M]
  • Implementation fidelity drops when teachers skip the modeling phase; students then apply strategy labels mechanically without understanding [-M]
  • Benefits diminish for students who already use these strategies spontaneously [~W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • CSR in content areas: applied to science and social studies texts, where "click and clunk" targets technical vocabulary
  • Digital adaptations: online platforms (e.g., CSR-based tools developed by the University of Texas, https://csrreading.org) embed clunk supports and gist prompts
  • Intensity: implemented as daily lessons over 8–15 weeks in efficacy trials, versus occasional use, which shows weaker effects
  • Pairing with writing: gist statements can be extended into written summaries

Target Learners¶

  • Upper-elementary through secondary students reading expository text [+S]
  • English learners and students with reading difficulties, who benefit from explicit fix-up routines and peer discussion [+M]
  • Mixed-ability groups: higher-achieving students deepen understanding by explaining strategies to peers Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension of expository text, especially main-idea identification and summarization
  • Metacognitive monitoring: recognizing and repairing comprehension breakdowns
  • Collaborative discourse skills: turn-taking, explaining, and building on peers' ideas

Instructions¶

  1. Teach the strategies — Model preview, click and clunk, get the gist, and wrap up through whole-class think-alouds over several days before any group work.
  2. Form groups and assign roles — Create heterogeneous groups of 4–5 with cooperative roles; establish individual accountability norms Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement. [+S].
  3. Preview — Students brainstorm what they know about the topic and predict content, activating prior knowledge.
  4. Read and click-and-clunk — Students read a section silently, identify "clunks" (breakdowns), and apply fix-up strategies with clunk cards; the clunk expert records words for class review.
  5. Get the gist — The gist expert leads the group in stating the main idea of the section in about ten words, with teacher circulation to give process feedback Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S].
  6. Wrap up — Students generate review questions and a summary of the whole passage.
  7. Fade supports — Gradually remove cue cards and role scripts as students internalize the strategy sequence Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [+M].

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — the closest relative; shares prediction, question generation, and summarizing but uses dialogue roles rather than CSR's fix-up and gist routines
  • Jigsaw — an alternative cooperative reading structure that distributes content rather than strategies across group members
  • Concept-Oriented Reading Instruction — combines strategy instruction with engagement and inquiry goals

Examples¶

  • Klingner and Vaughn's efficacy studies — Fourth-grade and middle-school classrooms in which CSR students, including English learners, outperformed comparison students on comprehension measures, with the largest gains for students with lower reading achievement.
  • CSR Colorado / csrreading.org — A statewide professional-development implementation providing CSR lesson templates, clunk cards, and coaching materials for middle-grade science and social studies teachers.
  • Content-area implementation — A middle-school science teacher applies CSR to a textbook chapter on ecosystems: students preview headings, log "clunks" like decomposer, agree on a gist for each section, and finish with wrap-up questions used as an exit ticket.
