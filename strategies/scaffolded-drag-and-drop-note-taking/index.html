Scaffold note-taking in learning apps with drag-and-drop information items rather than free-form input¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

When learners must collect and summarize information while reading, restrict note-taking to drag-and-drop selection of pre-defined information items instead of free keyboard input. This scaffolds the note-taking process — "a pedagogical process experts use to help learners perform tasks they cannot do by themselves" — so users can complete the task within their zone of proximal development. The article adopted this after focus groups showed frustration with free-form notes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The information items offered for dragging must correspond to the key facts the assessment will require

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • 5th grade students
  • adult literacy learners
  • struggling readers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • identifying and storing important information while reading
  • building an evidence-based argument

Claims¶

  • User testing showed free-form note-taking frustrated users, prompting a redesign to drag-and-drop scaffolding that reduced frustration and improved engagement [+W]
  • After app use, students were significantly better at identifying factors affecting sleep quality and benefits of later school start times, but not effects of sleep deprivation [+W]
  • Students in courses with guided note-taking and other active learning activities reported less difficulty with motivation and engagement after the shift online [+W]
