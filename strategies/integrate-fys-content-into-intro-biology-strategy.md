# Integrate rather than add FYS content when embedding first-year seminar activities in an introductory biology course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that instructors incorporating FYS activities into introductory biology "aim to integrate, rather than add, the FYS content by refocusing disciplinary content and assignments to encourage orientation to the campus, interaction with the biology community, and exploration of personal interests." Examples include a library-collaborative literature search assignment that teaches citations while introducing the library as a campus resource, and restricting final projects to on-campus research to promote networking and belonging. Having the biology faculty member teach both components maximizes integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness to refocus existing disciplinary content and assignments rather than adding separate FYS components
#### Constraints
- FYS activities in an introductory biology course may be viewed as competing with biology content and instructor time

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate students in introductory biology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- orient students to campus resources
- build interaction with the biology community
- support exploration of personal interests and career goals
