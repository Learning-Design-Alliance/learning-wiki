# Use editing checklists and correction symbols so students identify and correct their own mechanical errors rather than having the teacher correct them.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the editing stage, students use teacher-provided editing checklists to check and correct their own mistakes such as capitalization, punctuation, spelling, and grammar. The teacher does not edit students' writing directly but comments and uses correction symbols (e.g., S for spelling error, WO for word order, G for grammar mistake) to help students think about their mistakes and correct them by themselves. According to Tribble, editing checklists enable students to focus more on specific points in the editing stage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher provides editing checklists and correction symbols
- Students go through their writing line by line
#### Constraints
- Some students need a teacher conference to understand their mistakes and make necessary adjustments

### Target Learners
- EFL college students

### Target Learning Goals
- independent error identification and correction
- improved mechanical accuracy in writing
