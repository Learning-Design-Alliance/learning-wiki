Using virtual laboratories to enable otherwise impossible experimental work and dynamic submicroscopic visualization¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend integrating virtual laboratories into science and chemistry teaching, listing advantages: they "enable experimental work that would otherwise be impossible due to economical, spatial, time, and other reasons", enable visualization at all three levels, provide dynamic presentations of the submicro world of particles, contribute to better understanding, and serve as a motivational tool familiar to pupils. They state the findings "empirically verifies the need for educational strategy changes regarding science and chemistry didactics".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • advanced information and communication technology available to pupils; educational software with experiment collections

Constraints¶

  • classical experimental work remains the most commonly used form of practical work in elementary schools; experiments in schools are limited by safety, infrastructure, time and space

Target Learners¶

  • primary school pupils learning science and chemistry

Target Learning Goals¶

  • forming mental models at the submicroscopic level; achieving higher cognitive levels of analysis, synthesis and evaluation

Affordances¶

  • Three Levels Chemical Concept Presentation

Claims¶

  • The experimental group outperformed the control group on every one of the five visualization tasks individually [+W]
  • Seventh-grade pupils taught chemical content with a virtual laboratory acquired more knowledge than pupils taught without dynamic visualization [+W]
  • Pupils taught with a virtual laboratory solved tasks involving visualization elements more successfully than control-group pupils [+W]
  • The virtual-laboratory advantage did not hold on all visualization tasks: task 11 showed no statistically significant group difference [-W]
  • Combining analogy-based simulation with laboratory activities produces significantly higher corrected post-test achievement in simple electricity than either environment alone [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use computer-based simulations to teach chemistry topics that are difficult to demonstrate, as a gender-friendly bridge of the achievement gap
