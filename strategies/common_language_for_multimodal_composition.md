# Common Language for Multimodal Composition

## Description
Creating a shared vocabulary or metalanguage empowers learners to analyze and discuss their multimodal composition process. This involves selecting one mode (linguistic, visual, audio, gestural, or spatial), analyzing examples, and creating a class definition. Pointing out how each mode is used in different subjects and contexts helps learners understand its versatility and application.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires time for discussion and analysis. Needs anchor charts or digital platforms for recording terms and definitions.
#### Constraints
- May require significant time investment to build a comprehensive glossary. Learners may struggle with abstract concepts if not grounded in concrete examples.

### Affordances
- Provides a foundation for analyzing and discussing multimodal works. Enhances understanding of how different modes function and interact.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by allowing learners to choose the modes they want to analyze. Scaffolding can be provided by pre-selecting examples and guiding discussions.
