# Use short, hands-on, standards-integrated computer vision PD open to teachers of all subject areas rather than limiting it to computer science teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that computer vision professional development be hands-on, brief (two to three weeks proved sufficient to shift perceptions), and open to teachers of all majors and subject areas: "we recommend that computer vision educational experiences for K-12 students and teachers be open to all majors and subject areas," since isolating experiences to a specific group limits exposure and access. They further recommend developing integrated computer vision lessons aligned with academic standards and having administrators support teachers' efforts to thread computer vision concepts into standard content areas through policy and PD opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free, accessible tools requiring no programming knowledge
- Administrative support through policy and PD opportunities
#### Constraints
- There are no adopted standards for computer vision, which can pose a challenge for teachers implementing new curriculum
- Teachers are often required to stick to mandated curriculum and may struggle to find time and space for novel content

### Target Learners
- K-12 in-service teachers across subject areas
- Middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Computer vision knowledge and classroom integration
- Understanding of AI concepts through computer vision
