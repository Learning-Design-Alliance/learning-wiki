# Instructors use framing-based insights to devise strategic plans for in-class group work that promote productive framing and student-induced frame shifts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors use the study's insights "to devise a strategic plan for in-class group work to promote productive framing and effective frame shifts that can be induced by the students themselves". The strategy rests on the finding that group members discuss, share, negotiate or challenge each other's framing and that frame shifts can be driven by consensus (alpha) or by an approved bid (beta). It applies to naturalistic classroom settings where groups solve problems together on shared whiteboards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Group members must be able to discuss, share, negotiate or challenge each other's framing during problem solving.
#### Constraints
- The article states instructor interactions with groups affect students' framing, but instructor-initiated shifts were not investigated in this study, so this strategy addresses student-induced shifts only.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- productive epistemic framing during group problem solving
- effective student-initiated frame shifts
