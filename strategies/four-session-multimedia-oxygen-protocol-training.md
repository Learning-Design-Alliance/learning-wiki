# Deliver standardized oxygen-therapy training as four 45-minute small-group sessions combining lecture, discussion, and multimedia

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention trained nurses in five groups, each receiving four 45-minute sessions covering oxygen-therapy concepts, toxicity, and the nurse's role, delivered with "PowerPoint, colored photo, animation, and short videos" and methods such as group discussion, brainstorming, and reflective thinking. Sessions were held in hospital conference rooms and ended with a post-test identical to the pre-test.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions require hospital coordination with the medical director and nurse manager, and a post-test to assess knowledge change
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in Sakaka city only; the authors state results should be applied across the Jouf region to generalize

### Target Learners
- hospital nurses in ICUs, CCUs, emergency, pediatric, and medical/surgical units

### Target Learning Goals
- safe administration of oxygen therapy
- prevention and management of oxygen toxicity

- [Patients in the intervention group have fewer oxygen-therapy complications, with 62% having no complications versus 10.9% in the control group](../claims/protocol-intervention-reduces-oxygen-complications.md) [+W]
