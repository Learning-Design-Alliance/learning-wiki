# Extend competency-driven procedural training to other under-taught clinical skills and resource-limited settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend applying their competency-driven training model beyond radial hemostasis, noting it "could also be applied to other clinical settings such as arterial cannulation training, hemostasis protocols following central venous catheter removal, and hemostasis management following percutaneous vascular interventions." They emphasize it improves efficacy without added economic burden, making it relevant where advanced hemostasis devices are unavailable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of procedures currently learned by observation or empirical approaches, plus baseline assessment, didactic instruction, and supervised competency verification
#### Constraints
- The single-center evidence base involved anesthesiology residents only; the authors call for future work on augmented-reality pressure feedback and longitudinal competency maintenance

### Target Learners
- residents and early-career clinicians in settings where procedures are learned informally

### Target Learning Goals
- standardized procedural technique for under-taught bedside skills such as catheter removal and hemostasis
