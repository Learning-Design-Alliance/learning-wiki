# Design field experiments on AI use with attention to perception, group dynamics, an outside option, and role-switching alternatives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on the failed deployment of their randomized experiment, the authors recommend that future experimenters attend to how participants perceive fairness, not only its real aspects: "Perception, group dynamics, and the existence of outside options seem to be crucial elements." They advise notifying students before course registration, monitoring emotional conditions via weekly surveys or discussion sessions, and providing an outside option, which also helps defend the experiment legally. Alternative designs in which students switch roles during the experiment could prevent negative reception.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Notify students about a planned study before course registration, and monitor participants' emotional conditions closely, for example with weekly surveys or discussion sessions.
#### Constraints
- Role-switching designs "remain problematic if the difficulty of the material is uneven: students who are allowed to use AI during the more demanding period would be perceived as advantaged", and period-based designs make results harder to interpret because they may reflect differences in the material.

### Target Learners
- University students in field-experiment courses
- Researchers conducting behavioral field experiments in education

### Target Learning Goals
- Ethical measurement of student learning behavior
- Valid assessment of AI tools' impact on learning outcomes
