# Use the serial-cue method on an IWB with progressive patient reveal, hypothesis writing, and raw-data annotation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy structures each IWB/CRL session around the serial-cue method: one student plays a previously examined patient and progressively reveals clinical signs while others play the doctor and record information on the IWB. The facilitator prompts students to express and write clinical questions and hypotheses, then provides digitized raw test data onscreen for annotation of nonclinical signs. Sessions conclude by returning to the initial complaint so students prepare a summary, and the final paperboard is provided as a .pdf file.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator (physician or resident) who regularly asks students why they requested certain information or ordered a test
- One student willing to play the patient role and progressively reveal clinical signs
- Pre-digitized raw test data in JPEG format for onscreen display and annotation
#### Constraints
- Sessions in this study lasted between 60 and 90 minutes with four to seven students per session
- Because paperboards rather than sessions were analysed directly, some learning outcomes may have been underestimated

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in small-group clinical reasoning training

### Target Learning Goals
- Expressing clinical questions and hypotheses
- Ordering and interpreting diagnostic tests
- Identifying nonclinical signs on raw test data
