# Design social architectures that expose medium-performing students to similar and slightly higher-performing peers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study recommends designing online social environments so that medium-performing students are grouped with peers of similar and slightly higher performance, since peer influence was strongest for this group. The authors note that medium performers' social context "combines their sufficient level of prior knowledge with an optimal amount of similarity and difference." Low-performing students, by contrast, may benefit more from instructor presence and scaffolding than from peer interaction with similar peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of students' relative performance levels to identify medium-performing learners
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from pre-pandemic forum interaction data in three universities and may not generalize to all online learning contexts

### Target Learners
- Medium-performing university students in online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance improvement through peer interaction
