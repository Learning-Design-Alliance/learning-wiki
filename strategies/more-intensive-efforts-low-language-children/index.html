Provide more intensive language supports than conversation-based teacher PD for children with substantial language lags¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the conversation-based LLLI approach yielded no long-term vocabulary benefit and benefited only initially high-skilled children, the authors argue that "improving young children's language skills, speciﬁcally vocabulary, may require more intensive efforts than are currently being trialed." They suggest it may be time to consider fresh alternatives for children with the most intractable language problems, and point to avenues beyond the classroom such as elaborative reminiscing at home and frequent multi-turn caregiver-child conversations.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Approaches must go beyond improving children's engagement in high-quality conversations across the day, which the authors judge may not be sufficient for children with substantial lags

Constraints¶

  • The article frames this as a need for future work rather than a tested alternative

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children from low-SES backgrounds with low language skills

Target Learning Goals¶

  • expressive vocabulary
  • language skills supporting the transition to reading

Claims¶

  • The amount of language growth children made during the preschool intervention year did not predict kindergarten outcomes and did not alter the moderated treatment effects [+W]
  • The kindergarten benefits of the LLLI intervention on decoding and reading comprehension were conditional on children's initial preschool language skill, favoring children with high language skill [+W]
  • LLLI exposure in preschool produced no statistically significant main effects on kindergarten expressive vocabulary, decoding, or passage comprehension [+W]
  • Initial preschool language skill did not moderate the LLLI intervention effect on kindergarten expressive vocabulary [~W]
