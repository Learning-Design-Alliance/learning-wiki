Examine the causal relationship between arts involvement and academic performance with experimental designs controlling for IQ and motivation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend moving beyond correlational longitudinal surveys to designs that can identify causality in arts-transfer research. They state: "Future research should examine the causal relationship between art involvement and academic performance by controlling for other factors (e.g., IQ, motivation, etc.)". They further call for experimental designs with balanced participant numbers across comparison groups, noting this study's free-will club choice means effects may involve the choice to participate as well as participation itself.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Experimental or quasi-experimental designs that control for confounds such as IQ and motivation, and balanced numbers of participants across club-type and non-participation comparisons

Constraints¶

  • The article notes impacts may differ between race, regional and cultural backgrounds, requiring meta-surveys of these differences
  • The article states correlational designs cannot specify causal relationships

Target Learners¶

  • school-aged children in junior high school

Target Learning Goals¶

  • establishing causal effects of arts education on general academic performance

Claims¶

  • Extracurricular music participation shows significant positive indirect effects on general academic performance mediated by music grade points in a two-year longitudinal SEM [+M]
  • The direct effect of extracurricular music participation on general academic performance is significantly negative after controlling for SES and music score [~M]
  • Extracurricular visual arts participation shows significant positive indirect effects on general academic performance via visual arts grades, with a marginal total effect in the full sample [+M]
  • In a subsample excluding extracurricular sports participants, the total effect of visual arts ECA on academic performance is significantly positive [+M]
  • An inverse model assuming academic performance mediates the link between arts ECA and art grades finds no significant mediating role of academic performance [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Move beyond explorative expert-novice comparisons to hypothesis-driven designs investigating application of evidence-based pedagogical principles
  • Use longitudinal designs combining experimental manipulation with alternating measurement of motivation and achievement to establish causal reciprocity
  • Future research should replicate the model in diverse educational domains and use experimental and intervention methods
