# STEM education frameworks should be empirically evaluated against workplace data collected directly from employees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that frameworks used to define STEM outcomes, such as the 21st-century-skills frameworks and the ABET engineering criteria, be checked against standardized occupational data collected from job incumbents, because these frameworks "are developed by experts based on literature review or data collected from employers and educational leaders, rather than from data collected from employees." The study demonstrates the approach by analyzing O*NET ratings from about 50,000 workers and comparing the results with each framework's categories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a standardized, regularly updated occupational database such as O*NET with ratings collected from job incumbents and job analysts
#### Constraints
- The argument rests on one occupational database and one snapshot (O*NET 18.1, released March 2014) with a 2.95 importance cutoff chosen by the authors

### Target Learners
- STEM students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Alignment of STEM curriculum outcomes with workplace-required competencies
