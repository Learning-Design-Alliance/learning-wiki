# Design future interventions that nudge students toward exploratory, reflective practice behaviors (many sets/unsets with deliberate pacing) in open-ended tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes as future work to "design an intervention to lead students to practice behaviors indicative of improved learning." The high-learning behavior profile is exemplified by the thoughtful searchers cluster, whose defining feature is "using many sets and unsets and taking some time between the actions," interpreted as exploring options and reflecting before acting. A system could encourage such exploration and reflection during open-ended practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the ability to detect or log exploration actions (sets/unsets) and action pacing in real time to target the intervention.
#### Constraints
- The intervention itself was not evaluated in this article; the behavior-learning link was established post-hoc through clustering, and the fast explorers show exploration without reflection yields intermediate gains.

### Target Learners
- adult online learners in open-ended learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- effective exploration of the solution space in open-ended reasoning tasks
