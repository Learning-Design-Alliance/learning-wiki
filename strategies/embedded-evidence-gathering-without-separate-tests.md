# Gather formative evidence through embedded, discipline-specific tasks rather than separate tests or language assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief shows that evidence of content and language learning can be gathered through observing, questioning, listening, analyzing work, and conversations, interpreted against success criteria. It states: "teachers do not need to have a separate test or quiz to learn where students are in relation to the learning goals, nor do they need to have separate language activities or assessments." For example, students color-code reference chains in science texts, explain their thinking, or write their own texts, which the teacher reviews for evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers deliberately plan opportunities during lessons when student learning is visible or audible, such as ideas written, spoken, drawn, or acted

### Target Learners
- English learners, including those at mixed language levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Discipline-specific language comprehension and production, e.g., reference chains in science texts
