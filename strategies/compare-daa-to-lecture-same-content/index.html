Compare discovery-based activities to a lecture on identical content to isolate the benefit of direct manipulation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends evaluating DAA pedagogies against traditional instruction using controlled comparisons in which both conditions address the same content knowledge. In this study, both the DAA activity and the lecture addressed part-function relationships of the same camera, allowing evaluations on multiple dimensions of learning such as factual recall and multiple transfer measures. With this level of control, the authors argue, any resulting significant differences between conditions suggest benefits inherent to the manipulative process in DAA activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Both instructional conditions must be designed to address the same content knowledge
  • Well-established measures of motivation should be added to ensure a more complete picture of effects

Constraints¶

  • The previous study's comparison pushed for learning different content across conditions, raising the concern that results could be due to attention to part-function relationships rather than direct manipulation

Target Learners¶

  • first-year engineering students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • evaluating the pedagogical value of hands-on disassembly activities

Claims¶

  • On camera doctor defect-diagnosis questions the conditions did not differ overall, but DAA students generated better malfunction explanations [~W]
  • Gender differences in prior disassembly experience and perceived competence did not affect DAA learning or transfer outcomes [~W]
  • A DAA activity is rated significantly more motivating than a lecture on perceived sense of learning, enjoyment, and helpfulness [+W]
  • Prior knowledge favored the lecture condition at pretest and conditions were equivalent at posttest 2 [~W]
  • DAA students showed superior transfer to a variant design task even after controlling for relevant prior knowledge of the spring mechanism [+W]
  • DAA First students scored significantly higher than Lecture First students on the viewfinder variant design question [+W]
