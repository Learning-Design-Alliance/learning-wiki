# Attend to emotion transmission among students and the role of contagion in education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Educators and researchers should attend to emotion transmission among their students and the role of contagion in education", because the experiment showed peers' emotions differentially affect students' achievement emotions, mastery-approach goals, mental effort, and game performance. In practice this means recognizing that displayed enjoyment versus frustration during observational learning shapes what students feel, strive for, and achieve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning settings where students observe or interact with peers (or peer models) displaying emotions
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a virtual, unknown peer model in online observational learning of a science game; classroom transmission was not directly manipulated

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students
- Secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Regulating achievement emotions in learning settings
- Supporting mastery-approach goals and performance
