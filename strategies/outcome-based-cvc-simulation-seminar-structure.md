# Outcome-based simulation seminar combining a 20-minute anatomy lecture, expert demonstration, and 60 minutes of hands-on practice in equipped booths

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A competency-based training course introduced in 2017, required for newly hired residents within six months of employment and recommended for seniors with multiple punctures or incidents. It included "a 20-min lecture on the internal jugular vein's anatomy" and pitfalls of the short-axis out-of-plane technique, a demonstration by an expert, then "60 min of hands-on practice", with one to four trainees per seminar in booths equipped with a CVC simulator, ultrasound machine, and catheterization kit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Equipped booths with a central venous catheter simulator, an ultrasound machine with a linear array probe, and a central venous catheterization kit, plus an ultrasound-guided vascular access expert as demonstrator.
#### Constraints
- Seminar dates had to be coordinated with the availability of participants and instructors; the article notes task-based technical training alone cannot resolve all CVC placement issues.

### Target Learners
- newly hired junior and senior residents
- senior residents with multiple punctures or incidents

### Target Learning Goals
- needle and probe manipulation
- proper probe adjustment as the needle advances
