# Small Group Instruction

## Description
Small group instruction places a teacher or facilitator with a small number of learners (typically 3–8) who share a similar instructional need, for focused work on a specific skill or concept. The reduced group size increases per-learner interaction time, allows scaffolds to be calibrated to observed performance, and creates space for immediate, individualized feedback that whole-class formats cannot sustain.

## Design Implications

Small groups multiply opportunities for learners to respond, be observed, and receive corrective feedback — conditions strongly associated with achievement gains in explicit and responsive teaching models [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The mechanism is partly attentional and partly motivational: the teacher can monitor each learner's work closely, while learners cannot easily remain passive. Effectiveness depends on what happens *inside* the group — a small group delivering undifferentiated lecture gains little; the format amplifies good instruction rather than substituting for it.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A valid basis for grouping: current assessment data on the targeted skill, not static labels ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- Focused instructional materials matched precisely to the group's need
- A productive independent activity for the rest of the class, so the teacher can attend fully to the group
- A teacher fluent enough in the target skill to diagnose errors on the fly and adjust scaffolds ([Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M])

#### Constraints
- Grouping by fixed ability can depress outcomes and expectations for lower groups; groups must be flexible and frequently re-formed [~M]
- Off-task time rises sharply when the rest of the class lacks engaging independent work, eroding the benefit for the group itself [-M]
- Requires significant teacher planning and management skill; poorly run rotations consume time without instructional payoff [-W]
- Groups formed across very large skill ranges defeat the purpose — the teacher cannot calibrate to divergent needs simultaneously [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Teacher-led groups** (guided reading, reteach groups, intervention blocks) — teacher delivers targeted instruction
- **Rotational/station models** (e.g., station rotation within [Blended Learning](../patterns/blended-learning.md)) — small groups cycle through teacher-led, collaborative, and independent stations
- **Peer-led groups** — [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) structures shift the facilitation role to students with defined roles and individual accountability [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- **Conferring** — very small (1–3 student) groups for brief, focused coaching sessions

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners who need intensive, targeted intervention; small-group tutoring formats show consistent positive effects over one-to-many formats [+M]
- Learners with diverse needs requiring adjusted scaffolds and pacing
- Advanced learners ready for extension beyond whole-class pacing
- Less effective for learners who already master the content and would benefit more from independent or peer-extended work [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill mastery and remediation: closing specific gaps identified through [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)
- Fluency building through high-response-rate [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with immediate feedback
- Strategic reasoning: making thinking visible in a low-stakes setting where learners will risk partial ideas

### Instructions
1. **Diagnose** — use recent work or a quick [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) to identify a shared, specific need; form a flexible group around that need.
2. **Set a focused objective** — one skill or concept per session, stated to learners in accessible terms.
3. **Model or review** — briefly demonstrate or reactivate the target skill ([Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md)).
4. **Guided practice** — learners work while the teacher observes closely, prompting and scaffolding contingently rather than telling [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
5. **Feedback and release** — give immediate, task-specific feedback, then release learners to independent [Practice](../elements/practice.md) while noting who needs re-grouping.
6. **Regroup** — use the session's evidence to re-form groups; avoid letting groups become permanent tracks.

## Related Strategies
- [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) — peer-facilitated variant where structure and accountability replace teacher facilitation
- [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) — small groups are the natural scale for genuine discussion
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — coaching phase works best at small-group scale where the expert can observe and correct

## Examples
- **Guided reading (Fountas & Pinnell)** — the teacher works with 4–6 readers at a similar text level, prompting strategic reading while others rotate through independent literacy stations.
- **Do the Math Now! (SCHOLASTIC)** — a small-group intervention program for arithmetic fluency built around 15-minute teacher-led small-group modules.
- **Station-rotation blended classrooms** — e.g., implementations of the Khan Academy platform (https://www.khanacademy.org) as the independent station while the teacher pulls targeted small groups based on dashboard data.
