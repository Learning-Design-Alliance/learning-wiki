# Offer extracurricular vaccination seminars in the second half of the medical curriculum to build knowledge and positive vaccination attitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extracurricular seminars on vaccines for medical students, timed in the second half of the course of study, as a formative intervention to improve knowledge and attitudes toward vaccination. They argue such training equips future doctors to deal with vaccine hesitancy and to counsel patients, and that universities should build a structured platform for vaccinology education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery during the clinical phase of the university career, where intention to get vaccinated is greater
- Content covering both schedule knowledge and communication skills
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in an optional, self-selected sample at one Italian university

### Target Learners
- Medical students, especially those in the second half of their course of study

### Target Learning Goals
- Vaccination knowledge and recommendation behaviour
- Skills to counsel patients and respond to anti-vaccine arguments

## Claims

- [Before the seminar, medical students showed large gaps in knowledge of newly recommended vaccines (2% aware of rotavirus, 10% pneumococcal, 28% of Hib in the hexavalent vaccine for infants)](../claims/baseline-gaps-infant-vaccination-recommendations.md) [+W]
- [Students' self-assessed vaccination knowledge shifted significantly after the seminar, from 46% to 5% rating it poor/insufficient and 54% to 95% rating it sufficient or higher](../claims/self-assessed-vaccination-knowledge-rose-after-seminar.md) [+W]
- [Among future doctors, recommendations for MMR under one year of age decreased by 67% from before to after the seminar in a single-group pre-post study](../claims/seminar-corrected-mmr-timing-misconception.md) [+W]
- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, the proportion of students who would recommend pertussis and influenza vaccination during pregnancy rose from before to after the seminar (up 37% and 19%, respectively), as did awareness of Herpes Zoster vaccination over age 65 (up 22%)](../claims/seminar-increased-pregnancy-and-elderly-vaccination-recommendations.md) [+W]
- [Medical students' immunization-schedule knowledge scores rose from before to after an extracurricular vaccination seminar for all age categories in one uncontrolled pre-post study (p < 0.001)](../claims/vaccination-seminar-improved-schedule-knowledge-medical-students.md) [+W]
