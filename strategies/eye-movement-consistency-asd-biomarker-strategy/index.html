Use eye movement consistency during face processing as a cost-effective early biomarker for ASD and support visual-routine training for children with face-processing difficulties¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that low eye movement consistency in processing faces "may provide a new early biomarker of ASD that can be obtained through a cost-effective and community-viable eye tracking method". It further suggests that children with face-processing difficulties, such as those with ASD, may benefit from training that aims to facilitate the development of visual routine for faces. This rests on the finding that children with ASD showed higher eye movement entropy than matched TD children, particularly tied to autistic traits in social skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Eye tracking during a face recognition task, with entropy of the HMM summarising each participant's fixations as the consistency measure

Constraints¶

  • The biomarker and training suggestions are forward-looking; the article states future work will examine these possibilities

Target Learners¶

  • Children with autism spectrum disorders
  • Children with face-processing difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Development of consistent visual routines for face recognition through social exposure

Affordances¶

  • Dnn Hmm Visual Routine Model

Claims¶

  • Children with ASD show lower eye movement consistency than age- and IQ-matched typically developing children, with no difference in eye movement pattern [+W]
  • In typically developing children aged 6–11, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement consistency but not by eye movement pattern [+W]
  • In children, lower eye movement consistency correlates with autistic traits specifically in social skills [+W]
  • Lower eye movement consistency (higher C3 entropy) is associated with poorer selective attention and inhibitory control [+W]
  • A more eyes-focused scanning pattern and higher eye movement consistency are each correlated with better face recognition performance [+W]
  • In partially trained DNN+HMM models simulating children, eye movement consistency (lower entropy) predicts recognition accuracy but eye movement pattern does not [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use eye tracking as a cost-effective method for early screening or personalized diagnosis of face processing or social cognition impairments
