# Design pedagogical agents with facial expressions and consider learner-agent similarity to target motivation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The meta-analysis's moderator results support concrete PA design choices: agents that used facial expressions significantly improved broad motivation (g = 0.33) and self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.46), whereas agents without facial expressions had no significant effect on either outcome. For value beliefs, the authors recommend that "if a PA researcher is interested in impacting learners' value beliefs, careful consideration must be made regarding the agent's role and whether the learner will perceive some degree of similarity (e.g., demographic and/or psychological) between themself and the PA." They speculate such designs may significantly impact value beliefs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The PA must be a visible on-screen virtual character compared against a non-PA condition for effects to be assessed
#### Constraints
- The facial-expression moderator results come from observational moderator analyses, not randomized manipulation of facial expressions; the value-belief similarity recommendation is speculative and untested in this synthesis

### Target Learners
- learners in digital learning environments across grade levels, including adult learners outside postsecondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- self-efficacy expectations, broad motivation, value beliefs
