# Adapt journal club activities to student academic level and provide structured support for junior students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tailoring journal club design to students' academic level, prior experience, and learning style. It states that "Students in their third year or with limited experience may benefit from structured support, clear guidelines, and organized opportunities for participation," while "senior students may benefit more from independence, peer-led discussions, and the ability to critically engage with literature that is directly relevant to their preferred field." Instructors are also advised to ensure equal participation across genders in an encouraging environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor awareness of student academic level, prior experience, and gender learning patterns
#### Constraints
- Based on self-reported perceptions from a majority-male sample at a single institution; the authors call for further study with balanced gender and course representation

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students across academic years

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking and presentation skill development matched to student level
