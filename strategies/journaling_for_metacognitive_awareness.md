# Journaling for Metacognitive Awareness

## Description
Journaling involves writing or recording thoughts and feelings to develop metacognitive awareness and build self-regulation skills. Learners can use journals to reflect on their reactions to new or challenging situations, identify patterns in their behavior, and develop strategies for managing their emotions. Journaling can take various forms, including written entries, audio recordings, or video logs. Prompts can be used to guide reflection, such as asking learners to describe what they did in class, why, and how it helped them process emotions. The goal is to help learners become more aware of their internal processes and develop the ability to self-regulate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Journals (physical or digital), prompts to guide reflection, and a safe and supportive environment for sharing thoughts and feelings.
#### Constraints
- Learners may be hesitant to share their thoughts and feelings if they do not feel safe or supported. It requires time and effort to engage in meaningful reflection.

- Learners who struggle with self-regulation, particularly those who need to develop greater awareness of their internal processes, including students in K-12 and adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop metacognitive awareness, improve self-regulation skills, enhance emotional intelligence, foster self-reflection

### Affordances
- Promotes self-reflection, helps learners identify patterns in their behavior, builds metacognitive awareness, and provides a safe space for exploring emotions. It also allows educators to gain insight into the learner’s thought processes and provide targeted support.

### Personalization
- Journaling can be adapted to individual needs by using different formats (written, audio, video) and providing prompts that are tailored to the learner’s specific challenges and strengths. For learners who struggle with writing, audio or video journals may be more effective.
