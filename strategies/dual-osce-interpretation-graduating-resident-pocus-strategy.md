# Use a dual OSCE-plus-written-interpretation assessment, restricted to core skills, for evaluating graduating residents' POC ultrasound competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends assessing graduating residents with previously validated methods: an OSCE with direct observation of image acquisition on a standardized patient, plus a written multiple-choice image-interpretation exam, mirroring the 2008 CORD consensus recommendations. Because advanced-skill items depressed scores, the authors suggest "an edited version of the CORD OSCE that includes only core skills" for future studies, and propose the tool "may be developed further for other EM programs to use for graduating resident evaluation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- US-trained physician examiners with published checklists scored as one point per item
- A question bank of normal and pathological images for the interpretation component
- Definition of a competency threshold, which the article states does not yet exist
#### Constraints
- Reviewer bias is possible since OSCE reviewers were not blinded; the authors suggest independent US-trained reviewers in future studies
- Single-institution, small-cohort results; how the assessment works elsewhere remains to be seen
- How performance on these measures correlates with clinical performance is not yet established

### Target Learners
- senior emergency medicine residents nearing graduation

### Target Learning Goals
- core point-of-care ultrasound image acquisition
- core point-of-care ultrasound image interpretation
