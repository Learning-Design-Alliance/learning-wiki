# Embed graph problems in authentic life-science and engineering contexts to support perceived relevance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using graphs within problems that embed toy models in more realistic treatments so engineering students appreciate models, and including problems that pre-health-care and life-science students perceive as providing insights into rules they are given to memorize in other science classes, helping them feel physics has authentic value. The ion-channel Boltzmann-distribution problem is offered as a connection between graphs and equations that is authentic for life-science students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must connect to realistic situations or disciplinary rules students encounter elsewhere
#### Constraints
- Physics majors tend to accept relevance, so this strategy targets other populations such as engineers and pre-health-care students

### Target Learners
- life-science and pre-health-care students
- engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- seeing authentic value in physics skills
- connecting graphs, equations, and physical situations

### Affordances
- Physics Math Blend Graphs
