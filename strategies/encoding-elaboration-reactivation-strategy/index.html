Strategies

Use elaboration, reactivation of prior knowledge, and desirable difficulty during encoding to build connected, detailed memories¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The review recommends encoding techniques grounded in neural accounts of integration. "elaboration helps to relate a new memory to as many existing traces as possible, thereby presumably facilitating integration with prior knowledge"; reactivation of previous memories while learning new information is suggested to help integrate them with an existing schema; and learning should happen "at a desirable difficulty79, at an optimal 'distance' from the schema." The method of loci links new memories to spatial locations and can be easily trained.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • New information must be neither too alike existing schemas nor too distinct, since very distinct information cannot be linked well

Constraints¶

  • Presumed mechanisms are framed as suggestions; the review offers neural rationale rather than effect sizes

Target Learners¶

  • adult learners
  • students in educational settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • integration of new information with prior knowledge
  • long-term retention of detailed information

Affordances¶

  • Slimm Framework Mpfc Integration

Claims¶

  • Elaborative Interrogation Improves Learning [+M]
  • Distinctiveness aids retrieval without strengthening encoding, while novelty exposure enhances encoding of subsequently learned material [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Elaborative Interrogation
  • Sequence simpler desirable difficulties (retrieval, spacing, interleaving) during initial L2 vocabulary encoding, reserving generation for post-encoding consolidation
  • Optimize consolidation through sufficient sleep, spaced and interleaved study, and rest periods between learning
  • Apply retrieval practice (quizzes, peer teaching, flipped classroom) and monitor detail loss to balance gist and episodic memory
