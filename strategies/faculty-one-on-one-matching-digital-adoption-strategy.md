# Institutional expert support and one-on-one matching to help faculty adopt digital education tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that the biggest challenge to spreading educational innovation is the burden or fear of educators themselves, since medical school faculty are never professionally trained in pedagogy and digital tools add technical and psychological burden. It reports that "some colleges have secured the assistance of experts to help and support professors’ difficulties when embarking upon these innovations and changes," facilitated through a one-on-one matching service depending on the subject taught and the professor's digital skill level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions must absorb large labor costs to provide expert assistance during the transition
#### Constraints
- The article notes the more features a tool has, the harder it is to learn and the greater the burden on the instructor; it favors easy, free tools

### Target Learners
- medical and dental school faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- adoption of digital teaching and assessment tools by educators
