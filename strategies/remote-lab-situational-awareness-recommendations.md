# Design remote and virtual experiments for situational awareness and facilitated in-person social interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the evaluation, the authors recommend improving student engagement by devising greater situational awareness for the remote setting rather than relying on video conferencing to mimic co-located work: "It is preferable to devise greater situational awareness rather than rely on video conferencing to mimic co-located practical work." They further recommend enabling in-person social interactions that form before the activity, and including social interaction in virtual experiments normally treated as asynchronous tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments should include group work and facilitate social modes of learning
- Team formation should occur before the actual remote experiment activity
#### Constraints
- Adding talking heads to online meetings was rejected by students, who felt there were already enough visual clues during an observing session

### Target Learners
- Distance-learning undergraduate students in practical science

### Target Learning Goals
- Student engagement and learning outcomes in remote and virtual practical work
