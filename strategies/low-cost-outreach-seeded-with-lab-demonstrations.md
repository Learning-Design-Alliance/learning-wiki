# Departments can start informal outreach programs without significant budget or curriculum changes by seeding them with existing laboratory demonstrations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that the design principles on which these programs rest can become part of any physics outreach program at any institution. As they conclude, "Everyone can start an outreach program, and it doesn't even need a signiﬁcant budget or a change to current curriculum. Many demonstrations are part of common physics laboratories." These demonstrations can be used to seed experiential learning environments that promote student growth as physicists and community members.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to existing demonstration experiments from common physics laboratories and ongoing departmental support
#### Constraints
- The benefits reported are based on self-reported data from a single large land-grant institution

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate physics students at any institution

### Target Learning Goals
- physics identity development
- career readiness skills
- sense of belonging to the STEM community
