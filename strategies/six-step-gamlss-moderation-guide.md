# Six-step practical guide for applying GAMLSS to evaluate distributional moderation effects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article condenses the application of GAMLSS to distributional moderation into an implementable workflow: "TheapplicationofGAMLSStoevaluatedistributionalmoderationeffectscanbesummarizedinsixdistinctsteps" (Table 1). Step 1 evaluates the raw outcome distribution's measurement scale and distributional characteristics; step 2 selects candidate GAMLSS distributions, explanatory variables, and additive terms based on hypotheses and a priori theory; step 3 estimates unconditional effects for candidate models and compares fit with information criteria; step 4 assesses additive effects and local fit via wormplots and quantile residuals; step 5 adds treatment-by-moderator product terms and evaluates significance with bootstrap confidence intervals or likelihood ratio tests; step 6 interprets coefficients, computes effect sizes (Cohen's d, odds ratios, or risk ratios depending on link function), and performs post-hoc probing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Candidate distributions selected by measurement level (discrete, continuous, or mixed) and distributional features such as non-normality and ceiling/floor effects
- A priori moderation hypotheses and theory-based selection of covariates and additive terms before estimating conditional models
#### Constraints
- The guide is presented for school-based intervention research settings with nested classroom data structures

### Target Learners
- Students in preschool and early elementary classroom management intervention studies

### Target Learning Goals
- Fine-grained characterization of treatment effect modifiers in school-based intervention research
