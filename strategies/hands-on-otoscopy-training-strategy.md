# Incorporate hands-on active learning techniques (task trainer or peer-to-peer practice) into otoscopy skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating active learning techniques into otoscopy training: "These findings suggest that incorporation of one of these active learning techniques is important and can help guide development of optimal training in otoscopy skills at multiple levels." Hands-on interventions (task trainer or peer-to-peer practice) left students feeling more prepared than video instruction, though neither was superior to the other in comfort or subsequent exam counts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 90-minute workshop with standardized didactic presentation followed by a hands-on intervention, with otoscopes, insufflator bulbs, and optionally a task trainer available.
#### Constraints
- Training increased comfort but did not increase subsequent use of basic or pneumatic otoscopy in the clinical setting; barriers such as supervising-physician practice patterns persist.

### Target Learners
- medical students
- learners at multiple levels of otoscopy training

### Target Learning Goals
- performing basic and pneumatic otoscopy
- improved comfort with the ear exam
