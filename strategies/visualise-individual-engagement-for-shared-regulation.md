# Visualise individual engagement to support socially shared regulation and timely teacher support in collaborative learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends informing students about individual engagement during collaborative learning, drawing on social translucence theory: if a student cannot see others' engagement, they can hardly regulate their behaviour. Visualising individual engagement “might be a potential support for students to socially share regulated learning”, and for teachers it can act like “eyes on the back” to extend educators' monitoring capabilities so feedback can be timely, individual, and specific.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement must first be automatically detected and made visible to students and teachers during or after collaboration
#### Constraints
- The article states it did not explore how the approach generates visualization or feedback; co-design sessions are planned for future work
- The goal is increasing visibility of engagement, not correcting students' opinions about each other's engagement

### Target Learners
- postgraduate students working in collaborative groups
- teachers of large collaborative classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- socially shared regulation of learning
- awareness of peers' engagement
- timely individualized teacher support
