# Double-Entry Journals

## Description
This strategy encourages learners to record their responses to a text as they read. Learners write down phrases, sentences, or vocabulary from their assigned reading and their reaction to it. The purpose of this strategy is to give learners the opportunity to express their thoughts and become actively involved with what they are reading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Passage of text, journal or paper folded in half, time for individual writing and group sharing.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for learners who struggle with writing or expressing their thoughts. Requires careful modeling and scaffolding to ensure learners understand the process.

### Target Learners

- Middle school and high school learners; content area, ELA, and ELL learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve comprehension, vocabulary, and retention of content; foster a connection between reading and writing; encourage active involvement with text; promote collaborative learning.

### Affordances
- Improves comprehension by encouraging active engagement with text. Builds vocabulary through contextual analysis and personal responses. Enhances retention by linking text with personal thoughts and reactions. Fosters a connection between reading and writing.

### Personalization
- Scaffolded with pre-filled entries for struggling learners; gradually released responsibility as learners improve. Can be adapted for various types of written texts, read-alouds, or listenings.
