# Network-aware PBL facilitation: use SNA indicators to diagnose isolation and distribute participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends embedding SNA-informed feedback into PBL facilitation. Because SNA can differentiate forms of social prominence, educators can "identify students who are highly engaged, those who dominate discussions, or those who are socially peripheral and potentially at risk of under-participation", then guide targeted developmental interventions. The article summarises this as diagnosing isolation, distributing roles, and engineering equitable participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Complete peer nomination and collaboration-rubric data from whole PBL groups, and facilitators able to interpret centrality indicators.
#### Constraints
- The article notes adoption barriers including analytic complexity, software barriers, and ethical concerns, and states faculty development, accessible tools, and strong data governance can help address these challenges.

### Target Learners
- Medical students in face-to-face PBL groups, especially socially peripheral or underperforming students

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable participation, collaborative engagement, and early identification of learners at risk of marginalization
