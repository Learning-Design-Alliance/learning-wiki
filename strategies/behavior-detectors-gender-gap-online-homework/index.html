Build behavior detectors in online learning systems that log gender-relevant homework behaviors to target interventions that close gender gaps¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose follow-up studies of student behavior at action level — examining sequential patterns of help-seeking and problem-attempting, and building a dataset of login frequency and session duration by gender to predict learning — so that findings "can be informative for the development of behavior detectors in online learning systems like ASSISTments". Such detectors would let systems "provide interventions to improve learning outcomes and close gender gaps". This extends the current paper's finding that girls and boys differed in help-seeking and problem-attempting behaviors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • System log data capturing action-level sequences, login frequency, and working-session duration

Constraints¶

  • The current study lacked access to parent involvement, extracurricular activities, and student attitudes, limiting explanation of the observed differences

Target Learners¶

  • Middle school mathematics students
  • 7th-grade students in one-to-one computing environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mathematics achievement
  • Reducing gender gaps in STEM learning in out-of-classroom settings

Claims¶

  • The ASSISTments homework intervention effect on TerraNova math scores varied by gender: boys benefited more, narrowing the gender gap [+W]
  • The ASSISTments intervention raised TerraNova scores by 10.26 points and girls outscored boys by 5.21 points overall, both statistically significant [+W]
  • Girls made significantly fewer hint requests, fewer attempts per problem, and fewer bottom-out hint requests than boys while doing online homework [+W]
  • Among students performing proficiently or above, high-performing girls spent marginally significantly more time on homework than high-performing boys [+W]
  • Assignment-completion timing showed mixed gender results across performance groups, with girls overall completing more assignments on time at only marginal significance [+W]
  • Among 7th-grade ASSISTments users, there is no significant overall gender difference in homework time or number of problems completed [+W]
