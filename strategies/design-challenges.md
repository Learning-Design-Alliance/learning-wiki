# Design Challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A design challenge asks learners to produce a working artifact — a device, system, model, or solution — that must satisfy explicit criteria and constraints. Learning is driven by the iterative cycle of building, testing against requirements, and revising, with instruction positioned as a resource learners draw on when their designs fail.

## Design Implications

Design challenges sit at the productive end of inquiry-based learning: they work when the challenge is well-scaffolded and the target concepts are surfaced through structured reflection on design decisions [~M]. Unguided versions often fail because learners optimize for "it works" rather than understanding *why* it works, so the challenge must be paired with [Practice](../elements/practice.md)-like application moments and explicit concept extraction. Failure of an early prototype is pedagogically valuable only when learners are prompted to diagnose it against principles rather than trial-and-error around it.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A challenge with authentic constraints and clear success criteria, so design decisions map onto target concepts
- Iteration time: at least one build–test–revise cycle, with testing that produces informative feedback
- Structured reflection (design journals, decision rationales) that converts design activity into articulable knowledge
- Access to needed materials, tools, and just-in-time instruction

#### Constraints
- Cognitive overload is common: open-ended challenges force learners to manage goals, constraints, and domain content simultaneously [Cognitive overload degrades learning when working memory demands exceed capacity.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S] — mitigate with [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md), staged constraints, and worked starter examples
- Learners can succeed at the artifact while missing the underlying science; without explicit concept extraction, transfer is poor [~M]
- Weak or delayed feedback during testing lets misconceptions persist across iterations
- Novices given fully open design tasks often flounder; effectiveness increases with more guidance for less knowledgeable learners [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Engineering design cycles** (e.g., build-a-vehicle challenges) emphasizing test data and revision
- **Software/game design challenges** with runnable artifacts and rapid iteration
- **Design-for-others challenges** (design a playground, an app for a client) adding user-requirements reasoning
- **Paper/low-fidelity design** when materials or time are limited — the reasoning cycle matters more than the artifact

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners with enough domain knowledge to generate and evaluate design options; pure novices need substantial scaffolding first [~M]
- Learners motivated by [Authentic Audiences and Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — real criteria and clients raise investment [Authentic audiences improve the quality of student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M]
- Teams with heterogeneous skills, where design roles distribute cognitive load

### Target Learning Goals
- Applied problem solving: connecting concepts to functional requirements
- Systems thinking: understanding how components interact under constraints
- Iterative reasoning: hypothesis generation, testing, and revision from evidence
- Collaboration and project management practices

### Instructions
1. **Frame the challenge** — present criteria, constraints, and the audience; activate relevant prior knowledge ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
2. **Analyze exemplar designs** — have learners compare strong and weak prior solutions to extract design principles [Comparing and contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+S]
3. **Plan and build** — teams propose, justify, and construct a first design; require a written rationale linking choices to concepts
4. **Test and diagnose** — run designs against criteria; require diagnosis of failures in terms of underlying principles, not surface fixes
5. **Revise and re-test** — iterate at least once, documenting what changed and why
6. **Extract and share principles** — teams present designs and articulate generalizable lessons; instructor consolidates target concepts

## Examples
- **[Learning by Design](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1023905509753)** (Kolodner's LBD curriculum) — middle-school science units where students design vehicles and containers, then run "design rules" discussions to extract science principles.
- **[Engineering is Elementary](https://eie.org)** (Museum of Science, Boston) — elementary engineering design challenges integrated with science content.
- **[MIT D-Lab](https://d-lab.mit.edu)** — university courses where students design technologies with community partners under real constraints.
