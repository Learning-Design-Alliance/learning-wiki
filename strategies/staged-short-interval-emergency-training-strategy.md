# Use staged, short-interval, high-frequency emergency training with immediate practice while knowledge is fresh to consolidate first aid knowledge and skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends structuring emergency training as consecutive staged phases with short intervals between them, so that case simulation occurs while knowledge is still fresh and repeated practice consolidates memory. It reports that "One week of situational simulation training and two weeks of practice training has a positive impact on the consolidation and maintenance of knowledge in BLS, AED, and ALS", and that frequent refreshers at short intervals improve skills and confidence. The authors suggest hospitals conduct such training every year to maintain first aid knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training of knowledge and skills as indispensable components of emergency knowledge training programs
- Frequent intensive training, since emergency knowledge and skills not frequently used or practiced are likely to be gradually forgotten over time
#### Constraints
- The article notes training time and duration are critical points for long-term retention of improved skills, and that long-term retention and clinical response outcomes still need to be tracked over time

### Target Learners
- Hospital medical workers, including experienced staff, in emergency and pediatric care settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance and consolidation of BLS, AED, and ALS knowledge and skills
- Reduction of operational mistakes through shorter intervals between exercises
