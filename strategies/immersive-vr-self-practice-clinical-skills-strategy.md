# Use immersive and interactive VR self-practice as a practical and engaging way for novices to learn the fundamentals of the ABCDE approach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Replace or supplement equipment-based individual simulation practice with individual self-practice in an immersive, interactive VR application: students receive a brief introduction, then practice the full observation sequence on a virtual patient with automatic feedback, requiring minimal instructor support. The authors conclude that "VR solutions that is immersive and interactive can be used as a practical and engaging way to learn the fundamentals of the ABCDE approach."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An immersive and interactive VR application targeted at the learning objective, with automatic feedback; VR headsets and hand controllers; a brief introductory lecture and demonstration video before practice
#### Constraints
- Tested only for novices and one application; likely not relevant for experienced personnel or advanced observations; short follow-up; no systematic measure of environmental impact

### Target Learners
- first-year medical and nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- systematic clinical observation and the ABCDE approach
