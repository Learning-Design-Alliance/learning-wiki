# Make DTAR abstracts explicit and accessible: natural frequencies, defined test roles, careful conclusions, and stated limitations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends specific abstract-level features because many clinicians will read only the abstract. It advises "Presenting the results in more accessible formats, such as frequencies, rather than percentages", explicitly defining the roles of tests such as index test and reference standard, careful wording of conclusions, and emphasis on limitations of validity, reliability and applicability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Abstracts must anticipate the specific misinterpretations documented in the study, such as reference standard misread as comparator.
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as suggestions for readers with limited research experience; it also raises the question whether different decision-maker groups require different presentation formats.

### Target Learners
- healthcare decision makers: clinicians, policy makers, researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting diagnostic test accuracy evidence for healthcare decisions
