# Multi-length time-of-flight procedure: measure delay versus distance and fit a straight line

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete student procedure: students measure the difference in time of flight at several distances from a reference point (L = 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1 m), plot the data points, and fit a straight line to obtain the speed of light. The article explains that "Taking multiple measurements not only increases accuracy but decreases the probability of simple errors," and that measuring the transmission delay at various L makes it difficult to miss the finiteness of light's speed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reference distance point and a setup allowing the detector–mirror distance to be changed
- Students plot the data and fit a straight line
