# Pair perceptual scaffolding (checklists) with clinical-integration tasks to close the perception-to-diagnosis gap

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because interpretation and diagnosis scores were uncorrelated, the authors recommend combining perceptual scaffolding with integration tasks. They write: "Our data therefore reinforce recommendations to combine perceptual scaffolding (checklists, reading sequences) with integration tasks (clinical vignettes, decision algorithms, immediate feedback)." They also suggest flipped/asynchronous resources and a second layer of brief cases anchored to each image with spaced practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must already receive structured perceptual search instruction; the integration layer is added on top of it
#### Constraints
- The article states the assessment was short-term and that retention and transfer to real clinical performance remain to be established

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students
- undergraduate medical trainees learning image interpretation

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic reasoning
- transfer from perceptual findings to clinical decision-making
