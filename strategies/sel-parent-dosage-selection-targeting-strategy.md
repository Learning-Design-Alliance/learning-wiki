# Consider selection into parent program participation when interpreting dosage effects and targeting recruitment in SEL interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because parent dosage is not randomly assigned, this strategy uses inverse probability of treatment weighting to estimate dosage effects while addressing selection bias, and uses the resulting selection findings to inform "program recruitment, targeting, and intervention scaling and replicability." The article found that parents whose children were already advantaged were most likely to participate, so programs should anticipate that non-participating families' children may have the greatest need for school-based services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use of confounding covariates (child demographics, parent characteristics, teacher and parent reports) to model participation propensity
- Balance assessment between weighted groups
#### Constraints
- Causal inference is correct only if the appropriate assumptions for IPTW are met

### Target Learners
- low-income urban elementary school students and their parents

### Target Learning Goals
- improved academic achievement, sustained attention, and behavior through SEL programming
