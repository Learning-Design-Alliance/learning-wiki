# Validate simulated learner behavior against authentic student responses through longitudinal school deployment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes addressing the unvalidated nature of LLM-based learner simulation through embedded, practitioner-driven validation during real classroom deployment. In partnerships with 10 German schools, five teachers per school will integrate FACET over one semester, and "teachers will systematically compare simulated learner behavior against actual student responses, flagging implausible patterns and documenting divergences." This grounds simulation refinement in classroom reality while also evaluating workflow integration, teacher trust, and student feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School partnerships with teachers integrating the system into regular instruction over one semester; systematic comparison of simulated versus actual student responses.
#### Constraints
- The evaluation focused on grade-7 mathematics, demonstrating feasibility without establishing cross-class and cross-subject generalizability; agent simulation requires further validation with authentic student responses.

### Target Learners
- K–12 students across primary, secondary, and inclusive schools in diverse socioeconomic contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Refinement of learner simulation fidelity and evaluation of differentiated materials under authentic classroom conditions
