# Prioritise flexible, ergonomically adaptable haptic input devices and standardised, longer training protocols in future VR surgical training research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendations for VR surgical training research are twofold. First, on hardware: "Future work should prioritise the development of more flexible, ergonomically adaptable input devices that better replicate real-world surgical tools and allow for a wider range of natural hand positions and movements." Second, on protocol design: future research should implement "standardised training protocols of longer durations that provide consistent and extensive training experiences across all participants", including recorded videos or group teaching focused on haptic device usage, and should compare haptic versus non-haptic VR.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Addressing technical reliability issues such as glitches and unresponsiveness before integrating these systems into surgical education
#### Constraints
- The article notes fixed input devices do not accurately replicate the tools and dynamics of real-world surgery, and poorly calibrated haptic feedback may reinforce incorrect motor patterns

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical skill acquisition in complex open procedures
- competency-based progression in surgical education
