# KWL Charts

## Description
KWL (Know, Want to know, Learned) charts are advance organizers that help students activate prior knowledge, identify learning goals, and reflect on what they have learned. Before a lesson, students fill in what they think they know (K) and what they want to know (W). After the lesson, they write what they have learned (L). This process encourages students to connect new information to existing knowledge, increasing interest and focus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A chart divided into three columns (Know, Want to Know, Learned), reading materials, and class discussion.
#### Constraints
- May require teacher guidance to ensure students accurately reflect on their prior knowledge and learning.

### Affordances
- Increases engagement by connecting to prior knowledge, provides a clear structure for learning, and supports metacognition by prompting reflection.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to any subject. Students can work individually or in groups. The chart can be used for single texts or entire units.

- Completed KWL charts, student participation in discussions, and summaries of learning based on the chart.

## Impact
- Research supports that KWL charts make students think about what they already know, which makes them feel more comfortable with the new material they’re learning. As a result, their interest and focus may increase.
