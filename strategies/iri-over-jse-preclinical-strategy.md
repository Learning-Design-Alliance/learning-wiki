# Use the Interpersonal Reactivity Index rather than the Jefferson Scale for measuring empathy in pre-clinical students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
For students in the very early, pre-clinical phase of medical education, the article recommends the Interpersonal Reability Index approach: the IRI is targeted to general populations and captures both emotional and cognitive dimensions, whereas the Jefferson Scale "was developed to measure empathy in the context of patient care and therefore assumes familiarity with medical practice". The study used the Korean-validated K-IRI, whose four sub-scales showed Cronbach's alpha from .72 to .90.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated translation of the IRI for the student population
- Pre- and post-test administration at the first and final week of the course
#### Constraints
- The article notes that because empathy instruments differ in constructs, "assessing empathy using one measurement tool is not valid", so qualitative data were also collected

### Target Learners
- pre-clinical medical students without clinical exposure

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring development of cognitive and emotional empathy
