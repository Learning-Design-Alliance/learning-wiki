Identify specific comprehension deficits early and target intervention at comprehension rather than decoding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends early identification of children with specific comprehension deficits so that intervention can be targeted to comprehension rather than decoding. It notes that many current evidence-based reading interventions devote significant time to remediating poor decoding skills, which does not match the profile of children with adequate decoding but poor comprehension. The multi-disciplinary gene-brain-behavior approach is presented as moving towards "early identi ﬁcation of poor comprehension" before significant delays in later grades.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assessment that distinguishes comprehension from word reading/decoding, e.g., regression-based identification of poor comprehenders

Constraints¶

  • READ1 alleles in isolation will not identify children who will develop poor comprehension or benefit from reading interventions, per the authors

Target Learners¶

  • School-age children (8-15 years) with adequate decoding but poor reading comprehension

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • READ1 RU2Short presence significantly predicts poor comprehension despite adequate word reading after covariate control [+W]
  • Children with unexpected poor comprehension show a profound vocabulary deficit despite average-to-above-average word reading [+W]
  • The vocabulary weakness of children with SCD is best characterized as a developmental delay rather than a developmental deficit [~W]
  • The pattern of SCD weaknesses is consistent across identification criteria, but criteria identify widely differing proportions of children [~W]
  • Most children with SCD have vocabulary weaknesses, with intact vocabulary being rare [+W]
  • Children with specific reading comprehension difficulties show substantially poorer reading comprehension than vocabulary relative to typical readers [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Specifically assess mathematics in educational evaluations of children with reading difficulties, especially reading comprehension difficulties
  • Use a 30-minute neurophysiological assessment of consonants-in-noise coding for early identification of children at risk for language and literacy problems
