# Assigned Readings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Assigned readings are texts — textbook chapters, articles, cases, or primary sources — that learners are expected to study independently before instruction. The strategy shifts first exposure to content outside of contact time, freeing synchronous sessions for application, discussion, and feedback. It is the backbone of [Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md) designs and the standard preparation mechanism for [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md).

## Design Implications

Assigned readings only pay off when learners actually complete them and when class time exploits that preparation; unaccountable reading assignments are routinely skipped, and lecture that simply repeats the text adds no value [~M]. Completion and comprehension improve substantially when readings are paired with accountability structures such as pre-reading quizzes or guided questions [Daniel & Woody's work on pre-reading interventions](https://doi.org/10.1177/0098628313487467) [+S]. Because reading is a self-paced, high-load activity for novices, design choices that manage difficulty — chunked assignments, advance organizers, and annotation tasks — determine whether the reading builds usable knowledge or merely consumes effort [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Texts matched to learners' reading level and prior knowledge; dense primary sources need scaffolding
- An accountability or engagement mechanism — pre-class quizzes, reading responses, or in-class activities that visibly depend on the reading
- Class time that *uses* the reading (application, discussion, problem-solving) rather than restating it
- Guidance on what to attend to: reading questions, [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), or annotation prompts

#### Constraints
- Without accountability, completion rates drop sharply, especially for long or difficult texts [-M]
- Re-lecturing assigned content in class removes the incentive to read and wastes the preparation advantage [-M]
- Very difficult texts assigned without scaffolding produce shallow skimming or disengagement; the [expertise reversal effect](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) means supports that help novices (e.g., heavy guidance in the text) can slow more knowledgeable readers [~M]
- Long assignments undermine completion; spacing several shorter readings outperforms one massive one [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Pre-reading quizzes** — low-stakes checks before class; among the most reliably effective completion interventions [+S]
- **Guided reading questions or annotation tasks** — direct attention and prompt [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) during reading [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- **Just-in-Time Teaching** — learners answer warm-up questions on the reading; the instructor adapts the session to revealed misconceptions
- **Reading roles** — in discussion-based designs, each learner is assigned a perspective or section to report on

### Target Learners
- Learners with adequate decoding fluency and background knowledge to profit from independent text study; novices need more scaffolding than advanced readers [~M]
- Self-regulated learners benefit most; those with weak study skills need explicit reading guidance and accountability [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Less suitable as a sole strategy for learners with low literacy or heavy working-memory demands unless texts are chunked and supported [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Foundational knowledge acquisition: terminology, facts, and concepts that class time can then apply
- Preparation for discussion and case analysis
- Discipline-specific reading practices: interpreting primary sources, research articles, or technical documentation

### Instructions
1. Select or write readings at an appropriate difficulty level; chunk long texts into segments ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
2. Provide an advance organizer or reading questions that frame what learners should extract ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
3. Attach a low-stakes accountability task — a pre-class quiz or written response ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
4. Design the synchronous session to *apply* the reading: discussion, problems, or cases ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
5. Give feedback that references the reading so learners see the payoff of preparation ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)).

## Examples
- **Just-in-Time Teaching (Novak et al.)** — physics courses where students answer web-based warm-up questions on assigned readings before lecture; the instructor adjusts the session to common errors ([https://jittdl.physics.iupui.edu/jitt/](https://jittdl.physics.iupui.edu/jitt/))
- **Harvard Business School case method** — students receive assigned cases before class and are expected to be "cold-called" prepared; class time is entirely discussion ([https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/Pages/the-case-method.aspx](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/Pages/the-case-method.aspx))
- **Perusall** — a social annotation platform where assigned readings carry embedded questions and peer comments, with analytics on who read and where confusion clustered ([https://www.perusall.com](https://www.perusall.com))
