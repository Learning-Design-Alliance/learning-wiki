# Use single-photon interference videos alongside laser diffraction and soap-film experiments to interpret wave patterns statistically

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because single-photon detection is too expensive for classrooms, the approach shows videos of single-photon interference experiments in which images build one photon at a time, then interprets familiar laser activities in that light. The article states "we use videos of single photon interference experiments," asking students to note random photon arrivals and the final pattern. A human-hair laser diffraction activity lets students measure hair diameter from fringe spacing, and soap-film interference shows a permanent pattern despite at most one photon being in the film at any time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 1 mW green laser module or pointer, screen, human hair, soap bubble frame with copper wire ring, and access to single-photon interference videos
#### Constraints
- Care must be taken to avoid directly shining laser light at students' faces

### Target Learners
- school students in early years of science education

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting interference patterns as statistical arrival probabilities determined by the wavefunction rather than photons interfering with each other
