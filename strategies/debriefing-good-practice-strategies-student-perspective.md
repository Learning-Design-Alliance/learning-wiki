# Facilitators should ensure psychological safety, use open-ended questions, link debriefs to learning outcomes, limit error focus, and guide peer debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distils student-perspective findings into facilitator guidance for simulation-based learning debriefing: establish trust and psychological safety before debriefing, ask open-ended questions that encourage engagement and self-assessment, link the debrief to learning outcomes so it is meaningful, remain sensitive when addressing errors, and guide peer-led debriefing to ensure objectivity. As the authors state, "Facilitators should take meaningful steps to ensure that debriefing environments are considered safe places."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who establishes psychological safety and a common understanding of why debriefing takes place before the debrief begins
#### Constraints
- Peer-led debriefing can be unstructured and less objective than instructor-led debriefing, and its efficacy may depend on the learner's ability to reflect on their experiences

### Target Learners
- emergency medical care and healthcare students in simulation-based learning

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective learning from simulation experiences
- improved clinical performance through effective feedback and debriefing
