# Use short end-of-section exercises and computer plotting activities to build conceptual understanding of entangled wave functions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Each instructional section of the article ends with short exercises in which students plot, interpret in words, and calculate with entangled wave functions, including reproducing figures with a computer, writing Gaussian formulas for cat states, and predicting measurement outcomes. The article states these exercises help "students develop a conceptual understanding of entangle- ment." It also recommends teaching numerical calculations for their dual benefit of augmenting computational skills and building intuition for entangled two-particle systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to plotting tools; the article provides a Mathematica tutorial and simulation software in its electronic supplement
#### Constraints
- Students who have studied single-particle double-well and wave-packet scattering examples can make qualitative predictions without computational skills

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of entanglement, non-separability, and two-particle wave functions
