# Explicitly direct children to compare addends' magnitudes during arithmetic practice with linear materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that linear-spatial materials may have worked partly because they "supported the comparison of magnitudes," since the correlation between length and magnitude was more salient than that between surface area and magnitude in irregular materials. It recommends that teachers "explicitly direct children to compare addends" to strengthen the value of linear-spatial materials, potentially promoting understanding of relative magnitudes and of which sums are plausible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Linear-spatial materials in which length correlates visibly with the number of discrete units.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a possible additional mechanism and future direction, not as a manipulation tested in the study.

### Target Learners
- kindergartners
- early elementary school children

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of relative numerical magnitudes
- constraining arithmetic responses to plausible sums
