# Three-part course transformation combining enhanced preparation, in-class analysis, and project accountability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors restructured the junior-level Electronics course around three coordinated changes: enhanced student preparation for labs (prelabs requiring mathematical models and predictions, plus skill activities with screencasts), emphasis on collecting, analyzing, and reporting data during class time (enhanced notebooks, in-class comparison of measurement and prediction, graded participation), and increased accountability in student-designed projects (a three-stage proposal phase and weekly project updates). The transformation was "designed to ad- dress several goals including: (1) making the experience more authentic and aligned with current practices of ex- perimental physicists working with electronics."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough low-cost equipment for all students to complete the same lab activities each week; lab sections of no more than 20 students
#### Constraints
- The authors state formal error analysis, historical perspective on electronics, and solid-state physics details were explicitly not goals of the course

### Target Learners
- junior-level undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement in authentic scientific practices including modeling, design, and experimental skills

### Affordances
- Experimental Modeling Framework Zwickl
