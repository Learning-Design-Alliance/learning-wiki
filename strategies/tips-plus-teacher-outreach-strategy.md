# Pair weekly interactive homework with proactive teacher phone outreach to build parent–teacher communication

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's intervention gives all classes weekly TIPS interactive homework requiring parent–child collaboration, then has teachers of randomly selected classes proactively initiate phone or in-person conversations of at least five minutes about the assignments and the child's progress. The author reports the approach is low-cost: the teacher "averaged less than 30 minutes per student during the entire intervention". Phase 1 confirmed parents would welcome such contact, which was used to overcome teacher reluctance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments must not require reference materials or high subject-matter knowledge; parents need adequate completion time; teachers must be active rather than passive in initiating contact
#### Constraints
- Teachers could not reach about 10% of intervention parents; parents working very long hours were hardest to engage; conversation records were teacher self-reports

### Target Learners
- Parents of 8th grade students in low-resource, high immigrant and minority districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing parent–teacher bidirectional communication, homework submission, and homework grades
