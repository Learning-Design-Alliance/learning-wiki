Extend the task-based robotics methodology toward nonlinear individual educational paths¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

In the conclusion the authors name the perspective direction for further improving the proposed methodology. As printed, "The perspective direction of improving the proposed methodology is seen in the addition of its aspects related to the construction of a nonlinear individual educational path of studying robotics and engineering." This extends the course's stated valuable characteristics, which already include continuity between levels of learning and the possibility of getting knowledge at a time determined by the student, toward explicitly nonlinear individual trajectories. The article also lists uses of the results, including developing a methodical system for training robotics teachers and ensuring individualisation of the educational path through organised support of creative, cross-industrial, cognitive research activity.
