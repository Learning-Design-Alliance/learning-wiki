# Implement a four-week structured box-trainer curriculum covering camera navigation, hand-eye coordination, bimanual coordination, and intracorporeal suturing, with weekly timed assessment.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's curriculum consisted of a standardized 90-minute training session every Wednesday for four consecutive weeks on a standard laparoscopic box trainer (Ethicon), with a formal assessment on the Friday following each session. Performance was evaluated primarily by task completion time across four distinct laparoscopic skill modules, and "the scores from the four weekly assessments were aggregated to generate a composite performance score for each participant".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standard laparoscopic box trainer setup with monitor, camera unit, 0° and 30° laparoscopes, and standard instruments.
- Four distinct skill modules: camera navigation and visual orientation, hand-eye coordination, bimanual coordination, and suturing with intracorporeal knot tying.
- Weekly assessment protocol with task completion time as the primary outcome measure.
#### Constraints
- The four-week training period may not capture long-term skill retention.
- Task completion time captures only one dimension of surgical competence; more comprehensive tools such as OSATS, motion analysis, or force metrics would provide richer evaluation.

### Target Learners
- obstetrics and gynecology residents in standardized residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- basic laparoscopic skills: instrument navigation, precision cutting, pattern cutting, and intracorporeal suturing
