# Multiple Examples

## Description
When teaching a new technique, providing multiple examples helps students understand and apply the technique in their own writing. Definition alone is insufficient.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preparation of multiple examples of the technique. Time for analysis and practice in class. A supportive environment where students feel comfortable experimenting with new techniques.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming. Students may become overwhelmed by too many examples. Requires careful selection of examples that are relevant and accessible to students.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All grade levels, particularly beneficial for struggling writers and those new to a specific technique

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve understanding of writing techniques, enhance ability to apply techniques in writing, develop fluency in using new techniques, increase confidence in writing

### Affordances
- Provides a range of models for imitation. Supports understanding by illustrating the technique in different contexts. Increases confidence by showing that the technique can be applied in multiple ways.

### Personalization
- Examples can be tailored to student interests and writing goals. Students can be given opportunities to generate their own examples. Scaffolding can be provided for struggling learners.

- Research indicates that providing multiple examples improves understanding and application of new concepts and techniques.

## Examples
- If teaching figurative language, provide multiple examples of metaphors, similes, and personification. If teaching essay organization, provide examples of different ways to structure an introduction, body paragraph, or conclusion. If teaching real-world applications, provide examples of the skill used in varied situations and careers.
