# Use highly individualized instruction tailored to each specific student with ASD, aligned with their level of mathematical thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose that applying a "one-size fits all" approach to students with ASD may not serve them well, and instead teachers should utilize highly individualized instruction tailored to each specific student, perhaps aligned with the student's level of mathematical thinking. This recommendation arises from the wide variability in the three ASD participants' abilities, needs, and reactions to the intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must assess each student's individual abilities, strengths, and needs before selecting an intervention
#### Constraints
- Limited research exists on EBPs for students with ASD solving word problems, making it difficult to identify a single intervention that works for most students with ASD

### Target Learners
- students with autism spectrum disorders in elementary grades

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematical word problem solving
