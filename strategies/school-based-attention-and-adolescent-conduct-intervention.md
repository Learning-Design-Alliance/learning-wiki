# Sustained school-based support for children with attention problems, with conduct-problem intervention targeted particularly during adolescence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implication section recommends that, to promote academic achievement, schools provide sustained efforts to ameliorate attention problems throughout the academic trajectory, while conduct-problem interventions are positioned as most efficacious during adolescence, when the mediating pathway through conduct problems emerged. The authors ground this in the consistent mediating role of attention problems across both cohorts and developmental periods, and note that "school‐based interventions supporting children with attention problems ... are warranted for a lifelong impact on children's life opportunities".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

- Intervention content addressing attention problems specifically, independent of cognitive-ability support
#### Constraints
- The article notes the association between PGS for ADHD and ADHD symptomatology may be moderated by school-environment quality, where high-quality environments can decrease or render null the genetic association

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents in primary and secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- reducing the impact of attention and conduct problems on school outcomes
