# Book Clubs / Literature Circles

## Description
Book clubs, also known as literature circles, are a grouping system to organize active learning events based on student-selected areas of interest. Learners investigate content area topics by drawing on multiple texts, including websites, printed material, video, and music. They then share their learning using similar media, such as learning logs or blogs, discussing questions and observations about books they are reading. Group members share background knowledge and make inferences and predictions that strengthen verbal reasoning. Book clubs can be created within a classroom, grade level, or across different classes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of texts (books, magazines, websites, videos), computers with internet access, learning logs or blogs, and class time for discussions and activities.
#### Constraints
- May require careful structuring and organization to ensure effective group investigations. Students may need guidance in selecting appropriate materials and staying on track. Success depends on learners' ability to collaborate and engage in meaningful discussions.

### Affordances
- Provides opportunities for collaboration, discussion, and reflection. Students engage with multiple types of texts and genres to gather and synthesize information. Learners take ownership of their learning by selecting topics of interest and sharing their discoveries with peers.

### Personalization
- Learners choose topics of interest, types of texts, and methods of sharing learning. Flexible grouping allows for diverse perspectives. Can be adapted to fit various content areas and learning objectives.
