# Faculty consensus process for developing learning goals in large course transformation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Following the University of Colorado's Science Education Initiative approach, the designers interviewed faculty about what students should gain from the lab sequence — "in total, 15 interviews were conducted with faculty" — coded the interviews into themes, synthesized the goals under larger headings, and iterated them through informational meetings attended by roughly 10-12 faculty. The result was four broad goals (experimental process, data analysis, collaboration, communication) that also map onto the AAPT Lab Guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interested faculty willing to participate in discussions
- Curriculum and instruction experts who synthesize discussions into course structures
#### Constraints
- The article notes physics faculty are not often steeped in formal curriculum development, so synthesis is done by experts

### Target Learners
- Students in the largest-enrollment introductory physics lab course at an institution

### Target Learning Goals
- Aligning course design with departmental faculty values for laboratory learning
