# Broaden psychomotor ITS toward gross and open skills, complementary motor-ability tasks, and integrated training theories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the identified gaps, the review recommends exploring ITS for skill types currently neglected and for complementary physical activities. It states that "it might be worth exploring ITS for skills that, for example, are gross, and open" and that "Integrating tasks that target motor abilities, such as strength or flexibility, can be part of it." It further suggests that "The integration of theories in ITS from related research fields, such as training periodization, can be investigated," addressing the lack of structured training plans and the near-exclusive coordination focus found in the corpus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- ITS would need to move beyond single-skill, coordination-only task sets to offer varied tasks promoting skill proficiency
#### Constraints
- The review frames these as potential directions to investigate, not as tested interventions

### Target Learners
- Learners of psychomotor skills in domains such as sport, medicine, dance, and rehabilitation

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill proficiency development including strength, flexibility, and open-skill adaptation
