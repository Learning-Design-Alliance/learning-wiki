Combine teacher training, real-time data feedback, supervision, materials and lesson plans into one remedial literacy package¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends combining pedagogical and management elements rather than testing single inputs: "the Bonsaaso study combines different pedagogical and management elements such as teacher training, constant feedback and supervision, discussions of student-learning outcomes, materials development, and lesson plans". The package included a 90-minute daily English period, explicit phonics training, GES-format lesson plans using the 'I do, we do, you do' method, locally created materials, and weekly supervision visits with unannounced monthly observations and private corrective feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training on phonics and the five components of reading; supervision visits by GES and MVP staff; supplementary materials since textbooks were too complex for early readers; lesson plans in the GES-approved format

Constraints¶

  • The study objective was not to identify which component has the maximum impact on learning

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 3 and 4 primary students in low-income rural schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Basic English reading skills (letters, words, paragraphs, stories)

Claims¶

  • A multi-component literacy treatment nearly doubled the percentage of Grade 3–4 children classified as able to read compared to control [+W]
  • Non-readers fell by 30 percentage points in the treatment group versus 9 points in control [+W]
  • Grade-specific gains in the 'can read' category were much higher for treatment in both P3 and P4 [+W]
  • Most children remained unable to read simple sentences at endline regardless of group [-W]
  • The EGRS RCT found that the structured pedagogy model combining on-site coaching, quality materials, and scripted lesson plans was the most effective of the tested models for improving early grade reading outcomes [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design training opportunities to combine modeling with performance feedback, ideally as an initial workshop followed by one-to-one coaching
