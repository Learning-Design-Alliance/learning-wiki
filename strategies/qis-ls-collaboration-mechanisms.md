# Build QIS-LS collaboration through interdisciplinary groups within departments and centers that use shared empirical data exploration as a common ground

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes concrete mechanisms for bringing quantum information scientists and learning scientists into sustained collaboration. The first is "the creation of interdisciplinary groups within existing higher education institutions", modeled on the University of Washington's Physics Education Group (education research embedded in a disciplinary department with a Ph.D. track) and UC Berkeley's SESAME doctoral program (interdepartmental collaboration between education and disciplinary faculty). The second is creating QIS learning centers modeled on the NSF-funded LIFE Center, where "an effective strategy identified to promote interdisciplinary collaboration involved exploring and discussing empirical data from different individual investigators as a shared focal point", fostering mutual discovery and reciprocal expertise affirmation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cultural alignment and infrastructure support; cultivation of shared understanding, respect for different forms of expertise, and sustained investigative interactions around a common problem space
- For centers: funding structures that incentivize genuinely interdisciplinary work, as NSF renewal funding did for the LIFE Center
#### Constraints
- Interdisciplinary programs are frequently underfunded and vulnerable to closure; faculty, particularly early-career researchers, can find themselves isolated from disciplinary homes and unsupported by promotion systems

### Target Learners
- Pre-college learners ultimately served; university doctoral students trained in interdisciplinary QIS education research

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting learning of quantum concepts and practices and building institutional capacity for QIS education research
