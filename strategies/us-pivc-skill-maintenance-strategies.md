# Sustain simulation skills through ongoing practice opportunities, logbook signoffs, designated skills leads, collaborative partnerships and equipment maintenance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To ensure skill retention beyond the initial course, the article's maintenance domain recommends a set of concrete actions: providing "continual practice opportunities" such as self-directed drop-in sessions, implementing logbook signoffs so competence is signed off in clinical practice, appointing designated US skills leads to oversee training and equipment, fostering collaborative partnerships with partnering hospitals to scale faculty capacity, and maintaining US equipment as technology dates. Learners and educators jointly stressed these measures in focus groups and the post-training survey.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional roles (designated US skills lead) and session timing to reset and maintain equipment
- Partnership agreements with hospitals to expand faculty and manpower
- A logbook mechanism linking course completion to clinical practice signoff
#### Constraints
- Long-term impact on skill retention and patient outcomes has not yet been evaluated; the authors call for follow-up assessments in clinical placements

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students entering foundation training
- Faculty and clinical skills educators delivering US training

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention and continued refinement of ultrasound-guided cannulation skills in clinical practice
