# Brain-Based Learning Activities

## Description
Activities that engage multiple senses (visual, auditory, kinesthetic, tactile) to activate different parts of the brain, enhancing learning and retention. Involves getting learners talking, teaching each other face-to-face, and providing breaks for the brain.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities that involve movement, verbal interaction, and visual aids. Class time for learners to process information and rest their brains. A supportive and engaging classroom environment.
#### Constraints
- May require more preparation and classroom management. Some learners may be uncomfortable with movement or teaching others.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, particularly beneficial for learners who struggle with attention or need more active learning experiences

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve learning and retention, engage multiple senses, enhance social-emotional connection to learning, refocus attention

### Affordances
- Engages more parts of the brain during the learning process. Connects verbal, visual, and kinesthetic learning modalities. Promotes deeper understanding and retention. Provides opportunities for learners to teach each other, reinforcing learning.

### Personalization
- Adapting activities to fit various content areas. Small changes to make the activity accessible to all. Providing options for movement and expression. Using learner-generated content and examples.
