# Adopt PBP training in high-volume national surgical training programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating PBP into high-volume national training programmes, concluding that the findings "support PBP as an efficient, scalable, and economically viable model for high‐volume surgical training programmes". At scale, shared metrics, standardised proficiency benchmarks and metric-based deliberate practice would allow consistent implementation across multiple centres and amortise the fixed metric-development cost. The recommendation rests on the cost crossover beyond ~25 trainees and superior proficiency outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient trainee volume (beyond the ~25-trainee equivalence point) to amortise metric development and validation costs
- Shared, validated performance metrics and standardised proficiency benchmarks across centres
#### Constraints
- Single high-volume training centre (ORSI Academy); the article notes this may limit generalisability to environments with different resources, faculty expertise, or infrastructure
- Personal trainee expenses were not included, which the authors say makes the estimate conservative

### Target Learners
- Large cohorts of surgical and robotic trainees in national programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardised, quality-assured acquisition of robotic surgical skills to proficiency
