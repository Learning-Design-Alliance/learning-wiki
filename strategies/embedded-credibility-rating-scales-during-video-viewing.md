# Use concomitant sourcing prompts (embedded credibility rating scales during viewing) to direct young students' attention to source when watching videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy embeds source credibility rating scales directly in the video player so that students rate each speaker's expertise and trustworthiness while watching. In the study, after 10 seconds of viewing each excerpt a question appeared on screen, and students "were asked to give their answer to move on to the next video", forcing engagement with source information. The authors report this concomitant prompting improved recognition, convincingness choices and expertise justifications, and conclude it "seems promising to train young students to pay more attention to the source when watching videos online".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rating-scale questions must be embedded in the video player during viewing and answering must be required to proceed, so the prompt directly requires consideration of source information during processing.
#### Constraints
- Effects were moderate, possibly reduced because the conflicting-arguments material itself fosters source consideration; conclusions are limited to embedded sources (the on-screen speaker) and need replication with different age groups and video material.

### Target Learners
- middle-school students (seventh graders, average age 13)

### Target Learning Goals
- considering the source of information in videos: memorizing speaker identity, evaluating source credibility and expertise, and justifying convincingness judgments by source expertise
