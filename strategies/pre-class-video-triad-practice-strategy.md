# Use pre-class expert demonstration videos with in-class self-directed triad practice to increase efficiency of clinical skill teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends delivering clinical procedural skill teaching by having students watch expert-made demonstration videos before class, then practice in same-level peer triads, because increased student intake and limited campus capacity demand more efficient delivery. The authors state this model "is potentially an effective way to address the current challenges and improve the efficiency of clinical procedural skill teaching in medical schools", and nearly 80% of students complied with pre-class watching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Demonstration videos developed by clinical experts must be disseminated before class, and students given access to the assigned video in class if they did not watch it beforehand
#### Constraints
- The study had no control groups comparing triad with dyad or individual practice, and only immediate post-practice performance was assessed, with retention and transfer not evaluated

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in large-cohort programs

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of clinical procedural skills
