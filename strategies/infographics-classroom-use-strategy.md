# Use infographics in undergraduate mathematics courses to introduce topics, start discussions, and demonstrate applications of mathematics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating educational infographics into the classroom in several roles: "an interesting infographic can be used to introduce a new topic or to give a broad overview of a new subject," as a discussion starter where students answer questions or speculate, as a starting point for in-depth research, and as assessment by having students create their own. The twelve math-and-climate posters instantiate this for undergraduate mathematics courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need the competencies students identified: creativity (70%), deep knowledge of the discipline (60%), ability to analyze information (55%), and fluency in a variety of software programs (35%)
#### Constraints
- Students noted weaknesses: information not straightforward enough to understand (17%) and infographics overloaded with details (15%), which the authors link to the amount of information a single poster can represent and possibly instructor qualifications

### Target Learners
- undergraduate mathematics students
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating applications of mathematics
- engaging students with climate science content
