# Structure enacted modelling sequences around regular connections between the experimental world and the world of theories and models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The occultation sequence is based on modelling principles (Halloun & Hestenes, 1985; Sensevy et al., 2008) that "require regular connections by the learners between the experimental world and the world of theories and models". Learners first use well-defined words and compare and classify objects and actions in the experimental world; a model with specificities and general rules then emerges; the rules generate predictions tested against the experimental world; and failed predictions or model manipulations (enrichment, new predictions, applications to new objects) loop back to earlier activities. The Human Orrery serves as the enacted experimental world, a model of the Solar System from which learners build and test predictions such as transit periodicity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A model that represents salient characteristics of experimental objects, allows communication about object specificities, and unifies different classes of objects; an experimental world that cannot be accessed directly, so predictions must be tested through activity
#### Constraints
- The Human Orrery as experimental world presents only some specific features (e.g. distances and not sizes), and its 2D geometry limits which predictions (such as real transit frequency) it can support

### Target Learners
- 12-year-old middle school pupils
- 1st-year university students

### Target Learning Goals
- occultation and transit phenomena
- planetary relative motion
- model building and model validity
