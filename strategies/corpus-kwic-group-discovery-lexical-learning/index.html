Corpus-based group discovery of word collocations using KWIC concordance lines¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This is a learner-centered activity sequence in which students receive target lexical items, type a word into the corpus, display concordance lines in KWIC format, observe the lines to identify collocations, meaning, and part of speech, then discuss and report findings to the class. The article states students "become active 'digger' of vocabulary" rather than passive receivers of lexical information.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A short orientation on using the Wikipedia Corpus before beginning lexical teaching and learning.

Constraints¶

  • The strategy was less effective for low-level learners, who found the new method too challenging.

Target Learners¶

  • First-year university English majors in mainland China

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Lexical acquisition: word meaning, part of speech, collocations, and commonly used expressions

Claims¶

  • The experimental group's collocation scores improved significantly from pretest to posttest after corpus-based instruction [+W]
  • Corpus-tool instruction significantly improves EFL student teachers' production of verb-noun collocations in writing, with a medium-to-large effect [+W]
  • EFL student teachers hold positive views of concordancing for collocation learning and most intend future use [+W]
  • For advanced (Level I) learners, conventional instruction was slightly more suitable than corpus-based instruction, though the difference was not statistically significant. [+W]
  • Low-level (Level III) learners performed significantly better under conventional lexical instruction than under corpus-based instruction with the Wikipedia Corpus. [~W]
  • Corpus-based lexical instruction using the Wikipedia Corpus produced significantly higher lexical gains than conventional instruction for medium-level (Level II) learners. [+W]
  • Corpus-based instruction using COCA concordance lines improved EFL students' verb-noun collocation recognition more than traditional instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design curriculum materials that integrate concordancing programs and corpus-based activities into language programs
  • Integrate corpus consultation into EFL teacher training and collocation teaching
