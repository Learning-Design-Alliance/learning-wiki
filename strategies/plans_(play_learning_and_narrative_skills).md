# PLaNS (Play Learning and Narrative Skills)

## Description
PLaNS is a guided-play approach to early literacy in which children develop narrative and writing skills by building stories together through structured play — constructing settings and characters (often with physical materials or digital tools), acting out story events, and then drafting written narratives from the shared story. The teacher's role is to set up the play context, join or observe the play, and guide children's narrative moves with prompts and questions rather than direct instruction.

## Design Implications

Guided play combines children's self-direction with intentional adult scaffolding, and meta-analytic evidence indicates it outperforms both free play and direct instruction for early-years learning outcomes [Guidance during play improves learning outcomes relative to free play.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S]. Narrative play is especially productive for literacy: children who rehearse story structure orally through play show stronger narrative production when they come to write [Dialogic, interactive story experiences improve children's expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+M]. The play context also supports motivation by giving children autonomy over story content [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A rich play context (construction materials, props, figures, or digital story-building tools) that affords story creation
- Teacher guidance that is contingent — prompting, questioning, and joining the play rather than directing it [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Time for collaborative storytelling in small groups, followed by a bridge into writing
- A writing purpose and, ideally, an audience for the finished stories ([Authentic Audiences and Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))

#### Constraints
- Requires substantial planning and materials preparation; unstructured "story play" without teacher guidance yields weaker literacy gains [Guidance during play improves learning outcomes relative to free play.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [~S]
- Overly directive teaching during play collapses it into instruction and removes the motivational and collaborative benefits [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Less suitable where curriculum time is tightly prescribed to discrete writing outcomes; the oral-to-written bridge takes multiple sessions
- Children with limited oral language or play experience may need explicit modeling of narrative structure before contributing

#### Implementation Variability
- Physical play (blocks, small-world figures, role-play areas) versus digital story-building tools
- Whole-class shared story building versus small-group collaborative storytelling
- Acting out stories before writing ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) versus narrating over constructed scenes
- Duration: single enrichment sessions versus term-long narrative projects

### Target Learners
- Primary/early-years learners (roughly ages 4–9) developing narrative competence
- Reluctant writers, who can generate content orally through play before facing the blank page
- Children developing oral language; interactive story talk improves expressive language [Dialogic, interactive story experiences improve children's expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+M]
- Less appropriate for older learners who have automatized narrative structure and need genre-specific writing instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Narrative structure: setting, characters, sequence, problem–resolution
- Oral-to-written composition: translating told stories into written text
- Vocabulary and expressive language growth [Dialogic, interactive story experiences improve children's expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+M]
- Collaboration and perspective-taking through shared story construction

### Instructions
1. Establish a story-rich play context with materials that suggest characters and settings.
2. Launch a shared story premise; let children propose and negotiate plot events collaboratively.
3. Join the play as a co-player, using contingent prompts ("What happens next? Why does she want that?") to deepen narrative structure [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].
4. Have children [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) — dramatize key story events to consolidate sequence and character motivation.
5. Bridge to writing: children draft their own versions of the co-constructed story, with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) during drafting.
6. Share and celebrate finished stories with an audience; revisit and extend the play world across sessions.
