# Target educational interventions at areas of demonstrated unpreparedness and design transitional support deliberately

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that "Educational interventions are needed to address areas of unpreparedness (eg, multidisciplinary team-working, prescribing and clinical reasoning)" and that transitional interventions such as shadowing and induction should be carefully designed and implemented on both undergraduate and postgraduate sides. It also recommends future research adopt a programmatic, rigorous approach with clear definitions of preparedness, multiple stakeholder perspectives and multisite longitudinal designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should target the specific domains where the review found unpreparedness, such as prescribing, team-working and clinical reasoning
#### Constraints
- Evidence for assistantship efficacy is sparse; shadowing should reflect the new post and be reinforced with related teaching, since prolonged shadowing can become ineffectual

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students
- medical graduates entering Foundation training

### Target Learning Goals
- safe prescribing
- multidisciplinary team-working
- clinical reasoning and emergency management
- smooth transition into workplace practice

### Affordances
- Six Theme Preparedness Framework
