# Questioning Techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Questioning techniques are deliberate patterns of asking — before, during, and after instruction — designed to elicit learner thinking rather than simply check recall. They range from activating questions posed before new content, to Socratic probing during discussion, to self-explanation prompts learners apply to their own work. The technique lies less in the questions themselves than in their timing, cognitive demand, and the response conditions (wait time, follow-up probes) that surround them.

## Design Implications

Effective questioning converts passive reception into generative processing: answering a question requires retrieving, organizing, or elaborating knowledge rather than recognizing it [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Questions also function as formative assessment — the answers reveal misconceptions the instructor can act on in real time [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The cognitive demand of the question matters: low-level recall questions produce shallow processing, while questions requiring explanation, prediction, or comparison drive deeper construction of understanding [Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.](../claims/eliciting-self-explanations-improves-understanding.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions sequenced to build: activation before content, probing during, consolidation after ([Activation](activation.md))
- Sufficient wait time (typically 3+ seconds) after asking, so learners can compose substantive answers
- A response structure that distributes participation beyond volunteers (cold call, think-pair-share, response systems)
- Follow-up probes ("Why?", "How does that compare to…?") that press for reasoning, not just answers

#### Constraints
- Default wait time in classrooms is often under one second, which reliably reduces the length and quality of student responses [Increasing wait time improves the quality of student responses.](../claims/increasing-wait-time-improves-response-quality.md) [+S]
- Rapid-fire low-level recall questioning can feel punitive and suppress participation, particularly for anxious learners [~M]
- Questions pitched far above learners' current knowledge produce guessing rather than reasoning; effectiveness depends on calibration to prior knowledge [~M]
- Calling only on volunteers systematically skews formative information toward the most confident learners [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-generated**: Socratic sequences, hinge questions, anticipation guides before reading
- **Learner-generated**: question generation and peer questioning, which shift learners into the questioner role and support self-regulated learning [+M]
- **Embedded prompts**: written questions inserted into text or video that prompt [self-explanation](../elements/articulation.md) at key points
- **Digital**: classroom response systems (e.g., [Peer Instruction](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md) with clickers) that make every learner answer simultaneously

### Target Learners
- Learners of all levels benefit from well-calibrated questioning, but the payoff is largest when questions require generative answers rather than recognition [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]
- Novices need scaffolded question sequences; experts benefit more from open, ill-structured questions that provoke [cognitive disequilibrium](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [~M]
- Reluctant or anxious participants need low-stakes response structures (think-pair-share, written responses) before public questioning

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: questions requiring explanation and prediction force learners to connect ideas
- Prior knowledge activation: pre-questions prepare learners to integrate new content [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Metacognition and self-regulation: learner-generated questioning builds monitoring habits
- Formative diagnosis: surfacing misconceptions for immediate instructional adjustment [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Open with activation questions** — pose one or two questions that surface what learners already know or believe about the topic ([Activation](activation.md)).
2. **Ask at the right cognitive level** — favor "why," "how," "predict," and "compare" questions over yes/no or pure recall; reserve recall questions for checking prerequisites.
3. **Wait** — hold 3–5 seconds of silence after asking, and after a learner answers, before responding [Increasing wait time improves the quality of student responses.](../claims/increasing-wait-time-improves-response-quality.md) [+S].
4. **Distribute responses** — use think-pair-share, cold call with norms of safety, or response systems so all learners answer, not just volunteers.
5. **Probe for reasoning** — follow answers with "What makes you say that?" or "How would that work if…?" to elicit [articulation](../elements/articulation.md) of underlying thinking.
6. **Act on the answers** — treat responses as formative data; reteach or re-sequence when answers reveal misconceptions [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].
7. **Close with consolidation questions** — ask learners to summarize, generate their own questions about the material, or predict applications.

## Related Strategies
- [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) — questioning is the primary engine that drives productive discussion
- [Self-Explanation](../elements/articulation.md) — questioning turned inward; prompts asking "why does this work?" produce the same generative processing
- [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) — low-stakes questioning used to surface learner state and readiness
- [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) — sustained questioning of claims and evidence is the dialogic form of argumentation

## Examples
- **Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur, Harvard)** — conceptual questions posed via clickers, individual answer, peer discussion, re-answer; the question sequence is the core of the method ([https://blog.peerinstruction.net](https://blog.peerinstruction.net)).
- **Reciprocal Teaching (Palincsar & Brown)** — learners rotate roles including "question asker" while reading expository text, generating their own comprehension questions.
- **Hinge questions in formative assessment** — a single multiple-choice question with diagnostic distractors, posed mid-lesson, that determines whether the teacher moves on or reteaches (Black & Wiliam's "inside the black box" practice).
