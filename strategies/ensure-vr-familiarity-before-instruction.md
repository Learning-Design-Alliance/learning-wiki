# Ensure participant familiarity with VR before deploying it for instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because unfamiliarity with VR controls and novelty can overwhelm or distract learners, "efforts should be made to ensure familiarity before optimal efﬁciency is attained". In the study, participants who did not prefer VR mostly felt uncomfortable with the overwhelming sensory input or found the environment hard to navigate, while preferring the more familiar hands-on or desktop conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- learners need prior familiarity with VR controls and navigation before the technology's benefits can be fully realized
#### Constraints
- the authors note this applies currently, since control and novelty issues may one day no longer be relevant as the technology becomes more common

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students new to VR

### Target Learning Goals
- any physics or astronomy content delivered via VR simulation
