# Individual Deliberate Practice

## Description
Deliberate practice is a structured approach to skill development that focuses on intentional, effective, and consistent effort to improve performance. It involves breaking down skills into manageable parts, focusing on specific areas for improvement, setting goals, and receiving timely, descriptive feedback. This type of practice requires sustained, repeated actions to develop automaticity, which reduces mental load and facilitates the learning of more complex tasks. Key elements include goal setting, perseverance, and feedback in the moment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires structured training, expert feedback, and opportunities for repeated practice. Needs goal setting to give focus to practice. Requires short, sharp bursts of practice at regular intervals.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging to implement without expert guidance or feedback. Can be time-consuming and require significant effort. Overwhelming feedback can cause confusion and dishearten learners.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track the number of hours spent practicing, improvements in specific skills, and the level of automaticity achieved. Observe changes in performance and the ability to take on more challenging tasks.

## Impact
- Research shows deliberate practice leads to expertise and improved performance (Ericsson, 2007). Consistent practice with feedback promotes skill development and automaticity, improving performance in various domains (Lemov). Spaced practice has been shown to improve long-term learning and retention (Carpenter et al., 2012).
