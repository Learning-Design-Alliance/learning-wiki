# Record incorrect solutions with physical reasoning in students' worksheets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
During the problem-solving phase the teacher writes proposed solutions on the board without reasoning (brainstorming), incorrect solutions are excluded by physical reasoning, and students record in their worksheets both the incorrect solutions with the physical reasoning for their elimination and the correct solution. The authors list this as a benefit of P&E: "students write into their worksheets also incorrect solutions including physical reasoning and this helps them a lot in their further study", and they attribute the most significant year-to-year improvement to this worksheet practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A worksheet for each problem dealt with in the lesson, whatever the assignment format
#### Constraints
- Worksheet structure was developed gradually through several-year-long research and international project experience

### Target Learners
- first-year bachelor students in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- elimination of misconceptions
- physical reasoning for correct and incorrect solutions
