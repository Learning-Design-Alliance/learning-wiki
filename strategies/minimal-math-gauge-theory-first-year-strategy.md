# Teach gauge theory to first-year students using mathematically minimal analogue models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing gauge theory through intuitive analogue models (time zones, exchange rates) presented with as little mathematics as possible, so the concept becomes accessible to a wide audience before or instead of the abstract particle-physics formulation. The author reports this was used in a first-year particle physics and cosmology course and that students appreciated the effort and enjoyed the more detailed insight into modern particle physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful prior introduction of the abstract symmetry concept, since the symmetries encountered are more abstract than students are used to.
#### Constraints
- The author notes it is hard to make good exam questions on gauge theory at this level, and the questionnaire did not test actual understanding of gauge theory.

### Target Learners
- first-year bachelor physics students
- possibly earlier students if required
- scientifically interested populace outside universities

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding gauge symmetries, the construction of the standard model, and the role of the Higgs field

### Affordances
- Gauge Theory Local Symmetry Higgs Framework
