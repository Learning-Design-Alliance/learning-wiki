# Adopt a conservative abstract-screening strategy with a 'maybe' category and keyword exclusion filters to prevent premature exclusion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
At abstract screening, the team "intentionally adopted a conservative screening strategy, retaining studies for further review unless they were not clearly irrelevant at the abstract screening." They used a "maybe" or "uncertain" category as a safeguard against premature exclusion, and used keyword exclusion (e.g., flagging "elementary," "middle school," or "primary education" for a higher-education review) to reduce screening burden and improve consistency when abstracts were sparse or terminology differed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Review management systems that allow marking studies as 'maybe' and adding comments to justify decisions
- Shared screening guidelines and calibration exercises for interdisciplinary teams
#### Constraints
- The inclusive approach increases the number of studies requiring further review, adding workload and extending the screening timeline

### Target Learners
- novice researchers and interdisciplinary teams conducting abstract screening

### Target Learning Goals
- consistent and rigorous study selection in systematic reviews
