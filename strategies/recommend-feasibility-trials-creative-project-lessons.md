# Medical schools should investigate feasibility and acceptability of creative project-based lessons in their own curricula, using mixed-methods evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that "medical schools investigate the feasibility and acceptability of such creative project-based lessons in their own curriculums," with objective assessments of learning gain versus traditional or technology-enhanced approaches, mixed-methods evaluation, and head-to-head cost-effectiveness comparison with high-fidelity counterparts. It also calls for a taxonomy of lesson-design variants and reproduction of published designs with reporting of reproducibility and reception.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mixed-methods evaluation, since student interaction with a nontraditional learning modality is a complex phenomenon
#### Constraints
- Uncertainty remains around true cost-effectiveness, learning gain versus high-fidelity simulations, and generalisability across cultural backgrounds

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- medical curriculum design
- learning gain assessment
