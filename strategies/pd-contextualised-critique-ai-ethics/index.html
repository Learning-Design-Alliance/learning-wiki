Professional development for AI ethics should create spaces for contextualised critique and resistance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that ethical engagement with AI 'cannot be fostered through information-focused training alone'. Professional development should instead create spaces for contextualised critique, critical stance-taking, and resistance to oppressive practices and power imbalances, involving diverse actors—students, teachers, families, and marginalised communities—in questioning and co-designing equitable digital futures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Local scenarios and cases grounded in concrete decision-making to support co-design of ethical practices

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions that defining local parameters such as cultural values and policies 'may ultimately limit critical ethical decision-making'

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers
  • teacher educators
  • policymakers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical stance-taking on AI use
  • ethical agency in situated contexts

Affordances¶

  • Critical Ecological Activity Model Ai Ethics

Related Strategies¶

  • Structure AI-focused professional development as a supported trajectory of planning, reflection, design autonomy, and embedded critical AI literacy
  • Embed AI policy literacy, peer-led communities of practice, and shared AI resources into professional development
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
