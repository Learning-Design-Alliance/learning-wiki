# Design simulation-training studies in complex clinical settings with whole-team enrollment, learner and evaluator continuity, and confirmatory validation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors distill lessons from a study whose execution was disrupted by ICU staffing and trainee rotation into recommendations for future evaluations of simulation training. They advise studying "the entire interprofessional team" with a confirmatory factor analysis building on the validated survey, emphasizing "established continuity with the learners and the peer evaluators" in the design, training operational staff in data collection, and evaluating the impact on patient outcomes, which would require a larger trainee sample.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A larger sample size of trainees to assess patient outcomes
- Commitment from trainees for planned follow-up focus groups
#### Constraints
- Clinician availability, time, response rates, ability for follow-up, protocol changes, and data collection from clinical staff altered the original study approach

### Target Learners
- physician fellows and residents
- the entire interprofessional ICU team

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating the effectiveness of team-focused simulation training
- improving teamwork and communication in ECMO care
