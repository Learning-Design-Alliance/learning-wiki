# Adapt RaT curricula to local cultural, institutional, and workforce characteristics rather than directly adopting Western-designed programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the review's geographic-gap finding: Western-designed RaT curricula should not be transplanted without adaptation to local contexts. The review states that "educational approaches shown to be effective in Western settings may not fully account for differences in motivational drivers, professional identity formation, interprofessional dynamics, and clinical workflow structures within GCC healthcare environments." It calls for "locally developed approaches" reflecting cultural, institutional, and workforce characteristics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regional needs assessments to understand residents' educational roles and learning needs
- Culturally responsive assessment tools developed and validated locally
#### Constraints
- Existing RaT evidence is almost entirely Western and may not transfer to Middle Eastern training environments
- Limited attention to interprofessional teaching may inadequately reflect multicultural healthcare systems common in the region

### Target Learners
- Residents training in GCC and other under-researched healthcare systems, within multicultural clinical teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Teaching competencies aligned with regional and international frameworks including EmiratesMed and ACGME-International standards
