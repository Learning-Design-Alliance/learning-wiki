Mandate math word problems in the curriculum together with self-regulating and metacognitive strategy instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's closing recommendation is that "Mandatory inclusion of math word problems in the curriculum, as well as an application of self-regulating and metacognitive strategies, would facilitate learners' manner of reasoning and would increase learners' skills for generalizing knowledge and for solving the problems from daily life." It also urges that learners' individual differences in language, numerical abilities, and general cognitive abilities be considered when orienting them toward applying cognitive components of reading and mathematics, and that difficulties be understood first.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need support: the article asks how the program can be modified to train pre-service and in-service teachers.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes regular textbooks contain an insufficient number of math word problems, and intervention materials contained a high percentage of challenging problems.

Target Learners¶

  • primary and lower secondary school learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • math word problem solving
  • generalizing knowledge to daily-life problems

Claims¶

  • The intervention benefited learners of all success levels in fifth grade but only very-good performers in third grade [+W]
  • Learners benefited more from metacognitive instruction on complex, multi-operation and linguistically inconsistent word problems than on simple one-operation problems [+W]
  • Fifth graders receiving metacognitive instruction significantly outperformed control peers on post-test math word problem solving [+W]
  • No significant gender difference in math word problem performance was observed after the intervention in either grade [+W]
  • Mathematical metacognition statistically mediates the association between mathematics anxiety and children's word problem solving after controlling for IQ [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Foster autonomy and curiosity-driven learning by embedding metacognitive processes and self-regulation elements into education
