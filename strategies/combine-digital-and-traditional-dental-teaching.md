# Combine digital assessment systems with traditional teaching methods rather than replacing them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying digital evaluation systems as a supplement to, not a replacement for, traditional preclinical dental teaching. It concludes that "a flexible combination of multiple digital assessment systems and traditional methods could enhance the effectiveness of preclinical simulation teaching", noting traditional teaching retains irreplaceable aspects such as face-to-face emotional exchange, on-site practical guidance, and no additional equipment costs. The strategy pairs machine-scored objective assessment with instructor-led instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to digital assessment equipment and trained faculty able to interpret machine scores alongside conventional instruction
#### Constraints
- The article states its study cannot prove digital methods are indispensable, and notes open questions on cost-effectiveness, student satisfaction and potential adverse effects

### Target Learners
- dental students in preclinical operative dentistry
- clinician-teachers in dental schools

### Target Learning Goals
- cavity preparation skill acquisition
- objective and homogeneous assessment of preparation competence
