# Use tangible multi-tablet environments to foster consensus, task division, and reciprocal interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing collaborative gamified activities where interaction spans multiple devices and physical objects, as the tangible version of Quizbot demonstrated. The tangible version outperformed the tactile one in reaching consensus, task division, and reciprocal interaction, showing that "tangible interactions on top of a screen are more effective to support basic manipulations". This strategy involves scattering tablets on the floor with physical artifacts to encourage physical mobility and workspace awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires tablets scattered on a large area with physical objects representing game elements
- Requires a robot or physical artifact that children can manipulate to interact with digital content
#### Constraints
- The tangible version showed poorer time management than the tactile version, so activities requiring efficient time use may favor tactile interfaces

### Target Learners
- Primary school children aged 9-10 working in groups of four

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaboration skills including negotiation, planning, communication, and organization
