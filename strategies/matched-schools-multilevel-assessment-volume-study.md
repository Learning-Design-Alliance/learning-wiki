# Test the assessment-volume effect with multi-level modelling of individuals at schools matched on entry qualifications but differing in assessment time.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose a future study design to overcome the small number of institutions in aggregate-level correlations. They identify schools with "broadly similar entry qualifications but a wide range of assessment times" and predict that students at high-assessment schools (Queen's University Belfast, Newcastle, Bristol) should outperform those at low-assessment schools (Leeds, Sheffield, Manchester or St George's) on a comparable outcome measure, even taking individual entry qualifications into account. Analysis would use multi-level modelling with students clustered within schools for greater power.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A comparable outcome measure across schools, such as a Royal College examination, MSC-AA-generated MCQ items, or a future national licensing examination.
#### Constraints
- The present aggregate correlational study is exploratory because the number of institutions is small for assessing statistical significance.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students (A100 programmes)

### Target Learning Goals
- Medical knowledge and clinical skills assessed in postgraduate examinations
