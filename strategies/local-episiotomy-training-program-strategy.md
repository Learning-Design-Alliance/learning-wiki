# Develop and evaluate a local training program in normal vaginal birth without episiotomy, addressing attitudes and evidence generalisability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a practice-change strategy centred on training: because most clinicians report not being trained to minimise tears, they "propose to develop and evaluate a local training program which will include dialogue with medical and midwifery training programs in universities". The program must address existing attitudes and the reasons behind them, and the authors argue uncertainty about whether selective-episiotomy trial evidence generalises to South-East Asian women must also be tackled. They cite Norway's multi-pronged education program—covering selective mediolateral episiotomy technique, manual perineal support with visualisation, and accoucheur–woman communication—as a model that reduced severe tear rates while slightly raising episiotomy use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must address existing attitudes and the reasons behind them, not only technique
- Dialogue with university medical and midwifery training programs, since current Vietnamese textbooks and practical training advocate routine episiotomy
- Local evidence on perineal length and postpartum outcomes to allay concerns that Vietnamese women differ from trial populations
#### Constraints
- The authors state it remains unclear what episiotomy rate in Vietnamese hospitals would give the greatest benefit for the least harm
- Entrenched practices and attitudes indicate changing episiotomy practice in Viet Nam will not be easy; the authors advocate patience and small incremental changes

### Target Learners
- Obstetricians and midwives providing delivery care in Vietnamese maternity hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Performing normal vaginal birth with an intact perineum
- Minimising 3rd-4th degree perineal tears
- Selective (medically indicated) rather than routine episiotomy use
