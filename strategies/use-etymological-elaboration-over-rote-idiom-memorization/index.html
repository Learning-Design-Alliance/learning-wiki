Use etymological elaboration in class instead of rote memorization of idioms¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors recommend that "teachers of English use this technique in their classes and encourage students to use this helpful technique rather than asking them to memorize the idioms in a rote fashion". The strategy consists of presenting the origins of idioms and having learners work with them (e.g., matching idioms to origins) before or alongside meaning-focused practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to accurate etymological origins for the idioms being taught, presented so learners can engage with them (as in the matching exercise with an answer key).

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is one small quasi-experiment with 50 advanced EFL learners and 15 idioms.

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners in classroom settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding and retaining idiomatic expressions

Claims¶

  • Etymological elaboration improves EFL learners' comprehension of idioms [+W]
  • Etymological elaboration improves EFL learners' one-week retention of idioms [+W]
