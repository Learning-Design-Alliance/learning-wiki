Design clicker questions as multiple choice, starting with simple factual review questions and ending with application problems¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's integration strategy recasts warm-up, homework, and review questions into multiple-choice or simple-answer formats because clickers cannot accept fractions, rational numbers, or symbols. Teachers "started with some simple and primarily factual questions" to review and activate prior knowledge, then asked more challenging conceptual questions during the lesson, ending with application problems. The teacher's journal recorded that trial-group students made connections to prior knowledge under this design.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must receive thorough instruction on how to integrate clickers into the classroom properly; proper implementation is not merely adding clicker exercises to traditional lectures.

Constraints¶

  • Clickers do not allow students to submit complicated answers containing fractions, rational numbers, or special mathematics symbols such as % and π.

Target Learners¶

  • high school Geometry students, including ELL and Special Education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • geometry concepts, review of previously learned content, problem-solving application

Claims¶

  • Clicker use improved high school Geometry students' scores on warm-up and homework quizzes reviewing previously learned content [+M]
  • Classroom quizzing delivered by clickers, computer software, or paper improves student performance on classroom exams in middle school and college courses [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Retrieval Practice Warm Ups
  • Low Stakes Quizzing
  • Pair clicker questions with at-home follow-up reflections asking students to explain each statement
