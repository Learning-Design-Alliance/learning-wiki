# Use nurses' spiritual care model profiles to assess and develop spiritual care competence in palliative care nursing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that its findings be applied to workforce development: "The results can be used to assess and develop competence in spiritual care for palliative care nurses." In practice this means classifying nurses' understanding of spiritual care into the four models via open-ended survey questions, profiling their characteristics, and targeting development toward the more competent models C and D. The authors also flag that the capability of being more directive in spiritual conversations, as in Model D, may require more specific training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A survey eliciting what spiritual care means to the nurse and what they do to provide it, codable into the four models
#### Constraints
- The study used a convenience sample whose representativeness is unknown, single-item predictor measures that may have compromised criterion validity, and a sample size that required combining model groups

### Target Learners
- palliative care nurses providing spiritual care

### Target Learning Goals
- developing competence in assessing and caring for patients' spiritual needs at end of life

### Affordances
- Four Models Spiritual Care Competence Taxonomy
