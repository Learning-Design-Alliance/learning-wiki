# Support metacognitive awareness by providing time and place for reflection, regulation-practice tasks, and profile-sensitive support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implications recommend that "students should be provided with the time and place for reflection", that students "need to practice regulation of cognition by planning, monitoring and evaluating their learning processes", and that students with different learning profiles may need different kinds of support — some needing more support for regulation, others more knowledge about their learning. Teachers should also describe metacognitive awareness explicitly to students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need a clear understanding of the nature of metacognitive awareness and the pedagogical competencies to integrate its elements into teaching and assessment practices
#### Constraints
- The article notes higher education teachers are not necessarily sufficiently prepared to teach metacognitive awareness

### Target Learners
- University students, especially those in unorganized-deep and dissonant profiles

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive awareness
- Regulation of cognition
- Organized studying and deep approach
