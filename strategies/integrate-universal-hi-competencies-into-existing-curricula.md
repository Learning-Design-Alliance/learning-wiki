# Integrate a pragmatic set of universal HI competencies into existing postgraduate medical curricula rather than adding stand-alone HI courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating a simplified list of universal HI competency domains into pre-existing curricula for all postgraduate doctors, rather than relying on stand-alone HI training or exhaustive frameworks like IMIA. The authors state "our list of six universal HI competency domains offers a solution, to which areas of increasing importance can be added iteratively" and that postgraduate HI training "represents the best way to create change at scale, alongside training of leaders and individuals seeking more specialist training." They propose 20 universal HI competencies for integration (figure 3).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specialty organisations must identify opportunities to integrate HI competencies across multiple curricula; validation and adaptation overseen by postgraduate medical education leads in collaboration with professional HI standards bodies; implementation requires consultation with stakeholders including CCIOs, HDR-UK, NHS Digital, and the GMC
#### Constraints
- Generic, stand-alone competencies are likely to be ineffective because training curricula, trainee needs and working environments vary greatly

### Target Learners
- all postgraduate medical trainees in the UK

### Target Learning Goals
- universal health informatics competencies including information governance and security, secondary use of data, digital communication, and emerging technologies
