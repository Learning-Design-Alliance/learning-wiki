# Persuade students that homework is valuable by regularly incorporating homework discussions into lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For instructors who prefer intrinsic motivation, the article recommends building students' perception of homework's learning value: "significant increases in homework completion can be achieved by persuading students that homework is a valuable tool for learning physics. This might be done by regularly incorporating discussions of homework into lessons." This targets the observed correlation between section-average perceived value and completion rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular lesson time must be devoted to discussing how homework connects to concepts and exam performance.
#### Constraints
- The perception-completion relationship was weaker than the credit relationship and was measured over a narrow 2.5-3.5 Likert range in ungraded-homework semesters.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- completing assigned homework problems
