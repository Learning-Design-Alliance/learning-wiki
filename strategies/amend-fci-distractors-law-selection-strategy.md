# Amend FCI distractors and add free-text and law-selection sub-questions to assess conceptual understanding more deeply

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concept-inventory design strategy combining free-text versions of target items with law-selection sub-questions following each question. The authors recommend that "a relatively straightforward improvement would be to amend the distractors to bring them into line with the responses revealed by free-test and law-selection questions" and "encourage the use of concept inventory questions that delve more deeply into conceptual understanding".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Capacity to mark free-text responses, whether manually as in this study or automatically, given growing automatic marking accuracy
#### Constraints
- Particular care is needed when designing FT questions because "there are not predetermined options to scaffold a student's response"

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing misconceptions about Newton's laws beyond what multiple-choice responses reveal
