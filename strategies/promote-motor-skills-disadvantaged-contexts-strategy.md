# Promote motor skills in disadvantaged contexts as a route to narrowing SES-achievement gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that intervention studies aimed at promoting motor skills in disadvantaged contexts are needed to deepen understanding of SES-achievement gaps, building on its finding that sensorimotor connectivity change mediates the parental-education-to-grades link. It reasons that "fine motor skill proficiency is strongly correlated with academic achievement, particularly in reading and mathematics" and that educated parents may engage children in activities such as sports, drawing, or building with blocks that develop motor skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should target motor-skill development in disadvantaged households, since the article links parental education to motor development resources such as sports programs, playgrounds, and extracurricular activities.
#### Constraints
- The article calls for such intervention studies as future work; it did not itself test a motor-skills intervention, so effectiveness is not established by this study.

### Target Learners
- children and early adolescents from socioeconomically disadvantaged households

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement in reading and mathematics
- motor skill development
