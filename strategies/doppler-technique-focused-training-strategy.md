# Target Doppler measurement techniques — sample volume placement, VTI tracing, and Doppler angle — when training fellows in LVOT VTI measurement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because fellow error appeared most attributable to Doppler technique rather than image quality or systematic bias, training should emphasize the specific pitfalls the article enumerates: "sample volume placement too close or too far from the aortic valve, inaccurate tracing of VTI spectral display, failure to measure multiple samples in atrial fibrillation, and Doppler angle error." Quantitative Doppler techniques are not part of standard critical care training, so dedicated instruction in pulsed-wave Doppler technique is needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction addressing pulsed-wave sample volume placement, VTI spectral tracing, and Doppler angle selection
- Instructor review of fellow-acquired images to identify technique errors
#### Constraints
- The article recommends further research; the ideal balance of training resources and fellow proficiency remains undetermined

### Target Learners
- critical care fellows learning advanced critical care echocardiography

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate LVOT VTI measurement free of common Doppler pitfalls
