# Managers facilitate newly graduated nurses' continued learning by creating structures for reflection and mentorship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that managers overcome the university–healthcare gap by creating structures for reflection: "we suggest that the managers facilitate nurses continued learning by creating structures for reflection". Managers with extensive nursing experience can act as role models and mentors, making reflection and continued learning part of everyday learning. The conclusion adds that managers should take responsibility for offering the time needed to implement reflection sessions and individual planning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time to implement reflection sessions
- Involvement of experienced nursing managers as mentors
- Supportive structures for continued learning at department level
#### Constraints
- Regular reflection sessions were considered meaningful but difficult to arrange because of heavy workload; shortage of nurses reduced the number of experienced role models

### Target Learners
- newly graduated nurses transitioning from university education to healthcare work

### Target Learning Goals
- continued learning and professional competence development, including practical and leadership skills
