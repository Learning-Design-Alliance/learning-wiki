# Project-Based Transformation of Society

## Description
This instructional strategy involves students working in small groups to identify and propose solutions to crucial problems in their society. Students are tasked with transforming the United States by coming up with 15 crucial problems and 15 laws to address those problems. They then must decide how to enforce each law, such as by fines, rewards, prison sentences, reliance on propaganda, and economic stimuli. This involves identifying issues, developing potential solutions, and considering the practical and ethical implications of their proposals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient time for group work, access to resources for researching societal issues, and clear guidelines for ethical and respectful communication.
#### Constraints
- Potential for students to propose inappropriate or offensive solutions. Requires careful facilitation to ensure ethical considerations and prevent the reinforcement of harmful stereotypes.

### Target Learners

- High school students, particularly those in advanced English or social studies classes, but adaptable to college and adult learning environments.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance problem-solving skills, promote critical thinking about societal issues, foster collaboration and compromise, and encourage consideration of diverse perspectives.

### Affordances
- Promotes engagement and ownership of learning, encourages real-world problem-solving, and enhances understanding of complex social issues. It also fosters collaboration and communication skills.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different societal contexts and student interests. Teachers can adjust the scope and complexity of the project based on student abilities and available resources.

## Assessment Evidence
- Evaluate the thoroughness and feasibility of proposed solutions, assess the ability to consider diverse perspectives, and track participation in group discussions and reflections.

## Impact
- Experience shows that students appreciate how hard it is to compromise and solve national problems to everyone's satisfaction. A few students begin to question simplistic solutions. Some students are shocked to discover how many of their classmates think differently from them, which is not surprising, considering that many have not been allowed to talk about controversial subjects at school.
