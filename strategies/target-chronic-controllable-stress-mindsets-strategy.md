# Target source-specific mindsets for chronic controllable stressors in student stress mindset interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that stress mindset interventions for college students move beyond general mindsets and target beliefs about stress from chronic controllable sources, because these mindsets were most predictive of health. It suggests "interventions targeting mindsets for chronic controllable sources of stress may be more effective than general stress mindset interventions." The strategy rests on the study's correlational findings, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should assess and address source-specific mindsets (acute/chronic × controllable/uncontrollable) rather than only general stress mindsets, since general and source-specific mindsets were only weakly correlated.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and correlational; the article did not test an intervention targeting chronic controllable mindsets.

### Target Learners
- college undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- stress appraisal and coping
- mental and physical health
- academic performance
