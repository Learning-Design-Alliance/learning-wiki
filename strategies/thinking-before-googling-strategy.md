# Have learners generate or guess answers to broad brainstorming questions before searching the internet for specific answers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy asks learners to spend a few minutes generating or guessing their own answers to broad questions about a topic before searching online for answers to more specific problems. The article implemented it as a three-minute answer-generation task on three brainstorming questions, matched in duration to a keyword slide shown to the comparison group, and found it "proved successful in triggering curiosity" and improved recall. The authors recommend such structured interventions that activate internal cognitive processes to transform passive web searches into enriching learning experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must actually produce at least some answer rather than writing that they do not know; the article instructed participants to write at least one phrase and encouraged wild guesses
#### Constraints
- The recall benefit was demonstrated with an unfamiliar topic (modern pentathlon) in a 10-minute search task with undergraduates; metacognitive-judgment benefits did not emerge, and the mechanism remains partially supported only

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- college students encountering unfamiliar topics

### Target Learning Goals
- recall of information found through online search
- curiosity and engagement before searching

### Affordances
- Information Gap Curiosity Mechanism Answer First
