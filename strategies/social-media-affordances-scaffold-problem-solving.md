# Use social media affordances to scaffold problem finding and solution generation in problem-centered learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deliberately leveraging social media affordances at each problem-solving phase. For problem finding, association connects learners to authentic contexts, experts, and diverse stakeholder perspectives; visibility lets learners and facilitators monitor progress and provide just-in-time scaffolding; preservation and searchability let learners access how others defined similar problems, serving as a case library. For solution generation, association supplies alternative and critical viewpoints, and visibility exposes peer modeling, as when ESL students "witnessed peer modeling of not only paraphrasing but also reviewing of other students' responses."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators must plan, implement, and evaluate the experience, intentionally structure groups, and scaffold collaboration
- Learners need support to discern information quality, since social media amplify misinformation
#### Constraints
- Cognitive overload can especially be an issue when using social media to solve complex problems
- Instructional use may lead to distractions and blurred personal-formal boundaries

### Target Learners
- Novice problem solvers in K-12 through graduate problem-centered courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Articulating problem spaces and stakeholder perspectives; proposing, justifying, and evaluating solutions
