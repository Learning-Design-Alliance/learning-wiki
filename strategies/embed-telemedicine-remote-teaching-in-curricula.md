# Embed fluency in telemedicine and remote clinical teaching within medical school curricula to prepare for future disruptions and to serve dispersed teaching sites

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In their future implications the authors recommend that remote clinical teaching of the kind implemented during COVID-19 become a permanent curricular element. They argue that "fluency in telemedicine and the use of remote teaching such as this program should be embedded within medical school curricula", both because new or resurging pandemics pose an ongoing threat and because the program could facilitate distance teaching across geographically separated clinical teaching sites more economically and environmentally feasibly than the current mobile-tutor arrangement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online video platforms and trained tutors and simulated patients
- Institutional capacity to develop case content and schedules rapidly
#### Constraints
- Simulated patients in isolation do not replace real patient encounters
- The remote nature of the program prevented teaching of clinical examination skills

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students at geographically dispersed clinical teaching sites

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical history-taking and reasoning skills delivered remotely
- telemedicine fluency as a professional competency
