# Start with Yourself (Identity Mapping)

## Description
This principle encourages educators to reflect on their own intersectional identities and how these identities shape their perceptions and relationships with others. It involves activities like identity mapping exercises and constructivist listening dyads to explore personal identity markers (race, gender, income, citizenship status, etc.) and their impact on one's work as an educator. This practice is crucial for empathy work, as it helps educators become aware of their biases and assumptions, leading to more equitable interactions with students and families. The goal is to foster self-awareness and create a more inclusive and understanding learning environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a safe and supportive environment for self-reflection and sharing, as well as trained facilitators to guide the activities.
#### Constraints
- Can be emotionally challenging and may require significant time for reflection and processing. The benefits depend on the participants' willingness to be open and honest.
