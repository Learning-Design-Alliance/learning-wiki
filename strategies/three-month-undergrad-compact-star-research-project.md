# Run a three-month guided research project for undergraduates on a topic at the research frontier

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article documents a three-month research project for an undergraduate student in which a senior researcher proposes the topic and guides the work throughout. The topic was chosen so that it is "at the forefront of present active research" while remaining tractable, and the student produced a full report with derivations, figures and parameter studies. This is a concrete, implementable recipe for embedding undergraduates in live research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mentor who proposes the research topic and guides the student through it, as Schaﬀner-Bielich did for Macher
- A research question tractable with basic undergraduate coursework
- About three months of dedicated project time
#### Constraints
- The authors state the work is unfinished: "More reﬁnement in the calculations, and clariﬁcation of the results are needed and are left for future research projects of this kind."

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students (second year of the Magistère des sciences de la matière at ENS-Lyon)

### Target Learning Goals
- Independent research skills in astrophysics; applying thermodynamics, quantum statistics and relativity to an open scientific problem
