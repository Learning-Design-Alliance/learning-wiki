# Use controlled longitudinal intervention programmes to test causal effects of psychomotor stimulation on cognitive skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending observational longitudinal designs into intervention studies. They propose that "a longitudinal proposal such as the one in the present study in which, by establishing control groups, different intervention programmes can be carried out between the pre-test and the post-test throughout the three pre-school child stages" could clarify how motor stimulation relates to cognitive regulation, reaction capacity, attention, and later complex learning skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Establishing control groups between pre-test and post-test
- Carrying out different intervention programmes across the three pre-school stages
#### Constraints
- The present study did not carry out a physical activity programme between pre-test and post-test, so cause-effect relationships could not be established

### Target Learners
- Preschool children aged 3–6 in infant school

### Target Learning Goals
- Cognitive self-regulation, attention, and capacity to discriminate relevant stimuli in relation to psychomotor development
