# Resistance Reading and Counter-Narratives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Resistance reading and writing counter-narratives are strategies to develop learners' abilities to critique a text (or an image or other media) and to respond in writing, sharing their concerns and critiques. Resistance reading requires learners to analyze a book, song lyrics, a commercial, or a poem by first thinking about the context and purpose that informed the text — asking who created it, for whom, and whose interests it serves. Counter-narratives involve crafting a response to the text, either as a rebuttal or as a rewriting of the original from a different perspective, positioning learners as producers of meaning rather than passive consumers.

## Design Implications

Resistance reading treats texts as socially situated artifacts rather than neutral containers of information, drawing on critical literacy traditions [Freire, 1970; hooks, 1994]. Its effectiveness depends on structured support: unguided "critique this text" prompts tend to produce superficial reactions, while scaffolded analysis of authorship, audience, and omitted perspectives produces deeper critical reading [~M]. Writing the counter-narrative converts critique into generative composition, and the act of articulating an alternative perspective functions like self-explanation in that learners must reconstruct the text's assumptions before they can subvert them [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to texts or media rich enough to sustain critique (advertisements, news articles, canonical literature, historical accounts)
- Writing materials or digital tools for composing rebuttals and rewrites
- Explicit framing of the analysis moves — authorship, purpose, audience, power, omission — before learners attempt them independently
- A discussion structure where alternative readings can be voiced and contested safely

#### Constraints
- Requires learners to have strong reading comprehension skills; learners still decoding text have little working-memory capacity left for critical analysis [-M]
- May be challenging for learners to critique texts or media that they agree with — agreement suppresses the analytic distance the strategy requires [-W]
- Without grounding in evidence from the text, resistance reading can collapse into opinion expression or contrarianism rather than critique [-M]
- Can feel inauthentic or politically charged if the teacher pre-determines the "correct" critique, undermining learner ownership [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Learners can write a rebuttal as a letter to the author, an essay, or an op-ed article
- Learners can rewrite the original from a different character's, community's, or era's perspective
- Media forms can vary: song lyrics, commercials, image macros, textbook passages, historical monuments
- Pairing with [Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md) forces learners to argue a reading they did not choose, strengthening perspective-taking

### Target Learners
- Secondary learners (grades 6–12) with fluent reading comprehension
- Learners developing media literacy who encounter persuasive texts daily but rarely analyze them
- Multilingual learners benefit when analysis moves are modeled with accessible vocabulary and short, high-interest media texts [~W]
- Less effective for novice readers; comprehension must precede critique

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical literacy: identifying bias, framing, and power structures in texts and media
- Argumentation: constructing evidence-based rebuttals [Argument Construction](../elements/argument-construction.md)
- Perspective-taking and empathy: inhabiting voices omitted or misrepresented in the original [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) [+W]
- Writing voice and audience awareness: composing for authentic purposes beyond the classroom

### Instructions
1. Select a text or media artifact with a clear point of view or omission ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md)).
2. Learners read or view independently, [annotating](../principles/annotating.md) for authorship, purpose, audience, and what is left out.
3. Learners reflect individually on whose interests the text serves and what an affected party would say ([Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) — if not yet available, use structured reflection prompts).
4. Learners discuss their readings in small groups, comparing interpretations ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) in small-group format).
5. Optionally assign or let learners choose an opposing stance ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)).
6. Learners construct a counter-narrative: a rebuttal or a rewrite from another perspective, supported by textual evidence ([Argument Construction](../elements/argument-construction.md)).
7. Learners share counter-narratives and the class examines which critiques were most persuasive and why.

## Related Strategies
- Media analysis and deconstruction — shares the same analytic moves applied to non-print media
- Debate and argumentative writing — counter-narratives are a written, text-anchored form of rebuttal
- Culturally responsive literature discussion — supplies the texts and community norms that make resistance reading meaningful

## Examples
- **Rethinking Schools / Teaching for Change curricula** ([https://www.rethinkingschools.org](https://www.rethinkingschools.org)) — published lessons in which students read textbook accounts of colonization or labor history, identify whose perspective is centered, and write counter-narratives from Indigenous or worker viewpoints.
- **Facing History and Ourselves** ([https://www.facinghistory.org](https://www.facinghistory.org)) — students analyze propaganda images and write responses that reframe events from victims' perspectives.
- **Advertising critique units** — middle school students resistance-read a commercial, then storyboard a parody ad that exposes the persuasion techniques.
