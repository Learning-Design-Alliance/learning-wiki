# Use coding cards to scaffold the modify-stage toward modeling-based inquiry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's fifth design principle recommends revising coding cards for the modify-stage to pose questions requiring PSTs to use programming to explore the phenomena. The authors report this change "resulted in the PSTs displaying more TCK and CK while programming", with tasks revising underlying mechanisms rather than modifying animation esthetics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coding cards with small programming tasks focused on questions about the scientific phenomena, with an explicit phenomenon, a clear inquiry question and an epistemic goal
#### Constraints
- The need to explicitly address TCK during the modify-stage was indicated by the authors' results and the work of Lee et al. (2024) they cite

### Target Learners
- Pre-service science teachers at the master's level

### Target Learning Goals
- Technological content knowledge (TCK) and content knowledge (CK) for modeling scientific phenomena
