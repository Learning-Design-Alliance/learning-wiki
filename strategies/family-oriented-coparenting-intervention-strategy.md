# Family-oriented intervention combining co-parenting conflict reduction with engagement and depression support for adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that interventions aiming to improve adolescent academic performance take a family-oriented approach: identify adolescents from families with heightened co-parenting conflict, encourage harmonious co-parenting relationships and improve co-parents' capacity to handle childrearing disagreements, and simultaneously provide "depression interventions designed to reduce their depression levels and school engagement programs designed to promote their engagement in academic activities".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of adolescents from families with co-parenting conflict
- Professional support delivered through depression interventions and school engagement programs
- Co-parenting conflict reduction components such as improving co-parents' capacity to handle childrearing disagreements
#### Constraints
- The article states such strategies might alleviate negative effects; it offers recommendations, not tested intervention results, and notes its own cross-sectional design cannot establish causality

### Target Learners
- adolescents aged 10–15 in families with co-parenting conflict in mainland China

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance improvement
- academic engagement promotion
- depression reduction

### Affordances
- Family Individual Factors Academic Performance Mediation Framework
