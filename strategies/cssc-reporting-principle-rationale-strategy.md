# Embed reporting-principle rationale and peer-communication rehearsal in simulation scenarios and debriefings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To address observed knowledge gaps about reporting principles, the authors recommend that simulation training go beyond procedures. They write: "For training design, CSSC scenarios and debriefing could explicitly include the rationale for reporting principles, provide concrete examples of reportable events, and rehearse communication strategies when reporting involves peers or minor events." This targets the finding that nurses erred most on the non-punitive reporting principle item and showed concerns about offending colleagues when reporting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- CSSC scenario and debriefing time must cover safety-culture principles, not only procedural steps
#### Constraints
- Stated for training design within CSSC-style simulation programs; the article notes these are recommendations, not tested interventions in this study

### Target Learners
- actively employed hospital nurses participating in in-service patient-safety training

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding non-punitive, confidential, voluntary reporting principles; confident reporting involving peers and minor events
