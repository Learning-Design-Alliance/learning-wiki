# Relativity lab exercises in which students check whether other metrics fulfill the Einstein equations by modifying the given program sequences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the relativity lab classroom, the teacher proposes that students check whether other metrics fulfill the Einstein equations, reusing the article's program sequences with minor modifications. The article offers the Ozsvath metric, an homogeneous solution of the vacuum Einstein equations with cosmological term, as an exercise, warning that special care must be taken with the changing of the metric signature and the nondiagonal metric terms. This turns solution-derivation into an extendable student activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in the computer room with the REDUCE+EXCALC program sequences already presented for the Schwarzschild and de Sitter/anti-de Sitter cases.
#### Constraints
- For the Ozsvath metric, special care must be taken to the changing of the signature of the metric and to the nondiagonal terms of the metric.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in a general relativity course

### Target Learning Goals
- verifying that given metrics satisfy the Einstein equations
- gaining facility with computer algebra in general relativity
