# Introduce Long-menu questions as part of a mix of item formats to assess clinical reasoning more realistically

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend introducing Long-menu questions within a mixed-format exam, arguing that a variety of formats increases validity and that Long-menu items better approximate real patient management. They write that "the introduction of Long-menu questions could help assess clinical reasoning in a more realistic approach to patient management than other item formats inherited from paper based examination". Implementation requires building tailored answer lists and CBA practice sessions for students before the first summative assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tailored list of possible answers customized to the key element tested
- CBA exercise sessions prior to the first summative assessment so students learn the format's logic
#### Constraints
- Confirmatory studies are needed; the supporting evidence comes from a retrospective single-institution study at a single curricular level

### Target Learners
- Medical students in early clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and decision-making under conditions resembling real patient management
