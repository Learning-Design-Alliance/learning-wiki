# Develop adult nursing VR simulation programs by first analyzing clinical practice limitations and then building in students' enumerated needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that developers of an adult nursing VR-based simulation training program start from a clear analysis of conventional clinical practice's limits and of user needs, then design the program to reflect them. Students' enumerated needs include "an array of scenarios and skills; difficulty-specific learning scenarios; immediate feedback (e.g., like those in computerized games)", emergency and rare-case simulation, and patient–nurse communication practice. The authors also call for clinical specialists to devise scenarios and collaboration between clinical specialists and IT experts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of users' needs (nursing students) prior to development
- Active involvement of clinical specialists to devise scenarios
- Collaboration between clinical specialists and IT experts for technical accuracy
#### Constraints
- A cited study found VR programs can have physical side effects such as cybersickness, associated with use duration, visual design, and students' movements
- E-learning may restrict direct relationship-building, with one cited study reporting it may halt improvement of communication skills

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in adult nursing with clinical practice experience

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical nursing skills practice
- patient–nurse communication
- emergency response
- theory–practice integration
