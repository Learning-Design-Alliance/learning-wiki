Implement out-of-class English journal writing with weekly encouraging teacher feedback and two rotating notebooks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Students keep English journals outside class about topics of personal relevance, submitting one notebook while continuing to write in the other, and receive weekly elaborate and encouraging comments rather than error-focused correction. The article reports this as fostering fluency, accuracy, motivation, and rapport: "the students’ journal entries increased in length and the number of interesting ideas, but decreased in the number of errors". Feedback should concentrate on errors with global rather than local effects and avoid covering too many repairs at once.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students possess two notebooks so they can keep writing while the teacher reads and responds to the other
  • Teacher reads entries and returns them weekly with elaborate and encouraging comments and advice

Constraints¶

  • Feedback should concentrate on errors with global rather than local effects and not attempt to cover too many repairs
  • Students needed about three weeks to get accustomed to the activity; some never took to it

Target Learners¶

  • second-year EFL university students with extensive writing experience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English writing fluency
  • English writing accuracy
  • writing motivation

Claims¶

  • Journal writing with weekly teacher feedback fostered cooperative learning atmosphere and student-teacher rapport [+W]
  • Journal writing generated positive attitudes and intrinsic motivation for writing among EFL students [+W]
  • Thirteen weeks of journal writing reduced EFL students' writing errors more than regular writing instruction alone [+W]
  • Thirteen weeks of journal writing improved EFL students' writing fluency more than regular writing instruction alone [+W]
  • Journal writing raised EFL students' average writing scores more than regular writing instruction alone [+W]
