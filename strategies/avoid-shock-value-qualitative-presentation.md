# Critique of shock-value qualitative research presentation and recommendation to consider impact on diverse audiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper critiques qualitative research in PER that relies on shock-value and claims of -isms, presenting the worst things people say about marginalized students in what the authors call a "trauma-porn fashion" without focusing on dismantling structures. The authors find repeated presentation of such information unproductive and troubling, and ask researchers to think deeply about how presentation of results might impact a diverse audience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers must consider the emotional impact on audience members from marginalized groups before presenting participant responses containing racist, sexist, or homophobic remarks
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge nervous laughter is a natural human response for some people caught off guard, but ask for community awareness of colleagues who find such comments emotionally unsettling

### Target Learners
- PER researchers presenting qualitative research
- conference audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- ethical presentation of qualitative research
- supporting marginalized community members in academic settings
