# Prioritise course design elements (fieldwork, up-to-date resources, clear aims, syllabus adherence) when seeking to raise student satisfaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' own conclusion draws an actionable implication from the finding that course-related predictors fill the top-5 importance positions: students "were satisfied in all ﬁve aspects of their course if it allowed ﬁeldwork, applications, laboratory, discussion". They also highlight "a notable inﬂuence of up to date course resources and textbooks", the quality of class discussions, homework and assignments, teaching according to the announced syllabus, and clearly stated course aims and objectives. Only after these course considerations did instructor-related behaviours (commitment, relevant assignments, guidance and encouragement) appear. The strategy is to attend to these course-level design elements first when seeking to raise satisfaction.
