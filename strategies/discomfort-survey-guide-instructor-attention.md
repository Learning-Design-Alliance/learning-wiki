# Use weekly discomfort surveys to guide instructors toward topical areas students feel deserve attention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend monitoring student self-reported discomfort weekly so instructors and researchers can identify which topical areas students consider challenging at different points in the course. They state: "We believe our results can guide instructors and researchers towards topical areas that students may feel deserve attention." Discomfort is not treated as inherently bad; shifts in coded categories reveal what is top-of-mind for students, and increased physics discomfort during quantum computing and entanglement may even argue for teaching those topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A recurring low-stakes survey with both a numeric discomfort rating and a written explanation, so sources of discomfort can be coded and tracked by topic.
#### Constraints
- The article notes discomfort with quantum mechanics is common among physicists and could suggest an expert-like understanding, so discomfort alone should not be read as a defect.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- instructor awareness of which course topics students find challenging week by week
