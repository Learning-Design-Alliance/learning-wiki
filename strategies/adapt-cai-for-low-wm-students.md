# Adapt CAI conditions of use for students with below-average working memory capacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because CAI brought no benefit for students with below-average WM capacity, the article recommends adapting its conditions of use for these students, who are more likely to exhibit attentional problems. Suggested implementations include shortening or sequencing CAI sessions (e.g., 15-min sessions, 3 times per day) to relieve attention and memory load, considering the split attention effect by synchronizing audio and visual sources, and decreasing the number of elements presented on screen according to individual capacity. The article notes CAI is highly flexible, individualized and accessible outside school, making such adaptations feasible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of each student's WM capacity when configuring the CAI environment
#### Constraints
- The article cautions the achievement gap between low and high WM students may still increase even with more WM-adaptive CAI technology

### Target Learners
- middle-school students with below-average working memory capacity

### Target Learning Goals
- Science and Technology learning; preventing school failure
