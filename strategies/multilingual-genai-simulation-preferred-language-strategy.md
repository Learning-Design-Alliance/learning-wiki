# Deliver multilingual GenAI simulation training in the trainee's preferred language to standardize training across linguistic contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends leveraging the linguistic flexibility of large language models so that the entire training interaction, including feedback, is conducted in the user's preferred language. In the pilot, the core prompt instructed the system to inquire about the user's preferred language at the start of the session, allowing 27 psychiatrists from 20 countries to train simultaneously in a standardized yet locally adapted format. The authors frame this as challenging the traditional Anglo-centric model of medical education and as a step toward democratizing high-quality psychiatric training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A base model with inherent multilingual linguistic flexibility
- A core prompt instructing the system to ask for and conduct the whole session in the preferred language
#### Constraints
- The pilot established feasibility of linguistic adaptation, not deep cultural adaptation; subtle cultural nuances in expressing distress may not be fully captured
- No formal linguistic and cultural validation across all 20 languages was performed

### Target Learners
- early-career psychiatrists in international cohorts
- trainees in non-English-speaking countries

### Target Learning Goals
- suicide risk assessment training
- standardized clinical skills training across language barriers
