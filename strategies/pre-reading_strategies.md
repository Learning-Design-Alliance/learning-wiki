# Pre-Reading Strategies

## Description
Pre-reading strategies are structured activities completed before learners engage with a text: activating relevant prior knowledge, previewing text structure, pre-teaching critical vocabulary, and establishing a purpose for reading. They prepare the reader's knowledge structures so that incoming text can be integrated rather than processed word-by-word.

## Design Implications

Pre-reading activities work primarily by supplying or activating the schema that comprehension depends on — readers with relevant background knowledge understand and remember far more of the same text than readers without it [Activation of prior knowledge improves comprehension and recall.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+S]. Advance organizers — brief, abstract previews of how the material is organized — reliably improve learning when they map the structure of the upcoming text [Advance organizers improve learning outcomes.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. The purpose-setting function matters equally: telling readers what to look for directs attention and improves recall of targeted content, though at some cost to untargeted content [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of what the text demands: key concepts, unfamiliar vocabulary, and assumed background
- A brief activity (typically 5–10 minutes) that surfaces what learners already know, including misconceptions
- A clear purpose or guiding question that frames the reading ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), anticipation guides)

#### Constraints
- Activation of the *wrong* schema harms comprehension — a misleading preview or an anecdote that evokes irrelevant knowledge produces worse recall than no preview [~S]
- Pre-teaching too much vocabulary overloads learners; only high-utility, text-critical terms should be front-loaded, with the rest handled during reading [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Purpose-setting questions narrow attention: readers recall prompted content well but unprompted content poorly [~M]
- Organizers that add interesting-but-irrelevant detail reduce learning rather than helping [Irrelevant seductive details hurt learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Anticipation guides** — learners agree/disagree with claims about the text before reading, then revisit after; creates [Cognitive Disequilibrium](../principles/cognitive-disequilibrium.md) that motivates reading to resolve
- **Pre-questioning** — a question posed before reading directs attention to key content
- **Vocabulary pre-teaching** — 3–5 critical terms with student-friendly definitions and examples
- **Text preview / structural walk-through** — examining headings, figures, and layout to build a structural map ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) the text into navigable parts)
- **Analogy priming** — introducing a familiar analog before reading so the text maps onto it [Analogical reasoning improves transfer.](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Readers with low prior knowledge of the topic, who benefit most from background building [Activation of prior knowledge improves comprehension and recall.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers and multilingual learners, for whom vocabulary pre-teaching removes a primary comprehension barrier
- Less necessary for expert readers with strong domain knowledge, who generate their own previews and purposes [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension of informational and expository text
- Integration of new content with existing knowledge structures
- Vocabulary acquisition in context
- Metacognitive reading habits: previewing, purpose-setting, predicting

### Instructions
1. Surface prior knowledge and misconceptions with a quick prompt or anticipation guide ([Activation](../elements/activation.md); see [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md))
2. Pre-teach 3–5 text-critical vocabulary terms with examples, not definitions alone
3. Present an advance organizer that maps the text's structure ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
4. Set a purpose: a guiding question or task learners must answer from the text
5. During reading, have learners mark evidence and revisit predictions ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
6. After reading, close the loop: revisit the anticipation guide or question to consolidate and correct

## Examples
- **Anticipation guides** in science classes: students agree/disagree with claims like "heavier objects fall faster" before a physics text, then revise after reading — misconceptions are confronted rather than silently reinforced.
- **[Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org)** — publishes practitioner-tested pre-reading routines (previewing, KWL charts, vocabulary pre-teaching) grounded in comprehension research.
- **KWL charts** — learners record what they *Know*, what they *Want* to know, and later what they *Learned*; the K and W columns are the pre-reading phase.
- **Newsela** — leveled versions of the same article let teachers pre-teach core concepts once and have all students read at accessible levels.
