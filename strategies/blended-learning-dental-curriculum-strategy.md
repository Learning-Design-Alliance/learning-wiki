# Incorporate blended learning into the dental curriculum by combining the best features of classroom and distance learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, after the pandemic shift to full distance learning, dental educators integrate classroom and distance learning rather than rely on either alone. It states that "dental educators need to incorporate blended learning in the curriculum, to design the best features of classroom and distance learning to improve the overall learning environment." The survey supports this: students preferred classroom interaction for group discussion yet acknowledged distance learning's efficiency and time advantages, and the majority agreed blended learning could be implemented henceforth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Synchronous online sessions with a facilitator per group to preserve discussion format
- Reliable internet access and affordable connectivity for students
#### Constraints
- Psychomotor and laboratory skills sessions could not be replaced by distance learning during the pandemic
- Generalizability limited to a single university and preclinical students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative and problem-based learning
- self-directed learning
- preclinical dental science knowledge
