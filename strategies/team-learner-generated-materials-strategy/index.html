Use team-produced learner-generated materials built with user-friendly authoring tools as the in-class inquiry activity in a flipped language class¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implemented strategy: learners in teams study topics in phonetics and phonology by creating learning materials with authoring tools, so that the flipped class time is filled with "research, active learning and higher-order thinking skills". The strategy addresses the flipped model's problem of finding appropriate in-class activities and was evaluated positively by learners in survey and interview data.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • User-friendly authoring tools that learners can use to build learning materials
  • A flipped course structure with topics for learners to research and materials to produce in teams

Constraints¶

  • The radical changes introduced by the flipped model, new software, and a thinking-production-research lesson sequence may challenge students accustomed to traditional methods

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate Turkish learners of English in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • phonetics and phonology knowledge
  • research and inquiry skills
  • higher-order thinking skills

Claims¶

  • Radical changes from the flipped model, new software, and a research-and-production lesson sequence constitute a major challenge for students accustomed to traditional methods [-W]
  • Learner-generated materials produced with user-friendly authoring tools seem to be a good option for learners to get involved in research in a flipped class [+W]
  • Higher and lower Moodle scorers viewed flipped learning as almost equally valuable, but higher scorers talked more positively about how the course was delivered [+W]
  • Learners reported mostly positive attitudes toward flipped learning in the learner-generated-materials pronunciation course [+W]
