# Report both raw iCPR metrics and quality indices, and account for natural variation, when evaluating individual CPR skill performance over time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy directs resuscitation researchers and training designers to report raw chest compression metrics alongside guideline-based quality indices and to interpret changes against minimal detectable change values. The article concludes that "future studies should consider investigating both metrics and quality indices when reporting individual iCPR skills performance, in order to differentiate between natural variation and skill development or decay".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliability data such as MDC thresholds must be established for the metrics being monitored, so changes can be judged against natural variation
#### Constraints
- Previous studies converting metrics into quality indices without acknowledging natural variation may have reached erroneous conclusions, per the article

### Target Learners
- healthcare students learning infant basic life support
- BLS rescuers with no duty to respond to paediatric cardiac arrest

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition, retention and decay of infant CPR psychomotor skills

## Claims

- [Between-day changes greater than 3% (depth), 7% (rate), 21% (duty cycle) and 22% (leaning) of initial values represent true performance change, i.e. skill decay or improvement](../claims/icpr-between-day-mdc-true-change-thresholds.md) [+W]
- [Between-day iCPR reliability one week after training remains good to excellent, with compression depth the highest ICC (.92) and duty cycle the lowest (.75)](../claims/icpr-between-day-reliability-good-excellent.md) [+W]
- [Reliability is lower and variability higher once iCPR metrics are converted into guideline-based quality indices, with between-day rate-QI ICC falling to .52 and MDC reaching 101% of the mean](../claims/icpr-quality-indices-lower-reliability.md) [+W]
- [Because iCPR performance is highly repeatable over a week, performance changes after training can be attributed to true skill decay rather than inconsistent performance](../claims/icpr-skill-decay-not-natural-variation.md) [+W]
- [Within-day minimal detectable change is low for compression depth (6%) and rate (9%) but higher for duty cycle (15%) and residual leaning (22%)](../claims/icpr-within-day-mdc-metric-differences.md) [+W]
- [Simulated infant CPR performance shows a high degree of within-day reliability, with excellent ICCs for chest compression rate, depth, residual leaning and duty cycle](../claims/icpr-within-day-reliability-excellent.md) [+W]
