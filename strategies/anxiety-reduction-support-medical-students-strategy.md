# Anxiety-reduction support strategy for medical students, with special attention to females

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical schools address anxiety as an academic-support intervention, because its data show anxiety hinders performance more for females. Suggested measures include LASSI-based identification of struggling students, administrator check-ins about mental health, and anxiety-reduction practices. As the article states, "Medical schools can promote and encourage anxiety-reduction strategies for their students". Routine follow-up is advised to measure whether interventions succeed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early identification of students experiencing increased anxiety, via LASSI results or administrator conversations
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a single-institution correlational study; the article notes analyzing data from multiple institutions would strengthen the association

### Target Learners
- medical students in preclinical years
- female medical students with low LASSI Anxiety scores

### Target Learning Goals
- improved preclinical course performance
- improved USMLE Step 1 performance
- reduced test anxiety
