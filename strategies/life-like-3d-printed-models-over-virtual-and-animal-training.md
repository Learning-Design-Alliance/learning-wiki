# Use life-like 3D-printed organ models rather than virtual simulators or animal organs to train less experienced surgeons in minimally invasive procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend replacing wet lab animal models and virtual simulators with life-like 3D-printed replicas for surgical skills training, because animal use is restricted by welfare concerns and porcine and human hearts differ in anatomy and texture. They write that "this simulator can help in training younger and less experienced surgeons to learn and practice MICS procedures with life-like models rather than virtual simulators and animal organs."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A printed model with verified anatomical accuracy and tissue-matched material properties, plus access to real surgical instruments for simulation
#### Constraints
- The Clinical Perspective notes it remains to be seen whether this technology will enhance training sufficiently to justify the cost, and further studies are needed to evaluate its value

### Target Learners
- younger, less experienced surgeons
- surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- safe practice of minimally invasive cardiac surgery procedures before operating on patients
