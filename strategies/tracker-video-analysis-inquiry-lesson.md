# 60-minute inquiry lesson using video analysis and modeling with Tracker on real-life motion examples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable lesson recipe for upper secondary to JC physics on motion, in which student-groups conduct a scientific investigation using sports-science examples (long jump, billiard balls, butterfly swimming stroke, basketball throw, rotating fan). Teachers "introduce students to the pedagogy of video analysis and modeling using tracker if the motion is kinematics in nature". Students pose questions, plan and conduct the investigation, analyse and evaluate results, and communicate findings, with peer feedback and teacher mentoring throughout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording device, computers with Internet connection, a video sharing platform, and close mentoring, since the chapter notes "Close mentoring is required to guide student-groups through the entire process of investigation"
#### Constraints
- The video-analysis-and-modeling pedagogy is recommended only when the motion is kinematics in nature

### Target Learners
- Upper secondary to C1 physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Explaining the physics involved in a given activity using video analysis
- Scientific investigation skills: questioning, planning, conducting, analysing, communicating
