# Ask students to solve derivation problems individually immediately after the peer clicker sequence, announced in advance as a quiz, to consolidate learning and build self-reliance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors plan, in future implementations, to have students respond to the two-dimensional derivation question individually without support immediately after engaging with the three-dimensional clicker questions, having told them beforehand that they will quiz the derivations. They write that "immediate individual reflection after engaging with the CQS with their peers may be helpful in improving individual accountability and focus, and help students consolidate the concepts learned". This responds to the finding that many interviewed students needed interviewer guidance to do the calculations on the posttest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be told right before engaging with the CQS that they will have to do the derivations immediately after the CQS as a quiz.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a planned future implementation, not a tested intervention.

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students in a quantum mechanics course

### Target Learning Goals
- derive expressions for the Fermi energy, density of states and total electronic energy of a free electron gas without scaffolding support

## Claims

- [Upper-level undergraduates' scores on deriving the density of states and total electronic energy rose from below 10% after traditional instruction to approximately 50% after the CQS, in one single-group pre-post study with no comparison group](../claims/cqs-improves-derivation-scores-free-electron-gas.md) [+M]
