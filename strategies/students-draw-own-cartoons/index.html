Have students draw their own cartoons to express concerns and consolidate understanding of an issue¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Beyond reading cartoons, the article recommends student cartoon production as an activity. During the experiment, "the experimental group students were asked to draw their own cartoons about water issue" and the best cartoon was awarded. The authors report that students' drawings reflected their views of local water behavior and drove them to suggest solutions, which they link to literature on cartoons developing imagination and creativity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prior lessons on cartoon reading and drawing (two lessons in this study)

Constraints¶

  • Observed within the experimental group only; no comparison of drawing versus non-drawing conditions

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 4 students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Expressing concerns about an issue creatively
  • Applying understanding to the local environment

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement concept cartoons© by following the inquiry working procedure, praising all views, supporting discussion, and letting students practically verify their own assumptions
  • Allow learners to place their own characters into visual story tools to increase purpose and utility
  • Concept cartoon discussion routine: present, debate viewpoints, then student research without teacher explanation
  • Recommendations for future PBL and concept cartoon research and materials
