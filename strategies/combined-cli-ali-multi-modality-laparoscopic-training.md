# Combine articulating and conventional laparoscopic instrument training in a multi-modality curriculum for novices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that motorized articulating instruments be used for laparoscopic training purposes because novices valued their additional degrees of freedom despite slower performance. It states that "laparoscopic training with CLI and ALI should ideally be combined in a new multi-modality training curriculum to ensure an optimal training benefit with both types of instruments", citing multi-modality training for laparoscopic cholecystectomy as one approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instrument features such as ergonomics and weight would need improvement before ALI training use, per the article
#### Constraints
- ALI are not designed to replace CLI but to be an add-on to refine laparoscopic capabilities
- ALI appear to have their own distinct learning curve requiring skills distinct from conventional laparoscopy

### Target Learners
- laparoscopic novices
- medical students and residents without prior laparoscopic experience

### Target Learning Goals
- basic laparoscopic skills such as peg transfer, cutting, needle guidance, and suturing
