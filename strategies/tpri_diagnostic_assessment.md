# TPRI Diagnostic Assessment

## Description
The TPRI is an individually administered screening and diagnostic inventory of early reading development, used in kindergarten through grade 3. It consists of a brief Screening section identifying students at risk, followed by an Inventory section that assesses specific domains — phonological awareness, graphophonemic knowledge, word reading, fluency, and comprehension — so instruction can be targeted to each child's profile. Teachers administer it at the beginning, middle, and end of the year, using results to group students and select interventions.

## Design Implications
The TPRI operationalizes [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): its purpose is not grading but locating each learner on a developmental trajectory so instruction can be adjusted. Early screening matters because reading difficulties compound — students who fall behind in phonemic awareness and decoding tend to fall further behind without targeted intervention [Early identification and intensive intervention prevent entrenched reading failure.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Diagnostic profiles are actionable only when teachers translate them into differentiated instruction; assessment data without instructional follow-through yields little benefit [Foorman & Moats, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1177/07419325040250050401) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained one-on-one administration; fidelity depends on the teacher following standardized protocols and scoring rules
- A decision framework linking specific inventory results (e.g., failure on blending tasks) to specific instructional responses ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) support this translation)
- Time allocated for administration (roughly 10–15 minutes per child per wave) and for regrouping students afterward

#### Constraints
- One-on-one administration is labor-intensive; under time pressure, teachers may rush administration or skip the Inventory for screened-on students, losing diagnostic detail [-W]
- A screening score alone does not identify *why* a student struggles; interpreting results requires knowledge of reading development that not all teachers have [~M]
- Three assessment waves per year can reduce instructional time and produce testing fatigue in the youngest learners [-W]
- Results reflect a single administration moment; young children's performance varies with attention and fatigue, so a single low score should trigger monitoring, not immediate placement [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Screening-only use (identifying at-risk students) versus full inventory use (informing daily grouping)
- State-mandated implementation with prescribed intervention menus versus school-level discretionary use
- Paper-and-pencil administration versus the TPRI's electronic support tools for scoring and data management

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten through grade 3 students, particularly those at risk of reading difficulty [Early identification and intensive intervention prevent entrenched reading failure.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers whose profile of strengths and weaknesses can guide remediation; responsiveness to early intervention distinguishes readily remediated from difficult-to-remediate poor readers [Vellutino et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.88.4.601) [+S]
- Less informative for fluent readers beyond grade-level benchmarks, for whom the inventory adds little beyond confirming mastery

### Target Learning Goals
- Phonological and phonemic awareness
- Decoding and word recognition ([Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S])
- Reading fluency and listening/reading comprehension

### Instructions
1. Administer the Screening section one-on-one at the beginning of the year to identify students at risk on grade-appropriate tasks.
2. For students who do not screen on, administer the relevant Inventory sections ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)) covering phonological awareness, graphophonemic knowledge, and reading accuracy and comprehension.
3. Score against developmental guidelines and build a profile of each student's specific skill gaps rather than a single aggregate score.
4. Translate profiles into differentiated small-group instruction and targeted intervention; feedback to students should address the task and process level, not the child globally [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. Re-administer at middle and end of year; adjust grouping based on growth, and intensify support for non-responders ([Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) and [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) as needed).

## Examples
- Texas public schools have used the TPRI as a K–3 reading instrument under the Texas Reading Initiative, with results driving placement in intensive, research-based reading interventions [Foorman et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.90.4.690).
- A kindergarten teacher screens in fall; a student fails the blending and rhyming tasks, so the teacher forms a small phonemic-awareness group meeting daily for 15 minutes and re-checks at the mid-year wave.
