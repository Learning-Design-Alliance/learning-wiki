# Operationalize AI-supported SRL support through the detect–diagnose–act cycle, with the act stage developed as a pedagogical theory of support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper endorses Molenaar's (2022) detect–diagnose–act framework for using AI in instructional design: to detect current states, to diagnose what detected data mean with respect to conceptual criteria, and to act based on the diagnosis. It observes that current studies employ AI primarily for the detect and diagnose stages, while "the act stage remains conceptually and technically underdeveloped." The strategy calls for AI systems that intervene meaningfully — prompting reflection, suggesting strategies, or adapting task parameters — grounded in a robust pedagogical theory of support and human–machine collaboration with teacher agency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A robust pedagogical theory of support, plus pedagogical competencies for teachers to interpret multimodal diagnostic data and translate it into responsive action.
#### Constraints
- The paper states such a pedagogical theory of support is currently lacking, and most interventions remain generic rather than individualized.

### Target Learners
- Teachers and learners in multimodal, technology-enhanced learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- Translating multimodal SRL diagnostics into timely, individualized pedagogical support for self-regulation
