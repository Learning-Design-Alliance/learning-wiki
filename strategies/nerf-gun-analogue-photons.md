# Use toy Nerf gun projectiles as analogue photons to introduce the particle nature of light

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Einstein-First approach represents photons physically with small foam projectiles fired from toy guns, using a stream of bullets to represent a beam of light. As the article states, "To represent photons physically, we use small foam projectiles fired from toy guns called Nerf guns." Through this analogy students explore photography, light scattering, the uncertainty principle and the photoelectric effect, and learn that humans can sense the arrival of single photons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nerf guns with foam bullets (blunt or suction caps), whiteboards, and safety glasses as listed in the appendix
#### Constraints
- The model breaks down because the bullets have non-zero rest mass, cannot travel at light speed, and cannot produce interference patterns

### Target Learners
- school students in early years of science education

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the particle nature of light and photon properties including energy and momentum
