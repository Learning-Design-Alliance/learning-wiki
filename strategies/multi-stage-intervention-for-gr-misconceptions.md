# Use a multi-stage intervention rather than a one-off session to dispel gravitational-force misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the one-off 50-minute intervention failed to shift most students' descriptions of gravity away from force-based accounts, the authors recommend that "a multi-stage intervention may prove more beneficial for this". The article also notes that longer syllabus programmes allow more content, self-paced learning and deeper learning experiences, while one-off interventions still significantly impact engagement and future career choices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple sessions building progressively on curvature concepts rather than a single workshop
#### Constraints
- Based on a single school, selective grammar setting; the one-off format's limited effect on the source-of-force concept is the stated motivation

### Target Learners
- secondary school students across key stages

### Target Learning Goals
- replace force-based gravity misconceptions with spacetime-curvature understanding
