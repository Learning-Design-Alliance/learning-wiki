# Complex engineering problem mapping questions (P1-P7) in the project report to evidence Washington Accord attributes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
To make complex engineering problem attributes explicit and assessable, students answer mapping questions in the discussion section of their IEEE-template project report, covering depth of knowledge, conflicting requirements, alternate solutions, impact, ethics, stakeholder requirements and interdependence, then tick the applicable attributes in a mapping table with rationale and evidence. The article states students "had to answer complex engineering problem mapping questions where they would explain if they gained a depth of knowledge ( P1), if they faced conflicting requirements ( P2), if there was any better alternate solution or design other than the one they have implemented (P3)" and the remaining attributes through P7. This operationalises the Washington Accord definition of complex engineering problems within course assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must provide rationale and evidence for each ticked attribute in the Complex Engineering Problem Mapping table
- Project topics must be checked against the criteria before selection so that attributes such as P2 and P7 are typically satisfied by design
#### Constraints
- The mapping relies on students' self-report in the report's discussion section rather than independent assessment of each attribute

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate engineering students completing capstone-style lab projects

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflecting on depth of engineering knowledge applied
- Recognising conflicting requirements, stakeholder needs and system interdependence in their own designs
- Ethical and societal reasoning about engineering solutions
