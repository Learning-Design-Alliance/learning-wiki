# In-classroom teacher professional development in astronomy subject knowledge drives far larger telescope usage than traditional recruitment models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Bradford Robotic Telescope project provides professional development for a teacher in his or her own classroom, targeting astronomy subject knowledge because poor teacher perceptions of science are seen as discouraging students. The review reports that "This has resulted in a far larger usage of the telescope than other more traditional models in terms of number of students involved, projects undertaken, and activity on the website.", with in-classroom PD provided for 200 to 300 teachers per year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher PD about authentic inquiry-based science education "takes a large amount of time, eﬀort and resources", so sustained funding and staffing are needed.
#### Constraints
- The review notes teacher professional learning can have flow-on benefits for future students and a multiplicative impact, but the usage comparison is descriptive rather than a controlled evaluation.

### Target Learners
- high school science teachers
- high school students (School Years 9-12)

### Target Learning Goals
- teacher capability to support authentic research in the classroom
