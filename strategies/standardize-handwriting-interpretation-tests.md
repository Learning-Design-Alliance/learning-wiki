# Standardize handwriting-interpretation tests before using them as measures of form- or position-perception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
From their low and unstable correlations, the authors recommend that tests such as mirror-reading, illegible-writing reading, and handwriting pairing need standardization before serving as measures of spatial ability. They write that 'Low coefficients of reliability indicate, however, the need of standardization of such tests', and specify concrete fixes: specimens of the same length, length estimated in letters not words, and presenting letters singly or in small groups as Starch did when measuring writing legibility.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specimens of equal length measured in letters, and consideration of presenting letters singly or in small groups for form-recognition testing
#### Constraints
- The article reports that with its current unstandardized specimens, correlations between the tests were too low to support firm conclusions, and no conclusion about form habituation could be drawn from its difference-score correlations

### Target Learners
- adult university students

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring form-perception and position-shift interpretation
