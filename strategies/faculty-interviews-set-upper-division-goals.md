# Use faculty interviews to set learning goals and tailor content to the actual student population in upper-division courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The reformers interviewed seven physics faculty about the most important concepts and met with engineering professors asking "What do your students need to know about modern physics?" The engineering consensus prioritized applications of quantum mechanics (electron devices, lasers, STMs, MRIs), the quantum origin of molecular bonding and material structure, and experience solving differential equations, while de-emphasizing special relativity and abstract formalism. This process addressed the lack of consensus about content in advanced courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to faculty in both the teaching discipline and the students' home discipline
#### Constraints
- Unlike introductory physics, there is no general consensus about what should be taught in more advanced classes

### Target Learners
- engineering majors in upper-division physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- content relevance to students' professional field
- applications of quantum mechanics
