Strategies

Use predict-then-simulate tasks in which learners forecast outcomes and compare them against scientific model results¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q4 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In Activities 5 and the lake-level simulation, users predict how long it will take for lake levels to decline to 1,000 feet above sea level and which of four conservation options will save the most water; the simulation then runs and users earn points based on how closely their predictions match results from hydrologic modeling using Hadley Center 2-A1B data. The authors note that making predictions followed by simulation "promotes active cognitive processing and conceptual change (through cognitive conflict) when there is a discrepancy between predicted and actual results."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulation outputs based on scientific models (Hadley Center 2-A1B data) against which predictions are compared
  • Points awarded for closeness of predictions to simulated results

Constraints¶

  • The authors caution the simulation results are illustrative for pedagogical purposes only and used a fictional lake so results would not be quoted out of context

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding how scientific models are used
  • applying water-flow and climate concepts to prediction
  • conceptual change through cognitive conflict

Claims¶

  • Playing Losing the Lake significantly increased seventh graders' objective-test content knowledge relative to a control group viewing an earth science website [+M]
  • The game produced large gains on the constructed-response household conservation item but no significant effect on the Las Vegas water-conservation importance item [+M]
  • Playing the game produced a small but significant increase in student interest, driven by items about Las Vegas water sources and home water conservation [+W]
  • Knowledge gains from the game were largest on water-flow, weather-vs-climate, greenhouse-effect, and household-conservation items, but three misconceptions were not effectively addressed [+W]
