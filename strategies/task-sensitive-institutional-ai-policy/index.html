Design task-sensitive institutional AI policies rather than blanket permitted/prohibited distinctions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article argues that because faculty and administrative staff use AI while also holding stronger integrity concerns and norm endorsement, policy "requires a task-sensitive design, with different rules for idea generation, text editing, material preparation, assessment, fully AI-generated answers, and disclosure of AI use". It recommends that university policy address practices that have already developed rather than rest on assumptions about future adoption, and that stakeholder-aware policies address academic integrity requirements and transparent disclosure.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Policy must differentiate rules by task type, including assessment and disclosure of AI use

Constraints¶

  • The article states the cross-sectional data do not demonstrate causal effects of policy on trust

Target Learners¶

  • university students
  • faculty
  • administrative staff

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible and institutionally appropriate AI use in learning, teaching, and administration

Related Strategies¶

  • Designate permitted AI use per assignment using color-coded levels (red, yellow, green, blue)
  • Institutions should respond to generative AI in education with guided critical engagement rather than prohibition
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Prepare teachers to use AI expeditiously and provide AI-text detectors for evaluation, alongside updated academic-integrity rules
