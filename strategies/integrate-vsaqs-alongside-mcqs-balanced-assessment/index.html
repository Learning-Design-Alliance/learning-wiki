Integrate VSAQs for higher-order cognitive assessment while retaining MCQs for foundational knowledge testing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends a balanced assessment strategy: "Evidence from this review supports the integration of VSAQs for higher-order cognitive assessment while retaining MCQs for foundational knowledge testing, achieving a balanced evaluation strategy". This leverages VSAQs' strong discrimination and reduced cueing while preserving MCQ efficiency for broad knowledge coverage. The authors add that faculty development in writing high-quality VSAQs and investment in automated marking systems are needed to ensure feasibility and consistency.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • faculty training and standard-setting to maintain scoring consistency and fairness
  • investment in automated marking systems to ensure feasibility and consistency in large cohorts

Constraints¶

  • acceptability among students and educators and curricular integration remain ongoing concerns

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate and postgraduate health professions students in medicine, nursing, dentistry, and allied health

Target Learning Goals¶

  • higher-order cognitive assessment and clinical reasoning alongside foundational knowledge testing

Claims¶

  • VSAQ assessments maintain acceptable internal consistency (reliability) [+W]
  • Initial pooled analysis shows no statistically significant difference between VSAQ and MCQ scores but with extreme heterogeneity [~W]
  • Evidence on practice effects of VSAQs versus MCQs is mixed, with no consistent advantage for either format [~W]
  • After excluding the Dhok et al. (2023) outlier, students score significantly lower on VSAQs than MCQs [-W]
  • VSAQ assessments demonstrate strong discriminatory power between high- and low-performing students [+W]
  • Short-answer (production) and multiple-choice (recognition) retrieval practice produce no statistically significant difference in undergraduates' reading comprehension on a final MCQ test [~W]
