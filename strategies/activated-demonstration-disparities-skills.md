# Use activated demonstration at the bedside to build skills for disparity-sensitive care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For skill gaps, the article recommends bedside teaching, demonstration, and activated demonstration. In an activated demonstration, "the teacher asks the learner to observe the teacher perform a specific task" such as eliciting a patient's concerns about surgery, and afterward "activates" the learner "by asking him to describe and discuss his observations." Bedside rounds enable role-modeling; the teacher assigns roles to team members and debriefs the encounter, with the patient's involvement or privately.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diagnosed skill gap and a clinical encounter suitable for observation; the teacher previews goals and assigns roles before the bedside session
#### Constraints
- Suited to time-limited settings where a brief focused observation is feasible; the article presents it as one option among several (guided practice, reading assignments)

### Target Learners
- Residents
- Medical students
- Clinical teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Skills such as eliciting patient concerns and using the teach-back method with patients who have low health literacy
