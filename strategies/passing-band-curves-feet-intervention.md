# Discuss the soft 'feet' of filters' passing-band curves as an essential teacher intervention when teaching partial absorption

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is an explicit teacher-led discussion of the passing bands of various filters, focusing on the soft edges ('feet') of the curves and their multiplicative meaning. The article found that only these curve features reconciled the common presence/absence rules, the observed laser impacts, and a 'more or less' approach to absorption. It recommends preserving this intervention if the interviews support an Inquiry Based Science Education program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must present the passing-band curves and accompanying comments, since the article states that without such documents the surprise of the observations would not have been a decisive factor.
#### Constraints
- As with all teaching interviews, the article notes it is difficult to control all the components of conceptual input on behalf of the interviewer.

### Target Learners
- third-year university prospective physics and chemistry teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding partial absorption in colour phenomena
- moving from all-or-nothing to quantitative 'more or less' reasoning about light

## Claims

- [After testing predictions, all interviewed students distinguished two pigment groups by the brightness of the laser impact, evidencing 'more or less' absorption](../claims/brightness-two-pigment-groups-more-or-less-absorption.md) [+W]
