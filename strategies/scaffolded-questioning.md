# Scaffolded Questioning

## Description
Scaffolded questioning is the deliberate sequencing and adaptive adjustment of questions to move learners from what they can do independently toward reasoning they could not yet produce alone. The instructor begins with questions within reach (recall, observation), then steps up to prompts for explanation, justification, and transfer, adjusting support in real time based on learner responses — offering hints, simplifying the question, or handing back responsibility as competence grows.

## Design Implications

Scaffolded questioning operationalizes [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) through dialogue: the question sequence provides temporary structure that is withdrawn as learners take over the reasoning themselves [Contingent, responsive scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends on contingency — the next question must respond to what the learner just said, not follow a fixed script [Contingent, responsive scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Questions that require learners to explain and justify their reasoning produce deeper learning than questions answerable by recall alone, because explanation forces integration with prior knowledge [Self-explanation prompts improve learning.](../claims/self-explanation-prompts-improve-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor knowledge of the target reasoning path and common learner misconceptions
- A planned question ladder (recall → explanation → justification → application) with flexibility to deviate
- Adequate wait time after each question; learners need time to formulate answers
- A mechanism for responding to wrong or partial answers (hint, rephrase, counterexample) rather than supplying the answer

#### Constraints
- Rapid-fire questioning without wait time degrades into recall drill and increases anxiety, suppressing the reasoning the strategy is meant to elicit [-M]
- Overly frequent questions can fragment attention and add extraneous load for novices still building basic schemas [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [~M]
- If questions are answered *for* the learner after brief silence, the scaffolding never fades and dependence increases [-M]
- Less effective when learners lack the prior knowledge the questions are meant to activate; front-load with [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md) or [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) before questioning begins

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reciprocal questioning**: learners are trained to generate and ask each other questions (King's guided peer-questioning), shifting the scaffolding role to peers
- **Written/embedded prompts**: the same ladder delivered as prompts in materials or software rather than live dialogue
- **Harkness/Socratic seminar variants**: whole-group questioning where the instructor orchestrates rather than directs
- **Cold call with support**: naming a learner *then* offering think time or a partner rehearsal, combining accountability with scaffolding

### Target Learners
- Novices and intermediate learners who can engage with content but cannot yet structure their own reasoning [Contingent, responsive scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners with gaps in prior knowledge, when questions begin at the level they *can* answer and build upward [Activation of prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for experts, for whom guided questioning adds little beyond what they already do spontaneously [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: articulating relationships, causes, and mechanisms
- Metacognitive development: learning to monitor and justify one's own reasoning
- Disciplinary discourse: practicing the question-and-argument norms of a field
- Transfer preparation: questions that push learners to apply structures in new cases

### Instructions
1. **Map the reasoning path.** Identify the chain of ideas from what learners know to the target understanding, including likely misconceptions.
2. **Open with accessible questions.** Start with recall or observation questions every learner can answer, activating relevant prior knowledge [Activation of prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M].
3. **Step up the cognitive demand.** Move through explanation ("why does that work?"), justification ("how do you know?"), and application ("what would happen if…?"), using [Questioning](questioning.md) and [Probing](../elements/probing.md) to press for reasoning rather than answers.
4. **Respond contingently.** On errors, offer a hint, a simpler case, or a counterexample — never the answer — adjusting to the learner's current position [Contingent, responsive scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S].
5. **Fade the support.** As learners begin answering at the higher levels, hand over the questioning itself — asking them to justify, then to generate questions for peers ([Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md)).

## Related Strategies
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md) — the opening questions of a scaffolded sequence serve this function
- [Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented-feedback.md) — the instructor's contingent response to a wrong answer is feedback embedded in dialogue
- [Cold Calling](cold-calling.md) — an accountability mechanism often combined with scaffolded questioning, provided support follows the call

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching (Palincsar & Brown)** — small groups take turns leading discussion using trained question prompts (predict, clarify, question, summarize), with the teacher modeling and fading over roughly 20 sessions.
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction** — teachers sequence questions about children's solution strategies in arithmetic, moving learners from direct modeling toward abstract reasoning; see [CGI for Math](../patterns/cgi-for-math.md).
- **Socratic seminar in AP classrooms** — a prepared question ladder opens the seminar; the teacher intervenes only with follow-up questions when reasoning stalls.
- **Khan Academy hint sequences** — each successive hint is a written scaffolded question, decomposing the problem into a smaller step rather than revealing the solution.
