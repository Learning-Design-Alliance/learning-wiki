# For online lectures, decide between synchronous streaming and asynchronous delivery, and always post recordings for equity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises lecture-based course instructors to decide whether to deliver lectures synchronously (live streaming via platforms such as Zoom, BlueJeans or Skype) or asynchronously. It notes synchronous formats let students ask questions and interact, but stresses that "not requiring attendance during live stream and posting the recorded videos" is essential so students lacking resources or means to connect live can still access the materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A videoconferencing or streaming platform capable of recording streamed lectures
#### Constraints
- Some students may lack a computer with reliable internet connection or a quiet room at home, so live attendance cannot be required

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in lecture-based courses moved online

### Target Learning Goals
- access to lecture content regardless of students' resource constraints
