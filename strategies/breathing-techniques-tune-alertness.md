# Use exhale-dominant and inhale-dominant breathing to tune alertness to the calm-alert range for learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy uses short, structured breathing patterns to raise or lower (nor)epinephrine-driven alertness toward the calm-alert state the review says is best for learning. Exhale-dominant breathing slows heart rate to reduce internal stress, while inhale-dominant breathing quickly raises alertness. The review recommends instructors teach these tools and pair them with stress self-assessment prompts during homework, while noting they have not been tested in educational contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be able to notice when their alertness is too high or too low, e.g., via self-assessment prompts about stress level and time continuously working
#### Constraints
- The review states the authors are unaware of work on managing (nor)epinephrine levels conducted in educational contexts, and effects might vary

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- managing attention and reactions to failure during challenging learning tasks
