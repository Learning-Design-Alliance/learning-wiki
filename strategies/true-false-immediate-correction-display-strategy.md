# Use true-false examinations with immediate self-correction and public display of scores as a time-saving teaching and assessment routine

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a true-false examination routine in which students check 30 or more statements as true or false, correct their own papers as the instructor rereads each question with the proper answer, and see scores displayed on the board. The authors report that "It saves an enormous amount of time" and that the examination "is really an excellent teaching instrument" because immediate correction reveals haziness, misunderstandings and ignorance of facts and principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A period of 50 minutes is sufficient to give, score, tabulate and discuss the results of a test of 40 questions, using the article's generous time allowances of about one minute per question.
#### Constraints
- The listed advantages are explicitly not derived from objective data but are accumulations of opinions generally voiced by those who have given or taken such tests.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in content courses such as educational psychology

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring and giving feedback on mastery of course content, including application of facts and principles
