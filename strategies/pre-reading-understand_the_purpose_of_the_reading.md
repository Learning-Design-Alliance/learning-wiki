# Pre-Reading — Understand the Purpose of the Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Before reading, learners are given or generate an explicit purpose: a question to answer, a task the text will support, or a use for the information (e.g., "read to decide which proposal to fund"). The purpose is established through a short orientation — a framing question, a scenario, or a stated application — so that reading becomes goal-directed rather than passive decoding.

## Design Implications

Purpose-setting is a form of [Activation](../principles/activation.md): it primes relevant prior knowledge and gives learners a schema for selecting what matters [Activation improves learning outcomes.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]. It also functions as a lightweight [advance organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md), supplying structure before the text is encountered [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Because reading with a purpose narrows attention, it reduces extraneous processing on irrelevant detail [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M] and supports self-regulated comprehension monitoring [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md).

### Context
#### Requirements
- A purpose that is specific enough to guide attention ("identify the two causes of X") rather than generic ("read carefully")
- Alignment between the stated purpose and the post-reading task — a mismatch trains learners to ignore purposes
- Enough time for learners to restate the purpose in their own words before reading begins

#### Constraints
- Overly narrow purposes cause tunnel vision: learners retain goal-irrelevant content poorly, which is a problem when broad comprehension is the goal [~M]
- Purposes imposed without learner buy-in add compliance overhead rather than motivation; purpose works best when learners see the task as meaningful [~W]
- If the post-reading activity never uses the purpose, learners learn that pre-reading framing is decorative and skip it [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Instructor-provided purpose (fast, reliable) vs. learner-generated questions (slower, builds self-regulation)
- Scenario-based purposes ("you are the consultant…") vs. question-based purposes
- Written purpose statements, [KWL](../strategies/kwl.md)-style charts, or a one-minute pair discussion

### Target Learners
- Developing readers and novices in a discipline, who otherwise read everything with equal (usually insufficient) depth [Disciplinary literacy instruction improves comprehension.](../claims/disciplinary-literacy-instruction-improves-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Struggling readers, who benefit most from explicit attention direction because they cannot yet flexibly allocate effort
- Advanced readers may self-set purposes and find imposed ones constraining [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension with selective retention of goal-relevant information
- Self-regulated learning: planning and monitoring as habitual pre-reading moves
- Disciplinary literacy: learning that experts read differently depending on purpose

### Instructions
1. Present the post-reading task first, so the purpose is grounded in something learners must actually do ([Application](../elements/application.md))
2. State or elicit a specific purpose question; have learners restate it in their own words ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
3. Briefly activate relevant prior knowledge with a prediction or brainstorm ([Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md))
4. Have learners read with the purpose visible (posted, on a bookmark, in margin notes via [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
5. Debrief against the purpose: did the text answer it? What was left open?

## Related Strategies
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) — the companion pre-reading move; purpose directs attention, activation supplies content to connect it to
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — structural pre-reading frames that pair naturally with purpose statements
- [KWL](../strategies/kwl.md) — a routinized format in which "What do I want to find out?" is the purpose step

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching (Palincsar & Brown)** — students set a purpose question before each passage segment, then summarize and clarify against it.
- **[Reading Apprenticeship](https://readingapprenticeship.org/)** — disciplinary routines that open with "What is my purpose for reading this?" as part of metacognitive conversation.
- **Case-method pre-reading (Harvard Business School)** — students receive discussion questions with a case so they read as decision-makers, not spectators.
