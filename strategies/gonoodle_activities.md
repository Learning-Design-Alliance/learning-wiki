# GoNoodle Activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
GoNoodle ([gonoodle.com](https://www.gonoodle.com)) is a free digital platform of short interactive videos — dance breaks, mindfulness and breathing exercises, yoga, and movement-based academic content — designed for classroom use with children. Teachers deploy 2–10 minute activities as energizers, transitions, or calming routines, embedding physical activity and self-regulation practice into the school day rather than relegating it to recess.

## Design Implications

Brief bouts of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity acutely improve attention and executive function in children [Classroom physical activity improves attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M], and single exercise sessions can transiently enhance memory and cognitive performance [Acute exercise timing affects memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]. GoNoodle operationalizes this by making movement breaks fast, low-preparation, and repeatable — the design goal is not fitness but a cognitive reset that restores attention for subsequent instruction.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A screen or projector and internet access; a cleared space for movement
- Predictable routines: breaks scheduled at known transition points (after seatwork, before testing) rather than at random
- Clear start/stop signals so the activity does not dissolve into unmanaged play

#### Constraints
- Benefits are acute and short-lived; a single break does not build fitness or durable self-regulation skill [Acute exercise timing affects memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M] — effects depend on regular, scheduled use
- Poorly timed breaks (mid-task, or immediately before activities requiring calm focus without a settling routine) can disrupt rather than restore attention [-M]
- High-stimulation videos used indiscriminately can add arousal without purpose; energizing content and calming content serve different moments and should be matched to the goal
- Screen-based movement is a supplement to, not a substitute for, free play and physical education [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Energizers** (e.g., "Pop 'N' Bop," "Release Your Warrior") before or during long seated blocks to raise arousal
- **Settlers** (e.g., "Breathing Exercises For Focusing And Settling," "Nimble Nostrils") to down-regulate after recess or before assessments
- **Academic integration**: videos that embed counting, spelling, or content review into movement, pairing [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) with recall
- **Product design**: the same pattern — short, guided, movement-based interludes — can be built into learning apps as attention resets between tasks

### Target Learners
- Elementary-age children (K–5), who show the largest acute attention benefits from classroom activity breaks [Classroom physical activity improves attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]
- Learners with ADHD or high activity needs, for whom structured movement outlets support on-task behavior [+M]
- Whole classes during transitions, where a shared routine also supports group cohesion and emotional regulation

### Target Learning Goals
- Attention restoration and readiness to learn (a precondition, not a content objective)
- Emotional regulation and stress reduction through guided breathing and mindfulness [+W]
- Incidental academic content (counting, vocabulary) via movement-embedded videos [~W]

### Instructions
1. Identify the transition or low-attention point in the lesson where a break will do the most good.
2. Select an energizing or calming video matched to the goal — raise arousal before work, lower it after.
3. Establish the routine: signal, participate, and end with a clear return-to-seat cue ([Gain Attention](../elements/gain-attention.md) in reverse).
4. Follow the break immediately with focused instruction while the attention benefit is active [Acute exercise timing affects memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M].
5. Use calming activities as a recurring [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routine to build self-regulation habits over the year.
