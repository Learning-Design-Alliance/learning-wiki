# Use metaphor-based reflective tasks as a recurring part of language teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that reflection be built permanently into teaching and varied with creative methods. After the treatment, learners wrote metaphors for the pragmatic routines and their learning, which the authors say encouraged critical thinking and surfaced learners' views often absent from quantitative studies. Metaphors ranged from a car to a life buoy, coding reliably across two researchers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners are asked open reflection questions and then prompted to create a metaphor for what they learned
#### Constraints
- The article notes new reflection methods on varied phases and elements of language teaching could be employed in further studies

### Target Learners
- tertiary-level EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- speaking sub-skills such as pragmatic routines
- critical thinking through reflection
