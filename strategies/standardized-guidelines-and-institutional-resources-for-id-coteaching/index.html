Establish standardized evidence-based guidelines and adequate institutional resources to sustain co-teaching with individuals with Disorders of Intellectual Development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review concludes that the field needs "standardized, evidence-based guidelines and adequate institutional resources to support co-teaching involving individuals with Disorders of Intellectual Development in healthcare education." It reports that systemic barriers, exclusionary system structures, institutional practices, and professional attitudes can undermine the transformative potential of user involvement, and that inadequate investment in planning-phase time and resources can undermine effectiveness — not because of the concept itself, but because of insufficient support structures.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional commitment and overcoming persistent barriers
  • Additional time and resources in the planning phase for curriculum development and implementation

Constraints¶

  • Hesitancy toward these approaches has been observed among senior faculty members due to limited experience and implementation guidelines

Target Learners¶

  • medical and healthcare students
  • university faculty and curriculum planners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inclusive healthcare education
  • participatory curriculum development

Claims¶

  • Co-teachers with Disorders of Intellectual Development report mainly positive experiences of autonomy, relationships, and accomplishment, alongside initial discomfort and unmet training needs [+W]
  • A single three-hour communication skills session involving tutors with intellectual disability reduced medical students' self-reported discomfort interacting with people with developmental disabilities [+W]
  • Only a small proportion (16%) of reviewed interventions featured individuals with Disorders of Intellectual Development as co-teachers rather than in patient or recipient roles [+W]
  • Qualitative evaluations report that co-teaching by individuals with Disorders of Intellectual Development deepens students' understanding and increases empathy and comfort in interactions [+W]
  • A semester-long seminar co-taught by educational specialists with intellectual disabilities showed no statistically significant scale-level pre–post changes in pre-service teachers' inclusion attitudes or self-efficacy [~W]
