# Integrate structured active breaks into academic curricula to support student well-being and performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding short, structured physical activity breaks into the academic day rather than treating learning as purely cognitive. It argues the results "support the inclusion of movement-based practices to equip future managers with resilience, decision-making quality, and adaptability in volatile and uncertain contexts", positioning active breaks as a simple complement to traditional instruction. The tested recipe is four 15-minute breaks daily, every 60–90 minutes, blending aerobic exercise, stretching, breathing and mindfulness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor supervision, monitoring of intensity, and scheduling coordinated with the class timetable
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single pilot in management education; effects for decision-making and physiological regulation were only trends, and long-term sustainability was not assessed

### Target Learners
- university students, especially those in management and other high-demand academic programs

### Target Learning Goals
- stress regulation
- executive function
- decision-making quality
- psychological resilience
