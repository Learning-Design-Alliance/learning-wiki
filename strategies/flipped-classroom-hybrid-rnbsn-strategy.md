# Adopt a flipped classroom combined with hybrid teaching methods as a suitable learning strategy for RN-to-BSN nursing programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that combining a flipped classroom structure (online pre-class self-paced learning, in-class TBL and simulation, post-class online assignments) with hybrid teaching methods is an effective strategy for RN-to-BSN programs. They state that "The flipped classroom combined with hybrid teaching methods is a suitable and effective learning strategy for a registered nurse (RN) to Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN) program" and note it supports autonomy through self-paced pre-class learning, relatedness through peer discussion, and competence through reevaluating strengths and weaknesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online platform (e-campus) for hosting pre-class materials and post-class assignments, faculty training for consistency, and face-to-face sessions for TBL and simulation activities.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experiment at a single university; the authors note the generalizability of the findings is limited and recommend randomized designs and monitoring of online platform use in future research.

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses (ADN graduates) enrolled in two-year RN-to-BSN programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Core nursing competencies, metacognitive ability, self-directed learning readiness, and preparation for complex health care needs
