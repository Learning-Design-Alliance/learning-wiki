Use computer games as a supplementary practice method for basic mathematics skills¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests that computer games can be used as one method for students to practice basic mathematical skills after initial classroom instruction. In this study, students who had been taught by their teacher "as usual" were then assigned to practice through either a computer game program or classical homework exercises. The computer game approach produced learning gains equivalent to those from classical homework, supporting its use as a viable practice alternative rather than a replacement for teacher-led instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must first receive standard classroom instruction from a teacher before using games for practice

Constraints¶

  • No evidence that computer games outperform classical homework for basic mathematics achievement

Target Learners¶

  • Fifth-grade elementary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Basic mathematical skills including four operations of natural numbers

Claims¶

  • Both computer game practice and classical homework practice produce statistically significant pretest-to-posttest achievement gains in basic mathematics skills [+W]
  • Computer game-based practice produces no statistically significant difference in basic mathematics achievement compared to classical homework practice [~W]
  • Prior research on educational computer games shows mixed effects on cognitive and academic achievement outcomes [~W]
  • Prior studies reviewed in the article consistently report achievement advantages for computer-assisted mathematics teaching over traditional methods [+W]
  • Small sample size and slight pretest imbalance may have masked potential positive effects of computer games [~W]
