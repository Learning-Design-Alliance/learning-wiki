# Blindfold-dominant multisensory-motor letter games (~70% of session with eyes closed) followed by eyes-open mapping and perception-for-action

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Each 30-minute training session is a sequence of short game-like multisensory-motor activities on one letter pair. For around 70% of the session children wear eye masks and do air-writing, writing on paper, and perceiving letters on hands, with the letter sound pronounced continuously; the aim is to "reduce the dominance of the visual system over other systems" and favor attention on other sensory-motor systems. In the remaining ~30% the same activities are repeated with eyes open, plus a 'perception for action' game indicating letter parts with the left and right hands to map dorsal to ventral visual processing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions must use one letter pair at a time, reduced groups (8 to 15 children), eye masks, and training in the morning so it precedes the post-lunch nap opportunity.
#### Constraints
- To keep motivation high the authors reduced an initially planned 2-hour training to 30 minutes and alternated three letter pairs over the days; activities risk becoming repetitive.

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers in first grade (5-7 years old)

### Target Learning Goals
- Mirror discrimination for letters, letter writing accuracy, reading fluency
