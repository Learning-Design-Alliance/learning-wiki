# Promote regular family routines (morning, mealtime, bedtime, homework) as a possible avenue for narrowing school achievement gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing more routines, including morning, mealtime and bedtime routines, homework and school activities and household responsibilities, as a potential mechanism to narrow the school achievement gap between children of higher and lower educated parents. It postulates that some aspects of routines are amenable to school and community interventions, for example supervised homework routines at school. The authors explicitly label this speculation and call for randomized parenting-intervention trials for definitive causal evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Routines must be regular day-to-day activities such as regular mealtimes, bedtimes and shared family activities, as the article defines family routines
#### Constraints
- The article states this remains speculative and that only a trial where researchers randomize parenting interventions or emulated trials could provide definitive causal evidence
- The study sample has a relatively high socioeconomic background, which prompts caution when generalizing the results

### Target Learners
- children in primary school (ages up to 12–13 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- school achievement in language and mathematics
