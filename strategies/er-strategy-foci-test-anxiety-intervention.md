# Target rumination and catastrophizing reduction and putting into perspective enhancement in test anxiety interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the reciprocal and unidirectional relations found, the authors recommend that interventions to limit anxiety-related underachievement focus on specific emotion regulation strategies. The Educational Impact Statement states that "Strategies to reduce rumination and catastrophizing and enhance putting into perspective would be useful foci for intervention", because the first two strategies cycle positively with test anxiety while putting into perspective lowers it. This follows from the finding that strategies which are outcomes of anxiety are less useful intervention targets than those that cause it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must target strategies shown to precede changes in test anxiety, not merely follow it
#### Constraints
- Relations were contemporaneous within twelve-month waves in a sample of upper-secondary students in England; intervention efficacy was not itself tested in this study

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary students preparing for high-stakes exams

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing test anxiety and limiting anxiety-related underachievement
