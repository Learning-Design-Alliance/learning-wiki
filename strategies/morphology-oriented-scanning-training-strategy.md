# Teach morphology-oriented, non-linear scanning protocols and site-specific accuracy evaluation to novice intraoral scanner users

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "these results highlight the importance of morphology-oriented scanning protocols and site-specific evaluation during intraoral scanning procedures." Because non-linear strategies outperformed the linear path on trueness, precision, and time regardless of operator experience, training for inexperienced operators should emphasize non-linear (zigzag/sweeping) scan paths and awareness of how crowded or spaced dentition alters accuracy, rather than assuming experience alone ensures quality. The study also found localized posterior deviations not reflected in global RMS values, supporting teaching site-specific checks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized instruction on scanner handling and predefined scanning strategies before independent performance, as given to all operators in the study
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from in vitro conditions without saliva, soft tissue movement, or patient motion, and may not translate into clinically significant inaccuracies

### Target Learners
- inexperienced dental students learning intraoral scanning

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate and efficient full-arch intraoral scanning technique
