# Deliver community-based active learning health education combining lectures, discussion, self-planning and family homework for older adults

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends community health education for older adults that integrates individual active learning, family collaboration and community engagement rather than passive information provision. The program combined "health lectures, active discussions, heuristic questioning, and family homework", and participants were encouraged to reinforce learning through books, the Internet and family discussions. The authors state this format "is inexpensive and requires no special equipment", making it implementable as a prevention program or voluntary community health activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators with health backgrounds; alignment of components with TPB constructs (attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioural control)
#### Constraints
- Behaviour change lagged knowledge change in the trial; the authors recommend integrating behaviour-focused strategies alongside educational interventions for substantial behaviour improvement

### Target Learners
- Community-dwelling older adults, including those with chronic diseases and lower educational attainment

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehensive health literacy and proactive health awareness
