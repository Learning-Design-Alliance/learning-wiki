# Sustain MI skill acquisition with ongoing coaching, feedback, supervision and on-the-job practice rather than training alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that a one-off MI training embedded in a comprehensive program produced minimal skill maintenance at one year, and cites work suggesting training alone is insufficient. It states that "Ongoing coaching/ feedback, written material and supervision are also essential" and that continued support on the job is needed so MI skills "can be practiced and evaluated during daily routine over a longer period of time". Supervisors and colleagues should also support providers in performing MI.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be complemented by ongoing coaching, feedback, written material and supervision, plus opportunities to practice and evaluate MI skills in daily routine over a longer period.
#### Constraints
- The article reports this need in the context of primary care nurses in routine diabetes care; it does not report a tested effect of the added coaching itself.

### Target Learners
- Primary care nurses providing lifestyle counselling

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition and maintenance of motivational interviewing skills in daily practice
