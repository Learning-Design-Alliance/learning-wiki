# Recruit and retain underrepresented students in QIST through early outreach, funded research experiences, industry mentorship, and a code of conduct

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's inclusive-practices strategy spans recruitment (early, community-involved, hands-on and mentorship experiences, publicized job databases), retention (funding student activities, early research experiences, industry engagement, peer-leadership mentoring), and pedagogy. It states "It is crucial to reach out to students early in their academic career s, including during their high school years," and that "A key obstacle for undergraduates, and especially under-repres ented students, is ﬁ nancial stress," so faculty should implement student funding opportunities. Programs should adopt a code of conduct, such as four axiomatic beliefs adapted from Federico Ardila.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentorship responsibilities and DEI efforts should be shared across faculty to remain sustainable, since mentoring is usually not formally rewarded
- Retention requires ensuring students feel engaged in a welcoming and supportive community
#### Constraints
- DEI programs often lack long-term budgetary support, and some scientists view cohesion structures dismissively

### Target Learners
- underrepresented and underserved undergraduate students in QIST programs

### Target Learning Goals
- belonging and persistence in QIST fields; authentic research experience
