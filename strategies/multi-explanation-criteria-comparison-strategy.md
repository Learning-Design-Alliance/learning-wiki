# Interview-based teacher-preparation activity: compare several explanations of one phenomenon via a criteria grid before choosing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-phase activity for teacher preparation: present several explanations of the same phenomenon that all reach an accurate conclusion, co-construct a quality diagnosis with a criteria grid in interaction with an instructor, then ask participants to choose an explanation for themselves and for a target audience and justify the choice via the criteria. The article reports that "STs were offered three different explanations of how a tensiometer works, all with an accurate conclusion" and "a grid of criteria for critical analysis". Participants judged the approach valuable for deliberate, well-informed decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An interviewer or instructor leads the first critical-analysis phase so participants reach a consensual diagnosis before choosing
- Complementary conceptual information must be available on request
#### Constraints
- Results are preliminary: participants interacted closely with an interviewer, so the article notes these research questions are only a preliminary step towards what STs may do if left alone

### Target Learners
- beginning secondary physics and chemistry teachers (student teachers)

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of candidate explanations
- explicit justification of pedagogical content choices
