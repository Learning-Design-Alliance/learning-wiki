Managing a PBL class: heterogeneous small groups, facilitator monitoring, and a closing debriefing to correct misconceptions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors describe how they ran PBL sessions, influenced by the Buck Institute for Education approach: students worked in small heterogeneous groups balanced by ethnicity, gender, prior accounting exposure and entry qualification. Students discussed problems from the entry document, identified learning issues, and exited by written report or group presentation, while lecturers moved around monitoring and guiding time management. Each session ended with a debriefing the authors call "crucial to correct any misconceptions observed during the presentation/learning process". Continuous motivation and mindset preparation were treated as ongoing lecturer tasks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Continuous lecturer effort to prepare student mindset and keep students motivated, since students were used to traditional lectures
  • Groups encouraged to settle their own conflicts
  • Debriefing session at the end of each problem cycle

Constraints¶

  • Managing and getting the cooperation of group members was not an easy task, mirroring real-world group problems

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduate accounting students in classes of 30 to 50

Target Learning Goals¶

  • problem-solving skills
  • self-directed learning
  • collaborative teamwork

Claims¶

  • Open-ended student feedback yielded four themes: PBL is acceptable, challenging but beneficial, improves soft skills, and suits only students with basic accounting knowledge [+W]
  • Students perceived PBL as providing significant benefits to presentation skills, teamwork, leadership and ability to use IT [+W]
  • PBL students out-performed non-PBL students in final course grades in Financial Accounting Principles, with 14% versus 6% scoring A/A- and 3% versus 9% failing [+W]
