# Mapping Online Reading Behavior

## Description
Learners create visual maps to track the links and sites they visit while reading online texts. They start with a general research question and document each step and detour they take to find the answer, either by drawing or taking screenshots. Students share their maps and reflect on their decision-making process at each point.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to online reading materials and tools for creating visual maps (e.g., drawing software, mind-mapping tools, or simply paper and pencils).
#### Constraints
- Some learners may find it challenging to accurately recall and document their online reading behavior. The activity may also be time-consuming, especially if learners are easily distracted or tend to wander off-topic.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Middle school, high school, and adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance metacognitive awareness of online reading habits, promote self-regulation, and foster critical evaluation of information sources.

### Affordances
- Supports metacognition by making learners aware of their online reading processes, promotes self-regulation by encouraging them to monitor and adjust their strategies, and fosters critical evaluation of information sources by requiring them to reflect on the reliability and relevance of the content they encounter.

### Personalization
- The complexity of the research question and the level of detail required in the maps can be adjusted to meet the needs of diverse learners. Learners can also be given the option to use different mapping tools or formats based on their preferences.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review the maps created by learners to assess their awareness of their own online reading processes and their ability to make informed decisions about which sources to trust and which links to follow. Observe the quality of their reflections on their decision-making processes.

## Impact
- Research suggests that metacognitive reflection on online reading behavior can improve learners' comprehension, critical thinking, and self-regulation skills in digital environments.
