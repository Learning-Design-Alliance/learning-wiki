# Train citizen scientists in qualitative assessment by supporting three needs: diverse stream experiences, peer discussion, and narrative construction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes engaging learning-motivated volunteers in high-level qualitative assessment tasks and identifies what training must provide. As stated: "to successfully make qualitative stream assessments, volunteers need to: (1) experience a diverse range of streams, (2) discuss judgments with other monitors, and (3) construct internal narratives about water quality." Because in-person training of crowds at several streams is infeasible, the authors propose 360° panoramic videos, AR, and VR to remotely give learners experience evaluating different streams and receiving feedback from peers or experts. Training should include an assessment loop allowing learners to discuss and receive feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must experience first-hand (or virtually) a range of diverse streams
- Training must include an assessment loop with peer or expert discussion and feedback
- Training must help learners unite observations with background knowledge into temporal narratives
#### Constraints
- Citizen science training is often constrained by time and monetary resources
- Each candidate technology has drawbacks: 360° video is non-interactive, AR has imprecise rendering and poor multi-user support, VR is expensive and can cause simulator sickness

### Target Learners
- citizen science volunteers in water monitoring
- volunteers motivated by scientific learning

### Target Learning Goals
- qualitative stream assessment using the RBP
- constructing water-quality narratives
