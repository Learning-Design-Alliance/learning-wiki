# Connecting Math to ESOL

## Description
This strategy brings a little bit of math could make for some interesting discussions in English for Speakers of Other Languages (ESOL) contexts. By integrating math, students can practice language skills while addressing real-world mathematical applications. By incorporating the math, the ESOL teacher should not directly indicate to students that they are including math, and should prioritize making things fun and relevant to their lives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning and preparation, access to relevant resources and materials, and a supportive learning environment. Instructors need to be knowledgeable about both English language and math concepts and able to make connections to learners' lives.
#### Constraints
- Can be challenging to integrate math into English language instruction seamlessly. Requires careful planning and preparation to ensure that activities are appropriate for learners' language and math levels.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Provides a context for learners to practice their English language skills while learning math concepts. Enhances cultural awareness by incorporating learners' experiences and perspectives. Strengthens collaboration by encouraging learners to work together and support each other's learning.

### Personalization
- Instructors can tailor the activities to learners' individual interests, needs, and cultural backgrounds. Learners can choose topics and activities that are relevant to their lives.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track learner progress on language and math skills through observation, participation, and assessment tasks. Evaluate the quality of their final product, and evaluate their ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems. Measure gains in content knowledge, self-confidence, collaboration, and engagement through pre- and post-assessments.

## Impact
- Leads to increased learner engagement, improved understanding of math concepts, enhanced confidence in math skills, and promotion of transfer of learning to real-world situations. Increased learner outcomes and increased motivation. Increased ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems.
