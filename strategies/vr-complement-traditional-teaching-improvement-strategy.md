# Use case-based VR simulation as a complement to, not a replacement for, traditional pelvic trauma teaching, and improve it by enlarging the case database, widening disease coverage, and advancing ESP intelligence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendations combine a caution and an improvement agenda: "1 instructor cautioned that this innovative skill training should complement, rather than replace, traditional teaching methods, a sentiment echoed by other educational research." Teachers also recommended that "To enhance the virtual ESP simulation system, five improvements were suggested: enlarging the ESP case database, incorporating a feature for automatic and manual responses to technical queries, broadening the range of disease diagnosis and treatment simulations, expanding the ESP history collection database, and advancing the ESP's artificial intelligence for inquiry processing."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional integration with a virtual simulation experiment teaching sharing platform and unified identity verification, as implemented at Nanjing Medical University
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on pilot-study teacher opinion; the article states the system should complement rather than replace traditional teaching methods

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and graduate medical students in orthopedic surgery education

### Target Learning Goals
- Severe pelvic trauma clinical reasoning and procedural skills training

## Claims

- [Medical students' theoretical scores across all five severe pelvic trauma clinical skill domains rose from pre- to postsimulation in a 1-group pretest-posttest pilot of case-based VR simulation training](../claims/case-based-vr-pelvic-trauma-prepost-gains.md) [+W]
- [Learners reported difficulty communicating directly with the ESP (eye contact, body language, tone and intonation), so doctor-patient communication skills were not fully practiced in the VR simulation](../claims/esp-communication-limitation-doctor-patient-skills.md) [~W]
- [Emergency management of hemorrhagic shock and external fixation of pelvic fractures each improved significantly from pre- to postsimulation, with external fixation showing the largest mean gain (58.4 percentage points, Cohen d 5.3) and hemorrhagic shock the largest Cohen d (8.5)](../claims/hemorrhagic-shock-external-fixation-prepost-ttests.md) [+W]
- [Students rated the case-based VR simulation highly acceptable: 95% affirmed it simulated real-patient scenarios, and all 40 reported making real-life doctor decisions and finding it interesting and useful](../claims/vr-pelvic-trauma-student-acceptability-ratings.md) [+W]
- [Undergraduate and graduate students differed significantly on four questionnaire items (Q1, Q5, Q9, Q14), with undergraduates rating realism, difficulty fit, clinical-reasoning benefit, and cost reduction differently from graduates](../claims/undergraduate-graduate-rating-differences-vr.md) [~W]
