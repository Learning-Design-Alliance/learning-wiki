# Theory-guided methodological practices for developmental neuroimaging: theory-based age groups, parametric task design, and process-analytical inference

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that developmental cognitive neuroscience will grow by using constructivist-developmental frameworks so that (a) age-group selection is theory-based, (b) task processes are modelled from a from-within subjects perspective and measured parametrically, and (c) inferences are process-analytical and comparable across studies. Concretely, tasks for children should be short (30 to 60 min depending on age), use shorter runs (e.g., four 5-min runs instead of two 10-minute runs), include enough trials (e.g., 32 trials per category), be validated behaviourally outside the scanner, and use gradual, interval-scale parametric difficulty scaling rather than irregularly graded designs like the usual n-back task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must adhere to imaging-method constraints, avoid ceiling and floor effects for every age group, and be task-analysed and behaviourally validated outside the scanner
#### Constraints
- Parametric protocols remain rare in developmental neuroscience; age-range averaging across excessively wide spans produces spurious averages

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents participating in neuroimaging studies

### Target Learning Goals
- producing interpretable, developmentally anchored neuroimaging data usable in education and pediatric practice
