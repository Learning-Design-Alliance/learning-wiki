# Small-group, tutor-led VPS training sessions (5–9 students) as an accessible simulation strategy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy embeds short small-group sessions with a virtual patient simulator into health-professions curricula: a familiarization case followed by tutor-chosen clinical cases for groups of 5 to 9 students. The article reports that "the small-group environment contributes to active learning, enhances deep learning, and helps learners identify weaknesses," and that the sessions improved perceived learning process and curricular integration across nursing and medical students. Sessions were delivered by clinical tutors and could be integrated into the regular curriculum or offered stand-alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups of 5 to 9 students with a clinical tutor
- Access to the VPS in interactive table or computer format
- A brief, non-judgemental introduction to the VPS before training
#### Constraints
- Results "cannot be extrapolated to unsupervised VPS training or to individual or large-group VPS training"; most participants were pre-clinical students

### Target Learners
- nursing students
- medical students in pre-clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- perceived individual learning process
- curricular integration
- decision-making and non-technical skills practice
