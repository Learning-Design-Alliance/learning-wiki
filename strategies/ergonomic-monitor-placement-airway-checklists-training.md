# Incorporate ergonomic equipment-placement guidance into airway setup protocols, checklists, and resident training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend operational and curricular changes: "Incorporating ergonomic guidelines into standardized airway setup protocols and checklists may improve both procedural performance, operator ergonomics, and patient outcomes." They also suggest "Teaching resident trainees to deliberately optimize patient monitor positioning may instill safer habits that persist into clinical practice," and note adjustable ceiling-mounted or articulated monitors may be preferred where feasible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Airway setup protocols or checklists into which ergonomic guidance can be embedded
- simulation or clinical settings where monitor placement can be deliberately configured
#### Constraints
- Existing reviewed airway checklists rarely mention equipment positioning, and the one that did omitted what constituted an appropriate position

### Target Learners
- emergency medicine residents
- clinicians performing rapid sequence intubation

### Target Learning Goals
- safe airway management habits
- equipment setup optimization during procedural training
