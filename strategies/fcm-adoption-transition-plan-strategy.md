# Nurse educators adopt the flipped classroom with a comprehensive transition plan and e-modules for skill retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "nursing educators should use this model in their teaching strategies to engage students and improve learning outcomes", while cautioning that educators "need to develop a comprehensive transition plan that includes pre-lesson planning, active learning activities in the classroom, whether learning has occurred, and formative assessment strategies". For clinical practice relevance, it suggests educators ensure retention of learning by developing audio-visual media and e-modules for teaching different nursing skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A comprehensive transition plan covering pre-lesson planning, in-class active learning, learning checks, and formative assessment
#### Constraints
- Results are applicable only to the first-year nursing students surveyed at a state university and cannot be generalized to other nursing students

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement in nursing fundamentals learning
- retention of nursing knowledge and skills

### Affordances
- [Constructivist Flipped Classroom Nursing Design](../designs/constructivist-flipped-classroom-nursing-design.md)
