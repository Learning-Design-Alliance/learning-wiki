# Use outcome- and semester-specific empirical benchmarks from prior RCTs to plan minimum detectable true effects and to interpret new community college evaluation findings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
When planning an RCT, researchers should size the sample so the minimum detectable true effect is plausible given the distribution of true effects from past evaluations, choosing benchmarks specific to the outcome and timing of measurement. For interpretation, a new effect estimate and its standard error can be converted to an adjusted empirical Bayes estimate and located on the published distribution. The article illustrates this with the Detroit Promise Path evaluation, whose 1.8-credit effect corresponds to "an adjusted empirical Bayes impact estimate of 1.6 credits earned" at the 83rd percentile after two semesters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- MDTE calculations must be specific with respect to outcome and timing
- other design parameters are necessary to calculate the minimum detectable true effect
#### Constraints
- Benchmarks from prior studies inform what is realistically attainable but do not necessarily inform what effects are practically meaningful to decision-makers

### Target Learners
- community college students

### Target Learning Goals
- enrollment persistence
- credit accumulation
- credential attainment
