# Gradually introduce unpredictable elements into SBL to build adaptability for CALD students preferring structured preparation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators progressively introduce unpredictability into simulation activities so students accustomed to structured, thorough preparation develop adaptability and flexibility valued in occupational therapy practice. Suggested tactics include "introducing changes in simulated patients' behaviours or adjusting details of the case scenario during SBL activities", layered onto otherwise structured, low-stakes early experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SBL should incorporate structured and progressive experiences that begin with low-stakes activities
#### Constraints
- Responds to tension attributed to Confucian Heritage Culture preferences for step-by-step instruction; article frames this as a suggestion for educators

### Target Learners
- Occupational therapy students from CALD backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Adaptability and flexibility in unpredictable clinical situations
