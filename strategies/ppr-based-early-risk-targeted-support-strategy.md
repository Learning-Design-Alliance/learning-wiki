# Use multi-variable PPR-based risk analysis from admission onward to target support for students at risk of licensure examination failure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends identifying students at risk of failing the NMLE as early as admission using the multi-variable PPR rather than a single indicator such as the mock exam, and providing them guidance to overcome their shortcomings before the examination. It concludes that "Risk analysis based on multiple variables, such as PPR, can inform more effective intervention compared to a single variable, such as performance in the mock exam", and calls for "a consistent program of support" for high-risk students entering the program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated logistic-regression PPR formula using variables available at each time point, and institutional data on pre- and post-admission performance
#### Constraints
- The article states "A longer prospective study is required to confirm the validity of PPR" and that age and gender should not be used as selection criteria because of discrimination and bias

### Target Learners
- Medical students, especially those flagged as high risk of failing the licensure examination

### Target Learning Goals
- First-attempt success on the national medical licensure examination

### Affordances
- Ppr Predictive Pass Rate Model
