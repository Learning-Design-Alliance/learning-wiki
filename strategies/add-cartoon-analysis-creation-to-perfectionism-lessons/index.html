Strategies

Add cartoon analysis and creation to lessons about perfectionism while retaining bibliotherapy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend a combined approach: "we recommend that teachers not abandon bibliotherapy, but add cartoon analysis and creation as another effective strategy for teaching about the important concepts of perfectionism". Cartoon lessons involve discussing numbered cartoons with prompts, completing partly-finished cartoons, performing skits applying concepts to new situations, and co-constructing three-panel cartoon strips. This maintains student interest while conveying the same concepts bibliotherapy delivers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A pool of concept-focused cartoons; the study used cartoons peer-reviewed for content and humor by graduate students in a gifted education course

Constraints¶

  • Students with English as a second language may have difficulty with puns and subtle humor aspects of the cartoons

Target Learners¶

  • primary grade elementary students (grades 1-3)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding perfectionism concepts
  • recognizing healthy versus unhealthy perfectionism

Claims¶

  • Primary-grade students taught perfectionism concepts through cartoon analysis and production scored significantly higher on a posttest than students taught through bibliotherapy [+W]
  • Student-completed cartoons served as evidence that students in the cartoon condition understood the perfectionism concepts taught [+W]
  • Teacher observations indicated sustained engagement in cartoon lessons versus diminishing excitement and reported boredom in bibliotherapy lessons [+W]
  • Students rated the cartoon condition as more enjoyable than bibliotherapy, though the difference was significant only at the alpha = 0.10 level [+W]
