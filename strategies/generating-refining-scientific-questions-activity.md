# Opening activity of generating and refining scientific questions from authentic course material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
On the first day of the course, students anonymously write questions about authentic course material (images of Galaxy Clusters at different wavelengths, quasar spectra) shown without physical interpretation. Small randomly formed groups then refine one question by identifying what makes it investigable: a measurable variable, testability, and splitting into smaller units. The article states that 'a specific activity ("generating and refining scientific questions") is designed and offered as the introductory experience on the first day of the course', building ownership and equity since questions are anonymous and freely chosen.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic material shown without interpretation, anonymous question collection, small randomly formed groups, and facilitation or prompts
#### Constraints
- Not all questions are equally investigable, so refinement toward testable, measurable formulations is needed

### Target Learners
- University MSc physics students new to inquiry learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Practicing question-raising as a driver of learning
- defining physical versus observable variables
- taking ownership of the learning process
