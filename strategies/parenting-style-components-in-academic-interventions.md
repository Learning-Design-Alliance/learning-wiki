# Include positive parenting style components in interventions aiming to improve adolescent academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that "Interventions aiming at improving academic performance of adolescents should also encompass positive parenting style strategies". They point to existing parenting training programs such as the Triple P-Positive Parenting Program and Parent Management Training, which aim to empower parents, reduce parental stress and strengthen the parent–child relationship, arguing that competent parenting may positively influence academic outcomes among other child outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should foster effective and positive parenting and supportive parent–child relationships
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is a prospective observational study, so the recommendation rests on associational and mediation findings rather than a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- Adolescents and their parents, across socio-economic backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved academic achievement and stronger self-efficacy and intention towards getting good grades
