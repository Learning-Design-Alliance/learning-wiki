# Implement journal writing as self-assessment through a seven-step guideline set with weekly teacher feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article prescribes a concrete procedure for introducing journal writing as a self-assessment technique in a writing course: introduce the concept with modeling and assurance, state objectives, sequence "what questions" before "how questions", specify assessment criteria (effort and processing of course content, not grammar), provide cheerleading, instructional and neutral feedback, set a fixed time frame of about ten minutes at the end of each session, and review sample entries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher reads and responds to journal entries every week and holds a short class discussion the following session
- Students are told not to worry about grammar or spelling and to focus on expressing reflections freely
#### Constraints
- The article notes journal writing is difficult at first for students from teacher-centered educational systems that overlook teacher-student dialogue

### Target Learners
- Intermediate EFL university students in writing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflection on learning, self-monitoring and evaluation, intrinsic motivation, writing self-confidence
