# Analog dice-based creation of Erdős-Rényi random graphs as a playful bridge to computational graph generation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After introducing G(n,p) random graphs, pupils in four groups created undirected random graphs with six- and twenty-sided dice at different connection probabilities, drew them on the blackboard, and compared giant components and degree distributions with theoretical expectations. The authors evaluated: "it playfully connects known material, such as dice rolls and probabilities, with new concepts, such as random graphs." It also eases the transition to pseudorandom computer generation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dice and a blackboard for drawing the resulting graphs
- Prior introduction of the G(n,p) model and connection probability
#### Constraints
- The introduction of the binomial coefficient needed to explain the degree distribution was rather challenging for the majority of the pupils

### Target Learners
- high-school pupils with heterogeneous mathematics backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding random graphs and connection probability
- linking probability knowledge to network properties
