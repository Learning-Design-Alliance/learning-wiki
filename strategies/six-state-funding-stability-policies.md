# State leaders should assess six funding-stability policy areas to reduce district revenue volatility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The brief identifies six state policy areas that foster fiscal resiliency: guaranteed funding floors, rainy day funds, hold-harmless provisions for declining enrollment, reducing unfunded pension liabilities, flexible staffing and class size policies, and facilitating districts' ability to offload unused buildings. It states that "State education funding policies play a crucial role in ensuring the stability of funding for school districts, especially during economic fluctuations and periods of declining enrollment." The policies are presented not as prescriptions but as areas for state leaders to assess against their own context, listed in order of highest potential impact without undue burden on the state.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State leaders assess their current state-level policies in these areas to determine whether their districts are vulnerable to revenue volatility
- Leaders within each state determine which policies have the highest impact and are most feasible and appropriate within their state's context
#### Constraints
- The policies do not represent a set of specific strategies to apply; they are intended to prompt state-level self-assessment
- Not all policies will make sense for every state, and any policies to support revenue stability will have some drawbacks

### Target Learners
- State education leaders and policymakers
- K-12 students in districts facing revenue volatility

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining stable educational services and staffing during revenue declines and economic downturns
