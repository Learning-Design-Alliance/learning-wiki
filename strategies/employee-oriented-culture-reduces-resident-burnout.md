# Build employee-oriented hospital culture to reduce resident burnout and support training performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that hospitals establish an employee-oriented organizational culture in which residents are trusted, empowered, and treated equally. It states that "Hospitals should respect, help, and actively communicate with residents" and that "EOC should be established to reduce the occurrence of training burnout and improve residents' performance", motivated by residents' long working hours, heavy workload, and highly intensive work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hospital management adheres to the concept of respect for employees, allowing residents to participate extensively in clinical activities
#### Constraints
- The moderating benefit for punishment-based leadership applies in low-EOC contexts; EOC showed no moderating effect on reward behavior

### Target Learners
- resident physicians in standardized residency training
- clinical teachers and hospital administrators

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining resident well-being and training performance
- reducing resident burnout
