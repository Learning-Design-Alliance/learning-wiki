# Deliver short-term, intensive, simulation-based infectious disease emergency training with frequent drills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends personalized, practical training for nurses built around short durations and experiential methods: nurses "prefer training programs lasting 3 days to a week", and scenario-based simulation and emergency drills were the most selected methods. The authors argue such training enables personnel to gain emergency management experience, identify personal weaknesses, and improve future practice, and recommend strengthening ongoing drills and training programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support for regular drills and training; training designed around nurses' stated duration and method preferences
#### Constraints
- The article notes future research should explore the specific effects of various training types, so the most effective strategies are not yet identified

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in public hospitals, especially those lacking drill and training experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Infectious disease emergency response competence, including symptom identification, epidemic reporting, and handling sudden outbreaks
