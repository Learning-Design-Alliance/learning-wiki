# Use self-directed simulator practice as an adjunct to early colonoscopy training, with future curricula adding targeted instructor feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend structured simulator-based practice as a low-risk adjunct in early training environments where supervised clinical exposure is constrained, and suggest that "future curricula may benefit from combining self‐directed simulator practice with targeted instructor feedback." They also recommend integrating simulator-based insertion training with structured teaching on withdrawal quality and lesion recognition, since the two address different components of colonoscopy quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulator sessions with objective feedback; the trial's curriculum lacked expert coaching, individualized learning goals, and proficiency-based progression
#### Constraints
- The primary between-group endpoint was not significant, so the evidence is preliminary; durability beyond the first 15 post-training procedures is uncertain; extrapolation beyond Asian training environments warrants further study

### Target Learners
- early-stage gastroenterology trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- colonoscope insertion skill acquisition during the early learning phase
