# Use semantic mapping as a vocabulary teaching device in EFL classes to build deeper vocabulary knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that EFL teachers teach vocabulary through semantic mapping — descriptive or thematic maps, spider maps, problem and solution maps, and fishbone maps — instead of, or alongside, usual vocabulary instruction such as synonyms, definitions, dictionaries, and first-language equivalents. According to the discussion, "Teachers can make use of teaching vocabularies in semantic mappi ng way as a teaching device in th eir classes", because mapping creates new contexts, makes learning more interesting, and fosters deeper vocabulary knowledge for appropriate use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 45-minute portion of class time dedicated to grouping vocabulary items using graphic organizers, with teacher assistance fading to group work
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single study of 60 female adult intermediate learners at one language school in Tehran

### Target Learners
- intermediate EFL learners
- adult language learners

### Target Learning Goals
- general vocabulary achievement
- deep vocabulary knowledge in its evaluative and potency dimensions
