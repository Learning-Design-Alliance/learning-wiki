# Student Choice

## Description
Incorporating student choice involves allowing learners to make decisions about their learning experiences, such as selecting which strategy to use in problem-solving, choosing how to demonstrate their knowledge, or deciding the sequence of their activities. This empowers students to take responsibility for their learning, develop independence, and become self-directed learners. It fosters engagement, deeper thinking, and a sense of autonomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be willing to relinquish some control and trust students to make appropriate choices. Requires careful planning to ensure choices are aligned with learning objectives. Requires resources and materials to support a variety of choices.
#### Constraints
- If not implemented thoughtfully, student choice can lead to disengagement or a lack of focus. Some students may struggle to make choices effectively. Requires careful monitoring and support from the teacher.

### Affordances
- Enhances motivation by giving students a sense of control over their learning. Allows students to connect learning to their interests. Supports self-regulation and self-directed learning.

### Personalization
- Offer a variety of choices to cater to different learning styles and interests. Provide support and guidance to help students make informed choices. Allow students to reflect on their choices and their impact on learning.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student engagement and motivation levels. Collect student feedback on their choices. Assess student learning outcomes to determine the impact of choice on academic performance.

## Impact
- Research shows that student choice can increase motivation, engagement, and achievement. When students feel a sense of ownership over their learning, they are more likely to invest time and effort. A meta-analysis of research on choice in the classroom found a positive effect on student achievement (Patall et al., 2008).
