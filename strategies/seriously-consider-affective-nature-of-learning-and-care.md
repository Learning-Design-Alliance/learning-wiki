# Treat the affective nature of learning and care as a serious design consideration in intelligent learning systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends that the field of intelligent learning systems take the affective side of learning seriously rather than addressing only learners' cognitive abilities. It concludes 'there are exciting opportunities ahead in the field if we consider the affective nature of learning and care more seriously'. In practice this means extending system definitions of care with affective research from neuroscience and education, and designing for profound empathy rather than cognitive concern alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers need an elaborate definition of care that spans affective and cognitive aspects
#### Constraints
- Current systems' heavy cognitive focus means affective capability must be added rather than assumed

### Target Learning Goals
- affective engagement, empathy and care in learning interactions
