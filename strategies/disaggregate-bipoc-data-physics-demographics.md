# Disaggregate BIPOC data into Black and Indigenous subgroups when analysing physics-community demographics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future analyses of physics-community demographics break the broad BIPOC category into identity-specific groups, because stark representation differences between Black, Indigenous, and POC respondents "challenged the notions of BIPOC homogeneity". The authors state: "We therefore recommend that future researchers adopt a similar disaggregating approach when analysing data about the physics community in Canada, so that the most notable disparities are evident." This strategy makes disparities visible that broad-category aggregation conceals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Survey instruments must collect race/ethnicity at a granularity allowing Black, Indigenous, and POC subgroups to be separated, with anonymity safeguards for small groups
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges collapsing identities helps maintain anonymity and statistical power, and that grouping can perpetuate perceptions of BIPOC homogeneity

### Target Learners
- post-secondary physics students
- physics faculty and professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- equity, diversity, and inclusion in physics education and careers
