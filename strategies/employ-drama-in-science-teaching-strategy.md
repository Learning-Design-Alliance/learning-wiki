# Employ drama in teaching science to improve concept understanding and attitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study recommends employing drama in science teaching, specifically physical-simulation drama in which students use their bodies as models of non-observable entities. The author concludes that "drama has positive effects on improving students' understanding of scientific concepts and it has positive effects on changing students' attitudes towards science learning as well", and views drama-based science learning as a promising long-run strategy. Drama activities were also designed to create a collaborative learning context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Drama activities adapted to the specific scientific concepts being taught
- Teacher guidance through the dramatic scenes
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single quasi-experiment with only male students in one public school; the author calls for studies on female students and on other variables such as understanding of the nature of science

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade science students
- average and low achievers in science

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding abstract scientific concepts
- positive attitudes towards science learning
