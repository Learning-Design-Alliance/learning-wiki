# Develop tailored simulation-based training programs for single-incision surgical systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that unfamiliarity with single-site instrumentation can be addressed by building training programs tailored to the device, delivered through surgical simulators using standardized tasks. The authors state "a possible way to overcome such challenges could be the development of tailored training programs through simulation methods", framing simulation as "an effective way to deliver training, achieve mastery and skills". This follows their finding that SPIDER performance lagged conventional laparoscopy in both experience groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a laparoscopic box trainer or simulator with the single-site device mounted, and standardized tasks such as the FLS pegboard transfer and pattern cutting tasks
#### Constraints
- The article tested only two FLS tasks and a single test session, noting the number of reiterations needed for objective evaluation is hard to define

### Target Learners
- PGY1 general surgery residents
- experienced laparoscopic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- technical laparoscopic and single-incision surgical skills
- fundamentals of laparoscopic surgery task proficiency
