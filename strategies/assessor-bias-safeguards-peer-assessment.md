# Safeguard assessment objectivity in peer-assessment evaluations by separating teaching and evaluation roles, blinding assessors to group allocation, and standardizing assessor training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A set of procedural safeguards for keeping student assessments objective when an educational innovation is evaluated in a real-world course. The article reports that "a functional separation was established between teaching and evaluation roles", that assessors "were blinded to the students' group allocation", and that the identical core assessment team received standardized training on structured checklists, calibrating rating criteria and bias avoidance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An identical core assessment team across compared groups, with standardized assessor training focused on structured checklists, calibrating rating criteria, and recognizing strategies for bias avoidance.
#### Constraints
- The assessment tools were self-developed for formative assessment of this specific course and, per the authors, have not undergone comprehensive psychometric validation.

### Target Learners
- International medical students undergoing clinical skills assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- Fair and objective assessment of clinical examination competencies
