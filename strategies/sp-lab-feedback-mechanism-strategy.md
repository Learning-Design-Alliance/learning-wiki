# Build an explicit feedback mechanism into standardised-patient labs, since SPs cannot give skill feedback and its absence hinders learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because SPs are "trained to perform" and cannot comment on skill correctness, the article argues programs using SPs "there needs to be a feedback mechanism in which the student can adequately learn and seek further help." Lack of feedback was the most common negative theme (7/21 students), and the authors link feedback to self-efficacy, self-regulation and performance gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism by which students can receive feedback on skill correctness and seek further help, complementing the SP's professional/communication feedback
#### Constraints
- SPs instructed to respond as patients cannot pre-empt or hint at correct procedures, so skill feedback must come from another source

### Target Learners
- health professional students practising clinical skills with standardised patients

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills development
- self-regulated improvement of assessment technique
