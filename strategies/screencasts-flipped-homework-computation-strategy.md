# Use short screencasts plus flipped-classroom homework to deliver computational tool instruction without lecture time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recorded screencasts of an instructor working through problems to teach computational tools such as root finders (FindRoot) and numerical differential equation solvers (NDSolve), delivered through homework rather than lecture. They report that "Less than one hour of planning, recording, and editing was required to produce a single, polished five minute screencast." Early homework problems linked directly to helpful screencasts; later sets made no mention of them as tasks became familiar.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A common course schedule that leaves lecture time untouched, and homework problems developed from the computational learning goals
#### Constraints
- A lack of faculty expertise with the chosen environment (Mathematica) could make drawing from the limited resources challenging; screencasts existed only for Mathematica at the time of writing

### Target Learners
- middle-division undergraduate physics students in large lecture courses with no laboratory or recitation section

### Target Learning Goals
- basic fluency with computational tools for root finding, numerical ODE solving, plotting, and algebraic manipulation

## Claims

- [Final project performance was on par with homework and exam grades, with students strongest on physics, model, and working code and weakest on evaluating outcomes and synthesis](../claims/final-project-rubric-performance-pattern.md) [+M]
