Implement a web of supports for students in foster care: one-stop resource centers, school-based liaisons, tiered services, and cross-system collaboration¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends building "a ready web of supports" so students in foster care receive help promptly. Promising practices identified by coordinators include developing one-stop resource centers, co-locating education and child welfare staff, employing school-based liaisons trained to support students in foster care, and providing targeted social, emotional, and academic services within a tiered system of support. Policy recommendations include cross-agency structures such as a children's cabinet, community schools, local interagency transportation agreements, LCFF revision, state grant programs for collaborative case management, relationship-centered school practices, and professional development in trauma-informed, restorative, and social-emotional practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Effective coordination and collaboration of agencies and organizations at multiple levels
  • Data systems capable of connecting fragmented education and child welfare data

Constraints¶

  • The report notes LCFF does not necessarily provide additional resources for students in foster care, and transportation time and costs can make school-of-origin attendance challenging

Target Learners¶

  • Students in foster care in K–12 schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • School stability and engagement
  • Academic achievement
  • College access
  • Social-emotional and behavioral support

Related Strategies¶

  • Assign tiered fiscal responsibility: education funds Tier 1 and some Tier 2 supports, while the health system funds expanded Tier 2 and Tier 3 services
  • Develop data-sharing memoranda of understanding and shared evaluation across child- and family-serving agencies
  • Use linked cross-system data to target programs such as dropout prevention and postsecondary preparation for students in foster care
  • Broker a formal data-sharing agreement between child welfare and education agencies to identify and serve students in foster care
  • Use an early warning system based on linked school and human services data to target resources and lessen risks before they become more serious
  • Sustain cross-agency collaboration through a dedicated district leader and monthly partner meetings
