Provide explicit process prompting (including picture-to-text transcription guidance) when visual storytelling precedes writing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends explicit prompting at several points in the visual-storytelling-to-writing process. Users did not spontaneously use close-ups or extend initial sequences, and "the lack of resequencing and use of close-ups described above shows that users do need explicit prompting if they are to deploy a fuller range of visual techniques". The conclusion further suggests participants lacked picture-to-text transcription strategies, so explicit guidance for translating expressive visual choices into text, such as the software-mediated storywriting guidance of the HARRY system, may be valuable.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prompts must be made prominent enough that users consistently pay attention to them, since the current low-key design went almost unused

Constraints¶

  • Effects on written stories were not demonstrated in this study; the prompts' value remains to be tested
  • Feedback prompts for visual revision had not yet been developed, only feedforward prompts

Target Learners¶

  • primary school pupils aged 9 to 11 using cartooning or visual storytelling tools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • deploying a fuller range of visual techniques such as close-ups and shot sequencing
  • translating visual expression into written stories

Claims¶

  • No preparation process (software cartooning, paper cartooning, or teacher's normal planning) produced clearly different written stories [~W]
  • In the Ramayana sub-study, software-group stories scored higher for strong descriptions of characters or actions at both story stages [+W]
