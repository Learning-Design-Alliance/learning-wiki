Train normal peers, teachers and parents in peer tutoring to support children with ASD¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations call for building capacity around the child with ASD: training normal peers in non-verbal communication methods and including them with children with ASD, and "Conducting training courses to train teachers and p arents of children with autism spectrum disorder(ASD) on peer tutoring strategy." It also recommends more studies in other centers on verbal and non-verbal communication outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Peers must be trained in non-verbal communication methods before inclusion with children with ASD

Constraints¶

  • The authors note the need for further studies in other centers to investigate peer tutoring efficiency

Target Learners¶

  • children with autism spectrum disorder

Target Learning Goals¶

  • verbal and non-verbal communication

Claims¶

  • Peer tutoring produced statistically significant post-test gains in non-verbal communication for children with ASD relative to a control group [+M]
  • Non-verbal communication gains persisted at a two-month follow-up, with no statistically significant decline from post-test [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use peer tutoring as the recommended collaborative learning style after fading distinguishes expert and non-expert peers
  • Implement formal peer tutor training programs under faculty supervision within simulation curricula
  • Family Engagement Supporting Children With Disabilities
