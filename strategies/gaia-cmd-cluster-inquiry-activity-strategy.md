# Scaffolded guided-inquiry activity in which students build color-magnitude diagrams of star clusters from Gaia data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-hour class activity in which students formulate investigative questions about the relative ages, distances, and sizes of open and globular clusters, query the IRSA Gaia catalog using SIMBAD-derived angular sizes and parallax ranges, filter the data, and produce and interpret CMDs. It uses "a scaffolded guided inquiry style where questions are suggested or given", with exact plot syntax and field names provided so students can produce a CMD they can interpret.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior experience with right ascension, declination, angular size, HRD/CMD interpretation, apparent and absolute magnitude, and the distance modulus relationship
#### Constraints
- The activity was designed for a single hour-long session but often took two sessions for most students
- Students will not produce valuable results without scaffolding and guidance limiting the data

### Target Learners
- high school Astronomy 101 students with mixed math expertise and little computing experience

### Target Learning Goals
- applying knowledge of how astronomers determine cluster ages and characterize stellar populations
- statistical problem-solving with authentic data
