Use unobtrusive AI facial expression recognition alongside qualitative interaction coding to study emotion regulation in collaborative learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends combining AI-derived emotion measures with human-coded regulatory episodes to capture emotion regulation processes that surveys and interviews miss. The authors align FER output with 30-second coded segments and triangulate: "efforts have been made in the data analysis phase which employed external coders to evaluate and infer a person's subjectively experienced emotions to verify results." This methodological pairing is offered as a grounding for further development of AI in education to support learning regulation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Session video recordings with cameras on; a qualitative coding scheme for regulatory episodes; external coders to verify FER-derived emotion inference

Constraints¶

  • Technical issues may arise during platform-based recording, and facial expression may not fully reflect regulatory processes; the 30-second segment unit may need finer granularity

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education students in online collaborative learning

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Examining and supporting emotional regulation and socially shared regulation of learning

Claims¶

  • Surprise is the most frequently shared emotion among learners in synchronous online collaborative English discussion tasks [+W]
  • Emotional synchrony frequency differs slightly across regulatory activities but the metric does not discriminate SSRL, cognitive, and task-execution interactions [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Broaden co-regulation methodology using trace data, server logs, episodic coding and multilevel modelling
  • Assess social and group flow in real time through non-intrusive observation instead of interrupting learners with self-report surveys
  • Use multimodal data channels combining subjective and objective measures to overcome single-channel constraints in SSRL research
  • Triangulate physiological data with other CSCL data streams ( such as video or logfile traces ) to uncover otherwise unidentifiable psychophysiological reactions
