Explicitly teach discourse markers and compare English DMs with learners' first language¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that instructors explicitly teach the meanings and functions of discourse markers in different contexts, drawing on findings that explicit DM instruction benefits second language learners. It states that "it is recommended that the instructor compare the DMs in English with those in the students' first language", following Moradan (1995) and Nunan. The treatment in the study modeled this by presenting frequently used markers from authentic texts with the roles each plays in creating a meaningful text.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors should emphasize text markers that are frequently used and prevalent in written texts of any nature

Constraints¶

  • The article notes much more investigation is necessary to determine the precise role of learner training in improving writing skill

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners at intermediate level
  • Secondary and university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Writing ability
  • Awareness of discourse and cohesion
  • Appropriate use of discourse markers

Affordances¶

  • Dm Conjunctive Type Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Explicit discourse-markers instruction improves EFL learners' ability to produce cohesive and coherent text on discourse cloze tests [+M]
  • Learners' overuse and misuse of discourse markers is attributed to first-language translation, and uninstructed learners may overuse DMs believing this makes text more cohesive [+W]
  • Thai students overuse the causal connective because, a pattern the article attributes to L1 influence [+W]
  • L2 writers tend to overuse more discourse connectives than they underuse, and both L1 and L2 students have a limited range of DCs at their disposal [+W]
  • Post-test cloze means were 15.01 (control) versus 18.75 (experimental) after DM instruction [+M]
