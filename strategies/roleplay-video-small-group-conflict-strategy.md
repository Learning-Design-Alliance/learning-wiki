# Use role-play with video recording and small group discussion to teach interprofessional conflict communication

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The training replaced conventional lecture with autonomous student role-play: groups filmed good or bad examples of conflict resolution, peers evaluated performers on a worksheet, and facilitators led discussions on how communication could improve. The authors argue this combination "contributed to maximizing the educational effect," citing literature that role-play fosters empathy for each role and that more small group discussion improves students' reasoning skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups, a facilitator per room, two conflict cases, video recording, and worksheets for evaluating performers' communication
#### Constraints
- With 12–13 students per group, some students could only watch rather than perform; each group covered only one case and one good-or-bad variant

### Target Learners
- Medical students in early clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Interpersonal communication competence in interprofessional conflict situations
