# Provide specific support to preterm children with scholastic difficulties whose parents separated at elementary school age

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that separations between 3 and 5 years of age were associated with decreased school performance only among preterm children already exhibiting difficulties, the authors recommend targeted support. As stated in the Conclusions: "specific support could be given to young children who were delivered preterm who exhibit scholastic difficulties and whose parents separated when they were of elementary school-age." The strategy targets the subgroup at highest risk rather than all preterm children, since the effect was confined to children with low GSA scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of preterm children with scholastic difficulties (low GSA performance) whose parents separated between 3 and 5 years after birth.
#### Constraints
- The article does not specify what form the support should take or test its effectiveness; it is a recommendation following an observational association, not a tested intervention.

### Target Learners
- preterm children aged 3 to 5 years with scholastic difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- school performance, motivation, autonomy, and manual dexterity
