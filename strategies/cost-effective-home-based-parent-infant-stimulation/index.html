Cost-effective, home-based parent-delivered early stimulation for infants¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that a cost-effective intervention programme could enable parents to provide educational stimulation for their infants in a home environment, since intervention at such an early age does not form part of the formal education system. It suggests that support from private and non-government organisations to parents and primary caregivers would imply a potentially broader implementation of the Numbers in Nappies programme, which it describes as intellectually and economically accessible across socioeconomic sectors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parents or primary caregivers undergo training in the programme and deliver the daily sessions themselves

Constraints¶

  • The study recommends a more structured evaluation of the implementation of the programme by the parents, and notes results should not be generalised without replication on a larger and more representative sample

Target Learners¶

  • infants aged 3–12 months in home settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive processing potential
  • social–emotional functioning

Claims¶

  • Social–emotional functioning of intervention infants rose numerically from the high to the very superior range after the programme, which the authors attribute to added parent stimulation [+W]
  • After the intervention, the intervention group scored significantly higher than the control group on the Cognitive Scale, with no between-group differences before the intervention [+M]
  • Infants exposed to the Numbers in Nappies programme showed a statistically significant increase on the BSID (III) Cognitive Scale from before to after the intervention [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Interventions aimed at increasing parents' verbal engagement with their infants to strengthen processing skills and vocabulary growth
  • Deliver low-intensity parental engagement support via short, actionable school text messages rather than apps or face-to-face sessions
  • Non-intensive Home Numeracy Environment (HNE) Intervention Program
  • Promote a consistent bedtime routine as a feasible, cost-effective method to support early childhood development, particularly for at-risk children
