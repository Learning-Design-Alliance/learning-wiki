Use hypertext gloss software with clear task goals and salient, carefully consulted annotations¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

For CALL reading tasks, the article recommends a preliminary and clear statement of the task goal, specification of the steps underlying the task, and instructions that push students into careful consulting of annotations. It states that "The layers in the process of achieving the task must be specified and made easily perceptible to the learner. They must be perceptually salient." This is presented as supporting immediate retention and longer-term retention.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clear statement of the goal of the task to the student
  • perceptually salient task layers
  • instructions focusing students on consulting annotations

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate EFL learners in CALL environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary learning through hypertext reading tasks

Claims¶

  • The intentional group accessed 20.6% more glosses than the incidental group without corresponding test gains [~W]
  • Earlier meta-analyses, as reported second-hand, find that glosses improve L2 vocabulary learning and that text-and-picture glosses outperform single-mode glosses [+M]
  • Multimedia glosses improve EFL students' immediate vocabulary acquisition relative to reading the same texts without glosses [+W]
