# Scaffold open-ended computational projects with seed simulations, an example essay, and staffed helpdesk hours

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the studied implementation, open-ended computational essay projects were scaffolded by providing a project description, an example essay, and basic Jupyter-notebook simulations of electricity and magnetism phenomena. "These simulations were meant to act as “seeds” for the student projects", which students were encouraged to augment and build off. The first author also "staffed a special “helpdesk hour” for 2 hours twice a week" to answer questions about programming, physics, or project expectations, and students presented essays to peers in mock research-group meetings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Seed simulations spanning standard computational physics methods, an example essay, and staffed help hours during the project period; students worked individually or in pairs over approximately 4-6 weeks.
#### Constraints
- This scaffolding was used in a program where computation is already integrated throughout the curriculum since 2003, which the authors describe as a unique setting.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and engineering majors

### Target Learning Goals
- conducting open-ended computational investigations
- communicating computational work
