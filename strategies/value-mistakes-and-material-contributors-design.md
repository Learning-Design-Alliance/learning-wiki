# Design computational learning environments to treat seeming mistakes as expansion possibilities and surface material contributions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's findings inform design decisions for computational learning environments built on human-material collaboration. The article recommends "valuing seeming mistakes as a possibility for expanding conceptual productions within the learning environment," as when the gap pattern's obstructed heddle reorganized the algorithm. It also guides design toward facilitating student awareness of how materials contribute to computational design, and toward studying what youth learn when they identify materials as collaborators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning environments that integrate tangible materials whose material constraints can reshape algorithmic activity, such as looms and fabric
#### Constraints
- Implications are drawn from a single qualitative study in one charter school setting, so transfer is framed as future research relevance

### Target Learners
- middle school youth, especially those deciding whether to pursue STEM

### Target Learning Goals
- learning about algorithms and collaborative computational production
