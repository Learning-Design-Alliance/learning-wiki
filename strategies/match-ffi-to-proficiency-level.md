# Match FFI intensity and content to learners' proficiency level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the study found FFI more beneficial to intermediate than advanced learners, the authors recommend that syllabus designers and material developers incorporate FFI principles into instructional materials designed for particular proficiency levels. The article states such designers "can also benefit from the findinds of this study by incorporating the principles of FFI into their instructional materials designed for particular levels of proficiency."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional packages must be developed at a difficulty compatible with each proficiency level, as the study did with separate intermediate and advanced grammar materials.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one quasi-experimental study in an Iranian EFL context; the authors note advanced learners may face a ceiling effect.

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and advanced adult EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Grammatical knowledge development
