# Districts adopting intensive data-based instruction should secure teacher support and flexibility to resolve assessment-curriculum misalignment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report recommends that districts or schools considering an intensive data-based instructional program work with providers to identify what successful implementation requires, and that leaders ensure teachers get help when interim assessments misalign with curricula. Specifically, "teachers are provided with support to address it through planning and the freedom to reorganize their instruction, so that the assessment data they receive is instructionally useful." This follows from the finding that perceived alignment tracked teacher confidence and flexibility. Providers may also narrow focus to ready schools or adapt services for lower-capacity schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Providers and districts must identify implementation prerequisites before adoption; leaders must supply planning support and freedom to reorganize instruction when misalignment arises.
#### Constraints
- As an evaluation of one program in particular settings, the study cannot speak to the efficacy of data-based instructional programs generally.

### Target Learners
- students in Grades 3-8 in high-need schools

### Target Learning Goals
- making interim assessment data instructionally useful for closing student learning gaps
