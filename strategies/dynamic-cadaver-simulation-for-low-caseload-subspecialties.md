# Use dynamic perfused-cadaver simulation to overcome insufficient annual caseload in subspecialized surgical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that subspecialty caseloads during training are insufficient (for example, most adrenal centers performed less than 5 procedures per year) and that simulation could serve "as a core component of the modern surgical apprentice", allowing repetition of technical gestures without compromising patient safety and letting trainees detect and analyze errors and practice solving critical intraoperative situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a hyper-realistic dynamic model such as SimLife
- Availability of donated cadavers and dedicated budget for the perfusion module
#### Constraints
- Junior trainees would benefit more from simpler types of models, per the authors' stated limitation

### Target Learners
- Senior trainees subspecializing in endocrine surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- Mastering technical skills and managing critical intraoperative situations before operating on patients
