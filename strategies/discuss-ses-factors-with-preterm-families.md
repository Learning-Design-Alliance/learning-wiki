# Incorporate socioeconomic factors into outcome discussions with families of preterm infants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinicians and researchers treat maternal social factors as central when discussing preterm children's academic prognosis. It concludes that "Favorable social factors could possible overcome the adverse effects of prematurity" and that "The role of socioeconomic factors should be incorporated in discussions on outcome with families of preterm infants." The recommendation rests on the cohort finding that social factors outweighed prematurity in predicting achievement-test scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of a child's maternal SES and education level, as the study used insurance status and education level as its social-factor measures
#### Constraints
- The article states the role of social factors must also be considered in future studies evaluating the impact of prematurity on outcomes

### Target Learners
- preterm and extremely preterm (ELGAN) school-age children

### Target Learning Goals
- literacy and mathematics achievement from grade 3 through grade 8
