# Pair handmade-phantom training with post-training clinical observation and procedure-specific retraining, with standardized videos as an alternative for rare procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation training with handmade phantoms be followed by clinical observation of the actual procedure to maintain skills, and that rare, complex procedures such as pericardiocentesis need more frequent retraining, extended simulation sessions, or standardized videos in place of observation. It states that "the observed differences in learning suggest that training for pericardiocentesis may need to be conducted more frequently, incorporate extended simulation sessions, or utilize standardized pericardiocentesis videos as an alternative to clinical observation". Retraining intervals may need to differ between thoracocentesis and pericardiocentesis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Documentation of learners' clinical exposure to the procedures after training
- Determination of optimal retraining intervals, which the article flags as requiring further research
#### Constraints
- Handmade low-fidelity phantoms may not be adequate for complex, less frequently performed procedures such as pericardiocentesis
- Long-term retention and transfer to real patient care were not evaluated

### Target Learners
- Novice physicians and medical students learning US-guided procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of procedural skill after simulation training
- Prevention of skill decay for rarely performed procedures
