# Guided Math

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Guided Math organizes mathematics instruction around short, focused [small-group](../elements/small-group-instruction.md) lessons in which the teacher works directly with 4–6 students while the remainder of the class rotates through independent stations, games, and practice tasks. Groups are formed and re-formed based on ongoing assessment data, so instruction targets each student's current level of understanding rather than a single whole-class pace.

## Design Implications

Guided Math operationalizes [formative assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) and differentiated instruction: the teacher diagnoses misconceptions in real time and adjusts explanation and task difficulty within the group, which is difficult to do for 30 students at once. The station rotation structure depends on independent work that students can complete without help, so tasks must be within reach of working memory limits [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M] and free of unnecessary extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brief whole-class mini-lesson that establishes the day's focus before groups split
- Valid, quick assessment data (exit tickets, observation notes) used to form flexible groups
- Independent station tasks that are self-explanatory, self-checking where possible, and previously taught
- Clear routines and expectations so students can work without teacher support for 10–20 minutes
- Teacher-led group time that includes [modeling](../elements/demonstration.md), guided practice, and immediate [feedback](../elements/feedback.md)

#### Constraints
- Off-task behavior at unguided stations erodes the benefit; the structure fails without well-established routines and appropriately leveled independent work [-M]
- Fixed "ability groups" can entrench low expectations and widen gaps; groups must be flexible and re-formed frequently [~M]
- The teacher-led group receives limited minutes per week; complex new concepts usually need more sustained [direct instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) than a short guided session provides [~M]
- Requires substantial preparation of differentiated materials; poorly designed stations become busywork [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Guided Math with rotation (Sammons, 2010):** full station-rotation model with a schedule board, analogous to [Daily 5](../strategies/daily-5.md) structures in literacy
- **CGI-informed guided groups:** group work built around students' invented strategies and problem types, as in [Cognitively Guided Instruction](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md)
- **Push-in co-teaching model:** two teachers split the class so guided groups run simultaneously with shorter rotations
- **Digital stations:** adaptive platforms (e.g., [Zearn](https://www.zearn.org), [DreamBox](https://www.dreambox.com)) deliver leveled practice at one station while the teacher works with a group

### Target Learners
- Elementary students, where the structure originated and has the strongest evidence base [~M]
- Struggling students who need targeted reteaching in a low-stakes small setting [contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Advanced students, who can be grouped for extension rather than waiting on whole-class pacing
- Less effective without adaptation in very large classes where independent routines cannot be sustained

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: targeted practice with immediate correction
- Conceptual repair: diagnosing and addressing specific misconceptions
- Mathematical discourse: small-group settings lower the cost of explaining reasoning aloud

### Instructions
1. Assess current understanding with a short task or exit ticket and form flexible groups.
2. Deliver a 10–15 minute whole-class mini-lesson introducing the day's concept.
3. Rotate students through stations: teacher-led guided group, independent practice, math games or manipulatives, and (optionally) a digital practice station.
4. In the guided group, begin with a [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), move to guided practice with prompts, then release students to independent problems within the group.
5. Use observation notes from group time to re-form groups and plan the next cycle.

- **CGI classrooms (Carpenter et al.)** — teachers use knowledge of problem types and student strategy levels to target guided group questions
