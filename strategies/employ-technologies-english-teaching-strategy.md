# Employ new technologies in teaching English language skills and incorporate them into syllabi and teaching practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that computer-assisted grammar teaching outperformed traditional instruction, the researcher recommends "Employing the new technologies in teaching English language skills and other components of language" and encouraging students to work independently and devise language activities. A second recommendation is "Incorporating the new technologies in the syllabi and in teaching practices and citing websites relevant to language skills". A third advises teachers to vary methods and techniques according to students' needs and interests.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer labs and infrastructure; the article notes Jordan's Ministry of Education provided computer labs connected to the World Wide Web
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is one quasi-experiment on a single grammatical topic with 212 pupils in two schools

### Target Learners
- secondary EFL pupils in Jordan

### Target Learning Goals
- English language skills and grammatical structures
