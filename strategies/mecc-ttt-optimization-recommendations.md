# Optimizing MECC TtT delivery: skills-based experiential training, clear cascade expectations, trainer flexibility, and quality-based evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends action at programme, individual and organizational levels: "it would be helpful to reconsider the pedagogical approach to make it more accessible to non‐health care settings", review the reliance on theory and statistics in favour of a more reflective, skills-based approach such as HCS, allow trainers flexibility to tailor and own content, secure top-down organizational support enabling actual cascade, and measure impact rather than just numbers trained. Clear expectations of cascade should be given at sign-up stage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prospective MECC trainers should have teaching experience, passion for the topic, and managerial support before attending, per recommendations cited from the wider literature
- Post-programme support including peer support, technical support and follow-up
#### Constraints
- Flexibility to amend content was hampered by trainer concerns about compromising training quality and consistency, with a lack of guidance in navigating such worries

### Target Learners
- MECC TtT trainees and principal trainers across health care, local authority and voluntary and community settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Delivering high-quality MECC training and successfully cascading it within organizations
