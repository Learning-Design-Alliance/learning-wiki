# Use VR resectoscopic simulation to measure and improve novice technical skills before operating on patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends virtual reality resectoscopic systems as a way to let novices measure and improve technical skills outside the operating room, avoiding surgical risk to patients and consumption of operating room resources and time. Unlike time-intensive tissue laboratories, VR systems can, at least theoretically, be available to busy residents on a daily or nightly basis, potentially supporting more sustained retention of learned skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prescribed training program with supervised orientation and repeated practice runs, as in the study's two-phase design
#### Constraints
- The study was not designed to evaluate whether simulator training reduces operating room time, so that conclusion cannot be made; competencies such as resectoscope assembly, angled lenses, fluid management, and specimen removal are not covered

### Target Learners
- Novice hysteroscopists
- Obstetrics and gynecology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical hysteroscopic and resectoscopic surgical skills acquired safely before patient contact
