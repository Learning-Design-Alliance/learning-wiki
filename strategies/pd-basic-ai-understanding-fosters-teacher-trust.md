# Build teachers' basic AI understanding and self-efficacy through professional development to foster trust in AI-EdTech

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teacher training and professional development that develop a grounded, realistic understanding of AI as a tool — moving from unrealistic visions such as autonomous robots to realistic ones such as algorithms for decision-making — and activities that enhance self-efficacy. As the article puts it: "Teacher training and PD initiatives should aim to improve basic understanding of AI, rather than provide in-depth courses on AI foundations and techniques." PD should equip teachers with technological and pedagogical knowledge about AI-EdTech and address common misconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PD should focus on basic AI understanding and address misconceptions, drawing on emerging AI literacy frameworks and ethical principles for K-12 AI use
#### Constraints
- The article notes teaching K-12 teachers about AI is challenging and not yet prevalent in formal school settings, and that its evidence is a cross-sectional snapshot from non-representative convenience samples

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers across subjects and career stages

### Target Learning Goals
- Realistic understanding of AI and AI-EdTech; self-efficacy with AI-EdTech; willingness to adopt AI-EdTech in classrooms
