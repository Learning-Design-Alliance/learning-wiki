# Combine hands-on experiential engagement with peer-led teaching to build disposal knowledge and advocacy in cascade

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a structured, student-centred model in which students first gain real-world experience in medication take-back, reflect on it, then teach peers, creating "a cascading effect, broadening the reach of safe medication disposal education and fostering long-term behaviour change". Phase 1 experiences directly shaped Phase 2 workshop content, ensuring alignment between learners' identified needs and the educational focus. The authors argue this empowers future pharmacists to advocate for responsible disposal within their communities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with community pharmacies to co-design structured engagement programs, including mentorship and practical training
- Structured reflection built into the experiential phase
#### Constraints
- Self-selection bias in both phases; small samples (Phase 1: n = 35; Phase 2: n = 43); single national context (Malaysia) limits generalizability; no control group and only an immediate post-test

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- safe medication disposal knowledge
- public health advocacy
- environmental sustainability awareness
