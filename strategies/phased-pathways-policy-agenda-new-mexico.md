# Phased policy agenda for building a statewide pathways system: immediate low-cost COVID-recovery steps followed by longer-term investments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report lays out ten recommendations in two phases. Immediate low-cost steps include developing virtual CTE curriculum and remote-teaching capacity, convening a task force to build the state pathways framework, establishing quality and equity standards, adopting monitoring metrics integrated with existing data systems, and amending the Graduation Requirements Statute (§ 22-13-1.1) so CTE courses can satisfy some academic requirements and a college- and career-readiness diploma seal can be earned. Longer-term steps include demonstrations in 6 to 10 districts, incentivizing dual-credit articulation without double-funding, incentivizing employer engagement and work-based learning (including adapting the Job Training Incentive Program, which "reimburses 50–75% of wages of newly hired, eligible trainees for up to 6 months"), building educator capacity with dual academic and CTE credentials, and establishing a public–private College and Career Pathways Trust.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State direction and funding of the NMPED, collaboration across education, workforce, and economic development agencies, and a public–private funding partnership
#### Constraints
- Recommendations are framed within the pressures and limitations on state policy and school operations during the COVID-19 pandemic and revenue erosion

### Target Learners
- All New Mexico high school students, including those in small remote high schools

### Target Learning Goals
- College and career readiness
- High school completion and postsecondary transition
- Work-based learning participation
