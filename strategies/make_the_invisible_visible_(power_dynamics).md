# Make the Invisible Visible (Power Dynamics)

## Description
This principle focuses on bringing power dynamics to the forefront and acknowledging their presence in the design process. It involves naming the dynamics, rather than ignoring them, and understanding who has decision-making authority. It highlights different approaches to design, from user-centered design (where designers consult users) to co-design (where stakeholders become part of the design team) to user-created design (where students are entrusted with power). The principle also emphasizes the use of observation tools to assess how schools either affirm and support students in their personal identities or alienate and minimize who they are. The overall goal is to promote equitable design by challenging assumptions about power and belonging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a willingness to challenge existing power structures, as well as resources for conducting observations and facilitating discussions about power dynamics.
#### Constraints
- Can be challenging to implement if stakeholders are resistant to addressing power dynamics. Requires careful facilitation to ensure that discussions are productive and respectful.
