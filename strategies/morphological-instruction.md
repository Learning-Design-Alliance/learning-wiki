# Morphological Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Morphological instruction makes students aware of morphemes — the smallest units of meaning in words — beginning with inflections (–ed, –s, –es, –ing, –er, –est), which change number, person, or tense without changing part of speech, and extending to prefixes and derivational suffixes. It is carried out through [direct instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) on word structure, word sorting and analysis tasks, and structured [practice](../elements/practice.md) in spelling and decomposing words.

## Design Implications

Morphological instruction works because English orthography is morphophonemic: spelling encodes meaning as well as sound, so teaching learners to analyze word parts gives them a systematic basis for spelling and word meaning rather than rote memorization [Morphological interventions improve literacy outcomes.](../claims/morphological-instruction-improves-literacy.md) [+S]. Meta-analytic evidence shows interventions teaching morphemes improve word reading, spelling, vocabulary, and comprehension, with effects strongest when instruction is explicit and integrated with other literacy work [Goodwin & Ahn meta-analysis.](../claims/morphological-instruction-improves-literacy.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Word lists organized by morpheme family (e.g., *act, action, active, react*) rather than by superficial letter patterns
- Morphological charts or matrices showing how bases and affixes combine
- Explicit, systematic instruction — this is not learned incidentally from wide reading alone [Morphological interventions improve literacy outcomes.](../claims/morphological-instruction-improves-literacy.md) [+S]
- Ongoing feedback on spelling attempts with inflections ([Provide Feedback](../strategies/provide_feedback.md))

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness drops when instruction is incidental or unsystematic; scattered exposure to word parts does not produce measurable gains [Morphological interventions improve literacy outcomes.](../claims/morphological-instruction-improves-literacy.md) [-M]
- Inflectional morphology taught before learners have secure [automatic word recognition](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) can overload working memory; sequencing matters [~M]
- Overemphasis on rules with many exceptions (e.g., *y* → *i* before –ed) can produce overgeneralization errors; [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md) and contrasting cases reduce this [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- One-on-one or small-group instruction focused on the specific morphemes a learner misspells
- Whole-class word study routines (e.g., word sorts, morpheme matrices) in structured literacy programs
- Embedded in vocabulary instruction for content areas using derivational morphology (Latin and Greek roots)

### Target Learners
- Students in grades 2–6, when inflectional spelling is consolidating and derivational morphology becomes productive [Morphological interventions improve literacy outcomes.](../claims/morphological-instruction-improves-literacy.md) [+S]
- Struggling spellers and students with dyslexia, who benefit from meaning-based spelling strategies that supplement phonological decoding [~S]
- English learners, for whom morphological awareness transfers from many first languages [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Spelling accuracy with inflected and derived forms
- Vocabulary growth through recognizing shared morphemes across word families
- Word structure awareness as a foundation for reading comprehension

### Instructions
1. Select a target morpheme based on learner error analysis (e.g., past-tense –ed).
2. [Direct instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md): explicitly name the morpheme, its meaning or grammatical function, and its spelling conventions.
3. Model word analysis aloud — decompose *jumped* into *jump* + *ed* and explain why the spelling stays stable even though the pronunciation shifts.
4. Guided [practice](../elements/practice.md): word sorts, morpheme matrices, and sentence completion requiring correct inflected forms.
5. [Provide feedback](../strategies/provide_feedback.md) on spelling attempts, contrasting correct and incorrect forms ([Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md)).
6. Extend to derivational morphemes and word families to build vocabulary.

## Examples
- **Words Their Way** (Bear, Invernizzi, Templeton, & Johnston) — developmental word study program using word sorts organized by spelling and morphological patterns; widely used in grades 2–6. [https://www.pearson.com](https://www.pearson.com)
- **The Morpheme Matrix routine in structured literacy curricula** — students build words by combining bases and affixes on a matrix, testing which combinations are legal English words.
- **REWARDS** (Archer, Gleason, & Vachon) — intervention teaching multisyllabic word reading through prefixes, suffixes, and roots for intermediate and secondary students.
