# Base clinical teacher training on Bandura's four sources of self-efficacy to enhance physicians' teaching self-efficacy and their ability to detect student competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teacher trainings target both physicians' teaching self-efficacy and their ability to detect students' competencies. It states that "the main sources of self-efficacy constitute mastery experiences, positive vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion and the subjective interpretation of physiological and affective states during an action", and that "Teacher trainings should be based on these principles to effectively enhance physicians' teaching self-efficacy." This follows from the finding that self-efficacy predicted teachers' perceptions of student competencies, which in turn related to teaching quality ratings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training activities must provide mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, verbal persuasion, and attention to affective states during teaching, as the article specifies
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on an observational study in bedside teaching; the authors note their findings might be restricted to this lesson type and their assessment choices

### Target Learners
- physician clinical teachers (residents and consultants) in internal medicine bedside teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- teaching self-efficacy and the ability to detect students' competencies
