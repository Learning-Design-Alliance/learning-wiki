# Wildlife camera trap detection case study progressing from fox-versus-not-fox to individual species identification

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students use camera trap data and computer vision with Edge Impulse online tools to distinguish species, beginning with "a simple fox versus not-fox idea and expand to individual species identification using the Edge Impulse online tools". The article notes this idea is well known in conservation technology and cites EleTect as an example technique applicable to other camera-collected data. Campus and dorm surroundings are suggested as providing plentiful opportunities for this laboratory project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Check with research ethics departments, campus police, and groundskeepers before collecting data on or around campus
- Cameras placed around campus need tags for security reasons
#### Constraints
- The primary challenge or limitation of this case study is security when including camera trap information taken around campus

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in environmental or sustainability engineering

### Target Learning Goals
- applying computer vision to species identification
- understanding conservation technology applications
- designing data collection within ethical and security constraints
