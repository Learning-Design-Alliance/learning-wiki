# Staged, vertically integrated EBM curriculum mapped to the five EBM steps, with low-cost case-triggered activities for resource-constrained settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 4 mixed) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend organising EBM instruction around the five steps (Ask, Acquire, Appraise, Apply, Assess), concentrating preclinical teaching on question formulation and evidence retrieval and clerkship teaching on supervised appraisal and application. They propose a "case-triggered" activity in which students "formulate a focused clinical question, conduct a targeted search, critically appraise one relevant study using a standardized checklist, and translate findings to the index patient." In resource-constrained settings they advise open-access resources, structured search exercises, and brief case-based tasks embedded in existing teaching structures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires curricular integration of EBM training, faculty support initiatives, and improved access to scientific resources, as stated in the conclusion
#### Constraints
- Recommended for low-resource medical education systems; the Assess step is operationalized through low-burden clerkship routines such as brief reflective notes and simple audit-and-feedback sessions

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students (second to sixth year) in low- and middle-income settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidence-based medicine competencies: formulating clinical questions, retrieving evidence, critical appraisal, and application to patient care
