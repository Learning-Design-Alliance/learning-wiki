# Sustained Silent Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Sustained Silent Reading (SSR), also known as DEAR ("Drop Everything and Read") or free voluntary reading, is a regularly scheduled block — typically 10–30 minutes — during which students read self-selected texts silently. Teachers usually read alongside students, modeling reading behavior. The strategy prioritizes volume of reading and learner choice over instruction, assessment, or accountability during the reading period itself.

## Design Implications

SSR rests on the premise that reading volume drives vocabulary growth, fluency, and background knowledge, and that choice sustains the motivation to read [~M]. The evidence is genuinely mixed: correlational studies consistently associate voluntary reading volume with comprehension and vocabulary, but the National Reading Panel found that SSR programs without instructional components produced no measurable comprehension gains relative to comparison conditions [-S]. The most defensible interpretation is that SSR is a motivation and habit-formation structure, not a comprehension intervention — it works best as a complement to, never a substitute for, explicit instruction in word recognition and comprehension strategies.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A substantial, appealing, and varied classroom or library collection spanning difficulty levels and genres
- Genuine learner choice of text; requiring titles or assigning books converts SSR into [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md) and changes its function
- A protected, consistent time slot with no competing tasks, worksheets, or book reports
- Teacher participation — reading visibly alongside students rather than grading or monitoring
- A mechanism for students to find books they can actually read; self-selection fails when readers cannot judge text difficulty

#### Constraints
- The National Reading Panel's meta-analysis found no significant comprehension benefit from SSR alone when compared with regular instruction [-S] — reading practice without feedback does not by itself close skill gaps
- Struggling readers with weak decoding skills often fake-read or stall during SSR; unguided silent reading can leave them practicing failure [~M] — automatic word recognition is a prerequisite for silent reading to build comprehension resources [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Accountability add-ons (logs, quizzes, summaries) tend to undermine the intrinsic motivation that choice is meant to support [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M] — external evaluation shifts reading from interest-driven to compliance-driven
- Short or sporadic implementations (a few minutes, a few weeks) show no effects; benefits of reading volume accrue over years, not weeks [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Accountability-light conferencing**: brief one-to-one conversations about what students are reading, preserving choice while adding social accountability
- **Structured choice**: teacher-curated book bins or genre rotations that constrain choice to appropriate difficulty ranges
- **Audiobook-supported reading**: pairing print with narration for striving readers ([Audiobooks](../principles/audiobooks.md)) so they access age-appropriate content while decoding develops
- **School-wide models**: programs like DEAR or schoolwide silent reading blocks that extend SSR across all classrooms

### Target Learners
- Developing readers who have achieved basic decoding fluency and can sustain independent reading [~M]
- Reluctant or aliterate readers, for whom daily low-stakes access to self-chosen text rebuilds reading identity and habit [+W]
- Less suitable for beginning readers still acquiring phonemic decoding, who need explicit instruction first [Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading habit formation and intrinsic motivation to read
- Vocabulary growth and background knowledge through volume of exposure [~M]
- Reading stamina and fluency through extended practice
- Not appropriate as the sole vehicle for teaching comprehension strategies or decoding

### Instructions
1. **Build the collection.** Stock a range of genres, formats, and difficulty levels; include graphic novels, magazines, and nonfiction to widen entry points.
2. **Schedule a consistent block.** Set 10–20 minutes at a predictable time; consistency matters more than duration for habit formation.
3. **Teach book selection.** Briefly model how to preview and abandon books (e.g., the five-finger rule for difficulty) so students can self-match texts.
4. **Protect the time.** No worksheets, logs, or grades during reading; the teacher reads too, modeling engagement.
5. **Add light social accountability.** Use brief [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) or peer book talks afterward so students can share recommendations without converting reading into an assignment.
6. **Monitor and adjust.** Watch for fake-reading; confer individually with students who stall, and adjust text access rather than imposing requirements.

## Examples
- **DEAR (Drop Everything and Read)** — a widely adopted school-wide variant in which the entire school pauses for scheduled silent reading; see [https://www.readingrockets.org](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/assessment-and-evaluation/articles/sustained-silent-reading)
- **Krashen's free voluntary reading advocacy** — middle and secondary classrooms implementing pure FVR with no accountability, drawing on Krashen's synthesis of in-school and out-of-school reading research
- **Classroom libraries with conferencing** — teachers such as those described in Donalyn Miller's *The Book Whisperer* combine daily self-selected reading with brief status-of-the-class check-ins
