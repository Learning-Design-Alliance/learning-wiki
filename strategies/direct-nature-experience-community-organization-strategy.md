# Teach NbSP through direct nature experience with community organizations, supplemented by VR and digital options

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Stakeholders and the article's discussion recommend that educators of allied health professionals bring students into real nature and partner with community organizations delivering NbSP, rather than relying on a single lecture slide or purely virtual teaching. Participants argued educators should "not take away the opportunities where people can go into nature as many more benefits than in a virtual setting". The discussion suggests simulation days whose learning outcomes constructively align with module content, collaborative partnerships with NbSP providers, and VR or mixed reality as supplementary scaffolds for students who have not experienced nature connection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator topic knowledge and teaching skill to flex the curriculum environment
- Constructive alignment of sessions with module learning outcomes and assessment
- Partnerships with community NbSP providers
#### Constraints
- Logistics, minibus costs and organizational hire may exceed higher education budgets
- Unpredictable UK weather and heat extremes create challenging learning environments
- Educator workloads can impede the best pedagogic intentions

### Target Learners
- Pre-registration allied health professional students
- Higher education educators in health and social care

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding nature-based social prescribing and its benefits for clients
- Nature connectedness and its application in practice
