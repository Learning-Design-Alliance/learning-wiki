# On-site nurse mentoring programme with regular facility visits integrating case sheets as teaching aids

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A dedicated cadre of eleven nurse mentors, each responsible for 5-6 intervention PHCs, was recruited and trained for 5 weeks in essential clinical competencies and in how to effectively mentor PHC staff in clinical knowledge and skills, team building, problem solving and service delivery improvement. Mentors visited each assigned PHC for 2-3 days every two months, six times in the first year, using self-assessments, observations, clinical and case sheet audits, and interviews, and upgrading skills through case reviews, demonstrations, bed-side case discussions and small group teachings on partograph use and complication management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-trained, equipped mentors with transport and central support and funds for travel, per the article's discussion of supportive supervision
#### Constraints
- The article notes implementing such an intensive quality improvement programme is not easy in a large country with thousands of facilities, a largely rural population, isolated health workers and poor infrastructure

### Target Learners
- Staff nurses, mostly recently employed NHM contractual nurses, in 24x7 primary health centres

### Target Learning Goals
- Essential obstetric and neonatal care knowledge and skills, including AMTSL, partograph use, neonatal resuscitation and complication management
