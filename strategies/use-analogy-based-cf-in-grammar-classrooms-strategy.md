# Use analogy-based corrective feedback instead of traditional corrective feedback methods in young learners' grammar classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, based on its findings, teachers "instead of traditional methods of CF, use analogy-based corrective feedback in grammar classrooms". The strategy is implemented by embedding instruction on target structures into communicative tasks, observing learners, and correcting errors on target forms with a structurally similar synonymous example plus a prompt for learner revision. Teacher trainers are also encouraged to instruct teachers in focus on form options and to draw attention to analogy-based CF alongside other focus on form interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction on the target structures is embedded into communicative tasks during teacher-learner interaction
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one quasi-experimental study with one teacher, 45 elementary-level learners, and 8 sessions; the article states findings from one proficiency level cannot be generalized to other levels

### Target Learners
- young elementary-level EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- grammatical accuracy on target structures
