# Deliver novel psychomotor skill training asynchronously via a flipped-classroom, learner-centric model to reduce reliance on synchronous expert sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends providing procedural training through self-directed, asynchronous materials — a recorded didactic video plus independent practice with recorded performance reviewed by an expert — rather than 45-minute to 4-hour synchronous sessions. The authors state this "reduces the reliance on synchronous education sessions, with a 'flipped classroom' design" and incorporates adult-learning principles including scaffolding, experiential learning, self-controlled practice, and multimedia effect. They report significantly less content-expert time required compared to other nurse ultrasound-training initiatives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism for experts to review recorded learner performance and provide formative feedback until competence is demonstrated
#### Constraints
- Pilot was a single-center QI study with voluntary participation; findings support a need for further investigation in non-ICU settings and other facilities for generalizability

### Target Learners
- critical care nurses
- nursing staff in other clinical environments proposed by the authors

### Target Learning Goals
- novel psychomotor clinical skills such as ultrasound-guided peripheral IV placement
