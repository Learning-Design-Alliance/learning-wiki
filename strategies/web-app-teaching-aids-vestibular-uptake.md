# Use modern web-based or app-based teaching aids, such as video guides and smartphone training apps, to speed GPs' uptake of vestibular techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes "Modern teaching aids (internet-based or app-based)" as inventive methods to introduce these techniques to GPs. It cites evidence that "A smartphone app-based training aid for the Epley manoeuvre has already been shown to be more effective than written instructions," and notes a freely accessible online guide with a video is already available via the RACGP website.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning tools must show how to perform the techniques, e.g. a video demonstration of the manoeuvre
#### Constraints
- Treatment manoeuvres requiring less skill are adopted faster and require less practice, so aids alone may not overcome the skill demands of these techniques

### Target Learners
- GPs in daily practice learning new clinical techniques

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquiring and maintaining the skills to perform repositioning manoeuvres and vestibular rehabilitation
