# Incorporate simulation training into continuing professional development, differentiating communication approaches for doctors and nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding simulation-based life support training in continuing professional development to strengthen non-technical skills. It states that "incorporating simulation training as a standard part of continuing professional development can significantly improve healthcare teams' ability to respond in cardiopulmonary emergencies", with programmes explicitly addressing communication strategies and team dynamics and differentiating approaches for doctors and nurses based on their roles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training delivered by "ERC-accredited instructors" under a standardized quality assurance system with instructor accreditation, course-director quality control, and mandatory recertification every 3 years
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a quasi-experimental pre-post design with "the lack of a control group", non-probability consecutive sampling, self-assessment measures, and no long-term follow-up

### Target Learners
- doctors
- nurses in primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- communication styles in emergency care
- self-efficacy
- non-technical skills for cardiopulmonary resuscitation
