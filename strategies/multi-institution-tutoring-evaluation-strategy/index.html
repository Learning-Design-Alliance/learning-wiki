Evaluate tutoring outcomes at multiple institutions and compare effectiveness across tutoring forms¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's stated next steps for tutoring-effectiveness research are that "similar evaluations of student outcomes must occur at other institutions that provide tutoring services" and that, as the literature grows, "a meaningful comparison can be made about the comparative effectiveness of different forms of tutoring." Such analyses are framed as helping faculty and administrators make informed decisions about academic support services under tightening budgets.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tracking systems that record who uses the center, how often, and for what subjects
  • Merged usage and student outcome records

Constraints¶

  • Current findings apply only to the fall 2007 freshmen entering class, though the author suggests extrapolation to similar cohorts

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate students at colleges and universities providing tutoring services

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student retention, academic standing, and cumulative GPA
