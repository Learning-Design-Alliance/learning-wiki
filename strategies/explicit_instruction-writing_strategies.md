# Explicit Instruction: Writing Strategies

## Description
Explicit instruction in writing strategies involves directly teaching students specific methods for planning, drafting, revising, and editing their work. This approach emphasizes breaking down the writing process into manageable steps and providing clear guidance on how to execute each one. Strategies like Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD) can deepen understanding by systematically building background knowledge, discussing the strategy, modeling its use, memorizing key steps, providing ongoing support, and fostering independent practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires explicit instruction, modeling, guided practice, and opportunities for independent practice. May also require tools for planning, drafting, revising, and editing.
#### Constraints
- May be perceived as overly structured by some students. Requires consistent implementation and ongoing support to be effective.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->

### Affordances
- Provides a structured framework for learning and applying writing strategies, enhances self-regulation through goal setting and self-monitoring, and supports mastery through scaffolded practice.

### Personalization
- Strategies can be adapted to fit different genres and writing tasks. Scaffolding can be adjusted based on individual student needs, with more support provided initially and gradually reduced as students gain proficiency.
