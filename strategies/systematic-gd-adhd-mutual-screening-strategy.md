# Systematic screening for gaming disorder among students with ADHD and vice versa, with gender-sensitive interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates that academic settings account for the comorbidity of ADHD and gaming disorder by screening for each condition in students presenting with the other, and by tailoring interventions to gender. The abstract states the findings "underscore the need for gender-sensitive interventions" and advocate "systematic screening for GD among students with ADHD, and vice versa". This rests on the study's findings of elevated ADHD prevalence in the GD group and stronger ADHD-GD links in females.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions need a screening instrument for each condition; the study used the IGD-20 Test and the ASRS v1.1 as screening tools
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and from a single university site with a male-skewed convenience sample, which the authors state limits generalizability

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- protecting academic performance from the combined effects of ADHD symptoms and gaming disorder
