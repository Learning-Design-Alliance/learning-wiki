# Incorporate the psychodrama technique of doubling after role plays to deepen affective communication and foster a non-judgemental supervision atmosphere

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy modifies AFT by verbalising the affective experience exclusively via 'doubling', a psychodrama technique applied immediately after role plays. The article reports that "the most significant of which was the incorporation of the psychodrama technique of doubling after role plays to enhance the focus on affective communication and to foster a secure, non-judgemental atmosphere in both training and supervision." The modifications aim to enhance perception and verbalisation of emotions and minimise intellectualising after role plays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Role plays within supervision, group members willing to take patient and therapist roles, and a supervisor who prompts affective verbalisation rather than cognitive exchange
#### Constraints
- The article notes a tendency in supervision groups to rationalise, intellectualise and cognitively exchange concepts, which can conceal difficulties in opening up affectively or hide alexithymic tendencies

### Target Learners
- trainee psychotherapists in supervision groups
- psychotherapy supervisors

### Target Learning Goals
- perception and verbalisation of emotions in therapy
- access to affective blind spots in rupture situations
