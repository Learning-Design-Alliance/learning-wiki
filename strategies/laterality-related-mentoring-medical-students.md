# Provide early laterality-related mentoring to medical students and residents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that mentors attend to hand-dominance concordance during psychomotor skills training. It observed that students spontaneously "changed their places to sit next to their colleagues who had similar hand preference" and hypothesized learning is easier when trainee and instructor share dominance. Mentoring should begin early in medical school and continue during clinical residency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and mentors must be aware of student laterality and willing to discuss preference at the start of training
#### Constraints
- The supporting observations are informal observations from one training course, not a tested mentoring intervention

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students
- surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical psychomotor skills such as suturing, cannulation, catheterization and laparoscopy

## Claims

- [Left-handed students scored significantly lower than right-handed students on instrument handling in intravenous cannulation, the only statistically significant laterality difference found](../claims/cannulation-handling-significant-lh-disadvantage.md) [+W]
- [Only 3 of 27 left-handed students (11.12%) voiced laterality concerns or requested instrument changes during training](../claims/few-lh-students-express-laterality-concerns.md) [+W]
- [On laparoscopic simulator tasks, left- and right-handed students performed comparably, with shorter peg-transfer and clipping times and longer cutting times among left-handers, all statistically insignificant](../claims/laparoscopic-skills-laterality-comparable.md) [~W]
- [No significant difference in overall psychomotor skill performance between left-handed and right-handed first-year medical students after identical training in suturing, cannulation and urinary catheterization](../claims/lh-rh-medical-students-no-psychomotor-difference.md) [~W]
