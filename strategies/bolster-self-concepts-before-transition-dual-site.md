# Bolster academic self-concepts before the secondary transition and take a dual home-and-school approach for low-SES students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strengthening academic self-concepts before children start secondary school, especially for economically vulnerable children: "bolstering academic self‐concepts before starting secondary school could particularly help EV children maintain stable positive academic self‐concepts". It further recommends that "efforts to target socio‐economic educational inequality should take a dual site approach spanning home and school", citing home school community liaison officers in Ireland. Strategies engaging parents during the school transition are also highlighted as impactful across systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination between home and school supports during the primary–secondary transition period
#### Constraints
- The moderating benefit of teacher interactions for EV children was cumulative, requiring high parent expectations as well as high-quality teacher interactions

### Target Learners
- Children in the final years of primary school
- Students from economically vulnerable families

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining stable positive academic self-concepts
- Reducing socio-economic gaps in educational attainment
