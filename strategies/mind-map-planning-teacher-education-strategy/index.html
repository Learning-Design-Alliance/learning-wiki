Have pre-service teachers plan classroom activities with mind maps and model their use with students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "pre-service teachers should plan classroom activities by using mind maps, and also endeavor to ensure that these activities can be implemented in real classroom environments". It further suggests that activities in which teachers and pre-service teachers develop and use mind maps as planning tools with their students will help students understand mind maps for planning, goal-setting, exam preparation and group activities. The strategy positions mind-map planning as both a personal SRL tool and a modelling practice for future classrooms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction in mind-map construction and feedback on produced mind maps

Constraints¶

  • The article notes further studies are needed, particularly on metacognitive self-regulation, and that effects were measured only by students' personal assessment at course end

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers
  • teacher educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning
  • planning and time management
  • modelling self-regulation for students

Affordances¶

  • Pintrich Srl Strategies Motivational Beliefs Framework

Claims¶

  • Within the mind-map planning group, pre-to-post gains are significant on most self-regulation strategies and motivational beliefs, including a significant test-anxiety decrease [+W]
  • Task planning with mind maps raises post-test self-regulation strategy scores of pre-service teachers relative to a control group, except for rehearsal [+W]
  • Pre-service teachers' open-ended responses most frequently attribute to mind-map planning effects on metacognitive self-regulation, concentration and planned study [+W]
  • Mind-map task planning raises pre-service teachers' intrinsic goal orientation and self-efficacy more than direct task completion [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide professional development programs so teachers learn to implement self-regulated learning strategies in their lessons
