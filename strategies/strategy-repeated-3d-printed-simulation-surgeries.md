# Repeated hands-on simulation surgeries on a 3D-printed congenital heart disease model at approximately 1-week intervals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A training recipe in which each inexperienced surgeon independently performs the same simulation surgery three times, at approximately 1-week intervals, in an actual operating room with an assisting surgical nurse, with every session video-recorded and later rated by an experienced professor. The article found "further improvement in surgical skills from the second to third simulations, implying the additional effects of repeated experience of simulation surgeries", supporting spaced repetition of the full procedure rather than a single session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A patient-specific 3D-printed model suitable for suturing and dissection; an operating-room setting with nursing assistance; video recording and expert rating of performance
#### Constraints
- Model preparation has high costs and requires intensive labor, per the authors' stated limitations; the study used a single case, so varied models of the same disease entity might be needed

### Target Learners
- inexperienced cardiothoracic surgery residents and clinical fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- reduced operation time and improved surgical proficiency in congenital heart disease repair procedures
