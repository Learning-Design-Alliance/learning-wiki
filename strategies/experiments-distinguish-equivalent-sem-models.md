# Use targeted randomized interventions on perceived recognition, self-efficacy, and interest, combined via meta-analysis, to distinguish statistically equivalent SEM models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (4 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a set of experimental studies, each aimed at examining specific paths between the variables, to determine which of the statistically equivalent models is more causally accurate. As the authors put it, "The paths from perceived recognition to self-efficacy and interest could be investigated by implementing interventions targeting perceived recognition", and the self-efficacy-to-interest path by interventions focusing on self-efficacy. Suggested recognition interventions include instructors directly acknowledging students with tailored encouraging feedback and instructor workshops on recognition strategies; suggested self-efficacy interventions draw on mastery experience and vicarious experience. Synthesizing experiments manipulating the independent variable and the mediator through meta-analysis is said to yield robust mediation evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must target only the intended construct, with effects on downstream outcomes mediated through it; pre and post surveys of all variables in the model are needed, and control groups should be parallel classes taught by the same instructor or the same instructor's previous students.
#### Constraints
- The article notes observed intervention coefficients are not expected to precisely match the model's coefficients even if the model is causally accurate; coefficients serve only as one benchmark for comparison.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory calculus-based physics courses

- self-efficacy
- interest
- perceived recognition
