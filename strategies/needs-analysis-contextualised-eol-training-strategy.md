# Conduct a training needs analysis and deliver contextualised, structured end-of-life training combining didactic lectures with small-group case-based discussions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nurse educators begin with a local needs assessment, then deliver structured EOL training tailored to nurses in non-palliative settings. The recommended package pairs weekly 1-hour didactic lectures with weekly 30-minute small-group case-based discussions of 6-8 RNs, contextualised to real ward scenarios. The authors state: "nurse educators should first conduct a training needs analysis for their own EOL education and develop a structured EOL training which can be contextualised to their nurses".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A training needs analysis using a knowledge quiz such as the modified Palliative Care Quiz to identify gaps before designing content
#### Constraints
- Full ELNEC-style training was infeasible on general wards due to clinical manpower constraints, competing ward priorities and limited instructor availability

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in general medical wards without palliative care specialisation

### Target Learning Goals
- End-of-life symptom assessment, pharmacological and non-pharmacological symptom management
