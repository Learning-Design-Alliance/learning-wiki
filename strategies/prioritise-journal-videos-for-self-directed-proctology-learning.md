# Encourage trainees to prioritise journal-hosted proctology videos over freely available YouTube videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgical trainees using videos for self-directed learning select peer-reviewed journal video material first, because it scored higher on structured case context and outcome reporting. "Given the findings of this study, trainees should be encouraged to prioritise journal‐related over other freely available material for self‐directed learning." The authors still note YouTube "may still serve to widen access for trainees without full journal subscriptions".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to peer-reviewed journal video vignettes; trainees engaged in self-directed video-based preparation for proctology procedures
#### Constraints
- Recommendation rests on quality-of-content scores; the study did not evaluate educational relevance or accuracy, and view-count-based YouTube selection may introduce bias

### Target Learners
- postgraduate colorectal/general surgery trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- preparatory learning before assisting or performing proctological operations
