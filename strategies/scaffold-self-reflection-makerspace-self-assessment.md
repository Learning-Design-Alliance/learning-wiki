# Support self-reflection through scaffolding, explicit instruction, simplified language, customization, and gamification

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the case-study findings, the authors recommend that future work develop the process of self-reflection through scaffolded and extended tool use or direct instruction with examples, simplify the language of skill conceptualisations for younger learners, offer teachers customizable levels and questions, and retain gamification elements such as levels and badges. They also encourage future studies to consider how a self-assessment tool can appeal to students and increase motivation to participate in self-assessment and reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need time and scaffolding practices; the article suggests step-by-step guidance and examples on how to approach reflection could be taught explicitly before introducing the digital tool.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations arise from a single exploratory qualitative case study with teachers selected for their interest in assessment in makerspaces, and an unbalanced age representation favouring primary-school-age students.

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in makerspace contexts, especially younger learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-assessment and reflection on 21st-century skills development during making
