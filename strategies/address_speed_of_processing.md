# Address Speed of Processing

## Description
Addressing speed of processing involves identifying students' strengths and challenges related to processing speed (the pace at which one takes in information, makes sense of it, and begins to respond) and using aligned strategies to leverage student strengths to support student learning. This can include providing graphic organizers or guided notes during lectures, repeating key points multiple times and in different ways, providing manipulatives to make complex concepts concrete, and allowing ample think-time and time for re-reading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires educators to be aware of students' strengths and challenges related to processing speed, willingness to implement aligned strategies, and provide appropriate scaffolds and supports.
#### Constraints
- Identifying students' strengths and challenges related to processing speed can be time-consuming. Some students may be resistant to using strategies designed to support processing speed.

### Personalization
- Strategies can be tailored to individual student needs and strengths. For example, some students may benefit more from graphic organizers, while others may prefer manipulatives.

## Assessment Evidence
- Student work samples demonstrating the use of aligned strategies, student reflections on the effectiveness of the strategies used, educator reflections on the impact of the strategies on student learning

## Impact
- Research suggests that addressing speed of processing can improve learning outcomes for students who struggle with processing speed. Sheppard & Vernon (2008) conducted a review of 50 years of research of processing speed showing the relation to intelligence. Fry & Hale (1996) showed evidence that processing speed, working memory, and fluid intelligence develop over time. Kail & Salthouse (1994) stated that processing speed is a mental capacity.
