# Use the CoRP framework to identify students' covariational reasoning resources and target instruction within their zone of proximal development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the CoRP framework as an analytic lens on student reasoning to identify reasoning resources at various instructional levels, indicating opportunities for targeted instruction. The framework 'allows us to identify reasoning resources that stu- dents have about covariation at various levels of instruction,' offering a glimpse into reasoning within the learner's zone of proximal development. Instructors can use it to recognize physics covariational reasoning patterns and researchers to analyze student reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and researchers must learn the framework's three sections (PU, PMA, EB) and apply them to analyze student work on covariational tasks
#### Constraints
- Little work has been done in physics education research on how physics students' covariational reasoning progresses with instruction, so recognizing instructional change is difficult

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- algebra and calculus students preparing for physics
- upper-division physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- developing physics covariational reasoning for quantitative modeling
