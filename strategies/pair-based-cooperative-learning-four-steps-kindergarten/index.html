Structured pair-based cooperative learning procedure with four explicit cooperation steps for young children¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), q2–q4 · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The cooperative learning training followed the learning together approach (Johnson et al., 2008): students worked cooperatively in pairs, with different pairs formed for each session and heterogeneous pairs in terms of skill levels. Children were taught four explicit steps to cooperate effectively: listen and formulate an answer individually, share the answer by whispering to the partner, try to reach an agreement, and be ready to answer if asked. Positive goal interdependence and positive identity interdependence were established, pair scores were shared and commented at the end of each activity, and students were encouraged to celebrate other students' successes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A practical demonstration of the procedure by the instructor to provide the children with a concrete behavioral model

Constraints¶

  • In this study the cooperative approach did not yield a significantly larger improvement than individual training, and the authors state their data do not allow a definite conclusion about CL versus individual training for children under six

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten children aged 4-5

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early numerical competence

Claims¶

  • Cooperative Learning Improves Achievement [+M]
  • All three groups (cooperative learning, individual learning, and control) improved significantly in early numeracy from pre-test to post-test over 12 weeks [+W]
  • Among low-early-math-ability kindergarteners, supplemental training produced significantly higher improvement than an equally low-ability control group [+W]
