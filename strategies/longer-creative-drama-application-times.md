# Use longer creative drama application times to increase effect sizes in skill-development studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends extending the duration of creative drama applications, because skills are not prone to change in short periods and its moderator analysis found larger effects for longer applications. It argues that "longer application times are better than shorter application times in terms of increasing the effect size in experimental studies." The recommendation rests on the significant duration moderator finding (QB=13.554, p<.05).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applications must be long enough for skills to change; the article states skills are not prone to change in shorter time periods.
#### Constraints
- The article states the short-study duration finding cannot be clearly associated with something and calls for future research to focus more elaborately on the experimental application time variable.
- A prior meta-analysis on math achievement found no significant duration effect, which the article attributes to the large application times used there.

### Target Learners
- students from pre-school to university in formal education institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- social skills
- basic language skills
- higher thinking skills
