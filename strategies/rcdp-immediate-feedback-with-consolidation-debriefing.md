# Use RCDP with immediate directive feedback at scene interruptions, reserving debriefing for consolidation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adopting RCDP in nursing education because it provides immediate directive feedback during simulation, allowing repetition until correct execution, which the authors say enhances skill retention and self-confidence. In RCDP, post-event debriefing "takes over a complementary function, occurring after the complete conclusion of the scenario, aiming to consolidate and maximize the learning obtained during interruptions." Facilitators should therefore adapt debriefing to the purpose of deliberate practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A checklist listing the necessary actions to interrupt the scene when tasks are not performed adequately
- A facilitator trained in clinical simulation
