# Include formal training on the biases affecting self-perception in medical education to develop doctors' accurate self-perception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical education facilitate doctors' accurate self-perception by including formal training on the biases that affect self-perception, so that doctors learn about the inherent heuristics they use when reflecting on strengths and weaknesses. It further recommends that medical educators establish how feedback can be delivered in a way likely to be internalised to encourage behavioural change, and suggests revalidation's enforced reflection may offer opportunities for greater self-awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An understanding of the universal biases that affect self-perception, coupled with appropriate behaviour changes in response to feedback

### Target Learners
- medical doctors working in UK clinical practice, from foundation year two to consultant level

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate self-perception of competence and capacity to self-monitor
