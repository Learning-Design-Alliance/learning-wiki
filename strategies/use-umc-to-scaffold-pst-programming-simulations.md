# Use UMC rather than PRIMM to scaffold PSTs' learning with programmable simulations in science education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's first design principle recommends the use-modify-create approach as the pedagogical scaffold for the learning sequence. The authors found that with PRIMM, "the PSTs did not discuss science when working within the PRIMM model, as too much focus was on the programming itself", and advocate UMC as providing "a better balance between CK and TK". The strategy structures the sequence into use, modify, and create stages with science-focused tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A learning sequence where programming activities are connected to science content the PSTs know, so that TK can be integrated with existing CK and PCK
#### Constraints
- Results come from the prototyping stage of a design-based research project in one Norwegian teacher education context, and the authors welcome others to test generalizability in different contexts

### Target Learners
- Pre-service science teachers at the master's level

### Target Learning Goals
- Technological pedagogical and content knowledge (TPACK) for programming simulations in science education
