# Facilitate student questions during Lecture-Tutorials with Socratic-style questioning, starting by having students read the question aloud verbatim

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
When groups ask for help, the instructor's first move is to have students "read the Lecture-Tutorial question out loud and verbatim", which often resolves the question as it is read. If not, instructors diagnose with targeted 50/50 questions, have students articulate their confusion, or direct them to reflect on earlier questions. The authors frame the instructor's role as "a guide who employs Socratic-style questioning rather than an authority who provides answers", keeping students responsible for constructing their own knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must work through the Lecture-Tutorials before class to anticipate difficulties and plan 50/50 questions
#### Constraints
- Time is limited, so facilitation is described as emergency triage rather than extended re-teaching; re-lecturing is discouraged

### Target Learners
- collaborative student groups in Astro 101 lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual and reasoning development through guided self-explanation
