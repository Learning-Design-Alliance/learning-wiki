# See, Think, Wonder

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 2 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 3 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
See, Think, Wonder is a thinking routine developed by Harvard Project Zero's Visible Thinking project. Learners observe a stimulating image, artifact, or primary source, then respond to three prompts in sequence: *What do you see?* (pure observation), *What do you think about it?* (interpretation), and *What does it make you wonder?* (question generation). The routine is typically carried out with a three-column organizer, individual writing time, and then [peer discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and whole-class sharing.

## Design Implications

The routine makes thinking visible and separates observation from interpretation, which trains learners to distinguish evidence from inference. Because every response is grounded in what is directly observable, every learner can succeed from the first contribution, lowering the stakes for participation and normalizing tentative or partially formed ideas [active-learning-improves-exam-performance](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. The "wonder" step activates curiosity and generates authentic questions that can drive subsequent inquiry [activation-improves-learning](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M].

#### Constraints
- Poorly chosen images produce shallow engagement; the stimulus must be rich enough to sustain multiple interpretations [-M]
- Some students stall at surface-level observation ("I see a red shape") and need prompting or sentence stems to move to interpretation and questioning [-W]
- The routine opens inquiry but does not by itself build deep knowledge; without follow-up investigation, wonderings remain unresolved [-M]
- Overuse as a generic warm-up can make the three prompts feel formulaic, reducing genuine curiosity [-W]
- With sparse or overly familiar artifacts, "see" collapses into "think" and the observation discipline is lost [-W]
- Whole-group versions can be dominated by a few confident speakers; think-pair-share or written variants mitigate this [-W]
- If the instructor does not connect the routine to subsequent content, it functions as an isolated activity with little durable learning benefit [~M]

### Instructions
1. **Gain attention** — display the image or artifact without explanation or context ([Gain Attention](../elements/gain-attention.md)); silence and observation time first
2. **See** — learners record only what they objectively observe, no interpretation yet ([Individual Response](../elements/individual-response.md))
3. **Think** — learners record interpretations, using the stem "I think… because…" to force evidence-based reasoning
4. **Wonder** — learners generate questions the image raises for them
5. **Share** — [peer discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) of chosen entries, then whole-class sharing that surfaces [cognitive conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md) between differing interpretations
6. **Bridge** — connect the class's wonderings to the upcoming content or inquiry investigation
