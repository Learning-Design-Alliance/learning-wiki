# Use interruptible simulation in a safe environment so errors can be pointed out and corrected during practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 5 studies (4 qualitative, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends obstetric simulation as a way for students to practice new skills in a safe environment. "The simulation can be interrupted at any time to point out errors or correct them", allowing assessment and correction of students' skills in simulated scenarios, which the authors link to greater understanding and knowledge retention. The discussion also suggests extending the course or offering a greater diversity of practice scenarios to arouse students' interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation environment where sessions can be paused for error correction
- Supervising faculty able to assess and correct skills during scenarios
#### Constraints
- The study's before-and-after design without a control group limits causal claims about the strategy's effects

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in obstetric clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- obstetric procedural skills
- self-confidence in childbirth care
- communication skills with patients
