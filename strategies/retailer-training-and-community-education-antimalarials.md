# Target training interventions at untrained retailers and add community education that leverages customer demand to improve appropriate anti-malarial dispensing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interventions "should focus on the large population of untrained retailers, as their outcomes in terms of knowledge and dispensing practices are poorest", and that training be current and cover treatment of malaria in children plus referral practice for patients under five. It further proposes leveraging customer demand by focusing education efforts on patient communities, specifically targeting mothers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should be informed by evidence from medicine retailer behaviour and practice studies
- Training should be current and include specific information on appropriate treatment of malaria in children and referral practice for patients under five
#### Constraints
- All data were self-reported actions, not directly observed behaviours, and are subject to reporting bias
- The study was conducted over one geographic area, which may limit generalizability of study findings

### Target Learners
- Medicine retailers in private drug outlets, particularly untrained retailers
- Mothers and patient communities in malaria-endemic rural areas

### Target Learning Goals
- Correctly identifying and recommending the first-line anti-malarial for uncomplicated malaria
- Appropriate patient management including referral of children under five
