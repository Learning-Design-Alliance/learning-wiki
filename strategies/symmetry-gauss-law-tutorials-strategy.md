# Use tutorials that teach discerning charge-distribution symmetry, judging when Gauss' law is easily applicable, and distinguishing field from flux, with separate pre/post-tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (11 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors are implementing and refining tutorials in which students learn to "discern the symmetry of a charge distribution", determine whether Gauss' law can easily be applied to calculate the electric field strength, and predict the shape of the corresponding Gaussian surface when high symmetry exists. The tutorials also help students distinguish electric field from electric flux, learn the superposition principle, and revisit superposition after Gauss' law, for example to determine the net electric field due to two spheres of charge. Each tutorial has separate pre/post-tests, and preliminary results are encouraging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each tutorial has separate pre/post-tests that students take before and after working on the tutorials
#### Constraints
- Tutorials are still being implemented and refined; only preliminary results are reported

### Target Learners
- Students in calculus-based introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Discerning symmetry of charge distributions
- Determining when Gauss' law easily yields the electric field
- Distinguishing electric field from electric flux
- Applying the superposition principle
