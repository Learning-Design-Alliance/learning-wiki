# Instructors decide whether to intervene in tense small-group moments by weighing whether the escape hatch reopens or squelches discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors treat tense small-group moments as objects of careful attention, weighing “the social dynamics of the group, the content of their discussion, and the interaction of the two.” Whether to intervene depends on the escape hatch's function: one that lets debate continue from a new angle may not warrant intervention, while one that squelches discussion may.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor observation of small-group work as it unfolds
#### Constraints
- The article bases this recommendation on preliminary analysis of episodes from one focus group

### Target Learners
- instructors facilitating collaborative small-group work in quantum mechanics or similarly difficult content

### Target Learning Goals
- keeping group conflict productive for conceptual development rather than derailing reasoning
