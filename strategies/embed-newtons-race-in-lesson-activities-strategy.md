# Embed the intrinsically integrated game within a lesson with complementary learning activities to foster comprehension and transfer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because transfer from Newton's Race was limited to situations closely resembling the game, the authors recommend that "it is important to embed the game in the curriculum to improve comprehension and transfer." In such a lesson the game plays a central role and other learning activities complement the game; the lesson fits best at the lower classes of secondary education, where students start with Newtonian mechanics. The authors also suggest the counterintuitive steering could be addressed in an additional learning activity such as a classroom discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The game should play a central role in the lesson, with other learning activities complementing it
#### Constraints
- The study itself used no additional learning activities, so this recommendation goes beyond what was tested; no delayed posttest means retention effects remain unknown

### Target Learners
- students in the lower classes of secondary education starting Newtonian mechanics

### Target Learning Goals
- extending acquired Newtonian mechanics knowledge and transferring it to situations outside the game
