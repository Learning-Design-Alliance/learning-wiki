# Replace textbook problems with authentic real-world problems that require deciding what information is needed and justifying assumptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course replaced standard textbook problems with authentic real-world problems, which the authors chose because they "carry less resemblance to physics high school problems, and so even the students with the best high school physics instruction have little experience or skill in solving them". Authentic problems have multiple potential solution paths and require reflecting on prior knowledge, planning, deciding what information is needed, finding it, and making and justifying assumptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must pass the article's test: "Would someone other than a physics teacher care about the answer to this?"
- Problems must require most of the basic physics 1 material while being more realistic than textbook and exam problems, and be appropriately challenging for students' incoming level.
#### Constraints
- Quiz questions provided all the information needed, a pragmatic decision in response to student discomfort with having to find information adding to their stress at taking even a low stakes test.
- The article notes previous problem-solving work mostly used standard textbook problems, which require few of the decisions needed for real-world problems.

### Target Learners
- Introductory physics students, including those with extensive high school physics preparation and those with little

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying physics concepts to solve realistic, unconstrained problems as scientists and engineers do
