Use common elements results to redesign OSTA interventions by adding high-FV and removing low-FV elements, accompanied by quality measurement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends using its frequency count values to optimize interventions: "elements with high FVs can be added as these likely contribute to favorable outcomes, and/or elements with low FVs can be removed as they might be superfluous." Elements can also be tailored and assembled into new forms suitable for co-creation and user-centered design. Because causal inferences from specific elements are lacking, the review states implementation "should be accompanied by quality measurement and assurance."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Quality measurement and assurance should accompany implementation of common elements to counterbalance the lack of causal evidence

Constraints¶

  • Frequencies do not establish element effectiveness; results are hypothesis-generating and subject to publication bias

Target Learners¶

  • primary school children at risk of academic failure

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement across reading, math, and broader outcome domains

Affordances¶

  • Practice Process Implementation Element Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Training parents to provide homework support was the most frequent common practice element in effective OSTA interventions for reading, and all 11 interventions using it were effective [+W]
  • Training in parental school involvement at home, positive reinforcement, psychoeducation, and use of explicit goals showed positive frequency values across reading, math, and GPA outcomes [+W]
  • The most common combination in effective OSTA interventions was professionals training caregivers in parental school involvement at home and homework support combined with positive reinforcement [+W]
  • Quality monitoring was the most common implementation element and regular support to the intervention receiver the most common process element in effective OSTA interventions [+W]
  • Structured tutoring was the most common practice element but was ineffective in about 25% of studies using it, illustrating popularity bias in common elements reviews [-W]
