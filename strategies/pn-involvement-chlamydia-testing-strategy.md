# Involve practice nurses in chlamydia testing, supported by targeted training and reminder systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends expanding practice nurses' role in chlamydia testing in general practice, since PNs "with their demonstrated levels of chlamydia knowledge and willingness to offer testing opportunistically have the potential to make a significant contribution". It proposes strategies to increase PN involvement, supported by PN-specific education, financial incentives within the ACCEPt intervention, additional training, and formal recall/reminder systems to facilitate repeat testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PNs require additional training or skills to manage chlamydia testing and treatment, as 89% of surveyed PNs themselves indicated
#### Constraints
- Participating PNs were selected by their clinics for potential testing roles and may represent a more interested group than PNs generally

### Target Learners
- Practice nurses in Australian general practice
- General practitioners in Australian general practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Correct chlamydia knowledge including risk age groups and retesting recommendations
- Appropriate opportunistic testing and specimen collection practices
