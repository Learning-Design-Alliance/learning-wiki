# Combine an in-person didactic lecture, a virtual simulation experience, and a debrief to teach students to respond to racism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's intervention model pairs anti-racism didactic content with experiential virtual simulation practice and a facilitated debrief. "The intervention consisted of a 1.5 h in-person education session that included a education session (didactic lecture), a virtual simulation experience and a debrief that was focused on anti-discrimination and racism in clinical nursing settings." The didactic portion covers definitions of race, racism, microaggressions, and social justice plus the Microaggression Triangle Model; students then complete the simulation independently so each can explore response options for personal learning. This combination produced significant pre/post gains in cultural humility and cultural diversity awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A virtual simulation experience matched to learners' clinical context
- An in-person session led by a facilitator involved in developing the simulations
- Independent completion so each student can explore the options
#### Constraints
- The article notes this addresses an aspect of a larger problem: frameworks and simulations are described as band aids to the larger problem of racism in the health care system and nursing education

### Target Learners
- final-year baccalaureate nursing students starting their final clinical placement

### Target Learning Goals
- professionally responding to or navigating experienced or witnessed racism and hidden bias
- cultural humility and cultural diversity awareness

### Affordances
- Microaggression Triangle Model Strategies
