# Provide dual training in the educational context addressing both learning and coping-strategy development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that universities should pair academic training with support for students' coping and stress-management development. It states that "the relationship between coping strategies and satisfaction with e-learning represents the need to provide dual training in the educational context", and suggests the university be "an interactive community in virtual learning" that fosters coping strategies, stress-management tools, and adaptability beyond emergency settings. The recommendation rests on the study's own correlational findings linking coping, stress, and social presence with e-learning satisfaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to embedding coping-strategy and stress-management development alongside academic training, per the article's concluding reflection
#### Constraints
- Based on a convenience sample from a single university with self-report measures and a design that did not permit causal conclusions

### Target Learners
- University students, including medical and health-science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining e-learning satisfaction and student well-being during distance learning
