# Use interactive and clinically integrated teaching strategies, with librarian collaboration, to build EBP knowledge and skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that EBP teaching combine interactive methods with clinical integration and interdisciplinary collaboration with librarians. It concludes "there is a need to improve educators' consciousness of and competences in teaching EBP principles, which involves using interactive and clinical integrated teaching strategies." Concrete activities reported in the included studies include participation in clinical research projects, health needs analyses, oral presentations of research findings in clinical placements, and database-searching workshops delivered by library staff.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration between librarians, study skills advisors and academics; educator competence in EBP principles
#### Constraints
- The review notes clinically integrated strategies were given lower priority in the studies reviewed and that clinical preceptors may have insufficient knowledge to support students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- EBP knowledge and skills
- information literacy
- research utilisation in clinical practice
