Adapt worked-example sessions for high and low prior-knowledge students who did not benefit¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 8 studies (4 causal, 3 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 8 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the high and low prior-knowledge groups did not benefit significantly, the authors recommend redesigning the sessions for them. They suggest "maybe a smaller group session using the worked example worksheets with a focus on helping the students more would benefit the low prior mathematical knowledge group," and for high students "The addition of more challenging problems or tasks in SP session could be beneficial," or having high prior knowledge students serve as peer mentors to low prior knowledge students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Worked-example worksheets as the base material
  • class assistants or peer mentors to support smaller-group formats

Constraints¶

  • Both suggestions are proposals in the discussion, not tested in this study

Target Learners¶

  • college students with high or low prior mathematical knowledge in introductory mathematics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving the knowledge-regulation component
  • college algebra success for struggling students

Affordances¶

  • Icml Interactive Compensatory Model Learning

Claims¶

  • The middle prior-knowledge group benefited most from Supplemental Practice, significantly outperforming the middle control group on current points, test 3, final, quizzes, and course GPA [~M]
  • Students using worked-example worksheets in weekly Supplemental Practice sessions significantly outperformed control students on test 3 and the final exam in a large College Algebra course [+M]
  • Prior knowledge significantly predicted algebra posttest scores and learning gains, with high-prior-knowledge students scoring higher and low-prior-knowledge students gaining more [~M]
  • Prior knowledge moderated the effect of incorrect worked examples: students with low prior knowledge benefited most, and prior knowledge no longer predicted post-test scores in the incorrect examples group [~M]
  • Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~S]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adaptive systems should deliver prior-knowledge-tailored discrepancy prompts and regular self-assessment calibration prompts
