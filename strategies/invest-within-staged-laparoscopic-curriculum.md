# Position INVEST as an integrated component of a staged laparoscopic curriculum, not a stand-alone curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a balanced training program in which intraoperative video-enhanced training follows simulator-based preparation. They state that "a balanced training program commences with essential basic skills training on VR and/or AR simulators," that procedure elements should be practiced in box trainers with cadaver models, and that "Ideally, trainees should attend courses that use live animal models or human cadavers to perform specific procedures on healthy organs before they go to the operating theater to perform their first procedures on real patients with INVEST." In the trial this sequence was implemented by having all residents complete the SIMENDO proficiency-based curriculum before randomization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior basic laparoscopic skills training to a preset proficiency level before operating-theater procedure training
#### Constraints
- INVEST should not be seen as a stand-alone training curriculum, per the authors

### Target Learners
- Surgical residents transitioning from skills-lab training to operating-theater procedure training

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe and efficient acquisition of laparoscopic procedural skills across skill-based, rule-based, and knowledge-based behaviors

### Affordances
- Rasmussen Three Behavior Levels Laparoscopy
