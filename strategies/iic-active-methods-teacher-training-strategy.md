# Support the transition to active methodologies in IICs through teacher training courses, shared repositories of activities and formative-assessment questions, and institution-specific research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To move from traditional pedagogical approaches to active learning in intensive courses, the authors recommend building teacher capability and shared materials. They write: "It is also suggested to continue training higher education teachers through courses or workshops on various active methodologies for teaching physics, creating repositories available to teachers of both activities that use active methodological strategies and key questions for formative assessment." They further suggest continuing studies that integrate each institution's realities to identify the most effective methods for IICs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support for teacher training in active methodologies and for maintaining shared repositories
#### Constraints
- The article notes considerable resistance among teachers to adopting active methodologies, which require more time, resources and training

### Target Learners
- Higher education teachers of physics and STEM subjects in intensive course formats

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective integration of active methodologies and formative assessment in intensive instruction courses
