# Subject in-person-validated MCMR items to validity checks for online use, including student interviews about how students interact with MCMR items online

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation for assessment developers: MCMR items that already show validity evidence in person should be re-validated for online administration. They state that "even MCMR items with evidence of validity when administered in-person should be subjected to validity checks for online use," and that "we expect that student interviews will be necessary to understand how students are interacting with MCMR items online," with online interviews compared to existing paper-based interview data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing validity evidence from in-person administration, plus response-pattern data from both online and paper administrations to compare
#### Constraints
- The article states the mechanism of the online effect is not yet characterized and that additional analyses may provide insight but interviews are expected to be necessary

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing quantitative reasoning in introductory physics contexts
- validating assessment items for online administration
