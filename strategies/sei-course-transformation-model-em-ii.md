# Science Education Initiative model of iterative course transformation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article applies the Science Education Initiative model, an iterative process in which three key steps inform all aspects of the project: "establish explicit learning goals in collaboration with experienced faculty; apply education research to develop materials and teaching strategies to help students achieve those goals; use validated assessments to determine what students are (and aren't) learning." At CU this involved a two-day meeting of 15 physics faculty from eight institutions, interviews with six instructors and five students, and collaboration with non-PER faculty to establish learning goals and vet assessment questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with experienced faculty, including non-PER faculty, to establish explicit learning goals and vet assessments
- Instructor and student interviews to identify student difficulties before developing materials
#### Constraints
- Electrodynamics topics beyond electrostatics were often treated differently across institutions, so the assessment focused on core material likely to be covered in most courses

### Target Learners
- upper-division physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- faculty-consensus learning goals for advanced electrodynamics, including understanding the role of formal mathematics and the range of validity of derived equations
