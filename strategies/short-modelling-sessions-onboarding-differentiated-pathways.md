# Embed short modelling-focused sessions in onboarding to accelerate comprehension for less-prepared new hires, with differentiated challenges for stronger hires

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that organisations embed short, low-cost modelling interventions into onboarding because they "can help less-prepared new campus hires build the comprehension needed for maintenance and feature work", potentially reducing ramp-up time and early frustration. For stronger hires, the results "suggesttheimportanceofdifferentiatedchallenges", pointing to tiered activities or advanced modules. The intervention required only five sessions, making it a low-cost, logistically feasible complement to existing onboarding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured articulation template and modelling practice integrated with product familiarisation
#### Constraints
- In its present form the intervention may not provide sufficient challenge or depth for stronger learners
- Evidence base is a pilot without a control group and with single-rater scoring

### Target Learners
- new campus hires in industry onboarding programs, especially those with weaker initial comprehension

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension of large existing software systems
- systems-level thinking
