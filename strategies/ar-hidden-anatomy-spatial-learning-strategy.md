# Use AR visualization of hidden anatomy and simulated fluoroscopy to teach spatial reasoning in a radiation-free environment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using AR to integrate simulated fluoroscopic imaging so users can "practice radiographic navigation and three-dimensional spatial reasoning in a completely radiation-free and cost-efficient environment, without the need for additional physical imaging equipment." Visualizing otherwise hidden anatomy helps beginners understand spatial relationships and lowers the entry barrier to complex image-guided procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AR interface with color-coded overlays and an anchored virtual cylinder indicating optimal imaging orientation
#### Constraints
- Simulated X-rays replace real fluoroscopy, so transfer to real operating room performance was not evaluated in this study

### Target Learners
- beginners in image-guided surgery
- orthopedic trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- radiographic navigation
- three-dimensional spatial reasoning
