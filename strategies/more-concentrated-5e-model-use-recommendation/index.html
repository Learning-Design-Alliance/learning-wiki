Recommend more concentrated use of the 5E model in classrooms to support productive and effective education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the pooled positive effect sizes for achievement, retention and attitude, the authors recommend broader and more concentrated use of the 5E instructional model in schooling. The article states "it may be suggested that more concentrated use of the 5E model will contribute to ensuring that students experience a more productive and effective course of education in their classes and their academic work." The recommendation rests on the meta-analytic findings rather than on a new manipulation tested in this article.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implementation of instruction designed according to the five 5E stages

Constraints¶

  • The article notes some studies found no significant attitude difference, and that the model requires more teacher preparation time

Target Learners¶

  • students in Turkish primary, secondary and higher education classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement
  • retention of learned concepts
  • positive attitudes toward lessons

Claims¶

  • The 5E instructional model shows a large, significant pooled effect on academic achievement versus traditional teaching methods in Turkish studies (2008-2014) [+W]
  • The 5E model shows a large pooled effect on retention scores versus traditional methods, though the article's own Z-test is reported as non-significant [~W]
  • The 5E model shows a moderate pooled effect on attitude scores versus traditional methods, with the article reporting no statistical significance on its Z-test [~W]
  • Diagnostic analyses show the pooled effect sizes for achievement, attitude and retention are robust and not attributable to publication bias [+W]
  • The 5E and related models significantly improve mathematics achievement (g = 0.70), though sensitivity analyses suggest the estimate may be smaller in practice [+W]
