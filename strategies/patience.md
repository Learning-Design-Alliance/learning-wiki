# Patience

## Description
Patience is the deliberate instructional stance of allowing learners extended time, repeated attempts, and emotional support when working through tasks that are new and difficult to them — such as analyzing mentor texts or practicing higher-order thinking. It is carried out through adjusted pacing, individualized feedback, and celebrating effort and progress rather than only outcomes. It is not lowered standards: the teacher persists in the expectation while persisting with the learner.

## Design Implications

Patience functions as the affective substrate that keeps learners engaged long enough for cognitive strategies to take hold. Struggling learners' self-efficacy predicts whether they persist at all [Learner self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M], and perceived task value sustains engagement through difficulty [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]. A patient stance protects both: it signals that struggle is expected and recoverable, which supports a growth mindset and reduces the anxiety that otherwise short-circuits working memory during demanding tasks.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher commitment to supportive instruction across repeated attempts, not a single lesson
- A classroom environment where students feel safe to err publicly ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md))
- Time built into instruction for [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) and feedback cycles
- Framing of errors as information, so that patience reads as confidence in the learner rather than pity

#### Constraints
- Time-consuming; in coverage-driven schedules, patience is often the first casualty, and rushed pacing disproportionately harms struggling learners
- Some students need more intensive, structured support than patience alone provides — sustained warmth without [Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) becomes unproductive struggle [~M]
- Uneven distribution risk: without careful planning, patient attention clusters on the most visibly struggling students while others stall quietly
- Excessive tolerance of non-attempt can reinforce avoidance; patience must be paired with accountability for effort

#### Implementation Variability
- Pace adjustment: extending timelines, chunking tasks, or allowing resubmission rather than slowing the whole class
- Feedback style: effort-and-process praise ("your revision added evidence") rather than person praise, which can undermine growth mindset [~M]
- Structural patience: mastery-based re-attempt policies that institutionalize patience rather than depending on teacher disposition

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners and students who lack confidence, for whom early failure without support predicts disengagement [Learner self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- All grade levels; the stance matters most when tasks are novel relative to learner skill
- Anxious learners, for whom a low-threat environment reduces interference with performance

### Target Learning Goals
- Dispositional: persistence, growth mindset, tolerance of productive struggle
- Motivational: self-efficacy and task value in demanding domains [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Higher-order skills (analysis, interpretation of mentor texts) that require multiple iterations before success

### Instructions
1. Set an expectation that difficulty is normal and multiple attempts are part of the work, before the task begins.
2. [Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) — offer scaffolds and hints rather than solutions when learners stall.
3. Build in [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) so learners can monitor their own progress and notice growth [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].
4. Use [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md) and [Whole-Class Sharing](../elements/whole-class-sharing.md) to normalize struggle publicly and celebrate incremental progress.
5. Sustain [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) over time — feedback on process and strategy, with pace adjusted to individual needs.

## Related Strategies
- Formative feedback practices — patience is enacted through feedback cycles rather than one-shot evaluation
- Mastery-based re-attempt structures — institutionalize patience in assessment policy

## Related Elements
- [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — the ongoing, patient feedback relationship that carries the stance
- [Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) — patience without guidance becomes unproductive struggle
- [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) — lets learners see their own growth, converting patience into self-efficacy
- [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md) — normalizes struggle among classmates
- [Whole-Class Sharing](../elements/whole-class-sharing.md) — public celebration of progress and effort

## Examples
- A writing teacher returns drafts with process-focused comments and allows revision before grading, explicitly noting what improved between drafts.
- A teacher analyzing a mentor text with struggling readers re-reads and re-questions over several sessions, adjusting pacing, rather than moving on after one attempt.
- Reading Recovery (https://readingrecovery.org) — intensive one-to-one early literacy intervention built on patient, individually paced teaching with sustained teacher training in supportive response to learner errors.
