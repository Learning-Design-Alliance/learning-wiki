# Use a virtual-clinic-plus-eBook model for independent learning about rare diseases lacking patient contact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends the eClinic concept as "a useful model for independent learning about rare diseases in general, with specific applicability to lysosomal storage diseases and other inborn errors of metabolism". Because contact with affected patients is unpredictable and formal training exposure is unlikely to be comprehensive, the strategy pairs virtual clinical cases drawn from real-life situations with instantaneous access to comprehensive reference material, allowing learners to move back and forth between components as needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two general components: virtual patient presentations (eClinic) and immediate access to sizable resource information (eBook)
- Easy movement between the two components as the learner feels the need
#### Constraints
- Development of the program was both time consuming and costly, though the authors argue long-term broad use offers significant advantages

### Target Learners
- medical undergraduates
- postgraduate specialty trainees
- general physicians and non-geneticist specialists

### Target Learning Goals
- management of rare diseases rarely encountered in training
- early diagnosis to enable timely treatment
