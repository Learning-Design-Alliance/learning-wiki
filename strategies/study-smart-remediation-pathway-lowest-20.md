# Remediation pathway giving the lowest-performing students continuous support on study strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the first midterm, the 20% lowest-performing students of the Study Smart cohort were assigned to a remediation pathway in which they developed new implementation intentions, made a study calendar, received weekly reminders of the trained strategies, and created a recorded PowerPoint teaching course content to a (theoretical) family member. Only these students completed the additional exercises and received instructor feedback. The authors frame this continuous individualized support as a way to "support lower achieving students in their first year to adapt to university and increase success rates".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A first-exam rank cut to identify the lowest-performing students, an instructor providing feedback on study strategies, and course content suitable for teach-back videos and study calendars
#### Constraints
- The article cannot pinpoint which ingredient of the remediation track (individual teacher support, feedback on time planning, or increased effective strategy use) drove the improvement

### Target Learners
- lowest-performing (bottom 20–25%) first-year pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulated use of effective learning strategies
- academic performance improvement across subsequent exams
