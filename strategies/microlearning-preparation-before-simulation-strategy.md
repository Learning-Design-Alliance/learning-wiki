# Use microlearning-based preparatory materials to scaffold simulation-based communication skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Share short, accessible digital materials — videos under five minutes including right- and ill-structured communication examples, written documents of no more than two pages, and audio-embedded presentations — through a learning management system approximately two weeks before simulation sessions. The article reports that "microlearning appears to be a useful method for integrating self-regulated learning principles into communication skills learning materials," and that preparation with these scaffolding materials was related to positive emotions and increased motivation. Students study until they feel ready for the simulation, enacting the SRL cycle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An educational management system through which materials can be shared and accessed anytime and anywhere.
- Educators inform students to study the materials to prepare for simulation-based training.
#### Constraints
- The study did not assess the differential impact of individual components, so the contribution of microlearning materials alone could not be determined.

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for simulated patient encounters
- communication skills attitudes
- self-regulated learning
