# Cooperative Teaching

## Description
Cooperative teaching involves structuring learning activities so that students work together, teaching each other concepts and skills. This can include verbally or visually explaining problems, sharing knowledge, and assisting peers. The goal is to deepen understanding through active participation, social interaction, and peer support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured tasks or problems for students to explain. Time allocated for peer interaction and teaching. Instructor guidance and support.
#### Constraints
- Requires clear instructions and guidelines. Some students may dominate the teaching process. Requires monitoring and facilitation to ensure effective peer interaction.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance understanding of concepts, improve verbal and visual explanation skills, foster social interaction, increase knowledge retention, build peer support networks, and encourage active learning

### Affordances
- Enhances learning by engaging students in active teaching roles. Promotes deeper understanding through verbal and visual explanations. Improves retention by requiring students to articulate concepts. Strengthens social-emotional memories and fosters cooperative learning environments.

### Personalization
- Tasks can be tailored to different student abilities and learning styles. Roles and responsibilities can be assigned based on individual strengths.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student participation and interaction during teaching activities. Assess comprehension through explanations, problem-solving, and peer evaluations. Gather feedback from students on the effectiveness of cooperative teaching methods.

## Impact
- Research shows that students doing the teaching are often the ones doing more of the learning. Cooperative learning is a powerful memory-enhancer because learning experiences become more memorable when social-emotional memories are part of the learning event.
