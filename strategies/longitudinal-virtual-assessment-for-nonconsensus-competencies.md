# Assess competencies lacking vOSCE consensus through longitudinal virtual assessment programs aligned with knowledge progression

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
For competencies the panel could not endorse for point-in-time vOSCE assessment — such as bonding with patients, teamwork, and commitment to training — the article recommends implementing "more longitudinal virtual assessment programs in line with the progression of relevant knowledge," citing Blythe et al. (2021). The rationale is that "A single assessment would not be appropriate for some professional skills, and it is important to investigate the interactions of professionals with their patients and teams." The strategy fits the panel's judgment that certain competencies require direct observation, ideally in real scenarios over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions must sustain assessment infrastructure across time rather than a single examination event
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is directed at competencies for which no vOSCE consensus was reached (bonding, physical examination dexterity, teamwork, commitment to training)

### Target Learners
- medical students and health professionals in training

### Target Learning Goals
- professionalism, patient bonding, teamwork, and longitudinal clinical competencies
