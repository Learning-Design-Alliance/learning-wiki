# Integrate cadaver-based ultrasound-guided procedure simulation workshops into PA curricula as low-stress, repeatable practice before live-patient training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that ultrasound needle-guidance training move into the preclinical PA curriculum using formalin-embalmed cadaver simulation, so students can repeatedly practice tactile procedural skills without patient risk. They argue "training in ultrasound needle guidance needs to become a standard part of PA school curricula to best prepare PAs to safely perform procedures." The recipe includes a pre-training instructional video, a 20-minute one-on-one supervised practice period, and an independent-instructor skills assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One-on-one instruction, which the authors note is logistically challenging and time consuming for large cohorts
- Standardized teaching rubric and orientation video shared across instructors
#### Constraints
- Self-assessed confidence is not a true measure of competency
- Skill retention after the workshop was not investigated

### Target Learners
- physician assistant students
- medical trainees learning ultrasound-guided procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- safe development of ultrasound-guided procedural skills and self-confidence before live-patient practice
