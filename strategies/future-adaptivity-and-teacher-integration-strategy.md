# Extend AI-augmented textbooks with dynamic adaptivity, richer learner attributes, and teacher-facing integration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's future-work section recommends pushing personalization and adaptivity further in AI-augmented textbooks. It proposes researching additional learner attributes through both explicit and implicit signals, making the system more adaptive by dynamically adjusting material to assessment performance and individual needs, adding more interactive elements to obtain more learner signals, and embedding the experience in learning platforms that give teachers control and insights, with feedback processes designed to optimize learning efficacy. It also plans to analyze logged sessions of Learn Your Way learners for extensive information about component use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment components whose performance signals can drive dynamic adjustment of learning material; learning-platform integration points for teacher control
#### Constraints
- A more detailed controlled experiment to isolate component contributions is possible but would be costly, per the authors

### Target Learners
- Individual learners using AI-augmented textbooks, and teachers overseeing their learning platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- Optimizing learning efficacy by focusing on learning gaps and adapting material to individual needs and difficulties

## Claims

- [In a randomized controlled trial, students learning with Learn Your Way scored higher than Digital Reader students on both immediate and three-day retention assessments](../claims/learn-your-way-rct-higher-immediate-retention-scores.md) [+M]
