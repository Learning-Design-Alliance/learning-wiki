# Install heterogeneous teams by age and experience and invest in easy-to-use assessment and reporting systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the finding that juniors score higher on professional knowledge while seniors score higher on professional experience and skills, the authors advise combining the strengths of different occupational groups within the team. They state that "it is advisable to install a heterogeneous team with regard to age and experience" and that "investing in easy to use assessment and reporting systems might be beneficial to improve dementia care". The questionnaire can also be used in curricula to evaluate students and organize 'tailored' education for healthcare teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams combining Cure and Care, Therapy and Social Support disciplines with complementary competencies
- Assessment and reporting systems that are easy to use, preferably on a digital platform cutting across settings
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a cross-sectional self-report survey; the article notes the sample's representativeness is unknown

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals in dementia care
- Healthcare managers and organizations

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving interdisciplinary Bio-Psycho-Social practice
- Combining junior knowledge with senior skills through reciprocal training
