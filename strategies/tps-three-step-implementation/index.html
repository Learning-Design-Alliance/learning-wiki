Implement think-pair-share through a three-step sequence: individual thinking, pair discussion, and whole-class sharing with instructor feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes TPS as an active-learning strategy in which "Students study on a question posed by the instructor, initially individually, secondly in pairs or groups and finally together with the entire class". The instructor gives short presentations, poses concept tests, gives individual think time of 1-2 minutes, circulates among pairs listening for misconceptions, then leads whole-class sharing and gives general feedback. The article reports roughly 10 minutes per short presentation and 5-10 minutes per concept test.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Concept tests and problems based on Bloom's Taxonomy should be simple and comprehensible, with enough time given to identify key points
  • The instructor guides and helps the learning process rather than directly presenting knowledge, and evaluates understanding by listening and observing groups during discussion

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the application procedures may need to change for different disciplines and courses

Target Learners¶

  • first-year university science and engineering students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • conceptual understanding of classical mechanics
  • epistemological beliefs about physics and physics learning

Affordances¶

  • Tps Social Cognitive Cooperative Bases

Claims¶

  • Think-pair-share instruction produced higher conceptual learning in classical mechanics than conventional teacher-centered teaching in a quasi-experimental physics course [+W]
  • Think-pair-share instruction produced more positive epistemological beliefs about physics and physics learning than conventional teacher-centered teaching, including on the PSG, SM/E and CU sub-scales [+W]
  • Approximately 80% of voluntary TPS-group students expressed positive opinions of the strategy, citing improved understanding, participation, confidence and attitude [+W]
