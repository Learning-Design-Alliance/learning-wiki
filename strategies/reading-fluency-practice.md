# Reading Fluency Practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Reading fluency practice gives learners repeated, supported opportunities to read connected text aloud with feedback, targeting the three components of fluency: accuracy, automaticity (rate), and prosody. Core formats include repeated reading of the same passage until a criterion is met, paired or choral reading, and listening-while-reading with recorded models. The goal is not speed for its own sake but automatic word recognition, which releases working memory for meaning-making [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Fluency practice works because oral reading with feedback provides high-density trials of accurate word recognition, driving the orthographic mapping that underlies automaticity [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Repeated reading of the *same* passage builds performance on that passage efficiently, but transfer to new text requires practice across multiple passages at an appropriate difficulty level [~M]. Text selection and feedback quality matter more than repetition alone.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional-level texts (roughly 90–95% word accuracy); frustration-level text undermines gains
- A model of fluent reading — teacher, peer, or recorded audio ([Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md), [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md))
- Immediate, specific feedback on errors, not just timing ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md))
- A progress-monitoring routine (words correct per minute, prosody rubric) used formatively ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))

#### Constraints
- Repetition of a single passage yields passage-specific gains with limited generalization; vary texts [~M]
- Round-robin reading in whole class settings provides few practice trials per learner and creates anxiety; replace with paired or whisper reading [-M]
- Timed practice framed as competition can depress motivation for struggling readers [~W]
- For learners still decoding at the word level, fluency practice is premature — phonics instruction must come first [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Repeated reading** — learner rereads a passage 3–4 times until criterion rate/accuracy
- **Paired reading** — learner and fluent partner read in unison, then learner reads alone
- **Listening-while-reading** — recorded narration ([Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md)) provides the model; effective for older struggling readers [~M]
- **Reader's Theater** — repeated rehearsal of a script for performance adds purpose and prosody focus [~M]

### Target Learners
- Mid-elementary readers who decode accurately but slowly [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Older struggling readers and adolescents with dyslexia, using age-respectful texts and recorded models [~M]
- English learners, with attention to prosody patterns of English [~W]
- Not appropriate for beginners still mastering letter–sound correspondences [-S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Automatic word recognition and decoding consolidation
- Prosodic reading (phrasing, expression) that supports comprehension of syntax and punctuation
- Reading stamina and confidence with connected text

### Instructions
1. Diagnose current accuracy and rate with a brief one-minute probe ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)); select text at instructional level.
2. Provide a fluent model — teacher read-aloud or audio ([Modeling](../elements/modeling.md), [Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md)).
3. Have the learner reread the passage 3–4 times, in unison then alone, with immediate error correction ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md), [Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
4. Chart progress toward a criterion and move to a new passage once met ([Scaffolding](../elements/scaffolding.md), [Adaptive Difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md)).
5. Transfer to unseen text weekly to verify generalization, not just passage mastery.

## Examples
- **Read Naturally** (https://www.readnaturally.com) — combines audio modeling, repeated reading, and progress graphing; widely studied in Tier 2 interventions.
- **Great Leaps** (https://www.greatleaps.com) — timed one-minute probes across phonics, phrases, and stories.
- **Reader's Theater** in classrooms such as those described by Rasinski — script rehearsal builds prosody with an authentic performance purpose.
