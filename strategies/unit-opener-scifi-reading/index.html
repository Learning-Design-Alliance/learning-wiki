Begin each science unit with science-fiction reading to draw attention to the subject¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy recommends placing science-fiction reading at the beginning of each science unit so the reading draws student attention to the upcoming subject matter. The article's suggestion list states: "In science lessons, at the beginning of each unit, reading science -fiction books can get more attention to the subject." This positions the books as an advance organizer for the unit's science content, leveraging the curiosity the books arouse to focus students on the lesson topic.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A science-fiction book matched to the unit's science content
  • Time in the lesson schedule for reading

Constraints¶

  • The article's supporting evidence is from a study where books were read alongside the curriculum over an application period, not specifically tested as unit openers

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • attention to science topics
  • science curiosity

Claims¶

  • Most students reported in diaries that each book in a mostly science-fiction reading set increased their science curiosity, with 24 to 29 of 30 saying so per book [+W]
  • Content analysis of student diaries identifies specific science concepts that arouse the most curiosity, including extra-terrestrials, celestial bodies, space vehicles, genetics, and simple machines [+W]
  • Middle-school students who read a mostly science-fiction book set alongside the science curriculum gained significantly in science curiosity, in a study whose groups already differed at pre-test [+W]
