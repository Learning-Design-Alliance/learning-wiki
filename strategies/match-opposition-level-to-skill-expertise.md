# Match task opposition level to player expertise: greater numerical superiority for lower-skilled players

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends designing small-sided games whose level of opposition fits the players' skill level. For lower-skilled groups it concludes "it is necessary to use modified games with greater numerical superiority in attack (e.g. 3 vs. 1 or 4 vs. 2)"; for average-skilled players, coaches should design tasks with lower complexity than competition, with numerical superiority in attack, so players have more time to decide. The strategy rests on the finding that task difficulty must be adapted to skill level to improve perceptual and attentive abilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of the players' level of expertise before designing constraint modifications
- Coaches able to manipulate rules, player numbers and space in modified games
#### Constraints
- The study found numerical equality did not improve either group, and 14 sessions of moderate superiority were not enough for the low skill-level group

### Target Learners
- youth footballers of differing skill levels (U12)

### Target Learning Goals
- improved decision-making
- improved technical execution in game play
