# Strengths-Based IEPs

## Description
A shift in mindset where the Individualized Education Program (IEP) focuses as much on the learner’s strengths as it does on their weaknesses, leveraging abilities to help address needs. This approach involves identifying the learner's strengths, interests, and preferences, and using those insights to develop strategies for success. It promotes self-awareness and self-advocacy and requires IEP teams to gather more information, often from the learners themselves. By reframing the way strengths are discussed and weaving them into IEP goals, IEPs become more positive and actionable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to strength finder tools, a commitment from the IEP team to shift mindset, and the inclusion of learners in the IEP process.
#### Constraints
- Not yet widely used. May require teacher training in strengths-based IEPs and may require districts to rewrite their IEP templates.

## Assessment Evidence
- Whether IEP goals are written in a strengths-based manner, the level of learner involvement in IEP meetings, and the inclusion of the learner's strengths, interests, and preferences in IEP goals.

## Impact
- More positive and actionable IEPs with SMART goals built around what the learner can do and how the team can use that ability to work on an area of weakness.
