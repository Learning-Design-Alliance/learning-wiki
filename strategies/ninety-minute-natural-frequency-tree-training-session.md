# 90-minute risk-literacy training session built around natural frequency trees and PPV/NPV curves with small-group practice on real diagnostic tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The training session teaches two tools: natural frequency trees, to facilitate calculation of PPVs and NPVs, and PPV/NPV curves, to enhance understanding of the interplay between PPV/NPV, sensitivity, specificity and prevalence. Its structure is "a 15 min theoretical introduction, a 45 min small-group exercise in which students calculated the PPV/NPV of four commonly used diagnostic procedures (sigmoidoscopy/HIV combined test/neck fold-test/amniocentesis) and a subsequent 30 min discussion on the numerical and ethical implications of diagnostic risk assessment". Student engagement was increased by using real tests from areas of medicine taught in the final semester and dedicating the majority of the session to practice and discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires students to practise the tools on actual tests using their real statistical properties
- Embedded in a compulsory medical curriculum course
#### Constraints
- Tested only at a 90-minute retention interval in this study
- No evidence for long-term retention of PPV/NPV curves currently exists

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- calculating positive and negative predictive values
- understanding sensitivity, specificity and prevalence interplay
- Bayesian reasoning about diagnostic tests
