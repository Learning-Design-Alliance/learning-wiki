Provide scaffolding and a safe space for reflection to ensure depth despite large class sizes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors note that in large classes reflection tends to be very descriptive, as seen in Fekete et al. (2000) with hundreds of students. To ensure reflection with depth while carrying out other teaching responsibilities, "teachers can make use of teaching practices or learning activities that provide scaffolding and a safe space for reflection". Teachers should also consider the length, topic, means, and timing of reflection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teaching practices or learning activities that provide scaffolding
  • A safe space for students to think about their learning tasks or experiences

Constraints¶

  • Very large classes may limit reflection to descriptive levels

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education students in large-enrollment courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Deep and critical reflection on learning

Related Strategies¶

  • Define a specific scope for reflection in language classrooms rather than using open reflection
  • Prioritize meaningfulness of reflection over accuracy of language use when assessing learner reflection
