# Physics departments of any size can involve majors in facilitating informal outreach to build identity, belonging, and career skills without significant financial investment or curriculum changes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (11 for) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics departments involve undergraduate majors in facilitating informal physics outreach programs as a low-cost route to disciplinary identity, belonging, and career-readiness skills. As printed, "These benefits, which en- hance students’ university experience by involving them in outreach facilitation, can be achieved by physics de- partments of any size without requiring significant finan- cial investment or major curriculum changes." The national survey results, drawn from SPS chapters at institutions with variably developed programs, are offered as evidence that these benefits are not limited to departments with well-developed outreach programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Opportunities for students to interact with authentic public audiences, which the article identifies as a central feature of informal physics programs
#### Constraints
- The article notes the observed impacts are an intrinsic feature of audience interactions and may not be replicable within a formal curriculum

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors across institution types

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills
- physics identity
- sense of belonging
- career readiness
