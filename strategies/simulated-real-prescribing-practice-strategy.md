# Improve prescribing skills through simulated-patient role-play, real supervised prescriptions, constructive alignment and a final prescribing assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend, in line with the EACPT, four concrete improvements to pharmacology and CPT education: making CPT "a clear and visible programme integrated longitudinally throughout the entire medical curriculum, starting as early as possible"; having students "take part in role-playing sessions with simulated patients and write drug prescriptions for real patients during clinical clerkships, under supervision of an experienced clinician"; ensuring constructive alignment so skills are assessed with OSCEs and workplace-based assessments rather than MCQs; and implementing a valid, reliable prescribing assessment at or near the end of the curriculum, as in the UK Prescribing Safety Assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires CPT teachers and clerkship supervisors; skills assessment requires OSCE and workplace-based assessment formats rather than MCQs
#### Constraints
- The article notes integration can be challenging for schools with few CPT teachers; the recommended national assessment exists in some European countries but not all

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students, especially final-year students preparing to prescribe

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and rational prescribing skills and readiness for prescribing as a junior doctor
