# Match control games to the experimental intervention in fun, variety and novelty to preserve double blinding in cognitive training trials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that in game-based cognitive training RCTs, "the control games should be balanced in all aspects of fun, variety, complexity and novelty with the experimental game-based intervention", because mismatched fun can unblind families and skew adherence and parent ratings. It also suggests multiple converging symptom reporters (teachers and clinicians in addition to parents) to verify successful blinding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- a control activity that controls for placebo effects, practice effects, disciplined computer activity, research contact and motivation
#### Constraints
- a non-performance-adaptive version of the training as control can become boring and repetitive over long home-training periods, making adherence problematic

### Target Learners
- children with ADHD in home-based training trials

### Target Learning Goals
- valid evaluation of cognitive training effects
