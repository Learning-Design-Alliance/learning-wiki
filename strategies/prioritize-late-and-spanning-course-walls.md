# Prioritize redesign of late, back-to-back, and spanning Course Walls, where momentum recovery is least likely

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that when Course Walls are identified, designers prioritize the walls latest in the course. They argue students who pass one wall regain momentum only to hit the next with diminished momentum, and that "Late Course Walls are perhaps the worst situation for course providers, course designers, and students" because students give up after substantial course work. For spanning walls, they advise focusing on the group of lessons where the wall occurs, giving initial priority to the last few lessons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of Course Walls through graphing the Student Momentum Indicator on a lesson-by-lesson basis
#### Constraints
- The authors caution a course should not be redesigned for the sole purpose of reducing Course Walls, since making a course easy would eliminate walls at the cost of pedagogical and academic integrity

### Target Learners
- instructional designers of university-level online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing dropout rates through targeted course revision
