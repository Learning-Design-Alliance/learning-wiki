# Teach Self-Regulation Skills

## Description
Self-regulation is the ability to alter and regulate our emotional and behavioral responses. In this strategy, educators teach specific skills (like metacognitive awareness) and strategies to help students control their actions and reactions to engage positively in classroom activities. The focus is on helping students manage their responses in an emotionally safe context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an understanding of self-regulation principles, knowledge of specific techniques (e.g., mindfulness, journaling), and a safe, supportive classroom environment.
#### Constraints
- Requires ongoing practice and reinforcement. Effectiveness depends on student engagement and willingness to apply the strategies.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor student behavior and emotional responses in challenging situations. Observe student use of self-regulation strategies. Collect student feedback on the effectiveness of the strategies.

## Impact
- Research shows that self-regulation skills can be improved through targeted instruction and practice. (Bridgett, Burt, Edwards, & Deater-Deckard, 2015; Pintrich & de Groot, 1990; Zimmerman & Martinez-Pons, 1990)
