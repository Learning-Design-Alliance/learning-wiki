# Integrate voluntary handheld ultrasound practice into undergraduate eFAST curricula alongside digital self-study

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curriculum strategy in which students receive standardised digital preparation and, in addition, a handheld ultrasound device for voluntary self-directed practice before a supervised course day. The article's conclusion states: "These findings support the integration of handheld ultrasound into undergraduate curricula as part of broader competency-based training in clinical reasoning and procedural skills." In the trial, devices were distributed eight days before the course with a brief introduction and written guide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of handheld ultrasound devices for voluntary self-practice, a brief device introduction and written guide, standardised digital preparation materials, and email technical support
#### Constraints
- The trial was single-centre and simulator-based and did not evaluate long-term skill retention or transfer to patient care, so the authors state results should not be extrapolated to clinical performance

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Practical eFAST/POCUS competence, scan completeness, and examination efficiency
