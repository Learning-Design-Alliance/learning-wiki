# SPF learning cycle: begin problem solving in pictorial and visual language frames before algorithmic execution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a cyclical sequence for physics problem solving in which "the initial approach to problem solving – should typically begin with pictorial and visual language frames", because these provide immediate perceptual access and activate prior knowledge before abstract conceptual and mathematical reasoning. Engaging semantic frames before algorithmic execution is said to encourage learners to interpret equations in context, test limiting cases, and assess applicability, so equations acquire physical meaning rather than remaining isolated computational tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom problem-solving activities structured around SPF
- Students engage in exploration and resolution rather than a linear conceptual-to-algorithmic path
#### Constraints
- The cycle is presented as a theoretical exercise and representation aligned with SPF, not as an empirically tested intervention

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting mathematics with physical meaning
- expert-like problem-solving strategies
- metacognitive awareness of framing
