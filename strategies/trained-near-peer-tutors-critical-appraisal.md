# Deploy trained recently graduated doctors as near-peer tutors for critical appraisal skills teaching where faculty resources are limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends assigning trained near-peer tutors to teach critical appraisal skills: near-peer tutors were "as effective as and more readily accepted than staff tutors" in a randomized crossover trial, and the authors state the findings "support the broader implementation of peer-teaching in other areas of medical education". Tutors should complete a training-of-teachers programme focused on tutoring skills and group dynamics before taking groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Near-peer tutors receive a structured training of teachers course before tutoring
- Tutors have passed the same module and practised the skills themselves
#### Constraints
- Generalisation may be limited by student characteristics and EBM as a distinctive subject; FMUI students are a select group; the crossover design meant no student had only a near-peer tutor

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical appraisal of scientific papers and evidence-based medicine competence

### Affordances
- Cognitive Congruence Theory Near Peer
