Implement structured read-aloud with planned stops, guiding and higher-order questions, and modeled vocabulary meaning-finding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's intervention procedure is a structured read-aloud routine: teachers read aloud at least three days per week in 15- to 20-minute periods, and "teachers stop, ask guiding and higher -order thinking questions of the students to gauge and assist comprehension." At these stops teachers model how to use the text to find comprehension answers and address unfamiliar vocabulary, so students can later employ the patterns during silent reading, content reading, and testing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Time spent on read-aloud was to average at least three days per week for 15- to 20-minute time periods
  • Teachers in the treatment group received professional development before and during the study

Constraints¶

  • Although treatment differed in delivery by teacher, the strategy remained the same - read aloud

Target Learners¶

  • sixth-grade middle school students not receiving special education or additional reading intervention services

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension
  • vocabulary acquisition

Claims¶

  • Descriptive DORA gains in the treatment group were greater for males on comprehension and for females on vocabulary [~W]
  • Gender was not a significant factor in read-aloud treatment effects on DORA scores [~W]
  • Read-Aloud instruction produced a significant main effect on comprehension but not on vocabulary in middle school students [+W]
  • A year-long Structured Read-Aloud intervention produced significantly greater overall DORA gains than traditional middle-school reading instruction [+W]
