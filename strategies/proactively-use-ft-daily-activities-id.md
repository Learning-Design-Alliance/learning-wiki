# Proactively use mobile devices as functional technology in the daily activities of people with intellectual disabilities across settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers and practitioners extend mobile device use beyond classroom instruction into daily routines, work, and community settings to improve independence and integration, including participation in cyber communities. It states: "Researchers and practitioners should proactively use FT such as mobile devices in the daily activities of people with ID  in various settings in order to improve their independence and integration on and off the cyber communities." The recommendation rests on the meta-analytic finding that interventions were very effective and mostly confined to instructional use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practitioners and researchers adopt mobile devices in daily activities, not only as instructional technology in the classroom
- Exploration and implementation of appropriate apps and internet resources
#### Constraints
- The article notes research on mobile device effectiveness in various settings and functions beyond instructional technology is in a beginning stage

### Target Learners
- individuals with intellectual disabilities
- students with disabilities in school and community settings

### Target Learning Goals
- independence in daily activities
- social integration and community participation
- technology skills beyond assistive technology
