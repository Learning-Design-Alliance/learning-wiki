# EQ Feeling Chart

## Description
Pair students and check-in with each other using an EQ Feeling Chart. Facilitate discussion by providing questions to explore feelings and their origins. Students then share their observations with the whole class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EQ Feeling Chart (downloadable resource), time for pair discussions and whole-class sharing
#### Constraints
- Students may be hesitant to share their feelings with others, especially in a large group. It is important to create a safe and supportive environment where students feel comfortable sharing. Some students may not be able to identify their feelings or articulate them clearly.

### Target Learning Goals

- Communicate feelings, identify the source of emotions, promote sharing and empathy

### Affordances
- Provides a structured way for students to explore and express their feelings, fostering emotional awareness and empathy. Normalizes talking about feelings and empowers students to view their emotions as assets.

### Personalization
- The EQ Feeling Chart can be adapted to different age groups and developmental levels. Students can create their own feeling charts or use pre-made ones. Instructors can modify the discussion questions to suit the needs of their students.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student participation and engagement in discussions. Collect student feedback on the activity to assess its effectiveness.

## Impact
- The simple act of acknowledging our emotions helps calm the amygdala and reduce the intensity of the emotions we feel. By naming our emotions, we feel more in control. We are less likely to experience an emotional hijack when we name our feelings, and students can create space to make intentional choices about how to move forward.
