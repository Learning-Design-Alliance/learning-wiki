Incorporate processing instruction into EFL grammar curricula, tying grammar instruction to input¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's findings, the authors recommend that curriculum developers incorporate PI into EFL curricula and that grammar instruction be tied to input. They state that "In curriculum development based on PI, it is certain that PI requires a structural syllabus taught by means of SI activities" and that PI is entirely input-based, with structured input activities presentable in both written and oral form. The strategy targets teachers selecting techniques for teaching target grammatical features.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A structural syllabus taught by means of structured input activities, with explicit information on target structures followed by input-based rather than production-based practice

Constraints¶

  • The authors note many questions about PI remain unanswered and that changes in assessment measures might yield different results; assessment measures differing substantially from treatment activities should be devised

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving EFL learners' writing ability
  • noticing and using target grammatical structures

Affordances¶

  • Processing Instruction Vanpatten Model

Claims¶

  • The PI group performed significantly better than the TI group on the writing post-test after equivalent pre-tests [+W]
  • Processing instruction significantly improves Iranian EFL learners' writing ability from pre-test to post-test [+W]
  • Prior studies reviewed in this article report PI superior to mechanical output practice for acquisition, especially on interpretation tasks [+W]
