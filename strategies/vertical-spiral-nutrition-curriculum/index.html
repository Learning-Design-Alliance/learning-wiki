Integrate nutrition throughout medical training using a vertical spiral curriculum with practical teaching methods such as cooking lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends curricular innovations that embed nutrition across all years rather than as an isolated component. It highlights a University of Cambridge initiative where "a vertical spiral curriculum was introduced so that students could develop their nutrition competencies and build on them every year". Practical methods such as cooking lessons and use of the hidden curriculum across biochemistry and physiology are advocated to build confidence and personal health habits.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Curriculum redesign capacity and integration across specialties so nutrition concepts are revisited and expanded each year

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • nutrition knowledge for patient counselling
  • nutrition competencies across clinical specialties

Claims¶

  • The review reports that most UK medical students receive less than 2 hours of nutrition education and that 75% of US medical schools have no required clinical nutrition classes [+W]
  • The review reports that low confidence in counselling patients and perceived insignificance of nutrition contribute to the knowledge deficit among medical students and doctors [+W]
  • The review names stigma, personal health habits and clinical practice constraints (time, funding, guidance, low dietitian referrals) among the reasons for the nutrition knowledge gap [+W]
  • The review reports a lack of comprehensive guidelines and mandatory enforcement as a principal reason for inconsistent nutrition education across medical schools [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide dedicated nutrition counselling slots, extended consultations and postgraduate nutrition pathways including lifestyle medicine curricula and nutrition subspecialties
  • Adopt a dietitian–clinician co-teacher model and multidisciplinary teaching teams to clarify roles and increase appropriate referrals
  • Integrate culinary medicine into compulsory German medical curricula with prevention and interprofessional learning contexts
