# Shift the professional feedback culture from avoiding performance feedback to actively seeking feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that attempts to improve the effectiveness of peer assessment "should not be limited to the modification of the PA tool" but include a cultural shift among physical therapists in primary care. Physical therapists in communities of practice discussed cases regularly but lacked a culture of asking for and providing performance feedback; the recommendation is to move from avoiding performance feedback to actively seeking it, alongside adjusting the tight time schedule and number of cases to allow more elaboration and recuperation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An environment where mistakes can easily be made but participants' self-confidence remains undamaged.
#### Constraints
- Effective peer assessment practices are culture dependent, and the generalizability of the study's results might be limited because all participants were Dutch.

### Target Learners
- Physical therapists in primary care organized in communities of practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Guideline adherence and continuous quality improvement of clinical performance
