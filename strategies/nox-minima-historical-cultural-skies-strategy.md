# Use the dome to reproduce skies from past epochs and indigenous cultural asterisms for archaeoastronomy and ethnoastronomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The dome can represent skies from past epochs, valuable for archaeoastronomy, since Earth's axial precession shifts the celestial pole over roughly 13,000 years and proper motion gradually shifts stellar relative positions; these corrections can be computed using SkyField, with planetary and lunar positions available as far back as 3000 BC. For ethnoastronomy, "Asterisms represented as connecting lines between stars or sky regions can easily be incorporated into the nox-minima dome", with four Brazilian indigenous asterisms currently available: the Rhea, the Old Man, the Northern Tapir, and the Deer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SkyField computations for precession and proper motion corrections; access to indigenous asterism line data
#### Constraints
- Currently only four Brazilian indigenous asterisms are available; new asterisms from ongoing research can be incorporated

### Target Learners
- students in Brazilian classrooms
- archaeoastronomy researchers and presenters

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding axial precession and proper motion
- appreciating indigenous cultural perspectives on the sky
