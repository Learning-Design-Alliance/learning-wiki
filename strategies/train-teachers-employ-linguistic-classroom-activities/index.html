Strategies

Give linguistic classroom activity programs educational status in English teaching and train teachers through regular sessions to employ them¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends, on the basis of its findings, that linguistic classroom activity programs be given their educational value in general-education English teaching, and that teachers and supervisors receive regular training sessions in employing these activities. It also recommends "the integration of thinki ng in teaching English language" so teachers gain modern trends in active learning and thinking-skills development.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers and supervisors trained to employ linguistic classroom activities in English classes

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence comes from a single quasi-experimental group of third-grade intermediate students at one school in Makkah

Target Learners¶

  • third-grade intermediate students learning English as a second language

Target Learning Goals¶

  • oral linguistic performance skills in English (words, structures, ideas)
  • decision-making skill

Claims¶

  • Training in linguistic classroom activities is followed by statistically significant pre-post gains in decision-making skill, with eta squared of 0.91 [+W]
  • Training in linguistic classroom activities is followed by statistically significant pre-post gains in oral linguistic performance on the focus of ideas, with eta squared of 0.97 [+W]
  • Training in linguistic classroom activities is followed by statistically significant pre-post gains in overall oral linguistic performance in English, with eta squared of 0.97 [+W]
  • Training in linguistic classroom activities is followed by statistically significant pre-post gains in oral linguistic performance on the focus of structures, with eta squared of 0.95 [+W]
  • Training in linguistic classroom activities is followed by statistically significant pre-post gains in oral linguistic performance on the focus of words among third-grade intermediate students, with eta squared of 0.93 [+W]
  • Post-application scores in oral linguistic performance and decision-making are strongly positively correlated (r = 0.989) [+W]
