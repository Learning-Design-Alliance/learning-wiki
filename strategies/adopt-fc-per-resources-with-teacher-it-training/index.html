Adopt flipped classroom in preventive medicine courses according to institutional resources, with teacher information-technology training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 11 claims (11 for) · 3 studies (3 quant-synthesis), q3 · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that medical colleges "could consider promoting this model according to their specific circumstances", taking account of "resources such as faculty and facilities" when applying FC in relevant course teaching. It further recommends "emphasizing the development of teachers’ information technology teaching abilities to ensure the effectiveness of the reform". This is the authors' own forward-looking implementation recommendation drawn from the synthesis.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Faculty and facilities resources sufficient to run pre-class and in-class FC activities
  • Teachers with developed information technology teaching abilities

Constraints¶

  • The authors state findings are limited to studies conducted in China and should be interpreted with caution

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate preventive medicine students in Chinese medical colleges

Target Learning Goals¶

  • preventive medicine course outcomes including exam performance and core competencies

Claims¶

  • Flipped classroom improves cooperation and communication skills (pooled RR 1.60) [+W]
  • Flipped classroom improves independent learning and thinking ability (pooled RR 1.51) [+W]
  • Flipped classroom increases learning interest (pooled RR 1.42) but this pooled estimate shows significant publication bias [+W]
  • Flipped classroom significantly improves practical exam scores over lecture-based learning (pooled SMD 0.50) [+W]
  • Flipped classroom improves problem analysis and solving ability (pooled RR 1.47) [+W]
  • Flipped classroom improves teacher-student interaction (pooled RR 1.35), based on few studies [+W]
  • Flipped classroom significantly improves theoretical exam scores over lecture-based learning in Chinese undergraduate preventive medicine courses (pooled SMD 0.99) [+W]
  • Flipped classroom has a moderately positive effect on college students' academic performance (SMD = 0.66) [+W]
  • Flipped classrooms are associated with higher theoretical knowledge scores than lecture-based teaching in medical education (pooled SMD = 1.26), with very high heterogeneity [+W]
  • Flipped classrooms are associated with improved case analysis/problem-solving ability in medical education (pooled SMD = 1.50), though statistical significance was not retained in all leave-one-out analyses [+W]
  • Flipped classrooms are associated with higher learner satisfaction in medical education (pooled SMD = 0.69), with residents showing larger improvement than undergraduates (SMD 1.69 vs 0.37) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design flipped classroom instruction according to subject type, knowledge type, and students' independent learning ability
  • Promote the flipped classroom in low-resource settings using free digital tools, peer instruction, and group discussions.
  • Adopt flipped classroom instruction, with particular attention to at-risk students, supported by institutional infrastructure and teacher video-design training
