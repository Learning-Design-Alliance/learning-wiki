# Emphasis on the Learning Process

## Description
This strategy shifts learner attention from outcomes (grades, scores, rankings) toward the processes that produce learning — effort, strategy use, revision, and self-monitoring. It is enacted through process-focused feedback, reflective discussion, and assessment practices that reward improvement and strategy quality. Without this emphasis, learners may base self-perceptions of ability on grades alone, which promotes a fixed mindset and avoidance of challenge.

## Design Implications

Process-focused praise and feedback produce more persistent, mastery-oriented behavior than person- or grade-focused feedback [Praise for effort and strategy sustains persistence after setbacks better than praise for ability.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.1.33) [+S]. Framing goals around learning processes rather than performance outcomes improves novice achievement [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M], and feedback directed at the task and process level outperforms feedback directed at the self or the grade [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Structured self-monitoring of one's own learning process strengthens self-regulation [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular opportunities for structured reflection on what was done, what was difficult, and what strategies were used
- Feedback and grading schemes that reference strategies, progress, and revision — not just correctness ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))
- Instructor modeling of process talk: discussing one's own mistakes, revisions, and reasoning openly
- Classroom norms that treat errors as information rather than failure

#### Constraints
- Highly grade-driven learners may dismiss process talk as irrelevant until grading practices change to match; exhortation without assessment alignment is ineffective [-M]
- Effort praise applied indiscriminately can backfire — praising effort after failure can signal low ability expectations [~M]
- In high-stakes, norm-referenced environments, process emphasis conflicts with the reward structure and may be undermined by it [-M]
- Requires sustained facilitation skill; superficial "reflection" activities become compliance rituals with no learning benefit [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Written reflective journals vs. oral discussion vs. portfolio-based self-assessment
- Process-focused rubrics that explicitly score strategy use and revision quality
- Error analysis routines (e.g., exam wrappers, "my favorite mistake" discussions)
- Learning-to-learn framing at course start vs. continuous process reflection throughout

### Target Learners
- Novices, who benefit most when goals and feedback target learning processes rather than outcomes [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Grade-focused or fixed-mindset learners who attribute failure to stable ability
- Learners building self-regulatory habits; monitoring one's own process is a core self-regulation skill [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Less effective for advanced learners who already regulate their own learning well [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive: strategy evaluation, self-monitoring, reflection on learning
- Motivational: valuing learning over grades, growth mindset, persistence after setbacks
- Dispositional: intellectual risk-taking, comfort with productive struggle

### Instructions
1. Open the course by naming the learning process explicitly — how skills develop through effort, strategy, and revision — and connect this to learner beliefs about ability ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
2. During tasks, prompt learners to articulate their strategies and reasoning, not just their answers ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md))
3. Deliver feedback that names specific strategies and their effects, and identifies next process steps ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
4. After major assessments, run structured reflection on preparation strategies and error patterns ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or written [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) wrappers)
5. Align grading so that revision, process documentation, or improvement carries weight — otherwise the process message is contradicted by the reward structure

## Examples
- **Exam wrappers** (widely used in physics and math courses, e.g., at Carnegie Mellon's Eberly Center, [https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/examwrappers.html](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/examwrappers.html)) — students answer brief questions about how they prepared and what they would change before receiving their graded exam, redirecting attention from the score to the process.
- **Process-focused praise studies** — Mueller & Dweck's experiments showed that children praised for effort after success subsequently chose harder tasks and persisted longer than children praised for intelligence [+S].
- **Portfolio assessment with revision narratives** — students submit drafts plus a short memo describing what they revised and why, making the learning process an assessed artifact.
