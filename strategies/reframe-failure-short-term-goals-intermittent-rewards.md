# Reframe failure as part of success, break long-term goals into short-term controllable action goals, and use randomized intermittent external rewards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy package leverages the dopamine system to sustain motivation through failure-heavy learning. Learners frame failure as being on the path to success, set short-term goals within their control (such as a daily study hour) rather than outcome goals, and make external rewards intermittent (e.g., coin-flip rewards) so they stay satisfying. The review proposes formative assessment and sharing this information with students, while cautioning the interventions are largely untested in education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pursuits framed as valuable must be voluntary for dopamine to attach to them
- Framing difficult pursuits as valuable takes time; the review cites habits taking at least 18 days to form
#### Constraints
- The review states the efficacy of these interventions cannot be assumed because long-term behavior change is difficult and they have largely not been tested in educational settings

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining motivation and persistence through failure while learning
