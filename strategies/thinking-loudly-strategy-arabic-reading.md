# Use the thinking loudly strategy to move students out of isolation and build self-expression during reading lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Thinking loudly asks students to verbalize their thinking so teachers can see and correct their reasoning while students hear and monitor their own ideas. The author reports that "thinking loudly get the student out of the isolated atmospheres, create her communication means and improve the abilities of self-expression", enabling skills such as concentration, good listening and attention accuracy that supported the significant post-test gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must verbalize their thinking aloud so the teacher can review wrong or incomplete inference, as described in the article's account of the strategy
#### Constraints
- Tested only with second middle grade female students in Najran; the author calls for further studies on other variables like attitudes towards Arabic language and information retention

### Target Learners
- second middle grade female students

### Target Learning Goals
- analytical reading skills
- linguistic intelligence
- self-expression
