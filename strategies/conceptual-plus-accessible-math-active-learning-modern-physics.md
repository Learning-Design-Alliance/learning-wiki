# Combine conceptual-qualitative understanding with accessible mathematical treatment and active learning tasks when teaching modern physics to non-specialists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recipe for making GR and cosmology teachable at upper secondary level: "Targeted teaching and learning materials are focused on conceptual and qualitative understanding, while systematically combined with a mathematical treatment accessible at the upper secondary level, avoiding oversimplification." A key element is "an active learning approach, incorporating activities and tasks such as engaging applications related to current research, reflective exercises, thought experiments, and hands-on tasks", e.g. students verifying geodesic behavior with a ribbon on a three-dimensional plastic surface and calculating redshifts from telescope images.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials must avoid the two extremes the article identifies: purely qualitative 'zero equations' treatments and expert-level treatments
- Access to current research applications and data (e.g. satellite telescope images) to support engaging applications
#### Constraints
- The article warns that excessive simplification aimed at making modern physics accessible can have negative effects on students' understanding
- The rigorous mathematics of GR remains far too advanced for upper secondary students, so Newtonian treatments with qualitative justifications are used instead

### Target Learners
- Upper secondary non-specialist physics students with diverse backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual and quantitative understanding of modern physics topics
- Reactivation and strengthening of classical physics background through application to new contexts
