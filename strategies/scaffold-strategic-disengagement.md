# Adaptive systems should scaffold strategic disengagement as a reflective metacognitive choice rather than penalize it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue disengagement can be purposeful, goal-oriented self-regulation: LPK students deliberately skipped advanced tasks to manage cognitive load, and HPK students skipped foundational tasks for efficiency. They recommend systems make skipping a reflective act, for example via skip buttons with dropdown reasons (e.g., already mastered, feeling overloaded) instead of generic Next or Continue buttons. They argue "strategic pauses and task avoidance can foster metacognitive reﬂ ection and better goal alignment, ultimately enhancing learning outcomes".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interface elements that let learners articulate reasons for skipping (e.g., dropdown reason selection)
- A design stance that treats disengagement data as actionable learner information rather than mere off-task behavior
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a short, single-session study with a small, culturally homogeneous sample; the authors call for cross-cultural replication and longitudinal designs

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students using adaptive learning systems, at both low and high prior knowledge

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive regulation, learner agency, and adaptive expertise in deciding when to engage or strategically disengage
