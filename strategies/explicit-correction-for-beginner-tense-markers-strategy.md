# Use explicit correction rather than or-choice feedback with beginner EFL learners working on tense markers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study's results, the article recommends explicit correction — directly supplying the correct form, as in 'My father works' not 'my father work' — over or-choice feedback for beginner learners producing English tense markers in communicatively oriented classrooms. The author states the study "contributes to the arguments about the benefit of different types of corrective feedback by confirming that explicit correction is more beneficial compared to or-choice feedback to the beginner Iranian EFL learners".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback delivered immediately during communicative classroom interaction on targeted tense-marker errors
#### Constraints
- The author states these findings "may not be generalizable to other instructional contexts"; evidence comes from one small two-class study of beginners

### Target Learners
- Beginner Iranian EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate production of English tense markers
