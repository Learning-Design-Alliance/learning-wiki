# Organize mindfulness and adaptability courses and workshops for university students, especially new students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities actively build students' mindfulness and adaptability skills through structured offerings. It states that "university administrators facilitate the enhancement of students’ mindfulness and adaptability by organizing relevant courses and workshops", and its conclusion adds workshops "especially for new students" so they can adapt to the academic environment. Recommended techniques include being present at the moment, understanding emotions, practicing focus, and recognizing and controlling a wandering mind.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support from university policymakers and decision-makers to organize the courses and workshops
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a cross-sectional, self-report study in one Iranian medical sciences university; the authors state the results cannot be broadly generalized

### Target Learners
- University students, especially new students at medical sciences universities

### Target Learning Goals
- Enhancing mindfulness skills and adaptability to support academic achievement
