Use empirically derived post-intervention benchmarks to decide who exits versus continues to intensive intervention¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools distinguish students who have adequately responded from those who need sustained or individualized services using technically strong post-intervention benchmarks rather than arbitrary cutoffs. It states "research is needed to provide schools with technically strong post-intervention benchmarks for identifying students who are not adequately prepared to exit intervention." Requiring benchmark attainment before exit may also address intervention fade-out effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Benchmarks must be empirically derived and technically strong, differentiated by grade level and mathematics subdomain to forecast long-term success.

Constraints¶

  • The 25th-percentile-of-classmates criterion used in the study is acknowledged by the authors to be arbitrary.

Target Learners¶

  • at-risk first-grade students receiving standard (non-individualized) math intervention

Target Learning Goals¶

  • first-grade arithmetic skill and transfer to calculations and number knowledge

Claims¶

  • Inadequate response rates are far higher when indexed by final performance level than by normalized growth [+W]
  • Pre-intervention math performance does not forecast which at-risk students will respond inadequately to intervention [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate post-reclassification monitoring and support so students who exit EL status continue receiving integrated support in the general curriculum
  • Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)
  • Screen for and monitor children at risk of nonresponse to universal SWPBIS and target nonresponders for more intensive tiered interventions
  • Response To Intervention
  • Use nonresponse to generic tier-two preschool behavioral intervention as a trigger for formal ADHD diagnostic referral and more intensive tier-three intervention
