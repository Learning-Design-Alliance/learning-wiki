# Explain flipped classroom principles and the integration of online and in-person activities at the start of the course to foster favourable perceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, because students' perceptions of the learning environment related to their approach, time and achievement, teachers should explicitly explain how online activities complement in-person learning and introduce the principles of flipped classrooms early. They state that "teachers should explain the principles of flipped classrooms, especially when the majority of the enrolled students have not experienced this type of learning and teaching", and suggest giving students opportunities to apply knowledge from pre-recorded lectures to in-class activities, plus improving social presence via online learning communities or instant messaging chat groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to devote course time to explaining flipped classroom design and structuring online activities that complement in-person sessions
#### Constraints
- The article notes flipped classrooms do not always lead to students' satisfaction and desirable learning outcomes, so explanation alone is not assumed sufficient

### Target Learners
- higher education undergraduates, particularly those new to flipped classroom learning

### Target Learning Goals
- favourable perceptions of the integrated learning environment and informed choice of learning approach
