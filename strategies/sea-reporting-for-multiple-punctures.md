# Significant event analysis requirement for practitioners exceeding five puncture attempts, embedded in a nurse-recorded placement reporting system

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Since 2015, practitioners who performed more than five puncture attempts were required to report a significant event analysis, described as "a clinical audit methodology and tool used for reflective learning, managing medical risks, and improving patient safety". It operates within a reporting system in which a nurse records each placement's procedure details, puncture counts, and complications in a CVC Excel Chart reviewed by the committee every two months.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A committee-run reporting system integrated into nursing practice, with monthly data compilation and bimonthly committee review of accumulated data.
#### Constraints
- Record submission was only 70–80% since 2008, with some data missing before 2019; multiple punctures were defined as five or more attempts although no standardized definition exists.

### Target Learners
- practicing physicians performing central venous catheter placement

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective learning after adverse puncture events
- patient safety and risk management
