# Instructors develop personal PCK and PCK&S — including knowledge of common student reasoning difficulties — to design aligned prompts and respond to evidence in real time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies instructors' personal pedagogical content knowledge (PCK) — knowledge of, reasoning behind, and planning for teaching a particular topic — as a structural critical component, and PCK&S, its activation while teaching, as an instructional one. Both are needed "to make sense of, respond to, and use evidence of student understanding effectively in real time." The article recommends further teacher professional development, noting it is associated with growth in personal PCK, and points to programs such as the National Academies Summer Institutes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need both content expertise and pedagogical know-how to create prompts that align with learning outcomes and produce rich evidence, including common reasoning difficulties specific to core ideas
#### Constraints
- Some instructors may come to the classroom with a basic level of personal PCK needed to enact the practice, while others may not

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate life sciences students

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective design and real-time enactment of formative assessment and feedback
