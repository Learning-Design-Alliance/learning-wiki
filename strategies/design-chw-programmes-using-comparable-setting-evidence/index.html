When designing community-based health programmes, policymakers should take into account factors that increased CHW performance in comparable settings¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 10 claims (7 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that when designing community-based health programmes, "factors that increased CHW performance in comparable settings should be taken into account" to maximize programme outcomes. This means policymakers should consult evidence from similar contexts on incentive mixes, supervision frequency, continuous training, community involvement and coordination between CHWs and health professionals before fixing programme design. The review also calls for additional intervention research on training and supervision mechanisms and qualitative research to inform policymakers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Evidence on factors that increased CHW performance in settings comparable to the target programme must be available

Constraints¶

  • The review notes few studies tested which supervision or training approach worked best or how these were best implemented

Target Learners¶

  • policymakers and programme designers of CHW programmes in low- and middle-income countries

Target Learning Goals¶

  • CHW performance, motivation and retention in community-based health programmes

Claims¶

  • Higher education level, experience with the health condition, fewer household duties and income dependence were associated with better CHW performance, while gender, residence, age, marital status and social class had mixed effects [+W]
  • Embedment of CHWs in community and health systems diminished workload and increased CHW credibility [+W]
  • Clearly defined CHW roles and clear communication processes among health system levels could strengthen CHW performance [+W]
  • Community support in the form of feedback and rewards had a greater influence on CHW performance than support from the health system [+W]
  • Community selection of CHWs positively influenced CHW motivation or performance in some studies, but effects were not uniform [~W]
  • Community trust and respect from the community was an important non-financial incentive enhancing CHW motivation [+W]
  • A mix of financial and non-financial incentives, predictable for CHWs, is an effective strategy to enhance CHW performance, especially for CHWs with multiple tasks [+W]
  • Extended curative tasks increased CHW self-reported motivation and community recognition [+W]
  • Training positively influenced CHW motivation, job satisfaction and performance, though effects varied across studies [~W]
  • Intervention designs with frequent supervision and continuous training led to better CHW performance in certain settings [~W]
