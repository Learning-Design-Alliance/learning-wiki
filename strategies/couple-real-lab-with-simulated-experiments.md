# Couple real published laboratory experiments with simulated-experiments and graphical postprocessing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A teaching strategy in which students first meet recent, real research data — the Uppsala optical hydrogen-diffusion measurements — and then reproduce and validate them with a spreadsheet solution and an open-source simulation. The authors state that "better understanding results are obtained when coupling real laboratory and simulated-experiments, taking advantage of a graphical postprocessing 3D-suite and encouraging students to obtain representative plots as outputs." Students make their own hypotheses, change input parameters (initial concentrations, diffusion parameters, sample dimensions) and contrast their initial assumptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to published experimental data suitable as a template for the simulated experiment
- A graphical postprocessing 3D-suite so students can obtain representative plots as outputs
#### Constraints
- The strategy was developed and reported for a specific case (hydrogen diffusion through vanadium at the undergraduate engineering level); the article does not claim it generalizes beyond this setting

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting theoretical diffusion equations to real experimental data
- hypothesis-making and parameter exploration in a simulated experiment
