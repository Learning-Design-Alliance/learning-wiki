# Use scale results to give GPs individualised feedback reports and design targeted continuing education on identified competency gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose using the validated scale within a structured feedback loop: individualised reports highlight each GP's strengths and weaknesses, professional bodies then develop targeted workshops or online modules for the flagged gaps (e.g., screening scales, physical symptoms, differential diagnosis), and periodic re-assessment monitors whether educational interventions work. As the article states, "a structured feedback system can provide GPs with individualised reports, highlighting strengths and flagging specific areas for improvement".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Broad uptake of the scale so that aggregated, anonymised results can guide system-wide training initiatives
- A structured feedback system producing individualised reports
#### Constraints
- The scale identifies self-assessed gaps but the article reports no direct test yet of training programmes designed from its results
- Selection bias from purposive and snowball sampling limits the generalisability of the findings

### Target Learners
- General practitioners in primary healthcare, particularly in underdeveloped regions

### Target Learning Goals
- Depression recognition and management competencies in primary care
