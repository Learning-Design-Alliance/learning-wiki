Encourage teachers to incorporate the 7E learning cycle into science teaching and customize it to their own style¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the very large pooled effect size, the authors recommend that "teachers should be encouraged to incorporate this strategy into their teaching, and to gradually customize it into their own personalized teaching style". They further suggest including university-level researchers as counselors in in-service training courses to ease moving the findings to school teachers, and designing more elaborate 7E interventions to exploit the educational benefits given the inconsistency between effect sizes and durations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher adoption of the seven-phase cycle in science courses

Constraints¶

  • The authors note the inconsistency between effect sizes and durations and call for more elaborate 7E intervention designs

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science achievement

Claims¶

  • Duration of 7E interventions was not significantly correlated with effect size; short interventions did not yield smaller effects than long ones [+W]
  • The effect of the 7E learning cycle does not differ significantly by school level, publication type, or intervention duration [+W]
  • Most individual 7E effect sizes were positive, but three were negative, with effect sizes ranging from -.473 to 3.953 [~W]
  • The 7E learning cycle has a very large positive overall effect on students' achievement in science teaching (random-effects Hedges's g = 1.245) [+W]
  • Publication bias does not explain the significant positive outcomes of the 7E learning cycle in this meta-analysis [+W]
