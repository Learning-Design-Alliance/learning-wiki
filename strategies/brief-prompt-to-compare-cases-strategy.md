# Provide paired cases of a scientific phenomenon on the same page with a brief explicit prompt to compare them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's practical conclusion for rich domains is that extracting a common relational structure for deeper understanding "can be best sustained in the classroom by providing cases of examples with a brief prompt to compare them." The implementable recipe is presenting two analogous scenarios side by side on the same page and instructing students to compare the scenarios, think of what they have in common, and explain what is happening.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two analogous cases of the target phenomenon; students with at least minimal familiarity with the phenomenon
#### Constraints
- Superiority emerged only for some dependent tasks; for lower-grade or lower-knowledge students the prompt may be less beneficial (expertise reversal)

### Target Learners
- Primary and lower secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Deeper conceptual understanding of scientific phenomena via abstraction of relational structure

### Affordances
- Mutual Alignment Analogical Encoding
