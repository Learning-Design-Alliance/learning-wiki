Leverage sex-specific sedentary-behavior initiatives, such as embedding math challenges in leisure reading for girls and substituting TV time with math apps for boys¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that initiatives targeting sedentary behavior account for sex differences in their associations with mathematics ability. It states that "initiatives targeting SB should consider sex-related differences to increase their effectiveness" and offers two concrete examples: schools may "develop and provide literature in which math challenges are embedded" to leverage reading benefits for girls, and caregivers may "substitute 30 minutes of TV with math apps requiring active problem-solving" for males. These recommendations follow from the observed sex-specific associations.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Knowledge of which sedentary behavior types show sex-specific associations with mathematics outcomes, as identified in the study

Constraints¶

  • The authors caution that "the small effect sizes observed, particularly for mediation pathways, underscore the need for caution when translating these findings into intervention recommendations"

Target Learners¶

  • children and adolescents (cohort members aged 11–17)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematics ability / numerical reasoning

Claims¶

  • Leisure reading at age 11 is positively associated with mathematics ability at age 17 among female adolescents [+M]
  • TV viewing at age 11 is negatively associated with mathematics ability at age 17 among male adolescents [+M]
  • Sex moderated the associations between specific sedentary behavior types and mathematics ability, prompting sex-stratified analyses [+M]
  • Internet use at age 11 showed a positive adjusted association with mathematics ability at age 17 in females, though unadjusted correlations were null [+M]
  • Doing homework at age 11 was reported as predictive of poorer mathematics ability at age 17 among female participants [+M]
  • Internalizing problems at age 14 mediate the association between leisure reading at age 11 and mathematics ability at age 17 among males [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Target interventions at high-achieving girls in mathematics and low-achieving boys in reading, treating the two sex differences as separate problems
