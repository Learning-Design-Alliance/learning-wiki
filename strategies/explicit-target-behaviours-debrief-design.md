# Make target behaviours and change mechanisms explicit in simulation debriefing design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation trainers be explicit about what each training episode targets and how change is expected to occur: "trainers should be clear about a) target behaviours/learning objectives, and b) specific mechanisms of change". Debriefing after live video-recorded scenarios offers many possibilities for tailored behaviour change techniques such as habit formation through verbalising thoughts and taking time-outs, and restructuring social hierarchies to encourage speaking out about safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty facilitation of debriefs and clarity about whether episodes target technical skills, psychological skills, motivation or opportunity
#### Constraints
- The authors note some faculty debrief styles were more didactic, and some senior clinical faculty were not trained specifically in simulation debrief of non-technical skills

### Target Learners
- Multiprofessional healthcare staff in simulation-based training

### Target Learning Goals
- Non-technical skills such as verbalising thoughts, calling for help, teamwork, assertiveness and situational awareness

- Bcw Simulation Evaluation Framework
