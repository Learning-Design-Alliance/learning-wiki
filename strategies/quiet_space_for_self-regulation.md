# Quiet Space for Self-Regulation

## Description
Designating a quiet space in the learning environment where learners can take breaks to regroup and calm themselves. This area limits auditory, visual, and other sensory input, allowing learners to self-regulate when feeling overwhelmed. The space can be a corner with a comfortable seat or a small tent or canopy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A designated quiet space in the learning environment. Calming sensory tools and activities (e.g., pillows, bean bag chair, tactile bin, sensory bottles). A clear system for learners to request time in the quiet space (e.g., sign-up sheet, laminated card).
#### Constraints
- May not be accessible to all learners due to space constraints or logistical challenges. Some learners may be reluctant to use the quiet space due to stigma or social pressures. Requires clear expectations and guidelines for appropriate use of the space.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Learners who experience sensory overload, anxiety, or difficulty managing their emotions

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote self-regulation, reduce overstimulation, provide a safe space for learners to calm down

### Affordances
- Provides a safe and private space for learners to de-escalate and regain control. Reduces sensory input and promotes relaxation. Supports self-awareness and emotional regulation skills.

### Personalization
- Allow learners to personalize the quiet space with calming objects or activities that suit their individual needs. Offer choices in the type of space (e.g., tent, corner, desk with partition) and the sensory tools available (e.g., weighted blanket, fidget toys).

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' use of the quiet space and their ability to self-regulate. Collect feedback from learners on the effectiveness of the quiet space in helping them calm down and refocus. Track the frequency and duration of learners' visits to the quiet space.

## Impact
- Research indicates that providing quiet spaces in learning environments can reduce stress, improve attention, and promote self-regulation. Studies have shown that mindfulness and relaxation techniques can improve learners' emotional well-being and academic performance.
