# Set pre-reading questions spanning macroscopic background and textual detail to lead top-down and bottom-up reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Before reading, the teacher sets relevant questions that partly stimulate students' cognitive contexts, covering both macroscopic information (background, writing style) and details (meanings of linguistic units, specific events). From such two kinds of questions, readers alternate efficiently between top-down processing (general to specific, expectation-driven) and bottom-up processing (decoding words to sentences to text). The teacher may also pre-teach some low-frequency or difficult words while deliberately leaving others unexplained so students guess meaning from context and cultivate inference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions posed before reading that address both background/style and textual detail
- Teacher selection of which low-frequency or difficult words to explain versus leave unexplained
#### Constraints
- Bottom-up reading alone, as often adopted in traditional teaching, ignores the writer's ostensions and blocks readers' motives of inference

### Target Learners
- Chinese college English majors in reading courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reading efficiency
- constructing optimal relevance between cognitive context and textual information
- inferring word meanings from context

### Affordances
- Relevance Theory Ostensive Inferential
