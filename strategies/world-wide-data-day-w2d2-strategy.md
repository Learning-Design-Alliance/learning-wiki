# World Wide Data Day: a short, single-day masterclass experience in schools with authentic LHC data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
World Wide Data Day (W2D2), piloted by IMC in 2016, gives students a short, simple masterclass experience in their own schools. It takes place during a single day, midnight-to-midnight UTC, with students analyzing dimuon events in CMS and ATLAS masterclass data under their teachers' direction and connecting to short videoconferences with physicist moderators taking shifts by time zone. "The initial W2D2 pilot took place on December 2, 2016 with approximately 350 students at 20 schools across the globe." Students contribute to histograms of muon direction angles discussed with moderators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers direct the data analysis in school
- Physicist moderators available in shifts across time zones for short videoconferences
#### Constraints
- The measurement is deliberately limited to dimuon events readily discoverable with no adjustments to the event display, and simple direction-angle measurements

### Target Learners
- high school students in their own schools, including schools without access to host institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring muon direction angles from authentic LHC data
- interpreting combined histograms with physicist moderators
