Target the start of the academic year and residents' progression as focal points for improving patient outcomes of resident care¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its synthesis, the review identifies the periods and processes where patient outcomes of resident-delivered care are most amenable to improvement. It states that "the start of the academic year and residents' (individual) progression through residency training have the most potential as targets to improve patient outcomes of residents' delivered care." The review links this to prior reports of extra vigilance at academic changeover and to the need for experience to be supported with adequate faculty supervision.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Residents' level of experience (or inexperience) must be supported with adequate faculty supervision

Constraints¶

  • No firm conclusions about the degree of risk posed to patients can be made based on existing literature

Target Learners¶

  • residents entering or progressing through residency training
  • program directors scheduling supervision at academic year start

Target Learning Goals¶

  • maintaining patient safety during academic year transitions
  • supporting residents' learning curves in specific procedures

Claims¶

  • Individual progression of residents had a positive effect on patient outcomes, reported in seven studies [+W]
  • The start of the academic year is not without risk for patient outcomes, with five of 19 studies reporting negative findings around July [+W]
  • Progression through residency training had a positive effect on patient outcomes, with more senior residents showing better outcomes in six of ten studies [+W]
  • After new, modified or improved training programs, patient outcomes remained unchanged or improved in 16 of 17 studies [+W]
