# Implementation recommendations: a full-time dedicated lead, progressive deployment starting from a single course level, and free open-source tools commonly used in the discipline

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on EDUCE experience, the authors recommend that modular data science programs be led by a full-time individual: "We strongly recommend programs be led by a full-time individual as this person is responsible for overall curriculum flow and coordination". Curriculum should be deployed progressively, "starting with modules at a single course level and then adding the next levels as those students progress through their degrees", avoiding first-year challenges where 400-level courses had to cover both their own and introductory modules. Tools should be "(1) free and open source; (2) commonly used in the discipline; and (3) familiar to the teaching term", since the specific tool matters less than exposure and coding practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnerships with faculty in statistics, mathematics, and computer science should begin as early as possible
- A permanent lead is needed to keep modules updated with current software and methods
#### Constraints
- A dedicated lead may become unnecessary once a program matures, but then primary instructors must take over teaching and the curriculum risks becoming disjointed or outdated
- Early small diverse modules for 300-level students were outside the scope of EDUCE in its first years

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life sciences students
- faculty and programs implementing new data science curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- progressive data science competency development
- familiarity with a variety of tools
