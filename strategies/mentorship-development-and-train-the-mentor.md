# Establish mentorship development activities and train-the-mentor programs to expand and improve mentorship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend institutional investment in mentorship development: establishing activities "both to expand the pool of available mentors and to improve the effectiveness of ongoing mentorship activities." They note research demonstrating that faculty who participate in train-the-mentor programs become both more engaged and more successful mentors, and suggest preference for mentorship teams over single dyads, with clearer mentorship roles and promotion credit on non-investigator pathways.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty need to not only engage in mentoring but be trained to do it well
#### Constraints
- Dyad mentoring programs are met with only variable success, and likely reporting bias, per the literature the authors cite

### Target Learners
- academic medicine faculty, especially on clinical and teaching pathways

### Target Learning Goals
- mentorship quality
- academic advancement
- job satisfaction
