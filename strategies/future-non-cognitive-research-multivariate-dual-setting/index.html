Design future non-cognitive factor research as multivariate, dual-setting, and interprofessional with shared instruments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's suggestions section lays out how future research should improve on existing work: evaluate several non-cognitive variables simultaneously to build a multivariate prediction model, measure both academic and clinical performance in a single sample, collaborate across rehabilitation disciplines for larger samples, and reuse previously employed measurement tools. It states: "Future studies should build upon the existing work and consider evaluating several non-cognitive variables simultaneously to develop a multivariate prediction model for greater practical utility."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use of previously employed measurement tools to allow stronger comparisons between studies and reduce confounding factors

Constraints¶

  • Current evidence base is limited to single-factor, single-setting, single-discipline studies

Target Learners¶

  • graduate rehabilitation science students (PT, OT, SLP)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • predicting academic and clinical performance to inform admissions and student support

Claims¶

  • Emotional intelligence is not a strong predictor of clinical performance in rehabilitation science students [+W]
  • Grit shows the strongest relationship to academic performance of all non-cognitive factors examined in rehabilitation science students [+W]
  • Self-efficacy correlates with clinical performance in PT and SLP students but not OT students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Research and training programs should differentiate age periods, sporting disciplines, and compare physically active with cognitively active groups
