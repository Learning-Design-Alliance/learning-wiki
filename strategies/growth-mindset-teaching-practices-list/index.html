Growth-mindset-informed math teaching practices: norms, no fixed labels, mixed-ability grouping, process praise, and formative assessment with retakes¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report synthesizes the growth mindset literature into math teaching practices that reinforce a growth orientation: "establishing and reinforcing classroom norms" communicating that all students can learn math and that mistakes are learning opportunities; avoiding fixed ability labels; using mixed ability–level groupings with access to mathematically rich tasks; providing specific, process-based praise focused on mathematical thinking rather than solution correctness; and adopting formative assessment structures allowing multiple mastery demonstrations after support, such as assessment retakes or test corrections.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need sufficient training in mindset theory, since even familiar teachers may reduce growth mindset to generic optimism or associate it only with successful students

Constraints¶

  • Explicit mindset instruction may be difficult to sustain without concurrent shifts in classroom practice, since practices like ability grouping can contradict growth mindset messaging

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Growth mindset adoption
  • Persistence through failure
  • Math achievement
