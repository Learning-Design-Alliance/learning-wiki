# Integrate dedicated surgical care practitioners into robotic programmes to free trainees from the bedside assistant role and facilitate their transition to the console

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating dedicated surgical care practitioners (SCPs) into robotic programmes so that senior resident doctors are not routinely allocated to the bedside assistant role, which limits operating experience. It states that "Integration of dedicated surgical care practitioners (SCPs) into robotic programmes can provide consistent bedside expertise, improve operating theatre efficiency, and facilitate trainee’s transition from bedside assistant to console surgeon." Survey respondents also suggested prioritising other members of the multidisciplinary team for bedside assistant role allocation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated, trained bedside assistant during training cases, per the STS recommendation cited in the article
#### Constraints
- The article notes that not all respondents had received formal bedside assistant training themselves (69%, 40/58), demonstrating national variation in preparation for this technically demanding role

### Target Learners
- Cardiothoracic surgery trainees (ST1–ST8) in the UK and Republic of Ireland

### Target Learning Goals
- Increase hands-on console experience and support progression from bedside assistant to console surgeon
