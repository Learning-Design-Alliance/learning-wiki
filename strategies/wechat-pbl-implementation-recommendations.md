# Implementation recommendations for WeChat-based PBL: small teams, 1-week advance materials, active facilitation, and workload refinements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendations for implementing WeChat-based PBL, drawn from their trial experience. "It is advisable to keep the number of students in each team small, as smaller groups facilitate easier administration and instruction. Teachers should contact students at least 1 week in advance to provide questions or case reports." They further recommend that teachers act as a resource and facilitator responding promptly to inquiries, streamline preclass materials to reduce redundancy, schedule periodic guided learning sessions, and receive training for the innovative approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor training to embrace the innovative teaching approach
- Preclass materials streamlined to emphasize key concepts and reduce redundancy
- Rotating instructor schedule for online facilitation and clear expectations for response timelines
#### Constraints
- The perception that WeChat-based PBL occupied too much spare time underscores the need for careful consideration of time management and workload in educational program design
- Instructor workload in asynchronous chat facilitation was not quantitatively measured

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in ophthalmology

### Target Learning Goals
- active engagement with clinical cases
- self-directed learning
- balanced student and instructor workload
