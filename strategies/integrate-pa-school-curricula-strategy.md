# Integrate physical activity into daily school curricula to support students' health and academic development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools increase physical activity in daily curricula and design targeted PA programs to foster self-concept, health, and achievement. It states "schools can increase the proportion of PA in daily curricula to facilitate students’ comprehensive development in terms of PMH and AA" and that "educational administrators can design more targeted PA programs, such as personalized exercises or team-based activities." Parents and communities are also encouraged to support leisure-time PA.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination among schools, families, and communities to provide PA opportunities
- PA programs that offer mastery experiences and social recognition to build self-concept
#### Constraints
- In settings with limited sports resources, scarce extracurricular activity opportunities, or high academic pressure, the effectiveness of PA may be constrained

### Target Learners
- Junior and senior high school students in China

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement in core subjects
- Self-concept, self-confidence, and physical-mental well-being
