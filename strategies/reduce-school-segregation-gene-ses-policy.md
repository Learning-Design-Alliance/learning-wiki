# Reduce school segregation and extend compensatory funding as policies targeting genetic-risk-related inequality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Given the observed gene-school SES interaction, the authors argue that if genetic differences are treated as an inequality to be compensated, policies targeting school SES composition may be more effective than reducing school quality differences alone. They write that "it may be more worthwhile to reduce school segregation", including changing admission criteria and influencing parental school choice, and propose extending the existing disadvantaged-pupil funding scheme so that schools receive additional funding to compensate for genetic risks, using measured expressions such as low cognitive ability, dyslexia, or ADHD as proxies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires accepting the premise that genetic differences in educational performance are an unfair source of inequality that should be compensated
#### Constraints
- The article notes there are practical and ethical concerns with directly measuring genetic risks, so proxy measures of expressed risk are proposed instead
- The recommendation rests on the gene-school SES interaction observed in this single-country twin study

### Target Learners
- primary-school pupils in the Netherlands, especially those with genetic risks for lower educational performance

### Target Learning Goals
- equalizing chances to learn and perform well in school regardless of genetic and family-background differences
