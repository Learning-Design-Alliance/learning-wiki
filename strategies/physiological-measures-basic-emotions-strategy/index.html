Use physiological measures and spontaneous emotion language rooted in the basic emotions tradition in emotion-learning research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that education researchers shift from continued psychological measurement of multidimensional emotions toward "more extensive use of physiological measures and analysis of spontaneous emotion language, both rooted in the basic emotions tradition." It argues physiological measures such as EEG show promise for learning research—gamers do not report that such devices interfere with the pleasure of gaming—but only if paired with an emotion theory simple enough that results are consistent. It further calls for studies of anger, disgust and other negative emotions other than fear, given their frequency in everyday experience.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Physiological instruments must be used in conjunction with a theory of emotion simple enough that results are consistent and make sense

Constraints¶

  • The review notes objections that instruments such as EEG may be too invasive to constitute a realistic learning condition, and that lack of funding partly restricts such methods, though it argues money is not the only factor

Target Learners¶

  • learners in technology-supported learning environments studied in empirical research

Target Learning Goals¶

  • explaining and predicting relationships between emotional states and learning outcomes

Claims¶

  • Psychometric studies of the AEQ report most constructs falling below the conventional Cronbach's alpha cutoff of 0.80 [+W]
  • D'Mello's meta-analysis of 24 studies found negative correlations between learning outcomes and negative basic emotions and a positive correlation with flow [+M]
