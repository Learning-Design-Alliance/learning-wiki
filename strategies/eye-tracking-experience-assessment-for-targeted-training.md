# Use eye-tracking-based experience discrimination to identify radiologists who would benefit from additional training and to support knowledge transfer of search patterns

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying its encoding-plus-classification approach as an objective evaluation tool that can inform targeted interventions and training strategies in radiology education. The authors state the approach "can be extended for use in fields ranging from medical to educational" and that transmission of expertise from more-experienced to less-experienced individuals can be facilitated, streamlining learning and yielding more efficient and accurate search patterns. They also suggest future integration into real-time training tools for professions requiring visual expertise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Eye-tracking hardware in a reading-room-like setting; encoded fixation data and trained classifiers
#### Constraints
- The authors state the current evidence rests on small samples (2 and 8 participants) and plan to augment the research by increasing the number of radiologists to demonstrate generalizability

### Target Learners
- radiology residents and trainees
- professionals in fields requiring visual expertise such as aviation and ground transportation

### Target Learning Goals
- improving visual search patterns
- reducing perceptual errors in medical diagnosis
- tracking competency progression over time
