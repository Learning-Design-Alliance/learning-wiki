# Design lab activities so that different activities or activity phases target different types of iteration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that in courses whose goals include engaging students in the cyclic aspects of modeling, instructors distribute iteration types across the curriculum: "diﬀerent activities (or diﬀerent phases of the same multiweek activity) could target diﬀerent types of iteration." Some activities may even blend multiple types, as in a Malus's Law experiment where model revision and apparatus revision were coupled. The Modeling Framework can serve as a design tool for this purpose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course goals must include engaging students in the cyclic aspects of modeling
#### Constraints
- Recommended on the basis of a qualitative interview study limited to photodiode-based optics activities and instructors likely to enjoy reflecting on their teaching

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement in iterative modeling and experimentation practices
