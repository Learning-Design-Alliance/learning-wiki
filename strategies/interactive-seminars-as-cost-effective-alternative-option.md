# Offer interactive seminars as an option alongside small group tutorials where resources are constrained

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Interactive seminars accommodate large groups (about 80 students) while preserving active participation through audience questioning, incomplete handouts students complete during the session, clinical cases, debates, and audiovisual aids. The article recommends, since "the results showed no negative effects of this choice on test results", investigating whether seminars "can offer an acceptable, more cost effective alternative to small group tutorials" and offering them as an option.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior staff trained in interactive lecturing presenting the cases
- Techniques promoting interaction such as questioning the audience and incomplete case handouts
- Experienced teachers; the article states success "depends largely on teachers' experience in interactive teaching"
#### Constraints
- The article states the formats' success "cannot be transferred to other settings" without further study
- Exposure in the trial was limited to three two-hour sessions
- The block test measured predominantly cognition; other competencies were not assessed

### Target Learners
- year-three undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning for musculoskeletal problems
- retention of biomedical knowledge
