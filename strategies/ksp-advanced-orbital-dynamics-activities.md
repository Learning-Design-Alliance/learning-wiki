# Extend KSP experimentation to advanced orbital-dynamics questions: orbital energy, escape velocity, period-semimajor-axis relation, docking, Hohmann transfers, and the Oberth effect

## Description
For instructors devoting more time to orbital dynamics, the article lists advanced questions answerable by experimentation in KSP: which orbits have more energy, what happens beyond escape velocity, the period-semimajor-axis relationship, orbital docking, interplanetary Hohmann transfers, and the Oberth effect. The author notes KSP's planetary radius of 600 km lets students calculate semimajor axes and plot to derive the period relation, and that KSP accurately simulates the Oberth effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors who devote more time to the subject; Hohmann transfers can be calculated using online tools or by hand in an advanced course
#### Constraints
- Some activities suit advanced courses; the period-semimajor-axis derivation requires adding the printed planetary radius to the displayed altitude

### Target Learners
- college introductory astronomy students and advanced physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- orbital energy, parabolic and hyperbolic orbits, P proportional to a^(3/2), orbital rendezvous and docking, Hohmann transfers, the Oberth effect
