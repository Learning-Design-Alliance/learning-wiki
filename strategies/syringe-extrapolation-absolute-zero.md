# Estimating absolute zero by measuring gas volume in a frictionless syringe at different temperatures and extrapolating to zero volume

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For older students, this activity has learners grease a plastic syringe to make it close to frictionless, draw in a known volume of air, seal the tip, and measure the air volume in water baths at 0, 10, 22 (room temperature), 50 and 80 °C. Plotting temperature against gas volume should show a linear relationship; extrapolating to zero volume gives a temperature of approximately 270 °C on the x axis, compared with the actual value of absolute zero of -273.15 °C.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Plastic syringe, oil or grease, modelling clay/glue/syringe caps to seal the tip, waterbaths at 0, 10, 22, 50 and 80 °C
- Plotting temperature (x axis) against gas volume (y axis)
#### Constraints
- Intended for older students, ages 16–19
- The estimate is rough: approximately 270 °C versus the actual -273.15 °C

### Target Learners
- students aged 16–19

### Target Learning Goals
- absolute temperature and absolute zero
- linear temperature–volume relationship of a gas
