# Report video purpose, learner activity, context, outcomes and duration together, and design each video around a coherent learning unit rather than a minute limit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers and educators stop treating shortness as a standalone category. Its implications for practice state that "Researchers should report video purpose, learner activity, context, outcomes and duration together" and that "Educators should design each video around a coherent learning unit, not a minute limit". Evidence syntheses are advised not to pool studies solely because videos are labelled short, since the same label covers resources differing in temporal form and educational function.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specification of the video's educational function, the activity expected of learners, the context of use and the outcome examined
#### Constraints
- Measures of viewing or participation indicate engagement but do not in themselves demonstrate understanding, retention or transfer

### Target Learners
- researchers synthesising short-video evidence
- educators designing short instructional videos

### Target Learning Goals
- comparable reporting and coherent design of educational short videos

### Affordances
- Educational Short Video Working Definition
