# Tailor practice format to students' motivational profiles, particularly baseline self-efficacy and format favorability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends moving beyond general instructional claims and considering individual motivational differences when choosing between tutor-based and paper-based practice. Its findings show "the relative benefits of these formats are not uniform, but vary systematically with learners' motivational profiles", especially for students with low initial self-efficacy. The strategy is to align instructional formats with learners' motivational needs rather than assuming one format benefits everyone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of students' baseline self-efficacy and their favorability toward each practice format, measurable with the article's questionnaire approach
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single domain (linear algebra), one intelligent tutoring system, a five-day intervention, and an analytic sample of 54 of 97 students; generalization to other domains and ITS designs remains an open question

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade mathematics students, particularly those with low baseline self-efficacy

### Target Learning Goals
- Linear graph interpretation and construction skills
