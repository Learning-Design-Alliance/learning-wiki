# Keep a few carefully kept longitudinal individual records from birth rather than relying on one-time group measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author recommends systematic measurements of individual children at regular intervals, beginning at birth and continuing to maturity, as the data needed to draw conclusions about correlations between mental and physical development and about rhythms of growth and retardation. He argues that "Even a few records of this sort, if carefully kept, will be of much more importance" for working out details of mental and physical development and their interrelations than one-time measurements of large numbers of individuals of known age only.
