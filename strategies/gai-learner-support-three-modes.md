# Use GAI across educational processes as direct solutions, hints and instructional cues, and heuristic dialogues, with learner double-checking of outputs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 8 studies (2 causal, 2 review, 2 theoretical, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 8 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article categorizes GAI's learner-facing assistance into three content types: "direct solutions, hints and instructional cues, and heuristic dialogues." Direct solutions span math, coding, and essay writing (e.g., Quillbot improving paraphrasing skills); cues and hints include GPT-3.5 programming hints, GPT-3 pedagogical cues for children's curiosity, and code explanations; heuristic dialogues implement Socratic questioning, Q&A, and debates to promote critical thinking. Because solution accuracy varies with problem complexity and context, learners should double-check GAI-generated solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners double-check GAI-generated solutions because accuracy varies with problem complexity and context
#### Constraints
- GPT-based Socratic models showed declining efficiency with problem complexity, and human experts still outperformed them in Socratic debugging, sometimes generating repetitive or irrelevant questions

### Target Learners
- Novice programmers, math learners, English education majors, and children using GAI learning tools

### Target Learning Goals
- Problem-solving, writing skills, curiosity-driven question-asking, critical thinking, and self-regulated learning
