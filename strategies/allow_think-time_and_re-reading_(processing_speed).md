# Allow Think-Time and Re-reading (Processing Speed)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy builds deliberate pauses ("think-time") and structured re-reading opportunities into instruction so that learners with slower processing speed can encode, retrieve, and consolidate information without the confound of time pressure. It is carried out by extending wait-time after questions, offering extended-time or untapped assessment options, and designing materials that invite a second pass (annotated texts, recorded lectures, self-paced modules).

## Design Implications

Extending wait-time beyond the typical sub-second classroom pause changes both the quantity and quality of student responses: teachers who wait 3+ seconds get longer, more speculative, more evidence-based answers and more participation from students who otherwise never respond [Tobin's review of wait-time research](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543057001069) [+M]. Re-reading is more nuanced: it is a reliable comprehension aid for learners with low prior knowledge, but for fluent readers it is often a low-yield strategy compared with retrieval practice [Rereading shows weak benefits relative to more effortful strategies for skilled readers](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-009-9108-0) [~M]. The design goal is therefore not "more time everywhere" but *protected* time at points of high processing demand, paired with a purpose for the second pass ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), self-questioning) so re-reading is active rather than passive.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of individual processing needs, ideally from assessment data or learner self-report
- Flexible pacing structures: extended-time options, recorded or self-paced content, and permission to revisit materials
- Intentional, predictable pauses during instruction — silence must be genuinely protected, not filled by the teacher
- A purpose for re-reading (a question, annotation task, or summary) so the second pass is effortful rather than passive

#### Constraints
- Extended wait-time without teacher restraint collapses: teachers who wait 3 seconds after one response but then re-take the floor negate the effect [~M]
- Re-reading as a default study strategy produces illusory fluency — text feels familiar without being understood [Rereading shows weak benefits relative to more effortful strategies for skilled readers](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-009-9108-0) [-M]
- Extra time does not remediate underlying processing-speed differences; it removes the speed confound but the learner may still need compensatory strategies ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md), reduced text density)
- In fast-paced whole-class discussion, extending time for one learner can reduce momentum and engagement for others unless norms are established

#### Implementation Variability
- **Wait-time ladders**: 3 seconds after a question, 3 seconds after a student response, before teacher reaction
- **Untimed or extended-time assessments** for students with documented processing-speed needs
- **Recorded lectures and transcripts** ([audiobooks](../principles/audiobooks.md), captioned video) that allow pausing and re-reading at will
- **Structured re-reading protocols**: first pass for gist, second pass for annotation or self-questioning

### Target Learners
- Students with processing-speed differences, ADHD, or reading fluency challenges, for whom time pressure measures speed rather than knowledge [~M]
- Second-language learners who need an extra pass to decode before comprehending
- Less beneficial for learners with high fluency and prior knowledge, for whom extended pauses add little and may slow momentum [Rereading shows weak benefits relative to more effortful strategies for skilled readers](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-009-9108-0) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension of complex text or multi-step instructions
- Depth of reasoning: higher-quality verbal responses and more student-to-student exchange during discussion
- Self-regulation: learners monitoring their own need for a second pass rather than waiting for permission

### Instructions
1. Audit the lesson for points of high processing demand (new vocabulary, multi-step directions, complex questions) and mark where think-time will be protected.
2. After posing a question, wait a minimum of 3 seconds of genuine silence before calling on anyone; repeat the wait after the student answers, before evaluating [Tobin's review of wait-time research](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543057001069).
3. Give re-reading a purpose: pair the second pass with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) or a self-explanation prompt [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].
4. Break dense material into smaller units so re-reading targets a manageable span ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
5. Offer asynchronous alternatives — recorded explanations, transcripts, self-paced modules — so learners control their own pause button.
6. Follow up with [Practice](../elements/practice.md) and [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) to confirm that the extra time produced understanding, not just familiarity.

## Examples
- **Wait-time in science discourse**: Rowe's classic studies found that extending teacher wait-time from ~1 to 3+ seconds increased student response length, student-initiated questions, and participation by slower responders (Rowe, 1974).
- **[CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org)** — "Multiple Means of Action & Expression" explicitly calls for varied response times and self-paced options as a design standard, not an accommodation add-on.
- **Extended-time testing**: Standard accommodation for students with processing-speed diagnoses (e.g., 1.5× time on timed assessments), widely implemented in K–12 IEPs/504 plans and university disability services.
- **Recorded micro-lectures**: Instructors posting short captioned videos (e.g., via Panopto or YouTube) let students pause, rewind, and re-read captions — a scalable form of re-reading support.
