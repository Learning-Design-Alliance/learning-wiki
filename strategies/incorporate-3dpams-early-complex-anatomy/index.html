Incorporate 3DPAMs into anatomy teaching early in medical school, prioritizing complex anatomical areas¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends deploying 3DPAMs in the first years of medical school and reserving them for anatomically complex regions. It states "These data suggest that the first years of medical school are the best time to incorporate 3DPAMs into the teaching of anatomy", citing student survey data and converging meta-analytic evidence. Complex areas such as the neck or nervous system are named as reasonable choices for 3DPAMs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Models should reproduce complex anatomical areas to show benefit
  • Assessment bias should be limited, e.g. post-test assessment prepared by a third person

Constraints¶

  • A simple model presented to advanced students or residents would be useless for improving student results
  • Less complex anatomical areas may not require 3DPAMs

Target Learners¶

  • first-year medical students
  • novice anatomy learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding of complex anatomical structures
  • anatomy knowledge retention

Claims¶

  • 3DPAM pedagogical effectiveness appears best for complex anatomical areas and for students early in their medical studies [+W]
  • Meta-analyses report better post-test results for 3DPAM instruction, especially for complex anatomical structures and medical students [+M]
  • In seven reviewed studies 3DPAMs were not superior to other models on student knowledge tests [~W]
  • One hour of learning with a lower-limb 3DPAM showed no significant difference from one hour of dissection on the same area [~W]
  • 3D + PBL improves understanding of orthopedic anatomical structures [+W]
