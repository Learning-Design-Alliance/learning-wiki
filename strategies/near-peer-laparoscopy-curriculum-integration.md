# Integrate near-peer laparoscopic teaching into undergraduate surgical curricula using distributed weekly sessions with trained near-peer teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "These findings support the inclusion of near-peer teaching within undergraduate curricula." The implemented model used six weekly 30-minute sessions (three hours total) on a laparoscopic simulator, senior medical students with at least six months of peer-teaching experience as teachers at a 1:4 to 1:5 ratio, a consultant-led tutorial on station objectives, a teaching module for novice teachers, and two catch-up sessions that the authors credit with keeping attrition below 20%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Near-peer teachers with prior peer-teaching experience and basic surgical skills training, plus a teacher-to-participant ratio of about 1:4 to 1:5
#### Constraints
- Feasibility evidence only; the authors state a multi-centre randomised controlled trial powered using this study’s effect sizes is warranted to confirm efficacy

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students without prior laparoscopic experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic psychomotor skills and surgical confidence

### Affordances
- Cognitive Social Congruence Near Peer Teaching
