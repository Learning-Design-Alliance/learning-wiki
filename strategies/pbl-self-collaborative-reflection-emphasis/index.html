Include self and collaborative reflection in future PBL research and practice to evaluate students’ own learning and progress¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article observes that the reflexive part of PBL was not emphasized by the analyzed studies, likely because their quasi-experimental designs focused on quantitative effectiveness. Since "Self -assessment and collaborative reflec tion alongside teachers’ evaluation are important parts of PBL to evaluate students’ learning", the article recommends that emphasis on self and collaborative reflection be included in future research to evaluate students’ own learning and progress. It also suggests evaluation not be limited to academic performance but extend to factors that holistically affect students, including affective domains.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Reflection activities must accompany teachers’ evaluation as part of PBL implementation

Constraints¶

  • The analyzed studies’ quasi-experimental designs focused on the quantitative aspect of PBL’s effectiveness, which the authors suggest explains the omission of reflection

Target Learners¶

  • secondary students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-assessment
  • reflective thinking
  • holistic evaluation of learning

Affordances¶

  • Maastricht Seven Step Pbl Model

Related Strategies¶

  • Prepare students for collaborative learning before PBL cases and require reflection on the PBL process as assessment
  • Model Reflection
  • Assess motivational development with multi-faceted, ongoing, longitudinal measures including scaffolding observation
