# Sequence explicit quantitative problem-solving first, then scaffold students to draw conceptual inferences from the quantitative solution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends an implementable sequencing recipe for research-based curricula: "provide students scaffolding support to engage in explicit quantitative aspects of problem-solving followed by more conceptual aspects of problem-solving (that ask students to draw conceptual inferences)" based on a pedagogical analysis of the knowledge in quantitative problems, to avoid cognitive overload. Scaffolding should focus on strategies for making conceptual inferences from symbolic expressions, with prompt feedback that fades as students improve, and integrated problems should challenge students to differentiate easily misinterpreted related concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolding support with prompt feedback whose frequency decreases as students become better at making conceptual inferences
#### Constraints
- Integration must be commensurate with students' prior knowledge and skills to avoid cognitive overload

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate and graduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- drawing conceptual inferences from quantitative solutions
- deeper sense-making and knowledge organization in physics
