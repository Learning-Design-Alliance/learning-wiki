# Develop allergy/anaphylaxis educational programmes and revise undergraduate and postgraduate medical curricula to close the diagnosis and management gap

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building targeted educational programmes and changing medical curricula after documenting a common knowledge deficit: only 33% of surveyed physicians diagnosed anaphylaxis and only about 10% both diagnosed and appropriately treated the case. It states the data "highlight the need for educational programme development and changes to the undergraduate and postgraduate medical education curriculum", which should improve care quality for children with food allergy and may reduce morbidity and mortality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidelines must be promoted among Russian health professionals, since the article notes EAACI and RAACI guidelines are easily accessible online but not promoted well enough
#### Constraints
- The survey instrument was not standardised or validated, and the VrachiRF.ru distribution and 45% response rate mean results likely represent a best rather than worst-case scenario

### Target Learners
- practising physicians (paediatricians and other specialties) in Russia
- undergraduate and postgraduate medical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- recognise anaphylaxis against EAACI and RAACI diagnostic criteria
- select intramuscular adrenalin as first-line treatment
- differentiate sIgE from sIgG testing in food allergy diagnosis
