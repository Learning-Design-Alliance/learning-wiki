# Design distance learning strategies with robust, deliberate design rather than treating e-learning as an online continuation of face-to-face classes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, faced with the absence of short-term pandemic solutions, universities "propose distance learning strategies with a robust design, with the time necessary to create study plans that are well thought-out and durable." They stress that the emergency shift was "emergency" education, a temporary alternate delivery mode, and that true online design differs in rhythm, student-instructor relationship, pedagogy, instructor and student roles, synchronicity, online evaluation, and feedback source. Responsible parties should work on this for the next academic year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time to create well thought-out and durable study plans
- Attention to synchronicity, feedback sources, and the roles of instructor and student in online formats
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from an emergency context; the article notes two systematic reviews find no conclusive results on greater learning linked to e-learning for health professionals, highlighting poor study quality and contextual factors

### Target Learners
- Nursing students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining theory learning online
- Reducing student uncertainty about evaluation
