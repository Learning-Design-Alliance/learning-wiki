# Scaffold students toward meta-reasoning about when classical intuitions apply to quantum systems, building on what some students do spontaneously

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose that the spontaneous metacognitive negotiation of the classical-quantum boundary seen in some students can be extended to most students through instructional scaffolding, guided by the PER heuristic that 'what some students can do spontaneously, most students can do with proper scaffolding.' They plan future work to 'explore the instructional impacts of scaffolding to guide students towards the kind of “meta” reasoning' about classical and quantum mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolding should guide students toward thinking about when quantum systems are analogous to classical systems and when unique aspects of quantum mechanics come into play.
#### Constraints
- The instructional impacts of such scaffolding have not yet been tested; the spontaneity of the observed reasoning does not indicate all students would get there on their own or in all contexts.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students learning quantum mechanics

### Target Learning Goals
- conditional knowledge about when classical ontologies apply to quantum systems
- metacognitive monitoring of one's own physics reasoning
