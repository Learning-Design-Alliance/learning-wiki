# Tailor syndrome-specific rehabilitative interventions and school adjustment for Sotos syndrome to both the weaknesses and the strengths of the cognitive profile

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that its profile findings "may pave the way for tailoring syndrome‐specific rehabilitative interventions, which should consider weaknesses as well as strengths of the profile of SoS". In practice this means targeting the weakest areas found here — rapid verbal production, visuospatial memory, graphomotor control and mathematics — while building on relatively preserved abilities such as memory for faces and reading accuracy. The strategy is grounded in the study's own domain and subtest results rather than in an intervention trial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A comprehensive multi-domain neuropsychological assessment (as with the co-normed NEPSY-II battery) to identify the individual's profile of strengths and weaknesses before planning intervention
#### Constraints
- The article states the small sample size and high age variability ask for caution in generalising the results, and that longitudinal data are needed to define developmental trajectories

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents with Sotos syndrome aged 5–18 years, including those with intellectual disability, ASD or ADHD comorbidity

### Target Learning Goals
- rapid verbal production and speech
- visuospatial memory and graphomotor control
- mathematics and calculation skills
- school adjustment and daily living
