# Teacher professional development as the vehicle for transferring an advanced quantum curriculum into regular school practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The same educational framework was implemented in parallel in-service teacher training courses (27 hrs with 14 teachers in Genoa; 24 hrs with 14 teachers in Como) and student courses, keeping the conceptual progression and materials essentially unchanged. The article reports that "Four of the teachers who followed the course became autonomous instructors of the students' FSL course in 2026 in their own school", demonstrating that transfer of expertise from researchers to teachers is feasible. The authors argue this transfer is necessary if advanced quantum education is to move beyond isolated projects and become part of regular school practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers attend the in-service training course and acquire the skills needed to teach independently
#### Constraints
- The article notes the first courses were taught by academic personnel; sustainability depends on teachers becoming autonomous instructors

### Target Learners
- in-service secondary-school physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- independent delivery of a qubit-first quantum mechanics curriculum with authentic laboratory activities
