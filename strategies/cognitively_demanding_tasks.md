# Cognitively Demanding Tasks

## Description
Cognitively demanding tasks require a high level of cognitive engagement to complete. These tasks require students to make connections between and among mathematical ideas in new ways. Research has shown that using high cognitive demand tasks in ways that support that rigor will lead to increases in student learning. These tasks require students to read them, determine what the question is, and determine how to use the information provided in the task. High cognitive demand tasks often have multiple ways of finding an answer or multiple correct answers. When this is true, you and your students have much more to talk about. And many more student ideas come out in the conversation. All of this offers an opportunity to make sense of one another’s thinking and for each student to learn how to communicate about her or his own thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks that require cognitive engagement, questions that maintain high cognitive demand, focus on the Standards for Mathematical Practice.
#### Constraints
- May require careful planning and preparation by the instructor to ensure tasks are appropriately challenging. Requires a classroom culture that supports discussion and the exchange of ideas.

- Tasks can be adjusted to meet different levels of prior understanding. Encourages multiple approaches to problem-solving. Provides opportunities for various student ideas to emerge and be discussed.

- Assess the level of cognitive demand in tasks by observing student engagement and depth of discussion. Evaluate the types of questions that are asked and whether they raise or lower the cognitive demand. Assess student communication and use of mathematical terms and representations.
