# Use two smartphones — one as an on-cart accelerometer, one as a tripod-mounted video camera — plus free Tracker software for quantitative lab data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The activity mounts one smartphone on the cart to record acceleration with a free app such as Physics Toolbox or phyphox, while a second smartphone on a tripod records video analyzed in the free Tracker software; position data are exported to Excel and differentiated with finite-difference relations. The authors write that "the use of readily available mobile devices eliminates the need for specialized data-acquisition hardware while providing measurements of sufficient quality for quantitative analysis." Comparing the two independent measurements lets students evaluate experimental approaches and noise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free applications such as Physics Toolbox or phyphox for sensor data collection
- Tracker software for video tracking
- a computer with Excel for exporting and processing data
#### Constraints
- Accelerometer records show a higher level of noise than video-analysis results

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental data acquisition
- numerical differentiation
- comparing measurement techniques and assessing uncertainty
