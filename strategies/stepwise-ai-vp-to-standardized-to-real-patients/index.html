Sequence interview training stepwise from AI-VPs to standardized patients to real patients¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review proposes a progressive training pathway in which learners begin clinical interview practice with AI-VPs, move to standardized patients, and finish with real patient encounters. The authors state "A stepwise training pathway could be implemented, whereby students initiate their learning of clinical interviews with AI-VPs, subsequently progress to standardized patients, and ultimately transition to real patient encounters." This sequencing is intended to promote gradual complexity increase, reduce initial anxiety, allow consequence-free repetition, and consolidate interpersonal and clinical skills, aligning with experiential learning principles.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • AI-VPs must offer a standardized, repeatable, and immersive experience suitable for acquiring basic communication skills before real-patient complexity

Constraints¶

  • The review presents this as a proposed model, not a tested intervention; it is framed as in line with experiential learning principles rather than empirically validated

Target Learners¶

  • healthcare students
  • medical and nursing students
  • healthcare professionals in interview training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clinical interview skills
  • communication skills
  • empathy
  • reduced performance anxiety before clinical practice

Claims¶

  • AI-VPs delivering immediate, targeted feedback improve empathic communication in subsequent human-based interviews [+W]
  • Repeated exposure to AI-VPs is associated with improved perceived self-efficacy and more positive attitudes toward communication training [+W]
  • Evidence of transfer from AI-VP training to real clinical practice is scarce, and one cohort study found lower field assessment scores after AI-VP training [~W]
  • Live simulations with real patients showed the greatest effects among simulation types (g = 2.27, medical education only) [+W]
  • Controlled GenAI virtual-patient and communication studies reported short-term skill gains, but positive findings were not universal [+W]
