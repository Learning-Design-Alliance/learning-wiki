# Teach cosmology through an observational-evidence-first approach to explain observations and counter pupil misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring cosmology teaching around observational evidence supporting the Big Bang: the Hubble relation and cosmic microwave background at GCSE, with nucleosynthesis added at A-level. The authors state that "any successful cosmological theory (such as the Big Bang) must be able to explain the observations" and predict the approach "will help to deal with many misconceptions that pupils hold about cosmology". The strategy sequences evidence review first, then future evolution, then discussion of alternative cosmologies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A physics course with space for an astrophysics unit, taught after groundwork in classical physics such as forces
#### Constraints
- Cosmology education is typically only given to students expected to achieve the higher grades (C or above at GCSE level) and is usually not required in foundation level GCSE physics courses

### Target Learners
- GCSE physics students aged 14-16 expected to achieve grade C or above
- A-level (pre-university) physics students aged 16-19

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding of the observational foundations of the Big Bang model
- Understanding of the Hubble relation and cosmic microwave background radiation
