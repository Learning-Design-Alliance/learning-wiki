# Optional target-hitting extension uses calculated projectile ranges to confirm the accuracy of the height method

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
When time permits, students use their measured launch speeds to calculate the range of a projectile fired at an angle and place a target to be hit with their launcher. The article notes this "helps confirm the accuracy of the maximum height method" by turning the speed measurement into a predictive test.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first complete launch-speed measurements and be able to compute projectile range at an angle; a launcher and target are required.
#### Constraints
- The article states this is done only "when time permits".

### Target Learners
- Introductory college physics students
- Advanced high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying kinematics to projectile range prediction
- Validating a measurement technique
