# Four-step reform agenda: train teachers in research, prioritise problem-solving methods, support innovating teachers, and end bibliometric tyranny

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes four essential steps to transform educational research so it serves education and society: enhancing teacher training in educational research at all levels, highlighting methods' potential to solve educational problems, supporting primary and secondary teachers who want to innovate and investigate including dissemination of successful results, and ending the tyranny of bibliometric indicators. The author states it is 'advisable that those who carry out their daily work in that particular level should address these field studies', since teachers own daily experience inaccessible by other research methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers' daily experience must be systematized and organized for the objectives to be achieved
#### Constraints
- The author notes it is often unfeasible for research to serve both educational-social benefit and academic-professional goals simultaneously

### Target Learners
- Teachers and education professionals at early childhood, primary and secondary levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Educational research competence and innovation capacity of practising teachers
