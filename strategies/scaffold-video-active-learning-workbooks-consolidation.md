# Scaffold passive video watching with active-learning structures: staged release, workbooks with embedded self-marking questions, and assessment-linked consolidation tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because "The act of watching a video is a passive experience" and watching does not mean learning has occurred, the article recommends structures that generate cognitive activity. These include releasing content week by week with clear instructions, producing physical or digital workbooks linked to chunked videos with self-marking questions embedded via tools such as Panopto or Google forms, and setting consolidation tasks that prepare students for later assessment. Asynchronous tutor support via discussion boards, Padlet, e-mail and drop-in sessions maintains communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must link clearly to the course and application of core content so students want to watch
- Clear lines of communication with the tutor are needed for asynchronous support
#### Constraints
- Release of all content at once can be overwhelming; the strategies are presented as the authors' practice and literature-informed suggestions

### Target Learners
- higher education students, especially self-directed learners in large cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- active engagement with, consolidation of, and application of video-delivered core content
