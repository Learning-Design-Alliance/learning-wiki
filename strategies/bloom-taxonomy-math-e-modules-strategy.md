# Design mathematics e-modules around Bloom's taxonomy so each module supports every cognitive objective from knowledge to evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The mathematics e-modules for two bottleneck courses (MAT 1275 and MAT 1375) were designed based on Bloom's taxonomy of cognitive educational objectives. After using each e-module, a student should define vocabulary (knowledge), master computational skills through examples (comprehension), solve real-world problems (application), explain the derivation (analysis), produce new concepts from prior knowledge (synthesis), and compare mathematical methods (evaluation). The authors write that "the e-modules have been created to support each cognitive objective," and that modules include examples worked out in other disciplines so engineering-technology students can connect mathematics to their required courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-modules accessible anytime with Internet access, covering theory in an easy-to-understand form with worked examples from other disciplines
#### Constraints
- Mathematics instructors face classes of some forty students with mixed abilities from diverse disciplines, making discipline-focused instruction virtually impossible in class

### Target Learners
- engineering-technology students required to pass pre-calculus-level mathematics, many entering underprepared

### Target Learning Goals
- pre-calculus readiness and transfer of mathematics knowledge to engineering, computing, and physics
