# Use lecturer charismatic leadership and technology use to support behavioral engagement among lower self-efficacy students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's moderation findings support targeting lower self-efficacy students with charismatic lecturer behaviors and richer technology use. The authors state that "lecturer charismatic leadership has more impact on students to follow the rules of the online classes and complete their tasks on time when these students consider that the online courses are difficult and may not understand the most complex material in these classrooms." Similarly, lecturers who use technology to present content appealingly, engage students in collaborative learning, and introduce new technological resources have more influence on the behavioral engagement of lower self-efficacy students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers must exhibit charismatic leadership behaviors and actively use educational technology in online course delivery
#### Constraints
- The moderation effect is specific to behavioral engagement; no moderation was found for cognitive or emotional engagement

### Target Learners
- higher education students with lower learning self-efficacy in online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- behavioral engagement in online learning
- task completion and adherence to online class rules
