Combine clinical teaching models flexibly and in a context-sensitive way rather than relying on a single framework¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends "flexible, context-sensitive combination of clinical teaching models rather than reliance on a single framework," arguing that no single model addresses all dimensions of clinical competence. The strategy pairs complementary models: reasoning-oriented models may underdevelop procedural mastery, while procedural models may underdevelop reflective thinking and professional identity formation. Hybrid approaches combining reasoning, procedural competence, feedback, reflection, and workplace learning are presented as offering greater educational value in competency-based curricula.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clinical educators who can select and sequence complementary models suited to the competency domain and setting

Constraints¶

  • The review warns that direct transferability of models across contexts should not be assumed without adaptation, and that in low- and middle-income settings "heavy patient volumes, workforce shortages, and constrained infrastructure may limit implementation of highly structured or feedback-intensive models"

Target Learners¶

  • healthcare trainees across undergraduate and postgraduate clinical disciplines

Target Learning Goals¶

  • balanced development of clinical reasoning, procedural skill, and professional practice

Claims¶

  • The evidence base on clinical teaching models is limited by methodological weaknesses, descriptive orientation, and poor representation of low-resource settings [+W]
  • Evidence on clinical teaching models is concentrated in high-income settings: most included studies were conducted in the United States and none originated from Africa [+W]
  • Contextual resource constraints in low- and middle-income settings may limit implementation of structured, feedback-intensive clinical teaching models [+W]
