Fund residents with stipends and halve tuition to make a yearlong coteaching residency financially accessible¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Claremont compensates candidates to make a yearlong coteaching model accessible to those who could not afford to forgo income. All full-time residents receive stipends to help cover living expenses, and the university reduced tuition for teacher preparation candidates by half beginning in 2022. Table 1 lists an average living stipend of $30,000, grants up to $20,000, and scholarships up to $12,500. Faculty sought federal grants precisely to fund a high-quality paid residency pathway.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • External grant funding or LEA partner funding to sustain stipends

Constraints¶

  • The report notes growth is still needed for financial sustainability and that loss of the original Alliance partnership prompted new funding options

Target Learners¶

  • prospective teachers who cannot afford a year of unpaid preparation, including first-generation college graduates and candidates of color

Target Learning Goals¶

  • access to high-quality clinical teacher preparation

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide financial support and induction mentoring in exchange for a multi-year teaching commitment
  • Strategic staffing partnership model funds resident stipends while meeting LEA paraeducator and substitute staffing needs
