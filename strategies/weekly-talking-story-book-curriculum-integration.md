# Incorporate talking story books into EFL curricula on a weekly or day-to-day basis and have learners listen repeatedly for pronunciation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the conclusion the author recommends that curriculum designers and teachers "place a special focus on talking story books, read-aloud activities and audiobooks", that talking story books "should be incorporated in school curricula on a weekly basis or day-to-day basis", and that a young learner "should first listen to a talking story book several times to polish up their pronunciatory skills". The recommendation rests on the four-week intervention results showing significant gains across reading-based outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to talking story books or audiobooks suited to young EFL learners
- Regular curricular time slots for listening activities
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness was demonstrated in one public elementary school in Jeddah with 6th graders over four weeks; the author notes education experts may be reluctant to implement such ideas unless proved effective in practice

### Target Learners
- Saudi young EFL learners in elementary school

### Target Learning Goals
- pronunciation
- reading comprehension
- auditory memory and retelling
