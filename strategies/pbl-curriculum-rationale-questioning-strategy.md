# Educators should seriously and extensively question whether their PBL curriculum is grounded in information-processing or in constructivism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that present-day educators "seriously and extensively question the rationale behind their PBL curriculum", asking whether it is "primarily grounded in information-processing or in constructivism" and whether the educational consequences of that grounding are fully and openly understood. The paper argues that calling both camps "problem-based learning" risks leaving PBL a methodological shell devoid of theoretical support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An explicit decision on the cognitive-psychological grounding of the curriculum, with open understanding of its educational consequences
#### Constraints
- The author writes as a historian and does not purport to prescribe historical lessons, only to encourage this questioning

### Target Learners
- medical and higher-education students in PBL curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- coherent theoretical grounding of curriculum design
- alignment of PBL practice with intended learning outcomes
