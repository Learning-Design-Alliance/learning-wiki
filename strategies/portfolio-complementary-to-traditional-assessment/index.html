Use portfolio assessment as a complementary alternative alongside traditional timed writing assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article concludes that portfolio assessment "could be used as a complementary alternative along with traditional assessment to shed new light on the process of writing", supplementing one-shot tests with numerous drafts and writing assignments collected in the portfolio. It recommends investigating alternative ways of giving students grades in writing courses and notes that electronic portfolios add real-time interactive feedback requiring a new research agenda.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A well defined scoring guide or rubric developed by both the teacher and the learner

Constraints¶

  • More research with different research designs is needed to further examine the effects of portfolio assessment

Target Learners¶

  • EFL and ESL writing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Writing skill development and fairer assessment of writing processes

Affordances¶

  • Portfolio Assessment Process Framework

Claims¶

  • Portfolio-based writing assessment produced higher final examination writing scores than traditional non-portfolio assessment in an EFL composition course [+W]
  • A body of work standard setting process produced classification agreement rates ranging from 71% to 97% for an alternate portfolio assessment [+W]
  • Portfolio assessment is followed by a statistically significant posttest difference in idiom knowledge between Iranian upper-intermediate EFL learners who kept portfolios and those who received traditional instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Evaluating drafts, peer critiques and collaborative exercises alongside finished writing
