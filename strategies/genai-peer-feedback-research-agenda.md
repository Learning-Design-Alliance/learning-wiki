# Staged research agenda for GenAI peer feedback: feasibility, quasi-experimental quality and literacy studies, uptake trials, stakeholder perspectives, and equity analyses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article outlines a five-strand research agenda. Initial mixed-methods case studies should establish feasibility, usability, and classroom fit, using "usage logs (e.g., completion times, access frequency), short post-activity surveys, and semi-structured interviews". Quasi-experimental or randomized designs can then compare Trainer classes with business-as-usual peer review using established coding schemes and feedback literacy scales; Synthesizer studies can compare processing with and without synthesized reports using action plans, recorded performances, and self-report measures; interview and observation studies should examine stakeholder perceptions of redistributed authority; and multi-site designs should disaggregate outcomes by learner subgroups to detect equity gaps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Close collaboration between researchers, teachers, and students to keep AI-supported feedback grounded in pedagogical priorities
#### Constraints
- Early work should be small-scale classroom trials refining the tools and validating measures before more rigorous comparative designs are adopted

### Target Learners
- EFL/ESL learners across institutional types, proficiency levels, and cultural contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Feedback quality, feedback literacy, uptake, affect, and speaking performance outcomes
