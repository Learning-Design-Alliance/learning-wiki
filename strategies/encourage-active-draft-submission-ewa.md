# Teachers encourage active engagement: submit full essays or partial drafts to the AI assistant after asking planning or content questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that higher writing-quality students actively wrote and shared essay sections with EWA while lower performers only asked questions, the authors recommend that "teachers could encourage active engagement patterns, such as submitting full essays or partial drafts after asking EWA questions about planning or content". This lets students receive rubric-based feedback from EWA, "which they can then choose to accept, modify, or reject", keeping students in control of their writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AI assistant configured to give rubric-based feedback on submitted drafts
#### Constraints
- The recommendation derives from a single two-hour session with 32 students and log data only

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students using GenAI writing assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- improving essay organization and quality through iterative feedback on drafts
