# Simulation-based training curriculum for high-risk, low-frequency prehospital emergencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends simulation for rare catastrophic events: "For high-risk, low-frequency events, simulation of these events is a highly effective strategy to develop technical skills and communication tactics, ultimately improving outcomes." The curriculum pairs an anatomy overview and algorithm explanation with hands-on simulated response sessions and debriefings, ending with a one-page summary handout to reinforce learning. It was delivered by two otolaryngology head and neck surgeons with open discussion for tailored teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hands-on simulator allowing realistic practice of bleed identification and control
- Debriefing sessions after each simulation phase
- Expert facilitators with trauma management expertise
#### Constraints
- Cadaveric and live-animal models are limited by cost, availability, and ethical and biohazard considerations

### Target Learners
- athletic trainers
- first responders
- coaches
- referees
- the public

### Target Learning Goals
- emergency hemorrhage response
- technical skills and communication tactics
