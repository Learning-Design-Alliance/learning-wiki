# Promote healthy exam-period habits among medical students through awareness campaigns and health education programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the observed negative associations between exam-period tea, instant coffee, fast food, and water pipe consumption and grades, the authors recommend institutional health-promotion activity. They write that the findings "emphasize the need for further awareness campaigns and health education programs to promote healthy habits among medical students during exams." The strategy targets dietary and smoking behaviours during the prolonged 1–2 month exam periods characteristic of the Syrian medical curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to run awareness campaigns and health education programs during exam periods
#### Constraints
- The supporting associations are weak and cross-sectional; the authors state further longitudinal studies are needed to establish causal relationships

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students during exam periods

### Target Learning Goals
- healthy dietary and smoking habits during exam periods that may support academic performance
