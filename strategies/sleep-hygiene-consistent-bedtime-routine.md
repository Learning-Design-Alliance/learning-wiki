# Sleep hygiene practices such as consistent bedtime routines may support sleep, academic performance and mental health in children

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In discussing why remitting sleep problems might coincide with better school performance, the authors highlight sleep hygiene practices as a modifiable lever. They note that practices such as "maintaining a consistent bedtime routine and sleep schedule" have been found to improve not only sleep but also academic performance and mental health in children and adolescents. This positions sleep hygiene education as a practical school- and family-level strategy.
