Phased implementation of AI in nursing education: simulation for emergency care, adaptive platforms for at-risk learners, automated formative feedback, and multi-site pilots¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review recommends a staged adoption pathway for AI in nursing education. It states: "this study recommends: (a) integrating AI-powered simulation into emergency care training, (b) deploying adaptive platforms to support at-risk learners, and (c) using automated tools for real-time formative feedback." It further proposes that "The next step for educators is to initiate multi-site pilot programs over 6–12 months, evaluating improvements in learning outcomes, trust, and system integration," with diagnostic accuracy proposed as a measurable outcome of impact.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Evaluation of pilots over 6–12 months covering learning outcomes, trust, and system integration; diagnostic accuracy as a measurable outcome

Constraints¶

  • The review notes faculty preparedness gaps and technological inequities as risks that implementation must address

Target Learners¶

  • pre-licensure nursing students
  • at-risk learners
  • nurse educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • emergency care decision-making
  • formative feedback responsiveness
  • learning outcome improvement

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement AI simulations as complements within a stepped simulation continuum, with layered interaction modes and blended feedback
  • Co-create contextualized AI literacy policies via multi-stakeholder task forces with phased roadmaps
  • Adopt AI incrementally in waves from simple to complex tasks, on free-tier tools, with mandatory multi-step human review before any document is issued
