# Support physiotherapists' confidence in prescribing individualised exercise therapy rather than supplying fixed exercise lists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because participants' requests for ready-made exercise lists reflect insecurity rather than a genuine evidence gap, this strategy targets confidence in individualised prescription. The article states: "Future research and educational interventions should take into account physiotherapists' lack of confidence in prescribing individualised exercise therapy and aim to support the development of these skills." It advises devoting substantial time to explaining that one-size-fits-all exercises do not exist and that programs should fit patients' preferences and goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational interventions must devote a substantial amount of time to explaining or demonstrating the individualised-exercise message
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is no convincing evidence that one type of exercise is superior to another for LBP, so fixed exercise examples cannot be evidence-based

### Target Learners
- Licensed physiotherapists managing patients with low back pain

### Target Learning Goals
- Prescribing individualised exercise therapy aligned to patient goals and preferences
