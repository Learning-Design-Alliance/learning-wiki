# Use CSV-IE modules alongside MCQ-based assessments as complementary feedback for curriculum development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends pairing the CSV-IE with the GM-ITE so that the two provide complementary feedback: the GM-ITE focuses on knowledge acquisition while the CSV-IE targets applied clinical skills. The authors write that "The combined insights from both assessments can inform curriculum adjustments, ensuring a balanced emphasis on theoretical knowledge and practical competencies essential for clinical practice in alignment with Kane's validity framework." The CSV-IE can also identify skill gaps, such as rapid recognition of critical signs, not evident in traditional MCQ-based assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scoring capacity for free-text diagnosis responses; the article notes large-scale implementation faces an administrative load of scoring, suggesting automated scoring algorithms and selective sampling
#### Constraints
- The single emergency scenario primarily tests emergency diagnostic skills; including multiple scenarios is suggested for a more comprehensive measure

### Target Learners
- postgraduate resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- applied clinical skills
- clinical reasoning under time pressure
- diagnostic competence
