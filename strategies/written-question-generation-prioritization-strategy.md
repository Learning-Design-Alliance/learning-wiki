# Written-question generation and plenary prioritization as a low-cost small-group work activity suitable for large cohorts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
At the start of a small-group work session the tutor invites students to formulate an extra written question on the topic, stressed as "a deepening question on disease mechanisms and not mere factual knowledge". At the end, students individually write at least one question and the group selects the two most relevant after a short plenary discussion; selected questions are then open for discussion in the subsequent interactive lecture. The whole activity took a maximum of 10 minutes, with tutors in a facilitating role.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students prepare for small-group work via task-driven directed self-study so they can formulate deepening questions
- A tutor to invite and frame the question task and facilitate the plenary selection
- An optional subsequent interactive lecture in which selected questions can be discussed
#### Constraints
- Whether students actually raised the selected questions during the interactive lecture was not controlled for in the trial
- The demonstrated learning benefit was limited to male students at borderline significance; replication is desirable

### Target Learners
- (bio)medical bachelor students in large-cohort courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Deep conceptual understanding of disease mechanisms
- Active participation and small-group dialogue
- Metacognitive reflection on learning progress
