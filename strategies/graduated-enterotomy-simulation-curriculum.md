# Use a graduated simulation curriculum combining deliberate knot-tying practice, synthetic-model practice, and cadaveric porcine bowel repair within a 2-hour session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study implemented a staged curriculum: baseline assessment, coached practice on knot-tying boards until checklist-defined proficiency, instruction and 30 minutes of practice on a synthetic PVC enterotomy model, then cadaveric porcine bowel repair on flat and deep-cavity surfaces. Authors describe it as deliberate practice combining "peer feedback, self-reflection and a simulation platform" to build error awareness and skill within a 2-hour training session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Increased learner supervision, experienced instructors and higher costs for simulated models than other training programs
- A single consistent instructor for model repair instruction
- Blinded outcome assessment where feasible
#### Constraints
- Faster than didactic video coaching or resident-directed practice, but implementation costs more and demands more supervision
- Only the quality of the final repair product was assessed; the repair process itself was not evaluated

### Target Learners
- undergraduate (nonmedical or preclerkship medical) students

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical knot tying to defined proficiency
- hand-sewn enterotomy closure
