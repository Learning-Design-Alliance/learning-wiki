# Eight concrete recommendations for designing or expanding graduate QISE programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The roadmap closes with eight actionable recommendations for programs being designed or expanded now: "(1) adopt the startup model of team-based training as an organizing philosophy; (2) invest immediately in sensing and communication curriculum development; (3) build student agency into program governance, not just activities; (4) establish structural mechanisms for industrial engagement rather than depending on goodwill; (5) design for sustainability from year one; (6) develop graduate-level textbooks spanning all three QISE pillars: computing, sensing, and communications; (7) establish shared outcome assessment instruments across programs; and (8) develop structured mechanisms for faculty professional development in QISE." Each recommendation translates multi-year NRT operational experience into program-design actions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be in design or expansion phases where these structural choices are still open
#### Constraints
- The five-year NRT funding cycle is stated to be insufficient to produce the portable, validated curriculum artifacts that scale-up requires

### Target Learners
- graduate students in QISE and adjacent fields

### Target Learning Goals
- interdisciplinary technical and transferable professional skills across computing, sensing, and communications
