# Prepare CBL facilitators with clear learning outcomes, structured case handbooks, role-play training, and continuity of facilitator across a case

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distils facilitators' accounts of what supported effective facilitation into a preparation recipe: provide well-written case handbooks with clear learning outcomes, run pre-implementation in-person training including role-play of a typical session, and maintain the same facilitator for all sessions of a case. Facilitators suggested "having knowledge of the programme curriculum, clarity on learning outcomes and an awareness of the stage appropriateness of the content helped with setting boundaries for the case discussions". During sessions they advised setting clear expectations, being firm about structure, and using patience, prompts and pauses so all students speak.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators need training in the CBL process and managing group dynamics before sessions begin
- A handbook and case documents with clear learning outcomes must be provided for each session
- The same facilitator should be maintained for all sessions of a case
#### Constraints
- Anxiety about facilitating was only fully eased for some with real and repeated experience of facilitation, not by training alone
- The model requires a large voluntary time commitment from staff, which the article flags as a sustainability concern

### Target Learners
- first-year pre-licensure medical students in small-group CBL

### Target Learning Goals
- application of knowledge to authentic clinical cases
- collaborative and active learning
- clinical reasoning and readiness for clinical practice
