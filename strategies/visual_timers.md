# Visual Timers

## Description
Visual timers help teach the concept of time to children with special needs. It also gives them an understanding that every activity is limited to a set amount of time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visual timer (physical or digital), structured activities, clear expectations.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for all learners. Some learners may become overly reliant on the timer.

- <!-- TODO -->

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Learners with special needs, especially children with autism and ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Teach the concept of time, provide structure, support transitions, increase understanding

### Affordances
- Provides a visual representation of time, supports transitions, increases understanding of time limits, reduces anxiety, promotes self-regulation

### Personalization
- Different types of visual timers (physical, digital, apps) can be selected based on individual preferences and needs. Time intervals can be adjusted.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the learner's ability to transition between activities. Track compliance with time limits. Collect feedback on the effectiveness of the timer.

## Impact
- Visual timers can significantly improve understanding of time and support transitions for learners with special needs. They provide a structured and predictable environment that reduces anxiety and promotes self-regulation.
