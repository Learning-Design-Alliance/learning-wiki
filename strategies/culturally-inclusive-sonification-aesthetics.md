# Design sonifications with culturally inclusive aesthetics by mixing timbres, styles and temperaments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article observes that many outreach sonifications present dark soundscapes or a western classical/pop approach, and some creation tools imprint a cultural bias, for example quantizing sounds in twelve fixed chromatic notes per octave tuned in equal temperament. It encourages sound artists, engineers and composers with experience in or from different cultural backgrounds to create a more inclusive aural representation of the silent universe, mixing different timbres, styles and even temperaments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Involve sound artists, sound engineers, composers and artists with experience in or from different cultural backgrounds
#### Constraints
- Some tools rely on technical architectures that imprint a cultural bias scheme and are not particularly suited for other musical aesthetics

### Target Learners
- general public across cultural backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- inclusive aural representation of astronomical data
