# Differentiate GenAI training by students' likely work function and integrate GenAI literacy across courses with faculty development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that student training reflect the range of augmentation functions GenAI plays: future developers need deep machine learning competency, those augmenting software practices need tool-integration competency, and non-STEM users need enough knowledge of how tools work to know limitations and interpret outcomes. It also calls for training faculty on GenAI features and integration into teaching, including redesigned assignments that require GenAI use and reflection on how it works.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty who understand GenAI features and workplace integration, since faculty usually lack experience with new technologies
#### Constraints
- Access inequity: prohibitive cost means many institutions cannot afford applications and only students with means reap the rewards; environmental impact of training and use must be taken into account

### Target Learners
- engineering and computing students
- non-STEM students who will use GenAI
- faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- prompting skills
- understanding GenAI limitations and interpreting outputs
- integrating GenAI with professional practices
