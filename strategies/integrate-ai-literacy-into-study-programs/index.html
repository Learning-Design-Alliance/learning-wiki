Integrate AI literacy instruction into study programs alongside responsible tool design¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors argue that fostering responsible AI usage extends beyond tool design because students will continue using general-purpose LLMs regardless. They recommend that institutions integrate AI literacy directly into existing study programs, teaching students to 'criticallyevaluate AI outputs' and recognize the long-term value of cognitive engagement with automated answers, treating responsibility as both a system-side and learner-side challenge.

  • critical evaluation of AI outputs and valuing cognitive engagement

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutions should respond to generative AI in education with guided critical engagement rather than prohibition
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Enhance students' AI literacy while alleviating excessive academic stress to reduce over-dependence on generative AI
  • Target calibrated trust and anxiety alongside AI literacy instruction when supporting existing generative AI users
  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
  • Instructional designers use AI-ARC across analysis, design, development, and evaluation to align outcomes with responsible AI integration
  • Outline strategies and technological interventions to alleviate over-reliance and foster responsible use of AI dialogue systems
