Teachers ensure even delegation of construction responsibilities in making teams¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because possession of particular tools, such as shaping materials, "could... give the user the authority to control team activities", the article recommends that "teachers should ensure that the construction responsibilities are delegated evenly and provide everyone a chance to participate in joint fabrication work." This is an implementable facilitation move for open-ended making projects where participation is otherwise uneven. It complements other facilitation duties the article lists: sparking initial interest, introducing tools and materials, modeling, and assisting students through frustrating moments.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • the teacher facilitates teamwork and monitors who holds tools and performs fabrication tasks

Constraints¶

  • roles may legitimately vary across the project and some students may lead at times, so even delegation does not mean identical roles

Target Learners¶

  • adolescent student teams in collaborative making projects

Target Learning Goals¶

  • equal participation in fabrication
  • shared ownership of the co-invention

Claims¶

  • Off-task activity appeared more common in the larger six-to-seven-member teams than in the compact teams, though not in every larger team [~M] (participation is harder to keep even in larger teams)
  • Successful seventh-grade co-invention teams spent a much larger share of their embodied making actions on model making and experimenting than the unsuccessful team [+M]
