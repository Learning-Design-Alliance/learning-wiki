# Implement scientific and comprehensive pain management and control educational programs for emergency nurses and health workers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's authors conclude that because emergency nurses' pain management knowledge was low and attitudes only moderate, health systems should deliver "more scientific and comprehensive pain management and control educational programs to improve knowledge and attitude among health workers and nurses". The strategy targets in-service training for practising emergency-department staff rather than pre-service education alone. The authors also recommend organizations use staff attitude and opinion surveys to inform new plans and methods for pain management and control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be scientific and comprehensive, addressing the knowledge gaps the survey identified, including analgesic pharmacology and addiction misconceptions
#### Constraints
- The article's own data showed nurses who had previously participated in pain retraining courses had significantly less knowledge and more negative attitudes, so simple repeat course exposure without redesign may not improve outcomes

### Target Learners
- emergency-department nurses
- health workers in hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- pain management and control knowledge
- positive attitudes toward pain assessment and treatment
