# Racial Equity In Pbl Build Community Through Shared Ownership

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy embeds racial equity into project-based learning (PBL) by restructuring who holds ownership of the project. Instead of the teacher defining the problem, assigning roles, and evaluating against a single standard, students — particularly students of color, whose expertise is often marginalized in traditional schooling — co-define driving questions, negotiate interdependent roles, and present work to authentic audiences. Shared ownership converts the project from a teacher-directed assignment into a community endeavor in which every student's contribution is structurally necessary.

## Design Implications

Shared ownership operationalizes culturally relevant pedagogy's "academic success + cultural competence + critical consciousness" triad within PBL structures [Ladson-Billings, 1995] [+M]. It also draws on cooperative learning research: tasks must be *structurally* interdependent — not merely group-adjacent — for equitable participation to emerge, otherwise high-status students dominate and racialized status hierarchies reproduce themselves [~S]. Ownership supports motivation through autonomy and belonging, both of which show moderate positive effects on engagement and outcomes for historically underserved learners [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M] [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A driving question with genuine room for student co-definition, not a pre-scripted answer
- Structurally interdependent roles: each role's output is required for the final product, so no student is optional
- Norms and structures for equitable talk (e.g., assigned discussion positions, talking pieces) so airtime is not captured by dominant groups
- An authentic audience or purpose beyond the teacher, which raises the stakes of every contribution [Authentic audiences improve student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M]
- Teacher facilitation of status interventions: publicly naming low-status students' competencies ("smartness in multiple forms") to disrupt racialized ability hierarchies

#### Constraints
- Unstructured "choice" without role interdependence amplifies existing status hierarchies; students of color are often relegated to record-keeping or presentation aesthetics while white and high-status peers claim the analytical work [~S]
- Co-defining driving questions takes instructional time; teachers under accountability pressure may revert to teacher-defined projects, gutting the ownership mechanism [-M]
- Shared ownership without scaffolding for project-management skills overloads novices and can produce failure experiences that confirm stereotype threat rather than build efficacy [~M]
- If the teacher treats equity as a single "diversity project" rather than a design stance across all projects, students perceive it as tokenistic, undermining trust [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Co-constructed driving questions:** class negotiates the problem from shared community experience (strongest ownership, highest facilitation demand)
- **Choice within structure:** teacher frames the question; students choose sub-questions, roles, and products ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md))
- **Complex Instruction / status treatment:** teacher assigns competence and uses role structures (e.g., group roles with required deliverables) to equalize participation
- **Community-based projects:** partner with local organizations so student expertise as community members becomes an asset [Community-Based Learning](../principles/community-based-learning.md)

### Target Learners
- Students of color and other learners whose identities are underrepresented in curriculum materials; ownership structures position them as intellectual contributors rather than recipients [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Multilingual learners, who benefit from role-based entry points that value translanguaging and community knowledge as project assets
- All students in heterogeneous classrooms — but only when interdependence is structural, not nominal [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Content mastery through application rather than transmission
- Collaboration, negotiation, and shared decision-making
- Critical consciousness: analyzing problems that affect students' own communities
- Academic identity: seeing oneself as capable of rigorous intellectual work

### Instructions
1. **Open with community knowledge.** Begin from a problem students recognize from their own lives or neighborhoods; use [Activation](../elements/activation.md) to surface and honor prior knowledge as project raw material.
2. **Co-define the driving question.** Facilitate negotiation of the project question and success criteria; record student language verbatim on the project wall.
3. **Build interdependent roles.** Assign or negotiate roles whose deliverables are each required for the final product; use [Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md) and rotating leadership so no group is locked into low-status roles.
4. **Establish equitable talk norms.** Use structures like [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) rounds and assigned talk positions to distribute airtime; monitor and publicly interrupt status generalizations ("she's smart at this") by naming multiple forms of competence.
5. **Scaffold, then release.** Provide project-management scaffolds (milestones, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) protocols) and fade them as teams demonstrate self-direction.
6. **Present to an authentic audience.** Culminate with [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — community panels, real clients, published work — so ownership extends beyond the grade.
7. **Reflect on the community, not just the content.** Close with structured reflection on how the team shared ownership and whose knowledge shaped the product.

## Examples
- **EL Education (formerly Expeditionary Learning)** — network of schools using crew structures and long-term expeditions with authentic products for community audiences; student-led conferences and crew culture institutionalize shared ownership. [https://eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org)
- **Complex Instruction (Cohen & Lotan, Stanford)** — role-structured group work with explicit status interventions designed to equalize participation across race, class, and language status in heterogeneous classrooms.
- **PBLWorks (Buck Institute) Gold Standard PBL** — includes "student voice and choice" and "public product" as design elements; equity-focused districts adapt these to co-construct driving questions with students. [https://www.pblworks.org](https://www.pblworks.org)
