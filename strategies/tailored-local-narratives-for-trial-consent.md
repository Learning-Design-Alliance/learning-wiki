# Use innovative tailored local narratives with everyday examples to help potential trial participants understand trial procedures and their implications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that "Potential trial participants can be assisted to understand key clinical trial procedures, their justification and personal implications by using innovative tailored local narratives". Concretely, this means explaining scientific procedures through a story drawn from a domain every local person knows (agriculture in Malawi), told in the local language, without assuming pre-existing knowledge of research, and explicitly linking the story back to the actual trial and its procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Narratives must use accessible local language and examples that demystify research
- Content must cover procedures, purpose, justification and personal implications
#### Constraints
- The authors call for further testing in an active clinical trial setting with participants considering real participation, to test short-term recall and long-term memory

### Target Learners
- low-literacy adults considering or participating in clinical trials

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of trial procedures, their justification and personal implications
