# Inclusive Innovation Process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The Inclusive Innovation Process is a framework for growth with equity through a student, community, and educator-led research and design process. It unfolds in five phases — Connect and Commit, Inquire and Investigate, Design and Develop, Implement and Iterate, and Sustain and Scale — and treats students and community members as co-researchers and co-designers rather than subjects of change. The process emphasizes intentional inclusion, collaboration, and reflection to address equity-related problems defined by the community itself.

## Design Implications

The framework applies participatory design principles to educational change: solutions built *with* the people affected are more valid, more contextually grounded, and more likely to be sustained than solutions built *for* them [Israel et al.'s community-based research review shows co-ownership improves both research quality and uptake.](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.publhealth.19.1.173) [+M]. Distributing authentic leadership to students also builds self-efficacy and investment in outcomes [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M], and framing the work around problems the community values raises task value and engagement [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diverse team including students, educators, administrators, and community members, with students holding genuine decision-making power, not advisory-only roles
- Structured time for each of the five phases; the Inquire and Investigate phase requires real capacity for data collection and analysis
- Facilitation skilled in surfacing and negotiating power differences among stakeholders
- Mechanisms for ongoing reflection and iteration after implementation begins

#### Constraints
- The process is time-intensive and collapses without sustained commitment from all participants; tokenistic participation (students consulted but not empowered) undermines both the equity goals and the quality of solutions [-W]
- Power asymmetries between adults and students, or district staff and community members, suppress dissenting voices unless deliberately managed; without explicit norms, "inclusion" defaults to the most confident participants [-W]
- Solutions designed by consensus can drift toward the least controversial option rather than the most equity-impactful one [~W]
- Requires institutional tolerance for findings that implicate existing practice; districts that pre-commit to a solution defeat the inquiry phase [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Scope ranges from a single classroom inquiry to district-wide redesign; the five phases hold at both scales, but Sustain and Scale requires administrative sponsorship
- Student leadership can range from co-facilitation of professional development (e.g., students leading educator learning about racialized experiences) to full co-authorship of policy recommendations
- Data practices vary from surveys and focus groups to [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) and community asset mapping

### Target Learners
- K–12 and district-level participants; students benefit most when they hold authentic authorship over the problem definition [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Educators seeking to understand student experiences they do not share (e.g., racialized or disability-related experiences); student-led inquiry functions as perspective-taking evidence that adult training alone rarely provides [Building empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) [+W]
- Community partners and administrators building durable cross-role relationships

### Target Learning Goals
- Equity literacy: identifying and analyzing structural barriers in one's own context
- Research and inquiry skills: framing questions, gathering and interpreting evidence
- Collaborative design and leadership: co-creating, pitching, and iterating solutions
- Civic agency and voice: sustained belief that participation changes outcomes [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Connect and Commit** — convene a diverse team, establish shared norms, and surface power dynamics; use structured [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) to build trust before work begins
2. **Inquire and Investigate** — define the equity problem from student and community data; ground the problem in a concrete [Challenge identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md) process and gather evidence through [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md) across roles
3. **Design and Develop** — generate and prototype solutions, using [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) of comparable efforts to test assumptions
4. **Implement and Iterate** — pilot the solution, collect feedback, and refine; feedback should target the process and design decisions, not just outcomes [Feedback most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+M]
5. **Sustain and Scale** — institutionalize what works, document the process, and hand ownership to community stewards

## Related Strategies
- Youth-led participatory action research (YPAR) — the closest research tradition; the Inclusive Innovation Process operationalizes its principles for district settings
- Community-based participatory research (CBPR) — supplies the methodological grounding for the Inquire and Investigate phase

## Examples
- A school district convened a team of students, district administrators, teachers, community organizations, and the Center for Inclusive Innovation to address an equity-related problem; students led professional development on racialized experiences that educators rated as highly impactful.
- [Boston Student Advisory Council](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org) — students co-designed district policy (e.g., the "code of conduct" revisions), an example of the Connect and Commit through Implement and Iterate arc at district scale.
- [What Kids Can Do / Student Voice initiatives](https://www.whatkidscando.org) — published case collections of student-led school change efforts usable as design exemplars in the Design and Develop phase.
