# Avoid labeling student questions or problems as easy, trivial or obvious, and address observed bias or microaggression rather than staying silent

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises instructors and advisors never to label problems students struggle with as "trivial" or "obvious", because such comments can make students feel disparaged, create stereotype threat, increase anxiety and decrease physics self-efficacy. It also encourages established members of the field to tackle head-on any bias or microaggression they observe in their classes rather than staying silent, and to build inclusive environments by learning students' names and offering mentoring and support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must attend to the impact of their words on students rather than their intent, and ensure all students have opportunities to engage, e.g., contemplating answers in small groups with different groups asked to respond.
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as most critical for students from underrepresented groups including women, who enter with lower self-efficacy and fewer role models.

### Target Learners
- students underrepresented in physics including women
- first-year introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics self-efficacy
- sense of belonging and recognition
- willingness to seek help
