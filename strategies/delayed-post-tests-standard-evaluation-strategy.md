# Adopt delayed post-tests and longitudinal designs as standard practice when evaluating LLM writing interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future work adopt delayed post-tests as standard practice and design longitudinal studies capturing learning trajectories over time, because many reflection or LLM-writing studies evaluate only same-session outcomes while scaffolds are still in place. The authors argue this methodological gap may lead the field to overestimate intervention effects, since short exposure can boost performance without producing transfer once scaffolds are removed. They also recommend designing for repeated engagement rather than one-off interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluation designs must include a delayed post-intervention session in which learners write without tool support, as in this study's session up to 7 days after the intervention
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single study in which delayed gains were not significant, so the performance-versus-learning distinction it motivates remains an open question

### Target Learners
- vocational students
- learners using AI writing support tools

### Target Learning Goals
- durable reflective writing skill
- transfer of reflection ability beyond scaffolding
