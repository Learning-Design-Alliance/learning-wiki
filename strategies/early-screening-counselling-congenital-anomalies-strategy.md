# Early identification, continued screening and supportive counselling for children with congenital anomalies, especially those of lower socioeconomic background

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational and health systems identify children with congenital anomalies early and monitor them over time, stating that "Early identification and screening of children for any developmental problems and evaluation and re-evaluation for pre-existing or emerging impairments may allow appropriate interventions." It also recommends that positive information about achievable outcomes be shared with parents, noting it "is provided during counselling parents after prenatal diagnosis of a congenital anomaly or at postnatal diagnosis." The strategy targets children with anomalies, particularly those with lower maternal socioeconomic status.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Registry or health-system infrastructure enabling early identification and continued surveillance of children with congenital anomalies
#### Constraints
- The article notes that in countries with lower investment in education and health of children with congenital anomalies, lower academic outcomes would be expected, especially among children with lower socioeconomic backgrounds

### Target Learners
- Children with major congenital anomalies at the end of compulsory school, especially those of lower maternal socioeconomic status

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving educational achievement at the end of compulsory school and reducing disparities in further-education application
