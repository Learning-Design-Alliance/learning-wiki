# Choose or blend asynchronous and synchronous e-learning based on skills targeted, student and teacher preferences, and organisational needs rather than performance alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that decisions between asynchronous and synchronous delivery, or a blend of the two, be driven by learning goals and stakeholder needs rather than by measured performance, since the trial found no performance difference. They state the choice "should be based on the skills the students need to learn, students’ and teachers’ preferences and particular organisational needs rather than students’ performance". They suggest lecturers select which content to deliver in each mode according to teaching objectives, and propose blending to exploit both formats' strengths.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both delivery modes require trained lecturers; in this trial the two tutors "were thoroughly trained in both synchronous and asynchronous didactics methods".
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single-institution trial with speech therapy students; the authors state they are "not sure about the transferability/generalisability of our results".

### Target Learners
- healthcare professional students, e.g. speech therapy students on placement

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical record writing
- other clinical or theoretical skills delivered online
