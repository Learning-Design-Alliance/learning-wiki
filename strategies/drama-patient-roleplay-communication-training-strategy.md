# Use a practical role-play unit with standardized drama patients plus an online theory module for one-time communication training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention combined an online theory module (twice 45 minutes, aligned with NKLZ learning objectives) with a practical face-to-face unit in which students conducted counseling sessions with drama patients portraying standardized characters such as an anxious patient, an angry patient, an over-informed patient, a patient with possible HIV infection, a frequent talker and a quiet patient. Students received feedback from the patients, the assistant doctor and fellow students, and each case was recorded on video with consent. The article reports this one-time course already produced measurable subjective and external gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actors trained for educational role-play, standardized starting situations and roles, and structured feedback from patients, an assistant doctor and fellow students.
#### Constraints
- The article states longer training and further research are needed for sustainable behavioral changes and objective assessments; this was the only communication-skills event for the students.

### Target Learners
- Dental students in their first and last clinical semesters

### Target Learning Goals
- Structuring patient conversations, using nonverbal signals, applying questioning techniques, handling emotionally challenging situations
