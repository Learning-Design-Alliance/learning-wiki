# Incorporate formative active learning focused on clinical reasoning so students deliberately practise a hypothetico-deductive approach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that medical educators "incorporate formative active learning that focuses on clinical reasoning to allow students opportunities to deliberately practice clinical reasoning as they work through a hypothetico‐deductive approach". Their KFQ-based TBL session is the exemplar, and they suggest expanding it to other patient populations and specialties. The recommendation rests on their qualitative finding that students articulated reasoning strategies and experienced calibration, co-learning and trust during such sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formative (not summative) framing so students can practise and receive real-time feedback
- Clinical cases with key-feature questions targeting decision-making steps
#### Constraints
- How students translate classroom learning to authentic clinical encounters requires further study, per the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clerkship

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- clinical decision-making
