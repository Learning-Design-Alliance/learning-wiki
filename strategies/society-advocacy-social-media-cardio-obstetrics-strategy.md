# Use professional societies, advocacy programs, and social media to disseminate cardio-obstetrics knowledge and training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that professional societies develop jointly endorsed guidelines and collaborations, that advocacy efforts propagate programs like the Indiana-based OB Navigators program (home-visiting navigators for high-risk pregnant women through the first postpartum year), and that clinicians "raise awareness in cardio‐obstetrics using social media and making it a core part of their communication strategy." Social media connects clinicians and researchers with policy makers, medical societies, patient-advocacy foundations, and the public, exemplified by the #PregnancyCardioHealth hashtag.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Joint support from ACOG, American Heart Association, American College of Cardiology, ABIM, and ACGME; collaborations with specialty groups outside the United States
#### Constraints
- Existing guidelines for managing CVD in pregnancy have not been jointly developed or endorsed by major cardiology societies

### Target Learners
- Practicing cardiologists and obstetricians
- Policy makers and health professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- Dissemination of guidelines and best practices
- Timely recognition of adverse pregnancy outcomes
