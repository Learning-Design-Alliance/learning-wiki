# Use the derived C-SITS MDES formulae and plausible values, computable in widely accessible software, as a flexible basis for power analysis and as a template for building databases of plausible values

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that analysts use its formulae and plausible values for designing C-SITS studies: the formulae "can be easily programmed using widely accessible software such as Excel or R, or using statistical packages such as SAS, Stata or SPSS." The authors state the formulae and plausible values will provide a flexible basis for estimating MDES for various C-SITS designs and serve as a template for accumulating information into databases of plausible values that inform future study designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Design-stage data: pre-treatment outcome data for numerator R-squared terms, or a simulated dataset matching the planned treatment/comparison and pre/post-treatment ratios for denominator terms.
#### Constraints
- The autocorrelation design effect is set to 1 under a zero-autocorrelation assumption; the article notes autocorrelation present in data would inflate the variance of the treatment effect.

### Target Learners
- Education researchers and analysts planning quasi-experimental C-SITS evaluations

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate minimum detectable effect size estimation at the design stage of intervention studies
