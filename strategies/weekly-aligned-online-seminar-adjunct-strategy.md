# Add an online discussion-seminar adjunct aligned weekly with face-to-face dermatology teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using an e-learning program as an adjunct to traditional dermatology teaching: each week a new text, video, and online discussion forum address the same content as the concurrent face-to-face activities, with tutor feedback available via discussion boards or direct messages. The authors state that "the use of a e-learning program associated with a traditional course for medical students provides a way to improve the teaching of dermatology."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identical evidence-based content and textbook across formats; multiple-choice exams aligned to course objectives; tutor time to provide prompt feedback through the online channels
#### Constraints
- Online facilitation was "more challenging than face-to-face teaching for the design, organization, delivery, and engagement of participants in discussion"; prompt feedback is time consuming for instructors; the study measured knowledge gains only, not practical skills or long-term retention, and used a limited number of students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in visual specialties with limited curriculum time

### Target Learning Goals
- dermatology knowledge (examination, terminology, physiology, anatomy)
