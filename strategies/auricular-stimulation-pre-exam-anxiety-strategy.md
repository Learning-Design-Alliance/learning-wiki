# Apply auricular stimulation with indwelling fixed needles at vagally innervated ear points the day before a high-stakes exam to reduce exam anxiety and improve sleep quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A licensed acupuncturist applies indwelling fixed 'New Pyonex' needles bilaterally at auricular points "situated in the areas innervated by cranial nerves exclusively or receiving mixed innervation by the auricular branch of the vagal nerve", within about 10 minutes on the day before the exam. Participants were told to stimulate the needles for 3–5 minutes if they felt anxious, and needles were removed immediately after the exam. In the crossover trial this outperformed expressive writing and no intervention on state anxiety and sleep quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A licensed acupuncturist with experience in the technique applies the needles at the specified auricular points the day before the exam
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a small crossover trial; the authors state the small sample and prevalence of female participants preclude generalizability over the populations
- The authors state future studies should compare AS with commonly used treatments for exam anxiety such as relaxation techniques and biofeedback

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students facing high-stakes oral exams

### Target Learning Goals
- reduction of pre-exam (situational) anxiety
- improved quality of sleep the night before the exam
