Lecture Method Supplemented with Music (LMM)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

LMM is a teaching strategy in which the traditional lecture method is supplemented with music-centered lessons where songs are used to teach chemistry content. The article describes that "The teacher either composes or downloads the music from the internet; and either gives the students the pre-recoreded music to listen before or during the lesson", requiring no musical skill from the teacher. Students regularly sing the lyrics, promoting active involvement and engagement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The teacher does more than having students listen to music; songs are used to teach content and students interact with the music in a number of ways

Constraints¶

  • The method does not require any musical skill, as it is not dependant on music training

Target Learners¶

  • Senior secondary school chemistry students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved retention and understanding of difficult chemistry concepts such as electrochemistry

Claims¶

  • Students taught electrochemistry with lecture method supplemented with music (LMM) had significantly higher retention scores than students taught with lecture method supplemented with computer animation (LMC). [+M]
  • There was no significant interaction effect of instructional strategy and gender on students' mean retention scores in electrochemistry. [~M]
  • Female students had significantly higher electrochemistry retention scores than male students. [~M]
  • Within-gender comparisons showed males taught with LMM scored slightly higher than males taught with LMC, while females taught with LMM scored higher than females taught with LMC. [+M]
