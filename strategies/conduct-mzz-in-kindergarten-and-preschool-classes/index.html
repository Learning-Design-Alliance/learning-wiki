Conduct the MZZ program in kindergarten and pre-school classes to prevent calculating disorders¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's positive effects, the authors recommend that the MZZ "can apparently be successfully conducted in kindergarten and pre-school classes and it should be conducted there, too, because it is apt for preventing calculating disorders". The strategy targets children with strikingly poor early numerical skills at the transition to school, using small-group playful training sessions with structured representational materials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training should follow the MZZ manual's 24 sessions of 30 minutes in small groups of up to six children

Constraints¶

  • The article states training during the last four months of the pre-school year might bear a stronger impact than training nearly a full year before school start, as was the case in this study, and long-term effects remain undemonstrated

Target Learners¶

  • children at risk for mathematical achievement problems at school entry

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early number competencies
  • prevention of mathematical learning disorders

Claims¶

  • Screening 63 pre-school children with the EMBI interview identified about 30% as at risk using a cut-off of fewer than 9 of 15 relevant tasks [+W]
  • A significant group x measurement point interaction confirms the training effect in the main evaluation (F(1,6) = 21.55, p < .01) [+W]
  • The ANOVA showed no statistically significant main effect of group on EMBI performance (F(1,6) = 5.55, p > .05) [+W]
  • The full MZZ training program significantly improves early mathematical precursor skills in school-entry children at risk, with a large advantage over waiting controls (d = 4.6) [+W]
  • A shortened 18-session MZZ program replicated the training effect in the former waiting control group (d = 2.5) [+W]
