# Instructors can revoice student questions with hedging and take tentative ideas seriously to encourage sensemaking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implication for instructors is to overhear a good question, revoice it with added hedging ("what do you think," "any idea"), kneel to eye level, then echo students' tentative ideas and push them toward mechanism and prediction. The TA "takes his idea seriously by echoing it back to him and asking him to be more specific" and infers a prediction ("so like if we did it again…"). This recipe is drawn from what TA Joey actually did in Episode 2.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An active-engagement setting where students work on shared tasks and instructors can overhear group talk
- Instructors must follow up by taking hedged ideas seriously rather than dismissing them
#### Constraints
- The article warns that students who distance themselves too much "can come across as so dismissive of the activity that they discourage further contributions"

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students working in tutorial groups

### Target Learning Goals
- offering and evaluating ideas about physical mechanisms
- building norms for collaborative sensemaking

### Affordances
- Epistemic Distancing Discourse Moves
