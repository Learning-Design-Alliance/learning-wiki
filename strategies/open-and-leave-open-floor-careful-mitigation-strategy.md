# Three recommendations for effective feedback practice: open up the floor, leave the floor open, and be careful in mitigating feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the fine-grained conversation analysis of real-life feedback conversations, the authors advise feedback providers to first "open up the floor to residents" by asking questions to elicit information about performance and posing explicit evaluations only after enquiring about the resident's own perception. Second, providers should leave the floor open by continuing to ask questions that facilitate two-way dialogue and "explore issues brought up by the resident before dismissing them as irrelevant or not important". Third, providers should be careful in mitigating feedback, since hedging may be polite but can also distort the message.
