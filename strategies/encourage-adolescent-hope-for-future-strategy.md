# Encourage adolescents to develop hope for the future to support academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools and adults encourage adolescents to have hope for the future, operationalized as having decided what they want to become. It reports that "Overall, 2283 adolescents (65.6%) had some form of hope for the future" and that hope was associated with better self-rated academic performance, more studying and reading, and higher resilience. The authors note hope "can be inspired by adults in a comparatively easy way".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adolescents respond to questions or conversations about what they want to become in the future, as in the study's single-item measure
#### Constraints
- The evidence is cross-sectional; the authors state reverse causation is likely and call for randomized and longitudinal studies before causal claims

### Target Learners
- adolescents in junior high school (13–14 years old)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance
- time management for studying and reading
- resilience
