# Use cadaveric workshops to compensate for missed prior ultrasound course attendance when allocating educational resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends reallocating ultrasound training resources based on its finding that a single cadaveric workshop brought physicians without prior course attendance up to statistically indistinguishable post-course confidence levels compared with prior attendees. The authors state that "a single cadaver course was enough to compensate for the absence of previous course attendance and may therefore necessitate a revision of educational resource allocation", calling for a reevaluation of how educational resources are distributed to optimize procedural competence and patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to cadaveric specimens and high-resolution ultrasound devices
- Experienced instructors supervising hands-on practice in small stations
#### Constraints
- Evidence is limited to physiatrists and three lower extremity joints
- Confidence was self-reported; objective procedural accuracy was not assessed

### Target Learners
- Physicians without prior ultrasound course attendance
- Residents and early-career specialists

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural confidence in ultrasound-guided joint injections
