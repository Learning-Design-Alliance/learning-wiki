Build school staff capacity to support student leaders and sustain cyber leader networks across schools¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's forward-looking recommendations are to enhance staff capacity to enable student change agents and to sustain leaders' efforts by building cross-school networks. The authors write that "Further research is needed to determine how to enha nce the capacity of school staff to enable young people to adopt change agent roles", and suggest building "netw orks of cyber leaders across schools to communicate and share ideas via online social networks". Leaders themselves wanted to mentor new leaders and network with other schools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School staff willing and able to engage with and support student leaders; an online platform or mechanism for cross-school leader contact.

Constraints¶

  • The article frames both as directions for further research rather than tested strategies.

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school student leaders (grade 10 cyber leaders)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained student-led cyberbullying prevention and peer support

Claims¶

  • Most cyber leaders had not been cyberbullied in the past term, and female leaders reported significantly higher peer support than males [+M]
  • Most cyber leaders rated their contribution as Level 7 on Hart's Ladder of Participation, initiating and directing their own ideas with adults in advisory roles [+W]
  • After training, most student cyber leaders reported high self-efficacy to discuss and prevent cyberbullying and to lead whole-school activities [+M]
  • Cyber leaders reported the training was useful, felt committed to the role, supported by their school, and confident asking staff for help [+M]
  • Staff and parent support, recognition by the school, confidence, cohesive leader teams and time together enabled cyber leadership, while lack of staff support inhibited it [+W]
  • Cyber leaders believed peers found them more credible than adults, and other students proactively sought them out to discuss cyber issues [+W]
