Integrate physiological, dialog, video, and self-report data to analyze collaboration at individual and group levels¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends methodological approaches that integrate different data types, since 'Isolated verbal dialogues do not portray the complexity of the process, nor do they give us access to bodily performance in the process.' It proposes pairing analysis of physiological data relevant to interaction features with group dialogues, video observations, and guided self-reports to provide information for individual and group level analysis. This responds to the field's call for multimodal research designs addressing multiple layers of analysis.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interdisciplinarity and implementation of different methods of data integration, including physiological indices of social signal processing.

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions that bodily engagement or expression of bodily actions as a singular element of analysis does not guarantee the transparency of the meaning being elaborated.

Target Learners¶

  • students in face-to-face collaborative learning situations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding regulation, engagement, and intersubjectivity during group learning

Claims¶

  • Gestures and bodily actions lighten cognitive load and extend mathematical ideas across group members during collaboration [+W]
  • Physiological synchrony during collaborative interaction shows initial positive correlations with collaboration quality, task performance, and learning gains [+W]
  • Verbal-utterance-based methods leave collaboration in groups with asymmetrical interactions, such as young children, unexamined [+W]
