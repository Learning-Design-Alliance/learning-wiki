Use MetaLight's forest plot exercise feature for students to construct rather than interpret plots¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

MetaLight contains a forest plot exercise feature that enables students to 'draw' a forest plot based on descriptive text, rather than being presented with one to interpret. Point estimates can be dragged and dropped around the plot, the size of the central square can be resized, and confidence intervals can be increased and decreased. The 'construction' of a forest plot works well as a group exercise, with students directing where effect sizes should be located, how long their confidence intervals should be, and how large the red boxes should be.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to MetaLight in a PC lab setting or via projector/shared desktop display
  • The lecturer positions each graphical component and checks that students are in agreement as to their final locations

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • students learning meta-analysis in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding how individual studies are represented on a forest plot
  • understanding confidence intervals and study weighting

Claims¶

  • MetaLight's interface is designed to develop understanding of forest plots, statistical models, and funnel plot construction [+W]

  • Use MetaLight in lectures as a visual aid and in exercises and assignments
