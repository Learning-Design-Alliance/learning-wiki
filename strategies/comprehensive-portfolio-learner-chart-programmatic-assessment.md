# Implement comprehensive portfolios as learner charts within programmatic assessment, documenting progress longitudinally to service both learning and assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the comprehensive portfolio just as the patient chart: a learner chart that comprehensively documents progress in a learning trajectory lubricated by meaningful dialogue between learner and mentor in a trusting relationship. In programmatic assessment, decision-making is removed from individual assessments and based on longitudinally gathered information, making the portfolio the key instrument that fully services learning and assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration into the curriculum as part of an assessment program
- Mentor dialogue in a trusting relationship
- Longitudinally gathered assessment and learning information
#### Constraints
- Recent calls for programmatic assessment cannot succeed without such a learning chart, per the article

### Target Learners
- medical students in longitudinal integrated clerkships
- residents in competency-based postgraduate programs

### Target Learning Goals
- documenting progress in a learning trajectory
- supporting learning and assessment simultaneously
- discussing well-being, performance and future plans
