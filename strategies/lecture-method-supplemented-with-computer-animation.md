# Lecture Method Supplemented with Computer Animation (LMC)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
LMC supplements the lecture method with computer animation, a form of simulation instructional method using computer animation, graphics and cartoons in classroom instruction. The article states "Computer Assisted Instruction with Animation is a f orm of simulation instructional method that implies the use of computer animation, graphic and cartoons in classroom instruction", presenting concepts in a well-organized manner for greater clarity and easier understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools need a computer laboratory with functional computers; the purposive sampling criteria included a computer laboratory with at least 30 functional computers
#### Constraints
- The study confined LMC to teaching electrolysis and electrochemical cells at SSII level

### Target Learners
- Senior secondary school chemistry students

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved retention and understanding of difficult chemistry concepts such as electrochemistry

## Claims

- [Students taught electrochemistry with lecture method supplemented with music (LMM) had significantly higher retention scores than students taught with lecture method supplemented with computer animation (LMC).](../claims/lmm-higher-retention-than-lmc-electrochemistry.md) [-W]
- [Within-gender comparisons showed males taught with LMM scored slightly higher than males taught with LMC, while females taught with LMM scored higher than females taught with LMC.](../claims/within-gender-lmm-lmc-retention-comparisons.md) [-W]
- [There was no significant interaction effect of instructional strategy and gender on students' mean retention scores in electrochemistry.](../claims/no-significant-strategy-gender-interaction-retention.md) [~W]
