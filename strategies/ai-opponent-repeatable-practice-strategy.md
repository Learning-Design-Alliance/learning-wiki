# Use an AI opponent to provide repeatable, engaging practice of curriculum concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends an AI opponent as a way to give students repeatable practice: "The inclusion of an AI opponent allows for repeatable, engaging practice." Students take turns with the AI drawing and comparing cards containing Standard Model particle information, and are "advantaged by knowledge of the particles contained in the cards and learn while playing". This suits topics where curricula lack interactive resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AI deliberately tuned to educational goals rather than winning, per the article's design account
#### Constraints
- Students typically play only a few times, so a highly optimised AI could be counterproductive to the learning experience

### Target Learners
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- familiarisation with Standard Model particle properties
