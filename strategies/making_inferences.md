# Making Inferences

## Description
This strategy requires readers to evaluate or draw conclusions from information in a text, even when the author does not explicitly state it. Readers combine information from the text with their own background knowledge to 'read between the lines' and infer meanings. This is crucial because authors often provide clues rather than complete descriptions. Teaching readers to make inferences improves their ability to construct meaning from texts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need to be able to identify clues and implicit information in the text and connect it to their background knowledge. Instructors should model inference-making and provide opportunities for practice and feedback.
#### Constraints
- If learners lack sufficient background knowledge, they may struggle to make accurate inferences. Inaccurate or biased background knowledge can lead to incorrect inferences.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance reading comprehension by drawing conclusions from implicit information, combining text details with background knowledge, and constructing deeper meaning.

### Affordances
- Improves critical thinking by requiring readers to analyze and synthesize information, connects new information with existing knowledge, and promotes deeper comprehension of texts where information is not explicitly stated.

### Personalization
- Learners can focus on making inferences about topics that align with their interests and knowledge levels. Instructors can provide prompts or graphic organizers to support inference-making.
