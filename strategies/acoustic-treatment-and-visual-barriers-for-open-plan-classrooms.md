# Apply acoustic treatment, lowered ceilings and visual barriers when utilising open-plan classroom spaces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that where open-plan spaces continue to be used, "appropriate and adequate measures to facilitate speech access should be applied." These include acoustic treatment to maximise sound absorption of ceilings and walls, lowered ceilings to optimise listening conditions, and visual barriers or operable walls to minimise visual distractions from adjacent class groups. The strategy stems directly from the study's finding that open-plan settings carried higher background noise and slower reading fluency development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Open-plan classroom spaces hosting multiple class groups in one physical space
- Acoustic treatment materials and partition or wall options available to the school
#### Constraints
- The study enrolled only audiometrically normal children aged 7–10 without known cognitive or learning difficulties; vulnerable groups are likely to show even greater delays
- The study only considered reading fluency; other aspects of learning may be unaffected or even augmented by open-plan configurations

### Target Learners
- Primary school students aged 7–10 years in open-plan classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading fluency development
- Speech access and listening conditions in classrooms
