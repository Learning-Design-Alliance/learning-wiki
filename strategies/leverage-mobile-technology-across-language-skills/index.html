Language educators should leverage technology capabilities for teaching writing and other language skills, without overstating technology's role¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's pedagogical recommendation is that "Language educators should take advantage of the capabilities afforded by technology for teaching not only writing but also listening, speaking and reading." It pairs this with the caution that mobile technologies' role "should not be overstated," since technology is not a fix-it-all for all learning dilemmas and contexts. Success is stated to be contingent upon a conducive learning environment and a diligent teacher willing to take risks beyond traditional instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • a conducive learning environment
  • a diligent teacher who is willing to take risks and venture beyond the boundaries of traditional instruction

Constraints¶

  • the findings are small-scale in sample and duration, and the authors call for larger-scale research across diverse populations and skills for generalizability

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners at primary, secondary and tertiary levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing, listening, speaking and reading proficiency in English as a foreign language

Claims¶

  • The female class section scored significantly higher than the male section on all four writing aspects of the post-test after WhatsApp-based instruction, though not on the total score; sex and class section coincide, so the difference cannot be attributed to gender [+W]
  • After eight weeks of WhatsApp-based EFL writing instruction, one Jordanian eleventh-grade section's mean writing scores rose and the other's fell, with no pre-to-post significance test or control group reported [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use social media affordances to scaffold problem finding and solution generation in problem-centered learning
  • Teachers implementing TEPBLL should describe technological knowledge and learning objectives, match projects to proficiency levels, assign group roles, and provide staged progressive feedback
  • Design ASR-supported speaking instruction around feedback depth and reflection-task specificity rather than technical accuracy alone
  • Teachers facilitate AWT by supporting peer correspondence, collecting papers, and guiding error correction without direct interference
