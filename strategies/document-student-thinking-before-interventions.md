# Use interview- and free-response-derived statements to document student thinking before designing instructional interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors argue that designing effective instructional interventions requires first exploring and documenting the variety of student thinking about the target concepts. They did this by administering a research-based survey, built from free-response tests and semi-structured interviews, at two institutions in two countries in both mathematics and physics classrooms, to gauge the prevalence of correct and incorrect ideas and how they changed with instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Survey statements must be assembled from actual student responses and interviews rather than instructor intuition
#### Constraints
- The authors note further interviews are needed to more deeply explore student thinking and further validate the survey statements

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of mathematical tools (divergence and curl) in physics contexts
