# Apply scaffolding and social group-investigation models in gifted classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The essay recommends scaffolding, invented by Wood, Bruner, and Ross in 1976 to describe tutorial interaction between an adult and a child, as a curriculum-design framework for gifted classrooms because it allows direct monitoring of progress and gears current learning toward potential learning. It also endorses group investigation and thinking actively in a social context models, in which "Mutually beneficial activities are emphasized, self-efficacy promoted, and students' unique abilities and learning goals are respected."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation in conjunction with a solid system of recording student success
#### Constraints
- The essay presents these as applicable models, not as tested interventions in gifted settings

### Target Learners
- gifted students in self-contained or classroom settings

### Target Learning Goals
- development of higher levels of thinking through social transaction
