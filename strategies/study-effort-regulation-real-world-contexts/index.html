Study effort regulation in real-world, personally relevant contexts over longer timeframes, including via ecological momentary assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The commentary recommends moving beyond isolated laboratory tasks toward real-world learning situations where results matter to students' personal goals and engagement spans longer periods. It states we need to "more strongly consider real-world learning situations where results matter to students’ personal goals and where they engage with tasks over longer periods of time," enabling study of temporal dynamics of effort regulation, disengagement, and motivation-dependent effort investment. Ecological momentary assessment with mobile devices is named as a suited method.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Methods capable of capturing intraindividual variability of psychological states over longer timeframes, such as repeated mobile-device ratings

Constraints¶

  • In laboratory settings, opting out can hardly be observed, and tasks often hold minimal personal significance for participants, limiting ecological validity of effort-regulation findings

Target Learners¶

  • students in real-world educational settings pursuing personal as well as learning goals

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective allocation and withdrawal of effort across multiple, potentially conflicting goals

Claims¶

  • CLT lacks explicit assumptions about how cognition and effort are monitored and regulated, unlike SRL theories [+W]
  • Effort investment is driven differently in CLT (data-driven, task difficulty) versus SRL theories (goal-driven, motivation) [+W]
  • Subjective effort ratings are affected by invalid cues and only weakly valid as cues to judged learning outcomes [+W]
  • Physiological measures have limited validity for effort, especially for distinguishing data-driven from goal-driven effort [+W]
  • Task difficulty shows a u-shaped relation to regulatory activities, with competing CLT-based and motivational explanations that cannot yet be distinguished [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Study cognition with methods that allow real-world AI use, including longitudinal designs tracking self-regulation formation
  • Study creative metacognition in extended real-life processes rather than only in laboratory experiments
  • Integrate autonomy and agency paradigms, standardise instruments, probe non-demographic moderators, and extend evaluations to longitudinal real-world settings
  • Use multimodal data and time-sensitive analyses to capture situational motivational processes
  • Research agenda: investigate affective dynamics with within-person, longitudinal, and multi-channel methods
