# Engage students early in physics-related activities such as science fairs, demonstrations, and model building to develop physics identity and interest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that strategies to promote students' interest in physics should engage learners in hands-on physics activities from an early, pre-college age, aiming to develop a strong physics identity and intrinsic interest that could support later performance in physics courses and consideration of STEM careers. They particularly target girls and underrepresented groups, given the gender gap in physics-related majors, and suggest these activities be designed to develop stronger physics identity in female students during pre-college preparation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities should explain physics concepts and be offered during middle school and upper high school
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on the correlational finding that physics interest correlated with course grades, not on a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- Pre-college students, especially young girls and underrepresented groups in physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Developing physics identity, intrinsic interest in physics topics, and eventual pursuit of physics-related majors
