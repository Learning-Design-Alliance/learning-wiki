# Adapt assistance reactively to moment-to-moment fluctuations in affect and performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 5 studies (3 review, 1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
At the microscale (minutes to hours), the article recommends assessing fluctuations in task performance or affective-motivational factors 'on-line' (parallel to task progress) and reacting by giving or withholding assistance. Examples include GazeTutor, which uses eye-tracking to detect boredom and reengage learners via dialog/animation, and expert human tutors who use off-topic conversation or positive feedback to counteract significant negative affect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- On-line assessment of affective-motivational or process variables during task processing
#### Constraints
- High amplitude in intraindividual fluctuations indicates a need for instructional adaptations; fluctuations differ between people in amplitude and frequency

### Target Learners
- learners in digital tutoring environments and classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining engagement and supporting task progress despite affective fluctuations

- Dynamic Framework Personalized Education
