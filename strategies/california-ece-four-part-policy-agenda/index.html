Four-part state policy agenda: coherent administration, universal affordability, a well-qualified workforce, and quality improvement for all programs¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends that California "adopt a comprehensive approach to turn an uncoordinated set of underfunded programs into a true system of supports for children, families, and providers." Its four areas are: build a coherent system of ECE administration with state and county coordinating bodies and a one-stop shop; make ECE affordable for all children birth to age 5, including universal preschool for 4-year-olds and sliding-fee-scale care; build a well-qualified workforce through higher expectations paired with education supports and higher wages; and improve quality in all programs by raising minimum standards and ensuring access to coaching and job-embedded supports through QRIS.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State-level investment and legislation to fund coordinating bodies, expanded subsidies, workforce supports, and quality improvement activities

Constraints¶

  • The report frames some measures as immediate short-term steps, such as fully funding Local Planning Councils, reinstating centralized eligibility lists, longer contracts, and higher reimbursement rates, toward the longer-term goals

Target Learners¶

  • Children birth to age 5 in California, especially low-income children, dual language learners, infants and toddlers, and children who are homeless or in foster care

Target Learning Goals¶

  • School readiness and long-term development supported by access to high-quality early childhood education

Related Strategies¶

  • State and federal policy recommendations: recruit and prepare more candidates, retain new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improve working conditions
  • Use local resources to serve children birth through age 3 to stabilize community providers as 4-year-olds move into TK
  • Federal policy should expand access to high-quality, integrated early care and education with quality assurance and adequate ECE educator compensation
  • Federal strategy for coherent ECE governance through a coordinating body, unified family referral services, and integrated data systems
  • Federal strategy to raise ECE quality by setting higher CCDBG standards, minimum quality requirements for federally supported preschool, and funding coaching
  • Federal strategy to develop and support a well-qualified ECE workforce through compensation, career-ladder scholarships, preparation programs, and coaching
  • Six state policy steps to support early educator preparation, paired with adequate compensation and retention policies
