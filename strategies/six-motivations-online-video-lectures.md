# Six motivations for adding online video to lectures: impractical demonstrations, controlling time, visualisations, question setters, back-up for live demonstrations, and humour and interest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article enumerates potential motivations for using online video in physics lectures, each illustrated with a YouTube example, noting the examples "have vastly different origins, ranging from professionally produced videos and simulations through to amateur film clips". Uses include speeding up, slowing down or reversing time (time-lapse clouds, bullet footage, reversed firecracker for the second law of thermodynamics), scientist-contributed visualisations, videos that set a question introducing a new concept, fallbacks for failing live demonstrations, and adding humour and interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video selection matched to the concept being taught
- Compliance with copyright and terms of use for each video or web site used
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that amateur footage should not be overlooked, while noting some videos would consume too much lecture time if shown whole without cropping

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in physics and other disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- introducing new concepts via questions
- making technical concepts memorable
- connecting course content to everyday experience
