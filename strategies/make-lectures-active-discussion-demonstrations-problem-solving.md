# Make lectures more active through discussion, demonstrations, and problem-solving to improve attendance and engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators respond to students' attendance choices by making lectures more active and engaging rather than relying on didactic delivery. It states that "Educators are encouraged to consider ways in which to make their lectures more active through discussion, demonstrations, and problem solving activities," reflecting respondents' preferences for quizzing, modeling of critical thinking, and interactive formats. The strategy targets preclinical medical students in large lecture-based courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers willing to incorporate interactive elements such as questioning, modeling of critical thinking, and discussion into large-group sessions
#### Constraints
- The article notes traditional didactic lectures may no longer appeal to current medical students, based on self-reported attendance preferences in one institutional survey

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year medical students in large preclinical lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement with lecture content
- application of lecture content to clinical contexts
- active participation in class
