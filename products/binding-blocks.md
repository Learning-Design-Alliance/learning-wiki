## Description
Binding Blocks is a physical nuclear-physics chart that represents isotope mass excess and dominant decay modes with LEGO-like tower heights and colour-coded bricks for classroom and public outreach use.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Physical nuclide chart in which tower heights encode mass excess per nucleon and brick colours encode dominant decay mode**: This is the core representational design of the Binding Blocks chart: each isotope is a LEGO tower whose height carries a physical quantity and whose colour carries its decay behaviour. The article states that "The height of each tower gives the excess energy available in the nucleus for nuclear decay, ﬁssion, or fusion processes", while "The colours in the chart refer to the diﬀerent decay modes" — black for stable nuclei, light blue and red for beta decay, yellow for alpha decay, and others. Each brick layer corresponds to 25,000 GJ/kg, allowing everyday energy comparisons. (C. Aa. Diget et al. (2016))
- **Binding Blocks liquid-drop worksheets (Worksheet_Instructor and Worksheet_Student)**: The article provides two spreadsheets in Microsoft Excel and OpenOffice as Supplementary Material. Worksheet_Instructor contains data for several isotopic chains for tutor-supported group work, while 'Worksheet_Student is designed to be a standalone online activity, that individual students can run in an online setting - be it at home or in a classroom'. Both facilitate an optimisation exercise, a validation process against experimental data, and a prediction assignment on the limits of nuclear stability. (Pastore A et al. (2021))
- **Binding Blocks neutron-star spreadsheet worksheets (Minimum Mass and Maximum Mass tabs)**: The article introduces two spreadsheet-based online activities, provided as Supplementary Material, that extend the Binding Blocks project to neutron-star physics for secondary school students. The Minimum Mass tab "provides a direct calculation of NS for different values of aA and aV", and the Maximum Mass tab displays pressure profiles and masses from user-supplied density and radius values. The activities "can be easily adapted to different levels, age groups and discussion topics" and can be run individually online or in demonstrator-led sessions. (A Rios et al. (2021))
