## Description
GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal-enrollment platform, operated by CPS to centralize high-school applications, program rankings, deferred-acceptance matching, offers, and waitlists.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **GoCPS centralized application and single-best-offer platform**: GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal enrollment platform, adopted in 2017 to replace school-by-school applications with "a common application across all high school choice programs for incoming ninth grade students with a single deadline and single best offer." Students rank up to 20 of 273 choice programs (and up to six of 11 SEHS options); a computerized deferred-acceptance process assigns each student to the highest-ranked eligible program with available seats, and unassigned students go on ordered waitlists. The paper describes the platform's two application rounds, eligibility requirements, priority groups, and its four possible responses per ranked program (Offered, Higher (Lisa Barrow et al. (2018))
- **GoCPS centralized online high school application platform**: GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online platform that moved all high school program applications into a single system with one deadline and a single best offer per applicant. Students use it to "research school options, complete applications, learn about their application and offer status, and ultimately accept or decline admissions offers." It hosted nearly 290 programs including 11 SEHS programs, allowed ranking up to 20 choice and 6 SEHS programs, and centrally managed waitlists — an innovation because most other large urban districts did not include charter schools in their universal enrollment systems. (Barrow et al. (2019))

## Description
GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal enrollment platform, adopted in 2017 to replace school-by-school applications with "a common application across all high school choice programs for incoming ninth grade students with a single deadline and single best offer." Students rank up to 20 of 273 choice programs (and up to six of 11 SEHS options); a computerized deferred-acceptance process assigns each student to the highest-ranked eligible program with available seats, and unassigned students go on ordered waitlists. The paper describes the platform's two application rounds, eligibility requirements, priority groups, and its four possible responses per ranked program (Offered, Higher Rank Offered, Waitlist, Not Eligible).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must meet each program's eligibility requirements (e.g., NWEA MAP minimums, information sessions) to be considered for admission
- Programs' seat counts and pre-designated priority groups drive offer generation
#### Constraints
- The paper notes that despite streamlining, "the various program requirem ents, priorities, and selection rules are still complicated, which may be challenging for families, schools, and the public to understand clearly"
- Students may rank at most 20 programs, though the authors note most rank fewer and do not think the constraint is problematic

### Target Learners
- Incoming ninth-grade students in Chicago Public Schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable access to high school choice programs
- Simplified, transparent application and offer processes for students and families
