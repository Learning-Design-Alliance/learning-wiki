## Description
Clicker is Crick Software's accessible visual word processor with voice output, word-and-picture grids, and composition tools for supporting learners' writing and literacy.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Clicker visual word processor with voice output used for PictoPal on-computer activities**: Clicker is the software in which PictoPal computer activities were designed. The article describes it as "a visual word processor with voice output": the lower portion consists of a grid with cells containing words and images, and the upper portion is a writing window. Clicking on cells lets children put words and images in the writing window and hear the words spoken aloud, and children can print their resulting written products for authentic classroom use. (Amina Cviko et al. (2012))
- **Turning Point electronic voting system with PowerPoint-embedded MCQs for large undergraduate Mathematics lectures**: The article describes a radio-frequency EVS comprising handsets, a receiver and operating software, with Turning Point's "enabling software is embedded in Microsoft PowerPoint". Lecturers prepare multiple-choice questions as PowerPoint slides; students respond via handsets and responses are displayed as charts, letting the lecturer address misconceptions such as a common error on double integrals. At Loughborough, handsets are collected and returned lecture-by-lecture and purchased centrally by the department. (Samuel O. King et al. (2008))
- **Clickers (Student Response System) handheld transmitters with instant feedback and anonymous responding**: Clickers are "small transmitters about the size of a television remote control" used within a Student Response System, transmitting answers by infrared or radio frequency. Students respond anonymously and the cumulative class view appears on a public screen; teachers collect results instantly, can download and store them digitally, review individual scores, and re-teach concepts where students struggle. In this study they were used for warm-ups, homework quizzes, class work, test review, and summative assessment. (Ye Wang et al. (2014))
