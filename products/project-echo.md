## Description
Project ECHO is a case-based, multidisciplinary tele-mentoring programme run by the University of New Mexico's ECHO Institute that connects specialist mentors with community healthcare providers to build local service capacity.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Project ECHO as a case-based tele-mentoring model for extending specialist capacity to community providers**: Project ECHO (Extension for Community Healthcare Outcomes) is described as "a collaborative tele-mentoring program in which specialists train health-care workers to treat medical conditions". It is explicitly distinguished from telemedicine: rather than a physician treating a patient remotely, it "connects learners with experienced specialists in order to improve services", so learners can implement new services after participation. Sessions are case-based, multi-disciplinary, and aimed at community and rural providers treating conditions such as OUD. (Puckett et al. (2021))
