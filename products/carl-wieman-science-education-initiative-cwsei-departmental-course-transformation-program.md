# Carl Wieman Science Education Initiative (CWSEI) departmental course-transformation program with science education specialists

## Description
A departmental course-transformation framework and support program that helps faculty define learning goals, align instruction and curriculum, and use assessment evidence to refine undergraduate science courses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The CU Science Education Initiative three-phase course transformation model guides lab course redesign**: The article applies the CU Science Education Initiative course transformation model, which 'involves three iterative phases: defining learning goals, developing curriculum that align with the goals, and assessment.' The model is rephrased as three questions: what should students know, which instructional approaches improve learning, and what are students learning. Sustainability is built in because faculty are consulted early and assessments refine the aligned changes. The authors used it beginning in late 2010 to overhaul the advanced lab, with the first redesigned course taught in Spring 2012. (Benjamin M. Zwickl et al. (2012))
- **Carl Wieman Science Education Initiative model of science education specialists supporting faculty RBIS implementation**: In the CWSEI model at the University of British Columbia, "science education specialists are hired to work with faculty to transform courses taught by thosefaculty". These specialists are typically PhDs with training in RBISs and discipline-based education research; they help faculty customize strategies and troubleshoot implementation difficulties. The article reports, citing Wieman et al., that of 70 faculty who implemented RBISs through CWSEI, only one discontinued use, which the authors attribute to specialist support. (Melissa Dancy et al. (2022))
