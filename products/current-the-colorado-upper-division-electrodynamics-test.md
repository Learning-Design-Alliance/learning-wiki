# CURrENT: the Colorado UppeR-division ElectrodyNamics Test

## Description
An open-response, six-question assessment developed by Charles Baily and colleagues to measure upper-division electrodynamics students’ conceptual understanding and fundamental skills using consistent rubric-based scoring.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **CURrENT: the Colorado UppeR-division ElectrodyNamics Test**: The CURrENT is an open-response assessment of fundamental skills and understanding in core topics from advanced undergraduate electrodynamics, consisting of six multi-part questions. Its "open-response format follows from an expectation that more advanced students should be able to generate their own answers, and to justify their correctness." The focus is conceptual, though some mathematical manipulations are required; Q4 asks students to transform a curl equation to its integral form via Stokes' theorem. It was administered at CU and Institution X and scored with a consistent rubric. (Charles Baily et al. (2012))
- **CURrENT: an open-ended conceptual assessment for upper-division electrodynamics**: The CURrENT is an open-ended post-test diagnostic with six multi-part questions, an optional three-question pretest, and an accompanying grading rubric. It is designed to measure a representative sampling of skills and conceptual understanding in selected core topics from the second semester of E&M, covering electrodynamics (Griffiths Ch.7-12). The instrument is motivated by faculty-consensus learning goals and is intended to help measure the effectiveness of transformed pedagogy while providing insights into student thinking and difficulties. (Ryan et al. (2014))
- **Open-ended diagnostic with aligned rubric and multi-level scoring structure**: The CURrENT's design pattern is an open-ended format requiring students to justify answers with reasoning, structured as 6 multi-part questions with 15 sub-questions breaking down into 47 scoring elements, the smallest checkpoint where students get a score. An accompanying detailed rubric makes grading objective and straightforward, and an optional modified pretest makes selected questions accessible to students who have not yet seen the material. (Ryan et al. (2014))
