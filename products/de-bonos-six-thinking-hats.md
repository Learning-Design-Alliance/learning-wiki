## Description
A thinking and facilitation framework developed by Edward de Bono that organizes individual or group reasoning into six symbolic modes for parallel, structured consideration of an issue.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Six thinking hats technique as a multi-directional thinking framework**: De Bono's six thinking hats technique separates thinking into six modes represented by colored hats: white (objective facts), red (emotions and intuition), black (logical negativity), yellow (constructive benefits), green (creative alternatives), and blue (control of the thinking process). The technique "provides individuals with specific thought patterns and allows them to observe events from different angles", aiming to separate emotions from logic and creativity from knowledge accumulation. (Ercan et al. (2008))
- **Six Thinking Hats as a multi-perspective framework for structured diagnostic reasoning**: The Six Thinking Hats (STH) method, developed by Edward De Bono, is a problem-solving and decision-making tool in which six coloured hats each represent one perspective applied in turn to a case. The article describes it as encouraging "looking at a case from multiple perspectives", with white hat covering facts and information, red hat emotions and intuitions, black hat caution and critical thinking, yellow hat optimism and benefits, green hat creativity and innovation, and blue hat process and control. In this study it structures group discussion of a histopathology case within PBL. (Das Reena et al. (2025))
