# Workshop observation tool with two code sets for faculty engagement

## Description
An observation and coding instrument developed by Alice Olmstead et al. for analyzing the form and focus of physics and astronomy faculty engagement in professional-development workshops.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Real-Time Professional Development Observation Tool (R-PDOT)**: The R-PDOT is a descriptive, non-evaluative real-time observation instrument for faculty professional development workshops. Like COPUS, RIOT and TDOP, it "allows an observer to collect non-evaluative, quantitative data about what faculty participants experience during workshops," capturing two complementary dimensions simultaneously: type-of-engagement codes and focus-of-engagement codes. Each code set is intended to fully span the ways faculty are most likely to be engaged or to have their attention focused. (Alice Olmstead et al. (2016))
- **Two-dimensional coding of workshop engagement: type of engagement crossed with focus of engagement**: The R-PDOT captures workshop sessions along two complementary dimensions at once: how faculty are engaged (lecture, small group, large group discussion, presentations, independent work) and what their attention is focused on (e.g., education research theory, simulated instructional strategies, past experiences, student experiences, planning for future teaching). Unlike type-of-engagement codes, "more than one focus-of-engagement code can occur simultaneously for faculty—these categories are not exclusive." The pattern lets leaders examine the intersection of the two dimensions rather than each in isolation. (Alice Olmstead et al. (2016))
