# Peer-Led Team Learning (PLTL) workshop model

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
Peer-Led Team Learning (PLTL) is a structured undergraduate STEM workshop programme in which trained successful students facilitate small-group problem solving alongside course instructors and education specialists.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **PLTL peer-leader training program (two-credit 13-week course, weekly workshops, journals, and leader-built problem sets)**: The intervention trained peer leaders through "A two-credit course... that related educational research literature on students and learning to classroom applications in problem-solving activities", meeting weekly for 13 weeks with a learning specialist. Leaders debriefed on prior workshops, collaboratively solved instructor-generated problem sets with modeled problem-solving methods, then facilitated one-hour workshops for one to ten students without instructor presence. They also kept weekly written journals and co-developed an original problem set with a partner. (Snyder Julia J. (2015))
- **Peer-Led Team Learning (PLTL) model as peer-guided small-group problem solving situated in the zone of proximal development**: PLTL is a pedagogical approach providing small group instruction alongside traditional lecture, in which "students work in small groups of six to eight students led by an undergraduate peer who has previously taken and been successful in the course". Peer leaders are not content experts and do not provide answers; trained in learning theory and pedagogy, they guide students to construct their own understanding. The article frames PLTL with Vygotsky's constructivism: it "situates students in their zone of proximal development by presenting challenging problems that they can't solve easily on their own but can by interacting with peer members of the team", with the peer leader closer to students' ZPD than an instructor. (Snyder Julia J. (2015))
