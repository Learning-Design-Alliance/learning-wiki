# Discourse Completion Test (DCT)

## Description
A written situational assessment instrument developed and used by language-education researchers to elicit and evaluate learners' pragmatic speech-act production.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Discourse Completion Test (DCT) for assessing apology speech act appropriateness**: The study used a Discourse Completion Test as its pre- and post-test measure. The DCT is described as "a kind of questionnaire which contains series of concisely defined situations used to elicit special speech acts". The pre-test consisted of four apology scenarios varying social status and social distance, and the post-test comprised 12 situations. Responses were scored on a five-point analytic Likert appropriateness scale following Farahian, Rezaee and Gholami (2012), where "scale of 5 indicates 'completely appropriate'". (Shima Rajabi et al. (2015))
- **Discourse Completion Test for measuring interlanguage pragmatics**: A Discourse Completion Test (DCT) is a written questionnaire consisting of designed situations used to elicit specific speech acts, each containing a prompt to make it easy for participants to respond in writing. The study used a DCT adapted from Blum-Kulka (1980) to measure participants' initial interlanguage pragmatic knowledge prior to ILP instruction, chosen because DCTs are easy to use and provide rich data in a comparably short period of time. (Mahnaz Mahmoudi Ravesh (2017))
- **Open-ended Discourse Completion Test (DCT) with 12 compliment situations**: The study's primary instrument is an open-ended Discourse Completion Test adopted from Chen and Boonkongsaen's (2012) study, which took its 12 situations from Yuan (2002). As the abstract puts it, "The items in the DCT included 12 situations based on which the participants were required to give or respond to compliments". Part one (8 items) measures complimenting and part two measures responding to compliments; it was administered as pre-test and post-test, with contextual variables neutralized in analysis. (Saman Ebadi (2015))
