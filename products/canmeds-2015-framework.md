## Description
A physician-competency framework developed and maintained by the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada, defining seven professional roles and their associated competencies.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **CanMEDS 2015 framework as an organizing lens for patient-generated feedback content**: The CanMEDS 2015 framework defines physician competencies across seven roles. The article describes it as "comprised of seven roles: Scholar, Professional, Health Advocate, Communicator, Collaborator, Leader, and Medical Expert" and uses it deductively to categorize patient comments from low-rating reviews. It functions here as a coding taxonomy showing that lay patient complaints map onto formally defined physician competency domains, enabling frequency summaries such as Professional (59.7%) and Leader (45.3%). (Pulkki KH et al. (2025))
- **CanMEDS Physician Competency Framework as an analytical tool for curricular mapping**: The CanMEDS framework, developed by the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada, "describes key competencies, grouped thematically under seven professional roles" of the physician, each role represented by two to five key competencies. The study uses it as an analytical tool: module components are mapped to key competencies and results reported at the role level to increase reliability. The authors conclude it "is proved to be an appropriate analytical tool for presenting the acquisition of competencies" in the qualification programme, while noting its suitability for structuring teaching units is critically discussed. (Homberg Angelika et al. (2019))
- **CanMEDS Global Health Competency framework for introductory global health curricula**: The study operationalizes global health learning through the CanMEDS roles, "a physician competency framework describing the knowledge, skills and attitudes that physicians need to ensure better patient outcomes", comprising seven roles: medical expert, communicator, advocate, professional, collaborator, manager and scholar. Combined with the Ontario Global Health Education Competencies Framework, it structured both the e-learning modules, the knowledge quiz and the 22-item self-assessment tool, and framed the basic concepts students were exposed to (e.g., recognizing how one's own cultural biases affect patient interactions). (Gruner D et al. (2015))
