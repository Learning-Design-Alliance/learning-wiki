# 34-item Community of Inquiry Survey

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A validated survey developed by Swan et al. to measure teaching, social, and cognitive presence in Community of Inquiry-designed courses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **34-item Community of Inquiry Survey instrument**: A validated 34-item survey instrument developed to assess the three CoI presences (teaching, social, and cognitive). Swan et al. (2008) collaboratively developed it and "tested its efficacy on a multi-institutional, multi-disciplinary sample of students taking CoI-designed courses," validating the three-dimension framework. In the present study it was administered electronically to MBA students, with additional items added to capture prior online experience and course delivery method. (Honig et al. (2021))
- **Community of inquiry scale (Arbaugh et al. 2008, Turkish adaptation by Öztürk 2012)**: The study's outcome measure is "The five -point Likert type scale consists of 34 items with three factors": social presence (9 items), cognitive presence (12 items), and teaching presence (13 items). In the original version "the Cronbach's alpha value was calculated as .97", with sub-factor reliabilities of .88, .75 and .92. Items probe, for example, whether online discussions helped develop a sense of collaborating with others and whether the teacher kept the class focused on course work. (Aktı-Aslan et al. (2021))

## Description
A validated 34-item survey instrument developed to assess the three CoI presences (teaching, social, and cognitive). Swan et al. (2008) collaboratively developed it and "tested its efficacy on a multi-institutional, multi-disciplinary sample of students taking CoI-designed courses," validating the three-dimension framework. ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administration with reference to a specific course the student is currently taking or has most recently completed
#### Constraints
- The authors note the survey did not fully capture the nature of feedback MBA students sought, and that peer monitoring thoughts and actions seemed to go beyond what is measured by the CoI survey

### Target Learners
- higher education students in online and blended courses

### Target Learning Goals
- assessment of teaching, social, and cognitive presence perceptions
