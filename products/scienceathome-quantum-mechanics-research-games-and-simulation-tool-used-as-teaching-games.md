# ScienceAtHome quantum mechanics research games and simulation tool used as teaching games

## Description
A suite of ScienceAtHome citizen-science research games and simulation tools in which players contribute to quantum-computing research and which can also be repurposed for university teaching.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Human-computer hybrid optimization via citizen science games, with a player-participation taxonomy (flâneurs, average participants, heroes)**: The article's core framework: problems that can be represented as engaging game levels are given to human players whose solutions are combined with computer algorithms, "Helping AIs learn through real people's blooming buzzing mess of solutions". Local algorithms get stuck in local maxima while random algorithms are exceedingly slow; the aim is "optimization that is rational most of the time, but sometimes makes seemingly random errors or leaps of intuition". The framework also defines a participation taxonomy: curious drop-outs called flâneurs, average participants who might contribute by fluke, and the small percentage of tenacious skilled players called "heroes" who persist far beyond the game's user contribution threshold (UCT). (Andreas Lieberoth et al. (2014))

### Claims
