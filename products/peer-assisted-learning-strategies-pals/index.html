Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 16 claims (10 for, 4 mixed, 2 against) · 15 studies (8 quant-synthesis, 4 causal, 2 review, 1 qualitative), q2–q4 · 8 of 15 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A commercially distributed, scripted peer-assisted reading and literacy curriculum for kindergarten through upper elementary grades, developed by researchers including Patricia G. Mathes and distributed through the Vanderbilt Kennedy Center and Sopris West.

Components¶

  • PALS commercially distributed materials and cost structure: PALS materials "range from $15 to $35" and are distributed by the Vanderbilt Kennedy Center for Research on Human Development. Large-print lessons ($15) are recommended for kindergarten; 1st-grade materials consist of scripted lessons, teacher-directed decodable worksheets, and decoding lesson worksheets for student pairs; materials for 2nd grade and above consist of a teacher's manual with scripted lessons, with students using classroom reading material to implement the program. Kindergarten and first-grade materials were developed by Patricia G. Mathes and others under the Peer Assisted Literacy Strategies name, distributed by Sopris West. (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (2010))
  • Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) first-grade literacy program: PALS is a peer-tutoring technique in which "children work in pairs, taking turns as teacher and learner, to learn a structured sequence of literacy skills". The sequence covers "phonemic awareness, phonics, sound blending, passage reading, and story retelling", and "children use a simple error correction strategy with each other, under guidance of a teacher". The program requires no additional classroom staff or technology but does require on-site coaches. (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) (2001))
  • PALS: structured peer-tutoring program supplementing the primary reading curriculum: Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies and Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies are "peer-tutoring programs that supplement the primary reading curriculum" in which students work in pairs, alternately acting as tutor and tutee, on partner reading, paragraph "shrinking," and prediction relay activities intended to improve reading accuracy, fluency, and comprehension. Sessions last 35 minutes and are implemented three to four times per week, with separate versions for kindergarten, grade 1, and grades 2–6. Teachers receive an all-day training workshop with modeling and role-playing, and a team point system motivates accurate reading and paragraph summarizing. (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) — Beginning Reading)
  • PALS three-strategy reciprocal tutoring routine: partner reading and retelling, paragraph shrinking, and prediction relay: A recurring lesson-level structure in which paired students alternate tutor and tutee roles across three timed activities. During "partner reading and retelling, the stronger reader reads for five minutes, while the weaker reader serves as the coach by identifying errors, initiating correction procedures, and awarding points". In paragraph shrinking, readers generate main idea statements in 10 words or less for one point per correct response; in prediction relay, the reader predicts before reading half a page and evaluates the prediction afterward. Roles switch after each timed segment. (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (2010))
  • Peer tutoring as a primary-prevention evidence-based practice in first-grade math: At primary prevention, the classroom teacher supplements the district curriculum with a peer-tutoring program run "3 times per week for 30 minutes each session." Each session opens with teacher-led introduction or review, then higher-performing students are paired with lower-performing students to work on the lesson. Topics include number concepts, comparing numbers, addition and subtraction concepts, place value, double-digit addition and subtraction, and missing addends. (Powell et al. (2007))
  • Form tutoring pairs by ranking students by reading achievement and pairing across halves of the ranked list: how the teacher forms the tutoring pairs

Claims¶

  • Teachers and students report generally favorable impressions of PALS, with least favorability toward team-based structure components [+W]
  • PALS shows strong evidence of positive effects on first-grade literacy, with an average effect size of +0.23 across three studies [+W]
  • PALS effects on the learning-disability subsets analyzed separately were not statistically significant, while the full-sample effect was significant in one study (Fuchs et al., 1997) and not in the other (Saenz et al., 2005) [+W]
  • PALS shows no discernible effects on mathematics for students with learning disabilities [-W]
  • PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading comprehension for students with learning disabilities (average effect size 0.75, not statistically significant) [+W]
  • PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency for students with learning disabilities, with a substantively important but not statistically significant WWC-calculated effect size of 0.41 in Saenz et al. (2005) [+W]
  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed no statistically significant effects on seven alphabetics outcomes compared with adult tutoring [~W]
  • PALS showed no statistically significant effects on near-transfer and far-transfer reading fluency for first graders [~W]
  • K-PALS shows statistically significant positive effects on Rapid Letter Sounds for kindergarten students in all three training-support conditions [+W]
  • Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies showed a substantively important positive (but statistically non-significant) effect on WRMT-R Passage Comprehension for first graders [+W]
  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed a substantively important negative (but statistically non-significant) effect on reading comprehension versus adult tutoring [-W]
  • The WWC rates PALS as having potentially positive effects on alphabetics, no discernible effects on fluency, and mixed effects on comprehension for beginning readers [~W]
  • Effectiveness collapses when pairs are poorly matched or coaching is inaccurate — learners may reinforce each other's errors Peer-assisted learning interventions produce moderate positive effects on achievement. [~M]
  • Struggling readers and students with learning disabilities in inclusive classrooms, who gain from the high rate of supervised practice and immediate feedback Feedback is most effective at the task and process levels. [+S]
  • Average and high performers also gain, primarily through the Coach role Learning by teaching improves the tutor's own learning. [+S]
  • Self-regulation of reading: monitoring understanding and repairing breakdowns Contingent scaffolding improves learning. [+M]

Using it in practice¶

Instructions¶

  1. Train the class: model reader and coach roles, demonstrate coaching prompts and point-awarding, and have pairs rehearse with feedback (Modeling)
  2. Assign pairs and roles; pair stronger with weaker readers and alternate who goes first across sessions
  3. Run Partner Reading: the higher-performing partner reads first, the coach follows along, records errors, and awards points
  4. Run Paragraph Shrinking: partners take turns stating the main idea of each paragraph in ten words or fewer, earning points for accuracy
  5. Run Prediction Relay: partners predict what comes next, read to verify, and check their predictions
  6. Monitor and correct: circulate, listen to pairs, correct inaccurate coaching, and end with whole-class feedback (Provide Feedback)

Requirements¶

  • Appropriately leveled texts so both partners can read with support
  • Structured prompts and role scripts (what the coach says, when, and how)
  • Initial training sessions in which the instructor models coach and reader roles (Modeling)
  • Protected classroom time — typically 25–35 minutes per session, 2–3 times per week
  • Ongoing instructor monitoring and periodic whole-class correction of coaching errors
  • A validated PALS manual and materials for the grade band (K–6 reading, grades 2–6 math, high school variants)
  • Teacher-led training in coaching behaviors: prompting, praising, correcting errors without giving answers
  • Carefully matched pairs — typically a stronger and weaker student, with ability ranking done privately
  • A predictable session schedule (e.g., 2–3 sessions per week, 25–35 minutes) sustained over many weeks
  • Ongoing teacher monitoring and point-awarding to keep pairs on task
  • Careful pairing: a stronger reader with a weaker reader, with the stronger reader reading first as the initial model
  • Explicit training phase in which the teacher models each coaching move and students rehearse the scripts (Coaching and Think-Aloud by the teacher)
  • Short, timed activity segments with clear role-switch cues to keep both students engaged
  • A point system that rewards correct strategy use and specific, positive corrections, not speed or competition between pairs

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness collapses when pairs are poorly matched or coaching is inaccurate — learners may reinforce each other's errors Peer-assisted learning interventions produce moderate positive effects on achievement. [~M]
  • Some learners dominate while others become passive unless roles and points enforce participation from both partners
  • Requires substantial setup and monitoring; under-trained pairs produce superficial coaching
  • Less effective for tasks requiring genuine expertise the peers do not yet have — peers can support fluency practice and comprehension strategies but cannot model expert performance (Cognitive Apprenticeship elements still need the instructor)
  • Gains depend on fidelity to the scripts; loosely adapted "peer tutoring" without training and structure shows much weaker effects [-M]
  • Weaker students may simply imitate or comply without understanding if the coach lacks the skill to explain errors [-W]
  • Requires sustained implementation (typically 12–35 weeks); short trials rarely show measurable effects [~M]
  • Not a substitute for explicit, teacher-led instruction on new content — PALS is a practice and consolidation structure, not a first-teaching method [-M]
  • Gains shrink when pairs are poorly matched or when the stronger reader dominates without executing genuine coaching moves [~M]
  • The scripted routines constrain strategy depth; strong comprehenders may plateau because the prompts cap the sophistication of their questioning and summarizing [~M]
  • Requires sustained implementation — effects emerge over weeks of regular sessions, not single lessons [-W]
  • Without teacher monitoring, pairs drift off-script and the point system degenerates into off-task point-keeping [~M]
