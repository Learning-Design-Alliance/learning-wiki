## Description
A leadership-development programme run by New Leaders for New Schools that prepares and supports urban public-school principals through intensive training, a mentored residency, seminars, and leadership coaching.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **New Leaders for New Schools principal training program**: NLNS is a national, New York City-based organization begun in 2000 that promotes high academic achievement by 'attracting, preparing, and supporting the next generation of outstanding leaders for the nation's urban public schools.' Its Chicago program includes a five-week Foundations Institute summer session, a residency year working with a mentor principal as a member of the school's leadership team, four weeklong seminars, and weekly meetings with Leadership Coaches. The Fund has invested $800,000 and CPS $4,000,000 in NLNS to date. (Hart et al. (2006))

## Description
NLNS is a national, New York City-based organization begun in 2000 that promotes high academic achievement by 'attracting, preparing, and supporting the next generation of outstanding leaders for the nation's urban public schools.' Its Chicago program includes a five-week Foundations Institute summer session, a residency year working with a mentor principal as a member of the school's leadership team, four weeklong seminars, and weekly meetings with Leadership Coaches. The Fund has invested $800,000 and CPS $4,000,000 in NLNS to date.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection through a two-day process including interviews, written assignments, case studies, role-playing, and a presentation at Finalist Selection Day
#### Constraints
- At the time of the survey only 13 NLNS principals led Chicago schools, with an average tenure of a year and a half

### Target Learners
- Aspiring urban public school principals, often younger career-changers with fewer years of teaching experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Urban school leadership and instructional improvement
