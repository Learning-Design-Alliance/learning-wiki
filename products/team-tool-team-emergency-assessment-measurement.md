# TEAM tool (Team Emergency Assessment Measurement)

## Description
A validated questionnaire for rating team-level leadership, teamwork, task management, and overall non-technical performance during medical emergencies, developed for healthcare simulation assessment.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **TEAM tool (Team Emergency Assessment Measurement) for assessing non-technical skills in medical emergencies**: The TEAM tool is a validated questionnaire for evaluating teamwork in medical emergencies, used here to measure acquisition and retention of non-technical skills across the three simulation moments. As the article describes it, "It consists of twelve items, the first eleven evaluating the team's performance, using a Likert scale from 0 to 4 points in the following categories: leadership, teamwork, and task management." Question twelve scores the team's global non-technical performance on a 0–10 scale. The authors chose it for its prior validation, Portuguese translation, easy application, and free availability. (Friedlaender Rafaella Fadel et al. (2025))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) scale for assessing non-technical skills in critical situations**: The Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) scale, described in the article as "a globally validated scale for assessing TNTS in critical situations" with a Spanish version. It has 11 items in three sections — leadership, teamwork and task management — each scored 0 to 4, plus a 1-to-10 global rating, completed by all participant roles after each scenario. (Laurens L.M. et al. (2023))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM), German translation**: TEAM is an observational instrument of 11 items measuring teamwork behaviour of medical teams in critical situations, with 3 subscales (leadership, teamwork, task management), items rated 0–4, a sum score of 0–44, and a global rating scale of 1–10. The authors translated it into German using TRAPD methodology and validated it in a simulation study, concluding it "is a reliable and valid tool to assess teamwork performance that closes a gap in measuring teamwork behaviour in German-speaking countries." (Freytag Julia et al. (2019))
