# TEAM tool (Team Emergency Assessment Measurement)

## Description
A validated questionnaire for rating team-level leadership, teamwork, task management, and overall non-technical performance during medical emergencies, developed for healthcare simulation assessment.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **TEAM tool (Team Emergency Assessment Measurement) for assessing non-technical skills in medical emergencies**: The TEAM tool is a validated questionnaire for evaluating teamwork in medical emergencies, used here to measure acquisition and retention of non-technical skills across the three simulation moments. As the article describes it, "It consists of twelve items, the first eleven evaluating the team's performance, using a Likert scale from 0 to 4 points in the following categories: leadership, teamwork, and task management." Question twelve scores the team's global non-technical performance on a 0–10 scale. The authors chose it for its prior validation, Portuguese translation, easy application, and free availability. (Friedlaender Rafaella Fadel et al. (2025))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) scale for assessing non-technical skills in critical situations**: The Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) scale, described in the article as "a globally validated scale for assessing TNTS in critical situations" with a Spanish version. It has 11 items in three sections — leadership, teamwork and task management — each scored 0 to 4, plus a 1-to-10 global rating, completed by all participant roles after each scenario. (Laurens L.M. et al. (2023))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM), German translation**: TEAM is an observational instrument of 11 items measuring teamwork behaviour of medical teams in critical situations, with 3 subscales (leadership, teamwork, task management), items rated 0–4, a sum score of 0–44, and a global rating scale of 1–10. The authors translated it into German using TRAPD methodology and validated it in a simulation study, concluding it "is a reliable and valid tool to assess teamwork performance that closes a gap in measuring teamwork behaviour in German-speaking countries." (Freytag Julia et al. (2019))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) instrument for rating resuscitation team nontechnical skills**: The TEAM is a validated observational rating instrument for emergency team nontechnical skills, used in this study to score video-recorded resuscitations. It "comprises 11 items scored from 0 to 4 across 3 domains: leadership (2 items), teamwork (7 items), and task management (2 items), supplemented by a global rating of overall team performance (1–10)". Two trained senior emergency physicians scored all events independently and reached consensus scores; the global rating showed an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.899 (95% CI, 0.867–0.927). (Lien Chun‐Ju; Huang Shuo‐Kuen; Chang Jia‐How; Huang Edward Pei‐Chuan (2026))
- **Team Emergency Assessment Measure (TEAM) score as an observational instrument for team non-technical skills**: The TEAM score is a validated observational scoring system for emergency team performance: "an observer awards a score between 0 (never observed) and 4 (always observed) for 11 features showing positive team attitudes and behaviours". In this study it served as the primary outcome, scored by trained observers with an inter-rater correlation coefficient of 0.958 across the first four scenarios, after which single observers scored remaining scenarios. (Renza Metoděj et al. (2023))
- **TEAM instrument (Team Emergency Assessment Measure) validated for distributed team contexts**: The Team Emergency Assessment Measure is an observational instrument of 11 items in three domains (Leadership, Teamwork, Task Management), each rated 0 (never) to 4 (always), plus a 12th overall performance item rated 1–10. The study used it on video recordings of simulated scenarios after prior validation: "Before using the TEAM instrument in this study, we validated it as part of the TIGER research program to ensure its applicability to the distributed team setting." Two trained raters, blinded to randomization order, independently assessed the recordings. (Morian H et al. (2024))
