Description¶

TRIAD is a mathematics-intervention framework that links professional development, teacher knowledge, coaching, implementation fidelity, and child achievement in scaled-up pre-K and elementary implementations.

Components¶

  • Network of Influences framework for implementation and child achievement: TRIAD's theoretical framework is an elaboration of the Network of Influences model, illustrated in Figure 1 and described as "consistent with, but extends in levels of detail, such theories as diffusion theory and the overlapping spheres of influence." The framework distinguishes contextual variables (school, teacher, and child factors, e.g., socioeconomic status influencing initial math knowledge and achievement) from implementation variables that "the project can encourage and support, but cannot control absolutely." The figure caption states that "heavy arrows from professional development (J), to teacher knowledge (N), to implementation fidelity (O), to child achievement (R), indicate the strong effects in that path," while support from coaches has a strong effect on implementation fidelity and other factors are influential to a moderate degree. The framework applies recursively to the follow-through treatment and its evaluation. (Douglas H. Clements et al. (2013))

Claims¶

  • The Building Blocks pre-K implementation showed positive effects on pre-K mathematics (ES=.72) and kindergarten language competencies, with stronger first-grade effects for those with follow-through [+W]
