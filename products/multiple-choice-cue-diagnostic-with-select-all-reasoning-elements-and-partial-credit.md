# Multiple-choice CUE diagnostic with select-all reasoning elements and partial credit

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A multiple-choice adaptation of the Colorado Upper-division Electrostatics diagnostic developed by Wilcox and colleagues, using select-all reasoning elements and partial-credit scoring to assess physics students' solution-method choices and justifications.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The coupled multiple-response (CMR) CUE assessment**: The CMR CUE is a modified version of the free-response Colorado Upper-division Electrostatics Diagnostic in which students "select multiple responses and receive partial credit depending on the accuracy and consistency of their selections." Reasoning elements were drawn from codes of common FR student responses; full credit requires selecting all and only the elements forming a complete justification. The instrument is released via the PER@Colorado electrostatics page and Supplemental Materials. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2014))
- **Select-all reasoning-element multiple-choice format with accuracy-and-consistency partial credit**: An assessment item pattern in which a single-answer method question is paired with a set of checkable reasoning elements, and scoring rewards both the correctness of the choice and the coherence of the justification. The article states that "Full credit requires that a student select all (and only) the reasoning elements that together form a complete justiﬁcation", with partial credit for incomplete selections and, under rubric R2, deductions for inconsistent elements. It is designed to capture partially correct reasoning that standard multiple choice cannot. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2013))
