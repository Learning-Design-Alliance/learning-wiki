# Three-stage diagnostic test for identifying conceptual errors

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 30-item three-stage water-chemistry diagnostic test developed by Taş et al. for 8th- and 9th-grade students that combines answers, answer reasons, and confidence judgments to identify conceptual errors and guesses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Eighteen response types grouped into seven categories, including a learning-risk zone, as a diagnostic taxonomy for three-tier test responses**: The article assigns values to the three tiers (answer 0/1, justification 0/1/2, certainty 0/1/2), producing "a total of 18 response types", each given a key. These are grouped into seven response groups: good certain learning, good uncertain learning, weak learning, false positives, false negatives, the zone of learning risk, and non-developed learning. The zone of learning risk (categories -5, -6, -8, -9) represents "the probable presence of conceptual errors, alternative science models and weakly developed abilities, combined with medium and high degrees of certainty". (Buzzo et al. (2012))
