MAP Growth¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes Evidence · 79 claims (76 for, 3 mixed) · 32 studies (24 associational, 3 design, 3 theoretical, 2 causal), q1–q3 · 2 of 32 report an effect size · 77 claims rest on one study

Description¶

MAP Growth is NWEA's computer-adaptive assessment system, with national achievement and growth norms and reporting tools such as the MAP Growth National Dashboard.

Components¶

  • NWEA 2020 MAP Growth Achievement Status and Growth Norms for Students and Schools: The 2020 MAP Growth Norms are a norming study and released reference tables characterizing student and school achievement and growth in academic subjects on the MAP Growth scale. The report states that norms "characterize each score in terms of its re lative distance from other scores in a distri- bution of scores." The release covers mathematics, reading, language usage, and science, with appendices of status norms, growth norms, status percentiles, and conditional growth distributions and percentiles for students and schools. (Thum et al. (2020))
  • 2025 MAP Growth norms update with Enhanced Item Selection Algorithm (EISA) alignment: The 2025 MAP Growth norms update recalibrates the national reference group for RIT scores, achievement percentiles, conditional growth percentiles, and projected proficiency. It was prompted by post-COVID achievement and growth patterns falling "substantially below prepandemic trends" and incorporates technical enhancements, most notably that "the update aligns norms to the EISA, which strengthens alignment of MAP Growth to grade level content." NWEA updates its norms every three to five years; supporting documentation includes the 2025 Norms Technical Manual and Norms Toolkit. (Hashim (2026))
  • Adaptive interim assessment with an automated disengagement-detection engagement feature: The article evaluates an adaptive interim assessment administered to K-12 students both in school and remotely. Its engagement feature "automatically paused the test event of a disengaged student and notified the test proctor," enabling proctors to intervene whether they were "in the same room as the student" or proctoring remotely. The study used disengagement on this assessment to compare remote and in-school testing contexts. (Steven Wise et al. (2022))
  • Content Proximity item-selection algorithm for MAP Growth assessments: Content Proximity is a new item-selection algorithm used by MAP Growth assessments, introduced in a spring 2022 pilot study reported by NWEA. The article describes it as "this new item-selection algorithm" leveraged by MAP Growth assessments, and the pilot evaluated the resulting assessments' validity, reliability, and test score comparability. The supplied text prints no further detail about how the algorithm selects items or how it differs from prior approaches. (Patrick Meyer et al. (2023))
  • Technical appendix document detailing sample and methods for the Learning during COVID-19 achievement brief: A technical brief published by NWEA Research in July 2021, released alongside the research brief on COVID-19-era reading and math achievement. Its stated purpose is to "share more detailed results and to describe more fully the sample and methods used in the research included in the brief." It serves as the methods and detailed-results companion to the main brief on 2020-21 school-year achievement. (NWEA Research (2021))
  • MAP Reading and Mathematics computerized adaptive tests (NWEA): MAP tests are computerized adaptive assessments published by NWEA since 2000 for grades 3-9, intended "to provide educators with information to inform teaching and learning in Reading, Mathematics, and Science". The Reading test has about 40 multiple-choice items and four goals; the Mathematics test has 50 items and seven goals. Tests are aligned to state content standards, and "the marginal reliabilities of tests across 50 states and grades are consistently in the low to mid 0.90's". (Shudong Wang et al. (2013))
  • MAP Growth assessment dataset covering 5.5 million students in grades 3-8 in reading and math: The brief draws on the MAP Growth assessment program, NWEA's national assessment product, to examine pandemic-era learning. The analysis uses "data from 5.5 million students in grades 3-8 who took MAP Growth assessments in reading and math," comparing "gains across the 2020-21 school year and student achievement in spring 2021" to pre-pandemic trends. The dataset supports both within-year growth comparisons and spring achievement level comparisons. (Learning during COVID-19: Reading et al. (2021))
  • MAP Growth assessment dataset used to measure pandemic achievement effects (8.3 million students, grades 3-8): The brief's evidence base is a large-scale observational dataset of MAP Growth assessment records in reading and math. It covers "8.3 million students in grades 3-8 who took MAP Growth assessments in reading and math between 2018–19 and 2021–22" alongside "a similarly sized sample of students who tested between 2015–16 and 2018–19" as a pre-pandemic comparison. The dataset supports grade-, subgroup-, and school-poverty-level analyses of pandemic impacts and recovery. (Kuhfeld et al. (2022))
  • MAP Growth assessment: MAP Growth is an assessment product published by NWEA, listed as the product associated with NWEA research publications on achievement, growth, equity, and test engagement. In the supplied text it appears repeatedly as "Products: MAP Growth" across related technical reports, research briefs, and data tools, including national trend dashboards reporting "student academic achievement and growth for grades K-8." It is the assessment context within which NWEA research on test effort and subgroup score differences is situated. (James Soland (2019))
  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment data as the measurement basis for COVID-19 learning-loss research: The page situates the technical appendix within NWEA's portfolio of research using MAP Growth assessment data, including the "MAP Growth National Dashboard" cited as the data source for related post-COVID trend reports. MAP Growth serves as the achievement measure underpinning the brief's comparisons of 2020-21 gains and spring 2021 achievement to pre-pandemic trends and levels. (NWEA Research (2021))
  • MAP Growth assessment data as the evidence base for district-level absenteeism and recovery analysis: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product whose district-level data underpins this research brief. The brief states it is "Using MAP Growth data from 2,000 US school districts" to study the link between absenteeism and academic recovery in math and reading. The dataset spans a nationwide sample of US districts, enabling district-level comparisons of recovery trajectories. (Sofia Postell et al. (2026))
  • MAP Growth rapid-guessing invalidation criterion and four-level disengagement classification: MAP Growth test events carry both performance data (RIT score plus standard error) and engagement data (percent of item responses that were rapid guesses). The study formed "four levels of test-taking disengagement": serious (30% or more rapid guesses), moderate (11 to 30%), mild (1 to 10%), and zero disengagement. The invalidation criterion holds that when students rapid-guess on 30% or more of items, scores are apt to be so distorted they are too untrustworthy to report. (Wise (2019))
  • MAP Growth assessment as a data source for gender-gap trend analysis: MAP Growth is NWEA's national assessment used in this brief as one of three data sources for estimating gender gaps in 8th-grade STEM skills. The brief analyzes "five years of data from three national assessments – TIMMS, NAEP, and NWEA's MAP Growth" and separately draws on "a sample of MAP Growth data from two million students" for subgroup analyses. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2025))
  • MAP Growth assessments as the measurement instrument for cross-sector K-8 growth comparison: MAP Growth assessments are standardized tests administered to students in the study's national sample. The article uses scores from students "who took MAP Growth assessments" to "examine achievement growth over time between sectors," comparing Catholic and public school students from kindergarten through eighth grade in math and reading. (Dallavis et al. (2021))
  • MAP Growth linking study report with cut score tables, classification accuracy statistics, and proficiency projections: A state-specific report NWEA produces for each linking study that "explains the methodology, summarizes the study sample, presents the results, and provides evidence that shows how well the RIT scores can predict state summative performance." Each report presents sample demographics, descriptive statistics, fall/winter/spring RIT cut scores corresponding to state performance levels, classification accuracy statistics, and probabilities of achieving proficiency based on RIT scores. Results are also embedded in the NWEA reporting system and individual score reports. (Ann Hu (2021))
  • MAP Growth assessment and National Dashboard as the data infrastructure for NWEA COVID-recovery research: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product, and the MAP Growth National Dashboard is the data source NWEA researchers use for post-COVID academic recovery analyses. The page repeatedly names it: reports 'focus on academic growth trends post-COVID' 'Using data from the MAP Growth National Dashboard', and one related brief analyzed 'MAP Growth data from 2,000 US school districts.' The 2025 update to MAP Growth national norms and an enhanced item selection algorithm (EISA) are also named as changing how norm-referenced metrics are interpreted. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2022))
  • MAP Growth assessment and National Dashboard as data sources for NWEA COVID-recovery research: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product, and the MAP Growth National Dashboard is the data source NWEA researchers use for post-COVID achievement and growth trend analyses. The page repeatedly names it as the underlying data source: "Using data from the MAP Growth National Dashboard, this research brief highlights an urgent challenge in academic recovery: middle school reading." It supports NWEA's series of briefs on pandemic recovery, absenteeism, and classroom academic diversity. (Megan Kuhfeld (2022))
  • MAP Growth assessment data as the evidence base for national pandemic-recovery monitoring: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product whose nationwide score data underpins this brief's findings on pandemic recovery. The brief is described as sharing "several key findings" "Based on data from 2023-24," covering growth trends, pre-COVID gap widening, months-of-schooling catch-up estimates, and race/ethnicity comparisons. It functions here as the measurement instrument enabling longitudinal recovery monitoring. (Karyn Lewis (2024))
  • Same-day MAP Growth retest dataset spanning four test types and four testing terms: The study extracted MAP Growth Common Core test events in Reading 2-5, Reading 6+, Math 2-5, and Math 6+ from four testing terms: Fall 2017, Winter 2018, Spring 2018, and Fall 2018. Table 1 prints total test events ranging from 4,324,638 (Reading 6+) to 4,864,398 (Math 2-5), with retests within one day of 36,971 (Reading 2-5), 31,694 (Reading 6+), 16,106 (Math 2-5), and 16,938 (Math 6+), over 100,000 retests in total. (Wise (2019))
  • MAP Growth assessment scores as the achievement outcome data source: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product whose scores serve as the achievement outcome in this heat-achievement analysis. The brief states the study "combines daily temperature data from various sources with MAP Growth scores for grades 3–8 across six states", drawing on roughly three million test events from fall 2022, 2023 and 2024. It functions here as the student achievement measure linked to daily temperature exposure. (Sofia Postell et al. (2025))
  • NWEA MAP Growth vertically scaled assessment as a common comparison measure: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment used in this study as a common assessment against which SEDA's district growth estimates are compared. The article describes it as a "vertically scaled" assessment from which district growth estimates in mathematics and ELA can be derived, serving as an independent benchmark for the convergent validity analysis of SEDA. Its estimates show a high degree of congruence with SEDA's, with small systematic discrepancies in some contexts. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2019))
  • NWEA 2023-24 student achievement data brief on pandemic recovery: A research brief from NWEA reporting student achievement trends based on data from 2023-24. It shares key findings on growth relative to pre-pandemic trends, the widening gap between pre-COVID and COVID test score averages, and months of schooling needed to catch up. The brief is associated with the MAP Growth product line and topics of COVID-19 and schools, equity, and growth. (Karyn Lewis (2024))
  • NWEA chronic absenteeism research brief linking absenteeism to slower academic recovery: A research brief by Sofia Postell, Emily Morton, and Megan Kuhfeld examining how absenteeism relates to recovery from COVID-era learning disruptions. The page describes it as follows: "Using MAP Growth data from 2,000 US school districts, NWEA researchers analyzed whether persistently high absenteeism rates were associated with slower recovery in math and reading." It is listed with a companion technical appendix under the topics COVID-19 & schools and Growth. (Megan Kuhfeld (2022))
  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment as national data source for seasonal skill-growth analysis: The NWEA MAP Growth assessment supplies the recent national data on U.S. public school students used in this study's seasonal comparisons. The abstract states the analysis covers "numerous grade levels from the NWEA MAP Growth assessment" and four domains: math, reading, language use, and science. In the article it functions as the measurement instrument enabling school-year versus summer variance comparisons. (Dennis Condron et al. (2021))
  • School Challenge Index (SCI) and MAP Growth conditional growth metrics for school-level poverty and growth measurement: The School Challenge Index (SCI) is an NWEA metric created during the 2011 norms development to consistently measure the level of challenge a school faces across the United States. The article explains that "The SCI is primarily based on the percentage of students eligible for FRL, but is moderated by other factors, such as location, school level, Title I eligibility, and school type." MAP Growth supplies the achievement and growth data, with conditional growth index (CGI) and conditional growth percentile (CGP) metrics designed to compare the relative growth of different students fairly. The study used median achievement and growth percentiles per school as its school-level measures. (Andy Hegedus (2018))
  • Technical appendix document detailing sample and methods for the COVID-19 achievement divide brief: A technical brief published by NWEA Research in November 2022 accompanying the research brief 'The widening achievement divide during COVID-19.' Its stated purpose is to 'share more detailed results and to describe more fully the sample and methods used in the research included in the brief.' It is tagged under the topics COVID-19 & schools and Equity and is authored by Megan Kuhfeld, Meredith Langi, and Karyn Lewis. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2022))
  • ARLIS: Algorithm for Reward Listed Item Selection: ARLIS is the enhanced MAP Growth item-selection algorithm introduced by the Content Proximity Project. It is "a compensatory version of the maximum priority index (Cheng & Chang, 2009) that borrows ideas from reinforcement learning," which may be thought of as reinforcement learning with a greedy policy and frequent deterministic rewards. An item-selection engine acts as the agent choosing items from the pool; a reward is assigned to each content feature, a total reward (a weighted average of functions representing statistical and content requirements) is computed per item, items are listed in descending order of total reward, and the highest-value item is selected, with ties broken randomly (J. Patrick Meyer et al. (2023))
  • MAP Growth theory of action within a comprehensive assessment system: The report articulates a theory of action positioning MAP Growth as the interim component of a comprehensive assessment system alongside summative and formative assessments. Its premise is that "when MAP Growth is situated in a comprehensive assessment system and used for its intended purposes (to yield information about student learning and enable educators to make data-informed decisions about curriculum and instruction), all students learn." Intended uses are classified as instructional, predictive, or evaluative, with aggregation matched to the level of decision-making. (MAP Growth Technical Report for 2024–2025 (2026))
  • RIT vertical equal-interval scale grounded in item response theory: The RIT scale is the report's central measurement framework: an equal-interval vertical scale spanning grades K–12, derived from item response theory and the Rasch model. The report explains that "The RIT scale has two key characteristics. It is a vertical scale with equal-interval units," and that "the equal-interval characteristic of the RIT scale allows the measurement of growth over two or more time points (i.e., learning) by calculating the difference in scores." IRT places item difficulty and person ability on the same continuous scale, enabling adaptive testing and score comparability across item sets. (MAP Growth Technical Report for 2024–2025 (2026))
  • MAP (Measures of Academic Progress) computerised adaptive test system: MAP is a computerised adaptive assessment system published by NWEA since 1976, covering reading, language usage, mathematics and science. "MAP items are calibrated with the Rasch model (Rasch, 1961), and all MAP tests are vertically scaled", and the article reports that "the marginal reliabilities of test scores are consistently in the low- to mid- 0.90s range across grades, tests and states". Tests use fixed-length CAT with state-customised item pools organised into content-standard goals, and goal scores serve as the observed indicators in this study's MLGM analyses. (Wang et al. (2013))
  • MAP Growth computer-adaptive assessment and its national norms and dashboard: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product, administered remotely or in person, whose scores are interpreted against national achievement and growth norms and summarized through tools such as the MAP Growth National Dashboard. The page describes reports "Using data from the MAP Growth National Dashboard" and a technical report documenting "adaptive testing methods, reliability, validity, fairness, and score interpretation across grades and courses." It underpins the COVID-era achievement and growth analyses indexed here. (Technical appendix for: Learning during COVID-19: Initial findings on students' reading et al. (2020))
  • MAP Growth assessments as the data source for pandemic learning-loss analysis: MAP Growth is NWEA's assessment product whose scores supply the achievement and growth data used in this brief and related NWEA COVID-19 recovery research. The brief draws on "data from 6 million students in grades 3-8 who took MAP Growth assessments in reading and math" to compare pandemic-period outcomes with pre-pandemic trends. Related NWEA publications also use the MAP Growth National Dashboard for post-COVID trend analyses. (Karyn Lewis (2021))
  • MAP Growth interim computer adaptive assessment system: MAP Growth is an interim computer adaptive test administered up to four times per year in Math, Reading, Language Usage, and Science, with scores on the RIT scale. The report states it is "an interim computer adaptive test administered multiple times per year (fall, winter, spring, summer)" and that "Scores are reported on the RIT scale, an equal-interval vertical scale that allows comparison across grades and terms." It supports in-school and remote administration with trained proctors. (MAP Growth Technical Report for 2024–2025 (2026))
