# TEEMSS inquiry-based science curriculum with handheld computers and probeware

## Description
TEEMSS is a physical science curriculum for grades 3–8 that "utilizes computers, sensors, and interactive models to support investigations of real-world phenomena." It comprises 15 inquiry-based instructional units, five per grade band (3–4, 5–6, 7–8), each targeting the five National Science Education Standards. Every unit contains two one-week investigations with a discovery question, several trials, analysis, and ideas for further investigations. Student data are managed by software on handheld computers and can be transmitted to other students and the teacher; a web-based teacher-reporting tool supports assessment and class discussion. Developed by the Concord Consortium with National Science Foundation funding, the curriculum and software are available free at http://teemss.concord.org/.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Handheld computers or desktops with probeware such as sound graphers, thermometers, and sensors; teachers' guides with discussion guides, background material, assessment ideas, technology information, and suggested timelines are included
#### Constraints
- All classroom units use handheld computers to avoid the expense of networked desktop computers, though probeware and materials can be used on desktops as well

### Target Learners
- Elementary and middle school students in grades 3–8

### Target Learning Goals
- Inquiry skills and science content across the National Science Education Standards: Inquiry, Physical Science, Life Science, Earth and Space Science, and Technology and Design
