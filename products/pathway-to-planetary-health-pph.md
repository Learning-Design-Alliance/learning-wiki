# Pathway to Planetary Health (PPH) framework

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The Pathway to Planetary Health is a theoretical framework that "supports dietitians to undertake pro‐environmental change within the food system". It is described as "a non‐sequential, interconnected pathway" guided by six dimensions: Agency, Action, Ascension, Alignment, Alliance and Allyship, and Advocacy and Activism, each with descriptors and practice examples (e.g., Agency covers vision, self-belief, confidence and responsibility; Advocacy and Activism covers urgency, co-design and disruption). This study workshopped the framework with dietitians and evaluated its practical application and impact as proof-of-concept.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Proof-of-concept work to determine the guide's practical application and impact, as recognised by the authorship team that developed the PPH
#### Constraints
- Participants highlighted the need for other supports, including tools and institutional support, to translate the framework into practice

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Objectives
- pro-environmental food system change
- professional confidence and capacity for sustainability action

# Pair individual confidence-building with institutional supports, tools and co-design, since motivation alone is insufficient for food system change

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that individual agency must be complemented by structural supports: "motivation alone is not enough to enable dietitians to influence food systems, and other food system factors should be considered, including institutional supports and co‐design approaches". Participants likewise identified that "more tools and resources are required to support the workforce in achieving these changes". The principle directs capacity-building efforts toward organisational, educational and policy-level approaches alongside individual agency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supportive policies, professional development opportunities, tools and resources, and updating of university curricula to include planetary health concepts
#### Constraints
- These supports may be undermined if dietitians prioritise individual-level nutrition over systems-oriented strategies, per the authors

### Target Learners
- dietitians
- nutrition and dietetics workforce

### Target Learning Objectives
- sustained pro-environmental professional practice
- food system change capability
