# Force and Motion Microworld (FMM): a suite of computer simulation programs confronting alternative conceptions in mechanics through predict-observe-explain tasks

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Force and Motion Microworld was "a suite of computer simulation programs" developed to match and confront three prevalent alternative conceptions in force and motion: force-of-motion, motion-implies-force, and effects of force. It provided three contexts (model car, spaceship and skydiver) in which the effects of force on motion could be explored, and comprised 48 predict-observe-explain tasks across three programs (Model Car, Spaceship, Skydiver). Students worked in dyads, jointly predicting, explaining, running the program, and reconciling discrepancies, writing predictions and observations in worksheets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work collaboratively in dyads carrying out predict-observe-explain tasks according to a set of worksheets
- Tasks designed to provide cognitive conflicts that facilitated conceptual change
#### Constraints
- The research was restricted to collaborative learning within the classroom, with students grouped in dyads at the computer

### Target Learners
- Grade 10 science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Newtonian conceptions of force and motion
- Confronting force-of-motion, motion-implies-force, and effects-of-force alternative conceptions
