Connect with Kids¶

Description¶

Connect with Kids is a character-education curriculum developed for elementary and secondary students that uses peer-story videos, lessons, activities, and schoolwide and community outreach to promote prosocial attitudes and positive behavior.

Components¶

  • Connect with Kids character education program (videos, lesson plans, website, and outreach components): Connect with Kids is a character education curriculum for elementary (grades 3–5) and secondary (grades 6–12) students that "aims to promote prosocial attitudes and positive behavior" by teaching core character values. Lessons combine peer-to-peer videos based on real children's stories with story summaries, discussion questions, student activities, and vocabulary, reinforced by a website component and schoolwide and community outreach. The elementary program has eight units with 48 student activities; the middle/high school program has 36 lessons introducing 26 character traits with 104 student activities. (What Works Clearinghouse (2006))
  • Connect with Kids lesson sequence: trait introduction, video, teacher-led discussion, and delayed follow-up activity: Each Connect with Kids lesson follows a recurring instructional sequence: "Each lesson begins with an introduction of the trait being taught followed by the Connect with Kids video for that trait." The video is followed by teacher-led discussion using questions from the teacher resource guide, and one to two weeks later a follow-up activity in which students practice and demonstrate behaviors associated with the trait through cooperative learning exercises. Video dosage differs by level: three video segments per trait in the middle/high school program and one video per trait in the elementary program. (What Works Clearinghouse (2006))

Claims¶

  • The average effect across all four behavior outcome measures in the Page & D'Agostino study is positive and statistically significant (effect size 0.40) [+M]
  • Connect with Kids shows no statistically significant effects on elementary school students' assessments of their own or classmates' behavior [-W]
  • Connect with Kids shows statistically significant positive effects on middle and high school students' reports of their own and classmates' behavior [+M]
  • The WWC rates Connect with Kids as having potentially positive effects on behavior, limited by only one qualifying study [+W]
