IF-AT¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A commercial scratch-and-reveal multiple-choice assessment form made by Epstein Educational Enterprises that provides immediate item-level correctness feedback and supports partial-credit scoring.

Components¶

  • IF-AT (Immediate Feedback Assessment Technique) scratch-and-reveal answer-until-correct form: The IF-AT is a commercially-available "scratch-and-reveal"-type MC answer form: rows of boxes coated in opaque waxy covering, one keyed answer marked by a black star per question. Students scratch their chosen option; a star confirms correctness, while no star signals an incorrect choice and the student keeps scratching until the star is revealed — an answer-until-correct technique. This gives item-by-item feedback during the exam, enables self-tallying of scores, and supports partial-credit marking schemes without extra instructor resources. (Slepkov (2013))
  • IF-AT (Immediate Feedback Assessment Technique) scratch-off cards: IF-AT cards are commercially produced multiple-choice answer forms on which each answer option is covered by a waxy opaque coating similar to scratch-and-win lottery tickets. Students scratch their chosen answer: a star appears if correct, a blank space if not, and students "can then reconsider the options that remain and continue to scratch -off the boxes until the star is found". The authors obtained the cards from their campus faculty development center, sourced from Epstein Educational Enterprises, and used them as a low-cost review tool in introductory accounting. (Lawrence C. Mohrweis et al. (2015))
  • Use immediate feedback scratch-off cards to add active learning and peer-assisted feedback to lectures: The article recommends IF-AT scratch-off cards as an inexpensive, easy-to-use way for educators to enhance lectures with active learning: students work in groups, debate answers, and learn from mistakes as correct answers are immediately displayed. The authors write that "Scratch-off cards are a tool that educators may find very useful to enhance their lectures and add a component of active learning into their courses", and that the cards facilitate an environment in which peers assist learning. (Lawrence C. Mohrweis et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • IF-AT exam scores correlate with constructed-response quiz scores (r = 0.67), supporting validity [+M]
  • IF-AT-scored physics midterm and final exams yield good test reliability (α = 0.71 and 0.82; α50 = 0.86 and 0.90) [+W]
  • Partial credit accounts for only a small proportion of IF-AT exam scores, though weaker students depend on it more [+W]
  • Polychotomously scored IF-AT physics exam items show excellent discrimination, with a mean item-total correlation of r′ = 0.41 ± 0.13 across 45 items [+M]
  • Partial credit under the IF-AT is granted in a discriminating manner: top final-exam scorers earned 65% ± 23% of available partial credit versus 39% ± 10% for bottom scorers [+M]
  • Introductory accounting students who completed a group-based IF-AT scratch-off card review scored significantly higher on midterm objective exams than students in lecture-review control sections [+M]
