## Description
RESHAPE is a stigma-reduction training intervention for primary care practitioners in Nepal that uses locally grounded narratives and threat-domain targeting to address mental-health stigma.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **What-matters-most framework of stigma domains: survival, social, and professional threats**: The RESHAPE intervention is designed around a what-matters-most framework for understanding the origins of stigma among primary care practitioners, drawn from social neuroscience, social psychology, and medical anthropology. The framework organizes stigmatizing beliefs into "survival threats, social threats, and professional threats": survival threats include beliefs that people with mental illness are violent; social threats include beliefs that interaction could cause mental illness or loss of social status; professional threats include beliefs that providing mental health care is ineffective and burdensome. PhotoVoice narratives and aspirational figure discussions were structured to address these three domains, plus intersectional stigma from dual discrimination experienced by low-caste, ethnic minority, and women practitioners and patients. (Kohrt BA et al. (2021))
