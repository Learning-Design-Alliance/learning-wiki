Open executable module library of six Jupyter notebooks for AI in power systems¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An open library released by Junjie Yin et al. containing six reproducible Jupyter-notebook modules for hands-on AI-in-power-systems learning, runnable locally or through Google Colab.

Claims¶

  • Most surveyed power-and-energy respondents would use a power-specific hands-on AI tutorial, and few rate generic image-based AI examples as very relevant [+W]
  • A DQN-trained battery storage policy reduced complete cost by 5.9% relative to no storage on a synthetic 24-h training-day rollout [+W]
  • In the CNN 5-bus power-flow surrogate, pairing model capacity with sufficient training length improves voltage accuracy, and line flows are the more demanding test [+W]
  • With identical architectures and budgets, a PINN more closely reproduced swing-equation rotor angle and speed trajectories than an ordinary neural network [+W]
  • Module 3 code execution success dropped to approximately 60% before a missing-dependency infrastructure fix, recovering to match the 86–87% rates of Modules 1 and 2 [+W]
