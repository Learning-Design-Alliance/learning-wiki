# Complex Instruction framework

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A pedagogical framework developed by Elizabeth Cohen and colleagues that uses group-worthy tasks, delegated authority, and status interventions to promote equitable participation and competence expectations in collaborative learning.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Complex Instruction framework posits that status characteristics drive unequal group participation and empowers instructors to disrupt status hierarchies through group-worthy tasks, delegated authority, and status interventions.**: The essay draws on the Complex Instruction framework (Cohen, 1994; [Cohen et al., 1999](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167299258011); Lotan, 2003, 2006), which "posits that differences in participation stem from status characteristics (e.g., academic ability, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, gender, and popularity) that students apply to one another." It considers three key components: "group-worthy tasks, delegation of authority, and addressing status problems." The framework empowers instructors to disrupt status hierarchies and shift expectations around competence, promoting equitable participation in learning. (De Jesus M. M. et al. (2026))
