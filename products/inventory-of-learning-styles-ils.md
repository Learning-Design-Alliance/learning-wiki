## Description
A self-report assessment developed by Jan Vermunt to measure higher-education students' processing and regulation strategies, conceptions of learning, and learning orientations.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Inventory of Learning Styles (ILS) measuring four components of student learning patterns**: The ILS is the self-report instrument developed by Vermunt (1996, 1998) to measure the four components of student learning patterns on a larger scale: processing strategies (deep, stepwise, concrete), regulation strategies (self-regulation, external regulation, lack of regulation), conceptions of learning, and learning orientations. Its name was kept after the 2004 renaming of 'style' to 'pattern' to avoid confusion, though the authors note Inventory of Learning Patterns would have been a better name. A strong point is that "it was grounded in interview research with the target student population about their learning in natural, real-life settings". (Jan D. Vermunt et al. (2017))
- **Felder-Soloman Index of Learning Styles (ILS) four-dimension framework**: The ILS is a 44-item two-choice questionnaire classifying learners on four bipolar dimensions. As the article defines it, "The ILS examines students' learning styles in four dimensions including information processing (active-reflective), information perception (sensory-intuitive), information reception (verbal-visual), and information understanding (sequential-global)." Each student is placed in one pole per dimension but "has the ability to learn in the other group as well"; scores of 9-11 indicate strong preference, where unsupported preference "might experience learning difficulties". The study used a validated Persian version to predict satisfaction and performance in flipped versus face-to-face wire-bending instruction. (Baherimoghadam Tahereh et al. (2025))
- **Index of Learning Styles four-dimension learning style model**: The Index of Learning Styles (ILS) is an online questionnaire assessing preferences on four dimensions of a learning style model. As printed in the article, "The ILS consists of four scales: sensing-intuitive, visual-verbal, active-reflective, and sequential-global." It contains 44 items answered in a bipolar fashion, with each dimension scored to a maximum of 11; scores equal to or below 3 indicate a balanced style and larger scores a preference for one dimension. The study used it to check whether the course fulfilled students' needs and to test correlations with exam results. (Nkenke Emeka et al. (2012))
