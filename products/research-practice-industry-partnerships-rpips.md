# Research-Practice-Industry Partnerships (RPIPs) integrating problems of practice, learning sciences, and edtech development

## Description
RPIPs, emerging from research at the Creativity Labs at UCI, build on participatory design and RPP foundations and bring industry into a partnership built around feedback loops. The report states that "Research-Practice-Industry Partnerships (RPIPs) bring together authentic problems of practice and the learning sciences together with the edtech development process." Each party offers a unique value: edtech developers scale products but typically lack practitioner feedback or learning-science foundations; researchers know methods for analyzing user feedback but few designs scale; practitioners have first-hand knowledge but lack time or avenues to share it. The model ensures evidence of efficacy can immediately inform development so products are more likely to be used, used well, and have positive impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Edtech organizations and researchers must approach and engage educators with real curiosity, interest, and respect for the others' participation
#### Constraints
- The report cautions that such partnerships should be built and implemented thoughtfully; DLPs fit the role of industry in RPIPs

### Target Learners
- K-12 practitioners, edtech developers, and learning sciences researchers

### Target Learning Objectives
- Ensuring edtech products are used, used well, and have positive impact on learning

# Research-Practice-Industry Partnership (RPIP) model with four pillars for involving educators in edtech development

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The RPIP model extends the Research Practice Partnership framework by bringing in industry voices, offering researchers and educators an opportunity to apply their experiences to a real world product. "An RPIP consists of four distinct pillars: 1) professional learning for educators...; 2) collaborative research design; 3) feedback loops between educators, researchers, and developers; and 4) the resulting improved product and practice improvements." A neutral third party facilitates the relationship and maintains a balanced power dynamic between educators and developers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional learning sessions that connect educators' practice and pedagogy to the tool
- A neutral third-party facilitator to keep power dynamics balanced between educators and developers
#### Constraints
- Edtech development has traditionally occurred independently from classroom practice, with educator feedback limited to user surveys and stand-alone focus groups

### Target Learners
- K-12 mathematics students, reached indirectly through their teachers' participation

### Target Learning Objectives
- Improved edtech products that support student algebra learning
- Educator capacity to engage in design and research processes
