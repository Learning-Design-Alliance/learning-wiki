## Description
PHelpS is a peer-help support system developed by Vassileva et al. that uses inspectable task-based user models to match workplace employees seeking help with available, willing, and capable peer helpers.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **PHelpS peer help system matching ready, willing and able helpers via inspectable user models**: PHelpS helped Correctional Service Canada workers find peer helpers when stuck while using the OMS database. It relied on two minimalist AI methodologies: a knowledge representation scheme capturing authentic tasks at many levels of detail, and user models overlaying task hierarchies recording which tasks each worker had carried out and how well. The system recommended peers who were "ready (were online at the time of the help request), willing (were logged into PHelpS and had indicated availability to help), and able (who knew how to accomplish that step and had proved able to help others in the past)". Help seekers and helpers could inspect user models and choose or refuse help, an early use of open learner modelling. (Vassileva et al. (2015))
