## Description
A 33-item Likert-style instrument developed by Andrew Mason and colleagues to measure students' attitudes and approaches to physics problem solving, including diagram use, reflection, persistence, and representational preferences.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Expert-like attitudes and approaches to physics problem solving as a measurable construct with grade-level, school-type and gender dimensions**: The AAPS survey operationalizes students' attitudes and approaches to physics problem solving as a 33-item Likert inventory scored so that agreement with expert-normed statements counts as favorable. The article treats expert-likeness as multidimensional, spanning views about the role of equations and formulas, persistence on difficult problems, self-checking of solutions, use of diagrams and scratch work, and metacognition. Responses are normalized to +1, 0, -1 per item so group averages can be compared across demographics, and factor analysis reveals natural item clusters such as metacognition and novice-like approaches. The framework assumes "favorable" responses are defined "based upon the responses of experts similar to the earlier surveys". (Nuri Balta et al. (2016))
- **Attitudes and Approaches to Problem Solving (AAPS) survey**: The AAPS survey is a 33-item Likert-scale instrument (1 = strongly agree to 5 = strongly disagree) measuring attitudes and approaches to introductory physics problem solving. It was built by selecting 16 questions from the APSS survey and developing 17 additional questions focused on approaches such as use of diagrams and scratch work, gut feeling versus physics principles, reflection after solving, giving up after 10 minutes, preference for numerical versus symbolic problems, and enjoyment of challenging problems. It was validated through student and faculty interviews, think-aloud consistency checks, Cronbach's alpha, and cross-group comparisons, and favorable (expert-like) responses are published with the survey in the article's appendix. (Andrew J. Mason et al. (2016))
