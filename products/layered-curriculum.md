## Description
A three-layer instructional differentiation framework developed by Kathie Nunley that organizes basic knowledge, application, and critical-thinking activities while allowing students choice and requiring demonstrated progression.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Layered Curriculum as a three-layer C-B-A differentiation framework**: Layered Curriculum, developed by Nunley, divides a unit into three layers: "C Layer: Basic knowledge", "B Layer: Application" and "A Layer: Critical thinking". Students choose activities within each layer according to their own learning style, must reach a points threshold (65-70 points in layer C) to progress, and give an oral defense explaining where they had difficulty. In this study the framework was implemented in a five-week Human Body Systems unit with task lists handed to experimental students in advance. (Koc Akran et al. (2019))
- **Nunley's layered curriculum: a three-layer (C-B-A) differentiated curriculum model**: The layered curriculum, developed by K. F. Nunley, organizes teaching into three layers. Layer C is "the lowest layer in which all the students are required to be successful, and it includes basic knowledge and abilities", with many point-valued activities of which students complete 65-70 points' worth. Layer B contains application activities worth 15 points each, and Layer A, the highest layer, requires critical analysis, questioning and an original learning product. Students choose activities, keep them in a progress file, and are graded by criteria matched to layer difficulty. (Koc Akran et al. (2018))
