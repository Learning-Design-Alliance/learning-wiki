## Description
Andes2 is an intelligent tutoring system for supporting students solving introductory physics homework problems, including algebraic equation solving and validity checking.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Algebra subsystem for Andes2 physics tutoring system with four capabilities**: The article describes a subsystem that resolves algebraic questions for Andes2, an intelligent tutoring system for introductory physics homework problems. As printed in the abstract, it is "capable of 1) solving the systems of equations at the level of introductory physics problems, 2) checking the validity of equations the students enter". The author notes the capabilities "would be useful for any ITS which deals with problems involving complex systems of equations". (Joel A. Shapiro (2005))
- **Andes physics intelligent tutoring system with flag feedback and graded on-demand hints**: Andes is an intelligent tutoring system for physics used at the U.S. Naval Academy since 1999, covering nearly all topics of a two-semester introductory physics course except thermodynamics and modern physics. Its coached problem solving offers "instructional assistance at multiple levels of specificity": flag feedback turns correct entries green and incorrect entries red, and on-demand hints proceed through successive levels to a bottom-out hint that explicitly tells the student what to do. In this study Andes presented video-based examples and coached problem solving for four electrodynamics problems. (Robert G.M. Hausmann et al. (2008))
- **Andes Physics Tutor: an intelligent homework helper that grades entries red or green and gives principle-based hints on request**: Andes is an intelligent homework helper for physics that "replaces the pencil and paper that students would ordinarily use to solve physics homework problems." Students draw diagrams, enter equations, and define variables freely; Andes marks entries correct or incorrect by turning them red or green and gives principle-based hints when asked. It covers major introductory physics chapters except thermodynamics and modern physics, runs only under Windows, and is intended for college, high school, or distance-learning courses. (D. Sánchez-Guzmán et al. (2009))
