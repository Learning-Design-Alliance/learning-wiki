## Description
A longitudinal medical-education assessment developed at Maastricht University that repeatedly samples broad functional knowledge across academic years to measure student achievement and progression.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The progress test as a longitudinal indicator of functional knowledge in medical education**: The progress test (PT) is presented as a longitudinal assessment method that, unlike the cGPA, "assesses student achievement and progression". First introduced at Maastricht in the early 1970s to assess a problem-based learning curriculum, it "focuses on functional knowledge (knowledge that comes with repetition), provides valuable information that can be used for feedback and has research potential". The article uses it as a performance indicator against which pre-admission variables' predictive validity was tested. (Dabaliz Al-Awwab et al. (2017))
- **Dutch Interuniversity Progress Test item pool used as the experimental instrument**: The study used items from the Dutch progress test, which "consists of 200 multiple-choice questions" administered four times a year to roughly 10,000 students, covering the whole medical domain at end level. The researchers selected 250 questions from seven tests administered between 2005 and 2007, reduced them to 200 items with p-value > .25, and created two equal 100-item tests balanced on mean p-values, "I don't know" choices and distractor counts. (Cecilio-Fernandes D et al. (2017))
