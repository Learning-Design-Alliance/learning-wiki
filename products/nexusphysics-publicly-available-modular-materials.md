# NEXUS/Physics publicly available modular materials

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A modular package of WikiBook content, homework and recitation problems, clicker discussion problems, and laboratories developed by the NEXUS/Physics project for interdisciplinary physics courses serving life-science majors.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **NEXUS/Physics publicly available modular materials (WikiBook, homework and recitation problems, clicker problems, laboratories)**: The project released "a significant body of modular materials" including a WikiBook, homework and group-work recitation problems emphasizing biological content and sense-making with no plug-and-chug problems, collections of clicker problems for class discussion, and a set of laboratories. "All of these materials are presently available at the NEXUS/Physics website, http://nexusphysics.umd.edu." The authors intend the materials to be used as modular pieces or supplements and to migrate to an open IPLS distribution environment. (E. F. Redish et al. (2014))
- **NEXUS/Physics interdisciplinary introductory physics course for life sciences**: NEXUS/Physics is an introductory physics class developed for biology and life-sciences (pre-health-care) students, with calculus, biology, and chemistry as prerequisites so physics can have authentic value for biology students on topics like chemical bonding, diffusion, and entropy. Its goals are to "create prototype open-source instructional materials that can be shared", focus on interdisciplinary coordination of biology, chemistry, physics, and math instruction, and "emphasize competency-based instruction, building general scientific skills". (Edward F. Redish (2015))
- **NEXUS/Physics instructional materials and Living Physics Portal resources for math-in-physics teaching**: NEXUS/Physics is an introductory physics course designed specifically for life science majors, in which many of the article's ideas were developed. Its free web-based wiki text displays a tool icon every time an e-game is used, and materials for delivering instruction on the use of math in physics are available on the Living Physics Portal by searching "Using math in physics." (Edward F. Redish (2020))
- **NEXUS/Physics wiki and Living Physics Portal collections of anchor-equation readings and activities**: The article points to released instructional resources: the NEXUS/Physics wiki on ComPADRE (https://www.compadre.org/nexusph/), which hosts the 'Reading the content in…' pages and an Anchor Equations page, supplementary EPAPS materials with problems and activities, and a collection on the Living Physics Portal searchable as 'Making Meaning with Mathematics: Anchor equations'. These were developed in the context of a class for life-science majors. (Redish (2020))
- **NEXUS/Physics graph problem collection and supporting tools (PhET, Desmos, ComPADRE, Living Physics Portal)**: The article points instructors to released collections and tools for blend-building graph problems: the NEXUS/Physics problem collection in ComPADRE and The Living Physics Portal (which holds instructor solutions), the PhET simulations (e.g., Moving Man, an excellent way to generate interpretation problems), and the Desmos graphing calculator for exploring parametric dependence. The author states you can find many more blend-building graph problems in the NEXUS/Physics problem collection and build your own using these online resources. (Edward F. Redish (2023))
