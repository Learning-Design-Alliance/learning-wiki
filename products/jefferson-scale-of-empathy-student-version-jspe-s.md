# Jefferson Scale of Empathy – Student Version (JSPE-S)

## Description
A 20-item, 7-point Likert self-report instrument for measuring medical students' empathic orientation toward patients, developed by the Jefferson medical education research group.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Jefferson Scale of Empathy – Student Version (JSPE-S)**: The JSPE-S is the self-report instrument used by most included studies; the review describes it as measuring affective empathy and studying "the empathic relationship between the medical student and the patient". It consists of 20 items on a 7-point Likert scale, with a minimum of 20 and a maximum of 140 points, where a high score indicates a more empathic orientation. All cross-sectional studies except four, and all longitudinal studies except one, employed the JSPE-S. The review notes the scale focuses on thoughts and not actions, citing Sulzer. (Andersen Freja Allerelli et al. (2020))
- **Two-component conceptual structure of empathy: perspective taking (cognitive) versus compassionate care (emotive)**: The article adopts and tests a view of empathy distinguishing a perspective taking or cognitive component from a compassionate care or emotive component, measured via the Japanese student version of the Jefferson Scale of Empathy with 20 items rated on a seven-point Likert scale. Prior factor analysis "revealed three factors: perspective taking (10 items), compassionate care (8 items) and standing in the patient's shoes (2 items)". The study's confirmatory factor analyses supported this structure at both occasions with good fit, and used it to examine how each component relates to willingness to show empathic behaviour. (Son Daisuke et al. (2018))
