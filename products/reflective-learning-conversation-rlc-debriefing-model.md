# Reflective Learning Conversation (RLC) debriefing model

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A co-designed, progressive, multimodal post-simulation debriefing framework developed by Almomani Emad and an 18-member interprofessional working group for clinical educators working with diverse healthcare learners.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The learning conversation as a learner-centered simulation debriefing technique**: The learning conversation is the debriefing technique used in this trial: a learner-centered dialogue that stimulates reflection by letting learners identify and explore issues of particular interest, with the instructor using advocacy-with-inquiry to add learning points the learner has not raised. The article describes it as "a learner-centered dialogue that stimulates reflection by allowing the learner to identify and explore issues of particular interest." It was delivered by one of two experienced instructors in both timing conditions. (Schober Patrick et al. (2019))

### Claims
- [An RLC debriefing model produces significantly higher observed clinical reasoning, clinical judgment, and critical thinking than Norris and Bullock debriefing in interprofessional ALS simulation, from the second observation onward](../claims/rlc-debriefing-higher-reasoning-judgment-critical-thinking.md) [+M]
- [Post-course General Self-Efficacy is significantly higher after RLC debriefing than after Norris and Bullock debriefing, while pre-course self-efficacy did not differ](../claims/rlc-debriefing-gse-self-efficacy-gains.md) [+M]
