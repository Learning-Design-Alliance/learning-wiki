# COSTI (Classroom Observations of Student-Teacher Interactions)

## Description
COSTI is a low-inference, frequency-based observation instrument developed by Christian Doabler and colleagues to measure instructional behaviors and student–teacher interactions in small-group interventions.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **COSTI observation instrument for instructional intensity**: The Classroom Observations of Student-Teacher Interactions (COSTI) is "a low-inference, frequency-based observation instrument" used to measure instructional intensity in ROOTS groups. It targets seven instructional behaviors: teacher demonstrations, guided practice for groups and individuals, independent practice for groups and individuals, student mistakes, and teacher-provided academic feedback. Trained observers conducted all observations, with each ROOTS group observed three times across the 10 intervention weeks. (Christian Doabler et al. (2014))
- **COSTI-M classroom observation instrument for evidence-based teaching practices**: The Classroom Observations of Student–Teacher Interactions—Mathematics (COSTI-M) is "a validated, low-inference observation tool designed to document the frequency of evidence-based teaching practices." It targets four practices — teacher models, academic feedback, group responses, and individual responses — captured in real time and coded serially, with rates per minute computed as frequency divided by observation duration. It has evidence of predictive validity with a standardized mathematics achievement measure (p = .004, pseudo R2 = .08) and a battery of curriculum-based measures (p = .017, pseudo R2 = .05). (Doabler et al. (2018))
