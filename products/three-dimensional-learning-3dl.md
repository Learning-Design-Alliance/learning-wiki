## Description
A science-education framework originating in the National Academies' Framework for K–12 Science Education that organizes learning objectives, instruction, and assessment around the integration of Scientific and Engineering Practices, Disciplinary Core Ideas, and Crosscutting Concepts.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Three-dimensional learning (3DL) as the framework for university-level faculty discussions**: The article adopts three dimensional learning, at the core of the Next Generation Science Standards, as the framework for faculty discussions, bringing it to the university level. The three dimensions are scientific practices ("Knowledge of how science achieves its ends"), crosscutting concepts ("Concepts that bridge disciplinary boundaries"), and disciplinary core ideas ("Concepts that are essential to the study of a discipline"). A key point is that no dimension should be taught in isolation; all three should be blended into lectures, homework, in-class activities, and exams. (James T. Laverty et al. (2014))
- **Three-dimensional learning: scientific practices, crosscutting concepts, and disciplinary core ideas**: Three-dimensional learning is the organizing framework from the National Research Council's A Framework for K-12 Science Education, defining "three dimensions of science and engineering learning on which to base a curriculum." Scientific practices are "the disaggregated components of the process of science" that put knowledge to use; crosscutting concepts are "ways of thinking" that bridge physical, biological, and geological sciences; disciplinary core ideas are foundational concepts essential to a discipline. The article argues the framework "can and should be applied to introductory college science courses as well," because assessments drive the hidden curriculum and incoming students will increasingly have experienced this teaching. (James T. Laverty et al. (2015))
