# North Lawndale Cluster Initiative (NLCI): a vertically-integrated neighborhood school cluster strategy

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (3 for, 3 mixed, 3 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The NLCI was a foundation-funded initiative run by AUSL from 2013-14 through 2018-19 in four Chicago elementary schools and one high school. It aimed to advance "a cohesive, high-quality school and student support system by coordinating and aligning resources, programs, and partnerships to support students from pre-k through high school graduation." Its largest funding portion converted half-day pre-k to full-day pre-k; other supports included teacher professional development, City Year mentoring, social-emotional programs, a middle-to-high-school transition program, and college counseling partnerships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Funding for full-day pre-k classes and supplementary supports across all grade levels
- A managing organization (AUSL) to coordinate and align resources, programs, and partnerships across the cluster
#### Constraints
- The evaluation assessed the impact of funding a vertical integration cluster strategy in one neighborhood and should not be taken as an assessment of the effectiveness of the schools overall or of individual programs
- High student mobility and year-to-year variability in enrollment limited the strategy's ability to build over time

### Target Learners
- Pre-k through high school students in a low-income Chicago neighborhood, predominantly Black students from high-poverty census block groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved attendance, academic achievement, school climate, high school transition success, and college enrollment and persistence
