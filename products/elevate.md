# Elevate

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Elevate is a software-enabled continuous-improvement platform developed by PERTS and UCCSR that helps educators measure, disaggregate, track, and improve learning conditions over time.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Elevate continuous improvement platform**: Elevate is a software-enabled continuous improvement platform developed by PERTS and UCCSR. It “equips educators to use research-quality measures and analytics to assess, disaggregate, and track learning conditions over time,” letting educators test the impact of practices on conditions and work in teams to identify high-impact practices. Over the 2021–22 school year, more than 900 teachers at more than 150 schools used it with middle and high school students. (Gripshover et al. (2022))
- **Elevate survey for classroom-level student experience data**: Elevate is a student experience survey co-developed by the Consortium team and PERTS, introduced beginning in 2020 to a selection of CPS schools and classrooms, typically partnered with a school support organization with expertise in data use. It asks students about the same classroom learning conditions found on Cultivate but is administered by individual teachers to their own students, with classroom-level data shared with the teacher whose classroom is surveyed, who may then provide access to others. This contrasts with Cultivate's schoolwide model, making Elevate suited to teacher-level inquiry into learning conditions. (Faye Kroshinsky et al. (2026))
- **Elevate learning conditions framework (Teacher Caring, Meaningful Work, Feedback for Growth)**: The Elevate framework organizes classroom experience into measurable learning conditions that predict academic outcomes. The report examines “three learning conditions—Teacher Caring, Meaningful Work, and Feedback for Growth,” rating students' agreement on Likert scales and compositing the scores. It posits that positive conditions support learning by enhancing motivation and by mitigating anxieties that divide attention and reduce the brain's capacity to process information, and that conditions function as early, actionable indicators of learning. (Gripshover et al. (2022))
