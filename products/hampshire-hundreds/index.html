Hampshire Hundreds¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Hampshire Hundreds was a local-authority-led teacher-support programme for Years 5 and 6 that combined cluster meetings, pupil-vulnerability identification, lesson observation and co-planning, and research-informed feedback and questioning materials.

Components¶

  • Hampshire Hundreds intervention: three-stage teacher support programme with a pupil vulnerability index and feedback/questioning research materials: Hampshire Hundreds was a local-authority-led intervention for Years 5 and 6 in which lead teachers attended cluster meetings, observed disadvantaged pupils using a characteristics resource, and were given research material on feedback and questioning (e.g. Hattie, 2009; 2012), being "encouraged to consider how they could adapt Hattie's model of feedback and apply it in their lessons". Pupils were ranked by a teacher-completed vulnerability score of 0-20 summing barriers to learning such as FSM eligibility, EAL, or lacking self-esteem. Schools designed their own responses, so implementation varied substantially. (Sandra McNally et al. (2014))

Claims¶

  • The Hampshire Hundreds intervention showed no statistically significant impact on the attainment of disadvantaged pupils in reading and maths (effect size 0.03). [+M]
  • The intervention showed no statistically significant impact on other (non-disadvantaged) pupils in the same classes (effect size -0.02). [+M]
  • An additional analysis using Key Stage 2 administrative test scores also found indeterminate, close-to-zero effects for Year 6 pupils. [-W]
  • Subgroup analyses by gender, ethnicity, free school meal status and baseline ability all showed point estimates near zero with wide confidence intervals. [-W]
