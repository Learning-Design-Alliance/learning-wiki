# Writing to Read

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Writing to Read is a computer-supported beginning-literacy programme developed by IBM that progresses from sound-and-letter production to writing words, sentences, and stories, with supplementary practice activities and progress reporting.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Writing-to-read lesson sequence with speed-practice and simulated e-mail activities**: The program's instructional pattern moves from sound-level to text-level production: lessons "begin by having students say and write individual sounds and the letters that represent those sounds, and then progress to having students write words, sentences, and stories." Two supplementary activities extend the pattern: the Power Fountain activity for practicing "speed and accuracy in writing and typing" after attaining a certain level, and the E-mail Tower, "a simulated e-mail program," for practicing newly acquired skills by writing short messages. Teachers or parents can print individual or group progress reports. (What Works Clearinghouse (2007))

## Description
The program's instructional pattern moves from sound-level to text-level production: lessons "begin by having students say and write individual sounds and the letters that represent those sounds, and then progress to having students write words, sentences, and stories." Two supplementary activities extend the pattern: the Power Fountain activity for practicing "speed and accuracy in writing and typing" after attaining a certain level, and the E-mail Tower, "a simulated e-mail program," for practicing newly acquired skills by writing short messages. ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The software plus corresponding activity book lessons; a teacher or parent able to print progress reports; computer access at school or home
#### Constraints
- Speed and accuracy practice via the Power Fountain activity is available only after students attain a certain level; the E-mail Tower is described as optional

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers aged six to nine
- Struggling readers and writers

### Learning Goals
- Phoneme-letter association
- Sounding out, typing, and writing words, sentences, and stories
