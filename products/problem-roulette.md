# Problem Roulette

## Description
A University of Michigan web-based study service that gives authenticated learners random within-topic access to locally authored past exam problems and records responses while providing correctness and peer-performance feedback.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Problem Roulette, a free not-for-credit online practice-question platform for introductory physics exams**: Problem Roulette is a "free, optional, not-for-credit, test question practice software" offered to students in introductory physics and other introductory STEM courses. Students select a topic or exam period and are randomly served practice questions drawn from prior exams, with unlimited attempts and correctness feedback after each response. This study drew on four years of Problem Roulette data from Physics II, covering 1,685 students and 951 questions. (Nicholas T. Young et al. (2023))

### Claims
- [After adjusting for selection bias with beginning-of-term GPA, regular PR users show a grade point improvement of 0.22, significant above the random noise level of 0.04, with replication at 0.32 in Winter 2013.](../claims/gpa-adjusted-022-grade-boost-regular-pr-users.md) [+M]
- [Regular users of Problem Roulette in the Phys 240 electromagnetism course outperformed their complement by an average of 0.40 grade points (on a 4.0 scale), before adjusting for selection bias.](../claims/regular-pr-users-outperform-by-040-grade-points.md) [+M]
- [PR usage spiked immediately before scheduled exams, with activity peaking at 800 problems served per hour on exam day, and no formal course credit was attached to use.](../claims/pr-usage-spikes-before-exams.md) [+M]
- [Mean final course grade rises quadratically with the logarithm of term-aggregated practice-study volume, with an overall gain of 0.77±0.12 grade points between 1 and 1000 questions](../claims/log-quadratic-grade-gain-practice-volume.md) [+M]
- [Students perform about 8 percentage points worse on complex multiple-choice questions than on non-CMC questions in introductory physics](../claims/cmc-questions-8-points-harder-than-non-cmc.md) [+M]
