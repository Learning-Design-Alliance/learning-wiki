## Description
OpenSciEd is a freely available, Creative Commons–licensed middle-school science curriculum developed to address the Next Generation Science Standards.

## Components

- **OpenSciEd open science curriculum materials**: OpenSciEd is a middle school science curriculum described by the article as "a set of Creative Commons licensed, freely available curriculum materials addressing the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)". The materials are adaptable, extensive and detailed, and certified through a peer-review process for NGSS design. The paper treats OpenSciEd both as an intervention and, because of its openness, as an ecosystem supporting research and innovation. (A Field-Driven, Equity-Centered Research Agenda for OpenSciEd: Updated Version)
- **OpenSciEd teacher supports: unit-specific PD themes and professional learning activities**: OpenSciEd teacher supports are "a comprehensive system of materials and structured professional learning experiences that enable teachers to implement the OpenSciEd vision successfully." Unit-specific materials foreground four themes: content understanding, unit storyline, goals for specific lessons and activities, and logistical and materials strategies. Professional learning takes the form of four-day (or more) workshops in which teachers analyze video of problems of practice, focus on navigation routines, immerse themselves in content from the student perspective by switching hats, and collaborate and reflect with peers. (An Initial Logic Model to Guide OpenSciEd Research: Updated Version)
- **OpenSciEd research logic model with five components**: The white paper organizes OpenSciEd research using a logic model with a five-part structure. As the report states, "This logic model has five main components: design (distinctive principles and affordances), classroom (enactment), teacher supports, system-level (implementation), and outcomes." These are desired outcomes reflecting what designers hope to achieve rather than what outcome research has gathered evidence about to date. Collectively they are said to promote equitable participation by giving students agency and explicitly valuing their ideas, experiences, and backgrounds. Figure 2 summarizes six distinctive classroom features: the teacher as facilitator who choreographs learning experiences without prescriptive scripts; instruction driven by student questions supported by driving question boards; lesson-to-lesson enactment with "whole class navigation discussions to maintain coherence from the students' perspective"; practice-based activities engaging science and engineering practices; problematization moments that prompt students to identify gaps in understanding; and assessment opportunities such as consensus models and performance tasks applying concepts to new contexts.

# OpenSciEd's five distinctive instructional principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates the distinctive principles that define students' intended classroom experience with OpenSciEd: "these principles include (1) being coherent to students, (2) being phenomena-driven, (3) promoting evidence-based revision, (4) promoting consensus-building, and (5) embodying the vision of the Framework". Collectively they are said to promote equitable participation by giving students agency and explicitly valuing their ideas, experiences, and backgrounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional materials must be designed to preserve all of these principles during customization and adaptation
#### Constraints
- The article states customizations could compromise the integrity of the materials to the OpenSciEd pedagogical model
