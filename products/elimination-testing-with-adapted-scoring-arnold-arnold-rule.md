# Elimination testing with adapted scoring (Arnold & Arnold rule)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A multiple-choice assessment format in which students identify both possible and impossible alternatives and receive partial-credit scoring for eliminated distractors with a configurable penalty for eliminating the correct answer.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Elimination testing with adapted scoring (Arnold & Arnold rule) as an assessment design that rewards partial knowledge while penalising misconception at a settable penalty**: Elimination testing lets students mark each alternative as "could be" or "cannot be", allowing partial knowledge to be expressed by eliminating fewer than n−1 distractors. The Arnold & Arnold adapted scoring rule rewards partial knowledge with a mark depending on distractors eliminated, x, via score(x)=x(n−x)(n−1), while penalising any elimination of the correct answer at a penalty the examiner sets — here matched to negative marking's −1/(n−1), considered "fair" as it "results in an expected gain of zero when guessing". Honest reporting of partial knowledge yields exactly the expected score of guessing, "the need for guessing diminishes". The article positions this as an alternative to negative marking that discourages guessing without disadvantaging risk-averse students. (Vanderoost J et al. (2018))
