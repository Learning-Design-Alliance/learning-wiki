# Source-Specific Stress Mindsets Scales with four hypothetical academic stressor situations

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A researcher-developed multi-factor self-report scale by Jenkins et al. for measuring college undergraduates’ beliefs that stress is enhancing versus debilitating across academic stressor situations varying in duration and controllability.

### Claims

- [Controllability and duration interact: acute stress is seen as more debilitating than chronic stress only when the stressor is uncontrollable](../claims/mindset-duration-by-controllability-interaction.md) [+W]
- [Stress-is-debilitating mindsets relate to higher perceived distress and poorer health, with the chronic controllable mindset showing the only significant mindset main effects](../claims/debilitating-mindsets-linked-to-poorer-health.md) [+W]
- [Undergraduates rate stress from uncontrollable stressors as significantly more debilitating than stress from controllable stressors, with a large effect](../claims/stress-mindset-varies-by-stressor-controllability.md) [+W]

- [Stress mindsets moderate the link between perceived distress and health, with enhancing mindsets buffering but not eliminating the negative effect](../claims/stress-mindsets-moderate-stress-health-link.md) [+W]
