# Afterglow Access

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Afterglow Access is a University of North Carolina/Skynet web application that lets introductory astronomy students, including blind and visually impaired learners, explore astronomical images and perform measurements and photometric analyses through a browser.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Afterglow Access web application for student image exploration and measurement**: Afterglow Access is a web application, written in AngularJS, that lets students explore professional-quality images and make fundamental measurements from them without installing software. It is connected to Skynet's 100 TB RAID, so students need not download, store, and re-upload images, and heavy computation is handled server-side. Capabilities include brightness/contrast rescaling, angular distance measurement, image alignment and arithmetic, photometry of multiple objects across multiple images, and plotting light curves, periodograms, and period-folded light curves. (Daniel E. Reichart (2021))
- **Afterglow Access (AgA): web-based image-analysis application expanded for calibrated photometry, color imaging, radio images, and spectra**: AgA (afterglow.skynet.unc.edu) is the project's online image processing and analysis application, described as "a long-desired technology in astronomy education". Originally used to analyze monochromatic optical images, it is being expanded to handle color optical images incorporating astrophotography, radio images using new algorithms that automatically remove manmade radio interference, and spectra from optical or radio telescopes. During the first grant semester, programmers added calibrated photometry of thousands of stars per image for many images simultaneously, professional-level photometry and source-extraction algorithms, and color combination capabilities. (Daniel E. Reichart (2021))
- **Afterglow Access (AgA) web-based image processing application with calibrated photometry and new astrophotography capabilities**: Afterglow Access (AgA) is the project's "student-level, web-based, image processing and analysis application", originally developed with NSF funding. In Year 1 it was modified so students can carry out "calibrated photometry, of thousands o f stars per image, f or many images simultaneously", tied to modern catalogs, and professional-level photometry and source-extraction algorithms were added. Astrophotography capabilities are being added to support Modules 1-3 and 5-7, so students can transition to publishable research using the same application. (Daniel E. Reichart et al. (2022))
