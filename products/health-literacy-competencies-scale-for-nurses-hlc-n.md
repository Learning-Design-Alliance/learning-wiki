# Health Literacy Competencies Scale for Nurses (HLC-N)

## Description
The HLC-N is a 39-item, five-dimension Likert-scale assessment developed by Bao Xuemei et al. to measure health-literacy competencies among in-service registered nurses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Korean version of the Health Literacy Competencies instrument: a 43-item, four-domain self-report tool for healthcare professionals**: The Korean HLCs is a translated, expert-refined self-report instrument measuring healthcare professionals' health literacy competencies across four domains. The article reports that "The final instrument comprised 43 items across four domains: knowledge (11 items), attitudes (8 items), skills (12 items), and practices (12 items)." It was derived from Coleman's 94-item English HLCs via WHO forward- and back-translation and a three-round Delphi process, then psychometrically tested in a 359-professional web survey. (Kang Soo Jin et al. (2026))
- **Health literacy competencies (HLCs) four-domain framework: knowledge, skills, attitudes, and practice**: The HLCs framework, first established by Coleman and colleagues in the US, conceptualizes the health literacy competencies healthcare professionals need as four domains. The article states: "HLCs comprise four domains: knowledge, skills, attitudes, and practice." The instrument items cover knowledge of health literacy and risk factors, attitudes toward communicating with patients with limited literacy, communication skills such as plain language and teach-back, and practices in patient education. The study validated this structure empirically in Korea. (Kang Soo Jin et al. (2026))
