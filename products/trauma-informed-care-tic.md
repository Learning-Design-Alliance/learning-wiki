## Description
A SAMHSA-grounded framework for organizing trauma-aware care through realizing and recognizing trauma, responding appropriately, and resisting retraumatization, used by healthcare and other service organizations.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Trauma-Informed Care (TIC) as a framework grounded in SAMHSA's six principles**: Trauma-Informed Care (TIC) is a strength-based approach to care grounded in SAMHSA's six principles that centers on "realizing, recognizing, responding to trauma, and resisting re-traumatization". In prenatal settings, TIC has the potential to strengthen patient trust, engagement, satisfaction, and emotional safety, improve clinician responses to trauma disclosure, and support timely referrals to interdisciplinary resources. The framework also enhances workplace psychological safety and reduces compassion fatigue and burnout among clinicians. (Itani Mohammad S. et al. (2026))
- **Trauma-informed care as a framework defined by six principles guiding patient, provider, and system interactions**: The article presents trauma-informed care (TIC) as "a well-established clinical and organizational framework that guides interactions among patients, providers, and health systems". It acknowledges the potential for re-traumatization during care and incorporates strategies to enhance patient comfort, trust, and cooperation. TIC is defined by six principles: safety; trustworthiness and transparency; peer support; collaboration and mutuality; empowerment, voice, and choice; and consideration of cultural, historical, and gender issues. (Brown Taylor et al. (2026))
