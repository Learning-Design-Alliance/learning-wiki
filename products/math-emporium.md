## Description
A computer-assisted college mathematics programme originating at Virginia Tech that replaces traditional lecture with self-paced interactive software practice, mastery-oriented module progression, and on-demand instructor or teaching-assistant support.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Math emporium instructional method for college algebra**: The math emporium is a nontraditional, interactive instructional method for learning math, first implemented at Virginia Tech in 1997 and since adopted at many colleges and universities. In the emporium classroom students spend time learning content and working problems using interactive software, getting immediate feedback by soliciting help from instructors and teaching assistants rather than watching an instructor work problems on the board. In this study it was delivered via Pearson's MyLabsPlus across six modules, each containing a pretest, instructional videos, workbook exercises, homework, a quiz, a practice test, and a module test, with staffing at a minimum ratio of one instructor or GTA for every twelve students. (Cousins-Cooper et al. (2017))
- **The mathematics emporium model: computer-lab delivery with software instruction and just-in-time human support**: The emporium model delivers basic college mathematics courses in large computer labs where "the majority of instruction is provided through interaction with a computer program." The software provides "examples, explanations, videos, opportunities to practice, and varying degrees of feedback on incorrect solutions," supplemented by trained instructional assistants offering one-on-one "just in time" help. Twigg's stated success characteristics include students doing problems rather than watching lectures, spending more time on what they do not understand, receiving timely assistance, and mastering requisite skills before advancing. (Krupa et al. (2013))
