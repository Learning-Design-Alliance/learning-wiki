# First Step to Success

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 17 claims (16 for, 1 against) · 8 studies (7 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 5 of 8 report an effect size · 16 claims rest on one study

## Description
First Step to Success is a behavior-coaching programme developed for children at risk of aggressive or antisocial behavior, combining screening, classroom intervention, and HomeBase parent training.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **First Step to Success early intervention program (screening, CLASS classroom intervention, HomeBase parent training)**: First Step to Success is "an early intervention program designed to help children who are at risk for developing aggressive or antisocial behavioral patterns." A trained behavior coach works with the student, class peers, teacher, and parents for approximately 50 to 60 hours over three months. It comprises three interconnected modules — screening, classroom intervention (CLASS), and parent training (HomeBase) — in which students earn points toward behavioral goals, peers reinforce appropriate behavior, and parents are coached weekly in six competencies such as communication, cooperation, and limit setting. Implementation costs approximately $500 per student. (First Step to Success: WWC Intervention Report (2012))
- **First Step Next core component pattern: direct instruction, contingencies, peer and home support, rewards, and dense positive feedback**: The FSN revision preserved the core elements credited with the original program's efficacy: "direct instruction in school success skills, group and individual contingencies, peer and home support, school and home reward activities, and a dense schedule of positive feedback and descriptive praise." These components operate across classroom and home settings and were retained while streamlining the program for easier implementation with integrity. (Feil et al. (2020))
- **Preschool First Step to Success: a targeted tier-two behavioral intervention combining a classroom component and a home component**: Preschool First Step to Success is a secondary-tier intervention for children 3-5 years old with externalizing behavior problems that forges a home-school partnership among teacher, parents, and a behavioral coach. The classroom component "had three phases of 10 days each: (a) the coach phase, (b) the teacher phase, and (c) maintenance", using green and red cards, points and praise, group dependent contingencies, and a home-school reward system. In the home component, "caregivers meet for six home visitation sessions with the First Step coach to learn how to teach school success skills via reading, discussion, role play, and demonstrations", covering communication and sharing, cooperation, limit setting, problem solving, friendship making, and self-confidence. (Feil et al. (2016))
- **Preschool First Step to Success (PFS) intervention**: PFS is a secondary-tier, home-school collaborative intervention for behaviorally at-risk preschoolers. It combines a 30-day classroom component with a green/red card feedback and group-dependent reward system, coach and teacher phases, and a 6-8 week homeBase parent component teaching six skills including "communication and sharing, cooperation, limits setting, problem solving, friendship making, and self-confidence." A behavioral coach consults with teachers throughout. (Feil et al. (2014))
- **Preschool First Step to Success: a manualized collaborative home-and-school tier-two intervention**: First Step to Success is "a collaborative home and school intervention to help at risk children in getting off to a good start in school", targeting children who have difficulty adjusting to routine school demands. A teacher, parent, and coach triad teaches generic school success skills such as following directions, doing one's work, and getting along with peers. The program has three modular components — screening, classroom, and home. The classroom component is a red/green feedback-card game with points awarded in 30-second intervals and a child-selected rewarding activity when the daily criterion is met, plus a daily note home; the home component consists of six coach-led home-visitation sessions covering communication, cooperation, limit setting, problem solving, friendship-making, and confidence building. The coach implements the school component for 10 days, hands it to the teacher for 10 days, then feedback and reinforcements are phased out over 10 days. (Frey et al. (2015))
- **First Step to Success Preschool Version (FSS-PSV) early intervention program**: FSS-PSV is an early intervention program for preschool children at risk of antisocial behaviors that operates across the child's home and school environments. The article describes it as having "three interrelated dynamic modules: (1) Screening module, (2) Class module, and (3) Home module," delivered through seven small booklets. In this study, counselors modeled the class module for five days using a green-red feedback card and award menu, teachers continued it for 30 school days, and parents applied home module activity cards for six weeks covering communication, collaboration, rules, problem-solving, and friendships. (Çelik et al. (2016))
