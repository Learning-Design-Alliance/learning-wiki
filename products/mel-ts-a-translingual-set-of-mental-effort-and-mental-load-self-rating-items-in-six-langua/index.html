MEL-TS: a translingual set of mental effort and mental load self-rating items in six languages¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A multilingual self-report instrument developed by Tino Endres et al. for assessing learners' perceived mental effort and mental load during self-regulated learning.

Components¶

  • MEL-TS: a translingual set of mental effort and mental load self-rating items in six languages: The MEL-TS is a set of self-rating items assessing mental effort and mental load during self-regulated learning, developed in English, Dutch, Spanish, German, Mandarin Chinese, and French. The initial pool comprised ten items — "five for mental effort and five for mental load" — inspired by established measures (Paas, 1992; Klepsch & Seufert, 2021; Krell, 2017; Leppink et al., 2013), with reversed items to mitigate response bias. The final quantitative version used four items per construct plus a 'heart and soul' intensity item and a difficulty item, with all items available on the Open Science Framework. (Tino Endres et al. (2025))

Claims¶

  • The 'heart and soul' effort item is suitable for learning contexts in Mandarin Chinese and Spanish but not in the other interviewed cultures [+W]
  • In a preregistered within-subjects experiment (N = 131, eight measurements), the translated scale showed criterion validity via sensitivity to task complexity, extraneous load, and motivation [+W]
  • The scale showed convergent and incremental validity through medium-size correlations with established cognitive load measures [+W]
  • Bilingual interviewees across six languages all recognized the effort–load distinction but emphasized different aspects of each construct [+W]
  • Invested mental effort and perceived task difficulty are strongly correlated yet relate differently to relative task difficulty, indicating the two ratings measure different aspects of overall cognitive load [+W]
  • Effort was consistently described as willful, motivation-dependent investment, whereas load was described as imposed and difficult to control [+W]
