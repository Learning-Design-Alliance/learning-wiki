# Proficiency-based progression (PBP) training methodology

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 13 claims (13 for) · 7 studies (3 causal, 2 design, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q4` · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 13 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured surgical-skills training methodology developed by its proponents that decomposes procedures into validated binary performance metrics and uses metric-based formative feedback to guide trainees toward proficiency.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Proficiency-based progression (PBP) training: metrics-based advancement only upon demonstrated benchmark performance**: Proficiency-based progression is a training model in which trainees advance through a simulation curriculum only after demonstrating predefined, quantitatively defined performance benchmarks verified by instructors. Performance is measured with a task-specific system of 'Metrics' of three types, and trainees "should advance only upon demonstration of the proficiency requirements". The article applies it to surgical skills training for cardiac device implantation, with benchmarks set from experienced implanters' scores. (Mascheroni J et al. (2024))
- **Paradigm shift from volume-based Halsted training to proficiency-based surgical training**: The article frames a transition in surgical education from the volume-based Halsted model of "see one, do one, teach one" to a proficiency-based approach in which the goal is "an objectively validated level of expertise" rather than a given number of operations. Under this account, trainees should reach a validated competence threshold regardless of repetition count, individualizing training. The survey positions Swiss training as still numbers-based and chairs as favoring the shift. (Wczysla Karolina et al. (2024))
- **ISBAR PBP e-learning training and assessment programme**: An add-on e-learning programme translating four face-to-face case scenarios and their expert-determined weighted marking of steps, errors and critical errors into Articulate Storyline 2, using "multiple input Boolean triggers" for automatic recognition of performance components. Scenarios increase in complexity from dehydration to respiratory failure with sepsis, each representing an intermediate level toward proficiency. Learners receive immediate automatic feedback, repeat scenarios until benchmarks are met, and progress to more complex cases; all four scenarios must be completed at the required proficiency level to gain a certificate. (McCarthy Nora et al. (2023))
- **Proficiency-based progression framework with steps, errors and critical errors**: A training and assessment framework in which learning proceeds through intermediate levels on a planned pathway toward expert-determined proficiency benchmarks. Performance metrics are subdivided into three categories: a 'step' is a component of a task that should be performed; an 'error' is a component that should not be performed; and a 'critical error' is a component that could jeopardise the overall outcome of the domain event if omitted. The critical error category focuses evaluation on critical mistakes so that feedback can enable improved performance, and scaffolded exposure to the skill in increasingly complex situations aids learning and mastery. (McCarthy Nora et al. (2023))
- **Proficiency-based progression (PBP): metric-based simulation training to a predefined proficiency benchmark**: Proficiency-based progression is a training paradigm in which trainees must demonstrate a predefined proficiency benchmark, established from the quantified metrics of experienced implanters, before progressing to real patients. The article describes it as 'known as proficiency-based progression (PBP', an iterative process applied throughout training and for skill development on new procedures or devices, with simulation as the delivery vehicle. It 'ensures a more homogenous skillset, can be applied to any level of training'. (Mascheroni Jorio et al. (2021))
- **Proficiency-based progression (PBP) training as an outcomes-based training model**: Proficiency-based progression training is described as "a form of outcomes-based training that involves training individuals to achieve a proficiency benchmark". The process involves 'deliberate' practice against a set of clearly defined objective metrics, with the benchmark set as the mean performance of clinicians who undertake the procedure regularly in clinical practice. In this trial the model was applied for the first time to a non-technical skill, clinical communication, by having partners score each other's phone calls against predefined metrics and repeat cases until proficiency was reached. (Breen Dorothy et al. (2019))
- **Proficiency-based progression (PBP) simulation training**: Proficiency-based progression is a simulation training framework in which validated procedure metrics, derived from experienced and proficient practitioners, are used to construct a curriculum with metric-based performance feedback. As the article puts it, "The metrics are also used to establish performance benchmarks (ie, proficiency levels), which trainees must unambiguously demonstrate before training progression." Trainees do not progress to real patients until they can perform to a quantitatively defined level benchmarked on the objective performance of experienced practitioners on the same simulated procedure. (Crossley Robert et al. (2019))
