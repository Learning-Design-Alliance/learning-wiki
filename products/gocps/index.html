GoCPS¶

Description¶

GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal-enrollment platform, operated by CPS to centralize high-school applications, program rankings, deferred-acceptance matching, offers, and waitlists.

Components¶

  • GoCPS centralized application and single-best-offer platform: GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal enrollment platform, adopted in 2017 to replace school-by-school applications with "a common application across all high school choice programs for incoming ninth grade students with a single deadline and single best offer." Students rank up to 20 of 273 choice programs (and up to six of 11 SEHS options); a computerized deferred-acceptance process assigns each student to the highest-ranked eligible program with available seats, and unassigned students go on ordered waitlists. The paper describes the platform's two application rounds, eligibility requirements, priority groups, and its four possible responses per ranked program (Offered, Higher (Lisa Barrow et al. (2018))
  • GoCPS centralized online high school application platform: GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online platform that moved all high school program applications into a single system with one deadline and a single best offer per applicant. Students use it to "research school options, complete applications, learn about their application and offer status, and ultimately accept or decline admissions offers." It hosted nearly 290 programs including 11 SEHS programs, allowed ranking up to 20 choice and 6 SEHS programs, and centrally managed waitlists — an innovation because most other large urban districts did not include charter schools in their universal enrollment systems. (Barrow et al. (2019))

Claims¶

  • Lottery-based selection under GoCPS worked as intended: lottery numbers and offers were unrelated to student characteristics and to how highly applicants ranked programs [+W]
  • Most GoCPS applicants received an offer from a program they ranked: 93 percent in round 1 (51 percent at their top choice) and 85 percent in round 2 (61 percent at their top choice) [+W]
  • In points-based GoCPS programs, students were admitted strictly in order of application score within priority groups [+W]
  • Top-ranked program choices differed by student background: low-performing students were nearly three times as likely as high-performing students to rank a charter program first, and IB programs were most popular among high-performing students [+W]
  • Choice program demand varied widely: 19 percent of programs received more than 10 applications per seat while 8 percent received fewer applications than seats, with demand higher for Arts, CTE, points-based, and highly rated programs [+W]
  • Students found the GoCPS application platform logistically easy to use, but applications to schools with additional requirements caused difficulties [+W] — (also cited [~W])
  • Ninth-grade enrollment overall and by school type and accountability rating stayed relatively stable after GoCPS implementation [+W]
  • Almost all CPS eighth-graders (92 percent) used the GoCPS platform to apply to high school in its first year [+W]
  • Students expected acceptance or rejection notifications from all schools they applied to, and the single-best-offer notification design produced confusion and frustration [-W]
  • Students overwhelmingly relied on GoCPS, CPS, and school websites to learn about potential high schools, but found websites out of date and lacking student-voice information [-W] — (also cited [~W])
  • Confusion about the GoCPS true-preference ranking rationale was widespread among students, parents, and staff, and misinformation led some students into second-round applications [-W]
  • Many applicants failed to complete post-application admission requirements, with Black and Latino students least likely to complete them [-W]
  • Program demand varied widely: 21 percent of programs had more than ten times as many applications as seats, led by arts, CTE, and SEHS programs [+W]
  • Most GoCPS applicants received an offer from a preferred program: 51 percent from their top-ranked and 81 percent from a top-three ranked choice program [+W]
  • Black students and students from the lowest-SES neighborhoods ranked more programs but were less likely to rank a high-accountability school first [~W]
