# TA observation form and On/off task form for unobtrusive lab observation

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Two paper-based observation forms developed by Stang et al. for unobtrusively recording teaching-assistant activity and student on/off-task engagement during undergraduate physics laboratory sessions.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Five-minute-interval observation protocol for coding in-lab student actions**: An in-lab observation protocol, following the protocol used by Day et al., in which an observer documents each student's actions every five minutes using codes for equipment handling, laptop or personal device use, paper, desktop computer, and other behaviours. Cumulative codes form a student profile per lab period, normalized to fractions. The article used it in both semesters, with observers physically present in Fall 2017 and coding video in Spring 2018. A chi-squared reliability check found observer profiles were not significantly different (p > 0.1), after which single observers coded subsequent lab periods. (Katherine N. Quinn et al. (2018))
- **Cyclic in-class observation protocol with task-time recording and engagement histograms**: A measurement element used in this study: instructors observed student lab groups "at approximately 20-minute intervals and recording whether each student was actively involved in one of the following tasks: setting up equipment, data collection, note-taking, calculations, plotting graphs, or engaging in group discussions." Time spent per task was averaged across three experiments per group and plotted as histograms to compare engagement by gender, complemented by a survey on task preferences, comfort levels, and open-ended barrier questions. (Bilas Paul et al. (2025))
