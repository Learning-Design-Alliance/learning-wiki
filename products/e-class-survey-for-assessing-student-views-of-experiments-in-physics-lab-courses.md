# E-CLASS survey for assessing student views of experiments in physics lab courses

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 6 studies (4 associational, 2 design), `q2` · 5 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
E-CLASS is a pre/post survey assessment developed by physics education researchers to measure undergraduate students’ views, strategies, habits of mind, and attitudes concerning experimental physics.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **E-CLASS survey for experimental physics attitudes and practices**: The Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics (E-CLASS) assesses changes in students' attitudes about scientific laboratory practices before and after a lab course. It contains "23 core statements that are evaluated in all 4 quadrants" plus seven affect, confidence, and purpose-of-labs statements, with "60 Likert scale items on the pre-test and 106 Likert scale items in the post-test." Most students take 10-15 minutes to complete the end-of-semester survey, and it is designed to give researchers insight and instructors actionable evidence. (Benjamin M. Zwickl et al. (2012))
- **E-CLASS question categories as theme groups rather than correlated psychometric subscales**: E-CLASS organizes its 30 main concepts into categories such as Affect, Argumentation, Confidence, Experimental design, Statistical uncertainty, Systematic error, Troubleshooting, and Physics community, with upper-division lab learning goals marked in italics. The authors state these "are not categories in the sense of some earlier surveys where we expect a high degree of correlation among statements within a category, but are groups of questions surrounding a common theme." Statements within a category can represent distinct practices a course may emphasize to varying degrees. (Benjamin M. Zwickl et al. (2012))
- **Four-quadrant assessment structure: attitudes, expert views, grade importance, and practice frequency**: E-CLASS evaluates students in four aspects of their experience with experimental physics around each core statement: personal attitudes and beliefs, views of what experts would think, perceived importance for earning a good grade, and frequency of engaging in the practice. The first two quadrants are assessed pre and post semester, while the course-related quadrants are "assessed only at the end of the semester because they depend on students' experience in the class." This structure lets instructors see how course structure and grading influence attitudes. (Benjamin M. Zwickl et al. (2012))
- **E-CLASS: the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics**: E-CLASS is an online pre/post epistemology and expectations survey for undergraduate physics laboratory courses, adapted from the CLASS survey through iterative student interviews. It presents 30 pairs of personal and professional epistemology statements, 23 of which add a third expectations question, since "the E-CLASS assesses a third dimension of laboratory instruction, students' reﬂections on their course's expectations for earning a good grade." Content validity was established with 23 expert responses, and it reports class-level results to instructors as formative assessment. (Benjamin M. Zwickl et al. (2014))
- **E-CLASS: the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics**: E-CLASS is a "30 item, Likert-style survey designed to measure students’ epistemologies and expectations about experimental physics," with each item answered from the student's own perspective and as students predict an experimental physicist would respond. It is administered online pre and post each semester and includes a filtering question to eliminate responses from students who did not read the questions. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2015))
- **E-CLASS automated centralized administration and reporting system**: An automated online system for centrally administering the E-CLASS. Instructors complete a Course Information Survey whose answers 'generate unique pre- and post-survey links for each course'; surveys are hosted on Qualtrics and Python-based automation activates and deactivates links by date. Report software exports a static HTML report per course, and 'the automated system has reduced the administrative requirements to checking the system periodically'. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2016))
- **E-CLASS: the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics**: E-CLASS is a 30-item, Likert-style survey targeting students’ epistemologies, expectations, affect, and confidence regarding experimental physics. Items use a paired structure in which students rate agreement from their own perspective doing class experiments and that of a hypothetical experimental physicist. It was "validated through student interviews and faculty review, and has been tested for statistical validity and reliability using responses from a broad student population". This article analyzes a national E-CLASS data set for gender differences. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2016))
- **E-CLASS (Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics)**: E-CLASS is a 30-item Likert-style survey measuring students' epistemologies and expectations about experimental physics, plus affect and confidence when doing experiments. Students rate prompts such as "Calculating uncertainties helps me understand my results better." from both their personal perspective and that of a hypothetical experimental physicist. It was validated through student interviews and expert review and tested for statistical validity and reliability across institutions and course levels, and is administered through a centralized system with a matched pre/post design. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2016))
- **E-CLASS: Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics**: The E-CLASS is the survey instrument this study administers pre and post instruction in undergraduate physics lab courses. It is "a 30 item, Likert-style survey that targets students' epistemologies and expectations about the nature and importance of experimental physics", as well as affect and confidence. Students respond from their personal perspective and that of a hypothetical experimental physicist; scoring awards +1 for favorable, 0 for neutral, and −1 for unfavorable responses, giving a range of [−30, 30]. It was validated through student interviews, expert review, and statistical reliability testing across institutions and course levels. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2016))

## Description
E-CLASS is a pre/post epistemology and expectations survey for undergraduate physics lab courses. It uses 23 statements asked as paired questions about classroom and research contexts plus 7 affect statements, and a post-only question on what was important for earning a good grade. The article says it "assesses students views about their strategies, habits of mind, and attitudes when doing experiments in lab classes" and provides instructors with detailed feedback reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administered online at beginning and end of semester; instructors willing to give students credit or extra credit for completing the survey
#### Constraints
- Not tied to specific course content, so it measures epistemology and expectations rather than content mastery

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory and upper-division lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- students' epistemology and expectations about experimental physics, including strategies, habits of mind, and attitudes
