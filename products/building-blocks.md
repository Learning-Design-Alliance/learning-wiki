# Building Blocks

## Description
Building Blocks is a preschool mathematics curriculum and intervention developed by Douglas H. Clements and Julie Sarama that combines classroom activities with computer-based learning activities.

# Building Blocks preschool mathematics curriculum

## Description
Building Blocks is a mathematics curriculum that "aims to develop preschool children’s knowledge of mathematics using activities that are intentionally sequenced based on the developmental progression of children’s mathematical learning." It includes whole- and small-group instruction, center activities, and computer activities, plus weekly family letters, with teacher support through professional development. Activities were implemented over 26 to 30 weeks in the reviewed studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers implementing Building Blocks in their classrooms received training and coaching.
- Teachers regularly assessed children’s learning and adapted activities to build on and extend the level of children’s mathematical thinking.
#### Constraints
- The Building Blocks curriculum is designed for use in prekindergarten through grade 8. However, this intervention report provides evidence of its effectiveness only in prekindergarten.

### Target Learners
- preschool children, especially those from lower-resource communities

### Target Learning Goals
- early mathematical knowledge and processes

# Building Blocks multi-context implementation pattern: whole-group, small-group, centers, computer activities, and family letters

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The curriculum distributes mathematics learning across several classroom contexts: whole-group instruction during circle time (approximately 5–15 minutes, four or five times per week), small-group instruction with 3 to 6 children (approximately 10–15 minutes, two or three times per week), hands-on center activities, and computer activities that "automatically adjust when children progress well or need additional help." Weekly family/caregiver letters describe the mathematics and home activities. This pattern lets teachers adapt activities to children's developmental levels while other children work independently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers used the Teacher’s Edition to locate relevant activities.
- Teachers received 4–8 full days of initial training, monthly 2-hour refresher classes, and on-site coaching 1–2 times a month.
#### Constraints
- Center activities were mostly free-choice, so frequency and duration varied; the content and duration of professional development varied across the studies.

### Target Learners
- preschool children

### Learning Goals
- early mathematics knowledge aligned to learning trajectories
