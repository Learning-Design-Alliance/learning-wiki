# Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD): a six-stage explicit strategy-instruction model with graduated responsibility transfer

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
SRSD is an academic practice, developed by Karen Harris and Steven Graham in the early 1980s, that uses explicit instruction to teach students concrete academic strategies plus self-regulation skills such as goal-setting and self-monitoring. Instruction proceeds through six stages that "can be reordered, combined, changed, and repeated, depending on students' needs": develop background knowledge, discuss the strategy, model the strategy, memorize the strategy, support the strategy, and independent performance. During instruction "the teacher gradually transfers responsibility for implementing the strategy to students, until the students can adequately perform the tasks without support." The model is used with students in grades 2 through 12 in individual, small group, or whole classroom settings.

#### Requirements

#### Constraints

### Target Learners
- Students in grades 2 through 12, especially students with learning disabilities

### Target Learning Objectives
