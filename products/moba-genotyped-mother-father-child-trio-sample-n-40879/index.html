MoBa genotyped mother-father-child trio sample (N = 40,879)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A restricted-access Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort Study dataset containing genotype data for 40,879 mother-father-child trios, administered by the Norwegian Institute of Public Health for research on intergenerational and child-development outcomes.

Components¶

Claims¶

  • Within-family Mendelian randomization finds little evidence that mothers' or fathers' educational attainment causally affects children's depressive, anxiety, or ADHD traits at age 8 [+M]
  • Non-genetic models show small negative associations between parents' years of education and children's depressive traits, which attenuate once confounders are accounted for [+W]
  • Children's own polygenic index for educational attainment is negatively associated with their ADHD traits at age 8, independent of parental PGIs [+W]
  • In complete-case sensitivity analyses, maternal education showed unexpected positive associations with children's depressive and ADHD-inattention traits [+W]
  • Children's own polygenic index for educational attainment is negatively associated with their depressive traits at age 8 [+W]
  • Two-sample MR finds inverse-variance weighted estimates linking maternal education to fewer depressive but more ADHD-inattention traits, with other estimators consistent with the null [+W]
  • Within-family cogPGS effect on crystallized intelligence was roughly half the between-family effect, indicating passive genotype-environment correlation [+W]
