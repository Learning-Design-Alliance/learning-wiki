## Description
An openly released dataset from the study containing equity-lesson logs, tutor responses, human-scoring rubrics, and generative-AI prompts for research on tutor training and assessment.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Released dataset of equity lesson logs, tutor responses, annotation rubrics, and generative AI prompts**: The article contributes an open dataset supporting research on equity-focused tutor training and AI-based assessment. As the abstract states, "This work makes available a dataset of lesson log data, tutor responses, rubrics for human annotation, and generative AI prompts." The dataset underpins the study's analyses of 81 tutors' pretest/posttest performance and the LLM grading experiments, and is offered "to enhance transparency, reproducibility, and collaboration within the LAK community." It includes the complete few-shot scoring prompts for GPT-4o shown in Tables 3 and 4. (Do Tutors Learn from Equity Training and Can Generative AI Assess It?)
- **Open LLM-feedback research artifacts: dataset, prompts, rubrics, and lesson logs**: The authors release supporting materials for reproducibility: "We contribute an open dataset, annotation rubrics, and LLM scoring prompts". Lesson log data were recorded in DataShop, described as "a widely used repository of educational data in learning analytics research", and are accessible through a GitHub repository. The artifacts include the few-shot scoring prompts used to evaluate predict and explain open responses, lesson-specific reliability information, and statistical analysis code. (Thomas et al. (2025))
- **Open-source LLM prompting toolkit for assessing tutor moves in tutoring transcripts**: The article contributes a set of grading prompts and methods for using LLMs to identify and evaluate tutor moves in real tutoring transcripts. The authors state: "We propose a cost-effective prompting strategy and discuss practical implications for using large language models to support scalable assessment in authentic settings." All grading prompts are provided in a Digital Appendix, and the article states "This work further contributes LLM prompts to support reproducibility and research in AI-supported learning." The toolkit uses a two-stage filter-then-evaluate prompt design with a 2-point/1-point scoring system per transcript. (Danielle R. Thomas et al. (2025))
