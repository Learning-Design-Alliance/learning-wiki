# Watson–Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal (W-GCTA)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A paper-and-pencil standardized assessment developed by Watson and Glaser that measures critical-thinking subskills including inference, assumption recognition, deduction, interpretation, and argument evaluation.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **W-GCTA: Watson–Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal used to assess five critical thinking subtests**: The W-GCTA, developed by Watson and Glaser (1980), was adopted to assess learners' critical thinking skills through text-based exercises. It comprises five subtests totaling 86 items: evaluation of arguments (25 items), recognition of assumptions (16 items), deduction (25 items), inferences (20 items), and interpreting information (24 items). One point is awarded per correct answer. In this study Cronbach's alpha values "for the five sub-scales ranged from 0.792 to 0.865". Factor analytic studies are reported as supporting its construct validity and relationship with academic performance. (Mark Feng Teng (2022))
- **Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal (Form A) as the critical thinking measure**: The study measured critical thinking with the "Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal" (CTA) Form A, comprising 80 items in five subtests: Inference, Recognizing Unstated Assumptions, Deduction, Interpretation, and Evaluation of Arguments. It was administered twice, as pretest and posttest, to both groups. The article reports "The internal consistency reliability estimate of the CTA administered as a posttest in this study was 0.81 (Alpha)". Each subtest covers 16 items, e.g. Test 1 items 1-16 through Test 5 items 65-80. (Khodadady et al. (2011))
