# 360° Multisource Feedback (MSF) model

## Description
A multisource professional-performance assessment model that triangulates supervisor, coworker, and self-ratings of behavioral competencies, applied here to health professionals' nontechnical skills.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **360° Multisource Feedback (MSF) model for evaluating nontechnical professional competencies**: The 360° MSF model evaluates professional performance by triangulating multiple rater sources familiar with the role: "supervisors, co-workers, and the workers themselves (self-assessment)". Competencies are operationalized into behavioral indicators rated on a shared 10-point Likert-type scale divided into five levels, and all assessments are compared to obtain an overall performance score. The article applies it in nine phases over three years to evaluate whether a training intervention changed hospital professionals' nontechnical competencies, arguing triangulation lets each source's strengths compensate for others' weaknesses. (Sureda Elena et al. (2021))
- **Multisource feedback (360° evaluation): a multi-rater assessment framework for trainees' professional competencies**: Multisource feedback, "also known by some researchers as the 360° evaluation", is an evaluation process in which various raters fill out surveys to evaluate their medical peers and colleagues, providing feedback from individuals who are not the attending or supervising physicians. It assesses domains including clinical skills, communication, professionalism, collaboration, and patient management. In this study it was used, for the first time to the authors' knowledge, to select graduates for a higher-education interns' award. (Strachan Kathryn et al. (2017))
