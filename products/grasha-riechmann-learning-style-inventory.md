# Grasha-Riechmann Learning Style Inventory

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 60-item Likert-scale assessment developed by Anthony Grasha to measure students' social-interaction learning-style preferences across independent-dependent, avoidant-participant, and collaborative-competitive dimensions; this study used a Turkish adaptation.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Grasha-Riechmann Learning Style Inventory (Turkish adaptation) as the study's learning style measure**: The study used the Grasha-Riechmann Learning Style Inventory, a 60-item five-point Likert scale adapted by Sarıtaş and Süral (2010) with six dimensions of ten items each, with a printed Cronbach Alpha of .802. The article describes it as "a social interaction scale" positioning styles as independent-dependent, avoidant-participant, and collaborative-competitive on three major lines, each representing two opposite directions. Styles are scored at low, medium, and high levels using printed cut-off ranges (Table 2). (Balta (2018))
- **Grasha-Riechmann social-interaction learning style model**: The Grasha-Riechmann model classifies learners by their preferred social interaction in learning, which the article says "reflects the learners' abilities and preference to work independently, preferences to cooperate or to compete". Its classification rests on three dichotomous pairs: competitive-collaborative, avoidant-participant, and dependent-independent. The study operationalised it with the 60-item GRSLSS inventory on a five-point rating scale to profile students before the online vs in-class intervention. (Cimermanová (2018))
- **Grasha's five classic teaching styles model (expert, formal authority, demonstrator, facilitator, delegator)**: The study's organizing framework is Grasha's model, in which "Grasha's model includes five classic teaching styles": expert (knowledgeable subject expert giving correct information), formal authority (manager emphasizing strict rules), demonstrator (role model encouraging one effective approach), facilitator (guiding by questions, options and alternatives), and delegator (concerned with students' autonomy, helping only on request). The article operationalizes these as five eight-item subscales of the 40-item Grasha-Riechmann inventory and maps expert, authority and demonstrator to teacher-centered approaches and facilitator and delegator to student-centered approaches. (Dash Nihar Ranjan et al. (2020))
