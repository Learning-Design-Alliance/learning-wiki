## Description
A validated instrument developed by physics-education researchers for TAs and instructors that elicits and compares grading practices through individual written responses, small-group discussion, whole-class discussion, and follow-up mapping.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **GAIQ: group administered interactive questionnaire for eliciting grading goals and practices**: The group administered interactive questionnaire (GAIQ) is a validated professional-development and data-collection tool in which TAs first individually write essays and grade authentic student solutions, then discuss grading in groups of three with a whole-class discussion, then individually map their features onto a researcher-defined list. The article states it 'was previously developed and validated for use with TAs/instructors' and served a dual role as reflection activity and research instrument. (Emily Marshman et al. (2018))
- **The Group Administered Interactive Questionnaire (GAIQ), a group-administered alternative to individual interviews**: The GAIQ is a data-collection instrument that replaces the one-on-one discussion of an interview with a structured sequence of individual and group activities built around concrete instructional artifacts. As the authors state, "GAIQ took advantage of a methods course for physics graduate assistants" and "replaced the one-on-one discussion that takes place in an interview with a sequence of activities". It was used to investigate TAs' considerations about worked-out example solutions in introductory physics. (Edit Yerushalmi et al. (2011))
