# 25-item multiple-choice test on symmetry and Gauss' law

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A researcher-developed 25-item conceptual multiple-choice assessment for calculus-based introductory physics students covering symmetry reasoning, Gauss' law, electric fields and flux, and Gaussian-surface selection.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **25-item multiple-choice test on symmetry and Gauss' law with reliability α = 0.77 and average score about 56%**: The article presents a conceptual multiple-choice test assessing students' ability to exploit symmetry, predict electric field magnitude and direction using Coulomb's or Gauss' laws, distinguish electric field from electric flux, and identify when Gauss' law easily yields the field and appropriate Gaussian surface shapes. The "final version of the test contains 25 items" administered to 168 students in three introductory calculus-based courses at Pitt, including one honors course. The test was designed with attention to reliability and validity using a test blueprint, and "can be used as a teaching and assessment tool". (Chandralekha Singh (2016))

### Claims

- [Many introductory physics students incorrectly assume the electric field magnitude is the same at all points outside a sphere with discretely placed charges, overgeneralizing symmetry](../claims/students-overgeneralize-symmetry-field-magnitude-discrete-charges.md) [+M]
- [Introductory physics students commonly confuse the electric field at a point with the contribution to electric flux through an area, e.g., claiming the field is zero where the area vector is perpendicular to the field](../claims/students-confuse-electric-field-with-electric-flux.md) [+M]
- [Questions about net flux through closed surfaces and choice of Gaussian surfaces were extremely difficult, with students unsure which surfaces Gauss' law applies to](../claims/students-unsure-gauss-law-closed-surfaces-gaussian-surface-choice.md) [+W]
- [Students commonly confuse the electric field at a point with the electric flux through a surface, e.g., interpreting zero net flux as zero field everywhere](../claims/field-flux-confusion-misconception.md) [+W]
