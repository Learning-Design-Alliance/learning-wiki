# Enhanced Core Reading Instruction

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Enhanced Core Reading Instruction is a structured literacy intervention programme with professional development materials for teachers, interventionists, and literacy leaders.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Enhanced Core Reading Instruction professional development workbooks and Leadership Guide**: The program's materials include "professional development workbooks for teachers and interventionists and a Leadership Guide for literacy leaders." The workbooks are "designed to be used in a study group, self-study or as part of a facilitated training," with a recommended 3 days of study for Tier 1 instructors and 2 days for Tier 2 interventionists. (Enhanced Core Reading Instruction (2017))
- **Enhanced Core Reading Instruction program for struggling first-grade readers**: Enhanced Core Reading Instruction is "a program for struggling first grade readers" that focuses on Tier 2 supplemental teaching. It provides teachers with professional development and materials to improve the alignment of Tier 2 with Tier 1 core instruction, with small groups of 3-5 students pretaught content soon to appear in the core classroom. (Enhanced Core Reading Instruction (2017))
- **Enhanced Core Reading Instruction (ECRI) multi-tiered intervention**: ECRI is a multi-tiered reading intervention for first grade that enhances the existing Tier 1 core reading program and provides a Tier 2 small-group intervention aligned with it. Its three principles are increased explicitness of Tier 1 instruction, increased specificity via lesson maps and instructional templates, and alignment: "ECRI increased the alignment between Tier 1 instruction and Tier 2 intervention by implementing daily, 30-minute Tier 2 lessons using the same scope and sequence of skills from the core reading program." Implementation included teacher professional development, coaching, and instructional-assistant-led Tier 2 groups. (Fien et al. (2015))
- **ECRI explicit instructional design elements for restructuring core reading lessons**: ECRI restructured core reading programs around five explicit instructional elements: clearer learning objectives, more conspicuous teacher models, systematic connections between new and previously learned concepts, greatly increased guided and independent practice calibrated to deliberate and accurate conditions, and systematic review of previously learned material. The article lists them as: "(a) learning objectives for the reading block were made more clear for the students, (b) teacher models were made more conspicuous through visual models, verbal directions, and fuller and clearer explanations" and continued with connections, practice, and review elements. (Fien et al. (2015))
- **ECRI Tier 2 pre-teaching small group lesson structure**: The ECRI Tier 2 intervention is a daily 30-minute small group lesson for 3 to 5 at-risk students, led by instructional assistants, that pre-teaches the content of the next day's Tier 1 core lesson using the same instructional routines and language. The article states that "Students receiving Tier 2 intervention were pre-taught content in daily, 30-minute lessons in small groups comprised of 3 to 5 students." Lessons target irregular word reading, phonemic segmentation and blending, direct teaching of the target sound spelling, blending and decodable word reading, decodable text reading, dictation, and re-teaching based on error rates. (Fien et al. (2015))
- **ECRI lesson maps and instruction templates emphasizing explicit instruction features**: ECRI treatment classrooms use lesson maps and instruction templates to enhance content, design, and delivery of Tier I whole-group reading instruction using a district-adopted core program. The templates emphasize six features: "(a) learning strategies are made conspicuous; (b) instruction builds connections between new and previously taught content; (c) instruction activates student background knowledge; (d) instructional scaffolds are provided to promote success; (e) sufficient practice to achieve automaticity is provided; and (f) previously learned material is reviewed systematically." (Doabler et al. (2013))

### Claims
