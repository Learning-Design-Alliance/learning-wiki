# NKLM (National Competency-based Learning Objectives for Undergraduate Medical Education)

## Description
A German national competency-based framework that specifies learning objectives and milestone-based competencies for undergraduate medical education, including physician collaboration, management, and leadership roles.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **NKLM professional roles Collaborator and Manager (based on CanMEDs) as a framework for competency-based surgical education**: The NKLM (National Competency-based Learning Objectives for Undergraduate Medical Education) defines collaboration and management competencies in chapters 8 (Collaborator) and 10 (Manager), based on the CanMEDS framework. The Collaborator describes the work of physicians in an interprofessional health care team, whose central purpose is to prevent, negotiate and resolve interpersonal and interprofessional conflicts. The Manager role describes management and leadership competencies necessary in dealing with complex situations, including health economics, healthcare strategy, personnel and process management, and shared collaborative decision-making. Competency levels are defined at milestones, ranging from knowledge/understanding/basic skills to competency in practice, supervised and independent. (Rothdiener M et al. (2020))
- **GMA consensus statement "Practical Skills in Medical School" as a reference frame of 290 learning objectives**: The consensus statement, developed by the GMA within the National Competency-Based Learning Objective Catalogue for Medicine (NKLM), is a reference frame for practical skills teaching. The article states it "describes 289 learning objectives, 232 of which are defined as core objectives", each assigned to at least one of 16 organ systems, with objectives divided by type (core or elective), stage of medical education, and level of instruction (demonstration, supervised performance, skilled performance). In this study it served as the mapping reference against which the tutorial program was classified. (Blaum Wolf E. et al. (2012))
