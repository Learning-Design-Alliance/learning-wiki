POGIL (Process Oriented Guided Inquiry Learning)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

POGIL is a guided-inquiry instructional programme and activity design model developed and supported by The POGIL Project for collaborative content learning and process-skill development across disciplines.

Components¶

  • POGIL: a guided-inquiry pedagogy coupling process skills to content learning through a three-phase learning cycle: POGIL is a pedagogy, rooted in chemistry but used across disciplines, that "provides opportunities for improving process skills during content learning through guided-inquiry activities". Its materials use a learning cycle of three phases: exploration of a model, concept invention, and application. Students take individual roles within small groups (manager, reporter, skeptic) to establish positive interdependence, and well-designed activities target one or two of seven process skills: communication, teamwork, management, information processing, critical thinking, problem solving, and self-assessment. (Walker L (2017))
  • POGIL group scenario exercises with four rotating student roles in a fundamentals nursing course: The intervention consisted of six group case scenario exercises completed in 45-minute intervals after lecture, with 4-5 students per group assigned by the professor. Each student took one of four roles: the "Manager keeps everyone on task", the "Recorder keeps records", the "Presenter or team leader presents an oral report to the class", and the "Reflector observes and comments on group behavior and dynamics". Roles were chosen by students and rotated each exercise, and the professor facilitated, encouraging critical thinking and communication skills. The control group received the same six scenarios individually online. (Maureen C. Roller et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • POGIL instruction produces a small but statistically significant improvement in student achievement outcomes compared to standard lecture (g = 0.29) [+M]
  • Students in POGIL classrooms have roughly twice the odds of passing a course as students in standard lecture classrooms (OR = 2.02) [+M]
  • Nursing students who participated in POGIL group scenario exercises scored significantly higher on the ATI standardized national exam than non-participants [+M]
