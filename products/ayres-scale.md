## Description
A standardized handwriting-legibility rating scale developed by Leonard P. Ayres for comparing pupils' handwriting quality across ages, grades, and schools.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Ayres scale as a workable instrument for measuring and comparing handwriting legibility across ages, grades and schools**: The Ayres scale grades handwriting on eight supposed equal steps designated 20 to 90, with three specimen styles (vertical, medium slant and full slant) under each grade; grade 20 is very poor writing and 90 is excellence "seldom attained in the public schools." The article uses it to grade 966 specimens copied "in their ordinary hand," to compare grade with grade, age with age, and school with school, and to test the scale's practicability. It concludes the scale is workable but that subjective judging cannot be fully eliminated. (King et al. (1914))
- **Ayres-Hill standardized illustrative sentence list for the Springfield spelling scale**: A published instrument pairing words of the Ayres Springfield list with uniform illustrative sentences, printed grade by grade from Grade 3 to Grade 8 (e.g., "Let us fill the pail with sand." for FILL). The author offers it so that "Schoolmen, it is hoped, may find the words and the sentences a convenient instrument for further measurements in spelling," together with the compiled results that afford a basis of tentative standardization. (David Spence Hill (1917))
