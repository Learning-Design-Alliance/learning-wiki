# Twenty-five-problem mechanics categorization task with faculty-validated good/moderate/poor rating scheme

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An assessment instrument developed by Chandralekha Singh and collaborators that measures learners’ physics problem-categorization expertise using 25 introductory mechanics problems scored against expert-validated ratings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Paper-and-pencil problem categorization task with three-column response sheet for large classes**: A categorization instrument in which students group 25 mechanics problems based upon similarity of solution, creating as many groups as they wish and explaining their groupings without solving the problems. The response sheet "had three columns": a self-chosen category name, a description of why problems belong together, and the problem numbers in each category. It was administered in recitation classes with hundreds of students, adapting the Chi study's index-card sorting to a paper-and-pencil format, with 50 minutes allowed and identical bonus points regardless of performance. (Andrew Mason et al. (2012))
