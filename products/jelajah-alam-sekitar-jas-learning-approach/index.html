Jelajah Alam Sekitar (JAS) learning approach¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A nature-exploration biology learning approach developed and disseminated by the Biology Department of FMIPA UNNES in Indonesia, using learners' direct experience of the natural environment.

Components¶

  • JAS approach development and tracer study at UNNES Biology Department: The Biology Department of FMIPA UNNES has developed the Jelajah Alam Sekitar learning approach since 2005. A tracer study by Ridlo (2005) and Ridlo et al. (2016) shows that "since 2006 to 2016, JAS has been spread in eight provinces in Indonesia although most of it are in Central Java especially in Semarang", and that JAS has been researched from elementary school to university, tracked mostly with quantitative pre- and quasi-experiment designs. (S. Ngabekti et al. (2017))
  • Jelajah Alam Sekitar (JAS) approach with six defining characteristics: JAS is a learning approach that "uses the student's nature environment, whether the physical, social, technology, or culture environment as the learning object in biology" whose phenomena are learned through scientific work. It is characterized by six components — exploration, constructivism, science process, learning community, edutainment, and authentic assessment — integrated into indoor and outdoor learning. Developed by the Biology Department of FMIPA UNNES since 2005, it spread to eight Indonesian provinces and has been studied from primary school to college. (S. Ngabekti et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Time allocation and power are the dominant reported problems in implementing JAS [~M]
  • A meta-analysis of 103 JAS studies reports the approach's effectiveness in 97% of researches [+M]
  • Studies report experiment classes using JAS achieve higher average study results than control classes [+M]
  • JAS most frequently optimizes study results (52.83%) and student activity (40.57%) [+M]
