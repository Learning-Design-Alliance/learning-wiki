## Description
SPARK is a family–school–community early-literacy partnership programme that combines in-school tutoring, afterschool enrichment, and family engagement for early-grade students.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **SPARK family–school–community partnership model for sustained early literacy**: SPARK is a literacy development model combining "in-school tutoring, afterschool enrichment, and family engagement in concert" to build early-grade literacy skills while engaging families to support literacy at home. It was developed in response to mixed evidence that skill-based program impacts fade after students leave a program; it therefore works to build an environment around students conducive to learning to read and maintaining literacy development beyond participation. Literacy deficits are treated as a result of ecological processes (Bronfenbrenner, 1979) across school, family, and community microsystems rather than individual deficits. (Curtis Jones (2018))
- **SPARK early-grade literacy and family engagement program**: SPARK is an early grade literacy and family engagement program developed by Boys & Girls Clubs of Greater Milwaukee, loosely based on Reading Recovery. It combines in-school tutoring (students pulled out of non-core classes for 30 minutes, up to three times per week, for two years; the average student received 122.5 tutoring sessions, SD = 27.3) with a family engagement component in which each site has a parent partner who works with each participating student's family through newsletters, monthly family events, phone calls, emails, and home visits. The program seeks "to develop the literacy skills of early-grade students and to support families as they learn to support the literacy development of their students." (Curtis J Jones et al. (2016))
- **SPARK/MCLP early literacy program: in-school tutoring, family engagement, and assessment components**: SPARK, created by Boys & Girls Clubs of Greater Milwaukee in 2005, provides tutoring during the school day for 30 minutes, up to three times per week, for two years, overseen at each site by a program manager who is a certified teacher. The tutoring component is loosely based on the Reading Recovery program. A family engagement component assigns each site a parent partner who works with families through newsletters, monthly events, phone calls or emails, and two summer home visits, and all students are assessed with PALS at the beginning and end of the school year to determine needs and create individual lesson plans. (Curtis J. Jones et al. (2015))
- **SPARK tutoring lesson structure of six recurring sections combining assessment, differentiated word study, leveled reading, writing, and read-aloud**: Each SPARK tutoring lesson includes six sections: Familiar Activity, Running Record, Word Play, Reading at Instructional Level, Writing, and a Tutor Read Aloud. Word Play provides targeted, differentiated instruction on foundational reading skills through Word Sorts and Making Words, activities the article says combine both constructivist learning and structured instruction. Writing uses Elkonin boxes to help students encode words and build phonological awareness by segmenting words into individual sounds. (Curtis J. Jones et al. (2015))

### Claims
- [The SPARK program produced a small but statistically significant positive impact on reading achievement of 0.12 standard deviations in a two-year RCT](../claims/spark-rct-reading-impact-012-sd.md) [+M]
- [SPARK showed no statistically significant impact on regular school day attendance](../claims/spark-no-attendance-impact.md) [~M]
- [SPARK tutoring significantly improved early-grade reading achievement (MAP), with an overall effect of 2.8 RIT points (g = .23)](../claims/spark-improves-map-reading-achievement.md) [+M]
- [SPARK significantly improved early-grade literacy (PALS), with an overall effect of 5.7 scale points (g = .35)](../claims/spark-improves-pals-literacy.md) [+M]
- [SPARK significantly improved school attendance, with students averaging 5.8 fewer absences over two years (g = .25)](../claims/spark-improves-school-attendance.md) [+M]
