# The Future Ready Talent Framework (FRTF): a 12-talent, 4-cluster conceptual model for WIL stakeholders

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
The FRTF is a conceptual model organizing 12 talents into four clusters — Expand and Transfer Expertise, Develop Self, Build Relationships, and Design and Deliver Solutions. It was synthesized from 46 prior talent frameworks and future-of-work literature to identify talents mutually relevant to employers, students, and educators in WIL. The article states it "is not intended to be prescriptive" but "acts as a tool WIL stakeholders can use as they communicate with each other." Behavioral examples attached to each talent illustrate what the talents look like in practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires stakeholder consultation and steering-committee input to develop and refine talent labels and behavioral examples, as described for this framework
#### Constraints
- Discipline and Context-Specific Skills is excluded from sorting validation because there are not common cross-discipline, cross-contextual statements that can represent the talent

### Target Learners
- WIL students
- post-secondary students preparing for the future of work

### Target Learning Objectives
- Development and demonstration of future ready talents such as communication, collaboration, self-management, and critical thinking

# Use a shared talent framework to give WIL stakeholders a common language for communicating about talent

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because WIL programs rely on co-creation among employers, students, and educators whose perspectives on talent vary widely, the article recommends giving stakeholders a shared framework so they can coordinate efforts. It argues "Frameworks provide a common language that enables stakeholders to interact more efficiently," and states the FRTF's goal is "to provide a common ground for WIL stakeholders to discuss the talents that are important to them." Informal feedback from students, employers, faculty, staff, and administrators suggests the FRTF is useful in conversations about talent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires that the framework be developed with input from the stakeholder groups who will use it, via steering committees and consultation
#### Constraints
- The framework is explicitly not prescriptive; it acts as a communication tool rather than mandating talents or outcomes

### Target Learners
- WIL students
- post-secondary graduates entering the workforce

### Target Learning Objectives
- Communicating and demonstrating talents to employers; developing employability-relevant talents
