## Description
A seven-stage constructivist, inquiry-oriented instructional model developed by Eisenkraft for organizing science teaching through eliciting, engaging, exploring, explaining, elaborating, evaluating, and extending.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **7E learning model as a constructivist instructional sequence for science teaching**: The 7E learning model is a constructivist, inquiry-based instructional sequence for science teaching, described as an improved version of the 5E model developed by Eisenkraft. The article states its stages as "elicit, engage, explore, explain, elaborate, evaluate, and extend". In this study, multimedia materials (pictures, activity drawings, animations, simulations, video-films) were prepared for each stage of the model and used to teach the fifth-grade Matter's Change Unit. (Sarac et al. (2017))
- **7E instructional model: a constructivist seven-stage learning cycle adding elicit and extend to the 5E model**: The 7E instructional model is "based on the theory of constructivism" and was proposed in 2003 by Arthur Einsenkraft, who built on the 5E model and "added the two new stages of 'elicit' and 'extend'". Its seven stages are elicit, engage, explore, explain, elaborate, evaluate and extend. The article describes it as "systematic, cyclical, extensive and practical", enabling diverse teaching methods at each stage and encouraging inquiry-based learning that extends knowledge to multidisciplinary fields. In this study it was implemented in surgical-nursing clinical probation. (Cui Liping et al. (2024))
