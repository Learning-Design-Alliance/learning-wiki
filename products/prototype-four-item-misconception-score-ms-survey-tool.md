# Prototype four-item Misconception Score (MS) survey tool

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A four-item Likert-scale survey developed by Sikorski et al. to quantify university students' and game-industry professionals' misconceptions about video game development and compare scores before and after instruction.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Prototype four-item Misconception Score (MS) survey tool for video game development naïveté**: A prototype quantitative tool measuring students' level of video game development misconceptions, consisting of four Likert-scale statements (MS1–MS4) rated from -2 ('strongly disagree') to +2 ('strongly agree'). PCA on 175 response sets yielded factor loadings of '0.713, 0.693, 0.742, and 0.581', and Cronbach's alpha 'yielded a value of 0.62, a level considered acceptable for such a brief scale'. The mean of the four items serves as a composite Misconception Score, enabling objective evaluation of misconception reduction and comparisons across programs. (Sikorski et al. (2024))
