## Description
A free desktop virtual-reality simulation developed by Dominic McGrath and colleagues for exploring visual and conceptual effects of special relativity through interactive user-controlled scenarios.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Real Time Relativity (RTR) virtual reality simulation of special relativity**: Real Time Relativity is a desktop virtual reality package that simulates the visual effects of travelling at near light speed in a game-like world with user control over motion and view, with scenarios including clocks, planets and abstract shapes. The article states "the visual experience of the user is extremely accurate" and that some effects can be disabled to focus on particular concepts. It runs on a personal computer with a programmable graphics card and is available free from http://realtimerelativity.org/. (Dominic McGrath et al. (2009))
- **Python/Vispy animation toolkit for Special Relativity visualizations**: The article releases a set of Python scripts, built on the Vispy package combining OpenGL with Python, that generate interactive visualizations and animations of relativistically moving self-luminous wire-frame objects. As printed: "The code of this work comes in three parts, make_shape.py, SR_transforms.py, and animate_shape.py." The make_shape script produces points for rods combined into squares and cubes, SR_transforms applies Lorentz transformations and optical distortion, and animate_shape renders the animations. The scripts are made available online for personal or professional use, and shapes are built point by point so the mechanism is transparent to readers. (Jason Garver (2018))
- **Real Time Relativity: a first-person relativistic flight simulation**: Real Time Relativity is a "first person point of view game-like computer simulation of a special relativistic world" that lets students fly at relativistic speeds among planets, clocks, and buildings. It renders relativistic optical effects (Doppler shift, aberration, headlight effect) in real time using GPU-based environment mapping, and is freely available as an Open Source project. It was used in a three-hour first-year laboratory at the Australian National University structured to encourage exploration while requiring measurements compared to theory. (C. M. Savage et al. (2007))
- **Relativistic optics as a visual-observation formulation of special relativity**: The article formulates special relativity through a personal observer's visual observations rather than abstract inertial observers with distributed rulers and clocks. It presents the Doppler effect, relativistic aberration, and the headlight effect using 4-vectors, noting that "relativity gives a rich and unexpected visual environment." This approach underpins the Real Time Relativity simulation, making visual observations the basis from which theory is deduced, and connects relativistic optics to direct experience through analogies with non-relativistic aberration such as falling rain angles. (C. M. Savage et al. (2007))
