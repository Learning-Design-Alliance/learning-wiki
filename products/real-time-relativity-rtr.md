# Real Time Relativity (RTR)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A free desktop virtual-reality simulation developed by Dominic McGrath and colleagues for exploring visual and conceptual effects of special relativity through interactive user-controlled scenarios.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Real Time Relativity (RTR) virtual reality simulation of special relativity**: Real Time Relativity is a desktop virtual reality package that simulates the visual effects of travelling at near light speed in a game-like world with user control over motion and view, with scenarios including clocks, planets and abstract shapes. The article states "the visual experience of the user is extremely accurate" and that some effects can be disabled to focus on particular concepts. It runs on a personal computer with a programmable graphics card and is available free from http://realtimerelativity.org/. (Dominic McGrath et al. (2009))

### Claims
- [Students who completed the RTR experiment performed better on the final exam special relativity question (d = 0.33, p < 0.05)](../claims/rtr-final-exam-advantage-d-033.md) [+M]
- [Students who completed the RTR lab were less likely to regard special relativity as abstract than students who had not done the lab](../claims/rtr-reduces-perceived-abstractness.md) [+M]
- [Students who completed the Real Time Relativity lab scored significantly higher on externally set relativity exam questions than classmates who did not](../claims/rtr-lab-improves-relativity-exam-marks.md) [+M]
- [Concept-test scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test on time dilation and the relativity of simultaneity after the RTR lab, in one single-group pre-post study with no comparison group](../claims/rtr-concept-test-gains.md) [+M]
- [Most students who completed the RTR lab found it interesting and wanted more simulations in their studies](../claims/rtr-student-attitudes-interest.md) [+M]
