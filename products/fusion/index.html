FUSION¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes

Description¶

FUSION is a Grade 1 small-group mathematics intervention programme developed by Strand Cary et al. with 60 scripted explicit lessons targeting whole-number concepts and early operations.

Components¶

  • Fusion theory-of-change model linking intervention components through teacher-student interactions to proximal and distal outcomes: A three-level model specifying how the intervention is expected to produce achievement gains: "the theory of change for the Fusion intervention is composed of three key levels: (a) intervention components, (b) mediator variables, and (c) proximal and distal student outcomes." Its two components are whole-number content and explicit and systematic instructional design principles, hypothesized to raise the quality of teacher-student instructional interactions, which mediate conceptual understanding and procedural fluency and, in turn, distal mathematics achievement. The article uses the model to select measures and to guide formative evaluation of mediators. (Ben Clarke et al. (2014))
  • Fusion Tier 2 first-grade mathematics intervention curriculum: Fusion is a Tier 2 Grade 1 mathematics intervention for students at risk in whole-number concepts and skills. "Students are taught in small groups of approximately five students and receive 60 lessons, each lasting 30 min, delivered over a period of 20 weeks." Lessons include explicit introduction of new content and systematic practice and review in four to five brief scripted activities, using math models, teacher modeling, scaffolded examples, and academic feedback. Content draws on Operations and Algebraic Thinking and Number and Operations in Base Ten from the Common Core. (Ben Clarke et al. (2014))

Claims¶

  • Prior implementation studies and a single-case design indicate FUSION is a feasible, usable Tier 2 intervention with promise for improving student math achievement [+M]
  • The Year 3 pilot is a randomized control trial assigning at-risk first graders within schools to FUSION or at-risk control, with an additional average control condition [+W]
  • Educators tend to perceive meaningful effects of the developers' curricula on student outcomes, but standardized measures tend not to reflect the same level of impact as proximal assessments [+W]
  • At-risk first graders receiving Fusion showed significantly greater gains than control peers on the proximal conceptual-understanding measure ProFusion, with a large effect [+M]
