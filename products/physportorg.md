## Description
PhysPort.org is a website and resource platform that helps physics instructors find, select, adapt, and assess Physics Education Research-based teaching approaches and materials.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **PhysPort assessments collection for downloading research-based assessment instruments**: PhysPort's assessments section is the online companion resource to this Resource Letter, hosting the research-based assessment instruments (RBAIs) it reviews across mathematics, beliefs and attitudes, problem solving, scientific reasoning, lab skills, and observation protocols. As the article states, "More details about each of these RBAIs are available at physport.org/assessments, where verified educators can download most of the RBAIs." Not all instruments are hosted there: the article notes the Calculus Concept Inventory cannot be downloaded because the authors could not access it themselves. (Adrian Madsen et al. (2017))
- **PhysPort website for research-based physics teaching and assessment resources**: PhysPort (www.physport.org) is a website, jointly produced by AAPT and Kansas State University and funded by NSF, that helps physics educators "learn to apply research-based teaching and assessment in their classrooms". It is organized around Expert Recommendations, Teaching, Assessments, Workshops, and the PhysPort Data Explorer, including guides to over 50 research-based teaching methods, over 80 research-based assessments, video workshops, curricula, and podcasts. Originally called the PER User's Guide, it was first funded in 2009 and released in November 2011. (Sarah B. McKagan et al. (2020))
