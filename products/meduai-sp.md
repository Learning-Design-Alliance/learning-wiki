## Description
MeduAI-SP is a web-based multi-agent LLM standardized-patient training platform developed by Yang et al. for medical students to practise clinical interviewing, reasoning, and empathic communication through simulated encounters and automated tutoring and evaluation.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **AI-driven digital standardized patients (AI-SPs) as a conceptual framework for generative AI–empowered medical education**: The article defines AI-SP as "a digital interactive agent driven by generative AI, possessing stable personality traits and affective responsiveness, capable of engaging in multimodal, context-sensitive natural conversations with medical learners in repeatable, controllable simulated environments, and supporting parameterized configuration of clinical scenarios." The framework positions AI-SPs as an extension of traditional standardized patients rather than a replacement, elaborated across four dimensions: system architecture, multimodal interaction, personality modeling, and ethical considerations. It is intended for communication training, humanistic education, and emotional engagement in medical curricula. (Jia Chuanlin; Qi Lulu (2026))
- **Cerebral cortex–inspired multiagent framework with 5 specialized subagents for SP simulation**: The article proposes a cerebral cortex–inspired multiagent system (MAS) framework designed to simulate standardized patient (SP) clinical training behaviors. It maps 5 specialized subagents to brain-region functions: a character mapping agent for role traits and motivational states, a memory management agent for contextual encoding and retrieval, an information processing agent for query interpretation, a language generation agent for coherent responses, and a rethinking agent for performance monitoring and error detection. The framework operates in 3 stages—patient construction, diagnostic reasoning, and response generation—and incorporates persistent memory mechanisms to ensure dialogue coherence across conversational turns. (Qu Yufei et al. (2026))
