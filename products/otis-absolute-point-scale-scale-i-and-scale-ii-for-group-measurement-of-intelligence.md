# Otis Absolute Point Scale (Scale I and Scale II) for group measurement of intelligence

## Description
Arthur S. Otis's two-form group intelligence test for pupils in grades 3 through 8, measuring general intelligence and mental age through ten subtests per form.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Absolute Point Scale: ten objective tests compiled in duplicate forms for group measurement of general mental ability**: The scale is a battery of ten tests—Spelling, Arithmetic, Synonyms and Antonyms, Memory for Digits, Proverbs, Disarranged Sentences, Relation, Geometric, Following Directions, and Narrative Completion—compiled in duplicate. As printed: "The scale was compiled in duplicate. There were, in other words, two complete tests of each kind." The two forms of each kind were made as alike as possible without using the same material, and the ideas were drawn chiefly from the Stanford Revision of the Binet Scale, with the remaining tests designed especially for this study. (Otis (1918))
