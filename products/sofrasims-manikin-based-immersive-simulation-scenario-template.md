# SoFraSimS manikin-based immersive simulation scenario template

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A five-section scenario-design template developed and validated by the SoFraSimS expert group for healthcare simulation educators creating manikin-based immersive simulation scenarios.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Six parts required to adequately describe a simulation scenario, derived from the literature and published templates**: From their literature review and analysis of published templates, the authors identify six required parts of a scenario description: a complete layout with a concise title; practical elements; a well-identified learner population with curriculum context; clear, limited, observable and measurable learning objectives for technical and non-technical competencies; all material and human resources for an authentic case; and a detailed session timeline including a section suggesting improvements for future sessions. The article reports that "the literature review and the various published templates highlighted 6 parts required to adequately describe a scenario." (Der Sahakian Guillaume et al (2024))
- **Natural feedback: formalizing learning objectives as observable behaviors triggered by scenario events with anticipated impact on the simulation**: A scenario-writing pattern in which each learning objective is linked to observable learner behaviors, each behavior is triggered by an event embedded in the scenario, and the expected impact of those behaviors on the subsequent course of the simulation is scripted in advance. The article states the template "must describe the triggering events ('triggers') embedded within the scenario, which allow the emergence of the observable behaviors" and their expected impact (natural feedback). This aims to guarantee maximum control over the simulation experience for psychological safety and attainment of objectives. (Der Sahakian Guillaume et al (2024))
