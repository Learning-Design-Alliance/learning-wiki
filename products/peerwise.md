## Description
PeerWise is a freely available online tool developed at the University of Auckland that enables students to author, answer, rate, comment on, and follow peers' multiple-choice questions.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **PeerWise online tool for student-authored multiple-choice question repositories**: PeerWise is the technology platform used in this study's interventions. The article describes it as "a freely-available, online tool to facilitate cohorts of students writing their own MCQs and answering and commenting on those of their peers", incorporating social functionality such as rating, commenting and following contributors. Developed at the University of Auckland, at the time of writing it had nearly 100,000 student registrants, around 600,000 questions and approximately 12 million answers. (Bates et al. (2013))

## Description
PeerWise is the technology platform used in this study's interventions. The article describes it as "a freely-available, online tool to facilitate cohorts of students writing their own MCQs and answering and commenting on those of their peers", incorporating social functionality such as rating, commenting and following contributors. Developed at the University of Auckland, at the time of writing it had nearly 100,000 student registrants, around 600,000 questions and approximately 12 million answers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students contribute at minimum one authored question, answer five others, and rate and comment on three more per activity; a built-in scoring system can serve as the basis for assessment marks.
#### Constraints
- The system does not require the explanation field to be completed before submission; in this study explanations were encouraged as a required and important part of developing a question.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- question authoring, peer answering, commenting and rating as formative assessment activity
