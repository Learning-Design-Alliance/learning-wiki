# UN-affiliated Regional Centres for Space Science and Technology Education

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A network of UN-affiliated regional university education centres that delivers space-science and technology curricula for students and researchers in developing nations.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **UN-affiliated Regional Centres for Space Science and Technology Education offering nine-month postgraduate courses in four disciplines**: A network of regional centres established under the UN Programme on Space Applications in India (Asia and the Pacific), Morocco and Nigeria (Africa), Brazil and Mexico (Latin America and the Caribbean), and Jordan (Western Asia). Each centre "provides postgraduate education, research, and application programmes with emphasis on (i) remote sensing and geographic information system, (ii) satellite communications, (iii) satellite meteorology and global climate, and (iv) space and atmospheric science for university educators and research and application scientists." Since 1996 the centres have implemented nine-month postgraduate courses based on UN model curricula. (Hans J. Haubold (2002))
- **UN model-curriculum approach: discipline curricula grounded in physics, mathematics and engineering rather than tailored to specific missions**: An organizing approach for the centres' education curricula, developed by a 1995 UN Meeting of Experts in Granada and reviewed at regional and international educational meetings since 1995. The updated syllabuses "are based on physics, mathematics and engineering as taught in many universities around the world" and "are not tailored to any specific space-related project or mission." The approach was designed to address variation in education between nations and institutions, which had led to differences in course content and modes of presentation; the Frascati Meeting noted the model curricula had contributed to resolving such problems. (Hans J. Haubold (2002))
