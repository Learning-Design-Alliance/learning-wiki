## Description
Mentira (http://www.mentira.org) is a mobile game developed by Holden and Sykes for learners of Spanish, in which students collaboratively interact to solve a crime mystery in a virtual neighborhood by investigating and talking with members of their own and other families. Learners' interaction with the game required pragmatically appropriate information, and "Students were corrected by an online character when they selected speech that was inappropriate for the context," such as informal direct speech to a high-status character. The review reports the game was an effective tool to engage learners with sociopragmatic features of the language.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must interact with the game using pragmatically appropriate information suited to context and interlocutor status
#### Constraints
- The review notes that programming and developing online pragmatics games for individual groups of learners may not be feasible and affordable for all educators

### Target Learners
- learners of Spanish as a second language

### Target Learning Goals
- sociopragmatic appropriateness of speech in context
