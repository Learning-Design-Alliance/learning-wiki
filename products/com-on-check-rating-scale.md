## Description
A structured observer-rating instrument developed for simulated medical consultations to assess learners' delivery of difficult news and handling of patient emotions using SPIKES and NURSE components.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **COM-ON-Check observation framework grounded in the SPIKES six-step bad-news protocol with the NURSE emotion-handling model integrated**: The article structures its observer feedback tasks around a named assessment framework: the COM-ON-Check Rating Scale, which the article states is "theoretisch auf dem SPIKES-Modell zur Übermittlung schlechter Nachrichten aufgebaut". SPIKES comprises six steps (Setting, Perception, Invitation, Knowledge, Strategy and Summary) for structuring difficult conversations. The NURSE model of Back et al. is integrated into the observation tasks to guide handling of emotions: Naming, Understanding, Respecting, Supporting, Exploring. In this study these frameworks operationalize what peer observers watch for during simulated consultations and what feedback the practising student receives. (Gür Deniz et al. (2024))
- **ComOn check: a six-domain rating scale with 13 items on a five-point Likert scale for assessing medical students' general communication skills**: The ComOn check is a rating scale for evaluating general communication skills of medical students in doctor-patient encounters, developed over ten steps from prior oncology-specific checklists plus literature-derived items. As the authors state, "our checklist consists of a global rating and 12 specific items" covering initiating conversation, patient's perception, conversation structure, patient's emotions, end of conversation, and general communication skills, each rated 0 (poor) to 4 (excellent). It was designed for on-the-spot ratings within 5-10 minutes and for use in research and OSCE evaluation. (Radziej K et al. (2017))
- **ComOn check's design rationale: operationalizing observable general communication skills while balancing efficiency, detail, and psychometric quality**: The article articulates a framework of seven design requirements for communication assessment instruments: general application, efficiency, detailed evaluation, rated behavior, specific setting, psychometric properties, and qualified raters. The authors state the aim "to integrate the issues of general application, efficiency, purpose, detailed evaluation, setting, good psychometric properties, and qualified raters." The framework responds to shortcomings of existing interaction analysis systems, which are resource-intensive and often transcript-only, and to extensive checklists that lack a balance between efficacy and informative value. It operationalizes good communication through observable behaviors anchored to literature-derived models such as SPIKES, the Langewitz book metaphor, and the NURSE model. (Radziej K et al. (2017))
