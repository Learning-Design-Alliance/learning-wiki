# Early Education Essentials

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
Early Ed Essentials is a teacher- and parent-survey assessment system developed by the Ounce of Prevention Fund and the University of Chicago Consortium to measure organizational supports in school- and center-based early-childhood education settings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Early Ed Essentials teacher and parent survey measurement system**: The Early Education Essential Organizational Supports measurement system (Early Ed Essentials) is a set of teacher and parent surveys developed by the Ounce of Prevention Fund and the UChicago Consortium "to measure the organizational supports of school and center-based ECE settings." Its purpose is to give programs reliable, valid data to diagnose strengths and weaknesses in organizational conditions so staff can strengthen the conditions that enable effective teaching. The original teacher survey included 26 measures and 164 items; the original parent survey included 9 measures and 54 items. (Ehrlich et al. (2018))
- **Five Essentials—Early Education surveys: teacher and parent surveys measuring five organizational constructs in pre-k settings**: The Five Essentials—Early Education is a single survey tool measuring the five organizational constructs (effective instructional leaders, collaborative teachers, involved families, supportive environments, ambitious instruction) through the perceptions of teachers and parents in publicly-funded programs serving three- and four-year-olds. The teacher survey draws heavily on the K-12 5Essentials teacher survey "in use for over 20 years," while the parent survey was created anew. Questions are combined into measures analyzed with the Rasch IRT model, modeled after the K-12 5Essentials approach. (Ehrlich et al. (2016))

## Description
The Early Education Essential Organizational Supports measurement system (Early Ed Essentials) is a set of teacher and parent surveys developed by the Ounce of Prevention Fund and the UChicago Consortium "to measure the organizational supports of school and center-based ECE settings." Its purpose is to give programs reliable, valid data to diagnose strengths and weaknesses in organizational conditions so staff can strengthen the conditions that enable effective teaching. The original teacher survey included 26 measures and 164 items; the original parent survey included 9 measures and 54 items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must have at least three pre-k classrooms and at least six teachers so site-level scores are precise and respondent anonymity is protected
- A minimum of six educator or parent responses per site to protect anonymity and reduce imprecision of site-level measure scores
#### Constraints
- Designed for part- or full-day, publicly funded early education programs serving preschool children; child care and preschool programs without public early education funding were excluded

### Target Learners
- teachers and teaching assistants in pre-k programs
- parents of preschool-age children

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing organizational strengths and weaknesses to design improvement strategies that support effective teaching and children's learning

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designed for publicly-funded center-based programs (school-based or community-based) serving three- and four-year-old children; measures are constructed from survey items analyzed via the Rasch model
#### Constraints
- Intended for center-based services, not home-based services; the parent survey is intended for any adult caregiver, and the teacher survey is being tested with other pre-k staff

### Target Learners
- Three- and four-year-old children in publicly-funded pre-k programs (Head Start, state-, and locally-funded)

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring organizational conditions that support instructional quality and kindergarten readiness
