Pennsylvania teacher evaluation pilot dataset¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 6 studies (5 associational, 1 design), q1–q3 · 1 of 6 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A dataset covering teacher-observation and value-added evaluation measures for 6,676 teachers across 269 Pennsylvania districts during the 2012–2013 pilot and comparison period, assembled for the Pennsylvania teacher-evaluation pilot.

Components¶

  • Pennsylvania teacher evaluation pilot dataset covering 6,676 teachers in 269 districts across two school years: The report analyzed pilot data on two teacher performance measures: the observation-based FFT and value-added measures based on student test scores. The data cover "6,676 teachers from 269 districts in the state of Pennsylvania, including Pittsburgh public schools", spanning the 2012-2013 school year with comparison data from 2011-2012 from a previous pilot phase. (Stephen Lipscomb et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • Rating distributions and FFT-VAM relationships were broadly similar across the two pilot phases, though top-two-category ratings decreased somewhat in Pittsburgh [+W]
  • FFT scores showed acceptable-to-good internal consistency, with the overall PPR score more internally consistent than any single domain score [+W]
  • FFT-based teacher ratings in Pennsylvania's pilot were overwhelmingly concentrated in the top two performance categories, with less than 0.1 percent rated failing [+W]
  • FFT scores correlated positively with value-added measures, with domain correlations ranging from 0.19 to 0.22 and generally statistically significant [+W]
  • Mathematica developed and estimated VAMs for use in Phase 1 of the Pennsylvania evaluation pilot [+W]
  • Low observation scores are even more concentrated in high-poverty schools than low value-added scores [~W]
  • Observation scores are modestly positively associated with individual value-added (adjusted correlation 0.349) but only weakly associated with performance tasks (0.108) and school-wide literacy value-added (0.119) [~W]
  • Observation scores have a stronger relationship with school characteristics such as poverty than value-added scores [~W]
