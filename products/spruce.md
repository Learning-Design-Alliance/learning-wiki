## Description
SPRUCE is an assessment instrument for introductory physics laboratory students covering sources and handling of measurement uncertainty, repeated-measurement statistics, and model–data comparisons.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Four assessment-objective categories organizing measurement uncertainty content**: SPRUCE's assessment objectives are organized into "four main areas of concepts and practices": Sources of uncertainty (estimating size of uncertainty and identifying ways to reduce it), Handling of uncertainty (propagation and significant digits), Distributions and repeated measurements (mean, standard deviation, standard error, importance of multiple measurements), and Modeling (comparisons between explicit externalized models and data). These categories "resemble the dimensions and concepts developed to model MU content in secondary science education." The modeling category was excluded from SPRUCE's scope because its assessment objectives pertained primarily to explicit model-data comparisons. (Michael Vignal et al. (2023))
- **Assessment Objectives as measurable constructs structuring a laboratory assessment**: Within SPRUCE's Evidence-Centered Design, Assessment Objectives (AOs) serve as the constructs the assessment aims to measure, analogous to course learning goals. The article defines AOs as "concise, specific articulations of measurable desired student performances regarding concepts and/or practices targeted by the assessment." AOs were developed with input from introductory laboratory instructors about which aspects of measurement uncertainty they deem important, and each assessment item addresses at least one AO, focusing the instrument's scope on instructor-valued topics. (Gayle Geschwind et al. (2023))
