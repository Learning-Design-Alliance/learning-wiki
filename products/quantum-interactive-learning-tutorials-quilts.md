# Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs)

## Description
Chandralekha Singh's QuILTs are research-based interactive tutorial materials being developed for graduate quantum-mechanics instruction to connect formalism with conceptual understanding.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) under development for graduate quantum concepts**: QuILTs are instructional materials the author is "currently developing and assessing" in response to the documented difficulties. They "actively engage students in the learning process and help them build links between the abstract formalism and the conceptual aspects of quantum physics without compromising the technical content." (Chandralekha Singh (2016))
- **Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) for advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics**: Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) are research-based tutorials for one- or two-semester undergraduate quantum mechanics courses that target documented student difficulties and misconceptions. The article states that they "exploit computer-based visualization tools and help students build links between the formal and conceptual aspects of quantum physics without compromising the technical content." QuILTs create an active learning environment in which students confront misconceptions and draw qualitative inferences from quantitative tools, and they can serve as lecture supplements or self-study tools. (Chandralekha Singh (2016))
- **DPT QuILT: a research-based Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial on degenerate perturbation theory**: The DPT QuILT is a research-based guided-inquiry tutorial developed from a cognitive task analysis and documented student difficulties, iterated with three physics faculty members and tested in think-aloud interviews. "The QuILT uses an inquiry-based approach to learning and actively engages students in the learning process." It includes a pretest, small-group or self-paced tutorial work, and a posttest, with predictions, scaffolding, feedback, and checkpoints for reflection. Examples are restricted to a three-dimensional Hilbert space with two-fold degeneracy to keep calculations manageable. (Christof Keebaugh et al. (2017))

### Claims
- [Working through the time-development QuILT improved average performance from 53% to 85% on pre-/post-tests in an advanced quantum mechanics course](../claims/quilt-time-development-pre-post-gain.md) [+M]
- [Working through the Mach-Zehnder interferometer QuILT improved average performance from 48% to 83%, with all but one of 12 students earning perfect scores on three polarizer questions](../claims/quilt-mach-zehnder-pre-post-gain.md) [+M]
- [Students incorrectly use the diagonal elements of the perturbation H′ to decide whether degenerate perturbation theory is needed](../claims/students-focus-on-h-prime-diagonals-for-degeneracy.md) [+M]
- [Students wrongly judge a basis good from completeness of eigenstates of H0 alone, or by examining only H0 or only H′](../claims/students-misjudge-good-basis-from-eigenstates-alone.md) [+M]
- [Roughly half of students diagonalize the entire H′ matrix instead of only its degenerate subspace when finding a good basis](../claims/students-diagonalize-entire-h-prime-matrix.md) [+M]
