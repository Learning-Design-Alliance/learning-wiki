# Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
Chandralekha Singh's QuILTs are research-based interactive tutorial materials being developed for graduate quantum-mechanics instruction to connect formalism with conceptual understanding.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) under development for graduate quantum concepts**: QuILTs are instructional materials the author is "currently developing and assessing" in response to the documented difficulties. They "actively engage students in the learning process and help them build links between the abstract formalism and the conceptual aspects of quantum physics without compromising the technical content." (Chandralekha Singh (2016))
- **Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) for advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics**: Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) are research-based tutorials for one- or two-semester undergraduate quantum mechanics courses that target documented student difficulties and misconceptions. The article states that they "exploit computer-based visualization tools and help students build links between the formal and conceptual aspects of quantum physics without compromising the technical content." QuILTs create an active learning environment in which students confront misconceptions and draw qualitative inferences from quantitative tools, and they can serve as lecture supplements or self-study tools. (Chandralekha Singh (2016))
- **DPT QuILT: a research-based Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial on degenerate perturbation theory**: The DPT QuILT is a research-based guided-inquiry tutorial developed from a cognitive task analysis and documented student difficulties, iterated with three physics faculty members and tested in think-aloud interviews. "The QuILT uses an inquiry-based approach to learning and actively engages students in the learning process." It includes a pretest, small-group or self-paced tutorial work, and a posttest, with predictions, scaffolding, feedback, and checkpoints for reflection. Examples are restricted to a three-dimensional Hilbert space with two-fold degeneracy to keep calculations manageable. (Christof Keebaugh et al. (2017))
- **Double-slit experiment QuILT: research-validated guided inquiry tutorial with interactive simulation**: The DSE QuILT is a research-validated tutorial on the double-slit experiment with single particles sent one at a time through the slits, including a monochromatic lamp between slits and screen. It consists of "a pretest, a warm up, a main tutorial, an associated homework component, and a post test." The warm up and main tutorial use a computer simulation in which students manipulate the DSE setup (particle type, energy, slit width and separation, photon wavelength, lamp intensity) and observe the resulting screen pattern. Students predict outcomes, check predictions against the simulation, and reconcile any differences with scaffolding support. Development involved approximately 85 hours of individual think-aloud interviews and iteration with five physics faculty members. (Ryan Sayer et al. (2017))
- **MZI and DSE Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs)**: Two research-based interactive tutorials on the Mach-Zehnder interferometer and double-slit experiment, part of the Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial series. The QuILTs "use a guided inquiry-based approach to learning and often include interactive simulations" in which students make predictions, check them with simulations, and receive guidance to reconcile differences. Development included roughly 85 hours of individual think-aloud interviews for the DSE QuILT alone and consultation with five physics faculty members. Both are available to instructors on request. (Alexandru Maries et al. (2017))
- **Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial (QuILT) on strong and weak field Zeeman effects using DPT**: A Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial (QuILT) developed by the authors as a learning tool to help students develop a functional understanding of the corrections to the hydrogen atom energy spectrum due to the Zeeman effect. The article states "We used the difﬁculties as resources in developing a Quan- tum Interactive Learning Tutorial (QuILT)" and that "The initial results from the QuILT are encouraging." The difficulties documented in the written questions and interviews served as the design input for the tutorial. (Emily Marshman et al. (2020))
