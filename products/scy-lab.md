## Description
SCY-Lab is a digital learning environment developed by the SCY project that supports collaborative inquiry through mission-based activities, writing tools, annotation tools, and information resources.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **SCY-Lab forensic mission digital learning environment with designated support tools**: SCY-Lab is a digital learning environment from the SCY project containing tools supporting conceptual understanding and inquiry skills. Its forensic mission is "a highly structured environment featuring designated digital support tools" where most tools are text editors with "directive questions or sentence openers". In this study three tools scaffolded annotation of DNA profiles, writing conclusions, and producing an official forensic report, alongside information resources such as a forensics handbook and DNA animations. (Anniken Furberg (2016))
- **SCY pedagogical scenarios: reusable blueprints assembled from activity-ELO-tool triplets, LASs, and scenario templates**: Pedagogical scenarios are the SCY project's formalized instructional design method for building technology-enhanced learning environments. A scenario "presents the learning process as an organized assembly of elementary learning processes, each associated with a specific learning object and a tool for creating this learning object." The team defined 53 learning activities, each paired with the ELO it produces and a software tool; these triplets were clustered into 13 Learning Activity Spaces (Orientation, Management, Information, Conceptualization, Debate, Reflection, Analysis, Designing, Building, Experimentation, Evaluation, Regulation, Reporting), and 12 scenarios were defined as different assemblages of these LASs. Designers select a scenario and then add domain content; the SCY technical infrastructure instantiates it without programming. (Using scenarios to design complex technology-enhanced learning environments)
- **SCY mission interface: ELO-centered workspace with drawers and four corner services**: The SCY mission interface places the ELO under development at the center, with the creation tool integrated into the ELO and support facilities in extendable 'drawer' tabs to the sides (assignment, resources, feedback request, tagging, and an ELO-tied chat). Four services occupy the screen corners: an awareness service reporting on peer presence, an ePortfolio service for saving ELOs and requesting teacher assessment, a repository service for saving, searching, and sharing ELOs, and a mission map for navigation. The mission map "suggest to students a specific route through the mission, but students are free to diverge from that." Missions are structured at three levels: mission map, LASs, and ELOs. (Using scenarios to design complex technology-enhanced learning environments)
- **SCY design approach relative to ADDIE: collapsed phases with scenario selection early and infrastructure-driven implementation**: The article positions the SCY instructional design approach against traditional sequential ISD models labeled ADDIE (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation). It states that "All components of the ADDIE model are incorporated in the SCY approach, with defining learning goals in the beginning and implementation at the end," but with different emphases and collapsed steps: an existing pedagogical scenario is chosen quickly, learning goals are completed early partly from what the scenario offers, parts of design, development, and implementation are done together, the infrastructure takes care of implementation, and evaluation is realized formatively through rapid prototyping and summatively through ongoing experimental and field studies. The approach is characterized as providing templates or blueprints that designers adapt and fill with content, resembling ADAPT IT and the SimQuest authoring approach at a more detailed level. (Using scenarios to design complex technology-enhanced learning environments)
