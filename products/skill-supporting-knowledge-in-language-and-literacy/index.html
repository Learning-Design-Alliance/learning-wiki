SKILL (Supporting Knowledge in Language and Literacy)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q4 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

A structured elementary literacy intervention developed by Gillam and colleagues for improving narrative comprehension, production, and literate language in children with language and literacy difficulties.

Components¶

  • SKILL's theoretical grounding: ACT-R, the Embedded Processes model of working memory, and the Construction-Integration model: The SKILL program was designed from three cognitive models of how information is stored, activated, and used in comprehension and production: the Adaptive Control of Thought (ACT-R) model, the Embedded Processes model of working memory, and the Construction-Integration (CI) model. In all three, discourse comprehension and production are multilevel processes drawing on object, event, and language knowledge in associative networks in long-term memory. ACT-R frames story structure knowledge as "chunks" that "become increasingly large and complex as a function of experience listening to and telling more complex stories"; the CI model describes integration into a situation model. The three phases of SKILL were designed to transfer this orientation into learning activities building organizational frameworks in episodic memory. (Gillam et al. (2022))

Claims¶

  • SKILL intervention significantly improves oral narrative comprehension and production immediately after treatment in at-risk Grade 1–4 students [+M]
