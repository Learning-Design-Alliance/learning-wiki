# Programmatic assessment

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A centrally coordinated health-professions assessment framework that aggregates predominantly formative evidence, supports continuous feedback and remediation, and periodically informs summative progression decisions.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Programmatic assessment as a centrally coordinated, feedback-centred assessment system**: The article describes programmatic assessment as an innovative approach that applies current assessment principles so that assessment maximizes learning while supporting robust progress decisions. It features centralized institutional coordination, emphasis on formative assessment, individual events that "are not intended for decision making for approval or failure purposes", portfolio-based aggregation of predominantly formative data, periodic review for summative decisions, remediation plans, and continuous student–mentor dialogue. Quality criteria include validity, reliability, acceptability and positive educational effects. (Flauzino Rodrigo Humberto et al. (2025))
- **Base progression on performance stability rather than passing single tests (full programmatic assessment)**: The article advocates full programmatic assessment, "the ultimate expression of assessment for learning", in which all assessments are deliberately designed and integrated to develop and demonstrate each learning outcome, with individual assessments melting away into data on learner performance. In this paradigm, "progression is based on performance stability and not on passing single tests", aligning progression decisions with real-world workplace needs. (Frans A. Oliehoek et al. (2017))
- **Programmatic assessment integrates frequent formative feedback with summative decisions to optimise the learning function of assessment**: The article adopts programmatic assessment as an integral design of an assessment program that optimises its learning function, decision-making function and curriculum quality-assurance function. Formative assessments serve as learning activities while summative assessments support high-stakes pass/fail decisions. Frequent feedback on products, ways of working and team process gives students and teachers a clear picture of progress. (Pum Walters et al. (2021))
