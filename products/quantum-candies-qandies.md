# Quantum candies (qandies)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A superposition-free pedagogical model that uses colored and flavored candies to represent qubits and introduce complementarity, no-cloning, entanglement, and quantum key distribution to high-school students and the general public.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Correlated qandies and pseudo-entanglement as a model of quantum entanglement**: The article extends the qandy model to multi-qandy systems with a new machine generating four types of correlated pairs ({φ+}, {ψ+}, {φ−}, {ψ−}), in which each qandy in the pair can no longer be assigned a definite taste or color and only the correlations are defined. For {φ+}, "if the same test-action is performed on both qandies, the outcomes are always random but identical." These qandies possess what the authors call pseudo-entanglement, partially mimicking quantum entanglement's non-classical correlations between spatially separated systems. (Junan Lin et al. (2020))
- **The quantum candies (qandy) model as an intuitive pedagogical model for quantum information concepts**: The qandy model represents quantum systems as candies with two general properties, color (red or green) and taste (chocolate or vanilla), of which each qandy has only one specific property. As the article states, "if one looks at a qandy, it will appear as red or green, but one cannot taste it anymore" and tasting destroys the color. A qandy-making machine has four buttons ({C},{V},{R},{G}) and fixes only one property per candy. The article investigates, extends, and much expands this model, originally suggested by Kayla Jacobs, as an explanatory tool for quantum bits, complementarity, no-cloning, and quantum cryptography protocols. (Junan Lin et al. (2020))
- **Jacobs' qandy machine: candies with complementary color and taste properties modeling quantum uncertainty**: Kayla Jacobs' qandy model uses candies with two properties, color {Red, Green} and taste {Vanilla, Chocolate}, produced by a machine with four buttons. "Once a color is chosen, the taste is random" and vice versa, so a person cannot correctly guess both at one instant, which the article equates to the complementary principle in quantum physics. The model maps classical bits to color and taste pairs and was originally used to teach the BB84 quantum key distribution protocol to school kids. (Nikhitha Nunavath et al. (2024))
- **Lin–Mor correlated qandy pairs as pseudo-entangled states for illustrating entanglement-based protocols**: Lin and Mor extended Jacobs' model with a qandy machine that generates four types of correlated qandy pairs, denoted {ϕ+}, {ψ+}, {ϕ−}, {ψ−}. These pairs "acts as pseudo-entangled qandies and can be used for teleportation," mimicking quantum entanglement: measured individually they give random outcomes, but jointly they show correlations such as identical or opposite colors and tastes depending on the pair type. The article states correlated qandies are used for sharing pseudo-entanglement between communicating parties in QKD protocols. (Nikhitha Nunavath et al. (2024))
- **Lin–Mor–Shapira qandy pseudo-Bell measurement machine distinguishing correlated qandy pairs without looking or tasting**: The pseudo-Bell measurement is modeled as a black box that "distinguishes all the four correlated qandy pairs without looking or tasting at it." The machine's outputs are defined by rules: pairs prepared with identical colors map to {ϕ+} or {ϕ−} with same probabilities, identical tastes to {ϕ+} or {ψ+}, opposite colors to {ψ+} or {ψ−}, opposite tastes to {ϕ−} or {ψ−}, and a definite-color/definite-taste combination yields all four states with equal probabilities. In the teleportation protocol Alice sends her qandies to this black box to perform the measurement step. (Nikhitha Nunavath et al. (2024))
- **The quantum candies (qandies) analogy framework for explaining quantum information concepts to non-specialists**: Qandies are candies with special properties used as a quantum-classical analogy: "Quantum Candies or Qandies provide us with a lucid way of understanding the concepts of quantum information and quantum science in the language of candies." The framework maps quantum bits to candies whose color and taste cannot both be discovered simultaneously, mimicking uncertainty relations. It has been used for quantum cryptography concepts and, in this article, is extended to teleportation. The authors state the model is motivated by public funding of research, so "common people ought to know at-least some part of what the researchers are currently exploring." (Nikhitha Nunavath et al. (2024))
- **Qandy gates: color-switching and taste-switching gates and a qandy CNOT gate modeling quantum gates**: The article defines qandy gates as operations that "perform some qandy operation with respect to input qandies," with different qandies reacting differently to the same gate. Single-qandy gates are a color-switching gate and a taste-switching gate, "representing the Pauli X gate and Pauli Z gate in quantum computing," with input-output conditions given in Table I (e.g., X switches {R} to {G} while Z leaves it unchanged). A qandy CNOT taste-gate switches the taste of a target qandy when the control qandy is in the {V} state. (Nikhitha Nunavath et al. (2024))
