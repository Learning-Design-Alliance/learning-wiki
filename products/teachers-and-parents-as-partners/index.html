Teachers and Parents as Partners¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), q3 · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A University of Nebraska–Lincoln family-school partnership intervention in which a school specialist, teacher, and parent jointly identify needs, set goals, implement an individualized plan, and monitor student progress.

Components¶

  • Teachers and Parents as Partners (TAPP / Conjoint Behavioral Consultation): TAPP is a family-school partnership intervention developed at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln that addresses children's behavioral and academic challenges through an individualized approach. "Implementation of TAPP involves three meetings between an identified school specialist, teacher and parent with each meeting averaging 45-50 minutes," in which the specialist leads needs identification, goal setting, joint plan implementation, and progress monitoring. It has been extensively studied and implemented in rural communities where specialized support services are hard to access. (Teachers et al. (2012))
  • Conjoint Behavioral Consultation (CBC) as a strength-based, cross-system problem-solving model: CBC (also known as Teachers and Parents as Partners) is defined as "a strength-based, cross-system problem-solving and decision-making model wherein parents, teachers, and other caregivers or service providers work as partners and share responsibility for promoting positive and consistent outcomes" for children. Its theory of change holds that problematic behaviors ameliorate through a positive parent-teacher relationship operating via structured, collaborative, data-based problem solving, with evidence-based interventions implemented consistently across home and school. Parents and teachers serve as joint consultees with consultant support. (Sheridan et al. (2017))
  • Conjoint behavioral consultation (CBC) four-stage collaborative problem-solving intervention: CBC is a partnership-centered, indirect, ecological intervention connecting home and school for students with social–behavioral challenges or academic delays. It uses "a four-stage collaborative problem-solving format composed of conjoint problem identification, problem analysis, plan implementation, and plan evaluation," delivered by school-based consultants working with parents and teachers as partners. In this trial, an average of four structured meetings were held over about eight weeks, with home visits, classroom visits, daily home–school notes, and interventions built from positive reinforcement, antecedent control, skills training, and reductive techniques plus a home–school communication component. (S. Andrew Garbacz et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • TAPP improves school-related behavioral problems per teacher ratings in the rural trial (effect size = +0.45) [+W]
  • In a rural randomized trial, TAPP strengthens both teacher-reported and parent-reported family-school relationships [+W]
  • CBC improves teacher-reported school problems among rural K-3 students with disruptive behaviors more than business as usual [+M]
  • The teacher-parent relationship improves under CBC and partially mediates CBC effects on several student outcomes [+M]
  • CBC's benefit for teacher-reported social skills is greater for dyads with average or low congruent communication, with no significant CBC effect at high congruence [+M]
