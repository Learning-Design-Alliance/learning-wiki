# Classroom Observation Protocol for Undergraduate STEM (COPUS)

## Description
COPUS is a structured, non-judgmental classroom observation protocol and recording spreadsheet developed for observing undergraduate STEM teaching and student behaviors in timed intervals.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **COPUS classroom observation protocol**: The Classroom Observation Protocol for Undergraduate STEM (COPUS) is a structured observation instrument used in this study to record classroom behavior. "COPUS consists of 12 instructor and 13 student codes, for which the observer marks whether the coded behavior occurred or not" during two-minute intervals, with an activity counted if it occurs for at least five seconds. The authors chose it for its short training requirement, strong inter-rater reliability, and non-judgmental codes, and compiled code marks into COPUS profiles as percentages of class time. (Commeford et al. (2020))
- **COPUS bar-chart profiles as a measure of class-time distribution in active learning classrooms**: The Classroom Observation Protocol for Undergraduate STEM (COPUS) consists of "12 instructor and 13 student codes, for which the observer marks whether the coded behavior occurred or not during two-minute intervals". The CALEP project compiled these marks into COPUS profiles using a 'bar chart' method: summing marks per code column and dividing by the number of observation intervals, yielding the percentage of class time each code was present. The authors argue this shows the fraction of class time engaged in a method, unlike the 'pie chart' method of Smith et al. (Kelley Commeford et al. (2021))
- **COPUS-based fidelity measurement combining descriptive fractions and classroom observation networks**: The study uses the Classroom Observation Protocol for Undergraduate STEM (COPUS), "a structured observation protocol with 25 specified codes to characterize both instructor (12 codes) and student (13 codes) behaviors in the classroom", with observers checking codes in two-minute intervals. Fidelity is quantified two complementary ways: cosine similarity of fractions of class time per code (duration fidelity) and cosine similarity of weighted edge vectors in classroom observation networks where nodes are COPUS codes and directed edges are chronological transitions (transition fidelity). De-identified data and analysis scripts are available in a referenced repository. (Bukola et al. (2025))
