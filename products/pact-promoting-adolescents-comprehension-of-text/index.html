PACT (Promoting Adolescents' Comprehension of Text)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

PACT is a social-studies curriculum package developed by Jeanne Wanzek and colleagues that integrates supported text-based learning, discourse, and application practices into middle-school content units.

Components¶

  • PACT (Promoting Adolescents' Comprehension of Text) instructional practices for middle school social studies: PACT is a set of text-based instructional practices integrated within social studies content units. It is "intended to be integrated within social studies content units to increase engagement of all students in supported text-based learning, discourse-based processes, and application of newly learned content." In this study teachers delivered three 10-day U.S. history units using semi-scripted lesson plans, with coaches supporting implementation at least weekly. The program was evaluated in a class-level randomized trial against typical instruction using the same content. (Jeanne Wanzek et al. (2019))
  • Modified PACT intervention for eighth-grade social studies with English learners: PACT (Promoting Adolescents' Comprehension of Text) is a package of instructional practices modified for English learners by strengthening academic vocabulary teaching and peer discourse. The treatment "features three units, blending five components that work together and complement one another": comprehension canopy, essential words, knowledge acquisition through text reading, TBL comprehension check, and TBL knowledge application. It was delivered for approximately 20 weeks in treatment classes during regular social studies time, with the same content taught in comparison classes via business-as-usual instruction. (Vaughn et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Class-level initial background knowledge does not moderate PACT's effect on social studies content knowledge acquisition [+M]
  • Teachers implemented some PACT components at medium-high fidelity but struggled with knowledge acquisition and team-based application components [~M]
  • The modified PACT treatment improved eighth-grade students' social studies content knowledge acquisition relative to business-as-usual comparison classes (ES = .40) [+M]
  • The modified PACT treatment improved content-related reading comprehension (ES = .20) but not general standardized reading comprehension [+M]
