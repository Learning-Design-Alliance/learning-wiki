# 5CardsGame

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A card-based educational game for surgical technologist and other health-professions students, designed to integrate procedural knowledge and support clinical reasoning and retrieval.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Illness script theory and cognitive load theory as the theoretical basis for the 5CardsGame**: The article grounds the 5CardsGame in illness script theory, under which CIP promotes "the integration of knowledge and the consolidation of 'illness scripts'—mental frameworks that help learners organize and retrieve diagnostic information". Cognitive load theory informs the gamified presentation, on the premise that reducing cognitive overload through strategies such as gamification improves retention and engagement. The game aims to help students develop "surgical procedure scripts" synthesizing preoperative care, anatomy, surgical techniques, intraoperative care, and potential complications. (Sadati Leila et al. (2025))
- **Python-based evaluation software that computes the mathematically correct quantum state after each round**: The game includes evaluation software that simulates the quantum circuit formed by the played cards, measures the resulting state, and reports the winning state. "Python codes based on the mathematical framework needed in higher dimensions were exclusively developed for the card game." The software supports Easy, 2d and 3d modes, accepts the starting state and the played gates in sequence, and lets users "define the number of measurements or use the default value of 100"; the winning state is the state measured most often, and the complete pre-measurement state is displayed for inspection. (Lea Kopf et al. (2022))
- **Educationally oriented AI opponent design: rule-based play with strategic randomisation rather than win optimisation**: The article's AI design account holds that a game AI for learning should support educational goals rather than maximise winning. The AI "employs a deterministic rule-based approach with strategic randomisation for educational efficacy", following a rule hierarchy (annihilate opponent target cards, complete its own target, attack opponent cards, place or discard) and randomising low-stakes choices. Randomisation "prevents the AI from having an inherent advantage based on game knowledge that a new player lacks". (M. Attar et al (2025))
