## Description
A timed psycholinguistic assessment instrument that elicits and analyzes learners' spontaneous word associations to diagnose the organization and integration of concepts in a knowledge domain.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Word Association Test as a cognitive-structure diagnostic instrument for needs-assessment-driven curriculum design**: The Word Association Test (WAT) is a psycholinguistic instrument grounded in spreading-activation and associationist models of semantic memory, used here to map the organisational structure of students' AI cognition. The article explains that the test "measures cognitive structure not as the presence or absence of individual concepts but as the pattern of associative connections among them". Early, frequent responses indicate highly integrated concepts; late or rare responses indicate peripheral or fragmented understanding. The study operationalises this through a seven-stage protocol — normalisation to 65 canonical codes, frequency and Z-score banding, Kendall's τ with FDR correction, PROXSCAL multidimensional scaling, and hierarchical threshold network analysis — and links the resulting map directly to micro-credential design decisions. (İsmail Şan et al. (2026))
- **Five-type classification of word-association responses as an index of mental level or abnormality**: Otis proposes a taxonomy of association-test responses into five types: repetition of the stimulus, non-logical responses, sound reactions, more-than-one-word reactions, and normal single-word reactions matching the Kent-Rosanoff frequency tables. A child is assigned to a type only when "50 per cent, of a child's reactions must be clearly of a given type to consider the child as belonging to that type," with mixed types otherwise classified by their two most frequent components. The types are interpreted as marking stages of language development from imitation through sentence-thought to single-word analysis, and are examined for diagnostic and age-test value. (Otis (1915))
- **Kent-Rosanoff 100-word association list with standardized frequency tables**: The Kent-Rosanoff list of 100 stimulus words is a standardized word-association instrument with published frequency tables providing "a standard of comparison in the frequency tables published at the close of their work." Otis used it so that "a comparison with normal reactions could be made," administering the words individually in list order with stop-watch timing. Normal-type (Type V) responses were scored against the Kent-Rosanoff frequency tables, and new frequency tables compiled from the defectives and normal school children were placed on file at the Vineland laboratory. (Otis (1915))
