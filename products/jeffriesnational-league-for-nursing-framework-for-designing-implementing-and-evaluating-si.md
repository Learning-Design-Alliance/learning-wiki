# Jeffries/National League for Nursing Framework for Designing, Implementing and Evaluating Simulations

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A nursing-education framework developed by Jeffries and the National League for Nursing for designing, implementing, and evaluating simulation-based learning experiences.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Jeffries simulation framework as a design structure for simulation-based nursing instruction**: The Jeffries simulation framework is an experiential-learning-based framework for designing simulation-based instruction in nursing education. The article describes it as consisting of "context, background, design, educational practices, simulation experience and outcomes", and notes it is "based on experiential learning theory and is specifically designed for simulation‐based instruction". In this study, the framework structured the design of clinical scenario simulation cases used to train new graduate paediatric surgery nurses over 8 weeks. (Effect of the Jeffries Simulation Framework on the Core Competence of New Graduate Paediat)
- **Jeffries Simulation Theory as the theoretical bridge aligning BOPPPS phases with simulation design**: The study employs Jeffries Simulation Theory, whose core elements the article names as "Background, Design, Simulation Experience, Assessment, and Debriefing", systematically aligned with the six BOPPPS modules to form a closed loop of objective–simulation–reflection. Bridge-in and Objectives map to Background and Design; Pre-assessment refines Design by gauging baseline skills; Participatory Learning embodies the Simulation Experience; Post-assessment and Summary align with Assessment and Debriefing through structured evaluation and facilitated debriefing. The article describes the framework as grounded in experiential learning theory and widely adopted to guide simulation-based instructional design. (Li Mu (2026))
- **Jeffries design model as the methodological framework for scenario construction**: The study adopted the Jeffries design model as its methodological framework for building the simulated scenario, alongside the International Nursing Association for Clinical Simulation and Learning guidelines. The article states the model "works on student support, simulation objectives, problem-solving, fidelity and debriefing", and the INACSL guidelines structured the scenario around "the briefing, scenario in action and debriefing phases" with a basis in interprofessional education. These frameworks shaped the scenario's topics, timing and checklist domains. (Silva Aline Teixeira; Leal Laura Andrian; Ribeiro Maria Inês Lemos Coelho; Hilário Jeniffer Stephanie Marques; da Silva Monise Martins; Alves Mateus Goulart; Januário Gabriela da Cunha; Henriques Sílvia Helena (2025))
- **NLN/Jeffries Simulation Framework as organizing model for satisfaction in simulation-based assessment**: The study structures its survey and analysis around the NLN/Jeffries Simulation Framework, which "includes five components: participant, facilitator, educational practices, simulation design characteristics, and outcomes". The authors operationalized participant characteristics, psychological safety as an educational-practice variable, and satisfaction as the outcome, omitting the facilitator component because all students experienced the same learning conditions. In the framework, "simulation design is identified as one of the key factors contributing to ideal simulation experiences for nursing students". (Kyung Minjung et al. (2025))
- **Jeffries simulation framework for designing, implementing and evaluating simulations as teaching strategies in nursing**: The study was based on the simulation model proposed by Pamela Jeffries, which "provides a framework for designing, implementing, and evaluating simulations used as teaching strategies in nursing" and specifies relevant variables and their relationships to support research identifying effective and ineffective practices. Its key elements span eight areas: simulation design, outcomes and objectives, facilitation, debriefing, participant evaluation, professional integrity, interprofessional education enhanced by simulation, and a simulation glossary. The article used it, with INACSL guidelines, to guide the scenario's design and the teaching-learning process. (Coelho Tatiane da Silva et al. (2025))
- **NLN/Jeffries framework describing simulation design dimensions and educational practices**: The article applies the National League for Nursing (NLN) framework developed by Jeffries, which "underscores the specific features in simulation design and implementation of best educational practices to evaluate and conclude simulation‐based learning in undergraduate nursing education." It is operationalised through two instruments: the 20-item Simulation Design Scale, covering "objectives," "support," "problem‐solving," "feedback," and "fidelity," and the 16-item Educational Practices Questionnaire covering "active learning," "collaboration," "diverse ways of learning," and "expectations." The framework "has been developed based on theoretical and empirical literature to guide the development and assessment of learning processes." (Alsadi et al. (2025))
- **NLN Jeffries Simulation Theory four-step scenario structure (pre-briefing, briefing, scenario, debriefing)**: The article structures its scenarios on the NLN Jeffries Simulation Theory and INACSL guidelines, which conceptualize simulation-based teaching through "the following elements: the facilitator, the student, educational practices, simulation design (which includes objectives, fidelity, support for the participant , problem solving and debriefing) and learning outcomes." For elaboration, four steps were considered: pre-briefing presenting the simulated environment and resources, briefing with vignettes containing sufficient clinical-case data, the clinical simulation scenario itself, and debriefing in which participants reflect on the simulated experience with a facilitator. (Meszaros Mariana de Jesus; de Almeida Angélica Olivetto; da Silva Ruana Luiz Ferreira; Eduardo Aline Helena Appoloni; Lima Maria Helena de Melo; Oliveira-Kumakura Ana Railka de Souza (2023))
- **NLN Jeffries simulation framework with six components guiding high-fidelity simulation design**: The article grounds its instrument in the NLN Jeffries simulation theory, first published in 2005, which structures the development, implementation, planning and evaluation of simulation activities. As printed, "The six components that create the abovementioned simulation frameworks are context, background, design, educational practices, simulation experience, and outcomes". Teachers are advised to consider each element when designing curricula incorporating high-fidelity simulation, and the EPQ operationalises the educational-practices component. (Zalewska Katarzyna (2022))
