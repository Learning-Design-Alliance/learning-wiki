## Description
CUREs are course-based undergraduate research curricula defined by the Auchincloss et al. working group through science practices, discovery, broader relevance, collaboration, and iteration.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Five-dimension operational definition of CUREs**: The working group defines a CURE as a laboratory experience integrating five dimensions: use of science practices, discovery, broader relevance or importance, collaboration, and iteration. The report states that (Auchincloss LC et al. (2014))
- **Five-component definition of CUREs distinguishing discovery and broad relevance from other experimentation-based labs**: The article adopts the CURE literature's definition of Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences as courses defined by five principles: "Use of scientific practices", discovery, broad relevance, collaboration, and iteration. Discovery means students address novel scientific questions unknown to them (and often the instructor), while broad relevance means work with impact beyond the classroom for external stakeholders. The authors argue discovery and broad relevance most strongly differentiate CUREs from other experimentation-based labs and are the components that make CUREs resource-intensive. (Markert et al. (2026))
