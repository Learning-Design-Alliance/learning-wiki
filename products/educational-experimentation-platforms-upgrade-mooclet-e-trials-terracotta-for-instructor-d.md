## Description
The article names a growing set of educational experimentation platforms, built because mainstream systems "do not yet provide default instructor-directed infrastructures for A/B comparisons": "for example, UpGrade [16], MOOClet [37], E-TRIALS [20], and Terracotta [33], some of them building adaptive experimentation capabilities". They support instructors and researchers in embedding systematic comparisons and continuous improvement in real courses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **E-TRIALS: a free research infrastructure for running randomized controlled trials within the ASSISTments learning platform**: E-TRIALS is a research tool developed by ASSISTments that connects researchers with a large population of platform users so they can run learning-science experiments without coding. The article states it is "a free tool developed by ASSISTments to help researchers conduct randomized controlled trials in the realm of learning sciences." Researchers design conditions in existing problem sets, and the platform randomizes students to conditions, removing the recruitment burden. It is one of five IES-funded digital platforms supporting K-16 educational research. (Siedahmed et al. (2025))
- **E-TRIALS taxonomy of three experiment types: single support, support comparison, and problem varied**: The article organizes the experiments E-TRIALS supports into a three-type taxonomy: "single support, support comparison, and problem varied." Single support studies evaluate one type of student support message against the best-so-far control condition. Support comparison studies compare two or more support messages, such as hints versus explanations. Problem varied studies let researchers create their own problem content and recruit their own participants, accommodating participants from K-12 to higher education and subjects beyond mathematics. (Siedahmed et al. (2025))
