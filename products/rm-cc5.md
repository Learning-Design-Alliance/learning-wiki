## Description
RM-CC5 is a Grade 5 mathematics core curriculum that combines one-to-one adaptive technology, teacher data use, and implementation coaching.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Adaptive blended learning core-curriculum model combining three nested adaptive loops with complementary teacher and technology roles**: The article describes RM-CC5's program theory as "a conceptualization of adaptive, blended learning classroom models in which students interact one on one with technology as a primary mechanism for instruction, the system monitors student progress frequently, and the teacher and technology use data to adapt instruction to individual needs." It implements Aleven et al.'s three nested loops: immediate feedback while solving problems, technology-paced adjustment of instruction depth plus teacher dashboards for formative assessment, and implementation coordinators coaching teachers against a rubric. Blended learning frees the teacher from orchestrating the whole class while providing data for in (Nicole Shechtman et al. (2019))

# Adaptive blended learning core-curriculum model combining three nested adaptive loops with complementary teacher and technology roles

## Description
The article describes RM-CC5's program theory as "a conceptualization of adaptive, blended learning classroom models in which students interact one on one with technology as a primary mechanism for instruction, the system monitors student progress frequently, and the teacher and technology use data to adapt instruction to individual needs." It implements Aleven et al.'s three nested loops: immediate feedback while solving problems, technology-paced adjustment of instruction depth plus teacher dashboards for formative assessment, and implementation coordinators coaching teachers against a rubric. Blended learning frees the teacher from orchestrating the whole class while providing data for individualized decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers use the digital curriculum daily as the core resource with roughly 90-minute mathematics periods, supported by professional development and implementation coordinators
#### Constraints
- Prior research cited in the article indicates a risk that individualizing pace can lead struggling students to fall farther behind; the efficacy trial found no achievement advantage for this implementation

### Target Learners
- Grade 5 elementary mathematics students

### Target Learning Objectives
- Grade 5 mathematics achievement aligned with Common Core and state standards, including fractions and expressions building toward algebra
