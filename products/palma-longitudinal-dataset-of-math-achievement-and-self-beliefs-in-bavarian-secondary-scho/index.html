PALMA longitudinal dataset of math achievement and self-beliefs in Bavarian secondary schools¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The PALMA project dataset tracks mathematics achievement and self-beliefs across six measurement waves among secondary-school students in Bavarian schools, using standardized tests, grades, and student surveys.

Components¶

  • PALMA longitudinal dataset of parental expectations, aspirations, and mathematics achievement emotions: PALMA (Project for the Analysis of Learning and Achievement in Mathematics) is a longitudinal German dataset with annual parent and student assessments in mathematics. 'In total, 3,424 students (50.0% female) provided data for parental expectation, aspiration, and students' achievement emotions at least in one wave.' It includes single-item parental expectation and aspiration measures, end-of-year math grades, and the AEQ–M covering seven achievement emotions, drawn from 42 schools across three Bavarian tracks. (Sakaki et al. (2025))
  • PALMA longitudinal dataset (Years 5–9, German secondary students): The PALMA study (project for the analysis of learning and achievement in mathematics) is a longitudinal panel following German secondary school students with annual assessments from Year 5 to Year 10, representative of Bavaria's three school types. Across five assessments it covered 3,425 students, one parent each, and 419 mathematics teachers, with student, parent, and teacher surveys plus an IQ test and a Rasch-scaled standardized math achievement test. In this article it supplied 105 predictors (88 before dummy coding) for the machine learning analyses. (Lavelle-Hill et al. (2024))

Claims¶

  • Across basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs of five-wave secondary-school data, math self-concept and achievement show consistent reciprocal (bidirectional) directional ordering [+M]
  • Reanalyses of simulated and PALMA data provide converging support for the extended reciprocal effects model, including an effect of academic self-concept on achievement [+M]
  • In German secondary-school mathematics, effects of self-concept on achievement are lagged (lag1) whereas effects of achievement on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0) [+M]
  • Higher parental expectations contemporaneously predict more positive and fewer negative mathematics achievement emotions, with boredom an exception [+M]
