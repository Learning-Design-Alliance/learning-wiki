# Vision and Change framework

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The AAAS/NSF Vision and Change framework guides undergraduate biology curricula around five core concepts and associated competencies, and is used by biology societies and instructors to support student-centered course design.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Vision and Change framework of five core concepts for biological literacy and student-centered instruction**: The article describes the AAAS/NSF Vision and Change Report as a framework guiding undergraduate biology course content and pedagogy. It emphasizes "the implementation of five core concepts for biological literacy in biology courses": evolution; structure and function; information flow, exchange, and storage; pathways and transformation of energy and matter; and systems. The report also recommends shifting from faculty-centered lecturing to student-centered active learning, and advises moving away from presenting all the facts about a subject toward articulating clear, easily assessable learning outcomes. Society curricular recommendations, such as those from ASM and AAI, were modeled on its core concepts and competencies. (García-Ojeda ME (2022))
- **Vision and Change core concepts used as a conceptual learning framework in a disciplinary first-year seminar**: The course organizes biology learning around the five Vision and Change core concepts: "1) evolution; 2) information flow, exchange, and storage; 3) pathways and transformations of energy and matter; 4) structure and function; and 5) systems." Students also use a scale-versus-concept framework as a visual tool to organize knowledge, exploring each topic through multiple core concept lenses and at multiple scales. The article states frameworks lead to greater and faster learning gains by developing students' abilities to organize, connect, and relate concepts. (Wienhold Caroline J.; Branchaw Janet (2018))
