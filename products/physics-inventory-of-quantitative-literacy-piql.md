# Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy (PIQL)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
The PIQL is an 18-question multiple-choice inventory developed by Trevor I. Smith and colleagues to assess introductory undergraduate students’ quantitative reasoning in physics contexts.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy (PIQL) prototype assessment**: The protoPIQL is an 18-question multiple-choice inventory measuring Physics Quantitative Literacy, focusing on "reasoning with ratios and proportions, and reasoning about negative quantities." It mixes single-response and multi-response items across multiple physical contexts, and was piloted in three introductory physics courses. The authors intend it to measure PQL development "across the undergraduate physics curriculum." (Trevor I. Smith et al. (2019))
- **Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy (PIQL) assessment instrument**: The PIQL is a multiple-choice reasoning inventory under development to assess introductory students' physics quantitative literacy (PQL), which the authors consider "a conceptual blend between physics concepts and mathematical reasoning." It tests three key components: ratio and proportion (Q1-Q6), covariation (Q7-Q14), and negativity (Q15-Q20), and includes multiple-response questions instructing students to "select all statements that must be true." MR items are intended to reveal more about students' thinking than single-response items. (Trevor I. Smith et al. (2019))
- **Physics Quantitative Literacy as a conceptual blend of physics concepts and mathematical reasoning with three facets**: Physics Quantitative Literacy (PQL) is "the interconnected skills, attitudes, and habits of mind that together support the sophisticated use of elementary mathematics in the context of physics." The article treats PQL as a conceptual blend between physics concepts and mathematical reasoning, operationalized through three facets of quantification in introductory physics: reasoning about ratios and proportions, covariation, and signed quantities/negativity. The PIQL was designed around these three facets, with many items drawn from prior mathematics and physics education research. (Trevor I. Smith et al. (2020))
- **Physics Quantitative Reasoning RBA (reasoning inventory in development)**: A research-based assessment under development by a multi-institution collaboration, focused on "quantitative reasoning—rather than conceptual understanding—in physics contexts". It contains 20 items, six of which are multiple-choice/multiple-response (MCMR) items probing multiple facets of student reasoning. It was administered as a pretest at the start of each term over eight academic quarters in a three-quarter calculus-based introductory physics sequence, and moved to unproctored online administration in Spring 2020. (Alexis Olsho et al. (2020))
- **The Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy (PIQL), a reasoning inventory for quantitative literacy in introductory physics**: The PIQL is a multiple-choice reasoning inventory measuring physics quantitative literacy (PQL) in calculus-based introductory physics, available online for widespread use. The article states: "We introduce the Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy (PIQL) as a tool for measuring quantitative literacy, a foundation of mathematical reasoning, in the context of introductory physics." Unlike concept inventories, it assesses reasoning rather than procedural skill, spanning three facets: proportional reasoning, reasoning about signed quantities, and covariational reasoning. The steady-state version has 20 or 21 items, six of which are multiple-choice/multiple-response (MCMR). (White Brahmia et al. (2020))
- **Physics quantitative literacy as proceptual reasoning: a conceptual blend of mathematics and physical meaning-making**: The article grounds the PIQL in the framework of conceptual blending theory and proceptual understanding. Gray and Tall "define proceptual understanding as a combination of procedural mastery and con ceptual understanding", and the article treats reasoning about physics quantities as an inseparable blend of mathematical procedure and physical meaning, interpreted through Fauconnier and Turner's conceptual blending theory and Sherin's symbolic forms. Expert mathematization develops through creation of new blended cognitive spaces rather than addition of physics quantities to existing algebraic structures. The PIQL's three facets are designed to probe this blended reasoning. (White Brahmia et al. (2020))
- **Three-facet framework of physics quantitative literacy (PQL)**: Physics quantitative literacy (PQL) is defined as quantitative literacy situated in physics contexts, a conceptual blend between physics and mathematical reasoning. The framework, inherited from the PIQL, holds that the PIQL "was designed to measure three main facets of PQL: reasoning about signs, covariational reasoning, and proportional reasoning". In this framework proportional reasoning is the linear subset of covariational reasoning: "proportional reasoning describes covariational reasoning about quantities related by a linear function". (Charlotte Zimmerman et al. (2025))
