## Description
A QuVis Quantum Mechanics Visualization Project interactive simulation for teaching introductory quantum mechanics by comparing classical particles, electromagnetic waves, and single photons in interferometer experiments.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **QuVis Interferometer Experiments simulation with revised single-photon superposition depiction**: The Interferometer Experiments simulation is one of the QuVis Quantum Mechanics Visualization Project's research-based interactive simulations for learning and teaching quantum mechanics. It "compares and contrasts the behavior of classical particles, electromagnetic waves and single photons under the same experimental conditions", using simplified, idealized situations such as no background light and 100% efficient detectors to reduce complexity and cognitive load. After the interview study, Visualization III was "incorporated into all of the QuVis single-photon simulations". The simulations show each photon taking both paths of the interferometer after a beam splitter, consistent with the superposition-state mathematics and delayed-choice experiments. (Antje Kohnle et al. (2014))
- **Virtual Mach-Zehnder Interferometer simulator with single-photon source**: A freely available virtual Mach-Zehnder Interferometer simulator (free code, referenced from Müller and Wiesner) that allows the beam intensity to be diminished to the emission of a single photon at a time, the monophotonic regime the article's discussions center on. The article states "this simulator offers a much wider number of experiments with light and photons that can be discussed in a classroom", and the authors selected two of the simplest cases to show how interpretations allied to virtual simulations can support teaching effectiveness. (F. Ostermann et al. (2005))
