# Chariot AR Medical simulation software (Stanford Chariot Program)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A portable augmented-reality medical simulation platform developed and operated by Stanford's Chariot Program, providing holographic patients, monitors, medical equipment, and real-time remote instructor communication for healthcare training.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Chariot AR Medical simulation software on Magic Leap One headsets with holographic patient and monitor**: A portable AR medical simulation platform used in the trial: the Magic Leap One headset running "the Chariot AR Medical simulation software (Stanford Chariot Program) which integrates real-time communication into a portable AR medical simulator featuring a holographic patient, monitor, and other medical equipment." The holographic patient's chest was aligned over a real compression torso for tactile feedback, and each remote session used four headsets (learner, two actors, remote instructor). Orientation took approximately 5 to 10 minutes. (Gianotto-Oliveira R et al. (2026))
- **CHARM remote augmented-reality medical simulator for expert-facilitated neonatal resuscitation simulation**: The Stanford Chariot AR Medical (CHARM) simulator is "a portable AR medical simulator featuring a holographic patient, monitor, and other medical equipment" that integrates real-time communication. Delivered on the Magic Leap One headset, it projects holographic clinical elements into the physical room while remote instructors at Stanford control holographic imagery and facilitate case progression for co-located learners and standardized actors in Chile. In this study each resident played team leader in a scripted NRP scenario, with a structured session of orientation, a five-minute preparatory video, the 10–12 minute simulation, debriefing, and questionnaires. (Rojas Marcos et al. (2026))
