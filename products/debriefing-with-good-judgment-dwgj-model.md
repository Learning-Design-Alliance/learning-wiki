# Debriefing with Good Judgment (DwGJ) Model

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured simulation-debriefing framework developed for instructors that organizes debriefing into reactions, analysis, and summary phases and uses advocacy-inquiry in a psychologically safe environment.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Debriefing with Good Judgment (DwGJ) model of simulation debriefing**: The Debriefing with Good Judgment Model is a structured simulation-debriefing framework organized into three phases — reactions, analysis, and summary — in which the instructor fosters a relaxing ambiance and approaches learners "with true curiosity and feedback, using the advocacy and inquiry technique", while avoiding the urge to fix learners. The article adopted this model after hands-on simulation practice within CBL to develop residents' clinical reasoning. (Shao Wenming et al. (2025))

### Claims

- [DwGJ participants consistently outperformed controls across self-reported clinical reasoning dimensions of critical thinking, systematic thinking, and evidence-based thinking](../claims/dwgj-cbl-higher-self-reported-clinical-reasoning.md) [+M]
- [DwGJ residents reported higher course satisfaction and perceived learning outcomes, while ratings of instructors' teaching attitudes did not differ between groups](../claims/dwgj-higher-satisfaction-instructor-ratings-null.md) [+W]
