## Description
Two surveys, Improving Chicago Schools: The Students Speak, 1997 and Improving Chicago Schools: The Teachers' Turn, 1997, were administered by the Consortium on Chicago School Research to sixth-, eighth- and tenth-grade students and to elementary and high school teachers in spring 1997. The report states the purpose was "to collect reliable information on students' and teachers' views of the school environment, classroom learning, parent involvement, school governance, and the professional work life of teachers." Items were developed through stakeholder consultation with teachers, principals, central office staff, students, and Annenberg evaluation committees, drawing on the 1994 Charting Reform surveys.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools needed a teacher response rate of at least 42 percent or a student response rate of at least 50 percent to receive an individual report.
#### Constraints
- Several measures are comparable to the 1995 administration, enabling comparison across the two survey waves.

### Target Learners
- Chicago Public Schools students in grades 6, 8, and 10 and their teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Assessing school environment, classroom learning, parent involvement, governance, and teacher professional work life
