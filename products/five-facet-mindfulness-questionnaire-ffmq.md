# Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ)

## Description
A 39-item self-report assessment developed by Baer and colleagues that measures five facets of dispositional mindfulness, including observing, describing, acting with awareness, nonjudging, and nonreactivity.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Five Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ) as a measure of dispositional mindfulness in nursing students**: The FFMQ is a 39-item self-report questionnaire with 5-point Likert-type responses ranging from "1" (never or rarely true) to "5" (very often or always true), scoring in the range of 39–195. It measures five subscales: observing, describing, acting with awareness, nonjudging of inner experience, and nonreactivity to inner experience. In this study its internal reliability was α = 0.84 (subscales 0.71–0.87), and it was administered online via Microsoft Forms before mid-semester exams. (Mathkor Darin Mansor (2025))
- **Five-Facet Mindfulness Questionnaire (FFMQ) as a measurement instrument**: The FFMQ is a 39-item self-report questionnaire used in this study to measure the tendency to be mindful in daily life, with facets of observing, describing, acting with awareness, non-judging of inner experience, and non-reactivity to inner experience. It uses "a five-point Likert-scale ranging from 1 (“never or very rarely true”) to 5 (“very often or always true”)". In this sample the mean total score was 118.84 (SD 15.6), described as lying at the mid-point of the possible range and comparable with community validation data. (Alzahrani AM et al. (2020))
- **Mindfulness as a five-facet dispositional capacity measured by the FFMQ**: The article treats mindfulness as a personal tendency to attend to the present moment non-judgmentally, operationalized by the 39-item FFMQ. It states "The five facets are observing, describing, acting with awareness, non-judging of inner experience, and non-reactivity to inner experience", scored on a five-point Likert scale where a higher score indicates better mindfulness skills. The article also holds that mindfulness is an inherent human trait that can be developed with training, and cites proposed mechanisms of reduced rumination and increased positive reappraisal. (Alzahrani AM et al. (2020))
