# Native Skywatchers initiative

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An Indigenous-led astronomy education initiative founded by Annette S. Lee that uses Earth–Sky relationships and dual learning to connect Native knowledge with Western science for STEM education.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Earth-Sky mirroring (Kapemni) dual-learning framework**: The Native Skywatchers approach is organized by an Earth-Sky relationship framework in which the sky and stars are above, the Earth below, and the People fixed equally between the two, connecting them. Guided by the teaching "As it is above; it is below," the framework supports a "dual-learning" system that explores similarities between native ways of knowing and Western science, honoring both. The article states that "the Earth-Sky relationship serves as the foundational framework of the NSW approach," and that in this dual-learning environment stories from science and native cultures are woven together into a tapestry revealing deeper meaning than any thread alone. (Annette S. Lee et al. (2019))
- **Native Skywatchers indigenous star maps (Ojibwe, D(L)akota, and Cree)**: The Native Skywatchers initiative produced three indigenous star maps used as foundational astronomy teaching resources: the Ojibwe Giizhig Anung Masinaaigan and the D(L)akota Makoce Wicanhpi Wowapi, both delivered at the 2012 premier NSW Educator & Community Workshop, and the Cree Ininew Achakos Masinikan created in 2016. The article states that "Using the native star maps as a foundational resource, many additional related resources were designed and delivered to educators and communities." The maps weave cultural stories, language, and astronomical content into a single visual teaching tool. (Annette S. Lee et al. (2019))
- **Hands-on curriculum combining astronomy, culture, language and art developed to fill the lack of materials for the Minnesota state standards**: Alongside the star maps, the project developed "hands-on curriculum that combines astronomy, culture, language and art". The article reports a complete lack of materials addressing the new MN State Science Standards K-12 requirement that students understand that men and women of all cultures, including Minnesota American Indian tribes, have been involved in engineering design and scientific inquiry, using Ojibwe and Dakota knowledge of patterns in the stars to predict and plan. Regional teacher requests also motivated the materials' creation. (Annette S. Lee et al. (2013))
