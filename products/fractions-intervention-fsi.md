## Description
FSI is a teacher-delivered fraction-learning intervention for sixth-grade students with mathematics difficulties, designed to develop fraction concepts, arithmetic, fluency, and related attitudes.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **FSI theory of change linking intervention components, science-of-learning principles, cognitive processes, and proximal and distal outcomes**: The article presents a theory of change model (Figure 1) grounding the FSI in numerical cognition research. It maps three intervention components (fractions as numbers with magnitudes, fraction arithmetic computation, fractions applied to real-world situations) through science-of-learning principles and underlying cognitive processes (numerical magnitude understanding, proportional and multiplicative reasoning, mathematical equivalence) to proximal outcomes such as "Improved knowledge in: Fraction concepts" and distal outcomes including better preparedness for algebra. Teacher fidelity of implementation is named a possible mediator and student characteristics possible moderators. (Nancy C. Jordan et al. (2024))
