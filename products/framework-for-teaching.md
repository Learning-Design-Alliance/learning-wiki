# Framework for Teaching

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 16 claims (10 for, 5 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 16 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching is a framework developed by Charlotte Danielson for evaluating and supporting classroom teaching through observable components across four domains and four performance levels.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching as a classroom-observation evaluation tool**: The Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching divides teaching into four domains—Planning and Preparation, Classroom Environment, Instruction, and Professional Responsibilities—with principals rating observable components in the Classroom Environment and Instruction domains at four performance levels: unsatisfactory, basic, proficient, and distinguished. Chicago adopted it to replace a 30-year-old checklist, aiming to establish "a common definition of good teaching along a developmental continuum" and structure principal-teacher conversations about instruction. (Lauren Sartain et al. (2010))
- **Framework for Teaching (FFT) observation instrument with 22 components scored into four domains and an overall Professional Practice Rating**: The Framework for Teaching of Charlotte Danielson is an observation-based teacher performance measure whose "22 components" each "is designed to capture a separate teaching practice." The pilot used observation data to estimate four domain scores and one overall Professional Practice Rating (PPR) score, which were merged with value-added measures based on student test scores for analysis. (Stephen Lipscomb et al. (2015))
- **The Framework for Teaching observation rubric with 22 components in four domains**: The Framework for Teaching is a classroom-observation rubric used by principals to evaluate teaching practices. The report describes it as "a rubric that includes 22 components grouped into four broad teaching practice domains": planning and preparation, classroom environment, instruction, and professional responsibilities. It served as the observation instrument in Phase 2 of the Pennsylvania evaluation pilot. (Elias Walsh (2013))
