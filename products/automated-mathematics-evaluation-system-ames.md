# Automated Mathematics Evaluation System (AMES)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
AMES is a named system of randomly generated, timed multiple-choice mathematics remediation quizzes developed and used by David Toback and colleagues for prerequisite-math assessment in introductory physics courses.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Automated Mathematics Evaluation System (AMES) math remediation quizzes**: AMES is a set of ten-problem, ten-minute multiple-choice math quizzes assigned during the first week, limited to pre/co-requisite math topics. Every attempt draws at least one problem from seven areas including "simple algebraic expressions in one variable" through "simple differentiation and integration." Students must obtain 100% on ten separate quizzes drawn randomly from the same pool. The intent is to remind students of calculation tools they should already possess and remediate deficiencies early, with students encouraged to drop the course if they cannot perform well quickly. (David Toback et al. (2004))
