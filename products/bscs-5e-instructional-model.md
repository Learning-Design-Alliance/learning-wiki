## Description
A Biological Science Curriculum Study framework for sequencing instruction through engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration, and evaluation.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The 5E instructional model as a five-stage constructivist learning cycle**: The 5E instructional model, developed by Bybee at the Biological Science Curriculum Study, is a constructivist teaching sequence grounded in the view that knowledge is constructed by the individual rather than passively transmitted. The article states "This model is based on 5 different stages of learning: Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate and Evaluate." In this model the student plays an active role, the teacher acts as a guide, and learning is focused on process rather than product, associating the learning environment with the world outside the classroom. The meta-analysis treats this model as the intervention whose effects on achievement, retention and attitude are pooled across studies. (Özgür Anil (2015))
- **The 5E learning model: a five-phase constructivist instructional sequence for science courses**: The 5E learning model is a constructivist instructional model for science courses comprising five phases: introduction (engagement), discovery (exploration), description (explanation), expanding (elaboration), and assessment (evaluation). The article describes it as a way "to design science courses based on structural approach and cognitive psychology" (Bybee, 1997), in which prior knowledge is identified, a research question is posed, first-hand experience is gained under teacher guidance, and the concept is transferred to a new field and evaluated. (Nevin Kozcu Cakır (2017))
- **The BSCS 5E Instructional Model and its 3E and 7E variants**: The BSCS 5E Instructional Model is a constructivist framework for organizing and sequencing science instruction through five stages—engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration, and evaluation—in which "the teacher serves as a facilitator, guiding students through each 'E' stage of the learning process". The 3E variant retains only explore, explain, and elaborate, while the 7E variant adds elicit and extend stages to make evocation of prior conceptions and far transfer more explicit. All three build on constructivist learning theory and incorporate the How People Learn findings on engaging prior knowledge and metacognition. (Polanin et al. (2024))
- **The 5E learning cycle model: engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration, evaluation**: The 5E learning model is an inquiry-based instructional model composed of five sequential stages: "engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration and evaluation". Engagement activates prior knowledge and arouses curiosity; exploration has students researching answers through hands-on activities; explanation has teachers help students connect their explanations with scientific knowledge and address misconceptions; elaboration has students apply knowledge to new situations; evaluation checks targeted gains using complementary measurement throughout the process. The study implemented this model over five weeks for teaching factors affecting chemical equilibrium. (Senol Sen et al. (2016))
- **5E instructional strategy: a five-phase constructivist learning cycle (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)**: The 5Es instructional strategy is a learning-cycle model developed in the 1980s that encourages a constructivist approach while introducing aspects of behaviorism and cognitivism. Each stage describes a phase of learning: "Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, and Evaluate". Engage captures attention and activates prior knowledge; Explore provides a common concrete learning experience with active exploration; Explain introduces common terms in a more teacher-directed phase; Elaborate extends concepts to new situations in increasing order of difficulty; Evaluate involves both formal and informal assessment. The article applies it to Civic Education, a non-science subject. (AbdulRaheem et al. (2018))
- **The 5E instructional model: a constructivist five-phase learning cycle**: The 5E instructional model organizes teaching into five phases — Engagement, Exploration, Explanation, Elaboration, and Evaluation — each with stated aims, teacher roles, and activity examples. It is "derived from the Atkin and Karplus learning cycle" incorporated into the SCIS program, with engagement and evaluation added by BSCS. The article describes it as an approach that "maximizes opportunities for learner participation by engaging them with a series of activities that assist them to construct their own understanding," with teachers acting as facilitators. (Stephen Akinyemi Omotayo et al. (2017))
- **5E learning cycle instructional model**: The 5E learning cycle is an inquiry-based instruction model requiring "the instruction of five discrete elements": Engagement, Exploration, Explanation, Elaboration, and Evaluation. Engagement accesses prior knowledge through curiosity-promoting activities; Exploration provides shared activities in which misconceptions are identified and conceptual change facilitated; Explanation introduces concepts and lets students demonstrate understanding; Elaboration extends concepts to new situations; Evaluation offers formative and summative assessment with feedback. In this study the model structured eight weeks of fifth-grade science teaching. (Ali Abdi (2014))
- **The 5E instructional model (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate) as a constructivist framework for designing science guidance materials**: The 5E model is a constructivist learning-theory-based instructional framework whose phases are Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, and Evaluate. In this study it structured the development of guidance materials: each phase was populated with specific materials (a case study to attract attention in Engage, a worksheet in Explore, a conceptual change text in Explain, another case study in Elaborate, and a concept map in Evaluate). The article situates 5E among models such as 4E and 7E developed in line with the constructivist approach adopted in the science curriculum, and notes the 7E model's perceived complexity in the eyes of teachers. (Tülay Şenel Çoruhlu et al. (2017))
- **The 5E learning model as a constructivist science-teaching method with five phases**: The article describes the 5E model as "a science teaching method which depen ds on research -based constructivist learning theory and experimental activities", developed by Rodger Bybee of BSCS. It consists of five phases — Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate and Evaluate — in which lessons begin with an intriguing problem, students collaboratively generate ideas, teachers provide scientific explanations, students apply learning to new situations, and finally reflect on their understanding. In this study the model was operationalized with worksheets, cartoons, animations, demonstrations and group work. (Hatice Guzel (2016))
