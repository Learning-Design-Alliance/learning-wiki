# NWEA MAP Growth

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 33 claims (31 for, 2 mixed) · 19 studies (13 associational, 5 design, 1 causal), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 19 report an effect size · 33 claims rest on one study

## Description
NWEA MAP Growth is a computer-adaptive achievement assessment and associated longitudinal achievement database developed and administered by NWEA.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **MAP assessment dataset on reading growth for students with visual impairments**: The study used data from Northwest Evaluation Association's Measures of Academic Progress (MAP) assessment to analyze reading achievement for "224 students with visual impairments in grades 3 - 10, in four states over an eight-year time period." Growth was compared with "a nationally normed group of students from the general population." The dataset supports longitudinal trajectory analysis of an understudied population. (Beth Boroson et al. (2017))
- **MAP Growth reading and mathematics assessment dataset (7 million students, grades 3-8, 25,000 schools, 2020-21 to fall 2022)**: The brief draws on MAP Growth reading and mathematics assessments administered between 2020-21 and fall 2022 to 7 million students in grades 3-8 in 25,000 schools. The text states the data come from "7 million students in grades 3-8 in 25,000 schools who took MAP Growth reading and mathematics assessments between 2020–21 and fall 2022." It supports NWEA's ongoing research agenda on pandemic effects on achievement. (Karyn Lewis (2022))
- **Nationwide MAP Growth middle-school score sample**: The data source for this brief is a nationwide sample of MAP Growth scores for middle school students, which NWEA researchers used to study Algebra placement. The description states the researchers worked "Using a nationwide sample of MAP Growth scores for middle schoolers" to examine readiness and enrollment. It serves as the evidence base for the brief's placement and outcome analyses. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2026))
- **Longitudinal MAP reading dataset for 224 students with visual impairments**: A longitudinal dataset of reading achievement drawn from "Northwest Evaluation Association’s Measures of Academic Progress assessment" covering "224 students with visual impairments in grades 3–10, in four states over an eight-year time period." The article uses it to describe reading growth for this population and to benchmark it against a nationally normed general-population group. It is the evidentiary basis of the study's analysis. (Boroson et al. (2017))
- **National analysis dataset covering over 2,300 rural US schools' math and reading achievement**: The brief draws on a large national dataset of rural schools to describe student achievement. The announcement states it uses "data from over 2,300 rural schools across the US" to provide "unique insight into math and reading achievement of students in rural schools." The dataset supports descriptive analysis of rural achievement and growth patterns nationwide. (Johnson et al. (2022))
- **NWEA longitudinal growth database used to track top students' achievement trends**: The study's data source is NWEA's longitudinal growth database, which the description says was used to track achievement trends: "achievement trends from NWEA's longitudinal growth database were used to track students who scored at or above the 90th percentile on this assessment." It supports multi-year follow-up of individual students' percentile positions across the middle school and high school span. (Yun Xiang et al. (2013))
- **NWEA MAP Growth longitudinal dataset for DHH students, grades 2–8, seven reading and mathematics domains over 5 years**: This element is the longitudinal achievement dataset analyzed in the study: NWEA MAP® Growth™ data covering DHH students from grades 2 to 8. The article states it "provides an analysis of NWEA MAP® Growth™ data from grades 2 to 8 across seven reading and mathematics domains over a period of 5 years," making it a multi-domain, multi-year record of academic growth used to model how DHH students' reading and mathematics achievement progresses over time. (Stephanie Cawthon et al. (2022))
- **NWEA MAP Growth interim assessments**: NWEA's MAP Growth assessments are interim assessments used by states and districts during the 2020-2021 school year. The article describes them as the data source for investigating school-composition change: researchers "examine data from NWEA's MAP Growth assessments, interim assessments used by states and districts during the 2020–2021 school year". School-aggregate scores from these assessments are used by school officials to monitor school performance and make policy decisions. (Jonathan Schweig et al. (2022))
- **Spanish MAP Growth Math calibration dataset of 3,908 items and over 147,000 monolingual and 33,000 bilingual students**: The study's empirical basis is item and person data pulled from the NWEA Growth Research Database (GRD) covering five years of Spanish math testing. The item side comprises "3,908 Spanish math items across different tests" in the selected sample (3,955 collected), spanning 24 Spanish math tests from K-2 through 6+. The person side comprises 147,094 collected monolingual students (147,044 selected) and 33,624 collected bilingual students (33,607 selected), about 75% Hispanic across grades. The dataset supports the fixed-person-score calibration designs and all item and person results in the report. (Wang et al. (2020))
- **NWEA MAP Growth assessment and longitudinal achievement database**: MAP Growth is a computer adaptive test that measures achievement status and growth over time, vertically scaled to allow estimation of gains across time and aligned to state content standards, with scores reported on the RIT (Rasch unIT) scale. The study drew on the NWEA anonymized longitudinal student achievement database covering approximately 2.7 million grade 3-8 students in 17,000 public schools across the 2018-19, 2019-20, and 2020-21 school years, with percentile ranks computed against the 2020 NWEA MAP Growth norms from a pre-pandemic 2015-16 through 2017-18 norming sample. (Megan Kuhfeld et al. (2021))
