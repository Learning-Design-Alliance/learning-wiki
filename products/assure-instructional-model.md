# ASSURE instructional model

## Description
The ASSURE instructional model is a six-step lesson-planning framework developed by Heinich, Russell, Molenda, and Smaldino for analyzing learners, specifying objectives, selecting and using media, requiring participation, and evaluating instruction.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **ASSURE instructional model as a design guide for infographic-based lessons**: The ASSURE model, developed by Heinich, Russell, Molendo and Smaldino (1999), is a six-step instructional planning model the study adopted as the guide for its infographic experiment. The article states that the "ASSURE acronym has expressed about six steps or procedures": Analyze learner characteristics, State objectives, Select media and material, Utilize media and material, Require learner participation, and Evaluate and revise. It frames the model as planning suitable course and technology means to strengthen the education process. (Alqudah et al. (2019))
- **The ASSURE model as a systematic approach to using animation in instruction**: The article presents the ASSURE model (attributed to Okon 2010 and Heinich et al 2002) as a systematic approach to instruction with animated resources. Each letter stands for a step: "A" for analysis of learners' characteristics, "S" for stating instructional objectives, a second "S" for selecting instructional methods and technology, "U" for utilization, "R" for requiring teachers' and learners' participation, and "E" for evaluation and revision of the instructional process. The article frames it as coordinating all aspects of a problem towards specific objectives using available resources such as animated instructional materials. (Isaac Ali Kwasu; Ema Ema (2015))
