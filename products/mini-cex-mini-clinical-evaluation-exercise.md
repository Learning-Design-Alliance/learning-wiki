# Mini-CEX (Mini Clinical Evaluation Exercise)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 17 claims (14 for, 3 mixed) · 10 studies (6 causal, 2 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 10 report an effect size · 17 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured workplace-based clinical assessment tool developed for direct observation of real patient encounters and immediate formative feedback on learners’ clinical performance.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Mini-CEX as a formative workplace-based assessment and feedback tool for undergraduate clinical skills**: The Mini clinical evaluation exercise (Mini-CEX) is a workplace-based assessment tool in which faculty directly observe a real patient encounter and provide structured formative feedback. The article describes it as "a validated, assessment, as well as a teaching tool aimed at providing, formative feedback, on clinical skills through observation of real-life workplace encounters." In this study intervention students carried a hard copy of the Mini-CEX form, requested faculty assessment after patient interactions, received immediate verbal feedback documented on the form, and reflected on strengths and areas needing improvement, with a follow-up encounter about one week later. (Durrani Shazia Fakhir et al. (2025))
- **Researcher-made Mini-CEX checklist for scrub-nurse competence in orthopedic surgery**: A researcher-made workplace-based evaluation tool for directly observing operating room trainees acting as scrub nurses. "The Mini-CEX method was conducted using a checklist consisting of 31 items which evaluated six different domains", covering sterilization, patient and field preparation, instrument organization, team communication, in-procedure performance management, and completion of the procedure. Scoring uses a 5-point scale from 0 (not appropriate) to 5 (far above standard), with minimum and maximum scores of 0 and 155, followed by immediate written and oral feedback. Reliability was approved via Cronbach's alpha of 0.869. (Bagheri Sara et al. (2025))
- **Ultrasound-adapted Mini-CEX assessing six competency areas with immediate feedback**: An improved Mini Clinical Evaluation Exercise tailored to ultrasound practice, used both as an evaluation instrument and as a feedback mechanism during training. It assesses residents during routine ultrasound procedures across six areas: "medical history taking, ultrasound examination, doctor-patient communication, clinical reasoning, organizational efficiency, and overall performance", aligned with ACGME core competencies. Scores of 1–3 are deemed insufficient, 4–6 qualified, and 7–9 excellent. The authors state it provides "immediate feedback and guidance" by monitoring interactions with patients and colleagues and adherence to standard protocols. (Liu Rong et al. (2025))
- **Modified Mini-CEX scale for assessing clinical speculative thinking in oral and maxillofacial surgery postgraduates**: The modified Mini-CEX scale is a five-item workplace assessment instrument used in this study to score students' clinical speculative thinking. Each item is worth 20 points, covering "clinical decision-making ability, communication and coordination ability, adaptive ability, treatment operation level, and medical history writing level". Students were assessed while independently receiving patients in the emergency department, observed by two attending physicians who were trained on the scale, did not participate in the teaching, and were blinded to grouping; each student was assessed five times and average scores were used. (Gao Ning et al. (2024))
- **Mini-CEX form for evaluating community outpatient consultation ability**: The Mini-CEX is a workplace assessment tool used in this study to evaluate the diagnosis and treatment ability of general practitioners in community general practice clinics. The article states "The Mini CEX form includes 7 aspects: medical history collection, physical examination, humanistic care, clinical judgment, health education consultation, organizational effectiveness, and overall performance". It was administered at enrollment, 2 weeks and 4 weeks of training, with results fed back to residents. (Wang Yuanmei et al. (2024))
- **Mini-CEX as a workplace-based assessment of clinical skills in MSK outpatient encounters**: The mini clinical evaluation exercise is an observation-based workplace assessment in which a supervisor observes a learner during a real clinical encounter and rates performance across seven domains: history-taking, physical examination, communication, clinical reasoning and diagnosis, humanity and professionalism, management, and overall clinical competency. The article describes it as having "proven validity as an assessment of clinical skills through observation" and used it to compare groups after one day of simulation. (Yamauchi Kazuyo et al. (2021))
- **Factors influencing the educational impact of the mini-CEX: context, users, implementation, and outcome**: Drawing on a cited qualitative synthesis and meta-analysis, the article frames mini-CEX educational impact as shaped by four interacting factor clusters. It reports that the qualitative synthesis "revealed four themes and nine subthemes associated with the educational impact of the mini-CEX, including context, users, implementation, and outcome," and that mini-CEX quality and participant responsiveness were positively associated with educational outcomes. In the present study, the authors propose trainee knowledge base, supervisor-dominated one-to-one teaching, self-esteem, learning goals, and responses to feedback as the factors behind group differences. (Wu Yali et al. (2020))
- **Modified Chinese-language mini-CEX form with 4 domains and 18 subdomains on a 5-point scale**: The hospital adopted a modified Chinese-language mini-CEX form based on the original American Board of Internal Medicine instrument, used one to three times per discipline during rotations as both an assessment and a teaching tool. The article states it "includes 4 domains and 18 subdomains covering 6 dimensions of clinical competence in medical interviewing skills, physical examination skills, counseling skills, clinical judgment, humanistic qualities/professionalism, and organization and efficiency." Each subdomain is rated on a 5-point scale from poor performance to proficiency in all dimensions. (Wu Yali et al. (2020))
- **Pharmacist-specific Chinese-language Mini-CEX instrument with nine competency domains**: A workplace-based assessment form adapted from the physician Mini-CEX for Taiwanese PGY pharmacy trainees, covering "pharmacology knowledge, patient care knowledge, consultation skills, professional health education skills, management of drug distribution, organization and efficiency, professionalism, communication skills, and overall performance". Each competency is rated on a 1–9 scale banded as unsatisfactory (1–3), satisfactory (4–6), or excellent (7–9), benchmarked to a TSHSP stage 3 competent pharmacist. Descriptive items were adapted from ASHP guidelines, translated into Chinese, and validated via a two-round Delphi process with five experts. (Wei Chung-Jen et al. (2019))
- **Mini-CEX (mini-clinical evaluation exercise) workplace-based assessment tool**: The mini-CEX is a workplace-based assessment tool developed by Norcini et al to facilitate observation-based feedback and promote trainee self-assessment. It is conceptualised as an encounter in which "a supervisor observing a trainee performing a nominated, routine clinical task for approximately 10–15 min", with verbal and written feedback provided using a standardised marking template. In this study's setting, each advanced geriatric medicine trainee must complete four formative mini-CEX assessments per year. (Kipen Eva et al. (2019))
- **Mini-CEX with structured three-component written feedback used for PGY1 workplace assessment**: The mini-clinical evaluation exercise is a workplace assessment in which "a single assessor observes the trainee conducting a focused history-taking and physical examination" before the trainee presents differential diagnoses or a management plan. Seven itemized clinical competencies are rated on a nine-point scale, and feedback includes "positive feedback (particular strengths), suggestions for development, and an agreed educational plan of action". In this study it was completed weekly during one-month ED rotations by 67 assessors evaluating 273 PGY1 residents. (Chang Yu-Che et al. (2016))
