## Description
CALLA is Chamot and colleagues' instructional framework for explicitly teaching academic language-learning and metacognitive strategies through preparation, presentation, practice, evaluation, and expansion.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **CALLA model of metacognitive strategy instruction (Chamot and Rubin, 1994): five instructional phases**: The Cognitive Academic Language Learning Approach (CALLA) model proposed by Chamot and Rubin (1994) is a strategy-instruction model the article calls "the most effective strategy instruction model". It structures instruction in five phases: Preparation, in which students identify prior knowledge and the teacher explains metacognitive strategies; Presentation, in which the teacher demonstrates the strategies and explains how and when to use them; Practice, in which students use the strategies in regular class activities with teacher guidance; Evaluation, in which students self-evaluate strategy use with rating scales and checklists; and Expansion, in which students apply the strategies to new situations and tasks. The study implemented this model with the experimental group over two and a half months. (ABEER AL-GHAZO (2016))
