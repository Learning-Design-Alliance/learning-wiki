# National palliative care competency framework for undergraduate medical curricula

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A Dutch national framework developed by Pieters Jolien et al. that defines six palliative-care competency domains, 46 enabling competencies, and expected autonomy levels for undergraduate medical students and newly graduated doctors.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **National palliative care competency framework for undergraduate medical curricula (six key competencies, 46 items, six-point mastery scale)**: A validated competency framework organizing palliative care education around six key competencies — communication, advance care planning, pain and symptom management, working in a multidisciplinary team, end-of-life care, and personal development and well-being — each with three to ten enabling competencies, "resulting in a list totaling 46 items". Each item is rated on a six-point Likert scale from 'Not applicable' to independent workplace execution with a supervisor on call, so the framework specifies not only content but the level of autonomy expected at graduation. It was validated by a Delphi panel of five stakeholder groups and is intended "to guide teaching about palliative care" and serve as a basis for integrating palliative care into existing curricula. (Pieters Jolien et al. (2020))
