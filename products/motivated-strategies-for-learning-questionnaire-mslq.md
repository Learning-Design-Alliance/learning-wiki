# Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
The MSLQ is Pintrich and colleagues' self-report assessment of students' motivation and learning-strategy use, comprising motivation and self-regulated-learning scales for college and high-school learners.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **MSLQ-based self-regulation measure and self-developed ten-question geometry achievement test**: The study used "a part of the Pintrich and de Groot's motivated strategies for learning questionnaire (MSLQ)" together with a self-developed achievement test. The MSLQ version contained 22 questions on a five-point Likert scale measuring self-efficacy beliefs, beliefs about goals, and beliefs about values and interests. The achievement test consisted of ten questions, five measuring low levels of the cognitive domain and five the upper levels. Cronbach's alpha was used to assess validity of the results. (Ali Hatami (2015))
- **Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ) motivation section, Turkish adaptation**: A self-report questionnaire measuring motivational orientations on a 7-point Likert scale, whose motivation section contains 31 items across six subscales: intrinsic goal orientation, extrinsic goal orientation, task value, control of learning beliefs, self-efficacy for learning and performance, and test anxiety. In this study "only the motivation section of MSLQ was used to determine the change of students' perceived motivation", using Sungur's (2004) Turkish adaptation piloted with 324 students, with Cronbach alphas reported per subscale. (Yalçınkaya et al. (2012))
- **Motivated Strategies for Learning Questionnaire (MSLQ) subscales for motivation, self-efficacy, critical thinking, and self-regulation**: The MSLQ by Pintrich (1991) is a self-report instrument assessing college students' motivational orientations and learning strategies. The article states "The questionnaire consists of 33 items, and participants respond on a 7-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (absolutely disagree) to 7 (absolutely agree)." In this study it provided subscales for intrinsic goal orientation (4 items), extrinsic goal orientation (4 items), self-efficacy (8 items, α = .92), critical thinking (5 items, α = .86), and self-regulation (12 items, α = .86), using a Greek translation. (Stavropoulou G et al. (2025))
