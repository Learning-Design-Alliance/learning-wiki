# Way2Work Maryland

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
A U.S. Department of Education Rehabilitation Services Administration-funded demonstration programme implemented by the Maryland Division of Rehabilitation Services with the University of Maryland to enhance customized education, vocational rehabilitation, and employment services for high-school students with disabilities.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Way2Work Maryland model of essential services for students with disabilities**: Way2Work Maryland is a demonstration model for high school students with disabilities that enhances and customizes education and employment services to improve college and career readiness. Its essential services included "an early referral to the vocational rehabilitation agency, a match to a community rehabilitation provider, opportunities for multiple work experiences, and numerous service collaboration activities." The model was funded by a U.S. Department of Education, Rehabilitation Services Administration grant to the Maryland Division of Rehabilitation Services, with the University of Maryland overseeing implementation and providing training and technical assistance. (Frank Martin et al. (2021))
- **Way2Work Maryland program with three major components: early referral, multiple work experiences, and service collaboration**: Way2Work Maryland is a transition program for high school students with disabilities developed and implemented by the Maryland Division of Rehabilitation Services and the University of Maryland. It includes "three major components: early referral to the vocational rehabilitation agency, multiple work experiences, and service collaboration with integrated training and technical assistance." Services were provided through high school graduation or for 16 to 24 months depending on enrollment date. (David Mann et al. (2021))
- **Way2Work Maryland impact evaluation: randomized controlled trial with 401 enrolled students and 24-month follow-up**: The impact evaluation used a randomized controlled trial design in which the program "randomly offered some participants enhanced Way2Work Maryland services (the treatment group)" while others received usual services (the control group). Between September 2017 and early January 2019 the program enrolled 401 high school students. Data came from follow-up surveys with enrollees and parents conducted 24 months after enrollment, administrative data, and program enrollment data. (David Mann et al. (2021))
