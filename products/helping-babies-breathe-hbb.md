# Helping Babies Breathe (HBB)

## Description
Helping Babies Breathe is a low-fidelity simulation-based neonatal-resuscitation training programme developed for and implemented with birth attendants, particularly in limited-resource settings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Helping Babies Breathe: a low-fidelity simulation program for neonatal resuscitation in limited resource settings**: Helping Babies Breathe (HBB) is described as "a simulation program using low fidelity equipment to teach neonatal resuscitation in limited resource settings", training birth attendants in essential resuscitation skills and implemented globally. The review reports it has improved early neonatal outcomes and fresh stillbirths in several settings, though impact is hard to measure due to lack of reliable data, and it was found highly cost-effective in a Tanzanian rural hospital study. (Yousef Nadya et al. (2022))
- **Helping Babies Breathe second edition curriculum and facilitator flip chart with expanded facilitator role**: The second edition of HBB is a skills-based neonatal resuscitation curriculum for birth attendants in resource-limited settings, updated with 2015 ILCOR CoSTR science, a revised action plan, and a facilitator flip chart in which each page carries background information and educational advice for active learning techniques. The flip chart guides facilitators before, during, and after a course, encourages integrating workshops within local health care programs, and supports an expanded role in which facilitators enable learning and facilitate linkages with clinic leadership for ongoing practice and quality improvement. (Kamath-Rayne BD et al. (2018))
- **Helping Babies Breathe (HBB) simulation-based neonatal resuscitation training program**: Helping Babies Breathe is "a simulation-based neonatal resuscitation program for low resource settings" focused on the initial resuscitation steps and bag-and-mask ventilation within the first 60 seconds after birth (The Golden Minute). Its materials include a Learner Workbook, a Facilitator Flip Chart, an Action Plan poster with color-coded care paths, and the NeoNatalie neonatal simulator, whose evaluation signs (cry, breathing, heart rate, chest rise) trainers can manipulate. Skills are assessed with a 17-item Knowledge Check, a BMV performance test, and OSCE A and OSCE B scenarios derived from the NRP megacode. In this study it was delivered through a two-level master-trainer cascade with a maximum 6:1 trainee-trainer ratio, three-day hands-on facility courses, and refresher training. (Helping Babies Breathe (HBB) training: What happens to knowledge and skills over time?)
