## Description
I-BEST is Washington State's program integrating adult basic education and occupational skills training so students can, as the report states, "learn literacy, math, work, and college-readiness skills and move into living wage jobs faster." Its signature feature is team teaching by a basic skills instructor and an occupational instructor, plus a dedicated navigator providing career counseling and "fill-the-gap" financial support, tutoring, advising, and emergency child care and transportation. It was developed by SBCTC, first implemented in 2006–2007, and replicated as Accelerating Opportunity and ACE.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Team teaching by a basic skills instructor and an occupational instructor during at least 50 percent of occupational training class time (25 percent minimum in Accelerating Opportunity)
- A dedicated I-BEST navigator providing career counseling and access to fill-the-gap financial support
#### Constraints
- The effects of I-BEST are not known for technical skill proficiency, postsecondary degree attainment, and medium- or long-term employment and earnings outcomes
- Some programs require full-time enrollment while others are offered part-time in the evenings or weekends

### Target Learners
- Adults in need of basic skills instruction in math, reading, or job skills at community colleges
- Individuals with skill levels lower than normally required to enroll in college-level programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic skills in literacy and math in the context of occupational training
- Industry-recognized credentials, certificates, or licenses

# Contextualized integrated basic skills and occupational training model

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 6 studies (5 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 3 of 6 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
I-BEST rests on an instructional model that integrates adult basic education with occupational skills training instead of the traditional sequence in which basic skills courses must be completed before college-level occupational training. The report states I-BEST "uses a contextualized instruction approach, where students learn basic skills in the context of their course of study" — for example, emphasizing medical terms in a nursing program. This model "aims to improve the motivation and achievement of students by providing them experiences where they can see the usefulness of basic skills instruction in their chosen field," offering an alternative to a traditional track that generally results in low rates of advancement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of adult basic education and occupational skills training so students can pursue credit-bearing, short-term certificate programs as well as college degrees
#### Constraints
- The traditional track of providing adult basic education prior to occupational training generally results in low rates of advancement between basic skills and occupational training

### Target Learners
- Adults referred to basic skills training, most of whom never enroll in college-level courses under the traditional sequence

### Target Learning Objectives
- Literacy, math, work, and college-readiness skills learned in the context of an occupational field
