Evaluating the Relationships Between Poverty and School Performance interactive data visualization gallery¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An interactive data-visualization gallery released and operated by NWEA Research to let users explore relationships between school poverty, achievement, and growth in the report's sample.

Components¶

Claims¶

  • Heavy weighting of achievement data in U.S. school evaluation clouds how much learning is actually occurring, particularly in high-poverty communities [+W]
  • Growth measures offer a clearer, more accurate picture of learning than achievement data, while achievement data remains critical for tracking student progress [+W]
  • School poverty is only weakly associated with median student growth in a school [+W]
  • Sixty percent of the highest-poverty schools also have above-average student growth [+W]
  • School poverty (%FRL) accounts for about 50% of a school's median student achievement [+W]
  • Lowest-income schools show more variable growth than highest-income schools, with more schools both below the 50th and above the 82nd growth percentile [+W]
  • About 46% of the lowest 5% achieving schools show the same growth as the highest 5% achieving schools [+W]
  • Growth of schools selected by fall achievement differs by subject: bottom 5% math schools tended to grow worse, while reading showed similar average growth [+W]
  • School median student growth percentiles correlate with student disadvantage [~W]
