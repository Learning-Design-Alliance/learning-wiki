## Description
A curriculum guide issued by an education authority that specifies technology-education content, instructional approaches, course sequences, and time allocations for junior- and senior-high programmes.

## Components

- **Recommended scope and sequence with per-cluster course time allocations**: The guide's recommended scope and sequence specifies content emphasis and instructional approach at each level, with concrete scheduling for junior high cluster exploration courses. For example, the Manufacturing cluster offers an "Enriched program: 1 period daily for 36 weeks" and a "Minimum program: 1 period daily for 18 weeks," while Graphic Communications and Energy and Power clusters offer 18-week enriched and 12-week minimum programs; a period is considered the equivalent of 50-55 minutes. ## Description
The guide differentiates the function of industrial arts by school level: elementary school provides INSIGHTS into and AWARENESS of the industrial-technical society; junior high courses provide EXPLORATION of the adult world of work and support of SELF CONCEPTS during early adolescence; senior high provides concentrated, specialized study with career EXPLORATION IN DEPTH and BEGINNING SPECIALIZATION. Operational level goals and expected outcomes are listed for each level under career emphasis and industrial-technological emphasis headings.

#### Requirements
- Goals selected for each level must be consistent with the general goals identified for the total program
#### Constraints
- Senior high programs must accommodate a wide range of interests and life goals, offering vocational, avocational, and professional potential

### Target Learners
- Elementary, junior high, and senior high students

### Target Goals
- Career awareness and exploration, technological literacy, self-concept development

# Industrial arts pyramid program across operational levels

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The guide recommends a pyramid program structure (Figure 4) in which elementary level activities introduce industrial technology in the world of work with emphasis on self-concepts, insights, and awareness; junior high core courses provide comprehensive exploration of industrial-technological clusters; and senior high cluster specialization courses enable exploration in depth and beginning specialization. The pyramid concept is "aimed at helping all students to become familiar with the values of an industrial-technological society, to integrate these values into their personal value system, and to implement these values."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program offerings at each level must be consistent with operational level goals and the maturity of students
#### Constraints
- Elementary activities should not be rigidly structured or closely defined in scope and sequence because they depend on school philosophy, facilities, and teacher education

### Target Learners
- Elementary, junior high, and senior high school students

### Target Goals
- Career and industrial-technological awareness, self-concept development, exploration, and specialization
