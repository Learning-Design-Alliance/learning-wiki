## Description
AstroAnimation.org is an online collection created by the AstroAnimation program containing freely downloadable student animations of astronomy research for public outreach and educational use.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **AstroAnimation.org collection of forty-seven freely available student animations**: A released online artifact collecting the student films produced in the program. As the authors state, "As of early 2021, there are forty-seven animations covering a range of topics"; each film is typically about two minutes long and freely available for download under a licence allowing non-commercial use. The animations have been featured at science fiction conventions, art festivals, a STEAM festival, public outreach presentations, and in college and university science classes. (Arcadias et al. (2021))
- **Astro-Animation class and its freely available collection of 73 short animations**: An undergraduate course at the Maryland Institute College of Art, co-taught by an animator and a NASA astrophysicist, with "two interlocking parts, one focused on astronomy and one on animation". Students work with NASA scientist mentors for a semester, visit NASA Goddard to present and screen films, and produce animations on topics such as black holes and gravitational waves with "underlying scientific rigour". The collection of 73 short animations is freely available and used by scientists, teachers, and outreach specialists. (Laurence ARCADIAS et al. (2024))
- **Astro-Animation facilitator toolkit**: A support resource developed by the project for workshop facilitators, "including step -by-step instructions, visual aids, and evaluation materials". It emerged from the public workshops and traveling exhibition strand of the project and was iteratively refined: the outcomes section reports that feedback led to clearer animation instruction, improved technical workflow, and better facilitation strategies. It is intended to let others run Astro-Animation-style art/science activities in informal settings. (Laurence ARCADIAS et al. (2024))
