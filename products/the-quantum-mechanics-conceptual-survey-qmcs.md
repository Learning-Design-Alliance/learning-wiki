# The Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey (QMCS)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A 12-question research-based multiple-choice survey developed by McKagan et al. to assess sophomore-level students’ conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics and compare instructional methods.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The Quantum Mechanics Conceptual Survey (QMCS), a 12-question research-based conceptual survey of quantum mechanics**: The QMCS is a 12-question multiple-choice survey measuring students' conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics, intended, in the authors' words, "to measure the relative eﬀectiveness of diﬀerent instructional methods in modern p hysics courses". It was developed iteratively over roughly three years through faculty interviews, textbook and syllabi review, student observations and interviews, expert review, and statistical analysis. It is a survey rather than a concept inventory, covering a broad but partial range of modern physics topics. (McKagan et al. (2010))
- **Quantum Mechanics Survey (QMS): a 31-item multiple-choice conceptual survey of one-dimensional quantum mechanics**: The QMS is a 31-item multiple-choice survey assessing the conceptual framework of quantum mechanics of one particle in one spatial dimension, covering possible wavefunctions, bound and scattering states, measurement, expectation values, stationary states, the role of the Hamiltonian, and time dependence. The authors state: "we developed the Quantum Mechanics Survey (QMS), a 31-item multiple-choice test." Items were developed from instructor consultations, free-response questions, think-aloud interviews, and statistical item analysis, with distractors built from common misconceptions. (Guangtian Zhu et al. (2011))
- **Quantum Mechanics Survey (QMS): a 31-item research-based conceptual multiple-choice test on 1D quantum mechanics**: The QMS is a 31-item multiple-choice conceptual survey of non-relativistic quantum mechanics of one particle in one spatial dimension, covering possible wavefunctions, bound/scattering states, measurement, expectation values, the Hamiltonian, stationary vs. non-stationary states, and time dependence. Each question has one correct and four alternative choices. Its reliability coefficient is 0.87, "which is reasonably good from the standards of test design," and it was validated through instructor consultation, open-response questions, and think-aloud interviews. (Singh et al. (2010))
