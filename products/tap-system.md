A whole-school teacher evaluation, compensation, career-ladder, mentoring, and professional-development programme developed and operated by the National Institute for Excellence in Teaching.

## Components

- **Teacher Advancement Program (TAP): a whole-school teacher evaluation, compensation, and professional development model**: The Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) is described as "a whole-school approach to evaluating and compensating teachers and providing professional development opportunities". Its stated aims are twofold: "to both improve teaching and help schools attract and retain good teachers". The program operates at the whole-school level rather than targeting individual teachers in isolation. (Steven Glazerman et al. (2007))
- **Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) whole-school intervention**: TAP is a whole-school intervention that aims to improve schools by raising teacher quality. It provides teachers with "opportunities for professional growth, promotion to school leadership roles without leaving the classroom, structured feedback, and performance-based compensation." Chicago Public Schools began implementing TAP in 2007, and this report evaluates its first-year impacts in the district's K-8 schools. (Steven Glazerman et al. (2009))
- **Teacher Advancement Program (TAP): performance pay, professional development, and advancement opportunities tied to student achievement growth**: The Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) is a school-level teacher incentive model implemented in Chicago Public Schools as a Teacher Incentive Fund grantee. As the report describes it, "The TAP model offers teachers performance pay, professional development, and advancement opportunities based in part on their students' achievement growth." The program bundles financial incentives with career-advancement structures and embedded professional development, and its incentive component is tied to measured student achievement growth rather than to status measures alone. (Steven Glazerman (2010))
- **TAP's four-component whole-school design combining growth, leadership, feedback, and performance pay**: TAP bundles four school-wide components: professional growth opportunities, promotion to school leadership roles without leaving the classroom, structured feedback, and performance-based compensation. The report describes it as "a whole-school intervention that aims to improve schools by raising teacher quality," evaluating whether these combined changes affect teachers and students. (Steven Glazerman et al. (2009))
- **TAP: The System for Teacher and Student Advancement**: TAP is a whole-school reform model Ascension Parish implemented in two sanctioned high-poverty schools. “The TAP system focuses on four interrelated elements to improve teachers’ instruction”: ongoing applied professional learning, instructionally focused accountability, multiple career paths, and performance-based compensation. Evaluations inform professional development, advancement, and pay, and educators participate in regular collaboration and self-reflection within the school day. (Sarah Almy et al. (2012))
