# Physics Measurement Questionnaire (PMQ)

## Description
The PMQ is a free-response assessment developed for measuring undergraduate physics students’ understanding of measurement uncertainty through coded written explanations, often administered before and after instruction.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Physics Measurement Questionnaire (PMQ) with the new CU coding scheme**: The PMQ is a research-based open-response assessment of student understanding of statistical measurement uncertainty, based on a ball-rolling experiment, with probes covering decisions from data collection to comparing results. The authors created a new coding scheme of "12-16 codes per probe," each code aligned with the point paradigm, the set paradigm, or unknown reasoning, and consolidated probe responses into student-level paradigms (point-like, set-like, mixed). (Benjamin Pollard et al. (2020))

### Claims
- [Among students in a traditional large introductory lab course, overall paradigm distributions differed from pre-test to post-test, with more set-like and fewer point-like and mixed students, in one group with no comparison group.](../claims/pmq-paradigm-distribution-shift-toward-set-like.md) [+M]
- [Most students show inconsistent (mixed) reasoning across PMQ probes before instruction, and a sizable fraction remain inconsistent after instruction](../claims/pmq-mixed-inconsistent-reasoning-persists.md) [+M]
- [In one single-group pre-post study, PMQ signals were significant and sensitive to the range of student understanding in a large introductory lab course at CU](../claims/pmq-significant-signals-large-lab-course.md) [+M]
