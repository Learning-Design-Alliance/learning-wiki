# PRiSE (Physics Research in School Environments)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A UK programme bringing active-physics-researcher-mentored, open-ended research projects to 14–18-year-old school students through partnerships among researchers, schools, and teachers.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **PRiSE 'research in schools' provision framework**: PRiSE is "a collection of physics-based 'research in schools' projects (see Table 1) brought together under a coherent provision framework" — SCREAM, MUSICS, Planet Hunting with Python and ATLAS Open Data — running from the start of the UK academic year to before the spring/Easter break. Students work in groups of typically five, advised to spend 1–2 hours a week, supported by active researchers in defined roles (Outreach Officer, Project Lead, Researcher) and by teachers at all stages. It is free to schools, targets diversity via school-level metrics, and aims to build students' ability, confidence and skills toward physics/STEM aspirations and teachers' professional practice. (Martin O. Archer et al. (2021))
- **PRiSE multi-stage extended research-project pattern with layered support interventions**: A programme-level pattern in which school student groups undertake current-research projects across three sequenced activity stages — prescribed work, independent project, and writing up — scaffolded by a set of interventions (assignment, kick-off, school visits, webinars, ad hoc support, comments on drafts, and a concluding conference) plus recurring resources (project guide, poster/flyer, webpages, 'how to' guides). The prescribed stage exists because "independent research in STEM is probably unfamiliar to the students"; the independent stage lets groups "set their own research questions" to gain "independence and ownership". The pattern runs over roughly six months so schools get opportunities they could not provide without external input. (Martin O. Archer et al. (2021))
- **PRiSE: a scalable framework of university-mentored, school-based physics research projects**: PRiSE ('Physics Research in School Environments') is "a scalable framework for independent research projects based in current physics research that are mentored by active researchers". Students aged 14–18 work in groups 1–2 hours per week for about six months on projects such as SCREAM, MUSICS, PHwP and ATLAS, culminating in a conference on university campus. Support comes through workshops, school visits, monthly webinars, printed/multimedia resources, and ad hoc emails. (Martin O. Archer et al. (2021))
- **PRiSE framework of researcher-mentored independent research projects with a staged six-month support structure**: PRiSE (Physics Research in School Environments) is "a framework for independent research projects for 14–18 year-old school students that are based around cutting-edge physics research and mentored by active researchers". Four projects have been developed at QMUL since 2014: SCREAM (cosmic rays), MUSICS (magnetospheric physics), Planet Hunting with Python, and ATLAS Open Data. Support runs September to March through five annual intervention stages — assignment, kick-off, visit, comments, and conference — plus ad hoc communications, delivered via a suite of bespoke resources. (Martin O. Archer (2021))
