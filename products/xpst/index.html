xPST¶

Description¶

xPST is an authoring system and tutor engine developed by Stephen B. Gilbert and colleagues that lets instructional designers create embedded, model-tracing-like tutors for third-party software through TutorLink plug-ins and instruction files.

Components¶

  • The xPST architecture: a model-tracing-like tutor engine overlaid on third-party software via TutorLink plug-ins and a plain-text instruction file: xPST is an authoring system that produces tutors behaving like model-tracing tutors without a formal cognitive model: authors write an xPST Instruction File with Mappings, Sequence, and Feedback sections, and the xPST Tutor Engine checks each learner action and returns hints, just-in-time messages, or error marking. TutorLink plug-ins mediate between the engine and third-party applications, with a Listener Module that "eavesdrops on user 's actions" and a Presentation Module that displays feedback in the host interface. The article presents this as a way to let instructional designers build embedded tutors on existing software. (Stephen B. Gilbert et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • Students tutored by an xPST-created web statistics tutor (xSTAT) scored higher on exam transfer problems than untutored students [+M]
  • In the Paint.NET embedded-tutor comparison, task performance differed among book, movie, and ITS groups, with the book group scoring significantly higher than the movie group and the ITS group in between [~M]
  • Ten non-programmer graduate students quickly learned early Web xPST and produced mostly high-quality tutors, with authoring time dropping across successive tasks [+W]
  • Five non-programmers authoring with final Web xPST produced 74 effective statistics tutors in a month, with coding a small share of total authoring time [+W]
  • In a head-to-head comparison, xPST and CTAT both let non-programmers create high-caliber tutors, with no significant time differences by the third problem [+M]
  • Paint.NET ITS users rated the system's usability significantly lower than book and movie condition students [-W]
