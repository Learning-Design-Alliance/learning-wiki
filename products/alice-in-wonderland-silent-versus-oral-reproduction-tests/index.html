Alice in Wonderland silent-versus-oral reproduction tests¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A reading-comprehension assessment developed by Mead that has pupils read an Alice in Wonderland passage silently and orally, then reproduces and scores the points recalled under each condition.

Components¶

Claims¶

  • Sixth-grade pupils reproduced a greater percentage of possible points by silent than by oral reading, with every class and 71% of children doing better silently [+M]
  • Across grades 3–10 (grade 9 excluded), 15 of 17 classes and 70% of children did better by the silent method of reading [+M]
  • Per-grade results in Table I show the silent method yielded a higher percentage of points reproduced of points read than the oral method [+W]
  • Knowing that reproduction will be demanded slows reading rate across all readers and methods [+W]
