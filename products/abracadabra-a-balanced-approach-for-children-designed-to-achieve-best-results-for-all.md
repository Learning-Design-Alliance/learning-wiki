# ABRACADABRA (A Balanced Approach for Children Designed to Achieve Best Results for All)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
ABRACADABRA is a publicly available web-based literacy program developed for elementary students, including those with or at risk for reading difficulties, and provides customizable activities in phonological awareness, phonics, vocabulary, comprehension, and fluency.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **ABRACADABRA web-based literacy program**: ABRACADABRA (A Balanced Approach for Children Designed to Achieve Best Results for All) is a publicly available, web-based literacy program that was the most frequently evaluated program in this meta-analysis's corpus, used in 12 of the 50 publicly available treatment conditions. The article states it "was developed based on the recommendations of the NRP (2000) report and targets a range of skills, including phonological awareness, phonics, vocabulary, comprehension, fluency" and that its "Activities in ABRACADABRA can be customized to a student's specific abilities and challenges." The article cites a meta-analysis of 17 ABRACADABRA studies estimating a weighted average effect of g = 0.26. (Dahl-Leonard et al. (2024))
- **ABRA (Abracadabra) web-based balanced literacy programme and its matched non-ICT paper-and-pencil version**: ABRA is a 20-week online literacy programme of phonic fluency and comprehension activities built around age-appropriate electronic texts, delivered by a teaching assistant to groups of three to five pupils in four 15-minute sessions per week. The report describes it as "a modular 'game'-based balanced literacy intervention that is fixed in content". The non-ICT version matched every ABRA activity with a paper-and-pencil equivalent using the same stories, vocabulary, questions, words and letter sounds, differing only in delivery medium. Average cost per pupil per year over three years was £8.52 (ICT) and £8.49 (non-ICT), frontloaded into the first year. (Sandra McNally et al. (2016))
