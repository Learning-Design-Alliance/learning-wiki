# 30-question multiple-choice test on rotational and rolling motion concepts with concise justifications

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A researcher-developed conceptual physics assessment by Lorenzo G. Rimoldini and colleagues that measures introductory and intermediate students’ understanding of rotational and rolling motion through multiple-choice items requiring concise answer justifications.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **30-question multiple-choice test on rotational and rolling motion concepts with reliability α 0.68–0.82**: A 30-question multiple-choice test covering moment of inertia, rotational kinetic energy, angular speed/velocity, angular acceleration, torque, rolling (relative motion), rolling (role of friction and other parameters), and a sliding/tumbling cube on an inclined plane. The final version "was administered to 652 students from seven calculus- and algebra-based introducto ry physics courses (including one honor class)", with concise justifications required for each answer and distractor design based on difficulties identified in interviews and free-response questions. The authors state it "can help assess the eﬀectiveness of strategies to improve student understanding of these concepts." (Lorenzo G. Rimoldini et al. (2005))

### Claims
- [Written test scores were 44–61% on average in nonhonor classes and 75% in an honor class, with difficulties consistent with the interviews](../claims/rotational-test-scores-44-61-nonhonor-75-honor.md) [+M]
- [Students widely believed friction must slow rolling motion, and question (14) on friction in rolling was the most difficult test question outside the honor class](../claims/friction-slows-rolling-misconception-question-14-hardest.md) [+M]
