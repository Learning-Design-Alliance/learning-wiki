# eCREST (electronic Clinical Reasoning Educational Simulation Tool)

## Description
eCREST is an online patient-simulation software tool developed by its authors for clinical-reasoning training in medical and physician-assistant education.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **eCREST online patient simulation tool for clinical reasoning training**: eCREST (electronic Clinical Reasoning Educational Simulation Tool) is an online patient simulation training tool in which students enter a virtual waiting room, select a patient video, select 5 differentials, ask questions, order bedside tests and physical exams, revisit and revise their differential diagnoses, finalize a diagnosis and management plan, and receive GP feedback videos and reflection prompts. The article describes it as proprietary software available free to educational settings that contribute anonymized data. Cases were developed with clinicians, researchers, and patients, with expert GP panels defining essential questions, tests, exams, and relevant diagnoses. (Thorpe Alistair PhD; Kassianos Angelos P PhD; Plackett Ruth PhD; Krishnamurthy Vinodh PhD; Kambouri Maria A PhD; Sheringham Jessica FFPH (2025))
- **Clinical Reasoning Evaluation Simulation Tool (CREST) four-item essay-based assessment of clinical reasoning**: The study assessed clinical reasoning using four items of the Clinical Reasoning Evaluation Simulation Tool (CREST) developed by Liaw et al., adjusted for this study: data interpretation, linking signs and symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment. Students answered PPH vignette essays scored on a five-point Likert scale by two blinded faculty experts; the tool had "a content validity of 0.93" and Cronbach's alpha 0.92, with scores ranging 12–60 and an accomplishment rate of 30.0. (Ulfa Yunefit et al. (2021))
- **eCREST — the electronic Clinical Reasoning Educational Simulation Tool**: eCREST is an online patient simulation (OPS) in which students watch videos of actors playing patients presenting to their primary care physician with respiratory problems that could indicate serious conditions like lung cancer. "The student gathers information from the patient, while continually being prompted to review their differential diagnosis", then makes a final differential diagnosis and receives feedback after each of three cases. Cases were co-written with real patients who helped identify pertinent clinical and behavioural characteristics. eCREST prompts reflection throughout the consultation and provides feedback enabling reflection on performance afterwards. (Plackett R et al. (2020))
