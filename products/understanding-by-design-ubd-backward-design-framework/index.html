Understanding by Design (UbD) backward design framework¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A curriculum-design framework developed by Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe that guides educators to plan from desired results through evidence and learning experiences.

Components¶

  • Understanding by Design (UbD) and its backward design approach: Understanding by Design is a unit-design framework in which teachers act as designers who plan for meaningful, transferable learning rather than content coverage. It is dominated by the backward design approach, in which teachers determine which evidences they want to reach before planning what and how to teach. The framework consists of three stages: desired results, evidence, and learning plan, using GRASPS performance-task criteria and WHERETO principles. In this study, teachers integrated UbD into EFL Reading and Listening & Speaking courses to target student motivation and knowledge transfer. (Yurtseven et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • Iranian intermediate EFL learners taught writing through backward design improved significantly from pre-test to post-test, but not significantly more than a randomly assigned forward-design control group [~W]
  • Backward design instruction was not significantly more effective than forward design instruction for Iranian EFL learners' writing ability [~M]
  • UbD-integrated instruction significantly improves integrative motivation of A-level EFL students with a large effect size [+M]
  • Focus group interviews identify motivation (fun, attractiveness, clarity) and knowledge transfer (real life, speaking) as student themes about UbD [+M]
  • UbD-integrated instruction shows no significant motivation difference for C-level (lowest proficiency) EFL students [~M]
