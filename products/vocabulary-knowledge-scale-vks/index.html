Vocabulary Knowledge Scale (VKS)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A researcher-developed vocabulary knowledge assessment created by Paribakht and Wesche to track learners’ acquisition and development of targeted words.

Components¶

  • Vocabulary Knowledge Scale (VKS), 5-point self-report instrument adapted with 18 target verbs: The study used Wesche and Paribakht's (1996) 5-point self-report Vocabulary Knowledge Scale, adapted with 18 infrequently used target verbs drawn from two Family Guy episodes. The scale "allows to specify the stages of vocabulary acquisition from first exposure to production" and shows students' partial knowledge; levels run from not remembering the word to using it in a sentence. Responses at levels 3-5 were checked for accuracy and downgraded one level if incorrect. (Ali Karakaş et al. (2011))
  • Vocabulary Knowledge Scale (VKS) format test adapted to Turkish for social studies vocabulary: The VKS is a self-report vocabulary measure developed by Wesche and Paribakht (1996) with four stages ranging from "Do not know the word" to being able to use the word in a sentence. The researcher adapted it to Turkish and administered it as pretest and posttest for 15 target social studies words such as public opinion, democracy and sovereignty. Scoring runs from 1 point for unknown words to 4 points for correct original-sentence usage. (İlter (2016))

Claims¶

  • Classroom experiments test whether instructional procedures that increase the salience and cognitive processing of targeted words in reading texts can support L2 vocabulary acquisition [+M] (word knowledge tracked with the VKS)
  • Both subtitle and no-subtitle groups improved significantly on the VKS from pre-test to post-test after watching cartoons [+M]
  • Graphic-organizer instruction produces higher productive word knowledge (VKS Stage 4) than the C(2)QU contextual process [+M]
