# Diagnostic Analysis of Nonverbal Accuracy (DANVA)

## Description
The DANVA is a standardized assessment developed by researchers to measure facial and vocal emotion-recognition accuracy through emotion-labeled photographs and audio recordings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Diagnostic Analysis of Nonverbal Accuracy (DANVA) as a measure of adult facial and vocal affect recognition**: The DANVA is the standardized instrument this study used to assess nonverbal affect recognition in adults, measuring "nonverbal facial and vocal affect recognition skills" through 24 adult face photos and 24 audio-taped sentence recordings, each depicting happy, sad, angry, or fearful emotions at two intensity levels. Participants view each face for no more than 5 seconds or hear each recording, then identify the emotion. Scores are error counts by channel, emotion, and intensity. The article reports internal consistency of .78 to .90 for adult facial stimuli and .75 for vocal stimuli among middle-aged adults, with test-retest reliability of .84 over two months. (Miller et al. (2011))
