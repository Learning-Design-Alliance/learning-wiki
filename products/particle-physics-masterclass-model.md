## Description
A CERN- and Fermilab-associated outreach programme in which high school students analyze real collider data with physicist mentors and compare findings through an inter-institute videoconference.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **International Masterclasses**: International Masterclasses are all-day events in which students analyze event displays from LHC data and build statistical results including mass histograms, experiencing being "particle physicists for a day." The paper states that in International Masterclasses "students have the opportunity to analyze event displays from LHC data and use these to build statistical results, including mass histograms." Participants who have done this would likely find a discovery mass plot accessible. (Kenneth Cecire (2015))
- **International Masterclasses in particle physics (Pisa INFN implementation)**: The International Masterclasses for Particle Physics give high-school students the opportunity to "be particle physicists for a day": they attend introductory lectures on the standard model, accelerators and detectors, make measurements on real data collected at CERN by the LHC experiments, and present findings in a video linkup with CERN or Fermilab and student groups in other countries. The Pisa INFN unit has participated since 2005, hosting more than a hundred students aged 18-19 each year, selected by schools with attention to the school's gender distribution. A questionnaire given after the videoconference assesses the day's response. (Sandra Leone (2016))
- **The International Masterclass day: presentations, paired data analysis, tutor-supported measurement, group discussion, and a videoconference**: International Masterclasses follow a recurring one-day structure for high school students. Students attend presentations on experimental particle physics and the measurement they will analyze, then examine authentic event displays event-by-event, categorizing events and combining results. The article states that "students work in pairs—two to a computer and data set—to check each other as they work as well as make the analysis more efficient." The day ends with a discussion with tutors and a videoconference with up to four other institutions and physicist moderators at CERN, Fermilab, or TRIUMF. (K. Cecire (2017))
- **One-day particle physics masterclass: authentic data analysis in pairs with circulating physicist mentors**: A one-day event at a university or laboratory in which high-school students analyze authentic LHC event displays with physicist mentors. The day includes an ice-breaker, lessons on the Standard Model and data analysis, and a facility tour; students then "work in pairs (two students at one computer) to analyze events to help and challenge each other", relying on two-fold coincidence of opinions, while physicists circulate to help, and the day ends with a CERN-moderated videoconference. (K. Cecire et al. (2017))
