## Description
A validated German behavioural-marker rating system used by instructors to assess anaesthesiology students’ planning and problem-solving, teamwork and leadership, and team-orientation skills during simulation training.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **AS-NTS behavioural marker system for rating students' non-technical skills**: The German version of "Anaesthesiology students´ Non-Technical skills" (AS-NTS) is a validated behavioural marker system used by instructors to rate non-technical skills. It is composed of three dimensions — planning tasks, prioritising and problem-solving; teamwork and leadership; team orientation — rated on a five-point Likert scale (1 = very good; 5 = very poor) with behaviourally anchored rating examples clarifying what good or poor performance looks like on each dimension. In this trial it served both as the primary outcome measure and as the content students learned to apply for debriefing. (Moll-Khosrawi Parisa et al. (2021))
- **AS-NTS: a three-dimension behavioural marker system for rating undergraduates' non-technical skills in anaesthesiology simulation**: AS-NTS is a rating tool developed to assess non-technical skills in undergraduate emergency medicine and anaesthesiology education. It rates performance on three dimensions — "Planning tasks, prioritizing and problem solving", "Teamwork and leadership", and "Team orientation" — each on a five-point Likert scale, with behaviourally anchored rating examples clarifying what "good" or "poor" performance looks like. It was designed to be usable without video recording or extended rater training. (Moll-Khosrawi P et al. (2019))
- **Undergraduate-adapted NTS taxonomy: three symbiosed dimensions reflecting precursors of expert non-technical skills**: The article proposes that non-technical skills taxonomies built for experienced anaesthesiologists are "not one to one transferable to undergraduates" and should be adapted to precursors and underlying elements of skills. AS-NTS operationalises this by symbiosing "Decision making" and "Task management" into "Planning tasks, prioritizing and problem solving", and adding "problem solving" as "a less complex proxy of decision making" for undergraduate scenarios. (Moll-Khosrawi P et al. (2019))
- **Stepwise empirical development of a rating tool: literature review, expert group, focus group, field observation, then validation**: The article develops AS-NTS through four steps combining qualitative and quantitative methods: an expert-group literature search producing an NTS list, focus groups and half-structured interviews building a hierarchical structure, field observations testing whether skills were observable, and implementation and validation across 98 simulation scenarios. Skills not observable in enough field observations were excluded following the guideline that "items to be excluded, if they are not observable in at least 50% of field observations". (Moll-Khosrawi P et al. (2019))
