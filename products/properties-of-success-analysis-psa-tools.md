## Description
A three-part assessment-design toolkit created by Saplan, Abramovich, and Wardrip that helps makerspace and informal-learning educators articulate definitions of success and align assessment features with them.

### Claims

- [Educators perceive success in maker programs as observable at multiple timescales, from immediately in the moment to over multiple programs or years](../claims/success-multiple-timescales.md) [+W]
- [The subject of success in library makerspaces is often someone other than the participating patron, such as facilitators or parents](../claims/subject-of-success-beyond-patron.md) [+W]
- [The intended audience of assessment results, whether internal or external stakeholders, shapes what an assessment for a library makerspace should do](../claims/audience-shapes-assessment-design.md) [+W]
- [Library maker educators rely on both quantitative metrics and qualitative evidence to judge whether their definitions of success are met](../claims/quantitative-and-qualitative-evidence-makerspaces.md) [+W]
- [Library staff and maker educators describe success in maker programs through four themes: who demonstrates success, how it is demonstrated, what makes it visible, and with whom it is shared](../claims/four-themes-success-library-makerspaces.md) [+W]
- [Staff and educators describe success as demonstrated either through acquiring skills and mindsets or through participating behaviors, aligning with Sfard's acquisition and participation metaphors](../claims/success-acquisition-participation-metaphors.md) [+W]
