## Description
A student-centered instructional framework that moves initial content exposure outside class and uses facilitated in-class time for collaborative application and higher-order learning.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Flipped classroom model as a student-centered method that moves content delivery outside class and reserves class time for collaborative application**: The flipped classroom model (FCM) is defined in the article as "the student-centered method of teaching that enables students to absorb theoretical material on their own at home and apply what they learn in school". It moves content delivery outside formal class time via extensive notes, video-recorded lectures, or other means, and uses class time for collaborative activities. The article aligns it with Bloom's taxonomy, allowing higher-order thinking skills during class with teacher guidance and peer support. In this study, FCM was operationalized as weekly audio-PowerPoint materials delivered before in-class, teacher-facilitated interactive sessions. (Chikeme Paulina Chigwara et al. (2024))
- **The Inverted Classroom Model: a blended-learning method reversing the order of knowledge transmission and application**: The article defines the ICM as a blended-learning arrangement in which "a self-directed learning phase (individual phase) takes place before the classroom-learning phase". In the online phase factual knowledge is conveyed; the classroom phase is then used to assimilate and apply that knowledge, reversing the traditional sequence of lecture-then-self-study. The article grounds the model in Bloom's revised taxonomy: students accomplish lower-order processes independently before class and higher-order processes in class with peer and instructor support. (Tolks D et al. (2016))
