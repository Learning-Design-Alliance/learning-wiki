# Coursera HCI peer grading dataset: 63,199 peer grades from 7,240 students across two course offerings

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A research dataset released by Chris Piech and colleagues containing 63,199 peer grades from two Coursera Human–Computer Interaction course offerings using calibrated peer grading.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Coursera HCI peer grading dataset: 63,199 peer grades from 7,240 students across two course offerings**: Two consecutive Coursera offerings of the Human Computer Interaction course taught by Scott Klemmer, using a calibrated peer grading system. Per assignment, each student evaluated five randomly selected submissions (one a ground-truth submission) on a rubric and was evaluated by four classmates; the final score was the median of peer grades. The dataset comprises 3,607 students (HCI1) and 3,633 students (HCI2), 13,972 submissions, and 63,199 peer grades — described as "the largest peer grading networks analysed to date". (Chris Piech et al. (2013))
