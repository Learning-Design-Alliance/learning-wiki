# Coursera HCI peer grading dataset: 63,199 peer grades from 7,240 students across two course offerings

## Description
A research dataset released by Chris Piech and colleagues containing 63,199 peer grades from two Coursera Human–Computer Interaction course offerings using calibrated peer grading.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Coursera HCI peer grading dataset: 63,199 peer grades from 7,240 students across two course offerings**: Two consecutive Coursera offerings of the Human Computer Interaction course taught by Scott Klemmer, using a calibrated peer grading system. Per assignment, each student evaluated five randomly selected submissions (one a ground-truth submission) on a rubric and was evaluated by four classmates; the final score was the median of peer grades. The dataset comprises 3,607 students (HCI1) and 3,633 students (HCI2), 13,972 submissions, and 63,199 peer grades — described as "the largest peer grading networks analysed to date". (Chris Piech et al. (2013))

### Claims

- [Probabilistic peer grading models that estimate grader bias and reliability reduce RMSE against ground-truth grades by 33% and 31% on two HCI course offerings](../claims/probabilistic-peer-grading-reduces-rmse-over-30-percent.md) [+M]
- [Higher-scoring students tend to be more reliable graders (except the very best), and students are least biased when grading peers with similar scores](../claims/grader-grade-relates-to-grading-reliability.md) [+M]
- [Including grader bias and reliability as features improves prediction of future course participation from AUC 0.93 to 0.98, capturing an engagement dimension missed by assignment grade](../claims/grader-bias-reliability-predict-future-participation.md) [+M]
- [Estimating each grader's bias alone accounts for 95% of the RMSE reduction, while modeling grader reliability contributes little with only four grades per student](../claims/grader-bias-estimation-dominates-accuracy-gains.md) [+M]
- [Grading time shows a sweet spot: students grading around 20 minutes (time z-score near -0.25) have significantly lower residual standard deviations than slow graders, while snap graders are unreliable and inflate grades](../claims/grading-time-sweet-spot-twenty-minutes.md) [+M]
- [The model's posterior confidence estimates are conservative: over 95% of predictions the model claims 90–95% confidence in are correct](../claims/peer-grading-confidence-estimates-conservative.md) [+M]
