Mathematics Mastery¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Mathematics Mastery is Ark's whole-school mastery-based mathematics curriculum, supported by teacher training, curriculum-embedded professional development, and cross-school collaboration.

Components¶

  • Given its low per-pupil cost, Mathematics Mastery may represent a cost-effective change for primary schools to consider: The report concludes that, given the low per-pupil cost, Mathematics Mastery may represent a cost-effective change for primary schools to consider. First-year cost for a two-form primary school is approximately £7,460 including teacher training, giving an average per-pupil cost of around £131 in the first year, with cost per pupil likely to reduce in future years. The evaluators rate the evidence strength for the programme's effect as moderate and note the effect itself was not statistically significant in this trial. (Anna Vignoles et al. (2015))
  • Mathematics Mastery secondary implementation with training, resources and per-pupil cost of around £50 in year one: The evaluated implementation delivered the Mathematics Mastery programme to Year 7 pupils in 44 trial schools from London and the South East, with training and resources provided by the charity Ark. First-year cost is estimated at "approximately £7,460 in the first year for a secondary school, including teacher training costs", giving an average per-pupil cost of around £50 per year, likely to reduce in future years. The programme was later accredited by NCETM for CPD quality. (John Jerrim et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • Year 1 pupils in schools adopting Mathematics Mastery scored on average +0.10 standard deviations higher on the Number Knowledge post-test, an effect not statistically significant at the 5% level [+W]
  • Combining this Year 1 trial with a second randomised controlled trial of Mathematics Mastery in Year 7 yields a statistically significant average impact of one additional month's progress [+M]
  • Year 7 pupils in schools adopting Mathematics Mastery made a small, non-significant amount more progress than control pupils (effect size +0.06, about one month) [+W]
  • Process evaluation found a shift away from teaching procedures towards problem-solving, with increased use of discussion, objects and diagrams, but wide variation across schools [+M]
