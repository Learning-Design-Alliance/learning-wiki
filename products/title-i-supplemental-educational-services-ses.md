## Description
A federally mandated Title I programme administered through states and school districts in which approved public or private providers offer free tutoring and academic support outside the regular school day to eligible students.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Title I Supplemental Educational Services (SES) tutoring program**: SES are "tutoring or other academic support services offered outside the regular school day, at no charge to students or their families, by public or private organizations that have been approved by the state as SES providers." Districts must offer SES to low-income students in Title I schools missing AYP for a third year. In the study, services averaged 21.2 hours per student (SD 8.8), with 36 percent of participants tutored in both reading and math, 55 percent in reading only, and 9 percent in math only. (Deke et al. ## Description
SES are "tutoring or other academic support services offered outside the regular school day, at no charge to students or their families, by public or private organizations that have been approved by the state as SES providers." Districts must offer SES to low-income students in Title I schools missing AYP for a third year. In the study, services averaged 21.2 hours per student (SD 8.8), with 36 percent of participants tutored in both reading and math, 55 percent in reading only, and 9 percent in math only.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State-approved providers; districts must reserve up to 20 percent of Title I, Part A funds for SES and school-choice transportation
#### Constraints
- The study's estimates apply to students near the cutoff for an offer in oversubscribed districts, not to all eligible students

### Target Learners
- Low-income students in grades 3-8 in Title I schools that missed AYP for three consecutive years

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading achievement
- Mathematics achievement
