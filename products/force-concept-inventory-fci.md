# Force Concept Inventory (FCI)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
A standardized multiple-choice assessment developed by David Hestenes, Malcolm Wells, and Gregg Swackhamer to measure introductory students’ conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Force Concept Inventory pretest/post-test protocol for measuring Newtonian conceptual gains in introductory physics**: In this study the Force Concept Inventory (FCI) served as both pretest and post-test for examining 222 students in introductory physics courses taught by traditional lecture or Just-in-Time Teaching over five semesters. It provided both the overall normalized-gain comparison and the restricted Newton's Third Law item analysis, making it the measurement backbone of the study's instructional comparison. (S. P. Formica et al. (2010))
- **FCI-metric: compiled graphical item-characteristic-curve reference for the 30 FCI questions**: The FCI-metric (Fig. 4, parts a and b) is a compiled graphical representation of the fitted item characteristic functions of all 30 FCI questions, based on pre-test data from 2802 students (average score 15.80 of 30, SD 6.05). Each panel shows the ICF with "item parameters a, b, and c labeled on the graph", plus 56 grey error-bar points from proficiency bins of about 50 students each. It lets users compare question features and student performance against the large-scale norm. (Jing Wang et al. (2008))
- **Force Concept Inventory (FCI) as a criterion-referenced multiple-choice test of Newtonian force concepts**: The Force Concept Inventory is a standardised criterion-referenced multiple-choice test designed to assess student understanding of the most basic concepts in Newtonian physics, particular forces. As the article describes it, "The test has 30 questions covering six areas of understanding: kinematics, Newton's First, Second, and Third Laws, the super position principle, and types of forces". Each question has one correct Newtonian answer with distractors based on students' common misconceptions; a low score indicates an Aristotelian view while a high score (typically around 60% correct or higher) indicates Newtonian understanding. The article evaluates the Norwegian version of FCI translated by Angell and collaborators at University of Oslo. (J.R. Persson (2015))
- **Norwegian version of the Force Concept Inventory used at NTNU**: The article used a Norwegian translation of the FCI in its evaluation. As printed, "The Norwegian version of FCI used, was translated and developed by Angell and collaborators at University of Oslo [3]" (cited as a 2012 private communication). This instrument was administered voluntarily, with no extra credit given, as pre- and post-tests to three calculus-based introductory physics course groups at NTNU in Fall 2012, involving 182, 91 and 140 pre-test participants in TFY4104, TFY4115 and TFY4145 respectively. (J.R. Persson (2015))
- **Force Concept Inventory (FCI) six polarisation-inducing force-identification questions**: The Force Concept Inventory is described by the authors as "regarded as the de facto standard for concept inventories in Newtonian mechanics". Within its 30 questions, "six (that is, 20%) of the 30 questions basically ask only one thing: identify the forces acting on a given body". These six questions (sets 5, 11, 13, 18, 29, 30) were administered pre and post each second semester from AY 2011-12 to AY 2014-15 to 66 students, with item analysis focused on them. (Allan L. Alinea et al. (2016))
