# Force Concept Inventory (FCI)

## Description
A standardized multiple-choice assessment developed by David Hestenes, Malcolm Wells, and Gregg Swackhamer to measure introductory students’ conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Force Concept Inventory pretest/post-test protocol for measuring Newtonian conceptual gains in introductory physics**: In this study the Force Concept Inventory (FCI) served as both pretest and post-test for examining 222 students in introductory physics courses taught by traditional lecture or Just-in-Time Teaching over five semesters. It provided both the overall normalized-gain comparison and the restricted Newton's Third Law item analysis, making it the measurement backbone of the study's instructional comparison. (S. P. Formica et al. (2010))

### Claims
- [Introductory physics students taught with Just-in-Time Teaching showed larger overall Force Concept Inventory gains (37.6%±2.0%) than students in traditional lecture courses (17.9%±2.5%)](../claims/jitt-larger-fci-gains-than-lecture.md) [+M]
- [On Newton's Third Law questions specifically, Just-in-Time Teaching students gained 50.8%±4.1% while traditional lecture students gained only 6.6%±5.2%](../claims/jitt-newtons-third-law-question-gains.md) [+M]
