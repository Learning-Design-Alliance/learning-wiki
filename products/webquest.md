## Description
A technology-supported inquiry-oriented teaching framework introduced by Bernie Dodge that structures learning activities through introduction, task, process, resources, evaluation, and conclusion.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **WebQuest as a constructivist, technology-based teaching strategy with six structural parts**: The article presents WebQuest, introduced by Bernie Dodge in 1995, as "a new teaching strategy of the constructivist approach that uses computer environment", designed to ensure meaningful learning by combining technology with constructivism. It distinguishes short-term WebQuests (one to three class hours, acquiring and integrating information) from long-term ones (a week to a month, in-depth analysis), and defines the required parts: introduction, task, process, resource, evaluation, and conclusion. In this study the strategy was operationalized as non-routine, daily-life problem solving in groups with teacher-guided discussion. (Yenmez et al. (2017))
- **Web Quest Strategy as an inquiry-based, constructivist internet learning pattern**: The article presents Web Quest Strategy as "purposeful educational activities, guided by enquiry that depends on searching processes in the Internet to reach the correct information with less time and effort and to develop the students’ mental capabilities". Rooted in constructivist assumptions that learners build their own knowledge, it casts the learner as a traveler and explorer and comes in short-term (one to three classes) and long-term (one week to a month) forms. It is structured by six elements: introduction, task, process, sources, evaluation and conclusion. (AL-Edwan (2014))
- **Web Quest lesson plan structure: introduction, task, process, sources, evaluation, conclusion**: The article enumerates the recurring components of a Web Quest lesson: "Introduction: that provides the students with the cognitive background about the lesson's topic in a way that induces their motivation", followed by the task (the most important part, with sub tasks), the process (clearly explained steps and mechanisms), the sources (a curated resource list), evaluation (self-evaluation and teacher evaluation with rating scales and check lists), and a conclusion with recommendations and extension links. The researcher operationalized this structure in thirteen daily plans for the Natural Resources unit. (AL-Edwan (2014))
