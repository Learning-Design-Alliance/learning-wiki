# 25-item research-based multiple-choice test assessing conceptual knowledge of energy and momentum

## Description
A 25-item conceptual multiple-choice test developed by Chandralekha Singh and colleagues for assessing introductory physics students’ understanding of energy and momentum concepts.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **A research-based 25-item multiple-choice test assessing conceptual knowledge of energy and momentum**: The article introduces a research-based 25-item multiple-choice test exploring students’ conceptual knowledge of energy and momentum. It covers the work-energy theorem, conservation of mechanical energy, impulse-momentum theorem, and conservation of momentum, and can be administered as a pre-/post-test. The authors state it "can be used to compare the understanding of energy and momentum concepts in courses employing diﬀerent instructional designs and strategies." (Chandralekha Singh et al. (2016))
- **Energy and Momentum Conceptual Survey (EMCS)**: The EMCS is a validated conceptual assessment for introductory energy and linear momentum concepts: "It is a 25-question multiple-choice survey focused on energy and linear momentum." It uses the language typical of introductory courses and textbooks, and in this study it was administered as a paper scantron pre-test in the first week and post-test in the last weeks of both introductory and upper-level courses to benchmark conceptual growth and compare performance across levels. (Brundage et al. (2024))

### Claims
- [After traditional instruction, upper-level students score below two-thirds correct on 13 of 25 EMCS questions, and on every question where introductory students score below 50% post-test, upper-level students score below two-thirds](../claims/emcs-upper-level-below-two-thirds-13-questions.md) [+M]
- [Upper-level students' overall normalized gain on the EMCS after a traditional classical mechanics course was 14%, rising from 63% to 68%](../claims/emcs-upper-level-overall-gain-14-percent.md) [+M]
