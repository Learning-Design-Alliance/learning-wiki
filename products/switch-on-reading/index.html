Switch-on Reading¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Switch-on Reading is a one-to-one, 10-week graded-book reading intervention developed for weak Year 7 readers and delivered by trained staff using a prescribed session protocol and running-record assessment.

Components¶

  • Switch-on intensive targeted literacy intervention: Switch-on is "an intensive, targeted literacy intervention that aims to improve the reading skills of pupils who are struggling with literacy", delivered by specially trained Teaching Assistants in daily 20-minute sessions over a ten-week period. Two versions were trialled: Switch-on Reading (daily reading sessions) and Switch-on Reading and Writing (alternating writing and reading sessions). Sessions revise familiar texts, introduce new texts and vocabulary, practise phonics, and work on comprehension, with TAs using Running Records to monitor progress and tailor prompts. (Rakhee Patel et al. (2017))
  • Switch-on theoretical framework: zone of proximal development and the simple view of reading: The Switch-on manual and training grounded the intervention in two frameworks. Vygotsky's "theory of the zone of proximal development, which draws a distinction between what a learner can do independently and with help from an adult or more competent peer" underpins the one-to-one supported delivery. The "simple view of reading", which "distinguishes between language comprehension processes and word recognition skills and demonstrates that both are essential for effective reading", informed session content covering both comprehension and phonics. The training also explained the rationale for re-reading familiar books to build confidence and fluency. (Rakhee Patel et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Switch-on Reading produces a small positive impact on reading (+0.24) for Year 7 weak readers in a waiting-list RCT [+M]
  • Switch-on Reading and Switch-on Reading and Writing produced no additional reading progress compared with business-as-usual control schools in a three-arm school-randomised effectiveness trial [-M]
  • Implementation fidelity of Switch-on was compromised in some schools, with modifications to prescribed content, duration and format of sessions [~M]
