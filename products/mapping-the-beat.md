## Description
Mapping the Beat is a National Geographic Education Foundation-funded ArtsBridge supplementary American history curriculum that uses recorded music and themes of environment, identity, and movement for middle-school instruction.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Mapping the Beat eighth-grade music-history workshop curriculum**: Mapping the Beat is a supplementary American history curriculum developed with National Geographic Education Foundation funding via the ArtsBridge program, organized around "three concepts--environment, identity, and movement" modeled on the Geography for Life standards. The eighth-grade version studied here comprised nine 45-minute, two-period workshops (e.g., Instrument Migration, Work Songs, the Ngoni and the Atlantic Slave Trade, Underground Railroad, Civil War bands, Chinese in America, the Piano and the Industrial Revolution), taught with recorded music. Each unit opens with a conversation linking concepts to students' lives, then presents musical examples discussed as records of cultural migration, products of geographic environment, and cornerstones of identity. (Richardson et al. (2013))
