VARIA benchmark package with follow-up harness¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Ernesto Lee and colleagues' VARIA package provides assessment-variant blueprints, rubrics, canonical solutions, prompting strategies, evaluation metrics, and runnable follow-up analysis code for benchmarking LLM-generated construct-equivalent assessment variants.

Components¶

  • Released VARIA benchmark package with follow-up harness: The authors release the VARIA benchmark as a code package containing the three pilot blueprints with rubrics and canonical solutions (blueprints.json), a held-out distant-discipline fourth blueprint (Constructing a Historical Argument from Primary Sources) pre-registered as a generalisation probe, four prompting strategies, five complementary metrics, and an evaluation harness. They "release VARIA together with a ready-to-run follow-up harness" implementing per-variant cross-family judging, the full rubric-stability protocol, and multi-run and larger-N regimes (run_followup.py), plus gap_analysis.py for the paired permutation tests. (Ernesto Lee (2026))

Claims¶

  • Frontier LLM prompting strategies cluster at joint integrity scores of 0.81-0.88 while non-frontier references collapse to 0.50-0.55, a 25-31 point capability-tier gap [+W]
  • Under illustrative property thresholds, no prompting strategy passes all measured AIAA properties in every model-blueprint cell; few-shot anchoring passes only 2 of 9 cells [+W]
  • Few-shot anchoring trades surface diversity against construct equivalence and readability parity, with each direction significant at p = 0.004 relative to zero-shot [+W]
  • Individual model-strategy cells span J = 0.704 to 0.900, a model-by-prompt interaction wider than the tight strategy-level frontier band [+W]
  • Under LLM evaluators, structured rubrics produced larger negative deviations from the human baseline than free-form rubrics, the opposite direction of the human-evaluator pattern [~W]
