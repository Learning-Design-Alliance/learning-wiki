NASA-TLX multidimensional workload questionnaire¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 3 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A standardized six-subscale self-report assessment developed by NASA for measuring perceived workload across mental, physical, temporal, performance, effort, and frustration dimensions.

Components¶

  • NASA-TLX multidimensional workload questionnaire used as a common measurement instrument across design activities: NASA-TLX is a multi-dimensional rating procedure that assigns a total workload score based on a weighted average of six sub-scales: mental demand, physical demand, temporal demand, performance, effort, and frustration level. Its application has two sequential phases: weighing, consisting of 15 binary comparisons among the six dimensions after task execution, and estimation, in which the individual rates each dimension on a scale from 0 to 100 immediately after task completion. In this study it was adopted as an outcome evaluation instrument for comparing different design phases and activities, with 133 questionnaires delivered. (Christopher Nikulin et al. (2019))

Claims¶

  • NASA-TLX workload dimensions were significantly intercorrelated, including mental demand with effort (r = 0.768) and performance negatively with frustration (r = −0.443) [+W]
  • The AI+Robot condition is associated with lower perceived workload than AI-Only on effort, frustration, temporal demand, physical demand, and Raw TLX [+W]
  • Teacher perceived workload was negatively associated with student learning gains, especially for Mental Demand and Effort [+W]
  • Subjective cognitive workload decreases across teleoperation training and lower workload is associated with higher performance [+W]
  • NASA-TLX dimensions varied by design phase: frustration and mental demand rose considerably in the initial modules, and mental demand was lower during the final manufacturing steps [+W]
  • Using the AIED-U system yielded a positive indirect effect on learning gains through a reduction in the effort teachers reported [+W]
  • Designer workload across an 11-module instructional design model for product development averaged normal-to-high (50–75%) [+W]
