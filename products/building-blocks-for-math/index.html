Building Blocks for Math¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 12 claims (9 for, 3 mixed) · 7 studies (6 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3–q4 · 7 of 7 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A preschool mathematics curriculum from SRA Real Math that sequences instruction and software tasks along empirically based learning trajectories linking children's informal mathematical knowledge to formal concepts.

Components¶

  • Empirically based learning trajectories as the organizing framework of Building Blocks for Math: The curriculum is structured around "empirically based learning trajectories (i.e., what is appropriate for children at that age in terms of mathematics)", sequenced according to children's developmental progression. The software's management system presents tasks contingent on success along these research-based trajectories, and teacher training emphasized learning trajectories via a web-based application called Building Blocks Learning Trajectories. The framework connects children's informal math knowledge to more formal mathematical concepts. (SRA Real Math Building Blocks PreK (2007))
  • Building Blocks computer activities with automatic adjustment: The Building Blocks computer activities are learning tasks related to the day's mathematics topic that "automatically adjust when children progress well or need additional help." Children worked on them individually during center time, often while teachers led small-group instruction, for approximately 10–15 minutes twice per week. The report notes they are recommended (but not required) for effective implementation, and an online subscription can be purchased per student. (Building Blocksâ"¢)
  • Building Blocks preschool mathematics curriculum: Building Blocks is a mathematics curriculum that "aims to develop preschool children’s knowledge of mathematics using activities that are intentionally sequenced based on the developmental progression of children’s mathematical learning." It includes whole- and small-group instruction, center activities, and computer activities, plus weekly family letters, with teacher support through professional development. Activities were implemented over 26 to 30 weeks in the reviewed studies. (Building Blocksâ"¢)
  • Building Blocks multi-context implementation pattern: whole-group, small-group, centers, computer activities, and family letters: The curriculum distributes mathematics learning across several classroom contexts: whole-group instruction during circle time (approximately 5–15 minutes, four or five times per week), small-group instruction with 3 to 6 children (approximately 10–15 minutes, two or three times per week), hands-on center activities, and computer activities that "automatically adjust when children progress well or need additional help." Weekly family/caregiver letters describe the mathematics and home activities. This pattern lets teachers adapt activities to children's developmental levels while other children work independently. (page:designs/building-blocks-multi-context-implementation-pattern)
  • Build early education quality on ten research-based building blocks, from whole-child assessment to quality rating and improvement systems: Drawing on its prior report The Building Blocks of High-Quality Early Childhood Education Programs, the report specifies "the key elements of quality" in programs with exemplary results. These include "Using early learning standards and curricula that address noncognitive as well as cognitive dimensions of children's experiences," assessing the whole child, preparing and coaching teachers, attending to varying needs including English language learners, engaging families, providing sufficient learning time, small classes and low ratios, program-level assessments, and a quality rating and improvement system used for improvement and accountability. (Wechsler et al. (2016))
  • TRIAD scale-up model centered on learning trajectories: TRIAD (Technology-enhanced, Research-based, Instruction, Assessment, and professional Development) is a research-based model for scaling up successful interventions, created by synthesizing research and intended to generalize to other subject matter areas and age groups. Its specific goal is to increase mathematics achievement in young children, especially those at risk, by centering curriculum, pedagogy, technology, and assessments on a common core of learning trajectories: "Learning trajectories are at the core of the components of this intervention, guiding the curriculum, the instruction, the formative assessment, and the professional development." The model involves 10 research-based guidelines for scaling up. (Douglas H. Clements et al. (2013))
  • Building Blocks Software as adaptive supplemental numeracy CAI for kindergartners: Building Blocks Software (Clements & Sarama) is a computer assisted instruction program for early numeracy whose instruction is "adjusted to match the level of ability demonstrated by the child". In this study children worked individually during an ancillary computer block, 90 minutes a week for 21 weeks, with research assistants providing behavioral supervision and technical assistance. It was evaluated against an active literacy CAI control. (Foster et al. (2016))

Claims¶

  • In a 2006 randomized trial, Building Blocks for Math significantly outperformed business-as-usual on the Early Mathematics Assessment [+M]
  • In a 2007 randomized trial, Building Blocks for Math significantly improved geometry but not number outcomes [~M]
  • The evidence base for Building Blocks for Math is small and limited to the math domain [~W]
  • Building Blocks for Math outperformed Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Express in a head-to-head comparison on the Early Mathematics Assessment [+W]
  • Building Blocks for Math has positive effects on preschool mathematics achievement according to the WWC [+M]
  • In a 28-classroom cluster randomized trial, Building Blocks raised end-of-intervention REMA scores with an effect size of 1.07 (improvement index +36) [+M]
  • In a large-scale 106-classroom cluster randomized trial, Building Blocks raised end-of-intervention REMA scores with an effect size of 0.48 (improvement index +18) [+M]
  • Building Blocks has a potentially positive effect on preschool mathematics achievement, with a pooled effect size of 0.58 across three studies meeting WWC standards [+M]
  • The Building Blocks pre-K implementation showed positive effects on pre-K mathematics (ES=.72) and kindergarten language competencies, with stronger first-grade effects for those with follow-through [+M]
  • TRIAD's pre-K and follow-through effects show no persistent impact on the full sample in children's fourth-grade year [~M]
  • Supplemental Building Blocks Software CAI improves proximal numeracy outcomes in kindergarten children from Title 1 schools [+M]
  • Supplemental Building Blocks Software CAI improves distal broad math achievement (WJ-III Applied Problems) at kindergarten posttest [+M]
