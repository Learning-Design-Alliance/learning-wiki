# Sport Education Model (SEM)

## Description
A structured physical-education teaching model developed by Daryl Siedentop in which students participate in extended sport seasons, stable teams, formal competition, varied instructional roles, record keeping, and culminating festivities.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Sport Education Model as a structured, student-centered pedagogical framework with five key stages and six structural features**: SEM is "a distinctive and well-structured pedagogical framework" in which learners take on roles such as "players, coaches, officials, scorekeepers, and equipment managers" within stable groups, using "a progressive release of responsibility model" that shifts from teacher-directed instruction to autonomous student-led practice. The article describes "five key stages: teacher-directed instruction, team selection, modified preseason activities, formal competition, and cumulative events," and identifies structural features including "season, affiliation, formal competition, culminating event, record keeping, and festivity." The framework is used as the intervention whose moderating conditions the meta-analysis quantifies. (Yao Gege et al. (2025))
- **Sports education season structured in three four-week phases: skills, team formation with task roles, and tournament with celebration**: The article implements the sports education model as a 12-week season divided into three phases: basic information and skills, team formation with task distribution and student planning, and a preparation tournament with celebration practices. Students take on roles such as "coaching, refereeing, health representative, press representative, and composition committee membership", embedding personal and social responsibility in sport learning. (Nevruz Bilgin (2017))
