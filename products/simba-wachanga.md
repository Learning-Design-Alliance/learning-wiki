## Description
Simba Wachanga (Kiswahili for young lions) is a rites-of-passage program for African-American males, implemented in 1984 at the East End Neighborhood House in Cleveland, Ohio and existing as a network of organizations. It is "a full year program that parallels the school year with a minimum of four hours per week of activities," focusing on twelve to fourteen year old males (Cleveland accommodates ages nine through fourteen). It uses an interdisciplinary thematic approach with activities no longer than one hour, staffing of a Council of Elders and resource people as instructors and Big Brothers, and level progression symbolized by the red, green, and black of the African-American National Flag.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sponsoring organization and a recruited Council of Elders with time, skills, commitment, and an Afro-centric understanding; parental involvement via a signed parental and participant agreement
#### Constraints
- Programs vary in age-grouping, funding, curriculum, structure and sponsors; no standardized process exists; accommodating broad age groupings is difficult without school-like resources

### Target Learners
- African-American boys aged nine to fourteen

### Target Learning Goals
- Cultural, spiritual, social and educational development toward manhood
- Black male functionability across cognitive, physical, psychological, affective, territorial, cultural, and spiritual domains

### Affordances
- [Threefold Ritual Pattern Separation Transition Reincorporation](../theories/threefold-ritual-pattern-separation-transition-reincorporation.md)
