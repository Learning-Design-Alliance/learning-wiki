AI4K12 Five Big Ideas in Artificial Intelligence¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A framework developed by the AI for K-12 working group that organizes K–12 artificial-intelligence education around five thematic strands and four grade bands.

Components¶

  • Five thematic strands ('big ideas') organizing K-12 AI guidelines: The AI for K-12 working group frames its guidelines around five big ideas in AI: computers perceive the world using sensors; agents maintain models/representations of the world and use them for reasoning; computers can learn from data; making agents interact comfortably with humans is a substantial challenge; and AI applications can impact society in both positive and negative ways. The authors state the guidelines "will unpack AI's 'Big Ideas' along five thematic strands, and organize them by four grade bands". They chose this formulation over a traditional division into application areas because it "better meets the needs of K-12 students and teachers". (Touretzky et al. (2019))
  • The Five Big Ideas of AI framework organizes K-12 AI instruction around five ideas with grade-band progressions: The AI4K12 initiative's Five Big Ideas framework structures what should be taught in K-12 AI education. As the commentary describes, "The Big Five Ideas (perception, representation and reasoning, learning, natural interaction, and societal impact) act as the basis for their approach," with grade band progressions split as K-2, 3-5, 6-8, and 9-12. The commentary endorses the framework as intuitive and influential across the special issue, while stressing that classroom testing is needed to validate the developmental appropriateness of the grade bands. (H. Chad Lane (2023))
  • AI4K12 Five Big Ideas in AI as an organizing framework for K-12 AI learning progressions: The preface identifies the AI4K12 initiative's Five Big Ideas in AI as an organizing framework for K-12 AI learning progressions, used by Touretsky et al. with a focus on machine learning. The editors write that "Based on the AI4K12 initiative, they discuss how the Five Big Ideas in AI provide an organizing framework for K-12 AI learning progressions with a focus on machine learning." The framework thus structures how machine learning is sequenced for K-12 learners. (Ning Wang · James Lester (2023))

Claims¶

  • Existing K-12 computing standards give AI minimal coverage [~M] — the gap the guidelines were written to fill
  • Machine perception and learning depend on large, unbiased training data [+W] — the content of big idea 3, learning from data
