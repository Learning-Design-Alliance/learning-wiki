# Four-item perceived learner control scale for AI- and human-taught contexts

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A four-item psychometrically evaluated self-report scale developed by Fei Qin et al. to measure undergraduates' perceived control over participation, pacing, learning approach, and time allocation across AI- and human-taught contexts.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Four-item perceived learner control scale developed for AI- and human-taught contexts**: Because no existing validated scale captured learner control across both AI- and human-teacher contexts, the authors developed a four-item perceived learner control scale covering deciding how to participate, pacing, way of learning, and time allocation. Psychometric analysis supported a single factor: "The Cronbach's alpha was 0.823" and an exploratory factor analysis (KMO = 0.780; Bartlett's χ2 = 212.684, df = 6, p < .001) found one factor accounting for 66.15% of variance with loadings of 0.709–0.871. (Fei Qin et al. (2025))
