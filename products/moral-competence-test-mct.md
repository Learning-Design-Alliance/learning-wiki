## Description
The Moral Competence Test is a standardized moral-judgment assessment developed by Georg Lind that uses dilemma-based argument ratings to calculate a C-score for consistency in applying moral principles.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Moral Competence Test (MCT) with C-score as the moral competence measure**: The Moral Competence Test presents two moral dilemmas (a Worker's dilemma and a Doctor's dilemma), each followed by 12 arguments rated on a 9-point Likert scale. Its main outcome is the C-score, which "ranges from 1 to 100 and reflects the degree of consistency in applying moral principles across different arguments." Scores of 30 and above indicate high to very high moral competence, and a score above 20 is described as desirable for functioning in a free society. The study used the MCT to compare medical and psychology students across years and testing formats. (Zielina M et al. (2025))
