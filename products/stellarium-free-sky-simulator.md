# Stellarium free sky simulator

## Description
Stellarium is free, open-source planetarium software developed by the Stellarium team that simulates the sky for educational and observational use.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Stellarium free sky-simulation software as a data-collection and visualization tool in astronomy teaching**: Stellarium is a free program that simulates the sky, downloadable at www.stellarium.org, used throughout the three sequences for locating celestial structures, visualizing historical and simulated observations, accelerating time to record Io's eclipse exits, changing observer location to Jupiter's surface, and reading stellar data such as spectral type, magnitude and distance. The authors state that "a utilização de simuladores de céu, como o Stellarium, torna-se uma ferramenta essencial" in classroom work, and that it is "uma ótima ferramenta alternativa" for identifying lunar features. (Oliveira et al. (2020))
- **Stellarium Starlore culturally-specific packages provide star names, constellation art, and cultural traditions for dozens of communities**: Stellarium planetarium software features culturally-specific packages under 'Starlore'. These default plug-ins feature "star names, constellation art, and cultural traditions for dozens of Indigenous communities and ancient cultures", with examples including Maori, Lokono, Maya, Tongan, Navajo, Mongolian, Tupi, Ojibwe, D/Lakota, Boorong, Aztec, Egyptian, Norse, and Chinese. Additional plug-ins can be developed, enabling inclusion of Indigenous content from anywhere in the world. (Lee et al. (2020))
- **Stellarium planetarium software for practising night-sky identification**: Stellarium (Chéreau 2003) was used as the best way for students to practise night-sky identification short of going outside at night, in preparation for a night quiz at a dark location requiring them to identify ten objects correctly. The article states Stellarium had the advantage that the horizon, zenith, and cardinal points are clearly marked and comprehensible, whereas a 2D sky map, meant to be read as if lying on the ground looking up, can be confusing for students. Figure 1 shows a Stellarium image with an archaeological structure on the ground. (Jarita Holbrook (2020))
- **Stellarium web software as an embedded observation tool**: The book inserts ICT resource boxes (Recurso TIC) that direct students to a real observing tool. In the constellations section it introduces Stellarium: "Stellarium es un software astronómico que muestra los objetos visibles del cielo desde tu ubicación y de acuerdo a la hora de observación", giving its web address and an open task: "descubra qué constelaciones son observables en estas épocas del año". The tool grounds coordinate-system and constellation content in the learner's own sky and date. (El Equipo CosmoAmautas (2022))
