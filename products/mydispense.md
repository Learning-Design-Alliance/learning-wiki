## Description
MyDispense is a virtual community-pharmacy simulation platform developed and maintained by Monash University for practicing prescription processing and medicine-dispensing skills.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **MyDispense virtual community pharmacy simulation**: MyDispense is a virtual simulation tool used in one included study to teach dispensing skills. The review describes that "MyDispense simulated a community pharmacy offering representations of patients, prescriptions, products on shelves, and a high-fidelity interface to prescription processing". It is cited among tools that improved performance, confidence, and learning, complementing online activities with formative feedback, and was considered effective for developing dispensing abilities. (Ribeiro MAL et al. (2026))
- **MyDispense Germany: game-like dispensing simulation software and the MyDispense Netzwerk Deutschland**: MyDispense, a simulation software developed at Monash University, creates a virtual space in which medicines are dispensed, with dispensing situations in a community pharmacy, a hospital outpatient department, or a ward request. Since 2017 Universität Greifswald adapted it with the Australian developers to German conditions; "Seit 2019 steht so eine MyDispense-Version für Deutschland bereit." Exercises range from formal prescription checks to dispensing on prescription and self-medication, with automated feedback after each exercise and lecturer-assessed assessment exercises. Because interest existed at other sites, "wurde das MyDispense Netzwerk Deutschland gegründet". (Ritter Christoph (2025))
- **MyDispense virtual pharmacy simulation with communication and technical skill exercises integrated into IPPE2 and APPE courses**: MyDispense is "a digital education platform that enables students to practice the skills of a pharmacist, from novice to highly advanced, in a safe virtual environment that is web-based and highly accessible". Exercises simulated virtual patients seeking prescriptions and covered patient fact-finding, patient education, answering patient questions, prescription monitoring, labeling, and dispensing. At the University of Tabuk it was integrated for four weeks of thirteen-week IPPE2 and APPE courses, with a demonstration, tutorial exercise, formative assessment and summative assessment in sequence. (Palanisamy A et al. (2024))
