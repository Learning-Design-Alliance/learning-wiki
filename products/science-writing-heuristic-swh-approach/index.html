Science Writing Heuristic (SWH) approach¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Science Writing Heuristic is an instructional framework developed by Brian Hand and collaborators that structures science learning around student questioning, claims, evidence, argumentation, and metacognitive reasoning.

Components¶

  • Science Writing Heuristic (SWH): argument-based science inquiry built on questions, claims, and evidence: The Science Writing Heuristic (Hand & Keys, 1999) is a guided, argument-based approach to classroom science inquiry designed to involve students in inquiry, argumentation, and experimentation as a means of learning science and improving critical thinking. It rests on writing-to-learn strategies, science literacy, and inquiry-based instruction, and "requires students to use 'questions, claims, and evidence' to display their understanding of science content and concepts". Instruction is student directed and teacher guided, targeting science content knowledge, scientific processing skills, and general critical thinking skills. (Jonte C. Taylor et al. (2018))
  • SWH teacher and student templates scaffolding argument-based inquiry: The SWH uses paired teacher and student templates to scaffold teaching and learning during science instruction (Table 1). The teacher template guides teachers through phases from pre-laboratory activities through four negotiations phases to post-instruction exploration, while the student template walks students through beginning ideas, questions, tests, observations, claims, evidence, reading, reflection, and writing. "Using the templates, students develop questions, make claims, and provide evidence to support or reject claims." Teachers were taught to use the templates as pedagogical support but could adjust the sequence for classroom needs. (Jonte C. Taylor et al. (2018))

Claims¶

  • Students in SWH treatment schools gained about one-tenth of a standard deviation in Mathematics scores per quarter of exposure, erasing an initial disadvantage of roughly 0.4 SD after four semesters [+M]
  • The SWH implementation showed no effect on Science scores [~M]
  • Students with disabilities in SWH classrooms significantly outperformed comparison-group peers on post-intervention ITBS science scores [+M]
  • SWH treatment produced a moderate pre-post effect size (d = 0.740) versus a small effect (d = 0.260) for comparison instruction, and a moderate between-group effect (d = 0.400) [+M]
