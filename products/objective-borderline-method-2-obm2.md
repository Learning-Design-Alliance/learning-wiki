# Objective Borderline Method 2 (OBM2)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A pass/fail classification method developed by Shulruf and colleagues for reclassifying borderline grades in examinations such as clinical skills OSCEs using item-difficulty and student-ability indices.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Borderline (B) grade replacing the Borderline Pass (P-) grade in the OSCE marking schedule**: The UNSW Medicine OSCE marking schedule previously used four grades (F, P-, P, P+) converted to scores 3, 5, 7, 9. OBM2 implementation replaced the Borderline Pass grade with a Borderline grade indicating the examiner is "unable to decide on whether the student performance was a clear pass or a clear fail related to a particular assessment criterion (item)". This allowed examiners to give an undetermined grade when appropriate, reducing examiner bias and anxiety, and simplified the marking algorithm for administrators. (Shulruf Boaz et al. (2018))
- **Objective Borderline Method version 2 (OBM2): reclassifying borderline grades as pass or fail using item difficulty and student ability indices**: OBM2 is a decision-making mechanism, not a traditional standard-setting method, that resolves examiner uncertainty about borderline grades. It computes two OBM indices for each Borderline grade — item difficulty and student ability — and reclassifies the grade: "If Ability ≥ Difficulty then the B grade is reclassified as P grade, otherwise if Ability < Difficulty the B grade is reclassified as an F grade". It applies institutional pass/fail policy without changing predetermined standards, and requires items to underlie a single construct with adequate reliability. (Shulruf Boaz et al. (2018))
