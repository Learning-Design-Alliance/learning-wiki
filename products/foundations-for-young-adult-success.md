## Description
A UChicago Consortium framework that organizes developmental conditions and experiences supporting young people's successful transition to adulthood.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Ten developmental experiences framework: five action and five reflection experiences underpin social-emotional development**: Drawing on the UChicago Consortium's Foundations for Young Adult Success framework, the report organizes arts learning opportunities around ten developmental experiences. As printed: "These 10 developmental experiences include five action experiences (encountering, tinkering, choosing, practicing, and contributing) and five reflection experiences (describing, evaluating, connecting, envisioning, and integrating)." Arts education is argued to provide particularly rich opportunities for all ten, which the report uses to analyze how arts activities build social-emotional competencies. (Camille A. Farrington et al. (2018))
- **Integrated identity framework: young adult success as agency and motivation grounded in an integrated identity**: The article's central framework conceptualizes a well-prepared young adult as one with an integrated identity and the agency to use internal and external resources toward self-set goals. It defines success as "being poised to work toward and achieve the goals a person sets for herself," with goals emerging from values and purpose. The framework names key factors — knowledge and skills, awareness, mindsets, strategies, and behaviors and habits — that come together in an integrated identity enabling directed action across life domains. (Nagaoka et al. (2014))

# Ten developmental experiences framework: five action and five reflection experiences underpin social-emotional development

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing on the UChicago Consortium's Foundations for Young Adult Success framework, the report organizes arts learning opportunities around ten developmental experiences. As printed: "These 10 developmental experiences include five action experiences (encountering, tinkering, choosing, practicing, and contributing) and five reflection experiences (describing, evaluating, connecting, envisioning, and integrating)." Arts education is argued to provide particularly rich opportunities for all ten, which the report uses to analyze how arts activities build social-emotional competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need richer, more frequent, and more varied opportunities for action and reflection for development to occur
#### Constraints
- The framework was drawn from neurobiology/epigenetics and learning-sciences literatures as reported by Nagaoka et al. (2015), not developed from arts-specific experimental evidence

### Target Learners
- Children and adolescents in K–12 arts and general education settings

### Target Learning Objectives
- Social-emotional competencies developed through action and reflection experiences
