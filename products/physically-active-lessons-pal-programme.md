# Physically Active Lessons (PAL) programme

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A University of Bristol-developed primary-school programme that integrates short bouts of physical activity with literacy and numeracy instruction for UK pupils.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Cogni-Actif: a co-designed video-based physically active learning program with avatar Ratou**: Cogni-Actif is the PAL program developed through the co-design process: teachers described it as an "easy-to-use and fun way to promote learning through movement" that "combines physical and academic activities into 15-minute video clips". It comprises 24 sessions delivered over 8 weeks (three sessions per week) covering 32 listed academic lesson themes and 14 physical movements classified by intensity, guided by an avatar raccoon named Ratou. Teachers highlighted it as "an end-to-end solution that does not require any additional preparation from them". (Simard et al. (2023))
- **A socio-ecological PAL implementation framework spanning classroom, school, and national policy levels**: The article presents a research-informed PAL implementation framework (Fig. 1) underpinned by a socio-ecological framework, presenting key implementation drivers within the classroom, the school, and national policy. In the classroom context, "competence and confidence among teachers influence their willingness to implement varied PAL approaches across different school environments". School-level implementation is influenced by the senior leadership team, governors, school mission and vision, teacher performance management, school improvement priorities, and parents; national-level implementation depends on education and health policies and initial teacher training. Arrows demonstrate the direction and range of influence, with both bottom-up and top-down processes required for sustainable systems change. (Daly-Smith A. et al. (2019))
- **PAL teacher-training programme for secondary school teachers**: A two-session (2-hour each) after-school training programme teaching secondary teachers to integrate movement into academic lessons, so that, as the article defines it, "activity supports the delivery of academic material" rather than activity being separate from learning. The training drew on social cognitive theory, using barrier identification and modelling/demonstrating of PAL strategies, and was underpinned by a logic model linking teacher training to changes in students' sedentary time. It was tested in a feasibility study with maths and English teachers and a whole-school pilot trial. (Gammon Catherine et al. (2019))
- **Physically active academic lessons that preserve instructional time**: A lesson-level design pattern in which academic content is taught through movement rather than added as a separate activity break. The article emphasizes that "A + PAAC will alter the classroom environment by providing existing academic lessons taught using physical activity. Thus, the amount of time devoted to academic instruction is not diminished." Lessons are short (two 10-minute lessons per day, 5 days per week) and their scope is limited only by teacher creativity. (Donnelly Joseph E et al. (2013))
