## Description
A 67-item, 7-point self-report questionnaire measuring university students' academic adjustment, social adjustment, personal-emotional adjustment, and institutional attachment.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Baker & Siryk four-component model of student adaptation to the university environment**: The article adopts the Baker & Siryk (1986) conception of student adaptability as a multifaceted construct, holding that "a student must be able to align themselves with the requirements and conditions of the educational environment through adaptation processes". It divides adaptation into four components: academic adjustment (coping with educational conditions such as motivation, perseverance, and performance), social adjustment (coping with individual-social demands such as relationships and homesickness), personal-emotional adjustment (mental and physical health in the new environment), and institutional attachment (commitment to educational goals and attachment to the institution). The study measured adaptability with the 67-item Baker & Siryk questionnaire covering these four components. (Bordbar Shima et al. (2024))
