# State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A standardized self-report questionnaire developed by Spielberger that measures current (state) and general (trait) anxiety through separate 20-item scales.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) with STA-Y and TAI-Y subscales for measuring pre-OSCE anxiety**: The study used the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory developed by Spielberger (1983), comprising two 20-item scales: the State Anxiety (STA-Y) scale assessing current feelings and the Trait Anxiety (TAI-Y) scale assessing general feelings, each on a 4-point Likert scale with total scores ranging from 20 to 80. Internal consistency in this study was Cronbach's alpha = 0.894 for STA-Y and 0.886 for TAI-Y. The questionnaire was distributed online via Google Forms three days before the OSCE. (Kamel Senosy AM et al. (2026))
- **State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI) trait subscale as the anxiety measure**: The State-Trait Anxiety Inventory consists of two 20-item subscales measuring state anxiety (STAI-X1) and trait anxiety (STAI-X2), self-rated on a 4-point Likert scale, with subscale scores ranging from 20 to 80. The article describes it as having "good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha > 0.85 for both subscales) and well-established validity across different populations." In this study only the trait subscale was used, with printed alphas of 0.91 (X2) and 0.92 (X1), to compare anxiety across fields, years, and testing formats. (Zielina M et al. (2025))
