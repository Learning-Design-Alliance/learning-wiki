# U.S. Particle Accelerator School (USPAS)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)

## Description
A consortium-governed national graduate school run by major research laboratories and universities that offers rigorous, for-credit accelerator physics and engineering courses to graduate students, postdocs, and professionals.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **USPAS: a consortium-governed national graduate school of parallel rigorous for-credit courses hosted by major research universities**: The U.S. Particle Accelerator School, founded in 1982 and governed by an eleven-member consortium of nine DOE national laboratories and two NSF university laboratories, provides graduate-level courses in the science of particle beams and accelerator technologies “not generally available to the scientific and engineering communities.” Since a 1987 format change, each session consists of several rigorous courses running in parallel: one full course of at least 45 contact hours (3 semester hours of host-university credit) or two half-courses of at least 23 contact hours each (1.5 semester hours). Students earn credit by completing nightly homework and passing a final exam. The paper calls the USPAS rubric of rigorous, for-credit courses hosted by major research universities an essential aspect of formal accelerator education in America. (William Barletta et al. (2012))
