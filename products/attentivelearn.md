# AttentiveLearn

## Description
AttentiveLearn is a gaze-aware VR lecture and LLM-based mobile learning-assistant ecosystem developed by the study authors to provide attention-aware post-lecture quizzes and conversational support.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Framework for attention-based post-lecture personalization bridging in-situ immersive learning with ex-situ support**: The article introduces "a framework for attention-based post-lecture personalization in the context of immersive learning", instantiated with AttentiveLearn. It organizes the design into three layers: an In-Situ Data Acquisition layer (the immersive virtual classroom), a Data Translation layer (the pipeline converting raw signals into pedagogical attention insights), and an Ex-Situ Intervention interface (the mobile assistant delivering personalized quizzes and Q&A). The framework is intended to be generalizable, accommodating alternative biosignals or attention definitions in future iterations. (AttentiveLearn: Personalized Post-Lecture Support for Gaze-Aware Immersive Learning)

### Claims
- [Attentive-group participants rated attention-aware LectureQuiz as significantly more accurate at revealing attention gaps than non-attentive participants, and perceived accuracy correlated negatively with section-level ADI only in the attentive group](../claims/attentive-quiz-perceived-accuracy-higher.md) [+M]
- [Attentive-group participants reported higher overall engagement than non-attentive participants, with significant group differences on the focused attention and reward factor subscales but not on perceived usability or aesthetic elements](../claims/attentive-group-higher-engagement.md) [+M]
- [Attentive-group participants reported higher motivation than non-attentive participants on the MSLQ subscales of intrinsic goal orientation, self-efficacy, and task value](../claims/attentive-group-higher-motivation-mslq.md) [+M]
- [Learners' attention distribution index in VR lectures changed over weeks, with a significant increase from week 2 to week 3, but did not differ between attentive and non-attentive support groups](../claims/adi-changed-over-weeks-no-group-effect.md) [+M]
- [Exploratory evidence suggests low-attention learners in the attentive group showed a greater ADI increase from week 1 to week 3 than non-attentive counterparts, but the difference was not statistically significant](../claims/low-attention-adi-increase-not-significant.md) [~M]
