## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **STAR (Strategies and Techniques for Advancement in Reading) remedial reading program**: STAR is the intensive remedial reading program offered within High School Redirection schools "for students with serious literacy problems." It is part of the model's emphasis on basic skill development, with a particular focus on reading skills, and was listed among the key features that the Stockton, Wichita, and Cincinnati replication schools included as specified by the ASDP evaluation. The report does not describe STAR's internal instructional design beyond its role as the schools' remedial reading offering. (High School Redirection (2007))
- **STAR Reading assessment and Estimated ORF measure**: STAR is a computer-adaptive reading assessment by Renaissance Learning used here to measure estimated oral reading fluency (ORF) in correct words per minute. Items adapt in difficulty to student responses, and the diagnostic report provides grade equivalency, reading range, and estimated ORF. The article cites U.S. Department of Education reviews (one with n = 63,291) finding "convincing evidence" of STAR's reliability and validity. In this study students took the 25-question test before and after the intervention. (Meeks et al. (2014))
- **The STAR Educational Program: a seven-part theory-based blended learning intervention for primary care antibiotic prescribing**: The STAR Educational Program is a blended learning intervention for primary care prescribers comprising online reflection on clinicians' own practice, research evidence and guidelines, a practice-based seminar on the practice's own prescribing and local urine-sample resistance data, communication skills training with videos of simulated patients, reflection on own cases, a web forum and a booster session. The article describes "a seven part, theory-based blended learning program" evaluated in a randomised controlled trial with 244 GPs and Nurse Practitioners and 68 practices. (Bekkers Marie-Jet et al. (2010))
