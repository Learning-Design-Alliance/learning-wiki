Talk of the Town¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Talk of the Town is a community-led whole-school programme run with speech and language therapy support to improve children’s speech, language and communication skills through workforce development, early identification, universal provision, targeted interventions, and leadership support.

Components¶

  • Talk of the Town whole-school model with four strands of speech, language and communication support: Talk of the Town is a community-led, whole-school approach to supporting speech, language and communication (SLC) skills of children aged 0-19 in areas of social disadvantage. The report states "The Talk of the Town approach has four main strands or components of work": workforce development for all staff including a speech and language therapist one day per week; early identification of SLC needs; universal approaches and targeted interventions; and support for senior leaders to embed SLC in whole-school development planning. In the trial, targeted interventions (Talking Time, Talk Boost, a KS2 spoken language intervention, and Phonological Awareness) were linked back to universal classroom teaching. (Thurston (2016))
  • Talk of the Town programme cost structure: £13,244.46 per school per year and £50.97 per student: The report prints the yearly cost of delivering Talk of the Town to an intervention school. The yearly cost per school was £13,244.46, calculated by converting the 18-month cost to a 12-month cost and dividing by the 31 schools that received the intervention. The report states "The cost per student of £50.97 was obtained by dividing the cost per school by the mean number of pupils per school, which was 259.86." (Thurston (2016))

Claims¶

  • Talk of the Town showed no evidence of an impact on pupils' reading comprehension as measured by the NGRT in a cluster RCT of 2696 pupils [-M]
  • Talk of the Town showed no evidence of improving oral language skills among children with weaker reading comprehension (ACE secondary outcome) [-M]
