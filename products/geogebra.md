- **GeoGebra as the dynamic mathematics environment for function art creation and data collection**: GeoGebra is a free dynamic mathematics software integrating algebra, geometry, calculus, and spreadsheets. Students used it to create function art over one month following a 2.5-hour webinar. The Graphics view displays geometric representations, the Algebra view shows equations, and the Construction Protocol shows chronological creation order. Researchers used the Algebra view to tally functions by type and the Construction Protocol to examine step-by-step construction sequences. (Bautista et al. (2026))
- **GeoGebra open-source dynamic mathematics software**: GeoGebra, designed by Markus Hohenwater, is described as "an open-source dynamic mathematics software that incorporates geometry, algebra and calculus into a single, open-source, user-friendly package". It is free, connects geometry and algebra, and ships with wide-ranging online help, a 42-page pdf help manual, downloadable tutorials, and video-based step-by-step example lessons. In this study it served as the primary scaffold for exploring circles. (Shadaan et al. (2013))
- **Dynamic geometry environments as tools for model-data comparison in physics education**: The article frames the use of a dynamic geometry environment (DGE) such as GeoGebra as serving multiple goals at once in physics experimentation: speeding up data sampling, deepening competences in analytic geometry, showing unexpected applications such as understanding fitting, offering a captivating multimedia way of conducting an experiment, and strengthening computer expertness. Within this framing, GeoGebra is used to bypass mathematical difficulties and to obtain a visualisation of the physical meaning of fitting, connecting model building to empirical validation as in Hestenes' modeling perspective the authors cite. (Vera Montalbano et al. (2014))
- **Geogebra as an open dynamic-mathematics environment for modeling in mathematics and physics**: Geogebra, an open-source dynamic-geometry software, is presented as an open environment intended for use at any school level that dynamically unites geometry, algebra, spreadsheets, graphs and statistics. The authors describe it as widely used for building mathematical models and suitable for physics teaching, particularly vector modeling and geometric optics, with student-built examples including decomposition of weight force on an inclined plane and geometric modeling of the Mirascope. It can be used in problem-solving mode or in demonstrative mode where the teacher prepares a worksheet students interact with individually or in groups. (Lucia Lupo (2015))

## Description
GeoGebra is a free dynamic mathematics software integrating algebra, geometry, calculus, and spreadsheets. Students used it to create function art over one month following a 2.5-hour webinar. The Graphics view displays geometric representations, the Algebra view shows equations, and the Construction Protocol shows chronological creation order. Researchers used the Algebra view to tally functions by type and the Construction Protocol to examine step-by-step construction sequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to devices and internet connectivity
- Webinar training on GeoGebra interface, graphing, and transformations
#### Constraints
- Access to technology and internet was limited for some schools
- Grade 11 students had prior GeoGebra knowledge but all were trained in prerequisite skills

### Target Learners
- Grades 8-12 students in Philippine secondary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Function graphing and manipulation
- Understanding transformations (translations, dilations)
- Connecting algebraic and geometric representations
