# OSSCAR (Ophthalmic Simulated Surgical Competency Assessment Rubric)

## Description
OSSCAR is a 16-step, three-level rubric adapted from the ICO-OSCAR for grading ophthalmology residents' phacoemulsification performance in wet-lab simulation videos.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **OSSCAR: a 16-step simulated-surgery competency rubric adapted from the ICO-OSCAR for wet lab assessment**: The Ophthalmic Simulated Surgical Competency Assessment Rubric (OSSCAR) is a standardized 16-step grading rubric for phacoemulsification, adapted from the International Council of Ophthalmology's ICO-OSCAR for the wet lab setting. "The OSSCAR has three possible grades (0–2) for each surgical step, corresponding respectively to Novice, Advanced Beginner and Competent (total grading scale 0–32)." The course used it both to grade weekly video assignments and for masked pre/post assessment of simulation videos. (Geary Amelia et al. (2021))
- **Sim-OSSCAR: Ophthalmic Simulation Surgical Competency Assessment Rubric for manual small-incision cataract surgery**: The Sim-OSSCAR is a validated competency assessment rubric for simulated manual small-incision cataract surgery, used in this trial as the primary outcome measure. "Each of the 20 items in the matrix was graded on a modified Dreyfus score (novice, advanced beginner, and competent)." The minimum score was 0 and the total possible score was 40 per procedure; masked assessments were performed remotely by 2 independent expert SICS surgeons, with interobserver reliability κ coefficient of 0.86. (Dean William H. et al. (2021))
- **Sim-OSSCAR: a 20-item rubric for assessing simulated small-incision cataract surgery skill**: The SICS Ophthalmic Simulated Surgical Competency Assessment Rubric is a 20-step assessment matrix scored on a three-point ordinal scale (0, 1, or 2), adapted from the ICO-OSCAR for use with an artificial eye. The article states it was "deployed in conjunction with the use of an artificial eye specifically developed for SICS." Items include specific steps such as scleral tunnel and nucleus extraction plus global indices such as overall speed of procedure. (Dean William H. FRCOphth et al. (2019))
