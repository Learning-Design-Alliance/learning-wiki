EyeSi and HelpMeSee virtual reality cataract surgery simulators¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

EyeSi and HelpMeSee are virtual-reality cataract-surgery simulation systems developed and operated by their respective commercial and training organizations for ophthalmology residents and other surgical trainees.

Components¶

  • EyeSi Surgical Simulator for vitreoretinal training and assessment: The EyeSi Surgical Simulator (VRMagic, Mannheim, Germany) is a virtual reality simulator for ophthalmic microsurgical training. Five of the seven studies included in the review "all used the same virtual reality simulator, the EyeSi® Surgical Simulator," training skills subcategorized as complex procedures, intraocular navigation and instrument handling. The review concludes it can be used to assess and possibly improve basic and complex VRS skills, with expert groups showing no performance improvement across sessions. (Rasmussen Rasmus Christian et al. (2019))
  • Eyesi Surgical virtual reality ophthalmic surgery simulator: The Eyesi Surgical (VRmagic, Mannheim, Germany) is "a high-fidelity virtual reality simulator designed for practising intraocular procedures". It combines a mannequin head housing a model eye, an operating microscope, instrument tracking sensors, and software modules for cataract and vitreoretinal surgery steps; the system records performance metrics, enabling scores and feedback to be generated. It is the most extensively assessed ophthalmic simulator in the review, with 33 validity studies. (Lee Roxanne et al. (2020))

Claims¶

  • Virtual reality simulation training is associated with safer cataract surgery outcomes than supervised surgical training on actual patients alone [+M]
  • The EyeSi Surgical Simulator can differentiate VRS novices from experts, providing construct validity evidence [+M]
  • Repeated simulator attempts improve VRS performance, with expert groups showing no further improvement [+M]
  • No evidence currently supports transfer of simulator-based VRS training to the operating room [~M]
  • Twenty-eight studies of the Eyesi cataract training modules collectively demonstrated all five sources of validity evidence, with strong data for each parameter except response processes. [+M]
  • In a randomised controlled trial reported within the review, trainees who trained with the Eyesi achieved significant improvements in wet-lab capsulorhexis performance compared with a no-training control group. [+M]
