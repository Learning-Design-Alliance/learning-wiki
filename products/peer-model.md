## Description
The PEER Model is an evidence-based framework introduced and empirically tested by Krstić et al. for training students' collaborative problem-solving skills.

- **The PEER Model is a framework for training students' collaborative problem-solving skills**: The article introduces the PEER Model, described in its own title as an evidence-based framework aimed at training students' collaborative problem-solving skills. The title states the framework is both introduced and empirically tested in the article. The supplied text consists of the article's Open Practices Disclosure form, so the framework's internal structure and components cannot be described beyond what the title and disclosure print. (Krstić et al. (2025))
- **PEER/CROWD dialogic reading conceptual model**: The PEER/CROWD model, developed by Whitehurst and colleagues, structures dialogic reading as a reciprocal-dialogue process between caregiver and child during story sharing. PEER describes the interaction process — "Prompt the child to say something about the story, 2) Evaluate what the child says, 3) Expand on what the child says, and 4) Repeat and reinforce associations" — while CROWD names five evocative caregiver prompts: completion, recall, open-ended questions, wh- questions, and distancing. The article used this model as the conceptual basis for DialogPR item development. (Hutton John S et al. (2018))
