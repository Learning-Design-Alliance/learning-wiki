## Description
An eight-item self-report assessment developed by Klepsch and colleagues for measuring learners’ perceived intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load after a learning task.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Cognitive Load Questionnaire (CLQ) by Klepsch et al. (2017) for differentiated measurement of ICL, ECL, and GCL**: The cognitive load questionnaire (CLQ) is an eight-item self-report instrument with three scales: "A scale with two items asking for perceived ICL, a scale with three items asking for perceived ECL, and finally a scale of three items asking for perceived GCL." All items are rated on 7-point Likert-type scales. The article uses it across six studies to test whether intentional variations of one load type are reflected in the corresponding scale, finding prognostic validity to be good. (Melina Klepsch (2020))
- **Differentiated subjective Cognitive Load Questionnaire measuring ICL, ECL and GCL**: The study employed the paper-based Cognitive Load Questionnaire (Klepsch and Seufert 2012) to differentially assess the three types of cognitive load: three items each for intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load, rated on seven-point Likert scales after learning and after assessing learning outcomes. The authors note that "we were the first to use a differentiated measurement of the three types of CL in disfluency research". The instrument captures only subjective ratings of cognitive load, which the authors acknowledge may not coincide with objective load. (Janina Lehmann et al. (2015))
- **Four frequently used multidimensional subjective cognitive load questionnaires (Eysink 2009; Leppink 2013; Leppink 2014; Klepsch 2017)**: The meta-analysis examines four subjective cognitive load questionnaires that measure the cognitive load types separately with multiple items: the Eysink et al. (2009) six-item instrument (which also includes one item on perceived overall load), the Leppink et al. (2013) ten-item scale (three ICL, three ECL, four GCL items), the revised Leppink et al. (2014) version with added mental-effort items, and the Klepsch et al. (2017) instrument with ICL (two items), ECL (three items), and GCL (three items), which "is not specific to the learning topic and can therefore be easily adapted to the material used in a specific study." Across the meta-analysis sample, the Leppink (2013) questionnaire was used in 33 experiments, Leppink (2014) in seven, Klepsch (2017) in 20, and Eysink (2009) in 10. (Felix Krieglstein et al. (2022))
