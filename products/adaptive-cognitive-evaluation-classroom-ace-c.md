# Adaptive Cognitive Evaluation Classroom (ACE-C)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A group-administered iPad battery developed by Gallen et al. for measuring executive functions such as sustained attention, working memory, and multitasking in grades 4–8 classrooms.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **ACE-C: a group-administered digital executive function assessment battery for classrooms**: The Adaptive Cognitive Evaluation Classroom (ACE-C) is a digital assessment battery of nine tasks measuring executive functions such as attention, working memory, and multitasking, administered on iPads to groups of students in school settings. Tasks were "developed from cognitive assessments commonly used in lab-based settings and modified to include adaptive algorithms, motivational trial-by-trial and end-of-task feedback, and a user-friendly interface." Psychometric staircase adaptive algorithms let students perform the same tasks across time points without floor or ceiling effects. This study used its continuous performance task (CPT), adapted from the TOVA, to derive a sustained attention component score. (Gallen et al. (2023))
- **ACE-C continuous performance task (CPT) for group-administered sustained attention assessment in classrooms**: The Adaptive Cognitive Evaluation Classroom (ACE-C) battery includes a CPT adapted from the Test of Variables of Attention for iPad administration in group school settings. Participants "press a button with the index finger on their dominant hand when the symbol appeared at the top of the screen (target) and to withhold responses when it appeared at the bottom". It has a 'sustained' condition with targets on 33.3% of trials and an 'impulsive' condition with targets on 66.6% of trials, and yields RT, RTV and d-prime per condition. Adaptive algorithms with a psychometric staircase allow repeated assessment without floor or ceiling effects. (Contribution of sustained attention abilities to real-world academic skills in children.)
