# MAES (Self-Learning Methodology in Simulated Environments)

## Description
MAES is a structured six-phase, facilitator-guided methodology for self-directed peer learning through realistic clinical simulation, developed for healthcare education.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **MAES: Self-Learning Methodology in Simulated Environments**: MAES is a self-learning methodology for simulated clinical environments developed in 2013, built on problem-based learning, peer education, and clinical simulation in highly realistic environments. Its "structure of MAES is developed with 6 phases in a minimum of 2 sessions and the completion of homework by the student between sessions 1 and 2": establishing group identity, choosing a scenario, setting baseline learning objectives, designing the scenario at home, running the simulation, and a structured GAS debriefing. Students take an active role, reflecting in small working groups on their learning needs and seeking supporting evidence, guided by an expert facilitator. In this study MAES was applied for the first time to interprofessional CPR training with nursing and medical students. (Fenzi Giulio et al. (2024))
- **MAES©: self-learning methodology in simulated environments combining PBL, simulation-based learning, peer education and self-directed learning**: MAES© (Metodología de Autoaprendizaje en Entornos Simulados) is an active learning methodology for nursing simulation implemented at UCAM since 2013–2014. The article states that "MAES© is structured through the synergistic union of other active learning methodologies such as problem-based learning (PBL), SBL, peer education, and self-directed learning." Students are protagonists of the whole process, from choosing and designing a case to the simulated experience and debriefing, developing competences such as communication, reasoning, advanced technical skills and empathy through intrinsic motivation and autonomy. (Díaz Agea J.L. et al. (2019))
- **MAES© six-element session structure: autonomous teams choose a case, design a scenario for another team, experience it, and close with a structured debriefing including an expository phase**: The article states that "MAES© consists of six elements and is structured in a minimum of two sessions." In the first session a psychologically safe environment is created, autonomous work teams with a defined group identity are formed (Element 1), each team freely chooses a topic presented by the facilitator as a bait or hook (Element 2), and a brainstorming establishes what students know and want to learn (Element 3). Teams then commit to designing a scenario another team will experience within a week (Element 4). In the second session a different team runs the simulation with a simulated patient (Element 5), followed by a structured debriefing (Element 6) that includes an expository phase in which the designing team teaches the skills the whole group chose to learn, supported by reliable scientific evidence. (Díaz Agea J.L. et al. (2019))
