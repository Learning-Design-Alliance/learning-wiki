# Alliance for Resource Equity (ARE) diagnostic toolkit organized around ten dimensions of equity

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A toolkit developed by the Education Trust and Education Resource Strategies that helps district and school leaders diagnose inequities across ten dimensions of student experience and select guidebook actions for improvement.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Alliance for Resource Equity toolkit with ten dimensions of equity**: The Alliance for Resource Equity (ARE), a partnership between Ed Trust and Education Resource Strategies, offers a toolkit that "categorizes the student experience into 10 'dimensions of equity.'" It includes a diagnostic tool organized around key questions for identifying problem areas, plus practical guidebooks offering actions to address those problems. The brief highlights relevant ARE key questions and guidebook sections under each SEAD policy area for district leaders and advocates seeking local improvement. (The Education Trust (2023))
- **Alliance for Resource Equity (ARE) diagnostic toolkit organized around ten dimensions of equity**: The Alliance for Resource Equity, a partnership between Ed Trust and Education Resource Strategies, categorizes the student experience into 10 "dimensions of equity." Its toolkit includes a diagnostic tool organized around "key questions" for identifying problem areas, plus practical guidebooks offering actions to address them. - **Review both fiscal and non-fiscal resources, drilling down by account codes to find spending gaps**: The document recommends that determining the type of resources to include, "including fiscal and non-fiscal, is at the heart of the RAR." Fiscal review should examine expenditures via per-pupil expenditure reports and revenue allocations, with drill-down by program, object, and function account codes. Beyond funding, reviews should cover human resources, time, materials, programs, and partnerships. (Taylor et al. (2024))
