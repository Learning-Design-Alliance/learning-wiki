# Sector models

## Description
A set of true-to-scale paper or cardboard physical models developed by Zahn and colleagues to support conceptual, non-mathematical instruction in curved spaces and general relativity.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Sector models as physical models of curved spacetimes for teaching general relativity**: Sector models implement the Regge calculus description of curved spacetimes as physical models. A curved surface or spacetime is subdivided into small elements of area, edge lengths are determined, and flat pieces with the same edge lengths—the sectors—are constructed in the plane. The model is an approximation whose accuracy is set by the subdivision resolution, and it is computed true to scale so properties read off are quantitatively correct within the discretization error. Geodesics are constructed with pencil and ruler as locally straight lines continued across sector borders. (U Kraus et al. (2018))
- **Sector models as discrete models of curved spaces and spacetimes**: Sector models are created by subdividing a curved surface or spacetime into small elements of area and approximating each element by a flat piece with identical edge lengths and symmetry — the sectors. Arranged in a planar layout, they provide a true-to-scale representation whose accuracy is determined by the fineness of the subdivision. The article states: "Sector models are a class of discrete models for surfaces, spaces, and spacetimes that permit the tracing of geodesics with pen and ruler and the study of curvature in two and in three dimensions." Within a sector the geometry is Euclidean, so a geodesic is a straight line that continues seamlessly into neighbouring sectors; curvature is tested by joining four sectors at a common vertex, where a gap signifies positive and overlap negative curvature. (Sven Weissenborn et al. (2024))
