Freshman OnTrack¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes Evidence · 24 claims (23 for, 1 mixed) · 10 studies (5 associational, 2 causal, 2 design, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 0 of 10 report an effect size · 23 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A school early-warning indicator and dashboard system that combines student attendance, grades, discipline, and momentum data to monitor freshman on-track status and guide support.

Components¶

  • Freshman On-Track dashboard aggregating attendance, grades, discipline, and momentum scores into a single student view: The Freshman On-Track dashboard is a co-designed configuration solution that "aggregates attendance, grades, discipline, and momentum scores into a single student view," eliminating manual cross-platform data pulls and creating a holistic student profile. It provides an at-a-glance view of on-track status for individual students and groups, with weekly updates to identify students who need support. It was deployed at CICS Northtown Academy in January alongside end-of-quarter data review cycles. (From Fragmented Data to Actionable Insights: The CICS and Innovare Co-Design Approach to A)
  • The freshman on-track indicator: The on-track indicator is a binary baseline measure of acceptable freshman performance developed by the Consortium in 1999 and since adopted into the Chicago public high school accountability system. A student is on-track if two criteria are met: "The student has accumulated five full course credits" needed for tenth-grade promotion, and "no more than one semester F" in a core subject (English, math, science, or social studies). It combines credit accumulation across all credit-bearing classes with core-course failures counted by semester, and provides timely information about performance within students' first year, unlike dropout rates or test scores. (Allensworth et al. (2005))
  • Freshman OnTrack indicator: The chapter overview lists "Freshman OnTrack" among its publication tags alongside "indicators", "9th grade", and "high school graduation", positioning it as an on-track measure used in ninth-grade dropout prevention monitoring. The supplied text names it only as a tag and gives no further definition, so its content here is limited to that role in the Chicago case study framing. (Prevention Science in School Settings chapter (2015))
  • CPS Graduation Pathways data tools: freshman watch list, monthly freshmen success reports, and credit recovery reports: The Office of Graduation Pathways, working with Consortium researchers, developed a set of real-time data tools for identifying and monitoring ninth-graders at risk of going off-track. These included "a “freshman watch list” that flagged incoming ninth-graders at risk of weak attendance and poor academic performance," monthly "freshmen success reports" identifying students needing intervention, and a "credit recovery" report flagging spring-semester students needing to make up core credits. The tools filled a gap, since principals previously could not link cumulative days absent with course outcomes. (Roderick et al. (2021))
  • The on-track indicator: a quantitative ninth-grade measure of adequate progress toward graduation: The on-track indicator, developed by UChicago CCSR in the late 1990s, is "a simple quantitative measure of whether ninth-graders are making adequate progress to graduation based on their credit completion and course failures." A student is on-track if she earns enough credits for tenth-grade promotion and no more than one semester F in a core course. The summary reports on-track students are almost four times more likely to graduate, and the measure was designed to be "easily calculated, monitored, and ultimately acted and improved upon." (Roderick et al. (2014))
  • The on-track indicator as a first-year high school achievement measure: The on-track indicator is a binary first-year achievement measure used by the Consortium on Chicago School Research to judge whether ninth-graders are progressing toward graduation. The report defines it operationally: "In order for a student to be 'on track' to graduate, he or she must have no more than one 'F' in a core academic course and have enough credits to be promoted to the next grade." It is used here to track Vivaldi graduates' first-year achievement across destination schools, cohorts, and gender, and the report calls it strongly related to students' likelihood of graduating. (Miller et al. (2003))
  • Freshman and Sophomore Success Categories: a four-level indicator taxonomy for monitoring students: The report proposes parallel four-category systems for incoming sophomores (Freshman Success Categories) and for monitoring during sophomore year (Sophomore Success Categories). Each system layers GPA, course failures, and attendance onto on-track status: "This system contains four Freshman Success Categories: 1) Off-Track, 2) On-Track Warning, 3) On-Track Below 3.0, and 4) On-Track Above 3.0." The categories identify distinct groups needing different supports, from credit recovery for off-track students to grade maintenance for students above 3.0 seeking selective post-secondary access. (Seeskin et al. (2020))

Claims¶

  • Fragmented data systems made intervention planning slow and burdensome, with educators spending 2-3 hours building a single student intervention plan [+W]
  • Students off track for graduation after ninth grade are less than half as likely to graduate than their on-track peers, with disproportionate impacts on Black and Latino students [+M]
  • Freshman attendance below 85 percent predicts non-graduation even among students with similar grades [+W]
  • On-track status predicts graduation better than eighth-grade test scores [+W]
  • Freshman on-track students are over three times as likely to graduate in four years as off-track students in CPS [+W]
  • The on-track–graduation relationship remains strong after accounting for student background characteristics [+W]
  • The freshman on-track indicator predicts five-year graduation equally or more strongly for students with disabilities than for students without disabilities, with on-track students two to six times more likely to graduate [+W]
  • Citywide freshman on-track rates improved from 48 to 58 percent over a decade, with declines tied to increased course-taking [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track status predicts graduation more strongly than prior achievement, with a 55 percentage point gap after controls [+W]
  • Three freshman warning indicators—off-track status, any course failure, and attendance below 85 percent—identify 70 percent of eventual non-graduates [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track status predicts graduation more strongly than prior achievement, with a 55 percentage point gap after controls [+M]
  • Freshman OnTrack rates improved most among the highest-risk students, including Black and Latinx young men and the lowest-achieving entrants [+M]
  • CPS Freshman OnTrack rates rose from 58 to 89 percent between 2007 and 2017 while graduation rates rose 19 percentage points [+M]
  • Freshman OnTrack remains a strong predictor of high school graduation even as on-track rates have risen [+M]
  • Districtwide ninth-grade on-track rates rose 25 percentage points between 2007 and 2013 across racial, gender, and achievement subgroups [+M]
  • Ninth-graders who are on-track are 3.5 times more likely to graduate than those who are off-track [+W]
  • Freshman OnTrack remains a strong predictor of high school graduation even as on-track rates have risen [+W]
  • Systemwide CPS first-year on-track percentages ranged from 44.8 to 50.8 across the 1997–2001 cohorts [+W]
  • The on-track indicator is strongly related to students' likelihood of graduating [+W]
  • Vivaldi students' first-year on-track rates differed across destination high schools for the 1997 cohort [+W]
  • Vivaldi students' first-year on-track rates differed across destination high schools for the 2001 cohort [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track ELL students in Chicago were up to 3.5 times more likely to graduate than off-track students [+W]
  • Three freshman warning indicators—off-track status, any course failure, and attendance below 85 percent—identify 70 percent of eventual non-graduates [+M]
  • By the end of sophomore year, 84 percent of eventual non-graduates showed at least one warning indicator [+M]
  • Student performance declines from freshman to sophomore year, with course failures rising in every subject and attendance declining sharply [+M]
  • Gains in sophomore performance were unequal: about one in five Latino and Black young men were off-track at the end of sophomore year [+M]
  • Sophomore OnTrack rose from 61 to 85 percent and predicts graduation, but 30 percent of non-graduates were on-track as sophomores [~M]
