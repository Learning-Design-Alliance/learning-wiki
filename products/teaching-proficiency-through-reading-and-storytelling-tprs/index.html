Teaching Proficiency through Reading and Storytelling (TPRS)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TPRS is a language-teaching method developed by Ray and Seely that uses comprehensible target-language input, physical-response vocabulary introduction, collaborative story creation, and reading or retelling activities for second- and foreign-language learners.

Components¶

  • TPRS three-step storytelling methodology adapted for very young learners: TPRS (Teaching Proficiency through Reading and Storytelling) is a language teaching method built on Krashen's Natural Approach and Asher's Total Physical Response that provides "comprehensible input on high frequency vocabulary and structures in a highly contextualized and personalized way". It proceeds through three steps: establishing meaning (pre-teaching via L1 translation, gestures, and PQA), asking a story (storytelling with circling questions and student acting, where "learning is a by-product"), and reading. In this study the steps were adapted for illiterate four-year-olds, replacing the reading step with teacher read-alouds, crafts, songs, and games. (Ketevan Kara (2019))
  • TPRS is a suitable methodology for teaching vocabulary to very young learners and can be applied in kindergarten EFL curricula: Based on the observed gains and the students' enjoyment and motivation during TPRS classes, the article concludes that "TPRS is a sui table methodology for very young learners and can be applied in EFL curricula in kindergartens". It argues TPRS suits this age group because it provides concrete concepts, familiar contexts, and kinesthetic and explorative activities, and suggests a curriculum built on TPRS principles could facilitate teachers' work where no prescribed kindergarten EFL curriculum exists. (Ketevan Kara (2019))

Claims¶

  • Reading proficiency improved across all performance groups, with low performers reaching Grade 3b reading standards and top performers reaching Grade 6a–7a levels by Cycle 3 [+W]
  • Students' writing and storytelling developed substantially, with the majority group publishing 23 school-level stories in Cycle 1 and 38 in Cycle 2 before a school cap limited publications [+W]
  • TPRS treatment produces a short-term effect on very young learners' receptive and productive L2 vocabulary, with immediate posttest means higher than pretests in all weeks [+M]
  • Four-year-olds taught L2 vocabulary with TPRS largely retained it on delayed posttests two weeks later, in a single-group study with no control group [+W]
  • Receptive vocabulary learning benefits from TPRS instruction more than productive learning in very young learners [+M]
