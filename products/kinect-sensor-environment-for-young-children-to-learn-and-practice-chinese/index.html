Kinect-sensor environment for young children to learn and practice Chinese¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A Kinect-based educational environment described by Si (2014) that enables young children to learn and practise Chinese language and cultural skills through multimodal sensor input.

Components¶

  • Kinect-sensor environment for young children to learn and practice Chinese (Si, 2014): An environment described by Si (2014) for "young children to learn and practice Chinese," using a Kinect sensor that allows a wider variety of input than AIED systems have typically used. The editors expect this sensing technology to appear more frequently in future AIED systems, particularly for domains such as culture where verbal and non-verbal skills like proximity or gestures are teaching targets. (Amy Ogan et al. (2015))
  • Four Kinect-enabled multiplayer virtual Chinese practice environments: A Unity-based multi-user system with a central server and Kinect-equipped clients, plus a mouse-and-keyboard fallback, in which users control virtual characters with body movements and talk via integrated voice chat. Four environments were built: Cao Chung Weighing an Elephant, The Elephant and the Blind Men, a school café, and a chat room, each embedding narratives and puzzles. The environments were "designed as supplementary materials to the learners' regular Chinese classes" and support different levels of open-ended conversation with an emphasis on social interaction. (Mei Si (2015))

Claims¶

  • Using the virtual environments increased the proportion of actively engaged students in a month-long deployment with 6–8-year-olds [+M]
  • Half the students could produce simple two- or three-word Chinese sentences by the end of the evaluation, a skill developed through Environment 1 [+M]
