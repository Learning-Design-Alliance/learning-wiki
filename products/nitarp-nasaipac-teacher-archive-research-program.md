## Description
A NASA/IPAC-run year-long professional-development programme that pairs educators with astronomers to conduct authentic archival astronomy research and share it with their communities.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **The NITARP year-long teacher-scientist partnership model: educators experience the full research process through three trips and authentic original research**: NITARP is a professional development model in which small groups of predominantly high school educators partner with a research astronomer for a year-long original research project that "follows the research process, from writing a proposal, collecting data, analyzing data, writing up findings, and then presenting the work at a professional society meeting". The program runs January to January with three roughly week-long trips (AAS Bootcamp, Caltech summer visit with students, and a return AAS meeting to present results), and requires each team to present at least one science and one education poster. Its goal is to deepen teachers' understanding of the nature of research so their current and future students are affected via changes in teaching styles. (Rebull et al. (2018))
- **NITARP's calendar-year structure: proposal, remote work, intensive summer visit, and AAS presentation, with a mentor educator supporting each new team**: A reusable program-level design pattern: a cohort kicks off with a Bootcamp before the AAS winter meeting, writes a peer-reviewed proposal due in March, works remotely through spring, spends four days at Caltech in summer getting intensively into data reduction and analysis, and returns to the next AAS in January to present results in regular sessions. Each team includes a mentor educator who has been through the program before and "helps in translating the scientist to teachers (and vice versa)". The fourth summer day is deliberately run as "training wheels" time for the team to work on their own, with the mentor educator setting the tone. (Rebull et al. (2018))
- **NITARP year-long authentic research program for educators**: NITARP, the NASA/IPAC Teacher Archive Research Program run from Caltech/IPAC, partners small groups of educators with a research astronomer for a year-long authentic research project using data housed at IPAC, mostly professional infrared NASA data. The article states "NITARP partners small groups of educators with a research astronomer for a year-long authentic research project". It culminates in presenting a science poster at an American Astronomical Society meeting, where participants are treated like all other professional astronomers. (L. M. Rebull (2018))
- **NITARP: NASA/IPAC Teacher Archive Research Program**: NITARP is a year-long (13-month) PD program in which "Small groups of educators from all over the country are paired with a professional astronomer" to conduct authentic research with archival astronomy data. Teams write a peer-reviewed proposal, analyze data, and present science and education posters at the January AAS meeting alongside professional astronomers. Each team includes a mentor astronomer, a mentor teacher (alumnus), and typically 3 new educators. The program has worked with 103 educators from 34 states since 2005, who reach an estimated over 22,000 students/year, and NITARP teams have contributed 58 science and 68 education posters to AAS meetings and to eight refereed papers in major astronomy journals. (L. M. Rebull et al. (2018))
- **NITARP as an instantiation of the Yezierski & Herrington qualities of successful PD**: The article maps NITARP onto an enumerated list of qualities of successful professional development drawn from Yezierski & Herrington: cohort membership, collaboration with faculty, duration, rigor, support, treated as professionals, accountability, reflection, research, materials development, action research, and coherence. For each quality the article describes how the program implements it — for example, teams meet weekly for support and accountability, participants must defend their work at the AAS meeting among professionals, and the education poster requirement formalizes reflection. The program also aligns with NSTA position statement characteristics of sustained focus and collaboration. (L. M. Rebull et al. (2018))
