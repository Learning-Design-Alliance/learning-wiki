# General Medicine In-Training Examination (GM-ITE®)

## Description
The GM-ITE® is a nationwide standardized multiple-choice examination developed and administered in Japan to assess the test-based clinical knowledge of postgraduate year 1 and 2 resident physicians.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **General Medicine In-Training Examination (GM-ITE®) as a nationwide standardized knowledge assessment for Japanese residents**: The GM-ITE is a nationwide standardized assessment aligned with Japan's clinical training guidelines, administered at the end of PGY-1 and PGY-2 to evaluate resident physicians' test-based clinical knowledge. The 2023 version comprised 80 multiple-choice questions across four categories and six clinical fields, with 10 questions in English. It "does not aim to establish equivalence or superiority across groups, but rather provides a common assessment framework within which exploratory comparisons can be descriptively examined" and serves not as a pass-fail examination but to identify proficiency and weaknesses. (Ishizuka Kosuke et al. (2026))
- **Clinical simulation video embedded in the GM-ITE assessing non-verbal cue recognition**: A 5-minute video filmed from a resident physician's perspective showing a medical interview and physical examination of a 40-year-old man presenting with fainting after lower limb fracture, in whom the correct diagnosis was acute pulmonary thromboembolism. Tachypnea of "28 breaths per minute" was "not highlighted in the text, but presented as non-verbal cues," requiring observation without textual focus. Participants answered a free-text diagnosis question and listed three pertinent positive findings, with interrater agreement of κ=0.91 (Q1) and κ=0.82 (Q2). Produced by a professional television production company under JAMEP supervisors with professional actors and added heart-sound effects. (Shikino K et al. (2025))
