# MIXER (Moderating Interactions for Cross-Cultural Empathic Relationships)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A computer-based experiential learning application described by Hall et al. that places children in a virtual summer-camp scenario to practise intercultural conflict competence and empathic inclusion.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **MIXER experiential learning application**: MIXER (Moderating Interactions for Cross-Cultural Empathic Relationships) is a computer-based learning experience for 9–11 year olds set in a virtual summer camp where two teams of six intelligent agents play Werewolves with different rule sets representing two synthetic cultures or moral circles. The child user acts as an invisible friend advising the protagonist Tom via a tablet-based Pictorial Interaction Language of over 70 graphics, learning by observing and advising rather than playing. The scenario ends with Tom resolving the conflict by discussing the rule differences, reinforcing that apparent unfairness may reflect simple differences that can be resolved. (Hall et al. (2014))

### Claims
- [Children showed high engagement and enjoyment of MIXER, with nearly 90% wanting to use it again](../claims/mixer-high-engagement-enjoyment.md) [+M]
