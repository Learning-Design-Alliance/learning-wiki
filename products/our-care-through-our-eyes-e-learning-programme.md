# Our Care Through Our Eyes e-learning programme

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
A co-designed e-learning programme of three short packages that trains registered children's nurses to understand self-harm, communicate effectively with children and young people, and assess risk and manage safety; it was developed by children and young people, registered children's nurses, and academics.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **'Our Care Through Our Eyes' co-designed self-harm e-learning programme (three 15-minute RLO packages)**: A digital education intervention made of reusable learning objects — digital resources with a single learning objective engaging users in four learning components — co-designed by children and young people, registered children's nurses and academics. It consists of three short (15 min) e-learning packages covering understanding self-harm and care pathways, effective communication with CYP following self-harm admission, and assessing risk and managing safety. This study evaluated it in a new hospital setting with new outcome measures. (Singh‐Weldon J. et al. (2022))
- **'Our Care through Our Eyes' co-produced digital educational intervention (three reusable learning objects)**: A digital educational programme hosted on an online platform, composed of reusable learning objects co-produced with CYP service users, registered children's nurses and academics. RLOs are "short, self-contained, focused 'chunks' of e-learning underpinned by learning theory comprising multimedia rich content, activities and self-assessment". The final intervention consisted of three RLOs covering understanding self-harm and care pathways, effective communication, and assessing risk and managing safety, delivered through multimedia including videos of youth actors narrating CYP experiences. It was piloted with seven student nurses to test functionality, face validity and time requirement, and evaluated in the uncontrolled pre–post study. (Manning Joseph C et al. (2017))

### Claims
- [In one uncontrolled pre–post study, children's nurses' self-rated antipathy scores towards patients who self-harm, measured by overall SHAS scores, fell from 68.1 before to 62.8 after the 'Our Care Through Our Eyes' e-learning programme, with small decreases across most SHAS sub-scales](../claims/rlo-elearning-reduced-nurse-antipathy-self-harm.md) [+W]
- [Children's nurses reported that lack of confidence and knowledge were barriers to caring for CYP who self-harm, and valued the e-learning programme's communication tools and scenario videos](../claims/nurse-confidence-barriers-rlo-valued-tools.md) [+W]
- [Registered children's nurses who completed the co-produced digital educational intervention showed a statistically significant increase in knowledge of self-harm in CYP (Cohen's d = 0.69 in completers)](../claims/coproduced-rlo-knowledge-gains-self-harm-nurses.md) [+M]
- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, nurses' self-rated attitudes towards CYP who self-harm rose on the Effectiveness factor only from pre-test to post-test, with no significant changes on Negativity or Worry (completers d = 0.49)](../claims/attitude-effectiveness-improvement-completers.md) [+M]
- [Agreement with confidence statements improved in six of seven areas, significantly for perceived ability to care for a child, to communicate with parents, and to not make things worse](../claims/confidence-statements-significant-gains-three-areas.md) [+M]
- [Clinical behavioural intention subscale scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test among completers in a single-group pre-post study: beliefs about capabilities (r = 0.42), moral norm (r = 0.43) and beliefs about consequences (r = 0.49)](../claims/behavioural-intention-three-subscales-improved-completers.md) [+M]
- [Interviewed nurses reported skill development, empowerment and improved confidence, and reflection on their practice following the intervention](../claims/qualitative-empowerment-skill-development-reflection.md) [+M]
- [Self-efficacy for helping CYP who self-harm significantly decreased in the full follow-up sample (d = 0.29) but the decrease was not significant among intervention completers](../claims/self-efficacy-decrease-full-sample-not-completers.md) [~M]
