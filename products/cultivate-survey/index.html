Cultivate Survey¶

Description¶

The Cultivate Survey is a twice-yearly anonymous student survey developed by the University of Chicago Consortium on School Research to measure classroom learning conditions in grades 5–12.

Components¶

  • Cultivate student experience survey and reporting site: Cultivate is a student experience survey developed by the UChicago Consortium's Learning & Development Group, administered districtwide in CPS since fall 2022 to students in grades 5-12 twice a school year about one of their core subject classes. It asks students about nine classroom learning conditions and their own learning strategies and beliefs. The reporting site provides school-level reports depicting responses to each learning condition, an ordering of weakest to strongest conditions, changes over time, and disaggregation by grade level, subject area, or student demographics. Data is schoolwide and shared first with school leaders, and does not identify individual teachers and classro (Faye Kroshinsky et al. (2026))
  • Cultivate framework of nine classroom learning conditions plus learning strategies and beliefs: The Cultivate survey is organized around a research-based framework of nine classroom learning conditions—such as Student Voice, Supportive Teaching, and Classroom Community—each accompanied by a guide outlining the principles underlying the condition, research background, practice ideas, scripted activities, reflection questions, and additional resources. Alongside the learning conditions, the survey measures student self-reflections on their own learning strategies and learning beliefs (such as Belonging and Agency) in the context of the same core class. The framework positions survey systems as practical, actionable, and catalytic tools within a comprehensive approach to understanding and (Faye Kroshinsky et al. (2026))

Claims¶

  • The Cultivate Survey's impact question indicates whether students experience authentic voice and influence in school improvement [+W]
  • Schurz High School's Cultivate-driven feedback-for-growth improvement work coincided with improved student experiences and out-of-school suspensions dropping to one-half of the prior year's number [+W]
  • Students' survey reports of their school experiences predict later academic and well-being outcomes including attendance, grades, test scores, graduation, college enrollment, and well-being [+M]
  • All nine Cultivate learning condition measures show strong Rasch person reliability (0.82-0.87) in CPS administrations [+M]
  • Each individual learning condition predicts I am Valued gains, with Classroom Community the largest (15.0 middle grades, 16.9 high school percentile points) [+M]
  • Cultivate survey items function without systematic bias across student groups by race/ethnicity, gender, economic disadvantage, learning differences, or EL status [+W]
  • Students' experiences of learning conditions strongly and significantly predict their self-reports of school engagement [+W]
  • Within-year improvements in overall learning conditions predict large gains in students' I am Valued beliefs [+W]
