## Description
iOLab is a handheld wireless laboratory device with sensors and companion real-time data-acquisition software developed by Mats Selen and Tim Stelzer at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign for physics experimentation and analysis.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **iOLab handheld wireless lab device with multiple sensors and real-time data acquisition**: The iOLab is a handheld, wheeled laboratory device developed by Mats Selen and Tim Stelzer at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, sold as of 2019 for $199 or rented from $42 for 3 months. It is "a handheld device on wheels equipped with a variety of sensors communicating wirelessly to a USB dongle", and its free software collects and presents data in real time with built-in statistical tools for slope and area under the curve. The authors used its wheel, force, accelerometer, gyro, magnetometer, analog input, barometer, sound and light sensors across mechanics and E&M labs, judging it cheaper and more versatile than third-party lab kits. (Louis Leblond et al. (2020))
- **iOLab digital device as a multifunctional wireless lab tool for high school physics inquiry**: The iOLab, developed by the PER group at the University of Illinois, is a compact device with "consistent and reliable sensors", a force sensor and voltage pins, paired with an "intuitive graphical interface for real-time data analysis". The article states it "allows students to carry out laboratory exercises across the full range of high school Physics and beyond, whether at home or in the classroom, using a single device and reducing cognitive load". It was chosen to standardise sensor capabilities across students and avoid legal risks of personal smartphones. (E. Tufino et al. (2024))
