## Description
A National Guard-run residential programme for youth ages 16–18 who have left or been expelled from high school, combining GED preparation with quasi-military training and leadership, citizenship, service, life-skills, fitness, health, job-skills, and academic components.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Three-phase residential quasi-military program with post-residential structured mentoring**: The program organizes intervention delivery into three phases: a two-week residential pre-ChalleNGe introduction to rules and expectations, a 20-week highly structured quasi-military residential phase on a National Guard base or school campus, and a one-year post-residential phase. The article states "This phase involves structured mentoring with a mentor of the trainee's choosing," with staff maintaining at least monthly contact. The pattern pairs intensive residential structure with sustained community-based support during transition. (National Guard Youth ChalleNGe Program (2010))

## Description
A residential education and training program for youth ages 16 to 18 who have dropped out of or been expelled from high school. Its 20-week residential phase is quasi-military — cadets "live in barracks, have their hair cut short, wear uniforms, experience military-style discipline" — and "focuses on eight components: leadership, responsible citizenship, service to community, life-coping skills, physical fitness, health and hygiene, job skills, and academic excellence." Most of the day is spent on GED preparation. It costs about $14,000 per individual and is free to participants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must be unemployed, drug free, legal residents of the offering state, and not heavily involved with the criminal justice system
#### Constraints
- No requirements for military service; sites accept an average of 100 trainees per cohort

### Target Learners
- At-risk youth ages 16 to 18 who have dropped out of or been expelled from high school

### Target Learning Goals
- GED or high school diploma attainment
- job skills
- leadership
- responsible citizenship
- physical fitness and life-coping skills

## Description
The program organizes intervention delivery into three phases: a two-week residential pre-ChalleNGe introduction to rules and expectations, a 20-week highly structured quasi-military residential phase on a National Guard base or school campus, and a one-year post-residential phase. The article states "This phase involves structured mentoring with a mentor of the trainee's choosing," with staff maintaining at least monthly contact. The pattern pairs intensive residential structure with sustained community-based support during transition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors are selected by trainees, then screened and trained by the program; mentors meet trainees a minimum of four hours per month
#### Constraints
- The post-residential phase lasts one year; the residential phase is highly regimented

- High school dropouts and expelled youth ages 16 to 18

### Learning Goals
