## Description
Too Good for Drugs is a classroom-delivered drug prevention and character education program for elementary and middle school students, consisting of "10 lessons at each grade level lasting 30–45 minutes per lesson." All lessons are scripted and taught by trained teachers or off-site program instructors. Instruction centers on "interactive learning and skill-building exercises," with students engaging "in role-play and cooperative learning games" and applying skills to different contexts. Core values such as respect for self and others, empathic responding, and responsibility are integrated into lessons, and optional parental and community involvement components (parent newsletters, interactive family materials) are included.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scripted curricula delivered by trained teachers or program instructors; grade-level kits with student workbooks, measurable objectives, evaluation tools, and lesson extenders
#### Constraints
- The program may have changed since the studies were conducted; the WWC recommends asking the developer about the most current version and considering that student demographics and school context may affect outcomes

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students (grades 3-4)
- Middle school students (grade 6)

### Target Learning Goals
- Life skills and character values
- Resistance skills to negative peer influence
- Resistance to use of illegal drugs, alcohol, and tobacco
- Goal setting and decisionmaking skills
