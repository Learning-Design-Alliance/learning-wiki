PAUSE framework¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

A Vanderbilt University framework that guides recruitment and hiring teams through self-reflection and bias-interruption strategies.

Components¶

  • PAUSE framework for interrupting bias in recruitment and hiring decisions: The PAUSE framework, developed by Vanderbilt University, is presented as a tool that "can encourage critical self-reflection and interrupt the effects of unchecked biases." The guide recommends applying it to recruitment and hiring decisions to "reveal the ways in which bias might be manifesting in the process." It sits alongside measures such as the Harvard Implicit Association Test, which the guide calls "a starting point" for individuals and teams working to interrupt bias. (Equitable et al. (2023))
  • PAUSE self-reflection web tool: PAUSE (Patterns of AI Use: Self-Examination) is a public, no-login web tool that "delivers a short, free, no-login self-check, scores it entirely in the browser, and returns descriptive, domain-aware reflections". It pairs reverse-scored behavioural Likert items with a claim-evaluation reasoning probe, a 90-second Alternative Uses creativity probe, and a retrospective before-and-after block reported separately as a trajectory indicator. Domain readings are scaled 0-100 and mapped to four fixed descriptive bands, with no overall composite. The author frames it as a reflective design probe, not a validated instrument. (Mahbub Ul Alam (2026))
  • Citation-grounded behavioural item design with reverse scoring: PAUSE's items follow a reusable design pattern: every LLM-era item is anchored to a 2023-2026 paper, items are behavioural rather than dispositional, and roughly one third per domain are reverse-scored, since "Roughly one third of the items per domain (two or three of seven) are reverse-scored to counter acquiescence". Each scored item carries an expandable rationale panel and a reference panel quoting the anchoring study with a resolvable DOI, so respondents can inspect the evidence behind a question. All items include a not-applicable option, and unanswered items are dropped rather than imputed. (Mahbub Ul Alam (2026))
