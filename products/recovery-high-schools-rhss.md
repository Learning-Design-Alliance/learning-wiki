## Description
RHSs are secondary schools that "provide standard high school education and award secondary school diplomas, but also include therapeutic programming aimed at promoting recovery (e.g., group check‐ins, community service, counseling sessions)." First instituted in 1979, they are typically small (around 30–40 students on average), with 36 operating in the United States per the Association of Recovery Schools. They have two primary foci—meeting state requirements for a secondary diploma and supporting students in maintaining recovery—and most require some SUD treatment or minimum sobriety for admission.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Eligible RHS programs must have an explicit goal of providing academic instruction, a recovery‐oriented environment requiring previous treatment and/or commitment to sobriety, and meet state requirements for awarding a secondary diploma
#### Constraints
- If part of the public school system, RHSs cannot mandate previous treatment and instead rely on interviews to determine interest in maintaining sobriety; accreditation standards are new and only available in the United States

### Target Learners
- adolescents who have completed substance use treatment and are seeking to complete high school

### Target Learning Goals
- academic advancement toward a secondary diploma
- recovery maintenance and relapse prevention
