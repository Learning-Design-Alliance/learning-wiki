# HOPS (Homework, Organization, and Planning Skills)

## Description
A brief individual school-based intervention developed by Langberg and colleagues to teach middle school students with ADHD organization, homework-recording, and planning/time-management skills, with parent involvement and reward-based skill tracking.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **HOPS intervention: a 16-session brief school-based organizational skills intervention**: HOPS (Homework, Organization, and Planning Skills) is a 16-session individual intervention delivered by school mental health providers during the school day, with each meeting lasting 20 minutes or less. It covers three skill areas: "school materials organization and management, homework recording, and planning/time-management," supported by a point system with gift-card rewards and two parent meetings. In this trial it was implemented with an average adherence of 85.44% across sessions and providers. (Langberg et al. (2017))
- **HOPS program: 16-session individualized organizational skills training with three components plus parent and teacher consultation**: HOPS is a 16-session child skills training program for middle school students, delivered in sessions of about 20 minutes on an individualized basis (or in small groups), covering materials organization, homework recording and management, and time management. The article states "HOPS includes 16 sessions with the student, each about 20 minutes long. HOPS has three components: (1) materials organization, (2) homework management, and (3) time management." Each component uses operationalized checklists (organization checklist, homework planner and folder system, time management skills checklist) completed or checked at sessions, and the program adds caregiver consultations and, when feasible, teacher consultation to support generalization. (Nissley-Tsiopinis J. et al. (2026))
- **HOPS conceptual framework: behavioral therapy principles with cognitive-behavioral elements, linking proximal OTMP gains to distal academic outcomes**: The article's theory of change holds that HOPS works through behavioral strategies — verbal instruction, prompting, shaping, and contingency management with positive reinforcement for targeted OTMP behaviors — plus cognitive-behavioral strategies to modify negative, defeatist attitudes, with parent and teacher consultation fostering generalization and maintenance. The article states "The HOPS program is based primarily on principles of behavior therapy and incorporates elements of cognitive behavioral therapy." Improvements in OTMP skills and homework performance are the proximal outcomes expected to mediate gains in distal academic outcomes, with fidelity, engagement, and baseline severity (organization problems, ADHD, ODD, internalizing symptoms, medication status, SES) as moderators (Fig 2). (Nissley-Tsiopinis J. et al. (2026))
