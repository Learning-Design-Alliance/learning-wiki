# Teacher as Social Context Questionnaire (TASC) and Situations in School Questionnaire (SIS)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The TASC and SIS are questionnaire-based assessment instruments used to measure students' or teachers' perceptions of teacher autonomy support, structure, involvement, control, and chaos in K–12 and post-secondary educational settings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Teacher as Social Context Questionnaire (TASC) and Situations in School Questionnaire (SIS) as measures of autonomy support and structure**: The review identifies the two most frequently used measures of autonomy support and structure. The TASC (Belmont et al., 1988) comprises 41 items and three sub-scales (autonomy support 12 items, structure 15 items, involvement 14 items), usable as student or teacher report, with Cronbach's alpha 0.66–0.90 (student) and 0.73–0.90 (teacher). The SIS (Aelterman et al., 2019) is "a vignette-based questionnaire" with fifteen vignettes rated on a 7-point scale, with alpha 0.80–0.92 (student) and 0.73–0.85 (teacher). Both measure autonomy support and structure alongside their opposites, control and chaos. (Alexandra Patzak (2025))
- **Teacher as Social Context (TASC) questionnaire for perceived teaching style**: The Teacher as Social Context (TASC) questionnaire measures student perceptions of teacher support across the three Self-Determination Theory dimensions of involvement, structure, and support (autonomy). The article states: "The questionnaire comprised 24 items, with eight items each for involvement, structure, and support," answered on a 4-point Likert scale. A Persian version was used following International Test Commission guidelines; in this study Cronbach's alpha was 0.78 for involvement, 0.81 for structure, and 0.75 for support. (Afzali Leila et al. (2024))
