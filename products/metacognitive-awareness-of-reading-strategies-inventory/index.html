Metacognitive Awareness of Reading Strategies Inventory¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

MARSI is a self-report assessment developed by Mokhtari and Reichard to measure learners' awareness and use of global, problem-solving, and support reading strategies.

Components¶

  • MARSI-based questionnaire adapted to Vietnamese for measuring metacognitive reading strategy use: A 30-item, 5-point Likert questionnaire adopted from the Metacognitive Awareness of Reading Strategies Inventory (MARSI) by Mokhtari and Richard (2002), administered in Vietnamese with items clustered into three categories: Global Reading Strategies (items 1–13), Problem-Solving Strategies (items 14–22), and Support Reading Strategies (items 23–30). In this study it showed high internal reliability (α = .87) and was scored using high (≥3.5), moderate (2.5–3.4), and low (≤2.4) usage levels. (Thao M. T. Nguyen et al. (2011))
  • Turkish version of the Metacognitive Awareness of Reading Strategies Inventory (MARSI, 2002): The study used a Turkish adaptation of Mokhtari and Reichard's (2002) 30-item Metacognitive Awareness of Reading Strategies Inventory, with 13 global, 8 problem-solving, and 9 support strategy items, administered to both groups after the pre-test. The article reports a total internal consistency reliability (KR-20) of 0.84, with subscale alphas of 0.77, 0.49, and 0.46, and cites Öztürk (2012) reporting a Cronbach alpha of .93 for the Turkish version. (Altay et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Vietnamese EFL grade-11 learners report above-average overall metacognitive strategy use in reading [+W]
  • Metacognitive strategy use correlates positively with EFL reading comprehension achievement (r = .54) [+W]
  • Successful readers out-use less successful readers on global and support strategies but not problem-solving strategies [+W]
  • Learners' metacognitive reading strategy type (global, problem-solving, or support) showed no statistically significant effect on reading test scores [+M]
