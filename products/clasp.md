## Description
A three-quarter introductory physics curriculum for bioscience majors, developed and run at UC Davis and implemented in other university settings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **CLASP course series (Physics 7A/7B/7C): a large-enrollment reformed introductory physics course for bioscience majors**: CLASP (Collaborative Learning through Active Sense-making in Physics) is a three-quarter, one-year introductory physics course at UC Davis enrolling about 1700 students per year, fully institutionalized since 1996-97 as the physics requirement for biological science majors. Each quarter has five independent sections of roughly 300 students taught by two co-instructors and five TAs, with one 80-minute lecture per week and two 140-minute discussion/laboratory meetings, so less than a quarter of in-class time is lecture. The course was designated an exemplary practice course in a 2006 national CEPR review of 139 introductory physics courses, and has also been implemented at California State Uni (Potter et al. (2018))
- **CLASP (Connecting Libraries and Schools Project)**: CLASP was a three-year, $3.6 million pilot program initiated in 1991 by the DeWitt Wallace-Reader's Digest Fund to promote collaborative programs among the New York Public Library, New York City public schools, and neighborhood organizations. A total of 107 schools from three NYC school districts and 23 branch libraries were involved. Typical activities included parent workshops, class visits, craft programs, and summer reading clubs. CLASP also purchased 60,928 items to supplement branch collections and issued mini-grants to teachers for special projects. (Small et al. (1994))

## Description
CLASP (Collaborative Learning through Active Sense-making in Physics) is a three-quarter, one-year introductory physics course at UC Davis enrolling about 1700 students per year, fully institutionalized since 1996-97 as the physics requirement for biological science majors. Each quarter has five independent sections of roughly 300 students taught by two co-instructors and five TAs, with one 80-minute lecture per week and two 140-minute discussion/laboratory meetings, so less than a quarter of in-class time is lecture. The course was designated an exemplary practice course in a 2006 national CEPR review of 139 introductory physics courses, and has also been implemented at California State University, San Marcos.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Five-member groups at tables with a blackboard per group, and detailed student prompts (workbook-style) plus detailed instructor notes for each DL activity.
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is not a flipped classroom because there is currently no online component, though lecture time can have some similar features.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate bioscience and agriculture majors at a large research university

### Target Learning Goals
- Sense-making and conceptual understanding of physics models
- Preparation for later courses in the major
