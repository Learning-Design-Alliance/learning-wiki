# AI-Enabled EdTech Adoption Cycle

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A four-phase adoption framework described by Ruiz et al. for guiding education systems through vision and planning, implementation, ongoing evaluation, and scaling and sustaining of AI-enabled edtech.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **AI-Enabled EdTech Adoption Cycle (vision and planning, implementation, ongoing evaluation, scaling and sustaining)**: Appendix 1 of the brief outlines a four-phase adoption cycle for AI-enabled edtech: vision and planning, implementation, ongoing evaluation, and scaling and sustaining, each with key challenges and recommendations. It states that "Planning for AI-enabled edtech requires districts to move beyond reactive purchasing decisions and establish strategic frameworks that align technology with clear learning objectives and pedagogical goals." AI-enabled tools require specialized evaluation criteria including algorithmic transparency, bias mitigation, and data privacy. (Ruiz et al. (2025))
- **Four-phase AI-enabled edtech adoption cycle for districts**: The article organizes district adoption of AI-enabled edtech into a cycle of four phases: vision and planning, implementation, ongoing evaluation, and scaling and sustaining. Each phase pairs a goal statement with key challenges and recommendations, e.g. planning requires districts to "move beyond reactive purchasing decisions and establish strategic frameworks that align technology with clear learning objectives and pedagogical goals." The cycle frames adoption as continuous rather than a one-time purchase. (Ruiz et al. (2025))
- **Six-step edtech procurement process from needs analysis to sustain-and-scale**: The guide structures district edtech procurement as six steps: Analyze & Inventory, Discover & Decide, Design & Plan, Mobilize & Implement, Evaluate & Reflect, and Sustain & Scale. Each step frames key questions and points to practical resources and templates. The guide says the steps "represent a comprehensive process that districts and schools can engage with to discover, pilot, and procure edtech tools to meet a given need," grounding the articulated need in data before exploring solutions and returning to Discover & Decide if a pilot shows a tool is not the right fit. (Digital Promise (2026))
