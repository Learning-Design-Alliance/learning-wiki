# Brief Electricity and Magnetism Assessment (BEMA)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 30-item multiple-choice conceptual assessment of introductory electricity and magnetism developed for use with calculus-based college physics students and administered by instructors or researchers.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **First application of the BEMA concept inventory and Hake's Gain as Didactic Efficiency in an intensive instruction course**: The study used the BEMA (Brief Electromagnetism Assessment), a standardized 31-question multiple-choice concept inventory covering electrostatics, direct current circuits, magnetostatics and electromagnetic induction, administered as pre- and post-test. The authors state: "Two original contributions of this work have been, first, the application of the BEMA conceptual test in an IIC on Electromagnetism and, second, the calculation of Hake's Gain to measure Didactic Efficiency in learning based on BEMA results for an IIC." Hake's Gain is reinterpreted via Covian and Celemin as Didactic Efficiency ranging from 0 to 1. (Marcela Vallejo et al. (2026))

### Claims
- [Students completing the Matter & Interactions E&M curriculum score significantly higher on the post-instruction BEMA than students completing a traditional curriculum at all four studied institutions](../claims/mi-curriculum-higher-bema-posttest.md) [+M]
- [BEMA scores correlate substantially with course grades at both introductory and upper-division levels](../claims/bema-correlates-with-course-grades.md) [+M]
- [Hake's Gain was higher in the experimental group than the control group overall and in every thematic unit, with Electric Field and Magnetic Field gains near 0.3](../claims/hakes-gain-higher-eg-all-units.md) [+M]
