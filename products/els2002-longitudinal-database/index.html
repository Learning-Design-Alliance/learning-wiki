ELS:2002 longitudinal database¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A nationally representative U.S. longitudinal education dataset maintained by the National Center for Education Statistics, following Year-10 students from 2002 through young adulthood with survey, transcript, school, and parent data.

Components¶

  • ELS:2002 longitudinal database: The US Educational Longitudinal Survey 2002 is the public secondary dataset the article analyzes: "16,197 high school Year-10 students from 751 schools followed up through age 26". It used a two-stage complex sample design with sampling weights yielding a nationally representative probability sample of public, Catholic, and other private schools, with follow-ups 2, 4, and 10 years after baseline, transcript collection for more than 90% of baseline students, and a 78.2% age-26 response rate. (Herbert W. Marsh et al. (2023))
  • ELS:2002 nationally representative sample with an independent effects two-level hierarchical linear model: The study's data and analytic apparatus: the "large nationally representative ELS:2002 sample of students in grade 10 in 2002" analyzed with "an independent effects two-level hierarchical linear model". The model estimates associations of recreational computer use and video gaming with reading and mathematics achievement cross-sectionally and with mathematics growth from grade 10 to grade 12, while "controlling for multiple covariates of achievement". (Bowers et al. (2013))

Claims¶

  • Student use of computers for fun is positively and significantly associated with cross-sectional reading and mathematics achievement in high school, controlling for multiple covariates [+M]
  • Moderate levels of video gaming are positively and significantly associated with cross-sectional reading and mathematics achievement in high school, controlling for covariates [+M]
  • Recreational computer use and moderate video gaming were not related to growth in mathematics achievement from grade 10 to grade 12 [+M]
