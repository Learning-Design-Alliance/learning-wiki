# African School of Fundamental Physics and Applications (ASP)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A biennial three-week physics capacity-building school run for selected African MSc-level and university physics students, with international lecturers, multidisciplinary courses, and bursaries.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **ASP structured mentorship program pairing alumni mentees with volunteer lecturer mentors**: The ASP mentorship program is a continuous activity that runs between consecutive biennial schools. Every two years ASP selects alumni at the level of independent research as mentees, solicits volunteer mentors from ASP lecturers and organizers, and pairs mentees with mentors considering common research interests. In October 2020 a call was made for the second round of structured mentorship, and 20 mentees were selected and paired in February 2021, with the cycle running until 2023. The report notes the "accessibility of lecturers to mentor ASP students is a valuable addition to the ASP program". (Kétévi A. Assamagan et al. (2021))
- **ASP model of sustainable physics capacity development combining a biennial school, teacher workshops, outreach, alumni programs and government partnership**: ASP is a biennial three-week school in fundamental and applied physics, complemented by a one-week training workshop for about seventy high school teachers and a one-week outreach for over fifteen hundred high school pupils, plus continuous alumni activities (mentorship, short-term research visits, online seminars). Its central long-term objective is "to help improve physics education in Africa and in doing so, to contribute in a significant way to the development of science and technology on this continent". Appendix A enumerates the mechanisms, including building networks between African and international researchers, cultivating appreciation of physics among teachers and learners, and promoting partnerships with African governments and policy makers. (Kétévi A. Assamagan et al. (2021))
