## Description
A 40-item three-point self-report scale developed by the researcher to assess university students' communication skills across university settings.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Researcher-built communication skills scale for university students**: A 40-item self-report scale prepared by the researcher to measure communication skills including clarity, realism, briefing, honesty, consistency, friendliness and the whole message, across university settings such as the lecture, cafeteria, library, laboratories and university areas. Items use a three-point estimation scale (always, sometimes, rarely) scored 1-3. Validity was checked by 10 specialized university professors who judged each item, and reliability was estimated by test-retest and internal consistency. (Wael Mohammad Alshurman (2017))
- **Communication skill in palliative care as a complex psychological structure of cognition, emotion, behavioral intention, empathy, and contextual communication**: The article's conceptual framework defines palliative care communication skill as an individual's evaluative, relatively stable psychological attitude expressed in specific behaviors toward particular entities. The authors state it "is not a singular psychological phenomenon but rather a complex psychological structure composed of cognition, emotions, behavioral intentions, empathy, and contextual communication." Building on two core elements of attitude and ability, the framework organizes the scale's six dimensions and was operationalized through items derived from literature review, open-ended questionnaires, and expert consultation. (Qiu Lanying et al. (2025))
