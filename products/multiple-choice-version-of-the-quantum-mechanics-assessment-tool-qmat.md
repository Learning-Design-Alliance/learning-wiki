## Description
A conceptually focused multiple-choice assessment instrument developed by Sadaghiani et al. for measuring upper-division undergraduate students’ understanding of quantum mechanics.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **A multiple-choice version of the Quantum Mechanics Assessment Tool (QMAT) for upper-division quantum mechanics**: A conceptually focused multiple-choice (MC) assessment built upon the open-ended 14-item QMAT, intended to compare outcomes of curricular approaches in upper-division quantum mechanics. The article states the long-term goal of "an instrument capable of equally assessing outcomes from modern (discrete spin half basis) and traditional approaches (continuous single particle basis)". Construction is guided by elements of classical test theory, with distractors drawn from student responses and refined via interviews and expert review. (Homeyra Sadaghiani et al. (2013))

### Claims
- [A first 28-item multiple-choice version of the QMAT given to 61 students at two institutions showed strikingly similar item difficulty across institutions and low, comparable mean scores](../claims/mc-qmat-first-version-item-difficulty-similar-institutions.md) [+M]
