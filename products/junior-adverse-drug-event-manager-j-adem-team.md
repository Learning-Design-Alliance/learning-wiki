# Junior-Adverse Drug Event Manager (J-ADEM) team

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A student-run clinical pharmacovigilance programme in which supervised medical students screen for, diagnose, report, and follow up suspected adverse drug reactions.

### Claims

- [Medical students who participated in a J-ADEM team were significantly more competent than control students in managing ADRs and correctly applying all diagnostic steps (83.3% vs. 38.5%, p < 0.001)](../claims/jadem-students-more-competent-adr-management.md) [+M]
- [A J-ADEM student team feasibly detected and reported ADRs in hospital: 65 ADR reports from 136 screened patients, with high patient satisfaction and physician endorsement](../claims/jadem-team-feasibility-outcomes.md) [+M]
- [J-ADEM participation significantly increased students' use of all four BAT-M diagnostic steps when detecting an ADR](../claims/jadem-batm-diagnostic-skills-increase.md) [+M]
- [J-ADEM participation improved student attitudes toward ADR reporting, including awareness that reporting contributes to medication safety and that it is time consuming](../claims/jadem-attitudes-toward-adr-reporting.md) [+M]
- [After J-ADEM participation, students were more likely to intend reporting serious and new ADRs, but less likely to intend reporting all ADRs, than control students](../claims/jadem-intentions-reporting-serious-new-adrs.md) [~M]
- [J-ADEM students knew more about ADR reporting obligations, pre-marketing ADR detection, and where to report than control students after the program](../claims/jadem-knowledge-gains.md) [+M]
