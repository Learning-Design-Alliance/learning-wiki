## Description
A freely available nineteen-item Likert and forced-choice survey created by David Wittman to measure general-education students' attitudes toward the process and nature of science.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Nineteen-question science-attitude survey for general education astronomy, released for free use**: A custom set of nineteen Likert and forced-choice items probing attitudes toward the process of science, written because existing instruments (CLASS, MPEX, VASS) probe attitudes toward learning more heavily than attitudes toward science and none was appropriate for a general education astronomy course. Items include "Science is largely about memorizing facts" and "We can't know what happened in the distant past because we don't have any observations of that time". The author states "a copy of the survey also appears at the end of this preprint and is av ailable for anyone to use freely". It was administered on the first and last day of class with student IDs used to match pre and post responses. (David Wittman (2010))

### Claims
