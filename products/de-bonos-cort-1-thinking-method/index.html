De Bono's CoRT 1 thinking method¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A curriculum developed by Edward de Bono that teaches structured thinking tools, including PMI, CAF, OPV, FIP, AGO, APC, and C&S, for use in educational and decision-making contexts.

Components¶

  • De Bono's CoRT 1 thinking method as a teachable set of critical thinking tools for nursing decisions: The article presents the CoRT 1 thinking method (de Bono, 1972) as a set of thinking tools developed from different aspects of thinking, grounded in de Bono's belief that "thinking skills can be taught". The seven tools are P.M.I. (Plus, Minus, Interesting), C.A.F. (Consider All Factors), O.P.V. (Other People's Views), F.I.P. (First Important Priorities), A.G.O. (Aims, Goals, Objectives), A.P.C. (Alternatives, Possibilities, Choices) and C&S (Consequences and Sequel). In this study these tools structured both the critical thinking module and the analysis of students' health talks with patients. (Abdul Ghani Kanesan Abdullah et al. (2010))
  • CoRT BREADTH ten-lesson training program: The first part of De Bono's CoRT program, delivered here as ten training sessions over four weeks at three hours per week, each lesson with a plan and a worksheet for each group. The lessons teach thinking tools applied to communication: PMI (plus, minus, interesting), CAF (consider all factors), rules, C&S (consequence and sequel), AGO (aims, goals, objectives), planning, FIP (first important priorities), APC (alternatives, possibilities, choices), decisions, and OPV (other people's views). The article describes it as "Ten consecutive sequential lessons selected from the CoRT program for teaching thinking, which includes tools, objectives, examples, exercises, discussion, and follow-up". (Wael Mohammad Alshurman (2017))
  • CoRT six-part taxonomy for teaching thinking: De Bono's CoRT (Cognitive Research Trust) program organizes the teaching of thinking into sixty lessons distributed in six parts of ten lessons each, each part handling one aspect of thinking: breadth (broadening the circle of understanding), organization (identifying problem landmarks and developing solution strategies), interaction (discussion and negotiation, evidence and agreement), creativity (escaping limiting ideas and producing new ideas), information (collecting and providing information effectively), and action (the process of thinking as a whole from choosing the goal to forming an implementation plan). The article states "The CoRT program consists of sixty lessons distributed in six parts, each of which contains ten lessons" and describes each part's purpose and lesson topics. (Wael Mohammad Alshurman (2017))
  • De Bono's account that thinking is a teachable skill developed through practice: The article's framing theory, drawn from De Bono, holds that thinking can be taught because it can be treated as a simple process, and that lateral thinking (hyperlink thinking) looks at problems from different angles instead of only one method, in contrast to straight thinking that moves through successive logical steps. Thinking means dealing with a conscious awareness of thinking patterns, whose tools should be used deliberately. The article states that "De Bono (1989) believes that practice or training leads to mastery or perfection in the development of thinking skills", comparing learning thinking skills to learning to ride a bike or swim, where initial confusion gives way to skill. (Wael Mohammad Alshurman (2017))

Claims¶

  • Student nurses applied the seven CoRT critical thinking tools when delivering health talks to patients [+W]
  • A four-week CoRT BREADTH training program was followed by statistically significant pre-to-post gains in university students' communication skills [+W]
