Five IRT-calibrated curriculum-based measures of math problem solving¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Krawec et al.'s assessment consists of five equated curriculum-based measures, each using ten elementary and middle-school mathematical word problems to measure growth in math problem-solving performance.

Components¶

  • Rasch-calibrated five-item problem-solving pretest and posttest: The study used matched five-item problem-solving measures adapted from Verschaffel et al.'s instruments, scored as number correct. Psychometric evidence included Rasch analysis: "Reliability for the pretest and posttest was high, α = 0.96 and α = 0.97, respectively," alternate-forms reliability r = 0.97, and Rasch separation of 4.81, allowing respondents to be sorted into approximately four distinct groups. Item difficulties ranged from easier to moderate difficulty in logits. (Jonathan D. Bostic et al. (2016))

Claims¶

  • TTPS intervention students improved their problem-solving performance from pretest to posttest while comparison students did not [+M]
  • After controlling for pretest, TTPS intervention students solved more problems on the posttest than comparison peers [+M]
