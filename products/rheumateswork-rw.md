# Rheumates@Work (R@W)

> **Product or Programme** · [All products and programmes](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 14-week combined internet-based and in-person cognitive-behavioral physical-activity and self-management programme for children with juvenile idiopathic arthritis, developed and delivered by the programme authors.

## Components
<!-- A programme's own frameworks, tools, indicators and instruments, as its sources describe them -->
- **Rheumates@Work: a 14-week combined internet-based and in-person cognitive behavioral program for children with JIA**: Rheumates@Work (R@W) is "a combined internet-based and in person instruction model, an interactive, educational, and cognitive behavioral program to increase physical activity in 8 to 13-year-olds with JIA". Over 14 weeks it introduces weekly topics through film, animation, puzzles, spoken text, brain twisters, and assignments, guided by a cartoon figure called Buddy, supplemented with four group sessions and one chat session. Each participant gets a personal page showing their goal, joint status on a colored skeleton figure, activity diary results, and fitness test results. Weekly themes cover health education, emotions, barriers and benefits of activity, self-efficacy, peer support, SMART goals, setbacks, and perseverance. (Armbrust Wineke et al. (2015))
- **Pender's Health Promotion Model as the theoretical basis for designing a behavioral intervention for children with JIA**: The article bases R@W on Pender's Health Promotion Model, which "explains the factors and relationships leading to the improvement of health and quality of life by means of health promoting behavior". The model stems from social cognitive theory, takes self-efficacy as its main concept, and distinguishes three groups of determinants of health-related behavior: individual characteristics and experiences, behavior-specific cognitions and affect, and situational and interpersonal influences. The authors applied these determinants to the circumstances of children living with JIA and conclude the HPM is suitable for designing a behavioral intervention program such as R@W. (Armbrust Wineke et al. (2015))
