## Description
Antonenko et al. (2010) describe the possibilities offered by "electroencephalography (EEG) to provide a continuous and objective measure of cognitive load". Unlike offline mental-effort ratings, online process measures indicate cognitive load at specific points in time, allowing hypotheses to be tested in more detail. The element identifies which EEG components can assess load and reviews EEG studies of learning from hypertext and multimedia materials.

(2010) describe the possibilities offered by "electroencephalography (EEG) to provide a continuous and objective measure of cognitive load". The element identifies which EEG components can assess load and reviews EEG studies of learning from hypertext and multimedia materials. (Tamara van Gog et al. (2010))
- **P300 amplitude as an index of mental resource allocation in dual-task situations**: The framework treats the P300 component of the event-related brain potential to stimuli in a secondary task as a marker of how many mental resources remain for that task while a primary task is performed. As the article states, "The amplitude of the P300 component has been interpreted as an index for mental resources that are available for processing of the task at hand, with reduced P300 indicating reduced mental resources". Reduced P300 to secondary-task targets after training therefore indicates fewer resources allotted to the secondary task and, by inference, more available for the primary surgical motor task. (Thomaschewski M et al. (2021))
