## Description
The article proposes dynamical systems theory and associated analysis techniques as tools for examining behavioral patterns and variations within ITSs. It treats time as a critical variable, focusing on "the complex and fluid interactions that occur within a given environment rather than treating behavior as static (i.e., set or unchanging), as is customary in many statistical approaches." - **Dynamic analyses of log data (random walks and Hurst exponents) as stealth assessment of behavioral control**: This framework holds that log data from adaptive learning environments, combined with dynamic systems analyses, can unobtrusively classify students' behavior patterns as deterministic or random while they learn. Random walks visualize each student's trajectory across categorical choices over time, while Hurst exponents quantify persistence: "0.5 < H ≤ 1 indicates persistent (deterministic or controlled) behavior, H = 0.5 signifies random behavior, and 0 ≤ H < 0.5 denotes antipersistent behavior." The article presents this as a stealth alternative to obtrusive self-report measures, which it argues fail to capture dynamic, fine-grained behavior change. (Erica L. Snow et al. (2016))
