# Mediator–moderator distinction in randomized intervention research

## Description
The article defines an organizing distinction for intervention research: a mediator explains how or why a treatment works, while a moderator reflects who benefits. As printed, "a mediator explains how or why an intervention works, whereas a moderator explains who the intervention benefits or what conditions must exist for the intervention to be effective." Mediators are intermediate outcomes measured after treatment onset; moderators typically predate the intervention. Fidelity of implementation is given as a mediator example and special education status as a moderator example.
