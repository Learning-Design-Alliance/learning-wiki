Three-role ICT interactivity coding system (object, participant, and tool)¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study developed a coding system defining classroom interactivity as "actions which are performed by teachers and students once they perceive the supported features of educational technologies". Building on Kennewell and Beauchamp's framework of 20 actions, it distributes actions into three categories by the role ICTs play: object (interact about ICTs, e.g. selecting, comparing), participant (interact with ICTs, e.g. annotating, questioning), and tool (interact through ICTs, e.g. composing, editing). Actions are scored 1-3 points by category, and efficiency ratings weighted by occurrence yield an effective score.
