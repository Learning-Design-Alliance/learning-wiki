BC-SMD framework: hierarchical-model-based, design-comparable effect size for single-case designs¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The BC-SMD is an effect size metric for single-case designs defined by first fitting a hierarchical linear model to the SCD data, then using it to consider a hypothetical between-group design with the same population, intervention, and outcome. The article states it is "theoretically comparable to a standardized mean difference from a between-group design performed with the same population, the same intervention, and the same outcome measures." The framework models within-case and between-case variation and standardizes the treatment-phase mean difference by total variance, putting results on the between-group SMD metric so SCD evidence can enter meta-analyses and evidence-based practice reviews.

Claims¶

  • The BC-SMD extends to multivariate multiple baseline designs across participants, allowing estimation of effects on several correlated outcome measures in a single model [+W]
  • The BC-SMD extends to clustered multiple baseline designs via a three-level model on unaggregated student-level data, illustrated with a mathematics intervention study of 33 students in 12 groups in 5 schools [+W]
  • BC-SMD estimates can depend on the choice of hypothetical time points A and B when baseline time trends vary across participants or differ between phases [~W]
