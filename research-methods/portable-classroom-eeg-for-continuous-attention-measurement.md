## Description
The article argues that portable EEG provides an objective, real-time index of classroom attention that behavioral observation and self-report cannot supply, because those methods "are each indirect and cannot pick up the dynamic changes in student engagement". In their study, neural measures revealed that looking-away during group work may coincide with attentional engagement, and the authors conclude EEG signals "offer a powerful tool for understanding differences in student cognitive states as a function of classroom instruction that are unobservable from behavior alone".
