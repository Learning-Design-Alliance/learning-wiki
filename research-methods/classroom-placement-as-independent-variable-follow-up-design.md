## Description
The article argues that follow-up research in early childhood special education should treat inclusive class placement as an independent variable rather than only as an outcome index. The author submits that "the current data are sufficient to occasion an important rethinking, or at least some caution, in our field about inclusive class placement as solely an outcome index in our follow-up research", because placement was driven by district policy and child progress showed no obvious relationship with placement. The matched-pairs results are the empirical basis for this recommendation.
