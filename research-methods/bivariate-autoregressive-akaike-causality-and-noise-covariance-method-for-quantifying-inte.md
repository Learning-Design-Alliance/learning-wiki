# Bivariate autoregressive Akaike-causality and noise-covariance method for quantifying interactive scaffolding

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The article proposes "a methodological framework for detecting the interactive scaffolding process in teaching behaviour using an imitation learning paradigm". Simultaneously recorded teacher and learner manual movements are modelled with a bivariate autoregressive model; Akaike causality quantifies bidirectional information transfer in each direction, and the model's noise covariance captures "the simultaneous component of their movement that was not explained by the bidirectional causality but identified as commonly shared driving information". Applied to a Tower of Hanoi demonstration-shadowing task, the framework maps lateral-motion influences to solution transfer and vertical-motion influences to timing monitoring.
