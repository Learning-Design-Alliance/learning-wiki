Behavioral choice measure of maintained situational interest¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article operationalizes maintained situational interest not by verbal self-report but behaviorally: participants decided which of six described seminar topics (Experiment 1) or which of six take-home texts (Experiment 2) they wanted to engage with, and the count of yes-responses was the measure. The authors argue this is "not a merely verbal measure" but "a direct measure of whether participants want to engage further with this topic", providing a link to future interest-related behavior. The measure correlated significantly with topic interest and triggered situational interest in both experiments.

Accounts¶

  • Behavioral measure of maintained situational interest based on choices of future seminar topics or take-home texts: The article operationalizes maintained situational interest not by verbal self-report but behaviorally: participants decided which of six described seminar topics (Experiment 1) or which of six take-home texts (Experiment 2) they wanted to engage with, and the count of yes-responses was the measure. The authors argue this is "not a merely verbal measure" but "a direct measure of whether participants want to engage further with this topic", providing a link to future interest-related behavior. The measure correlated significantly with topic interest and triggered situational interest in both experiments. (Demian Scherer et al. (2023))
  • Willingness-to-wait paradigm for situational reading enjoyment: An online task in which participants read forty synopses of unfamiliar books taken verbatim from an online book merchant, rate enjoyment on a 1–9 scale, answer one literal and one inferential comprehension question, and then choose to "wait" 3–6 s or "skip" to see the book cover. The authors present it as "a paradigm to measure situational enjoyment during reading", giving "an elegant experimental means of exploring the factors that influence our enjoyment and motivation for reading". (Amrita Bains et al. (2023))

Claims¶

  • Related decorative pictures do not increase triggered or maintained situational interest; in Experiment 2 they reduced maintained interest [+M]
  • Rated enjoyment of a synopsis predicts readers' willingness to take on a temporal cost (waiting for more book information) in both experiments [+M]
  • Higher synopsis enjoyment predicts better comprehension of that synopsis, even controlling for reading ability in Experiment 1 [+M]
