## Description
The article uses the learning curve as its central organizing measure for judging surgical training approaches: it "applies the learning curves as a measure to demonstrate their teaching ability" and compares simulation types by analyzing their learning curves. Learning curves track how operative performance (e.g., operating time, conversions, complications) changes with accumulated operative experience, allowing identification of case thresholds at which performance stabilizes. The review surmises the applicability of different surgical simulation types by comparing and analyzing learning curves across procedures such as laparoscopic hemihepatectomy, pancreatoduodenectomy, and robotic pancreatoduodenectomy.

- **Experience-group learning-curve method with an experienced-surgeon quality reference for assessing surgical trainees**: The article devises learning curves by categorizing trainee operations into "experience groups" where all trainees share the same operation count ("all first TVT operations labeled as “experience = 1,”" and so on), plotting outcomes operation by operation against the averaged results of Sweden's most experienced surgeons with 95% confidence intervals, and additionally comparing group averages for operations 1–10 and 11–50. The experienced surgeons' averaged results are labeled a "quality reference" and regarded as a desirable goal for trainees. This method handles wide intra- and interindividual scattering of individual learning curves. (Nüssler EK et al. (2021))
