Three-level hierarchical linear modeling for meta-analyzing single-case experimental design data¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article uses a three-level hierarchical linear model in which DTT implementation fidelity observations (Level 1) are nested within participants (Level 2) within studies (Level 3). Phase is dummy-coded, time is centered on the last training session, and a moderator term tests BST or individual components. The authors state "HLM is able to analyze clustered data, or observations within participants and participants within studies", allowing effect sizes to vary across participants and studies while estimating one overall intervention effect. Lag 1 autocorrelation was modeled to control Type I error and improve power.

Accounts¶

  • Three-level hierarchical linear modeling for meta-analyzing single-case experimental design data: The article uses a three-level hierarchical linear model in which DTT implementation fidelity observations (Level 1) are nested within participants (Level 2) within studies (Level 3). Phase is dummy-coded, time is centered on the last training session, and a moderator term tests BST or individual components. The authors state "HLM is able to analyze clustered data, or observations within participants and participants within studies", allowing effect sizes to vary across participants and studies while estimating one overall intervention effect. Lag 1 autocorrelation was modeled to control Type I error and improve power. (Joelle Fingerhut et al. (2022))
  • HLM-based meta-analysis of single-case design studies as a method for quantifying intervention evidence: The article develops and demonstrates a three-level hierarchical linear modeling approach to meta-analyzing single-subject design studies, where "Level 1 represents the data points, or repeated measures within persons", Level 2 represents students, and Level 3 represents studies. HLM was chosen because "HLM analyses can account for complex data structures (missing data, varying intervention lengths) likely to be found in single-case-design studies". The authors argue this method shows promise for addressing criticisms of statistical analysis of single-subject designs and supports continued inclusion of such designs in meta-analyses. (Pyle et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Behavioral skills training and other trainings raise DTT implementation fidelity from a low baseline of 40.61% to over 90% accuracy by the end of training [+M]
  • In a meta-analysis of single-case studies, participants trained with complete behavioral skills training had significantly higher DTT implementation fidelity than those trained with only one, two, or three BST components [+W]
  • DTT implementation accuracy increases by 1.33 with each additional observation during the intervention phase [+W]
  • DTT implementation fidelity declines slightly after training ends, with a statistically significant difference between intervention and maintenance slopes [+W]
  • DTT implementation fidelity declines slightly after training ends, though the decline may reflect outliers [+W]
  • Modeling as a training component shows no statistically significant effect on DTT implementation fidelity [+W]
  • Rehearsal as a training component shows no statistically significant effect on DTT implementation fidelity [+W]
  • The Daily Report Card significantly improves behavior for students with ADHD, increasing desirable behavior by approximately 30 percentage points from baseline to intervention [+M]
