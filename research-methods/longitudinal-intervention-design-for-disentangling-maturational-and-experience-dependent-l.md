# Longitudinal intervention design for disentangling maturational and experience-dependent learning

## Description
The review recommends repeatedly measuring the same individuals over time, ideally with an intervention and matched comparison, when studying learning in a brain that is simultaneously maturing. It argues that "longitudinal intervention designs are one possible method to disentangle maturational and experience-dependent processes in the brain", because concurrent behavioral and neuroimaging measures reveal whether brain change is coupled or uncoupled with behavioral change. This design lets researchers compare the magnitude of experience-dependent learning against the magnitude of developmental change.
