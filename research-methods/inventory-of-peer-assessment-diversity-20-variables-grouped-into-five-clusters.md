# Inventory of peer assessment diversity: 20 variables grouped into five clusters

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article develops a classification framework for describing peer assessment practices, replacing Topping's discrete-variable typology because 'the range of variation appeared to no longer be a list of separate values but rather a continuum, or sometimes even a multi-dimensional space'. Eight new variables were added and another eight extended with extra sub-dimensions, five original variables were absorbed into larger entities, and the 20 resulting variables were grouped into five clusters: decisions concerning use, links to the learning environment, interaction between peers, composition of assessment groups, and management of the assessment procedure.
