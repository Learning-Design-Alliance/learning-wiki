# Five control criteria framework for judging degree of control in media comparison studies

## Description
The review proposes that a media comparison is adequately controlled when five criteria are met: matched activities, matched non-IVR activities, matched practice with the dependent measure, matched time spent learning (within 20% difference), and matched content. The article states "To determine the degree to which the IVR and conventional conditions were controlled on instructional method and content in each article, five control criteria were assessed." Comparisons are then graded from fully controlled to fully not controlled, with a second approach flagging comparisons whose control is difficult to determine due to missing information.

- **Three-part equivalence framework for causal interpretability of teaching-method comparisons**: The article names and defines three conditions that must hold for differences between control and test arms to be attributable to the new teaching method: structural equivalence (comparable composition with respect to confounders such as age, gender, educational background), equivalence of treatment conditions (all students receive the same conditions apart from the new method), and equivalence of observations (situations always observed and assessed according to the same rules). Randomization and, where feasible, stratification serve structural equivalence; detailed study protocols serve treatment-condition equivalence; standardized assessment and blinded evaluators serve observational equivalence. Failure of any one produces blended effects that cannot be attributed to the method. (Dreyhaupt Jens et al. (2017))
