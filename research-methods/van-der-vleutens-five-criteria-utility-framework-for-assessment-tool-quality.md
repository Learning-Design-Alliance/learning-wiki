# Van der Vleuten's five-criteria utility framework for assessment-tool quality

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The article adopts van der Vleuten's criteria as its framing model for assessment quality, outlining "five key criteria that collectively define the utility of an assessment tool": validity (accuracy in measuring intended targets), reliability (consistency over repeated administrations), educational impact (contribution to learning outcomes), acceptability (stakeholder perceptions of fairness, transparency, relevance), and cost (financial, time, and effort resources). The study applies this lens by evaluating SBMCQ quality through item analysis and stakeholder (faculty) perceptions.

- **Five criteria of a good assessment tool in medical education: validity, reliability, acceptability, feasibility, and educational impact**: The review adopts a framework in which the usefulness of an assessment method in medical education is judged against five elements. As the article states, "validity, reliability and acceptability are considered parts of the criteria used for determining the usefulness of a particular method of assessment, together with the feasibility and educational impact those five elements are the criteria of good assessment tool." The review applies this framework by evaluating structured viva on the first three criteria against agreed medical education standards. (Abuzied Abdelhamid Ibrahim Hassan (2023))
- **Van Der Vleuten's utility index for evaluating assessment instruments**: The utility index is a widely accepted framework, described by Van Der Vleuten, for appraising an evaluation instrument. The review uses it to appraise each technical skills-assessment tool, defining validity (with content, construct, and concurrent subtypes), reliability, feasibility/acceptability, educational impact, and cost-effectiveness. The authors state it is "a widely accepted framework for assessing an evaluation instrument" and summarize evidence for each tool against these criteria. (James H.K. et al. (2020))
