Rapid cycle product efficacy assessment and feedback loop for ed-tech pilots¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A district-centric, practitioner-focused process in which university researchers help districts pilot ed-tech products, gather mixed-methods efficacy data across five dimensions (student learning, student engagement, teacher support, teacher satisfaction, administrator satisfaction), and feed synthesized findings back to developers. The team "provided feedback via phone or face-to-face meetings to engage the product developers in open and frank discussions about the positives as well as the problems teachers and students encountered using the product." Feedback led to concrete design-change proposals for some products and pilot-process improvements for others.

Accounts¶

  • Rapid cycle product efficacy assessment and feedback loop for ed-tech pilots: A district-centric, practitioner-focused process in which university researchers help districts pilot ed-tech products, gather mixed-methods efficacy data across five dimensions (student learning, student engagement, teacher support, teacher satisfaction, administrator satisfaction), and feed synthesized findings back to developers. The team "provided feedback via phone or face-to-face meetings to engage the product developers in open and frank discussions about the positives as well as the problems teachers and students encountered using the product." Feedback led to concrete design-change proposals for some products and pilot-process improvements for others. (Gadgil et al. (2016))
  • Rapid cumulative micro-RCT evaluation architecture: signal, replication, accumulation: An evaluation architecture for rapidly evolving educational AI in which practitioner-led micro-randomised controlled trials are repeated across cohorts, contexts and product versions. Its value lies "not in replacing definitive evaluation with small studies, but in enabling a rapid, cumulative evaluation architecture in which randomised estimates can be generated, replicated and updated". Three linked stages structure the model: an initial trial generates a provisional causal signal; replication tests whether direction and magnitude recur; accumulation characterises the distribution of effects across versions, pedagogy, populations and implementation. Successive estimates are represented through forest plots and cumulative meta-analysis, retaining individual estimates rather than allowing a pooled mean to obscure heterogeneity. (Wayne Harrison et al. (2026))
  • Iterative data-driven validation pattern: create, test, revise from performance and feedback, and retest until gameplay statistically matches external criteria: This design pattern structures assessment-game development as repeated cycles in which the game is created, tested with students, revised based on logged performance and user feedback, and retested until gameplay is statistically matched to independent ratings of the target skill. The article states it "utilized an iterative data-driven validation process in which the game was created, tested, revised based on student performance and feedback, and retested." Two iterative evaluations with third- and fourth-graders refined dialogue choices, verified challenge difficulty, and produced the scoring algorithms. (Melissa E. DeRosier et al. (2012))
