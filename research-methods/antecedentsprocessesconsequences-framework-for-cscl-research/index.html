Antecedents–processes–consequences framework for CSCL research¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article argues that fundamental understanding of CSCL requires simultaneously studying antecedents (student-, group-, task-, and technological characteristics), processes (descriptions and qualities of interactions and individual learning activities during collaboration), and consequences (e.g., individual achievement, group performance, perceived efficacy) of collaboration, and whether processes mediate antecedents' effects on consequences. Table 2 enumerates the antecedent elements, such as prior knowledge, group size, task complexity, and availability of collaboration scripts, scaffolds, and awareness tools.
