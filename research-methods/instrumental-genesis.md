## Description
Instrumental genesis, developed in France by Pierre Rabardel and colleagues, describes how the agent-artifact connection evolves over time through usage and re-design. The editorial argues it can more naturally be applied to the small-group collective level central to CSCL than affordances theory (individual-focused) or structuration (societal-level), introducing it to the CSCL community at methodological, technological, and theoretical levels.

- **Instrumental genesis as the theory of artifact-agent connection best suited to CSCL's small-group level**: Instrumental genesis, developed in France by Pierre Rabardel and colleagues, describes how the agent-artifact connection evolves over time through usage and re-design. The editorial argues it can more naturally be applied to the small-group collective level central to CSCL than affordances theory (individual-focused) or structuration (societal-level), introducing it to the CSCL community at methodological, technological, and theoretical levels. (Gerry Stahl (2012))
- **Instrumental Genesis as a framework for how collaborators enact design practices**: Instrumental Genesis (IG), grounded in Activity Theory, models the joint evolution of artifacts with their uses within specific collaborative environments, treating technology in collaboration as a first-class contribution. A central concept is how artifacts mediate joint activity, with the editorial highlighting four forms of mediation: Epistemic, Pragmatic, Reflexive, and Inter-personal. The framework was applied to what is termed the preparation phase in CSCL, involving students, instructors, and design researchers. (Carolyn P. Rosé et al. (2019))
