## Description
The article uses the education production function, "widely used in labor economics to understand the organization and deployment of resources in education", as its framework for the potential impact of increasing instructional time. Educational output is a function of child, family, household, and school inputs, and the function is "typically modeled as increasing in any particular x, but at a decreasing rate". The framework motivates testing whether full-day kindergarten sits at diminishing returns.
