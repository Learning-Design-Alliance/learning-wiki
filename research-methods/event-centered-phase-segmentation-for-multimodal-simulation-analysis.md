# Event-centered phase segmentation for multimodal simulation analysis

## Description
A reusable analytic pattern in which a simulation scenario is divided into three scripted segments so behaviors, regulation strategies and physiological responses can be compared across critical points. The article explains: "We used the patient’s deterioration and scripted events to guide the segmentation of data, for example, we may use the “patient’s” sudden drop in pulse or the anaphylactic shock that tightens the airway to initiate the transition from the ‘‘Beginning’’ to the ‘‘Escalation’’ of a simulation." Segments were chosen for comparable length and to identify the stakes at each point, with the 'Peak' marking the convergence of medical decision-making and execution of patient care.

- **Event-level aggregation of gaze parameters using participant-defined event segmentation as the unit of analysis**: The article proposes analyzing gaze metrics (fixation duration, saccade amplitude, pupil diameter) aggregated within participant-defined events rather than across heterogeneous timelines, drawing on Event Segmentation Theory, which "posits that we engage in segmentation in a hierarchical manner" into coarse and fine events. Students segmented their own first-person eye-tracked simulation footage into meaningful events and rated each event's outcomes, giving more units of analysis for multilevel modeling. The study is described as the first in which participants segmented their own prior experience from a first-person viewpoint. (Lee Madison Mason et al. (2025))
