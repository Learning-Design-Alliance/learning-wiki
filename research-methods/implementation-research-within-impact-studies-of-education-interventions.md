# Implementation research within impact studies of education interventions

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The guide is a practical resource for researchers who conduct implementation analyses as part of impact studies of education interventions. It states that such analyses 'can help educators know whether a tested intervention is likely to be a good fit for their own settings' and that it 'provides steps and recommendations' across specification, measurement, analysis, and reporting. It packages 'key definitions, examples, templates, and links to resources' for that purpose.

- **Evaluate curriculum programs as implemented, since implementation similarity can mask theoretical differences between programs**: When comparing curricula, program effects depend on how programs are actually enacted in classrooms, not only on their theoretical design. The Pittsburgh comparison found no achievement difference between Everyday Mathematics and Harcourt Math, and the authors suggest this "may be related to the fact that" the programs "were more similar in practice than they are in theory." Evaluations should therefore attend to implementation when interpreting program comparisons. (Catherine Nelson et al. (2007))
- **A two-level fidelity-of-implementation conceptual framework for education RCTs: teacher implementation supports and curriculum implementation**: The article proposes a conceptual framework for studying implementation within an impact study at two levels. The first level measures the transfer of program knowledge from the implementation agent (e.g., an implementation director) to teachers via training, community of practice, and coaching; the second measures teachers' delivery of the curriculum to students. The framework maps FOI components (dosage, responsiveness, adherence, quality, adaptations) onto each program element at each level, and extends to monitoring comparable practices in the control group. As the authors state, the framework "highlights the importance of studying implementation at two levels: (1) the program implementation supports for teachers, and (2) teacher implementation of the curriculum in the classroom with students." (Choles JR et al. (2022))
- **Build FOI measure sets that are comprehensive and multi-informant, triangulating observer, teacher, and implementor reports**: The article recommends that implementation measure sets within impact studies be comprehensive and triangulated. The authors set two explicit goals: first, at least one measure for each FOI component for each program element within each program level; second, "when feasible, we believe there should be more than one measure for an FOI element, and that these multiple measures should be from different reporters or sources (i.e., multi-informant)." In their study, third-party observers rated training and community of practice sessions, teachers self-reported responsiveness and quality, and the implementation director reported on coaching sessions. (Choles JR et al. (2022))
