Four-theme framework of instructional design variables for competency-based simulation research¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review inductively clusters the design variables compared across included studies into four main categories: "instructional strategies", "simulation models", "learner engagement", and "assessment models", each with named subdomains (e.g., feedback source, intensity, timing, automation; simulator type, cost, fidelity; stakes and motivation; minimum passing standards and validity). The authors recommend this framework, as presented in their Table 3, as a structure for future comparative efficacy studies of instructional design features.

Accounts¶

  • Four-theme framework of instructional design variables for competency-based simulation research: The review inductively clusters the design variables compared across included studies into four main categories: "instructional strategies", "simulation models", "learner engagement", and "assessment models", each with named subdomains (e.g., feedback source, intensity, timing, automation; simulator type, cost, fidelity; stakes and motivation; minimum passing standards and validity). The authors recommend this framework, as presented in their Table 3, as a structure for future comparative efficacy studies of instructional design features. (Vermylen JH et al. (2026))
  • Instructional design feature set for simulation training programs: The review assessed each training program against a set of instructional design features partially based on Issenberg et al: "Clinical variation, Curriculum integration, Instructor feedback, Group practice, Individualized learning, Mastery learning, Prestudy, Range in task difficulty," alongside general features such as duration and assessment method. Across the 14 studies, apart from clinical variation (present in nine studies) and prestudy (present in 10 studies), there was no dominant pattern of any other instructional feature. The set operationalizes how SBT programs can be described and compared. (Gerretsen Eveline C.F. MSc et al. (2023))

Claims¶

  • Automated versus nonautomated feedback in competency-based simulation shows a negligible, non-significant pooled difference [+M]
  • Self-regulated versus instructor-guided feedback in competency-based simulation shows a negligible, non-significant pooled difference [+M]
  • The highest-quality FB SBT studies with the most relevant outcomes shared curriculum integration and a gradual increase in task difficulty [+M]
