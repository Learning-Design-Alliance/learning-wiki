# Artifact-comparison approach for eliciting instructors' beliefs

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's underlying account is that instructors' beliefs and values are exposed when they make and justify instructional decisions about concrete artifacts rather than answer abstract questions. As the authors write, "The instructor solutions that respondents were asked to make judgments about were carefully designed to activate, in an imaginary classroom setting, the instructional decision-making that takes place in an authentic classroom" and "Through making and justifying instructional decisions, research subjects expose the beliefs and values that underlie these decisions". The approach was used first in interviews with physics faculty and then adapted for the GAIQ with TAs.
