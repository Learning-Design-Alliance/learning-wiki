# Marginal structural models with inverse probability of treatment weighting

## Description
The article adapts a counterfactual framework for time-varying treatments in which "Inverse probability of treatment (IPT) weighting is an alternative approach specifically developed to adjust for confounding by time-varying covariates." A marginal structural model specifies the logit of graduation as a function of duration-weighted exposure (the average of wave-specific quintile treatments). Weighting builds a pseudo-population in which treatment is unconfounded by measured covariates, allowing an unadjusted outcome model to yield unbiased causal effect estimates.

- **Marginal structural models with inverse probability of treatment weighting for estimating controlled direct effects in the presence of a mediator**: The article applies marginal structural models (MSMs) to estimate the controlled direct effect of temperament on child outcomes while accounting for parenting practices as a mediator. MSMs are "a model for potential outcomes rather than observed outcomes" and use "stabilized inverse probability weights" to control confounding of both the exposure-outcome and mediator-outcome associations. Setting the mediator to a uniform value (its mean) enables estimation of the direct effect not mediated by parenting, avoiding the bias that standard regression adjustment for a mediator can introduce. (Chong Shiau Yun et al. (2019))
