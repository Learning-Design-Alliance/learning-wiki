## Description
The article argues that in areas like retention, the lack of a documented regularity makes theory formulation inefficient, and that psychology advances efficiently by first documenting and accumulating quantitative regularities and only then developing theories to account for them. It states that "quantitative descriptions in a form that is as theoryneutral as possible are needed before theory can be efficiently developed." The method demonstrated is an exhaustive, non-selective search of existing literature, fitting many functions without favoring any on theoretical grounds.
