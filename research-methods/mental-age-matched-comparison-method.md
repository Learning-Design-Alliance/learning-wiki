## Description
The author argues that comparing feeble-minded children with normal children of the same chronological age reveals only differences attributable to developmental level, not to feeble-mindedness itself. Characteristics such as inability to learn, lack of attention, and incapacity for sustained effort "characterize a six-year-old child compared with a ten-year-old child." Only by matching on mental age and initial ability can genuine differences in learning capacity be detected, which is how the four groups in this study were constructed.

<!-- How each source describes or uses the method -->
- **Compare learners of different ability groups at the same mental age, not the same chronological age, to isolate differences in learning capacity**: The author argues that comparing feeble-minded children with normal children of the same chronological age reveals only differences attributable to developmental level, not to feeble-mindedness itself. Characteristics such as inability to learn, lack of attention, and incapacity for sustained effort "characterize a six-year-old child compared with a ten-year-old child." Only by matching on mental age and initial ability can genuine differences in learning capacity be detected, which is how the four groups in this study were constructed. (Woodrow (1915))
- **The matched-groups criterion: ranking test elements by their weight on hereditary brightness versus environmental training**: The article proposes "A new criterion ... which will rank tests with reference to the weight placed on hereditary brightness rather than on environmental training." It works by selecting two groups of children matched in total intelligence-test score but differing widely in chronological age, then comparing their average scores on each test element. Elements on which the Young Bright group exceeds the Old Dull group are judged to favor native intelligence; the reverse order ranks elements by dependence on training. This loads age and training factors in opposite directions, unlike the usual criteria of age-increase and coherence. (Chapman et al. (1921))
