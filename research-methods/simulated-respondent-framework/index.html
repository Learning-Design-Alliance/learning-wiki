Simulated-respondent framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The framework bins student ability into L labeled intervals (Table 2, from Foundational to Elite mastery), prompts the LLM to answer as a student of a given ability level with the correct answer supplied, and trains it with an MSE loss on next-token option probabilities against interval-averaged MCM/3PL probabilities. As the abstract states, "By learning to reproduce the systematic error patterns of students across a discrete range of abilities, the LLM implicitly captures the underlying response probabilities encoded in the 3PL and MCM curves", allowing difficulty to be approximated from predicted option probabilities.

Claims¶

  • A fine-tuned Qwen3.5-9B simulated-respondent model predicts item difficulty with Pearson correlation 0.85, exceeding MathBERT (0.68) and MetaMath (0.75) baselines [+M]
  • The simulated-respondent approach recovers the guessing parameter c at correlation 0.48, far above regression baselines, suggesting it captures genuine response behavior [+M]
  • Discrimination parameter recovery is weak (Pearson 0.31 for Qwen3.5-9B, below MathBERT's 0.35), with slope information partially lost through ability discretization [-M]
  • Simulating higher-cognitive-ability students is easier: simulation quality correlates positively with student cognitive score [~W]
  • Under a five-band discretization of difficulty, the simulated-respondent model reaches quadratic weighted kappa 0.835 versus 0.692 (MetaMath) and 0.625 (MathBERT) [+W]
