## Description
The article organizes its evaluation of VR-based teaching around "three dimensions of learning experience, i.e., (1) learning outcome, (2) students performance in classroo m tasks, and (3) quality of the experience". Learning outcome is captured by self-reports and a comprehension quiz; performance by accuracy and efficiency on Sillito-style maintenance tasks; and quality of experience by validated instruments covering engagement, satisfaction, perceived workload (NASA-TLX), immersiveness, and presence. This framing extends a practitioner-focused prior study to an educational setting.
