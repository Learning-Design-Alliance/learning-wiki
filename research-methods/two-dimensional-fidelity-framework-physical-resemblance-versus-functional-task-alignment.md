## Description
The article adopts Hamstra et al.'s stratification of simulation fidelity into two dimensions: "physical resemblance – characterized by the tactile, visual, auditory and olfactory appearance of the simulator relative to the simulation concept in mind" and "functional task alignment – the simulator's functional property in alignment with the learning objectives at hand". This distinction underpins the study's argument that low-fidelity basic manikins can achieve functional alignment (e.g. compression rhythm mastery in CPR) at low cost, while VPS supplies clinical information and haemodynamics that a high-fidelity manikin would otherwise relay.

- **Two-dimensional fidelity framework for medical simulators: physical fidelity and functional fidelity**: The article describes simulator fidelity, defined as the degree of exactness with which something is reproduced, as having two dimensions. It states that "Physical fidelity reflects the quality of the simulator to imitate physical characteristics like haptic and optical impressions" while "Functional fidelity describes the simulator's ability to demonstrate complex scenarios". The study operationalizes both dimensions in its expert rating protocol: experts rated skin, face, mouth, gingiva, tongue, glottis, vocal cords, airway, and anatomy after visual inspection, then performed airway maneuvers and rated functional fidelity. The article cites Curtis et al. for the claim that high physical fidelity is crucial for developing psychomotor skills and functional fidelity for building cognitive comprehension.
