## Description
The classical twin design exploits that "twins share either all (identical or monozygotic twins) or half (non-identical or dizygotic twins) of their genes". Comparing monozygotic and dizygotic twin correlations partitions variance into additive genetic influences (A/heritability), shared environmental influences (C), and non-shared environmental influences (E). Heritability is indicated when the monozygotic correlation exceeds the dizygotic correlation; shared environment is estimated by subtracting heritability from the monozygotic correlation, and non-shared environment by subtracting the monozygotic correlation from one.

(Hart et al. (2021))
- **Twin-sibling extension of the classical twin design separating twin-specific from sibling-shared environmental effects**: The article extends the Classical Twin Design by adding non-twin siblings, enabling decomposition of grade variance into additive genetic (a2), non-additive (d2), twin-shared (ct2), sibling-shared (cs2), and non-shared environmental (e2) components. The article states that "The incorporation of sibling data allows the separation of shared environmental effects into two parts", with ct2 common only to twins and cs2 common to twins and siblings, yielding "more accurate heritability estimates". Variance components were estimated with SEM (AMOS, FIML) on residualized school-report grades. (Eifler Eike Friederike et al. (2019))
