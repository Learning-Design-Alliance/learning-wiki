## Description
The article recommends that studies of far-transfer interventions such as chess instruction adopt what Gobet and Campitelli (2006) call the "ideal design": pre-test and post-test, random allocation, and two control groups — a do-nothing group and an active control group. The active control group is "necessary for removing the possibility of a placebo effect", since mechanisms such as instructors' motivation, motivation induced by a novel activity, and educators' expectations can otherwise produce positive results unrelated to the instruction itself.
