## Description
The article reformulates ethnic achievement gap decomposition as a mediation problem in which the school's ethnic composition mediates the relationship between student ethnicity and test scores. The total gap is split into a direct effect and an indirect effect: "the direct effect is the within-school gapβW and the indirect effect ωβ C represents how much more minority students are affected by the school contextual effect of ethnicity" given their additional exposure to minority students. The three existing decomposition approaches are shown to be mathematically equivalent to this mediation problem, and the framework is used purely as a device to derive extensions to more levels and more groups.
