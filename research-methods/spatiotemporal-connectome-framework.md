## Description
The spatiotemporal connectome is an analytical framework that characterizes resting-state functional connectivity with a structural prior: neural activity is chunked into time windows, connectivity matrices (506 × 506 regions) constrained by structural connectivity are translated into connected components (CCs), and CCs are classified by the eight functional systems of Yeo's atlas plus the cerebellar system. From these, two group-level metrics are derived: "system diversity (SD) and spatiotemporal diversity (STD)", where SD informs about "integration and segregation" and STD about "temporal stability". The article uses these metrics to compare Montessori- and traditionally schooled students at global and functional-system scales.
