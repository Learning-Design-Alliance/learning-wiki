## Description
The article sketches a theoretical framework in which student meaning-making during problem solving is modeled as coordinating semiotic resources across modes. The framework holds that "the social semiotic approach focuses on all types of meaning-making practices that are accomplished through different semiotic modes" including visual, verbal, written and gestural modes. Each resource couples a conceptual idea with a representation mode and carries a disciplinary affordance; coordinated resources build compound representations used to solve problems.
