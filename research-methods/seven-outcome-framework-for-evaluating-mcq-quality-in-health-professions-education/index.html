Seven-outcome framework for evaluating MCQ quality in health professions education¶

Description¶

The article identifies "seven pre-defined outcomes" for assessing MCQ quality: "question relevance, question clarity, distractor quality, question accuracy/correctness, item difficulty analysis, and item discrimination analysis (point biserial correlation and item discrimination index)". The item difficulty analysis assesses the proportion of examinees who respond correctly to a given item, whereas the item discrimination index reflects how well a given item distinguishes between high- and low-scoring students. This framework provides a structured approach for evaluating generated examination questions.

Claims¶

  • Faculty-generated SBMCQs showed superior discrimination indices and distractor effectiveness compared with ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs [+W]
  • Most LLM-generated MCQs from video transcripts met established quality criteria, with 84.05% rated clear, 86.38% containing plausible distractors, and 93.39% directly answerable [+W]
  • 47.08% of the 242 correct MCQs met all evaluated criteria and only four items exhibited three or more flaws [+W]
  • ChatGPT 3.5 performs worse than ChatGPT 4 and Llama 2 on question clarity and distractor quality [+W]
  • Llama 2 generates MCQs of worse quality than humans on question clarity and distractor quality [+W]
  • Faculty-generated SBMCQs met all three ideal item-analysis criteria more often than ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs (19 vs. 9 of 40) [+W]
