# Three-level hierarchical linear modeling for meta-analyzing single-case experimental design data

## Description
The article uses a three-level hierarchical linear model in which DTT implementation fidelity observations (Level 1) are nested within participants (Level 2) within studies (Level 3). Phase is dummy-coded, time is centered on the last training session, and a moderator term tests BST or individual components. The authors state "HLM is able to analyze clustered data, or observations within participants and participants within studies", allowing effect sizes to vary across participants and studies while estimating one overall intervention effect. Lag 1 autocorrelation was modeled to control Type I error and improve power.

- **HLM-based meta-analysis of single-case design studies as a method for quantifying intervention evidence**: The article develops and demonstrates a three-level hierarchical linear modeling approach to meta-analyzing single-subject design studies, where "Level 1 represents the data points, or repeated measures within persons", Level 2 represents students, and Level 3 represents studies. HLM was chosen because "HLM analyses can account for complex data structures (missing data, varying intervention lengths) likely to be found in single-case-design studies". The authors argue this method shows promise for addressing criticisms of statistical analysis of single-subject designs and supports continued inclusion of such designs in meta-analyses. (Pyle et al.
