Taxonomy of teacher intervention types during “making rounds” in computer-based collaborative learning¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper organizes its analysis into distinct types of teacher intervention during rounds: ratifying pupils' work, reminding pupils of neglected task aspects, making suggestions, maintaining classroom control, and making whole-class announcements. Through "a close analysis of different types of teacher interventions into pupils working in pairs with a storyboarding tool", each type is illustrated with transcribed video fragments. The taxonomy describes what teachers actually do, not what they are supposed to do.

Claims¶

  • During collaborative learning with computers, teachers' concerns of classroom management and pedagogy are typically intertwined [+M]
  • Tensions between teachers' and pupils' perspectives during collaborative learning do not take the form of antagonistic struggles [+M]
  • When correcting pupil work, the teacher revoices rather than dismisses pupils' solutions, challenging them toward more sophisticated ones [+W]
  • Making rounds minimizes off-task behaviour because pupils monitor for the teacher's approach and adjust their behaviour [+W]
