## Description
The review recommends that family medicine education research be explicitly theoretically informed, arguing that articulation of underlying assumptions is needed for researchers to critically engage with study findings. The authors state: "research should be explicitly theoretically informed; this can be achieved by engaging scholars with diverse areas of theoretical expertise." The principle addresses the observed lack of stated research paradigms and epistemological assumptions in the field.

- **Explicitly ground education research in theoretical frameworks**: The review recommends that family medicine education research be explicitly theoretically informed, arguing that articulation of underlying assumptions is needed for researchers to critically engage with study findings. The authors state: "research should be explicitly theoretically informed; this can be achieved by engaging scholars with diverse areas of theoretical expertise." The principle addresses the observed lack of stated research paradigms and epistemological assumptions in the field. (Webster F et al. (2015))
- **Research worldviews in HPE: ontology, epistemology, methodology, axiology, and four prominent worldviews with historically associated methods**: The article defines worldviews through Varpio et al.'s dimensions: ontology (nature of reality), epistemology (what can be known), methodology (how knowledge is accessed), and axiology (research values). It then organizes four prominent worldviews—"positivism/post‐positivism, interpretivism, critical inquiry, and pragmatism"—each with key assumptions and historically associated methodological approaches (quantitative designs and psychometrics for positivism; case studies, ethnography, interviews for interpretivism; mixed-methods typologies for pragmatism; co-production and decolonising methodologies for critical approaches), illustrated in Table 1 and Figure 1. (Lazarus Michelle D. et al. (2025))
- **Researchers should explicitly state which worldviews they engaged and at which stage of the research process, in all psychometric studies**: The article calls for researchers to be "upfront and transparent about their worldviews and research approaches in all (not just some) psychometric studies," also acknowledging the qualitative studies upon which scales were built. The rationale is that worldviews have historically been implied as tied to positivism, that worldview–method tension can cause misinterpretation or dismissal of others' work, and that explicit statements reduce assumptions about the research process. The authors also urge readers to "refrain from placing a value judgment on the quality and rigor of research simply based on the worldview applied." (Lazarus Michelle D. et al. (2025))
