McNay's four-type organisational culture taxonomy combined with Structured/Unstructured e-learning policy types as an analytic framework¶

Research Method · All research methods

Description¶

The study frames institutional elearning contexts using two axes: an elearning policy typology (Structured versus Unstructured, defined by policy document, formal support unit and institution-wide LMS) and McNay's (1995) taxonomy of organisational cultures. The article describes the four cultural types: the collegium has "loose institutional policy definition, informal networks and decision arenas, and innovation at the level of the individual or department"; the bureaucracy has loose policy but strong regulation; the corporation has tight policy and tight implementation with top-down directives; the enterprise has a well-defined policy framework with students as clients. Institutions are classified by combining both axes.

Claims¶

  • Institutional elearning policy is associated with more courses using ICTs, with Structured Corporate institutions reporting the highest course use and the Unstructured Bureaucratic institution the lowest [+M]
  • Variety of ICT use shows a reversed pattern: students in Structured Corporate institutions report the most varied use, but staff variety is highest in the Unstructured Collegium institution [+M]
