# Spikiness as a quantified contrast between eureka learning and gradual learning in tutor logs

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Spikiness is a reusable analytical pattern for distinguishing sudden from gradual learning within tutoring data. It is "defined as the maximum value of P(J) for a student/KC pair, divided by the average value of P(J) for that same student/KC pair", bounded between 1 (minimum spikiness) and positive infinity. Plotting P(J) over practice opportunities yields 'spiky' graphs with sudden peaks, indicating eureka learning, versus flatter graphs indicating gradual learning. The pattern was applied to knowledge-component-level and student-level analyses in both tutors.
