Three-category design taxonomy for GenAI-supported virtual patients¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review organizes the design choices of GenAI-supported virtual patients into three categories: "The designs of GenAI-supported virtual patients can be classified into three distinct categories: (1) input, (2) output, and (3) avatar." Input modalities were text (11 of 15 studies), voice (5), or hybrid (1); output corresponded closely to input mechanisms; and avatar design distinguished 6 studies using 3D-embodied virtual patients from 9 using nonembodied interfaces. Accounts¶

  • Three-category design taxonomy for GenAI-supported virtual patients: input, output, and avatar: The review organizes the design choices of GenAI-supported virtual patients into three categories: "The designs of GenAI-supported virtual patients can be classified into three distinct categories: (1) input, (2) output, and (3) avatar." Input modalities were text (11 of 15 studies), voice (5), or hybrid (1); output corresponded closely to input mechanisms; and avatar design distinguished 6 studies using 3D-embodied virtual patients from 9 using nonembodied interfaces. The review uses this taxonomy in Table 2 to characterize the field and support its design-implementation research question. (Jiang Juming et al. (2026))
  • Classification of virtual patient training interventions into virtual standardized patient, interactive patient scenario, and virtual patient game designs: The review applies an updated classification of virtual patient interventions, based on Kononowicz et al. developed from Talbot et al.'s model, to categorize the VR training designs in the included studies. It distinguishes three designs: the virtual standardized patient, "a conversational agent that simulates clinical presentations for training purposes"; the interactive patient scenario, which "primarily uses text-based multimedia, enhanced with images and case histories"; and the virtual patient game using 3D avatars for clinical reasoning and team training. Four studies used VSPs, two IPS, and two VP games. (Sten CW et al. (2024))
