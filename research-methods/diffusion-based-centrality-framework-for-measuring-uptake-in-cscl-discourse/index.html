Diffusion-based centrality framework for measuring uptake in CSCL discourse¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A framework representing threaded CSCL discussion as a directed post-reply network in which centrality measures operationalize the uptake of ideas and stimulation of further discussion. It combines diffusion-specific measures — "Maximum Neighborhood Component (MNC) is the cardinality of connected neighboring nodes excluding the target node", coreness ("similar to H-index"), cross clique connectivity, and diffusion degree — with traditional degree, closeness, betweenness and eigenvector centralities, positioning uptake capability as a function of position, connectedness and embeddedness in the network.

Claims¶

  • A worked network example shows diffusion-based centralities capturing uptake and argumentation more accurately than traditional centrality measures [+W]
  • Diffusion-based centralities show moderate-to-strong statistically significant positive correlations with the frequency of information sharing and argumentation utterances in CSCL [+M]
  • Diffusion-based centralities elucidate information exchange and uptake in large-scale collaboration, validated against achievement and qualitative analysis, according to the editors' account of Saqr and López-Pernas's study [+W]
