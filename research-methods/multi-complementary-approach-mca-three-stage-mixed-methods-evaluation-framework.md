## Description
The MCA is the article's own methodological framework for combining quantitative and qualitative evidence under a single roof, grounded in constructivist learning theory and the Gestalt holistic approach. It "consists of three basic stages, which allow access to pre-complementary, post-complementary and complementary information": a literature stage (meta-analysis plus meta-thematic analysis), an application stage (an experimental implementation plus participant interviews), and a synthesis stage combining both. The authors present MCA as distinct from standard mixed-methods approaches because single data sources are deemed insufficient and results are gathered "under a single roof".

- **Data triangulation of quantitative and qualitative sources for evaluating training where clinical outcome data are unreliable**: The article presents data triangulation as an evaluation approach that synthesizes multiple data sources — video-coded simulations, real-time live delivery observations, and semi-structured interviews — to generate a better-informed evaluation. It argues this "offers a powerful tool for guiding this process" in settings such as Bihar where birth registries are unreliable. The authors apply it by presenting a triangulated discussion of simulation data, live delivery data, and mentor-identified barriers for each key resuscitation skill. (Vail Brennan et al. (2018))
