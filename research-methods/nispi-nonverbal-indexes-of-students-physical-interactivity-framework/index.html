NISPI (Nonverbal Indexes of Students' Physical Interactivity) framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The NISPI (Nonverbal Indexes of Students' Physical Interactivity) framework codes students' states in 30-second windows as passive (0), semi-active (1) or active (2) based on hand activity with objects and head direction, then derives group-level indexes for synchrony, individual accountability, equality and intra-individual variability. The article states the coding scheme "is simple enough to be automated with existing learning analytics systems" and comprehensive enough to interpret key CPS aspects. It was applied to high school and university groups in practice-based learning and validated against human observers' CPS rankings.

Claims¶

  • Groups judged high in CPS competence by human observers show higher percentages of active synchrony (222 states) than low-competence groups in practice-based learning [+M]
  • High CPS groups show lower intra-individual variability of physical interactivity than low CPS groups [+M]
  • High CPS groups have higher and more similar member mean physical interactivity scores than low CPS groups [+M]
  • Individual accountability measured via the NISPI coding scheme alone is less valuable for predicting CPS competence, with high index values in groups not rated high [~M]
  • Synchrony, equality and intra-individual variability are good predictors of CPS competence, while individual accountability measured alone is less valuable [+W]
