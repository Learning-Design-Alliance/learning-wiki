## Description
The article argues that the correlation rP with posttest knowledge and the correlations rG (rAG, rNG) with knowledge gains are non-redundant and carry different meanings, so researchers should ensure that "these correlations should always be reported and interpreted together." Reporting only rP invites misinterpreting stability of individual differences as evidence that prior knowledge drove learning, since rP can be high even when nobody learned anything.
