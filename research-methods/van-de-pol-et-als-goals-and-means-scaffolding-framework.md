## Description
The review adopts Van de Pol et al.'s (2010) framework, which distinguishes scaffolding goals from scaffolding means and specifies characteristics of scaffolding, as its analytic lens. It uses this framework "to synthesize the various forms that scaffolding of language takes in the teaching practice of subject teachers teaching in bilingual secondary education contexts". The framework structures the review's findings into characteristics, means and goals of language scaffolding.

- **Four-facet taxonomy of (non)adaptive teacher support**: The article adopts Van de Pol et al.'s distinction between four facets of (non)adaptive support, operationalized in the QTSA. "Adaptive support means increasing teacher regulation upon low student understanding" (A+) and decreasing regulation upon high understanding (A-), while non-adaptive behavior is increasing regulation upon higher understanding (NA+) or decreasing regulation upon lower understanding (NA-). Providing adaptive support requires accurately judging students' understanding and adjusting regulation accordingly, which is difficult because teachers often overestimate students' understanding. (Korteland et al. (2024))
