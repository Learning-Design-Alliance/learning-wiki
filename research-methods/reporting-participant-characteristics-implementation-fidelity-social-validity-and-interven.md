## Description
The article recommends that intervention research with students with or at-risk of EBD consistently report participant demographics, fidelity of implementation, social validity, and intervention duration. It argues these data are needed "to help identify for whom and under what conditions interventions are most effective" and to ensure practices are acceptable and consistently used. Few studies across the included reviews reported these variables.

It argues these data are needed "to help identify for whom and under what conditions interventions are most effective" and to ensure practices are acceptable and consistently used. - **AL2 studies must substantiate pre-treatment competence level of participants according to an explicit evaluation metric**: The authors recommend that AL2 studies substantiate the pre-treatment competence level of participants according to some explicit evaluation metric. They argue that "it does not suffice to simply refer to learners as 'advanced' when well-established external criteria exist, such as the ACTFL and CEFR scales." This principle addresses the pervasive design flaw of unsubstantiated proficiency claims identified across the reviewed studies. (Burston et al. (2018))
- **Report research populations explicitly, including preparation and demographic details, so adopters can assess generalizability**: The article recommends that the PER community improve its characterization of research subjects by being more explicit about who the populations are, including details thought to influence results. Currently, descriptions are often vague, for example a study said to be conducted at a large Midwestern university, sometimes without even naming the courses. Without sufficient information about the research population, potential adopters of materials cannot assess whether similar results would be expected with their own students. (Stephen Kanim et al.
