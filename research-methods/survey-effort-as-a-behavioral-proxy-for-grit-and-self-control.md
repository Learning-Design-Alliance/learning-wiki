# Survey effort as a behavioral proxy for grit and self-control

## Description
The article advances a framework in which effort students exert on surveys serves as a behavioral proxy for grit and self-control, because "surveys administered in classrooms can feel like schoolwork or homework". It operationalizes this through two measures: item non-response, the percentage of answerable questions left blank, and careless answering, captured by "residuals from each of these regression models to capture the extent to which the response to a particular item is unpredictable" within reliable scales. The framework holds that these proxies supplement or replace biased self-reports and can be computed from previously collected survey data lacking direct trait measures.
