# Four-level research validation classification for assessment instruments: gold, silver, bronze, and research-based

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The article defines a research validation classification based on how many of its seven research validation categories apply to an assessment. "RBAIs will have a gold level validation when they have been rigorously developed and recognized by a wider research community. Silver-level RBAIs are well-validated, but only by the developers and not the larger community." Bronze-level assessments have some validation but missing pieces, and research-based validation means an assessment is likely in early stages. The classification is grounded in "the steps to developing a good research-based assessment."

- **PhysPort research-validation categories and gold/silver/bronze levels for research-based assessments**: PhysPort evaluates the level of research validation for an assessment using seven categories: questions based on research into student thinking, studied with student interviews, studied with expert review, appropriate use of statistical analysis, administered at multiple institutions, research published by someone other than developers, and at least one peer-reviewed publication. Assessments meeting all seven categories receive gold-level validation; those with 5-6 categories are silver, 3-4 are bronze, and 1-2 are research-based validation, indicating early-stage instruments. (Adrian Madsen et al. (2019))
- **Research-validation level taxonomy for assessment instruments: gold, silver, bronze, and research-based**: The article organizes assessment instruments by how rigorously they were developed and validated, using seven categories (e.g., questions based on research into student thinking, student interviews, expert review, multi-institution use, and peer-reviewed publication by someone other than developers). Validation levels are assigned by category count: "RBAIs will have a gold level validation when they have been rigorously developed and recognized by a wider research community." Silver RBAIs are well-validated but missing some piece, bronze assessments have partial validation, and the lowest level indicates instruments still in early stages. A parallel set of categories applies to observation protocols. (Adrian Madsen et al. (2017))
