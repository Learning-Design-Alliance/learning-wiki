Thinking-aloud protocol scoring system for math metacognitive skillfulness¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An observational coding system in which thinking-aloud protocols are analyzed for spontaneous metacognitive activities during math problem solving. "Metacognitive skillfulness was divided into four subscales: orientation (O), planning and systematical orderliness (P), evaluation (Ev), and elaboration (El)", with math-specific and general example activities for each (e.g., goal setting, making a sketch, checking an answer by recalculating). Scoring is two-step: utterances are coded per subscale for frequency, and each subscale receives a qualitative score on a five-point scale (0–4), with judges avoiding confounding scores with correctness of content. Interrater reliability was 0.95 and 0.98 for qualitative scores.

Claims¶

  • The think-aloud measure predicts word problem solving performance, explaining 33 percent of variance [+W]
  • A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported [+W]
  • Several sophisticated metacognitive regulation activities showed little to no variance among grade 5 students during think-aloud problem solving [+W]
