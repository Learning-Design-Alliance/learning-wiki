## Description
Bayesian Networks are presented as both a specification language and a computational mechanism with practical value in designing group and learning support systems: they have "been frequently used to build student models (Millán et al., 2010) and for assessment (Almond et al., 2015)". The article argues the technique can also serve to formalise collaborative learning theories, and notes its methodological basics can be learned in a couple of weeks with support from many software tools including several R packages — comparatively low threshold and high ceiling.

- **Belief networks as a framework for representing and reasoning about uncertainty in student modelling**: The article presents belief networks as a way to represent and reason about uncertainty, which it identifies as a significant factor in modelling students. The framework structures student models and allows a system to use a systematic approach when gathering information about the scope of the student's knowledge. It serves as the organizing framework for the article's discussion of student modelling. (Jim Reye (2004))
- **Bayesian network parameter learning for educational data mining of gaming and help utility**: The article extends Bayesian network parameter learning—following Beck et al. (2008) and the BNT-SM toolkit—to educational data mining, applying it to a novel data set and to analyzing the impact of gaming and help types. Bayesian networks are described as established in user modeling for representing and reasoning about user states, but their EDM application is recent, though it "shows high promise: DBN parameter mining obtained comparable results to other, arguably more established, techniques". The authors used observable binary nodes for student class, problem class, and gamed turns. (Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010))
