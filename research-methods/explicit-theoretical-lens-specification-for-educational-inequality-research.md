## Description
Because disciplines frame inequality differently—some problematizing unequal outcomes, others unequal treatment or unequal provision—researchers should make their framing explicit. The article states "the need to explicate the lens through which the problem of inequality in education is studied," since answering "what needs to be equal to speak of equality" makes clear whether unequal treatment and/or outcomes are problematized or strived for.

The article states "the need to explicate the lens through which the problem of inequality in education is studied," since answering "what needs to be equal to speak of equality" makes clear whether unequal treatment and/or outcomes are problematized or strived for. (Louise Elffers et al. (2024))
- **Wright's three theoretical approaches to social inequality as a lens on neighbourhood effects research**: The article uses Wright's typology of three theoretical approaches within the sociology of class, social mobility and inequality to explain why neighbourhood effects research overlooks affluence. The individual-attributes approach focuses on meritocratic resources shaping life chances; the Weberian opportunity hoarding approach studies how higher strata protect access to prestigious positions via economic, cultural and social capital and exclusion mechanisms; and the Marxist approach analyses domination and exploitation. The article argues quantitative neighbourhood effects research mixes the first two, and that Bourdieu's social and cultural capital concepts implicitly embody opportunity hoarding, so studying poverty in isolation disregards affluent social actors. (Troost et al. (2023))
