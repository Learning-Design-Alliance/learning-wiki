# Taxonomy of hedge classes in peer tutoring: Subjectivizers, Apologizers, Propositional hedges, and Extenders

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes hedges in peer-tutoring dialogue into annotated classes derived from Rowland (2007) and aligned with Fraser (2010) and Prince et al. (1982): Subjectivizers are "Words that reduce intensity or certainty", Apologizers are "Apologies used to soften direct speech acts", Propositional hedges are "Qualifying words to reduce intensity or certainty of utterances", and Extenders indicate "uncertainty by referring to vague categories". In the corpus the Extenders class was merged into Propositional hedges due to inconsistent annotation, yielding four final classes: Propositional Hedges, Apologizers, Subjectivizers, and Not hedged.
