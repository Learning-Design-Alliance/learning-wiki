Five-pattern taxonomy of AI-use learning behavior¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes a framework characterizing how students interact with an AI assistant during assignment work, distinguishing limited engagement from "proactive and critical engagement." Annotators labeled chat events for prior attempt, request purpose, and answer modification, then aggregated to student-level patterns: "Abstention denotes completing the assignment without using the assistant despite having GPT access. Rote-adoption denotes direct use of AI-generated answers with minimal independent reasoning. Active-trial denotes independent attempts before consulting the assistant." Verification and error-correction capture checking and revising AI outputs. The framework organizes the study's behavioral annotation, regression, and mediation-style analyses.

Claims¶

  • Among GPT-assisted students, proactive and critical engagement patterns are associated with higher exam scores while limited engagement shows little benefit relative to control [+M]
  • Students from higher-ranked universities and with stronger prior knowledge are more likely to adopt proactive and critical AI engagement [+M]
