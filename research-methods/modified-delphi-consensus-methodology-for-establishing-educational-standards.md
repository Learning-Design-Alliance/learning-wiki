## Description
The article employs a modified Delphi consensus approach, described as a systematic method that has demonstrated robust reliability in establishing educational standards across surgical specialties through structured expert consultation. The protocol uses a sequential multi-phase design: a preliminary competency framework, a first round with categorical maintain/remove/modify assessment, and a second round with binary agree/disagree responses. "Participant selection is one of the main sources of bias, since the technique relies heavily on the expertise of the panel."

- **Two-round modified Delphi procedure with consensus meeting for curriculum development**: The study's curriculum-development method: a three-stage modified Delphi in which a literature review drafts competencies, two email-based questionnaire rounds refine them, and an in-person consensus meeting finalizes items. Round 1 comprised "12 closed-ended questions and eight open-ended questions, organized into four categories, six items, and eight sub-items"; round 2 expanded to 13 sub-items. Items were rated on a five-point Likert scale, and consensus was assessed with the Consensus Deviation Index (CDI), where "A smaller CDI value indicates a higher level of consensus among experts." (Lin Chia-Hui et al. (2026))
- **Three-round modified Delphi e-survey quantifying EES difficulty with an international expert panel**: The study artifact is a three-round modified Delphi e-survey. Round#1 had a 5-physician working group list basic skills and procedures; round#2 had panelists rank the 23 basic skills from least to most difficult; round#3 had panelists review the ranking and rate the 26 procedures' difficulty on a 6-point Likert scale. Of 59 experts identified, "15 (25%) experts from 5 different countries participated in the round#2 of the Delphi method", with respondents kept anonymous to each other and consensus defined at ≥80% agreement. (Favier V et al. (2025))
- **CAMES Needs Assessment Formula applied through a modified Delphi process for prioritising procedures for simulation-based training**: The Copenhagen Academy for Medical Education and Simulation (CAMES) Needs Assessment Formula (NAF) is a consensus methodology the article describes as a "modified Delphi method" consisting of three iterative survey rounds. It quantifies procedure importance across four equally weighted domains (Doctors, Frequency, Risk, Feasibility), each given 25%, producing an overall score that generates a prioritised procedure list. Domain scores are obtained on 5-point scales, with a steering committee quantifying feasibility factors of cost, availability and suitability. The article states this approach "is well established" and aligns with comparable studies in the literature. (Roche Adam F. et al. (2025))
- **Modified Delphi methodology as a structured group-consensus technique for building expert agreement on assessment content**: The article defines the Delphi technique as "an iterative structured group communication method to solicit expert opinion about new or complex problems, conducted through a series of questionnaires (typically three to four rounds) with controlled feedback each round". Anonymity, controlled feedback and statistical summarization are presented as features that reduce group-consensus biases and let panelists revise judgements across iterations. The authors apply it in two stages: an internal institutional panel to draft the RARP inventory, and an external expert panel to evaluate and edit it. (Morris Christopher et al. (2017))
