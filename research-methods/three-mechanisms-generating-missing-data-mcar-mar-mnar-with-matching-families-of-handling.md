## Description
The article presents a taxonomy of three mechanisms by which missing data arise in longitudinal research. MCAR holds when "the cases for which the data are missing can be thought of as a random sample of all the cases"; MAR ties missingness to observed study variables; MNAR ties it to unobserved values such as change over time. Each mechanism maps to techniques: listwise deletion for MCAR; maximum likelihood, multiple imputation and their auxiliary-variable variants for MAR; and pattern mixture and selection models for MNAR. The tutorial uses this framework to organise its sensitivity analysis of learning-strategy growth.

- **Rubin's three-classification taxonomy of missingness mechanisms: MCAR, MAR, and MNAR**: The article presents Rubin's classification of missingness mechanisms. As printed: "Missing completely at random (MCAR): all of the cases have the same probability of being missing", while MAR means missingness is independent of the missing value but conditionally dependent on other observed variables, and MNAR means missingness depends on both observed and unobserved data. The article argues concept-inventory data with grade-skewed participation is MAR when grades explain the missingness, illustrated by splitting simulated data by grade so collected and missing distributions match within each grade. Simulation studies allow researchers to build datasets whose missingness mechanism is known by design. (Jayson Nissen et al. (2019))
