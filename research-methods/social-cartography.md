The authors describe it as 'a methodology which attends to the spatial dispersion of ideas in such a way that their value and power relations are made explicit.' # Social cartography as a postmodern methodology for excavating dispersed knowledge in educational policy discourse

## Description
Social cartography is a methodology, grounded in an anti-foundational perspective and Foucault's notion of spatial dispersion, for representing the interrelations of discourse communities in educational policy studies. The authors describe it as 'a methodology which attends to the spatial dispersion of ideas in such a way that their value and power relations are made explicit.' Maps are heuristic devices that 'discover intellectual communities and relationships, illustrate domains, suggest a field of interactive ideas,' and remain open to revision by readers. The method is demonstrated through mapping environmental education-related discourse.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A discourse field with multiple competing perspectives (mininarratives) to be located spatially; texts whose truth and value advocacies can be named and positioned
- Willingness of mappers and readers to treat every map as provisional and open to continuous revision and interrogation
#### Constraints
- The authors state the mapping is conceptual, not phenomenographic, and thus 'simply indicate interrelations of the larger discourse itself' without greater breadth and depth of research
- Mapping ideas on a flat, two-dimensional surface is acknowledged as problematic; readers called for a third dimension the first author could not yet formulate

### Target Learners
- comparative educators and educational theorists
- educational policy studies researchers

### Target Learning Objectives
- making explicit the truth and value positions that implicitly inform pedagogical and policy choices
- understanding discourse communities and their interrelations
