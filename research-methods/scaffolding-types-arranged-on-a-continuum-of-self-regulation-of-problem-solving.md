## Description
The article proposes ordering scaffolding techniques by the degrees of freedom they leave the learner: "we suggest locating the scaffolding types on different positions on a scale of self-regulation of problem-solving, with examples located from a rather low level followed by role assignments and prompts with increasing levels of self-regulation potential." Reflection phases and unscaffolded problem-solving sit at the high end. The continuum builds on scaffolding as a temporary shift of control from learner to teacher or advanced peer, and it underpins the meta-analysis's moderator analysis of which scaffolding type fits which knowledge level.
