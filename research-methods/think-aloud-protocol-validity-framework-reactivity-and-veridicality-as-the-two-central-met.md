# Think-aloud protocol validity framework: reactivity and veridicality as the two central methodological concerns

## Description
The review organizes methodological concerns about think-aloud protocols around two constructs. Reactivity "concerns whether reporting cognitive/metacognitive processes alters the processes being reported (e.g., reading process) and/or its outcomes (e.g., comprehension or remembering of reading texts)", while veridicality "concerns whether the cognitive/metacognitive processes can be accurately and completely reported". The framework explains why design choices like training and prompting involve trade-offs: solutions to one concern may create risks for the other, a dilemma the authors highlight for self-regulated reading research.

- **Think-aloud methodology as a tool for identifying myside bias and the conditions that facilitate or impede its change**: The article proposes the think-aloud methodology, in which participants read sentence-by-sentence flashcards and verbalize thoughts and feelings, as a way to assess myside bias during reading rather than only in argument generation or evaluation tasks. It argues the method captures not just final judgments but the reasoning behind them, and that "it uncovers when and under which conditions myside bias occurs, changes, or fails to change." The study applied it pre- and post-intervention with authentic biased historical texts. (Iordanou et al. (2025))
