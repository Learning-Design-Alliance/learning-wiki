# Explicit vs. implicit interventions as a two-branch taxonomy of school-based creativity support

## Description
The review organizes school-based creativity support into two branches. Explicit interventions are 'intentionally designed programs or instructional units aimed at developing creativity as a specific learning objective', typically structured, time-bound, and measurable. Implicit interventions are 'not framed as direct creativity training but are embedded in everyday classroom practices and broader school environments', operating through pedagogical strategies and contextual conditions. The taxonomy builds on Jeffrey and Craft's distinction between teaching for creativity and creative teaching, and structures the review's three research questions.
