# Two-minute directional on-/off-topic interaction video-coding procedure

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors developed a coding procedure in which two-minute clips are the unit of analysis, coded in BORIS software. Each interaction is coded once per clip as directional, with an identified source and targets, and classified as on-topic (related to the activity, the program, or STEM education) or off-topic (unrelated to education). A detailed codebook described "what counted as an interaction and how it might vary for hearing and DHH individuals". Inter-rater reliability testing with three coders on seven random clips improved agreement from 88% to 98% after revising code definitions.

- **Video-coded interaction typology for building classroom social networks (on-task / on-topic / off-topic)**: A method for generating classroom social networks from video rather than surveys: interactions (spoken or ASL) are coded in 100-second bins using BORIS event-logging software, with directed ties from source (speaker/signer) to targets (listeners). Three tie types are coded: "on-task, which are speciﬁcally about the given assignment; on-topic, which are about STEM and the program more generally; andoﬀ-topic, which includes all other interactions". Bin grain size was chosen iteratively so network graphs are neither empty nor saturated, and 20% of video was double-coded with inter-rater reliability above 80%. (C. A. Hass et al. (2018))
