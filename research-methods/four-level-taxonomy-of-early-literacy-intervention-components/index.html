Four-level taxonomy of early literacy intervention components¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A standard-nomenclature taxonomy developed for IES with input from early literacy experts and intervention developers, piloted by the synthesis team to code what interventions implemented. "The component taxonomy defines four levels of intervention components," with "component type" at the highest level identifying "the broad strategy used by the intervention"; five component types (instructional practices, non-academic supports, organizational structures, educator supports, and assessment and placement) contain "the 15 component domains", which break down into 81 components and additional component approaches. The team coded presence or absence of components and aggregated to the domain level for the meta-analysis.

Claims¶

  • Intervention components examined in the synthesis had a limited role in explaining variation in intervention effects on alphabetics outcomes [~M]
  • Component-type implementation varied widely across early literacy interventions: instructional practices were universal (100 percent) while non-academic student supports were least common (38 percent) [+W]
  • Early literacy interventions in the synthesis were mostly multifaceted, delivered to small groups or individuals, and often required personnel beyond classroom teachers [+W]
  • Components related to using student assessment data to drive instructional decisions, including grouping students, are positively associated with early literacy intervention effects on alphabetics outcomes [+W]
  • Non-academic student supports, including social-emotional learning strategies and parent and family outreach, are positively associated with early literacy intervention effects on alphabetics outcomes [+W]
