Thick Authenticity framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study operationalizes Shaffer and Resnick's (1999) 'thick authenticity' framework, which the article describes as "a comprehensive view of authentic learning, defined as 'thick authenticity.'" It comprises four interconnected dimensions: personal authenticity (alignment with interests, values, goals), real-world authenticity (tasks mirroring professional contexts), disciplinary authenticity (thinking in the modes of a discipline), and authentic assessment (assessment reflecting real-world knowledge application). The researchers coded 108 relevant interview segments against these four dimensions to assess student perceptions.

Claims¶

  • High school students perceived interest-driven data exploration activities in a data science curriculum as personally authentic, though not uniformly [+M]
  • Working with live, real-world datasets promoted students' perception of real-world authenticity and career transferability in a high school data science curriculum [+M]
  • Students most strongly felt like data scientists during the open-ended final project, supporting disciplinary authenticity [+M]
  • A culminating final project with a structured reflection script served as authentic assessment mirroring real-world data science practices [+M]
