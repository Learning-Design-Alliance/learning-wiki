# Embedded experimental designs in digital learning platforms

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This pattern describes embedding interventions directly within platform infrastructure—modifying problem formats, feedback types, or visual scaffolds—and observing effects during authentic learning activities. The paper highlights several variants: RCTs, A/B testing (e.g., E-TRIALS in ASSISTments), factorial designs (Terracotta), and adaptive learning interventions. As the authors state, 'One important affordance of some DLPs is the ability to conduct randomized experiments or structured comparisons at scale.'

- **Embedded experimental designs: randomized experiments and structured comparisons run inside DLPs during authentic learning**: This pattern describes embedding interventions directly within platform infrastructure—modifying problem formats, feedback types, or visual scaffolds—and observing effects during authentic learning activities. The paper highlights several variants: RCTs, A/B testing (e.g., E-TRIALS in ASSISTments), factorial designs (Terracotta), and adaptive learning interventions. As the authors state, 'One important affordance of some DLPs is the ability to conduct randomized experiments or structured comparisons at scale.' (Wei et al. (2025))
- **Collaborate with the DLP team to align planned experimental comparisons with platform capabilities and constraints**: The report's third consideration is that researchers should hold ongoing conversations with the platform team, since interventions must fit existing content and structure. Researchers "may need to refine their interventions/planned comparisons based on DLP capabilities and other limitations," and must learn what content can be created or modified. Higher-customization studies may require active recruitment or additional consent processes rather than drawing on the full user base. (Considerations for Conducting Research in Digital Learning Platforms)
- **Embed experiments seamlessly into the natural flow of platform instruction**: The report's first emerging insight is that classroom-embedded experiments "can and should be seamless and non-disruptive, requiring minimal, if any, burden on the part of the instructor." Embedded manipulations operate naturalistically within the existing flow of instruction, preserving the integrity of students' learning experiences without requiring teachers to modify practice or manage groups. Group random assignment keeps students in the same class or school in the same condition, avoiding perceived unfairness and visible differences, and supports ecological validity and generalizability. (Classroom-Based Experimentation in a Digital Learning Platform)
