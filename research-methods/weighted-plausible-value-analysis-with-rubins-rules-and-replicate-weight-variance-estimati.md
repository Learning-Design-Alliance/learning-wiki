# Weighted plausible-value analysis with Rubin's rules and replicate-weight variance estimation

## Description
The article recommends that secondary analysts of PVs weight student observations to restore population representativeness, repeat each analysis separately across all PVs (usually 5 to 10), and aggregate results using Rubin's rules with replicate-weight variance estimation. Without these steps, analysts 'risk underestimating the amount of uncertainty in their findings', which can lead to overstated claims.

- **Account for both sampling and imputation error when analyzing plausible values, using weights and Rubin's rules**: The article recommends that secondary analysts of PVs weight student observations to restore population representativeness, repeat each analysis separately across all PVs (usually 5 to 10), and aggregate results using Rubin's rules with replicate-weight variance estimation. Without these steps, analysts 'risk underestimating the amount of uncertainty in their findings', which can lead to overstated claims. (Dumas et al. (2026))
- **Multivariate multilevel model of combined TIMSS&PIRLS achievement with plausible values handled by multiple imputation**: A two-level multivariate model in which Reading, Math and Science scores are treated as a multivariate outcome measured at student level, with students nested within classes. Each outcome has its own covariate vector and the student and class error vectors have 3x3 covariance matrices, allowing estimation of residual correlations between outcome pairs at both levels and F tests of coefficient equality across outcomes. Estimation is performed separately for each of the five plausible values and combined with Multiple Imputation formulas, which the article says "yield correct standard errors accounting for both the variability in imputing the scores and the variability in estimating the model parameters". (Grilli et al. (2015))
