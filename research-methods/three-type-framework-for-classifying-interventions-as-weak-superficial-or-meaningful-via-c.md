# Three-type framework for classifying interventions as weak, superficial, or meaningful via correlation patterns

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines "three possible types of a SD intervention" diagnosed via intra-group correlations among pre (quiz), self-diagnosis (SD), and post (transfer) scores. A "weak" intervention does not affect the gap between low and high achievers; a "superficial" one lets low achievers score high on diagnosis without affecting later achievement; a "meaningful" one brings even less achieving students to perform meaningful diagnosis and affects later achievement. Each type carries predicted correlation patterns (Table 1), e.g. for meaningful interventions a positive SD–post correlation with non-significant pre–post correlations.

- **Framework classifying interventions as weak, superficial or meaningful via hypothesized correlation patterns**: The authors sketch a framework for analyzing the effects of various interventions by "classifying the interventions as “weak”, “superficial” or “meaningful”". For each type they hypothesize the kinds of correlations (e.g., positive or negative) that would result between the quiz score and the self-diagnosis score, and between the self-diagnosis score and the midterm score, and then analyze their data to understand the nature of the intervention and its impact on student learning. The full development appears in a companion paper. (E. Yerushalmi et al. (2009))
