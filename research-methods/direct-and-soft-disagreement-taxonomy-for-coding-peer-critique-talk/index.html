Direct and soft disagreement taxonomy for coding peer critique talk¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study adapts Pomerantz's strong/weak disagreement distinction into a two-category coding scheme for dissenting turns in peer critique. Direct forms — direct negation, challenging rhetorical wh-questions, and sensemaking challenges — were "employed for immediate and overt contradiction with prior statements without delay." Soft forms — hedging, yes-but partial agreement, posing an alternative idea, indirect challenge, and prosody — "were used to imply disagreements rather than overtly contradict others' ideas." The authors preferred the term soft over weak, and expanded the framework with codes emerging from the data; each turn could receive one or more codes.

Claims¶

  • Fifth graders in peer critique predominantly used soft (implied and/or mitigated) rather than direct forms of disagreement [+M]
  • Students disagreed more directly with invisible out-of-group peers than with present groupmates [+W]
  • Mixed and exclusively soft disagreement episodes featured more extended turns and more perspectives than exclusively direct episodes [+W]
  • Collaboration was sustained after both direct and soft disagreements, with no rude behaviors observed [+W]
