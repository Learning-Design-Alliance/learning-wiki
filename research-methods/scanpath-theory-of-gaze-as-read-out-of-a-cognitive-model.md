# Scanpath theory of gaze as read-out of a cognitive model

## Description
Scanpath theory holds that a scanpath is "a 'repetitive sequence of saccades and ﬁxations, idiosyncratic to a particular subject [person] and to a particular target pattern'" and that scanpaths reflect "the read-out of the internal representation of pictures, the so-called 'cognitive model'". The article applies this framework to real-world teaching: if expertise and culture shape teachers' cognitive models, expert and same-culture teachers should show more similar gaze sequences. Its string-edit-distance method represents gaze sequences as letter strings over coded gaze targets, preserving order information that aggregated looking-time measures discard, and the study's intra-individual similarity finding is presented as evidence that top-down cognitive models drive real-world classroom gaze.
