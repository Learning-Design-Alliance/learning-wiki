# ACER framework for analyzing students' use of mathematics in physics

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ACER framework, constructed by Caballero et al. leveraging task analysis and resource theory, organizes investigation of how students "Activate, Construct, Execute, and Reflect upon" mathematical resources in upper-division physics. It is not a model or rubric for student reasoning but "a scaffold for organizing the elements of a student's solutions," letting instructors pinpoint difficulties at key solution nodes. This paper demonstrates its application to the Coulomb's Law integral for continuous charge distributions.

- **ACER framework: four components for analyzing students' use of mathematics in upper-division physics**: ACER is a theoretical framework organizing how students employ mathematical tools in complex upper-division physics problems, comprising "Activation of the tool , Construction of the model , Execution of the mathematics , and Reflection on the results (ACER)." It is "grounded in task analysis... and resource theory," and was motivated by observing difficulties in student Taylor approximation solutions. It serves as "a scaffold onto which elements of a student's solution can be organized by researchers or instructors," helping describe where students are challenged and why. (Caballero et al.
