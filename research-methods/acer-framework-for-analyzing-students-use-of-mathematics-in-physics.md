# ACER framework for analyzing students' use of mathematics in physics

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ACER framework, constructed by Caballero et al. leveraging task analysis and resource theory, organizes investigation of how students "Activate, Construct, Execute, and Reflect upon" mathematical resources in upper-division physics. It is not a model or rubric for student reasoning but "a scaffold for organizing the elements of a student's solutions," letting instructors pinpoint difficulties at key solution nodes. This paper demonstrates its application to the Coulomb's Law integral for continuous charge distributions.

- **ACER framework: four components for analyzing students' use of mathematics in upper-division physics**: ACER is a theoretical framework organizing how students employ mathematical tools in complex upper-division physics problems, comprising "Activation of the tool , Construction of the model , Execution of the mathematics , and Reflection on the results (ACER)." It is "grounded in task analysis... and resource theory," and was motivated by observing difficulties in student Taylor approximation solutions. It serves as "a scaffold onto which elements of a student's solution can be organized by researchers or instructors," helping describe where students are challenged and why. (Caballero et al. (2012))
- **ACER framework: Activation, Construction, Execution, Reflection for analyzing mathematical tool use in physics problem solving**: The ACER framework organizes physics problem solving with mathematical tools into four components: "activation of mathematical tools, construction of mathematical models, execution of the mathematics, and reflection on the results." It was developed from expert problem solving and must be operationalized per tool and context via modified task analysis, producing an expert-consensus outline of key solution elements used as a coding structure for student work. In this article it structures the analysis of delta-function difficulties and informed interview protocol design. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2015))
- **ACER framework operationalized for boundary conditions in electrodynamics**: ACER (Activation, Construction, Execution, Reflection) is an analytical framework that 'characterize[s] student difficulties with upper-division problem solving by organizing the problem-solving process into four general components'. The authors extend it from mathematical tools to a physics concept, operationalizing it for BCs with elements such as C1 (writing complex field expressions), C3 (setting up equations at the boundary), E1 (simplifying exponentials), and R2 (checking limiting behaviors). It provided 'an organizing structure for our analysis that helped us identify nodes in students' work where key difficulties appear' and informed interview protocol design. (Ryan et al. (2017))
- **ACER framework for describing student use of mathematics in physics problem-solving**: The ACER framework articulates what it means to use math in physics through four components: "Activation of the tool, Construction of the model, Execution of the mathematics, and Reflection of the results." Each component is an epistemic frame consisting of subcodes (resources) between which students shift while solving problems, such as making assumptions or developing representations under Construction. The authors adopted ACER to analyze written and verbal work products because its components aligned with their evidence statements, it remains accessible to instructors unfamiliar with the underlying theoretical constructs, and it organizes written work products, extending its utility to introductory-level mathematical reasoning. (Amali Priyanka Jambuge et al. (2021))
