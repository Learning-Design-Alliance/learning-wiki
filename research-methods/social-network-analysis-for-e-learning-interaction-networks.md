## Description
The review frames social network analysis (SNA) as a methodological approach for describing and analysing network behaviours in e-learning. It notes SNA "has proven extremely powerful at describing and analysing network behaviours in business, economics and medicine, but its application to e-learning has been relatively limited." The review positions SNA as a way to understand and improve e-learning systems, operationalised through measures such as centrality and density applied to learner interaction networks, most often in forum-based one-mode networks.

- **Social network analysis as an approach to understanding and improving e-learning systems**: The review frames social network analysis (SNA) as a methodological approach for describing and analysing network behaviours in e-learning. It notes SNA "has proven extremely powerful at describing and analysing network behaviours in business, economics and medicine, but its application to e-learning has been relatively limited." (Cela et al. (2015))
- **SNA-based technology-enhanced assessment framework for collaborative competence in PBL**: The article operationalises social network analysis as an assessment output for PBL: directed, weighted group networks are built by combining a validated five-item collaboration rubric with ranked top-three peer nominations into hybrid edge weights (a 50:50 mixture). Centrality measures act as "assessment outputs, providing each student with quantifiable indicators of their collaborative engagement and social positioning within the group", mapped to CBME competency domains such as peer-recognised collaborative reliability and brokerage. (Ahmad Azman Ahmad Hathim et al. (2026))
- **Graph theory talk-turn methodology for quantifying small-group discussion dynamics**: The article adapts graph theory, modeling group participants as nodes and defining a talk-turn edge when "one participant talks after another." Directed, weighted edges track the sequential order and frequency of talk-turns, and parameters (degree, density, degree centrality, centralization, subgraphs) quantify individual participation and group-level dominance. The authors position this as a quantitative complement to discourse analysis, developed through three data-collection iterations and two refinements of the calculations. (Chai A. et al. (2019))
- **Released MATLAB and R analysis scripts for graph theory talk-turn data**: The article released custom data-processing and analysis scripts as part of its methodology. The MATLAB script "takes the talk-turn data in the question-and-response format and generates an edge list and a corresponding weight list for the edges," which serve as inputs for an R script using the igraph package to calculate the graph theory parameters. All scripts and source code at the time of publication are available online in the Supplemental Material. (Chai A. et al. (2019))
