# Four shifts for establishing ground truth in AIED: diagnostic IRR, transparent reporting, LLM risk mitigation, and validity-first evidence

## Description
The article's central framework proposes four practical shifts for establishing ground truth in AIED labeling: (1) treat IRR as "a diagnostic signal to localize disagreement and refine constructs rather than a mechanical acceptance threshold"; (2) require transparent reporting of rater expertise, codebook development, reconciliation procedures, and segmentation rules; (3) mitigate risks in LLM annotation through bias audits and verification workflows; and (4) complement agreement statistics with validity and effectiveness evidence, including uncertainty-aware labeling, criterion-related checks, and close-the-loop evaluations. The framework is grounded in the distinction between noise and bias and illustrated through multimodal tutoring case studies.
