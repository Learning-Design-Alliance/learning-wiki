# Centrality measures as operationalization of the participatory and social dimensions of CSCL

## Description
The article organizes SNA-based CSCL measurement around two dimensions. The participatory dimension is captured by degree centralities: outdegree is "frequently calculated as the number of out-posts generated by a learner" and indicates quantity of participation, while indegree counts replies received and indicates prestige or worthiness of contribution. The social dimension is captured by closeness, betweenness and eigenvector centralities; the article states that "Eigenvector centrality captures the social positioning of the students more reliably than the other centrality measures" because it weighs the centrality of a learner's contacts. The study validates which configurations best measure each dimension.

- **Researchers in CSCL settings with central collaborative modules should rely on degree and eigenvector centralities as indicators of success**: Based on meta-analytic findings across 69 course offerings, the authors recommend that "researchers with similar contexts (e.g., CSCL where the collaborative module is central to the course design) rely on degree and eigenvector centralities as indicators for success while exercising caution when using short-path centralities (closeness and betweenness)." This principle is grounded in the consistent positive correlations and reasonable predictive intervals observed for degree and eigenvector measures. (Mohammed Saqr (2022))
- **Social Network Analysis centrality measures as a framework for quantifying collaborative learning interactions**: The article describes SNA as offering "methods for the study of social networks in the form of visualization and mathematical analysis." Centrality measures provide "a quantification of node connectedness, interactions, position, or importance in the network." Six centrality measures within three categories (degree, short-path, and eigenvector-based) have been used in CSCL as indicators of student success, each operationalizing a distinct aspect of participation and social positioning. (Mohammed Saqr (2022))
