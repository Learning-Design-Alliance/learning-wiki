## Description
The article describes concept network analysis (aka knowledge structure analysis) as a method for measuring multiple document integration by converting students' learning products, such as summary essays, into networks with nodes representing core concepts and edges representing semantic relationships. Quantity of integration is the proportion of integrative links, semantic quality is the similarity of integrative links to an expert referent network, and structural quality is graph centrality similarity (GCsim) to the expert network form. The article uses this approach to evaluate how reading purpose and expert networks influence integration.

- **Knowledge point network (KPN) model for quantifying examination knowledge structures**: The KPN is a network model of an examination in which "knowledge points (concepts, laws, etc.)" are nodes and links are added "when these points appear in the same question." Topological quantities such as degree, centrality and community structure are then used to examine the structural properties and evolution of examinations. The authors applied it to 35 NCEE physics papers and argue it "can objectively and comprehensively quantify the knowledge structures and examination characteristics." (Min Xia et al. (2024))
- **Concept-network methodology for curriculum analysis: contents as nodes, epistemological relationships as edges of three types**: A methodological framework representing curricular contents as nodes and their epistemological relationships as links, so that centrality measures identify key concepts and community detection reveals coherent groupings. Relationships are coded as "a connection with dependency", "a connection without dependency", or "a disconnection". The authors applied it to Spanish upper-secondary Physics and Mathematics curricula, building directed and undirected networks and comparing isolated versus integrated analyses to inform competency-based curricular design. (Paula Tuzón et al. (2025))
