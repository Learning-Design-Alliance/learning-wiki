## Description
The article applies G-DINA, a model in the family of cognitive diagnostic models, as a confirmatory test theory for selection data. It requires two inputs: a pre-specified Q-matrix (blueprint) and binary or ordinal applicant performance data. The article states it is "capable of dealing with multidimensionality between as well as within items", which Cronbach's alpha and the Omega coefficient cannot, because selection items each measure multiple competencies in varying combinations. The model tests whether the expected structure is found in the data and proposes alterations to the Q-matrix.
