# Thick Authenticity framework

## Description
The study operationalizes Shaffer and Resnick's (1999) 'thick authenticity' framework, which the article describes as "a comprehensive view of authentic learning, defined as 'thick authenticity.'" It comprises four interconnected dimensions: personal authenticity (alignment with interests, values, goals), real-world authenticity (tasks mirroring professional contexts), disciplinary authenticity (thinking in the modes of a discipline), and authentic assessment (assessment reflecting real-world knowledge application). The researchers coded 108 relevant interview segments against these four dimensions to assess student perceptions.
