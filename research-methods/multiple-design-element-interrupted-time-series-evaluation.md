# Multiple-design-element interrupted time series evaluation

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the basic single-group ITS depends on extrapolating the pretest functional form as a counterfactual, an assumption the authors call problematic, evaluators should add multiple design elements to the basic ITS structure. Each element limits alternative interpretations, and together the elements provide a strong causal warrant when predictions are complex and results cohere with them.

- **Strengthen short interrupted time series evaluations by adding multiple design elements rather than relying on the basic single-group ITS structure**: Because the basic single-group ITS depends on extrapolating the pretest functional form as a counterfactual, an assumption the authors call problematic, evaluators should add multiple design elements to the basic ITS structure. Each element limits alternative interpretations, and together the elements provide a strong causal warrant when predictions are complex and results cohere with them. (Manyee Wong et al. (2015))
- **Causal pattern-matching as a warrant standard for augmented interrupted time series designs**: The article frames causal inference in augmented ITS designs as a pattern-matching exercise: multiple design elements each generate predictions, and the causal claim is warranted when the predictions are complex, the observed results "cohere" with the predictions, and no alternative explanation can fit the same pattern of predictions even if it fits some of them. No single element is sufficient; the warrant is collective. The framework is illustrated with No Child Left Behind. (Manyee Wong et al. (2015))
- **Interrupted time series analysis as a method for measuring the impact of training courses on trainee outcomes**: The article applies interrupted time series regression to evaluate a training intervention against trainees' natural learning curves. A linear regression models the outcome with three covariates: procedure number relative to the course, a variable capturing how the gradient changed post-course, and a dichotomous course-attendance variable that "looked for any step-change improvement immediately after the course". The authors argue this method enables estimation of post-course performance while accounting for pre-course trajectories, and propose it "may be used across different training courses for research and quality assurance purposes". (Siau K et al. (2020))

# Causal pattern-matching as a warrant standard for augmented interrupted time series designs

## Description
The article frames causal inference in augmented ITS designs as a pattern-matching exercise: multiple design elements each generate predictions, and the causal claim is warranted when the predictions are complex, the observed results "cohere" with the predictions, and no alternative explanation can fit the same pattern of predictions even if it fits some of them. ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple design elements must be added to the basic ITS structure so that they engender complex, jointly discriminating predictions.
#### Constraints
- No added design element is perfect by itself; the strong warrant is conditional on complex predictions, coherent results, and no alternative fitting the same pattern.

### Target Learners
- Education researchers and evaluators of state and national programs

### Target Learning Objectives
- Designing and interpreting interrupted time series evaluations so that causal conclusions are warranted
