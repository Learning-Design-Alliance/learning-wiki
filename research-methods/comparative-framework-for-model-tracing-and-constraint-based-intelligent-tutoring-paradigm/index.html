Comparative framework for model-tracing and constraint-based intelligent tutoring paradigms¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article contrasts two approaches to building intelligent tutoring systems: the "well-established model-tracing paradigm" and "the relatively newer constraint-based paradigm". It evaluates the proponents' claim that constraint-based tutors "afford performance at levels comparable to that of model-tracing tutors, but with significantly less development effort", and organizes the comparison around remediation quality, domain feasibility, and development effort.

Accounts¶

  • A comparative framework contrasting the model-tracing and constraint-based intelligent tutoring paradigms: The article contrasts two approaches to building intelligent tutoring systems: the "well-established model-tracing paradigm" and "the relatively newer constraint-based paradigm". It evaluates the proponents' claim that constraint-based tutors "afford performance at levels comparable to that of model-tracing tutors, but with significantly less development effort", and organizes the comparison around remediation quality, domain feasibility, and development effort. (Viswanathan Kodaganallur et al. (2005))
  • Combining model tracing and constraint-based modeling to generate feedback on multistep reasoning: This framework combines two ITS design paradigms so that constraints check the internal consistency of a (partial) solution — identifying missing elements and inconsistencies — while model-tracing elements provide hints on appropriate next steps and diagnose specific common errors via buggy rules. In this article the combination was implemented in a domain module for hypothesis testing with 36 expert rules, 16 buggy rules, and 49 constraints. The authors found "constraints to be very useful for identifying missing elements and inconsistencies in students’ solutions", while model tracing allowed addressing specific errors and providing hints. Unlike prior systems (ActiveMath, Invention Lab) where the paradigms reasoned about separate domain aspects, this design integrates them to cooperatively reason about the domain as a whole. (Tacoma et al. (2020))

Claims¶

  • Two characteristics of the problem domain are key in distinguishing which tutoring paradigm is appropriate for a given domain [+M]
  • Model tracing is superior for targeted, high-quality remediation, and this superiority increases with the complexity of the solution process goal structure [+M]
  • The constraint-based tutoring paradigm is feasible only for domains in which the solution itself is rich in information, whereas model tracing has no such restriction [+M]
  • Students receiving intelligent ITS feedback made significantly fewer errors in hypothesis-testing logic than students receiving verification feedback only [+M]
  • ITS feedback availability interacted with task number so that experimental-group students increasingly outperformed controls in solving tasks over time [+M]
