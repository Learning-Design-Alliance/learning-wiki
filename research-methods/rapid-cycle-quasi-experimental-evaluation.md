## Description
The article advocates rapid-cycle quasi-experimental studies (e.g., pre-post with non-equivalent control groups) as an agile, low-cost way to evaluate early program impact. It argues that because RCTs 'necessitate a substantial investment of time, human capital, and resources, often spanning across multiple years,' rapid quasi-experiments provide 'useful data early in a project without the high costs, in time and money, required by traditional RCTs,' and that multiple studies across sites create multiple lines of evidence.
