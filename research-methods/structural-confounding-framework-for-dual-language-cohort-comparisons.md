## Description
The article presents a methodological account of how "performance differences can emerge in dual-language contexts where instructional methods and language tracks are structurally confounded." When cohort membership, instructional language, and instructional sequence are not independent, observed contrasts reflect combined effects rather than a single causal factor. The framework calls for interpreting such results as adjusted cohort-level contrasts rather than isolated instructional effects.
