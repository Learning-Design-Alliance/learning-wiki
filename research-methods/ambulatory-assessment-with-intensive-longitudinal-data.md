## Description
Ambulatory assessment is a measurement approach in which intensive longitudinal data are captured in real-life settings with repeated within-subject assessments of physical activity (e.g., via accelerometry) and cognitive performance (e.g., via a smartphone app) at high measurement density, such as sub-daily or daily. The article proposes it to complement between-subjects analyses with within-subject analyses of "the differences between days with less or more physical activity", and recommends continuous monitoring of activity level and context across intervals of at least 7 days before, during, and after the summer break.

- **Experience sampling method (ESM) for capturing macro-level emotion dynamics in language learning**: ESM, also known as ecological momentary assessment, originated in psychiatry and collects real-time data on participants' behaviours, experiences and psychological states in natural environments. Participants are prompted "multiple times per day over a specified period ranging from days to weeks", via fixed, random, or event-contingent prompts on smartphones or wearable devices. The review proposes ESM to fill the gap of methodologies examining emotion dynamics from a macro, developmental perspective in language studies, illustrating a hypothetical two-week exam-period design with prompts after language activities plus contextual questions. (Peng Wang et al. (2024))
- **Experience sampling protocol with single-item state measures on handheld devices for classroom goal and emotion assessment**: The study's measurement instrument is an experience sampling procedure in which students carried iPod Touch 4G devices running iDialog Pad software for ten school days, activating them at the start of 45-minute classes and completing a digital questionnaire at one random audible signal. State goals and emotions were assessed with single-item measures because multi-item scales could compromise state validity by prompting reflection and recall biases. Of 1779 initiated questionnaires, 1409 (79%) were completed, averaging 11.74 per student. ICC(2) reliability of aggregated state scores ranged from .734 to .921. (Goetz et al. (2016))
- **Smartphone experience-sampling application for measuring multiple interests over time**: The study used "experience-sampling methodology through a smartphone application" to measure students' multiple interests and their development across contexts and over time. Students reported interests at two time points three months apart, yielding 7,226 interest reports from 204 Dutch high school students. The tool enables capturing interest dimensions in situ rather than via retrospective questionnaires. (Akkerman et al. (2020))
