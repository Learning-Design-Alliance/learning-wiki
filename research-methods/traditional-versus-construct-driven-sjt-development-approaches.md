# Traditional versus construct-driven SJT development approaches

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The article distinguishes two SJT developmental approaches. Traditional SJTs "mimic samples of previous work experience" with expert-evaluated situations and responses scored into a mixture of skills "often not further specified". Construct-driven SJTs follow personality-test procedures: they start from defined constructs, use skill-activating situations, and score each skill separately. The article applies this framework to medical admission via the HAM-SJT (traditional) and CD-SJT (construct-driven, agency and communion).

- **Traditional versus construct-driven approaches to SJT development**: The article distinguishes two SJT developmental approaches. Traditional SJTs "mimic samples of previous work experience" with expert-evaluated situations and responses scored into a mixture of skills "often not further specified". Construct-driven SJTs follow personality-test procedures: they start from defined constructs, use skill-activating situations, and score each skill separately. The article applies this framework to medical admission via the HAM-SJT (traditional) and CD-SJT (construct-driven, agency and communion). (Mielke et al. (2025))
- **Staged SJT development process: specification, faculty item writing, concordance, psychometric evaluation, piloting**: A reusable multi-stage process for building a selection SJT: define a test specification from curriculum analysis, faculty scenario writing with cross-writer evaluation, an expert concordance stage to set the scoring key, initial psychometric evaluation with students, then pilot testing with applicants. The article states development "started by defining the test specification, which outlines key aspects such as the non-academic domains to be assessed, the test length and the context within which scenarios are set." (Soemantri Diantha et al. (2022))
- **Combining inductive (critical incident) and deductive (theory-based) approaches in SJT development**: The article's development pattern pairs an inductive approach, in which critical incident interviews with subject matter experts supply realistic scenarios, with a deductive approach, in which established theoretical models generate the response option categories. The authors state this combination "allows us to benefit from the advantages of both methods and results in an SJT with realistic contextualised scenarios measuring an explicit construct". The inductive side contributes contextualisation, which the article links to stronger predictive validity, higher perceived job-relatedness and lower susceptibility to socially desirable responding. (de Leng W E et al. (2018))
