Five-domain validity framework for simulator evaluation¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review organises simulator validation evidence using five validity domains defined from Van Nortwick and McDougall: face validity (realism to non-experts), content validity (expert-rated teaching effectiveness), construct validity (differentiating expertise levels), concurrent validity (correlation with a gold standard such as human anatomy), and predictive validity ("the extent to which performance on the simulator predicts future performance in real clinical settings"). Each domain carries specified assessment methods, e.g. I-CVI ≥0.80 for content and task time, errors, or OSATS scores for construct validity. The review uses this framework to categorise and rate all 29 simulators.

Accounts¶

  • Five-domain validity framework for simulator evaluation (face, content, construct, concurrent, predictive): The review organises simulator validation evidence using five validity domains defined from Van Nortwick and McDougall: face validity (realism to non-experts), content validity (expert-rated teaching effectiveness), construct validity (differentiating expertise levels), concurrent validity (correlation with a gold standard such as human anatomy), and predictive validity ("the extent to which performance on the simulator predicts future performance in real clinical settings"). Each domain carries specified assessment methods, e.g. I-CVI ≥0.80 for content and task time, errors, or OSATS scores for construct validity. The review uses this framework to categorise and rate all 29 simulators. (McCarthy Roisin et al. (2026))
  • Dual-framework evaluation model: Messick's five-source validity framework combined with adapted McGaghie translational outcome levels: The review organises its appraisal of every simulator around two complementary frameworks. Validity is judged against Messick's modern validity framework, whose five sources are content, response processes, internal structure, relations to other variables and consequences, each rated 0–2 on a validated scale. Effectiveness is judged against "an adaptation of McGaghie's proposed levels of simulation-based translational outcomes", ranging from internal acceptability (level 1) through contained, downstream and target effects to collateral effects (level 5). (Lee Roxanne et al. (2020))

Claims¶

  • Of 939 screened studies, only 17 articles validating 29 airway simulators met inclusion criteria, and most studies assessed only one or two validity domains [+M]
  • Validity evidence across ophthalmic simulators is sparse: only two studies provided evidence for all five sources of validity assessment, and the Eyesi Surgical is the most extensively assessed model with 33 validity studies. [+M]
  • Effectiveness evidence for ophthalmic simulators mostly stops at level 2 (contained effects), with isolated exceptions at level 3 (Sophocle) and level 5 (Eyesi for cataract surgery). [+M]
