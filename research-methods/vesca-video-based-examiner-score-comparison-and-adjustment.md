# VESCA (video-based examiner score comparison and adjustment)

## Description
VESCA is a three-phase method for measuring and adjusting examiner-cohort effects in fully nested, multi-circuit OSCEs. It involves "(i) a subset of students were filmed ‘live’ when performing the majority of stations in their real OSCE; (ii) examiners from each of the separate parallel circuits of the examination scored station‐specific common‐comparator videos", and (iii) statistical analysis (MFRM for global scores, LMM for percentage scores) uses the partial crossing to estimate and adjust cohort effects. The article presents it as novel because scoring station-specific common-comparator videos overcomes the fully nested design without assuming equal student ability across cohorts.

- **Gold-standard video-assessor checklist comparison of feedback accuracy**: A measurement element used in the trial: students' examinations were video-recorded and rated by two independent video assessors with binary checklists, producing a "gold-standard rating against which PI and medical staff feedback could be compared". Feedback was then content-analyzed against the same 147-item checklist, with items categorized into major key steps, minor steps, and communication items. Inter-rater reliability was satisfactory to good (ICC 0.52 and 0.58). (Krautter Markus et al. (2017))
