# Prediction versus explanation as two cultures of statistical modelling in educational psychology

## Description
The article articulates a framework distinguishing explanatory from predictive statistical modelling. Explanatory approaches build parsimonious, theory-derived models to understand mechanisms, assessing fit on the same data used to build them; by contrast, "the predictive culture does not prioritise explaining the mechanisms underpinning a phenomenon: it instead optimises directly for accuracy on future data". The framework centres on overfitting — where a model predicts training data but fails to generalise — and holds that both approaches are needed, since prediction errors can point to gaps in explanatory models.
