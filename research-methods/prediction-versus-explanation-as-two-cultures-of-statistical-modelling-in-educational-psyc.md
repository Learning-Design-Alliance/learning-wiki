# Prediction versus explanation as two cultures of statistical modelling in educational psychology

## Description
The article articulates a framework distinguishing explanatory from predictive statistical modelling. Explanatory approaches build parsimonious, theory-derived models to understand mechanisms, assessing fit on the same data used to build them; by contrast, "the predictive culture does not prioritise explaining the mechanisms underpinning a phenomenon: it instead optimises directly for accuracy on future data". The framework centres on overfitting — where a model predicts training data but fails to generalise — and holds that both approaches are needed, since prediction errors can point to gaps in explanatory models.

(Michael Noetel et al. (2023))
- **Neural networks (multilayer perceptron) models as a prediction-oriented approach to early identification of learning difficulties**: The article frames deep neural networks as machine-learning models that, unlike classical linear, inference-focused statistics, can identify linear and non-linear associations, interaction effects, or combinations of these, and can handle many correlated predictors. A multilayer perceptron learns connection weights from labelled training data and is then applied to a held-out testing sample to forecast a dichotomized outcome. The authors position this as a complement to inference models: "Prediction allows, for example, the identification of whether an individual will experience MD without requiring an understanding of the underlying mechanisms". They also name the approach's key limitation: neural networks are often referred to as black boxes. (Psyridou et al. (2024))
