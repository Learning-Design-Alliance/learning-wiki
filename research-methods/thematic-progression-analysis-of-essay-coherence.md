## Description
Thematic progression (TP), first proposed by Daneš (1974), concerns "the way that the texts develop the ideas they present" in Theme-Rheme structure. The study identifies five TP patterns (linear, constant, summative, split Theme, split Rheme) and three types of unmotivated Themes (back, contextual, new), counting connections between contiguous T-units. The framework is used to code 150 essays and quantify how instruction changes learners' information structuring.

- **Thematic progression patterns as an analytic framework for English essay writing**: Thematic progression (TP), first proposed by Daneš (1974), concerns "the way that the texts develop the ideas they present" in Theme-Rheme structure. The study identifies five TP patterns (linear, constant, summative, split Theme, split Rheme) and three types of unmotivated Themes (back, contextual, new), counting connections between contiguous T-units. The framework is used to code 150 essays and quantify how instruction changes learners' information structuring. (Jing Wei (2017))
- **Theme Theory and Danes' thematic progression patterns as a framework for textual coherence**: Theme Theory, from Systemic-functional Linguistics, divides each clause into a theme (the point of departure) and a rheme (what is said about it), with simple, multiple and clausal theme types. Thematic progression (TP), defined by Danes as "the choice and ordering of utterance themes, their mutual concatenation hierarchy", describes how themes and rhemes chain across clauses to display connexity. The article uses six TP patterns (linear, constant, focused, derived-theme, split-rheme, summarized) both to analyze texts and to structure writing instruction aimed at discourse-level coherence. (Liu Jingxia et al. (2013))
