# P-curve analysis

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
P-curve analysis is a tool for assessing whether a set of statistically significant findings contains evidential value. It examines the distribution of significant p values: "True effects, those that differ from zero, lead to right-skewed p-curves (e.g., more .01s than .04s). Nonexistent effects lead to flat p-curves". Many forms of p-hacking lead to left-skewed p-curves, so a right-skewed observed p-curve rules out selective reporting of significant results as the sole explanation.
