# Measurement-artifact framework for longitudinal achievement-gap growth estimates

## Description
The article presents a framework identifying two measurement artifacts that distort gap-growth estimates: test score scaling (whether the scale is a vertical interval measure of ability) and changes of test form between school year and summer. It argues that "the way that tests are designed, administered, and scaled can have profound implications for the apparent magnitude and timing of growth in test score gaps." The framework is applied to reevaluate widely cited studies including the BSS and ECLS-K analyses.
