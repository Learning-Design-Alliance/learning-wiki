# CUSUM learning-curve analysis for procedural proficiency benchmarking

## Description
The article applies cumulative sum (CUSUM) methods as a quantitative framework for defining surgical proficiency. For a binary outcome, CUSUM(i) = CUSUM(i − 1) + [X(i) – p0], where the curve peak "identifies the proficiency point, i.e. the case after which performance was consistently better than the cohort average"; for operative time the trough marks the inflection. The authors offer this as "a reproducible framework for benchmarking THD proficiency" and a pragmatic reference point for training, credentialing, and audit.

## Accounts
<!-- How each source describes or uses the method -->
- **CUSUM learning-curve analysis as a framework for benchmarking surgical proficiency**: The article applies cumulative sum (CUSUM) methods as a quantitative framework for defining surgical proficiency. For a binary outcome, CUSUM(i) = CUSUM(i − 1) + [X(i) – p0], where the curve peak "identifies the proficiency point, i.e. the case after which performance was consistently better than the cohort average"; for operative time the trough marks the inflection. The authors offer this as "a reproducible framework for benchmarking THD proficiency" and a pragmatic reference point for training, credentialing, and audit. (Galli Andrea Cesare et al. (2026))
- **Institutional proficiency assessment method: pre-defined benchmarks with moving-average analysis over blocks of 20 cases**: A method for benchmarking institutional rather than individual learning: "Institutional proficiency was pre-defined as an average conversion rate < 10% and a severe complication rate < 10%, based on the literature and the approach of this comparative analysis." Moving average blocks of 20 consecutive cases were used to calculate block-wise conversion and severe complication rates, with the first chronological case where both moving averages crossed below threshold counted as institutional proficiency. Estimated blood loss and operative time served as secondary outcomes, with case-mix adjustment using the IWATE difficulty score. (van Dorst R. W. J. J. et al. (2026))
- **Cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis as a method for quantifying procedural learning curves**: CUSUM analysis is presented as a quantitative method for assessing learning curves of procedures. The article defines it as follows: "CUSUM analysis quantifies the learning process by calculating the cumulative difference between individual procedure times and the cohort mean. A negative slope indicates performance improvement, with a TP indicating proficiency attainment." In this study, pooled mean CUSUM curves over 60 consecutive cases per operator were used to locate turning points and divide experience into learning and proficiency phases. (Cheng X et al. (2025))
- **Learning curve CUSUM method for defining procedural competency**: A statistical monitoring technique for defining when a trainee reaches a predefined proficiency level: "The learning curve cumulative summation (LC‐CUSUM) approach was used to determine the point at which a skill achieved a predefined level of proficiency." The score changes by −0.087 per success and +0.560 per failure, with proficiency declared when it crosses the lower decision limit of −1.25. (Chaiperm Tanchanok et al. (2025))
- **Risk-adjusted CUSUM (RA-CUSUM) monitoring of surgical learning curves with risk adjustment via logistic regression**: RA-CUSUM is a sequential statistical monitoring method the article applies to define surgeons' learning curves. CUSUM "uses a cumulative sum of deviations from a predefined standard to track a surgeon's performance over time", detecting small shifts in process means; risk adjustment defines the curve "according to case mix and the likelihood of adverse events", making the analysis "less susceptible to outliers or selection bias". The article applies it to operative time, length of stay, ICU nights, lymph-node yield, anastomotic leakage, major complications, and textbook outcome, using varying-coefficient logistic regression models adjusted for age, sex, BMI, tumor size, ASA and UICC score, and comorbidities. (Seika Philippa et al. (2025))
- **CUSUM analysis with piecewise linear regression as a method for mapping surgical learning curves**: Cumulative sum (CUSUM) analysis, originally developed for quality control in manufacturing, computes the running sum of deviations of each case's operative time from the mean, so that a downward slope indicates a shorter-than-expected operation time and an upward slope a longer-than-expected one. The article applies it to a chronological series of robot-assisted sacrocolpopexies and uses piecewise linear regression to locate breakpoints separating Learning, Proficiency, and Competency phases. The authors state it is "able to detect even the subtle shifts in parameters of any given procedure" and can "allow real-time monitoring of surgeon proficiency and competency by detecting fine patterns after controlling for random variations". (Park Kena et al. (2024))
