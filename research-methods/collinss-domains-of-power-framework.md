## Description
Collins's Domains of Power framework, applied to physics learning environments, holds that power is organized across four interconnected domains. As the article states, "She argued that the four domains we need to consider are: interpersonal, cultural, structural, and disciplinary." In this context the interpersonal domain concerns how students and faculty interact, the cultural domain the physics culture, the structural domain the structure of the learning environment, and the disciplinary domain how instructors enforce norms. The authors use it, together with HELPIEE and Standpoint theory, to analyze the interviewed women's experiences.

- **Domains of Power framework: four domains (interpersonal, cultural, structural, disciplinary) for analyzing how power is organized in physics learning environments**: The Domains of Power framework, introduced by Collins and applied to physics by Johnson, holds that power in a context is organized across four domains. The article states: "She argued that the four domains we need to consider are: interpersonal, cultural, structural, and disciplinary." The interpersonal domain concerns power between individuals, the cultural domain group values, the structural domain how power is organized in structures, and the disciplinary domain how rules are enforced and for whom. The article uses this framework as its primary lens for comparing the three departments, noting instructors have power over shaping the narrative in each domain and that the four domains interact. (Lisabeth Marie Santana et al. (2024))
- **Domains of power framework as an analytical lens on physics learning environments**: The article adopts Collins's domains of power framework, previously applied to physics culture by Johnson, as an analytical lens alongside standpoint theory and Schlossberg's transition theory. It holds that "there are four domains describing how power is expressed": the interpersonal domain (between individuals), the cultural domain (group values), the disciplinary domain (how rules are enforced and for whom), and the structural domain (the organization of power). The authors map women's experiences affecting self-efficacy and belonging at transition points onto characteristics of one or more domains, e.g., lounge misconduct as disciplinary and structural, and instructor classroom behavior as interpersonal. (Sarah Lindley et al. (2025))
