## Description
The article frames the Angoff method not as a single technique but as "a flexible family of approaches with distinct outcomes." The family includes Traditional Angoff, Modified Angoff, Angoff (Yes/No), Angoff Group (Yes/No), Three-level Angoff, and Mastery Angoff, each differing in how judges estimate borderline candidate performance. The choice of variant allows for the deliberate setting of more lenient or stringent standards, making variant selection a conscious decision aligned with assessment purpose and context.

(Kannan et al. (2025))
- **Simplified three-level Angoff standard-setting method (YES/NO/MAYBE with weights 1/0/.3, no consensus meetings)**: A simplified modification of the Angoff standard-setting method in which panellists individually rate each test item 'YES', 'NO' or 'MAYBE' as to whether the minimally qualified but still acceptable candidate would answer correctly, weighted 1, 0 and .3 respectively, with total scores summed from weighted responses. In the Swedish re-certification examinations, "Training and consensus have been redacted, which serves to balance a manageable workload." The study used 10–12 panellists per test, within the recommended range of five to fifteen. (Dalum Jesper et al. (2024))
