Distributional treatment effects framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The framework defines an intervention effect in its most general form as a difference in post-intervention outcome distributions between treatment and control conditions: "aninterventioneffectisestablishedthroughfocusingonthedifferenceinthepost-interventiondistributions(P)oftheoutcomeacrossinterventionandcontrolconditions." Beyond the mean (ATE), it formalizes effects on variance (ATE2), skewness (ATE3), kurtosis (ATE4), and the probabilities of reaching the scale minimum and maximum (ATEf, ATEc). Variance effects indicate whether the intervention works predictably for most students or induces heterogeneity; skewness and kurtosis effects indicate differential or extreme differential responses; endpoint effects capture floor/ceiling change. The article also presents four prototypical moderation patterns combining mean shifts with variance, skewness, kurtosis, and endpoint changes.
