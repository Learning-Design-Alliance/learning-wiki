Body-to-Body Coupling (BtBC) via Motion Energy Analysis¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article introduces BtBC as a dyadic measure derived from video-based Motion Energy Analysis, "a computer-vision method based on the assessment of differences in sequences of frames in video recordings, requiring no motion capture devices on instructors or learners". Motion in instructor and learner ROIs is cross-correlated over 30-s moving windows across 251 time lags, yielding an overall BtBC index averaged over all lags. The framework positions "the real-time interaction of teaching and learning bodies" as important for learning, with instructional approach shaping whether coupling emerges.

Claims¶

  • Body-to-body coupling between instructor and learner is greater during scaffolding than explanation instruction [+W]
  • Learner-leading BtBC is larger under scaffolding; instructor-leading BtBC shows only a trending effect, and instructor-leading BtBC fails to predict learning under explanation [+W]
  • Directional leader–follower BtBC patterns decode instructional approach better than individual motion, with peak performance at non-zero time lags [+W]
  • Instructors moved more when using an explanation approach than a scaffolding approach, but dyad and learner motion did not differ between approaches [+W]
  • Learning outcome can be predicted from overall BtBC via support vector regression [+W]
  • Instructor motion is positively associated with BtBC only during scaffolding, not explanation [+W]
  • Instructional personalization did not significantly affect learning outcome or BtBC [+W]
