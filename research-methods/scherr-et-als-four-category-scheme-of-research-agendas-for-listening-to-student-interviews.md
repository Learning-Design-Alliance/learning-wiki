# Scherr et al.'s four-category scheme of research agendas for listening to student interviews

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that observers of student interviews bring one of several primary research agendas, listed as: "1. Conceptual knowledge of physical mechanism", "2. Source of knowledge", "3. Knowledge construction", and "4. Beliefs about knowledge". Each agenda directs which student statements an observer attends to and considers data; the authors code one student's utterances against these four categories to illustrate that different agendas yield very different judgments of the same excerpt. The scheme is offered as an incomplete list of possible agendas, intended to illustrate rather than definitively classify.

- **Explicitly consider possible research agendas before and during analysis of student interview data**: The principle holds that researchers should deliberately surface and weigh the alternative agendas they might bring to listening to students, because implicit agendas filter which statements are heard and recorded. The article argues that "Conscious consideration of possible research agendas widens the range of opportunity for productive research." Even in favorable one-on-one interview conditions, accurate listening requires careful effort, and unexamined agendas may control attention inappropriately. (Scherr et al. (2002))
