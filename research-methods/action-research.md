## Description
The article defines action research as systematic inquiry conducted by teachers to understand and improve their own school contexts, drawing on Freire's critical pedagogy. It frames action research as a 'critical' tool for empowering educators and underserved communities to define and analyze their own social problems, develop new understandings, and act. Change is assumed to come from the ground up rather than top-down, and inquiry must be a collaborative partnership with colleagues at the school. The article further draws on Cooper and Gause's concept of 'collaborative activism' as the essence of transformative leadership.
