Continuum of evaluative complexity spanning ten levels from cognitive assessment to normative/consensus assessment¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review establishes a continuum of evaluative complexity (Table 4) ranking assessment approaches from knowledge-centred measurement to assessment at educational-systems level. As printed: "Analysis of the identified strategies allowed establishment of a continuum of evaluative complexity, from approaches centred on knowledge to integrated models of assessment in real contexts (Table 4)." The ten levels run from cognitive assessment (low complexity), through skills, attitudinal, real performance-based, applied clinical, programmatic, population/institutional, and validated-instrument assessment, to longitudinal assessment and normative/consensus assessment. Levels were assigned by degree of contextual authenticity, integration of multiple competency domains, educational scope and proximity to real-world professional performance.
