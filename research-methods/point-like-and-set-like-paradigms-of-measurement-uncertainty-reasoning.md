# Point-like and set-like paradigms of measurement uncertainty reasoning

## Description
The point and set paradigms, "developed by researchers at the University of Cape Town and the University of York", describe "two opposing ideas about uncertainty". A point-like reasoner believes "a single measurement can yield the 'true value'" of a quantity and considers individual measurements independently; a set-like reasoner recognizes no single measurement yields the true value and considers "a distribution of measurements with an associated mean and spread". Set-like reasoning is associated with expert-like understanding, and the paradigms organize PMQ coding of student explanations.

Set-like reasoning is associated with expert-like understanding, and the paradigms organize PMQ coding of student explanations. (H. J. Lewandowski et al. (2017))
- **Point and set paradigms of statistical measurement uncertainty reasoning**: The point and set paradigms characterize two philosophical perspectives on statistical measurement uncertainty. The point paradigm holds that a single trial could completely represent the true value, with deviations due to mistakes; the set paradigm holds that "no individual measurement can yield its true value," so multiple trials must be considered as a distribution. The set paradigm is considered more aligned with expert-like reasoning, and the transformed course's learning goal was that students demonstrate set-like reasoning. (Benjamin Pollard et al. (2020))
