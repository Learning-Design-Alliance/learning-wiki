# Point-like and set-like paradigms of measurement uncertainty reasoning

## Description
The point and set paradigms, "developed by researchers at the University of Cape Town and the University of York", describe "two opposing ideas about uncertainty". A point-like reasoner believes "a single measurement can yield the 'true value'" of a quantity and considers individual measurements independently; a set-like reasoner recognizes no single measurement yields the true value and considers "a distribution of measurements with an associated mean and spread". Set-like reasoning is associated with expert-like understanding, and the paradigms organize PMQ coding of student explanations.

- **Point and set paradigms of measurement uncertainty reasoning**: The point and set paradigms, "developed by researchers at the University of Cape Town and the University of York", describe "two opposing ideas about uncertainty". A point-like reasoner believes "a single measurement can yield the 'true value'" of a quantity and considers individual measurements independently; a set-like reasoner recognizes no single measurement yields the true value and considers "a distribution of measurements with an associated mean and spread". Set-like reasoning is associated with expert-like understanding, and the paradigms organize PMQ coding of student explanations. (H. J. Lewandowski et al. (2017))
- **Point and set paradigms of statistical measurement uncertainty reasoning**: The point and set paradigms characterize two philosophical perspectives on statistical measurement uncertainty. The point paradigm holds that a single trial could completely represent the true value, with deviations due to mistakes; the set paradigm holds that "no individual measurement can yield its true value," so multiple trials must be considered as a distribution. The set paradigm is considered more aligned with expert-like reasoning, and the transformed course's learning goal was that students demonstrate set-like reasoning. (Benjamin Pollard et al. (2020))
- **Point and set paradigms of student procedural reasoning about measurement**: The article describes two paradigms of student procedural reasoning about measurement and uncertainty, identified from Physics Measurement Questionnaire responses by prior researchers. Point reasoners "tend to emphasize individual measurements in interpreting data," believing any single measurement could yield the true value and attributing deviation to mistakes. Set reasoners "view each individual measurement as an estimate of the quantity of interest," regard uncertainty as a natural part of experimentation, and use statistics such as the mean and standard deviation. The set paradigm is treated as the expertlike approach toward which instruction should aim. (Emily M. Stump et al. (2023))
