# Socially Mediated Metacognition Framework (SMMF) coding scheme

## Description
The Socially Mediated Metacognition Framework treats metacognition at the group level as necessarily expressed through collaborative interactions. The article states that "This framework leverages the fact that metacognition at the group level is necessarily expressed through collaborative interactions," and adopts it because upper-division laboratory work is collaborative. It employs Goos et al.'s two-level coding scheme: transcripts are coded line by line for metacognitive function (new ideas versus assessment of information and approaches) and for three transactive exchanges (self-disclosure, feedback requests, and other-monitoring). The authors apply it to a 90-second troubleshooting vignette to categorize what individuals contribute and how partners understand one another.

- **Socially mediated metacognition (SMM) framework adapted to collaborative troubleshooting**: The article applies the socially mediated metacognition framework of Goos et al., in which "mediation of metacognition occurs through discussion among students." The framework codes individual conversational turns as metacognitive moves (new ideas, assessments) or transactive moves (self-disclosure, other-monitoring, feedback requests), and identifies metacognitive nodes and transactive clusters as reciprocated exchanges. The authors adapt it to a new context: "we map the SMM framework to yet another context: the cognitive task of troubleshooting." They add a fourth transactive move type, idea requests. (Van De Bogart et al. (2017))
