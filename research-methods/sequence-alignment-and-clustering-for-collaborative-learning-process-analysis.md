# Sequence alignment and clustering for collaborative-learning process analysis

## Description
The article adapts sequence alignment from bioinformatics: group action logs are human-coded into contribution types — "coordination, monitoring, minor contribution, and major contribution" — plus a "gap" code for more than 24 hours of inactivity. Pairwise edit distances (a Levenshtein variant, with gap operations costed 2) form a similarity matrix that is clustered with partitioning around medoids (PAM), with k chosen by silhouette analysis. The authors claim this method "can serve as a kind of microscope to capture differences in the underlying collaboration processes", and distinguish it from sequential pattern mining, which finds recurring subsequences rather than whole-string similarity.
