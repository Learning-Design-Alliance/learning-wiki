# Brier-score decomposition into calibration, resolution, and knowledge as metrics of detection metacognition

## Description
The article uses probability judgments to reveal metacognitive states of mind, scoring them with the Brier score, which "can be decomposed into three components, calibration (or reliability), resolution, and knowledge (or uncertainty)." Calibration measures whether confidence judgments correspond to relative frequencies; resolution measures the variance of correct answers associated with different probability judgments, so high-resolution individuals "are better able to discriminate between correct and incorrect judgments by assigning them different probabilities." Judgments are binned into six confidence ranges (50–59% through 100%), with lower calibration scores and higher resolution scores indicating better metacognition; knowledge, capturing outcome uncertainty, is excluded as not an element of metacognition.
