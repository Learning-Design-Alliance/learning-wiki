## Description
The article presents fidelity as a two-part construct adapted from the flight simulation literature: physical or engineering fidelity, the degree to which simulators duplicate the appearance of the real system (covering environmental fidelity), and psychological fidelity, "the degree to which the simulation participants perceive the simulation as an authentic surrogate for the task being trained". It also notes the recent introduction of sociological fidelity, which "expresses the interactions between learners in order to create authenticity with high levels of social realism", and argues the current physical/psychological understanding may not adequately explain learning in inter-professional simulation.
