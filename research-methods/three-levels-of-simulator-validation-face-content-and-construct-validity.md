# Three levels of simulator validation: face, content and construct validity

## Description
The review organizes simulator validation into "different levels of validation of increasing value and complexity": face validity describes the realism of a simulator, content validity describes the relevance of the simulator content for the real task, and construct validity describes the extent to which groups with more experience perform better than groups with less real-life experience and how far training-session results reflect the trainee's actual skill. The review applies this framework to neonatal echocardiography simulators, reporting that face and content validity have been tested via standardized questionnaires and construct validity by expertise-group comparisons.

The review applies this framework to neonatal echocardiography simulators, reporting that face and content validity have been tested via standardized questionnaires and construct validity by expertise-group comparisons. (Singh Yogen et al. (2025))
- **Face, content and construct validity framework for evaluating surgical simulators**: The review adopts a three-part validity framework as the minimum criteria a simulator should meet to justify widespread use. "Face validity refers to user opinion of a simulator's realism while content validity is based on expert opinion as to how well the simulator's content reflects the necessary skills and knowledge required. Construct validity is demonstrated when nonexperts are outperformed by experts in standardised tasks." The framework is applied throughout to rate VR, bench, cadaveric and 3D-printed simulators, alongside cost-effectiveness and availability considerations. (Chahal Baldev et al. (2023))
- **Face, content, and construct validity framework for surgical simulator validation**: The article employs a three-part validation framework commonly used in the surgical simulation literature: face validity (subjective user satisfaction with realism), content validity (judgment by expert surgeons with at least 10 years of experience about whether the simulator allows learning of basic laparoscopic skills), and construct validity (the ability of the simulator to differentiate operators by their actual surgical experience). The authors state that "for the validation of the LABOT, we used the following validity models: 'face validity,' 'content validity,' and 'construct validity'". (Soriero Domenico et al. (2020))
- **Face validity as questionnaire-based assessment of a simulator's verisimilitude**: Face validity is presented as one type of simulator validation: it "describes the verisimilitude and appropriateness of the simulator's psychomotor fidelity and is assessed through responses to questionnaires by surgeons". Although a subjective measure of how realistically a simulator recreates a procedure, the article frames it as an important step that must be taken to justify financial investment in VR simulators, using a threshold of Likert ratings of 7 or above as an acceptable realism level. (Bartlett Jonathan D. et al. (2018))
