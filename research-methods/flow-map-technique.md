## Description
The flow map technique elicits and quantifies a learner's cognitive structure by recording the student's sequential answers to questions and charting the statements with direct and cross links between them. As the article puts it, "With the flow map, students’ thought patterns are revealed in succession and correlated with each other by cross-links." Four parameters are calculated: size (number of ideas), richness (recurrent linkages), integratedness (richness/(size + richness)), and correctness (misconception rate). In this study flow maps were built from voice-recorded achievement-test responses before and after an 8-week unit, and used to detect misconceptions and track cognitive-structure development.

- **Alluvial diagrams (ggalluvial in R) for tracking individual pre-to-post FCI answer choices, including non-attempt responses**: Alluvial diagrams visualise flows of students' answer choices between the pre-test and post-test states, with strata sized by cluster and flows connecting the two axes. The article states they "highlight the shifts in students' conceptual frameworks and the persistence of certain misconceptions over time". The authors generated them with the R ggalluvial package, and note the method captures non-attempt (NA) responses that previous studies often did not record. They communicate changes without relying on the reader's statistical expertise, targeting educators and policymakers. (Anna Chrysostomou et al. (2024))
