Hierarchy of research evidence in developmental education: experimental, quasiexperimental, nonexperimental, and descriptive designs ranked by rigor¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article describes a hierarchy of evidence, defined largely by quantitative researchers, in which "experimental research designs featuring random assignment are considered the most rigorous, followed by quasiexperimental and nonexperimental research designs; descriptive research is considered the least rigorous methodology." Experimental designs assign a matched experimental group and control group so outcome differences are attributed to the intervention. Quasiexperimental designs, notably regression-discontinuity studies using placement cut scores, apply similar statistical controls without random assignment. The article uses this hierarchy to explain why practitioner-oriented and quantitatively-oriented researchers reach opposing conclusions.
