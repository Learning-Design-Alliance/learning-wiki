## Description
The article develops a replication method for within-study comparisons in settings with control-group noncompliance. Instead of requiring the nonexperimental estimate to match the complier average causal effect, the approach uses "nonexperimental methods to produce an estimate that can be compared to the experimental intent-to-treat (ITT) impact estimate rather than the CACE." This aligns the causal estimands when some control-group members are always-takers who receive treatment anyway.

## Description
The article develops a replication method for within-study comparisons in settings with control-group noncompliance. Instead of requiring the nonexperimental estimate to match the complier average causal effect, the approach uses "nonexperimental methods to produce an estimate that can be compared to the experimental intent-to-treat (ITT) impact estimate rather than the CACE." This aligns the causal estimands when some control-group members are always-takers who receive treatment anyway.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an experiment with random assignment in which the ITT estimate can be computed, and nonexperimental data on the same population including pre-treatment measures
#### Constraints
- Designed for contexts where control-group noncompliance (crossover) produces divergence between the CACE and the nonexperimental estimand

### Target Learners
- students in schools subject to randomized evaluation

### Target Learning Objectives
- accurate estimation of intervention effects on student achievement
