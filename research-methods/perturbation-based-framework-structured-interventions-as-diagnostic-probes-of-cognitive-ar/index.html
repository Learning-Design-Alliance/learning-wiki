Perturbation-based framework: structured interventions as diagnostic probes of cognitive architecture¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article advances a framework in which interventions that alter physiological or motivational states in predictable ways serve as diagnostic tools for probing the organization of cognitive systems, analogous to lesion studies and pharmacological or stimulation manipulations. Its core inferential logic is that "What unites these approaches is not the severity of the manipulation, but its structured nature." When constructs change in parallel, shared mechanisms are implicated; when they decouple, claims of functional unity are weakened. Null effects and selective effects are both treated as theoretically informative outputs of this framework.

Accounts¶

  • Perturbation-based framework: structured interventions as diagnostic probes of cognitive architecture: The article advances a framework in which interventions that alter physiological or motivational states in predictable ways serve as diagnostic tools for probing the organization of cognitive systems, analogous to lesion studies and pharmacological or stimulation manipulations. Its core inferential logic is that "What unites these approaches is not the severity of the manipulation, but its structured nature." When constructs change in parallel, shared mechanisms are implicated; when they decouple, claims of functional unity are weakened. Null effects and selective effects are both treated as theoretically informative outputs of this framework. (David Moreau (2026))
  • Design interventions to maximize theoretical insight, not only efficacy: The article proposes that when interventions are leveraged as probes of cognitive architecture, their design should prioritize interpretability alongside feasibility. It enumerates principles: "First, temporal specificity is critical." — aligning assessment timing with the perturbation's time course; treating content alignment between intervention context and measurement as a design variable; combining broad perturbations with specific measurement; and anticipating null effects in the inferential framework. Dissociations across aligned and non-aligned measures provide direct evidence about contextual recruitment of cognitive processes. (David Moreau (2026))
  • Test cognitive-conflict necessity in simple, successful learning scenarios with manipulation checks and independent tracking of unlearning and re-learning: The article argues that weak effects of cognitive conflict interventions in complex classroom scenarios are uninterpretable because learning has multiple determinants. It recommends that "One response to this problem is to study simple situations in which there are fewer factors that determine the outcome, and those factors can be identified, measured, or controlled." It further recommends studying scenarios where learning is already successful, implementing an accurate manipulation check that learners experienced conflict, and using assessments that track reliance on the prior and new conceptions independently of each other. (Ramsburg et al. (2015))
