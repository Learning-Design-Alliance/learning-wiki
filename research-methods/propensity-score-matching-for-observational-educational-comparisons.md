## Description
The study's analytic approach uses propensity score methodology (Rosenbaum, 2002) to construct a counterfactual comparison group of promoted students similar to retainees on 43 pre-retention covariates. The authors state: "We use two different propensity score methodological approaches to estimate the average treatment effect on the treated: (1) nearest neighbor (one to one) matching without replacement and (2) caliper matching with replacement." Regression-adjusted matched estimates with probability weights and covariate adjustment are planned, and retained students outside the area of common support are excluded.

Regression-adjusted matched estimates with probability weights and covariate adjustment are planned, and retained students outside the area of common support are excluded. (Hwang et al. (2016))
- **Boosted regression and propensity score matching design for estimating persistent pre-K effects**: The study's identification strategy uses boosted modeling to estimate propensity scores, then nearest neighbor one-to-one matching with replacement to select comparison children never exposed to Tulsa pre-K, followed by frequency-weighted OLS with standard errors clustered by child. The article notes boosted regression "can use all available covariates and is not subject to the particular modeling choices made by the analyst". Separate propensity models were estimated for each outcome, cohort, and subsample, with covariate balance assessed and multiple imputation for missing covariates. (Carolyn J. Hill et al. (2015))
