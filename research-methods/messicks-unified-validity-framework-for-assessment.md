## Description
The commentary grounds its critique in Messick's unified validity framework, which "conceptualizes validity as integrated sources of evidence encompassing construct representation, sources of construct-irrelevant variance and the social consequences of score use." Within this framework, speededness represents a systematic source of construct-irrelevant variance targeting response process and consequential validity, undermining equity without enhancing inference about clinical competence. The framework is used to argue that time limits reduce assessment validity while adding inequity.

- **Messick's unified validity framework as an organizing structure for simulator-based assessment validation**: The study structures its validity evidence according to Messick's framework, described as "the contemporary standard for assessment validation in medical education", covering five sources: content, response process, internal structure, relations to other variables, and consequences. Each source was operationalized concretely — Delphi-derived metrics for content, standardized instructions and automated capture for response process, Cronbach's alpha for internal structure, group contrasts for relations, and a CGM pass/fail standard for consequences. (Jacobsen Mads Emil et al. (2026))
- **Messick's five-aspect validity framework as an organizing structure for gathering peer-assessment validity evidence**: The article employs Messick's validity framework as the systematic structure for collecting validity evidence about peer assessment in PtBL. It organizes evidence into five aspects: "1) Content, ensuring test items align with the intended construct through established instruments and expert reviews; 2) Response Process, emphasizing data integrity, clear instructions, and comprehensive rater training; 3) Internal Structure, assessed through psychometric measures"; 4) relations with other variables; and 5) consequences. Each Results section maps onto one aspect. (Lertsakulbunlue Sethapong; Kantiwong Anupong (2025))
- **Messick's validity framework (internal structure, relations to other variables, response process) as the organizing structure for assessing OSAUS-HMD**: The study organizes its psychometric evaluation of the OSAUS scale for ultrasound HMD competence using Messick's category framework, analyzed "according to three domains (internal structure, relation to other variables and response process)". Internal structure covers internal consistency and inter-rater reliability; relations to other variables covers criterion-related comparisons with GRS and expertise groups; response process covers rater training, blinding and bias-reduction strategies. The content and consequences domains were not explored. (Gomes Sara Hora et al. (2023))
- **Messick's five-source validity framework as the organizing structure for VR assessment validation**: The study structures its validity investigation around Messick's framework, which "consists of 5 sources of validity evidence regarding content, response process, internal structure, relations to other variables, and consequences." Each source is addressed with a dedicated analysis: expert-written curriculum-based scenarios for content, standardized instructions and equipment for response process, Cronbach α for internal structure, between-group score comparisons for relations to other variables, and contrasting-groups standard setting with false-positive/false-negative reporting for consequences. This framework serves as the article's organizing taxonomy for gathering validity evidence before implementing VR-based assessment. (Marie Høxbro Knudsen et al. (2023))
