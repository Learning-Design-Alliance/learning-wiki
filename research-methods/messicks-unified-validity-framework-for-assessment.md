## Description
The commentary grounds its critique in Messick's unified validity framework, which "conceptualizes validity as integrated sources of evidence encompassing construct representation, sources of construct-irrelevant variance and the social consequences of score use." Within this framework, speededness represents a systematic source of construct-irrelevant variance targeting response process and consequential validity, undermining equity without enhancing inference about clinical competence. The framework is used to argue that time limits reduce assessment validity while adding inequity.

Each source was operationalized concretely — Delphi-derived metrics for content, standardized instructions and automated capture for response process, Cronbach's alpha for internal structure, group contrasts for relations, and a CGM pass/fail standard for consequences. (Jacobsen Mads Emil et al. (2026))

It organizes evidence into five aspects: "1) Content, ensuring test items align with the intended construct through established instruments and expert reviews; 2) Response Process, emphasizing data integrity, clear instructions, and comprehensive rater training; 3) Internal Structure, assessed through psychometric measures"; 4) relations with other variables; and 5) consequences. Each Results section maps onto one aspect. (Lertsakulbunlue Sethapong; Kantiwong Anupong (2025))
