# Funneling-versus-focusing questioning-pattern coding scheme

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper operationalizes two contrasting teacher questioning patterns drawn from the math education literature (NCTM, 2014; Herbel-Eisenmann and Breyfogle, 2005). Focusing means "attending to what the students are thinking, pressing them to communicate their thoughts clearly, and expecting them to reﬂect on their thoughts and those of their classmates", treating student contributions as resources for collective sensemaking. Funneling is "a set of questions to lead students to a desired procedure or conclusion, while giving limited attention to student responses that veer from the desired path". The paper develops a coding instrument requiring an utterance to relate to math, follow up on the previous student utterance, and include a question.
