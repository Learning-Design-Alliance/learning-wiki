# Taxonomy of four graphical vector addition methods with representational features expected to encourage or discourage each

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines four graphical vector addition methods — head-to-tail, components, bisector, and general direction — by the observable solution behavior they involve, and lists in Table 1 the representational changes (contact versus separated vectors, grid presence, symmetry, arrangement type) expected to encourage or discourage each method's use. For example, head-to-tail use is encouraged by a "Head-to-tail arrangement" and discouraged when "Vectors are separated ( not touching)" or "on different grids." The framework structured the interview design and the categorization of student responses.
