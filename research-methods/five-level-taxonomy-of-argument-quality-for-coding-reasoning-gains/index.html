Five-level taxonomy of argument quality for coding reasoning gains¶

Research Method · All research methods

Description¶

The review codes argumentative reasoning gains on five levels combining structural and functional elements: main (claims/reasons), elaborated (evidence, questions, TAP structure), complex (internal relationships and quality of evidence/justification), dialogic (transactivity, critique, rebuttal), and dialectic (revision or integration of alternative theories, quality of counterarguments, critical questions). The authors state: "we had previously identified five types or levels of arguments, comprising both structural and functional elements, namely: main, elaborated, complex, dialogic, and dialectic". Table 1 details the codes per level, e.g., level 4 codes T, CAR, CAR/E for dialogic argument.

Accounts¶

  • Five-level taxonomy of argument quality for coding reasoning gains: The review codes argumentative reasoning gains on five levels combining structural and functional elements: main (claims/reasons), elaborated (evidence, questions, TAP structure), complex (internal relationships and quality of evidence/justification), dialogic (transactivity, critique, rebuttal), and dialectic (revision or integration of alternative theories, quality of counterarguments, critical questions). The authors state: "we had previously identified five types or levels of arguments, comprising both structural and functional elements, namely: main, elaborated, complex, dialogic, and dialectic". Table 1 details the codes per level, e.g., level 4 codes T, CAR, CAR/E for dialogic argument. (Chrysi Rapanta et al. (2022))
  • Five-level analytical framework for assessing argumentation quality (Toulmin-based): The study scored student-written arguments using the framework of Erduran, Simon, and Osborne (2004), built on the Toulmin Model in which "data, claim, and warrants form the basis of an argument". Arguments were first resolved into Toulmin components, then assigned to five levels: Level 1 is a simple claim versus counterclaim; Level 2 adds data, warrants, or backings without rebuttals; Level 3 includes weak rebuttals; Level 4 has a clearly identifiable rebuttal; Level 5 displays more than one rebuttal. In this study only Levels 1-4 were used because no student reports reached Level 5, and levels were scored 1-4 for t-test analysis. (Tuba Demircioglu (2015))
