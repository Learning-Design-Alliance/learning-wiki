# Four-type validity framework for evaluating surgical training models

## Description
The review organises its evaluation of body donor models around four validity types drawn from simulation literature: "face validity, construct validity, content validity, and predictive validity". Face validity concerns resemblance to a real-life procedure, construct validity the ability to distinguish experience levels, content validity the capacity to enhance knowledge and skills across settings, and predictive validity the translation of improved skills to real-life clinical settings. The review sorts its 55 included studies into groups by which validity type they assess, and uses this framework to diagnose where evidence is strongest (face validity) and weakest (predictive validity).

- **Face, content, and construct validity framework for evaluating simulation models as educational tools**: The review organises simulator evaluation around three validity types: "Face validity examines the realism of a simulator and difficulty level similarity in comparison to real training tasks, whilst content validity assesses the model's effectiveness during a specific skill training to improve participants' techniques". Construct validity is the objective method "that considers the ability of a simulator to differentiate levels of skill competence". The review applies this framework to cadaver, animal/placenta, synthetic, and VR models across four skull-base procedures, and names predictive validity as an ultimate, unassessed fourth type. (Chawla Shreya et al. (2022))
