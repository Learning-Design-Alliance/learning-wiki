## Description
The review organizes the 54 included studies' educational interventions along stated dimensions: strategies were presented "by serial cue or whole clinical cases that presented as process-oriented or knowledge-oriented". It also charts intervention duration, number of clinical cases, and whether the same cases were used for pretest, intervention and posttest, providing a dimensional map of the intervention space for clinical reasoning teaching in undergraduate medical education.

- **Serial-cue versus whole-case distinction in paper-based clinical reasoning instruction**: The article adopts Schmidt and Mamede's distinction between two families of instructional approaches for paper-based clinical cases. In the serial-cue approach, "Information about the patient only becomes sequentially available in the course of a student’s engagement with a case", instantiated as hypothetico-deduction; in the whole-case approach, "the case in full" is presented before students engage, instantiated as self-explanation. The organizing dimension is whether case information is disclosed step-by-step or available from the start. (Al Rumayyan Ahmed et al. (2018))
