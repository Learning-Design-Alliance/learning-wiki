# Semantic similarity heuristic for interpreting Cronbach's alpha

## Description
The article proposes a heuristic for judging when Cronbach's alpha can be read as a reliability index: researchers should examine how semantically similar a test's items are. When items vary only in wording but not content, as in the Satisfaction with Life Scale, wording variation cancels measurement error across items and alpha is meaningful. The article states: "a good heuristic is to examine semantic similarity among items: the higher the similarity, the more accurately alpha indicates reliability." For knowledge tests, whose items tap different facets, the heuristic signals that alpha should not be read as reliability.
