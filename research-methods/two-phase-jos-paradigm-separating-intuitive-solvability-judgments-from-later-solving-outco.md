# Two-phase JOS paradigm separating intuitive solvability judgments from later solving outcomes

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The two-phase paradigm separates the JOS phase, where participants make intuitive solvability judgments for an entire set of problems, from a subsequent solving phase where solving outcomes and effort regulation are measured. This design avoids the confound of interleaved paradigms, in which "JOSs may be influenced by solving attempts, and vice versa", allowing reasoners to bootstrap their JOS intuitions. The article uses this paradigm across three experiments with anagram durations parametrically manipulated across blocks.
