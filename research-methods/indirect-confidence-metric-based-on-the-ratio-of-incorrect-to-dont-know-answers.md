# Indirect confidence metric based on the ratio of incorrect to Don't Know answers

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a non-self-reported, non-comparative measure of confidence in knowledge: "the ratio of Incorrect to “Don’t Know” answers in any knowledge questionnaire" with a True/False/Don't Know format. The rationale is that an incorrect answer corresponds to an overestimation of one's knowledge, so the normalized proportion of incorrect answers over all non-correct answers serves as a calibration error, ranging from 0 (no incorrect answers) to 1 (only incorrect answers). It was applied to five surveys and validated against a direct overestimation measure in the Lackner survey.
