# Multi-channel sequence analysis with mixture hidden Markov models

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The article offers a detailed methodology for studying how engagement and achievement evolve together across a full program: two chronologically ordered state sequences (engagement and achievement channels) are aligned per course, and a Mixture Hidden Markov Model — "a combination of LCA and HMM" — clusters multichannel trajectories jointly while estimating transition probabilities between hidden states. The method models "several variables evolving in parallel" and reveals for whom and when engagement-achievement relations change across a program.

- **Behavior modeling of MOOC students as session-level time distributions clustered by MMM and HMM**: The article defines a session as "a sequence of clicks from one student separated from that student's other click sequences by more than one hour of inactivity" and a behavior as a distribution over clicks within a session. Behaviors are clustered into ten states with a multinomial mixture model (static) or a hidden Markov model (dynamic, modeling transitions between successive sessions). The learned HMM states correspond to interpretable behaviors such as lecture-focused, quiz-focused, forum-focused, and browsing sessions, with transition probabilities showing quiz and forum sessions tend to follow themselves. (Yohan Jo et al. (2018))
