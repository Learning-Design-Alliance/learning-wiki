# A priori intervention-and-consequence specification for educational causal analysis

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The paper's central methodological recommendation is that researchers begin with an explicit question about the potential consequences of potential actions before choosing correlations or models. The author argues that in all the critiqued papers "there was no clear specification of which actual interventions and consequences would be considered", and that implicit assumptions were used to justify applying causal language to collections of correlations. Imagining a randomized controlled trial, even when infeasible, helps clarify the question, and causal graphs then guide which combinations of correlations best estimate the effects of possible actions.
