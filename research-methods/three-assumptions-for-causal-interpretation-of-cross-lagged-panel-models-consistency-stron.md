# Three assumptions for causal interpretation of cross-lagged panel models: consistency, strong ignorability, and positivity

## Description
Drawing on the Rubin causal model and potential outcomes, the article specifies the assumptions under which cross-lagged coefficients may be read causally. It states that "Usami et al. (2019a) outlined three assumptions that need to be satisfied in order for CLPMs to identify causal effects: (a) consistency, (b) strong ignorability/no unobserved confounding, and (c) positivity". Consistency ties observed outcomes to potential outcomes, strong ignorability requires all confounders to be measured, and positivity requires covariate overlap across treatment values. The article argues these are often unrealistic in REM research.
