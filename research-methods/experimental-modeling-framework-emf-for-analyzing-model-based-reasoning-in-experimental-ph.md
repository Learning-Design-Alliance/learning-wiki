## Description
The EMF describes how physicists iteratively revise system models, physical apparatus, and measurement tools in response to discrepant measurements and predictions. It defines models as "abstract representations used to explain aspects of the real world and predict scientific phenomena" and divides systems into a Physical System and a Measurement System. Modeling proceeds through five phases: Model Construction, Prediction, Comparison, Proposal, and Revision. The authors use it as an a priori analysis scheme for think-aloud troubleshooting interviews.

- **Experimental Modeling Framework (EMF) for physicists' model-based reasoning about experimental systems**: The EMF describes how physicists iteratively revise system models, physical apparatus, and measurement tools in response to discrepant measurements and predictions. It defines models as "abstract representations used to explain aspects of the real world and predict scientific phenomena" and divides systems into a Physical System and a Measurement System. Modeling proceeds through five phases: Model Construction, Prediction, Comparison, Proposal, and Revision. The authors use it as an a priori analysis scheme for think-aloud troubleshooting interviews. (Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer et al. (2015))
- **Experimental modeling framework for constructing, testing, and refining models of measurement and physical systems**: The article uses a framework describing the iterative process of experimental modeling in physics, adapted from Zwickl et al. The framework describes "the iterative process of constructing models of the measurement and physical systems, comparing measurements to predictions, proposing reﬁnements to address discrepan- cies, and making revisions to models and/or apparatuses." It includes model construction for both measurement and physical systems, abstraction between interpretation and prediction, and a comparison step that triggers proposals for revision. The authors used this framework as a guide to rewrite all lab guides, making modeling steps explicit early in the semester and fading guidance later. (H. J. Lewandowski et al. (2015))
- **Modeling Framework for Experimental Physics**: The Modeling Framework describes the dynamic process through which experimental physicists develop and refine models and apparatus. It subdivides the target system into a physical system and a measurement system, each with its own model, and describes modeling as involving phases of "model construction, interpretation, prediction, comparison, proposal, and revision." Resolving discrepancies between prediction and measurement requires revising along one of "four pathways of revision": refine the measurement system model, measurement system apparatus, physical system apparatus, or physical system model. The article uses this framework as an a priori coding scheme for students’ model-based reasoning while troubleshooting a circuit. (Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer et al. (2016))
- **Modeling Framework for Experimental Physics (EMF)**: The Modeling Framework for Experimental Physics is a diagrammatic framework capturing the components of model-based reasoning in lab settings. Its major feature is "consideration for the measurement apparatus, in addition to the physical system", with both sub-systems given equal representation. The process runs from model construction (principles, concepts, limitations/assumptions, parameters) through prediction and interpretation of data, to a comparison evaluated for sufficient agreement, and, if agreement is inadequate, to four distinct revision pathways forming a recursive cycle. In this article the EMF is used both to guide the design of transformed lab activities and as a coding scheme to assess students' documented modeling in lab notebooks. (Jacob T. Stanley et al. (2017))
- **Modeling Framework for Experimental Physics**: The Modeling Framework describes the cyclic process physicists employ when refining models and apparatuses. Its subtasks are to "make measurements, construct models of both the measurement and physical system apparatuses, make comparisons between data and predictions, propose causes for discrepancies between data and predictions," and enact revisions. It distinguishes measurement system models, used to interpret data, from physical system models, used to make predictions. In this study its subtasks served as first-pass interview codes. (Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer et al. (2017))
