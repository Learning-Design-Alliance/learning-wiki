## Description
The article uses "Constraint-Based Modelling (CBM) to model the domain knowledge and generate student models". The authors had previously applied CBM in tutors teaching SQL and English punctuation rules, and present KERMIT as evidence that CBM "can be used to support students learning design tasks, which are very different from domains we dealt with in earlier tutors". - **The IRT+CBM framework treats constraints as psychometric items, enabling data-driven assessment of constraint quality**: The article proposes a framework that combines Constraint-Based Modeling with Item Response Theory, based on an analogy between constraints and items: both are 'measurement instruments that, though in different environments, collect evidence of knowledge.' A Constraint Characteristic Curve is defined for each constraint, representing the probability of satisfying it as a function of knowledge, allowing IRT mechanisms such as calibration and information analysis to be applied in CBM tutors. The framework replaces heuristic estimates of constraint mastery with a well-founded probabilistic technique. (Gálvez et al. (2013))
