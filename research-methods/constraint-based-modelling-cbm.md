- **Constraint-Based Modelling as a domain-modelling and student-modelling approach extended to design tasks**: The article uses "Constraint-Based Modelling (CBM) to model the domain knowledge and generate student models". The authors had previously applied CBM in tutors teaching SQL and English punctuation rules, and present KERMIT as evidence that CBM "can be used to support students learning design tasks, which are very different from domains we dealt with in earlier tutors". CBM thus functions in the paper as a reusable framework for domain and student modelling across qualitatively different task types. (Pramuditha Suraweera (2004))
- **The IRT+CBM framework treats constraints as psychometric items, enabling data-driven assessment of constraint quality**: The article proposes a framework that combines Constraint-Based Modeling with Item Response Theory, based on an analogy between constraints and items: both are 'measurement instruments that, though in different environments, collect evidence of knowledge.' A Constraint Characteristic Curve is defined for each constraint, representing the probability of satisfying it as a function of knowledge, allowing IRT mechanisms such as calibration and information analysis to be applied in CBM tutors. The framework replaces heuristic estimates of constraint mastery with a well-founded probabilistic technique. (Gálvez et al. (2013))
- **Constraint-Based Modeling with ideal solutions for tutoring ill-defined design tasks**: The article describes the constraint-based approach used by KERMIT and EER-Tutor for teaching conceptual database design, an ill-defined task with huge solution spaces. Instead of requiring a problem solver, the tutor checks whether the student's schema is correct by matching it to constraints and a pre-specified ideal solution: "The ideal solution captures the semantics of the problem without the need for a problem solver". Syntactic constraints check ER-model syntax while semantic constraints compare the solution to the problem requirements, recognizing alternative correct solutions by looking for equivalent ways of specifying ER components. (Antonija Mitrovic et al. (2016))
- **Constraint-based modelling (CBM): student modelling via constraints on correct solutions**: CBM is a student modelling approach proposed by Ohlsson as a way to overcome limitations of earlier approaches such as bug libraries. The article states that "A constraint-based tutor only needs a set of constraints that describe features of correct solutions", eliminating the need for bug libraries and for a runnable domain module. Because CBM does not require a problem solver, it "can be applied to open-ended tasks for which there is no algorithmic solution or executable expert model". SQL-Tutor was built to test whether CBM was as promising as it seemed, and the approach was later adopted by many researchers beyond the authors' group. (Mitrovic et al. (2015))
