M3 evaluation framework (Micro, Meso, Macro) for mobile learning interventions¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The study adopts Vavoula and Sharples' (2011) M3 evaluation framework, which provides a "structured format to assess usability, educational and organisational impact and their inter-relationships". Micro-level evaluates individual user experience of the technology via end-activity evaluations; meso-level examines student experience via group and teacher interviews; macro-level evaluates effects on attitudes and performance via pre-post designs with experimental and control groups. The authors report it was "valuable for data validation and triangulation".

Accounts¶

  • M3 evaluation framework (Micro, Meso, Macro) for mobile learning interventions: The study adopts Vavoula and Sharples' (2011) M3 evaluation framework, which provides a "structured format to assess usability, educational and organisational impact and their inter-relationships". Micro-level evaluates individual user experience of the technology via end-activity evaluations; meso-level examines student experience via group and teacher interviews; macro-level evaluates effects on attitudes and performance via pre-post designs with experimental and control groups. The authors report it was "valuable for data validation and triangulation". (Fabian et al. (2018))
  • M3 evaluation framework for mobile learning across micro, meso and macro levels: The M3 evaluation framework structures evaluation of mobile learning technology at three levels: micro (usability and utility of individual activities), meso (the learning experience as a whole, including learning breakthroughs and breakdowns), and macro (longer-term impact on established educational and learning practice). It operates through a three-stage process of capturing user expectations, capturing reality through observation, and examining gaps between them. The article applies it throughout the Myartspace project lifecycle, from requirements analysis through design, implementation and deployment. (Vavoula et al. (2009))
