Four-process framework for collaborative problem solving in ill-structured STEM tasks (P1–P4)¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article operationalizes Ge and Land's four problem-solving processes for ill-structured engineering tasks: P1 (exploring and representing the problem, including joint FBD construction), P2 (planning how to solve, considering multiple approaches), P3 (attempting to solve with justifications), and P4 (evaluating the solution and considering alternatives). The framework holds that "Quality verbal interactions in collaborative problem solving can trigger these four essential processes that engage groups in more effective problem solving." An idealized distribution (0.5 P3, 0.25 P4, 0.125 each P1/P2) is proposed as a target for group engagement.

Claims¶

  • Groups' distribution of the four problem-solving processes deviated less from the ideal distribution (lower Gini coefficients) on scaffolded than non-scaffolded tasks [+M]
  • Scaffolded ill-structured tasks elicit a higher proportion of solution-evaluation (P4) turns and a lower proportion of problem-solving (P3) turns than non-scaffolded tasks [+M]
