Independent replication of educational intervention studies¶

Research Method · All research methods

Description¶

The editor argues, quoting Wainer, that replicability is the principal epistemological tenet of modern science: small initial studies tolerate false positives, and other investigators should rush to replicate so anomalous results are brushed aside. He suggests educational psychology should "demonstrate the stability of potentially useful interventions through independent replication" and states that EDPR encourages such replications.

Accounts¶

  • Demonstrate the stability of potentially useful interventions through independent replication before trusting them: The editor argues, quoting Wainer, that replicability is the principal epistemological tenet of modern science: small initial studies tolerate false positives, and other investigators should rush to replicate so anomalous results are brushed aside. He suggests educational psychology should "demonstrate the stability of potentially useful interventions through independent replication" and states that EDPR encourages such replications. (Daniel H. Robinson (2012))
  • Replication studies in educational psychology should use a multi-experiment structure that first replicates the original method and then extends it to identify boundary conditions of the effect: The editorial sets out the journal's expectation for replication manuscripts: a successful replication article requires at least three experiments, the first reproducing the original study's method with only participants changed, later ones extending the method. The stated aim is that 'we can begin to identify the boundary conditions of the effect'. (Daniel Robinson (2009))
  • Replication studies should be conducted to make educational technology research more scientific and ground large-scale implementation: The editorial argues that replications add confidence in findings, enable generalization beyond the original setting, and can identify biases in the original study. ETR&D "urges researchers to conduct such studies so as to make educational technology research more scientific and provide a firm and convincing foundation for large-scale implementations and impact studies." (J. Michael Spector et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • A brief online empathic-discipline exercise for teachers halved suspensions in one randomized field experiment, while its preregistered replication found a small, marginal overall effect but narrowed group disparities [+S]
  • A brief values-affirmation writing exercise raised African American seventh-graders' grades in two small field experiments, but a large same-district replication found no effect [+M]
  • Education research has had negligible impact on educational practice, a pattern confirmed across multiple reviews [+M]
