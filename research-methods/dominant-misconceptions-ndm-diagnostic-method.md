# Dominant misconceptions (nDM) diagnostic method

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
A diagnostic method for FCI pre-test answers in which each question's largest incorrect response is normalised and questions above a threshold are flagged as dominant misconceptions. The authors apply it to three UJ cohorts, where 'The DMs are normalised by subtracting away the %(correct answer) and a threshold is chosen where anything above this 'value' is deemed a DM.'

<!-- How each source describes or uses the method -->
- **Dominant misconceptions (nDM) diagnostic method for FCI pre-test answers**: A diagnostic method for FCI pre-test answers in which each question's largest incorrect response is normalised and questions above a threshold are flagged as dominant misconceptions. The authors apply it to three UJ cohorts, where 'The DMs are normalised by subtracting away the %(correct answer) and a threshold is chosen where anything above this 'value' is deemed a DM.' (Chrysostomou et al. (2023))
- **Normalised dominant misconception (nDM) measure with a 50% threshold for identifying dominant incorrect FCI responses without paired data**: The nDM is a normalisation of the largest incorrect FCI answer choice, computed per question from five options plus non-attempt (NA), with a threshold (chosen as 50% here, following Martín-Blas et al. and Bani-Salameh) above which an incorrect choice is deemed dominant. The article states "this normalisation allows for the identification of DMs even in the absence of paired data". It amplifies misconceptions drowned out by correct answers and exposes subdominant ones, and can be applied at pre-test alone. (Anna Chrysostomou et al. (2024))
