# Early-warning indicator construction

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A Consortium report cited in the paper outlines five characteristics of effective indicators: predictiveness, usability and clarity, real time/right time availability, direct causal linkage to outcomes, and malleability. Predictiveness alone is insufficient — researchers must also show which indicators are less predictive and therefore poor targets — and even rigorously predictive indicators fail if they are not understandable, simple, and applicable in schools, since complexity that becomes a barrier to action undermines use. Indicators should be available in real time or at the right time, should have a direct causal linkage so that improving them changes the ultimate outcome, and must be malleable, meaning educators can move them with known strategies. The paper grounds the framework in the assumption that indicators' predictive power is practical rather than deterministic: indicators estimate likely outcomes to trigger support, with the goal that the predicted outcome does not correspond with the student's actual outcome.

(Moeller et al. (2018))
- **Early warning and college readiness indicator systems: data organized into advance predictions of attainment to trigger strategic action**: The report defines an indicator system as more than data: "At its most basic, an indicator provides a prediction of educational attainment (e.g., high school graduation, college graduation) well in advance of the outcome." Predictions are estimates that let practitioners change a student's trajectory, and only strategic use of information within data systems turns a piece of data into an indicator. The report distinguishes early warning indicators, which signal likelihood of high school graduation, from college readiness indicators, which signal likelihood of obtaining a college degree, and describes three uses: identifying students in need of intervention, focusing and assessing school improvement, and holding schools accountable. (Allensworth et al. (2018))
- **Taxonomy of indicators of distress and indicators of failure in charter schools**: The report distinguishes leading 'indicators of distress' — characteristics occurring early in a school's decline that 'tend to be more difficult to measure and easier to influence than lagging indicators' — from lagging 'indicators of failure,' output data points occurring later that are easier to measure but 'require more substantive interventions to influence.' Distress indicators are organized into seven categories: leadership, governing board, operations, finance, talent, culture, and instruction, derived from a literature review and authorizer perspectives, and categorized using the Four Domains for Rapid School Improvement framework adapted to the charter context. (National Charter School Resource Center et al. (2020))
- **Early warning system methodology applied to school-level indicators of distress**: The report proposes adapting early warning system methodology — commonly used to identify students at risk of not completing high school — to school-level indicators. An early warning system is defined as 'a process for identifying patterns and characteristics from previous events that turned out to be risky, testing those patterns in a local context to identify specific indicators and thresholds for risk, and then using the characteristics in a systemic way to identify scenarios of risk and to efficiently target interventions.' This report is presented as only a first step toward such a system for schools in distress. (National Charter School Resource Center et al. (2020))
- **Early warning system process for identifying school risk patterns and thresholds**: The report defines an early warning system as "a process for identifying patterns and characteristics from previous events that turned out to be risky, testing those patterns in a local context to identify specific indicators and thresholds for risk, and then using the characteristics in a systemic way to identify scenarios of risk and to efficiently target interventions." The toolkit applies this process to charter school authorizing, helping authorizers spot declining schools before failure. (Aimee Evan et al. (2021))
- **Leading indicators of distress versus lagging indicators of failure**: The report distinguishes two kinds of school-quality signals. Indicators of distress are "characteristics that occur early in a school's decline suggesting a school that is struggling to achieve or maintain high levels of quality," while indicators of failure are "output data points that occur later in a school's decline to measure how a school performed." Distress indicators are harder to measure but easier to influence; failure indicators are easier to measure but require more substantive interventions. (Aimee Evan et al. (2021))
- **Predictive-model approach using school and child welfare agency data to flag near-term academic risk**: The report describes an approach for developing a predictive model that identifies students likely to have near-term academic problems, including absenteeism, suspensions, poor grades, and low performance on state tests. It is intended for administrators, researchers, and student support staff in local education agencies who want to identify at-risk students early. The model draws on data shared between schools and child welfare agencies. (Julie Bruch et al. (2020))
