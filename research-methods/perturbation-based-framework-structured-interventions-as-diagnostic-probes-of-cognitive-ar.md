## Description
The article advances a framework in which interventions that alter physiological or motivational states in predictable ways serve as diagnostic tools for probing the organization of cognitive systems, analogous to lesion studies and pharmacological or stimulation manipulations. Its core inferential logic is that "What unites these approaches is not the severity of the manipulation, but its structured nature." When constructs change in parallel, shared mechanisms are implicated; when they decouple, claims of functional unity are weakened. Null effects and selective effects are both treated as theoretically informative outputs of this framework.
