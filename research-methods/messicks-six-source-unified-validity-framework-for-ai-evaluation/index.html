Messick's six-source unified validity framework for AI evaluation¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article anchors its validity discussion in Messick's unified theory of validity, which holds that validity is a property of inferences and decisions based on scores, not of instruments. It enumerates six interlocking evidence sources (content, substantive, structural, generalizability, external, and consequential) and gives each an AI analogue, such as item-level factor analysis for structural evidence and out-of-distribution testing for generalizability evidence.

Accounts¶

  • Messick's unified validity framework applied to AI evaluation, with six sources of construct-validity evidence: The article anchors its validity discussion in Messick's unified theory of validity, which holds that validity is a property of inferences and decisions based on scores, not of instruments. It enumerates six interlocking evidence sources (content, substantive, structural, generalizability, external, and consequential) and gives each an AI analogue, such as item-level factor analysis for structural evidence and out-of-distribution testing for generalizability evidence. (Peng Wang (2026))
  • Messick's unified construct-validity framework as a six-domain framework for appraising surgical assessment tools: Messick's unified theory of validity, as applied in this review, appraises assessment quality across six domains: "content, response process, internal structure, generalisability, relations to other variables, and consequences". The review graded each domain as reported-limited, reported-partial, reported-strong, or not reported, and used the framework to compare manual, kinematic, and AI-based tools within a common structure. It was chosen to enable multidimensional evaluation beyond isolated performance metrics, which the authors argue is particularly important for AI systems where internal accuracy may be robust while educational and safety implications remain untested. (Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A. et al. (2026))
  • Messick's five sources of validity evidence as the charting framework for assessment validity: The review categorizes validity evidence using the five sources from Messick's framework and the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing: content, response process, internal structure, relations with other variables, and consequences. In contemporary frameworks, "validity is a characteristic of the proposed interpretation of scores" for a specific assessment use rather than a property of the assessment tool itself. Each study's evidence was rated on a four-point scale adapted from Ghaderi et al. and Beckman et al. (Tappan Rachel S. et al. (2026))
