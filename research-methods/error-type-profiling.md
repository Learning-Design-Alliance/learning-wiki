## Description
Error-type profiling is the analytic lens the article introduces to judge fairness interventions by how they redistribute false positives and false negatives across groups, rather than only by whether fairness metrics converge. The article introduces it as "a lens that translates fairness metrics into the terms advisors actually encounter: which students appear on caseloads and which are never seen." With predicted-successful as the positive class, a false negative is a successful student predicted at-risk who occupies a scarce advising slot and risks stigmatization, while a false positive is a struggling student predicted successful who never appears on any advisor's caseload.

- **Error Importance Model Accounting for Carelessness in Automated Assessment**: Not all errors students make are of the same importance, and not all are due to lack of knowledge. Some are due to carelessness or inattention. The system distinguishes two types of single-error answers: Type I (only one operation required to achieve matching) and Type II (only one node relabeling operation for two consecutive nodes that have been switched). Special edit operations (Second_Node_Insertion, Second_Node_Relabeling, Goal_Node_Relabeling) carry a cost of 2 versus 1 for basic operations, reflecting that errors in selecting the second node or goal node are considered more serious. (Grivokostopoulou et al. (2016))

It states: "Errors were categorized according to the operative phase: preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative." Tables 1–3 enumerate specific errors per phase, e.g. empty grasp, backwall suture, tight suture tear, and crushing patency tests. (Esanu V et al. (2026))
- **Error analysis as a diagnostic technique for recognizing, classifying and interpreting learner errors**: The article adopts error analysis, operationalized through its error analysis system (EAS), as the diagnostic framework underlying constructive feedback.
