# Adapted conjecture map framework for learning scientists and computer scientists

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 2 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
An adaptation of Sandoval's conjecture mapping tool, created by Chang and Roschelle, that supports interdisciplinary design-based research teams in connecting learning theory to technical design. It distinguishes back-end technical decisions (e.g., training data for an AI model) from embodied front-end decisions users directly interact with, and it explicitly includes both beneficial and harmful consequences of technology as embodiments or mediating processes. In this report the framework structured a one-hour collaborative session in which the PECAS Mediator team filled out the map together on a Jamboard template.

- **Conjecture Mapping operationalized for physics assessment task design**: Conjecture mapping is a design-based research technique that conceptualizes how a learning environment functions by relating design features, participant processes, and intended outcomes. The paper operationalizes it for physics task design: the four task features form the embodiment, the sensemaking elements are the mediating processes, and the cognitive outcomes (new knowledge, sophisticated epistemology, enhanced content understanding) are the outcomes. The paper states the technique highlights relationships through six elements including a high-level conjecture, embodiment, mediating processes, outcomes, design conjectures, and theoretical conjectures. (Amogh Sirnoorkar et al. (2023))
- **Conjecture mapping: an operational procedure tool for articulating and testing design conjectures in design-based research**: Conjecture mapping, attributed to Sandoval within the design-based research tradition, is presented as "an influential and clearly defined operational procedure tool" for making an intervention's theoretical basis explicit. The article states that "Conjecture maps function as a logic grammar through which learning scientists can articulate the theoretical and design conjectures embedded in a learning environment, while also guiding methodological choices to examine how these conjectures manifest in practice." They link high-level theoretical commitments, specific environment features (tools, materials, tasks, participant structures), mediating processes, and desired learning outcomes. The article specifies they are tools for conducting research on how environments support learning, not for designing the environments themselves. (Coelho et al. (2025))
- **Modified conjecture mapping with valence tracking for affect-centered design analysis**: This article adapts Sandoval's conjecture mapping for retrospective analysis of affect. It renames embodiments as design elements (resources, course structure, ethos), broadens mediating processes to include student experiences alongside student artifacts and student interactions, and creates separate conjecture maps for instructor intent and student impact. Its key novelty is valence: "tracking whether the connection has a positive or negative underlying tone, known as valence", so that hedonic variation in how a student enacts design and process conjectures can be represented without letting positive and negative affect cancel out. (Sarah McHale et al. (2026))
- **Narrative-form conjecture mapping with design elements, enactment processes, and valence-coded connections**: This analytic method adapts Sandoval's conjecture mapping for narrative analysis of individual student experiences. Rather than iteratively revising one map, the authors "create a conjecture map for each narrative that describes a different perspective on the same multi-day activity" and analyze why the paths to outcomes differ. Embodiments are recategorized as design elements (Resources, Activity Structure, Ethos), mediating processes as enactment processes (Student Artifacts, Student Interactions, Student Experiences), and outcomes are PCL and PI components, with connections coded for positive or negative valence. (Sarah McHale et al. (2026))
