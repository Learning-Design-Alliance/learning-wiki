## Description
The editorial's closing design recommendation for CSCL research: computer-supported collaborative learning opens a multi-dimensional shared world in an evolving, co-created context, where "Knowledge, roles, space, and time are not simple givens whose characteristics can be assumed". Each must be studied in each case through detailed analysis of the situated interaction. The editorial adds that many research questions can still be rigorously pursued within established perspectives, and the journal welcomes both kinds of studies.

- **Study knowledge, roles, space, and time through detailed analysis of situated interaction rather than assuming their characteristics**: The editorial's closing design recommendation for CSCL research: computer-supported collaborative learning opens a multi-dimensional shared world in an evolving, co-created context, where "Knowledge, roles, space, and time are not simple givens whose characteristics can be assumed". Each must be studied in each case through detailed analysis of the situated interaction. The editorial adds that many research questions can still be rigorously pursued within established perspectives, and the journal welcomes both kinds of studies. (Gerry Stahl et al. (2010))
- **Ethnomethodology as epistemic framing for CSCL research**: Stahl situates ethnomethodology (EM) as a foundational epistemic orientation for CSCL, defining CSCL as the study of joint meaning and grounding CSCL in EM's explicit study of those methods. This defines CSCL's rational grounding in ethnography, where EM defines attention to methods of intersubjectivity, and links CSCL to conversation analysis as a strand of EM focused on sequential analysis of interaction. (Gerry Stahl (2012))
- **Analyze educational technology use from the participants' perspective rather than imposing preconceived codes**: Stahl argues EM rejects coding schemes, predefined hypotheses, and researcher-imposed frameworks; instead research should use thick description to analyze what learners make mean, what participants can see, and how they display mutual meaning as accountable. In CSCL this means studying learner actions and discourse from the learner's perspective without pre-structuring analysis. (Gerry Stahl (2012))
