# Sensitivity analysis across estimators and cluster-level weighting schemes for clustered RCTs

## Description
Because standard errors from alternative estimators of treatment effects in clustered RCTs can differ, the report's key recommendation is that analysts "test the sensitivity of their impact findings using different estimation methods and cluster-level weighting schemes". This guards policy conclusions against dependence on a single estimator choice.

- **Test the sensitivity of RCT impact findings using different estimation methods and cluster-level weighting schemes**: Because standard errors from alternative estimators of treatment effects in clustered RCTs can differ, the report's key recommendation is that analysts "test the sensitivity of their impact findings using different estimation methods and cluster-level weighting schemes". This guards policy conclusions against dependence on a single estimator choice. (Peter Z. Schochet (2009))
- **Align analytic assumptions across methods when comparing impact estimation approaches**: When evaluating whether choice of impact estimation method matters, the article indicates analysts should align the underlying analytic assumptions across the methods being compared. It notes the design- and model-based methods are most similar "when the underlying analytic assumptions (such as the weights used to aggregate clusters and blocks) are aligned". This makes method comparisons interpretable rather than confounded by differing weighting choices. (Tim Kautz et al. (2017))
