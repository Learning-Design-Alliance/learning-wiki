PEA (Policy, Enforcement, and Authority) analytic lens¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

PEA organizes assistance governance around three design questions: what forms of assistance are allowed or restricted (Policy), how those boundaries are implemented (Enforcement), and who can control them during use (Authority). The authors state: "This What–How–Who structure was initially informed by the 5W1H design heuristic". The three dimensions were specified before corpus coding, while subcategories and decision rules were refined iteratively from the 90-system corpus. PEA is offered as a vocabulary for comparing and designing learner-facing LLM programming support systems.

Claims¶

  • Authority over assistance boundaries is highly centralized in system logic in LLM-based programming support systems [+W]
  • Policy–enforcement pairings are dominated by prompt-based constraints, and policy themes differ in enforcement breadth [+W]
  • Prompt-Based Constraint is the dominant enforcement mechanism in LLM-based programming support systems [+W]
  • Policy and enforcement counts were not strongly associated across systems [+W]
