Two-form AI difficulty ruler (solving-based and review-based)¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article distinguishes two forms of the AI ruler. The solving-based ruler has AI read problems, generate code, and submit to the online judge, producing process data such as pass rate, attempts, running time, and error type. The review-based ruler has AI provide structured scores for concept, implementation, debugging, complexity-risk, reading, and overall difficulty without submitting code. The article states the solving-based evidence "constitutes the empirical basis for choosing a unified reviewer in the subsequent review-based extension," while the review-based form is low-cost and scalable but lacks real judging feedback.

Claims¶

  • Review-based AI overall difficulty correlates strongly with problem-level student pass rate across 79 parallel-class exam problems [+M]
  • AI review difficulty also correlates with problem-level non-attempt rate, providing process evidence beyond pass rate [+W]
  • The same review ruler supports longitudinal cross-semester problem tracking for one teacher's Data Structures and Algorithms B course [+W]
  • Exam-level AI difficulty correlations with student performance are directionally consistent but weaker and only exploratory [~W]
  • The AI review ruler's problem-level correlation weakens in an introductory course, where exam-level correlation is near zero and cohort composition dominates [~W]
