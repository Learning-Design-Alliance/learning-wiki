## Description
A Student Digital Twin is defined as "a continuously evolving model of an individual learner's academic performance, learning behavior, career interests, degree progression, financial circumstances, co-curricular engagement, and workforce preparedness." Its purpose is "to simulate": answering counterfactual questions such as what happens if a gateway course is repeated with tutoring, or if the student changes majors. The paper specifies three design properties: fidelity matched to purpose, calibration of predicted probabilities to observed rates, and validation of simulated causal claims against real outcomes. It cautions the twin "is not the person" and should be a decision aid, not an oracle.

- **What separates a digital twin from a model (Wright & Davidson, engineering measurement science)**: The authors identify three parts of a digital twin: a model of the object, an evolving set of data relating to the object, and a means of updating or adjusting the model dynamically in line with those data. A twin must be tied to an object that actually exists ("a digital twin without a physical twin is a model"), and the approach is most useful when the object changes over time, making the initial model invalid, and data correlated with that change can be captured. They warn that vague use of the term risks it being dismissed as hype, and that successful deployment needs trust in the model, in the data and in the algorithms that update the model from the data. The paper is about engineering and does not discuss education. (Wright & Davidson (2020))
- **Student Digital Twin: a continuously updated learner model built for counterfactual simulation, not dashboard display**: A Student Digital Twin is defined as "a continuously evolving model of an individual learner's academic performance, learning behavior, career interests, degree progression, financial circumstances, co-curricular engagement, and workforce preparedness." Its purpose is "to simulate": answering counterfactual questions such as what happens if a gateway course is repeated with tutoring, or if the student changes majors. The paper specifies three design properties: fidelity matched to purpose, calibration of predicted probabilities to observed rates, and validation of simulated causal claims against real outcomes. It cautions the twin "is not the person" and should be a decision aid, not an oracle. (Kaushik Dutta (2026))
- **Digital twin as the National Academies define it, across science and engineering**: The committee defines a digital twin as a set of virtual constructs that mimics the structure, context and behaviour of a natural, engineered or social system, is updated dynamically with data from its physical counterpart, has a "predictive capability" beyond the available data, and informs decisions that realize value; it treats the two-way interaction between the virtual and the physical as central. Its findings and conclusions relevant to a learner twin: a digital twin is more than modelling and simulation (Finding 2-1); it should be built at a fidelity and resolution that is "fit for purpose" for the decision at hand (Conclusion 3-1); verification, validation and uncertainty quantification must be a continual process that adapts as the physical counterpart, the models, the data and the decision task change (Conclusion 2-2), and are required to establish trust (Conclusion 2-4); and, since publicity around digital twins was felt to outweigh the evidence base of success, the aspirational should be separated from the actual (Conclusion 2-5). The report covers engineered, natural, biological and social systems and does not address education or learners. (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2024); read from the report's summary chapter)
