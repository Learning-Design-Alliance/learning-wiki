## Description
The study adopts critical realism as its theoretical approach, carried out through a realist inquiry approach. As the article states, "Realist inquiry concerns what works for whom, under what conditions, how, and why by focusing on relationships between contexts, mechanisms, and outcomes." Context covers fixed settings like VR equipment and variable human factors such as cultures and trained behaviors; mechanisms operate at a psychological level to explain how and why people respond to interventions; outcomes are the intended and unintended impacts of the intervention. The authors use this framework to structure the discussion of VR's acceptability and applicability for MCI training.

- **Realist context-mechanism-outcome program theory of supervision training, differentiated by intervention duration**: The article develops a modified program theory (MPT) explaining how supervision training works, for whom and in what circumstances, using realist logic in which interventions lead to outcomes through mechanisms operating in contexts. It states: "Interventions are thought to lead to outcomes through the operation of mechanisms, that is, the resources proffered by an intervention and the ways in which this influences participants' reasoning." The theory distinguishes causal pathways for short-duration interventions (pedagogy and supervisor-characteristic mechanisms) from extended-duration interventions (facilitator-characteristic mechanisms), built from 74 final CMOCs identified as demi-regularities across 29 outputs.
