## Description
A district-centric, practitioner-focused process in which university researchers help districts pilot ed-tech products, gather mixed-methods efficacy data across five dimensions (student learning, student engagement, teacher support, teacher satisfaction, administrator satisfaction), and feed synthesized findings back to developers. The team "provided feedback via phone or face-to-face meetings to engage the product developers in open and frank discussions about the positives as well as the problems teachers and students encountered using the product." Feedback led to concrete design-change proposals for some products and pilot-process improvements for others.

- **Rapid cumulative micro-RCT evaluation architecture: signal, replication, accumulation**: An evaluation architecture for rapidly evolving educational AI in which practitioner-led micro-randomised controlled trials are repeated across cohorts, contexts and product versions. Its value lies "not in replacing definitive evaluation with small studies, but in enabling a rapid, cumulative evaluation architecture in which randomised estimates can be generated, replicated and updated". Three linked stages structure the model: an initial trial generates a provisional causal signal; replication tests whether direction and magnitude recur; accumulation characterises the distribution of effects across versions, pedagogy, populations and implementation. Successive estimates are represented through forest plots and cumulative meta-analysis, retaining individual estimates rather than allowing a pooled mean to obscure heterogeneity. (Wayne Harrison et al. (2026))

## Description
A district-centric, practitioner-focused process in which university researchers help districts pilot ed-tech products, gather mixed-methods efficacy data across five dimensions (student learning, student engagement, teacher support, teacher satisfaction, administrator satisfaction), and feed synthesized findings back to developers. The team "provided feedback via phone or face-to-face meetings to engage the product developers in open and frank discussions about the positives as well as the problems teachers and students encountered using the product." Feedback led to concrete design-change proposals for some products and pilot-process improvements for others.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an external research partner with capacity to design measures, collect data, and synthesize findings
- Multiple sources of qualitative and quantitative data, including interviews, surveys, observations, and developer-provided learning analytics
- Willing product developers who engage with feedback
#### Constraints
- Common measures across districts were not feasible given product diversity and the one-year time frame, so designs were tailored per district
- Careful attention to context is warranted before extrapolating findings to other situations

### Target Learners
- K-8 students in participating pilot classrooms

### Target Learning Objectives
- Evaluating whether ed-tech products meet district instructional and curricular goals before purchase
