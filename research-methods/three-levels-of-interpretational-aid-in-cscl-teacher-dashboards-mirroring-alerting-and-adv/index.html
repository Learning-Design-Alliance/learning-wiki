Three levels of interpretational aid in CSCL teacher dashboards: mirroring, alerting, and advising¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Building on Soller et al.'s algorithm for collaboration support tools, the article distinguishes dashboards by which agent - tool or teacher - performs detection and interpretation. "Mirroring dashboards" display analyzed data for the teacher to peruse at will; "alerting" dashboards additionally flag groups deviating from a standard; "advising" dashboards "not only display information and alert to certain events, but also assist in the interpretation" by providing advice about what is happening or what action to take. This typology structures the three experimental conditions.

Accounts¶

  • Three levels of interpretational aid in CSCL teacher dashboards: mirroring, alerting, and advising: Building on Soller et al.'s algorithm for collaboration support tools, the article distinguishes dashboards by which agent - tool or teacher - performs detection and interpretation. "Mirroring dashboards" display analyzed data for the teacher to peruse at will; "alerting" dashboards additionally flag groups deviating from a standard; "advising" dashboards "not only display information and alert to certain events, but also assist in the interpretation" by providing advice about what is happening or what action to take. This typology structures the three experimental conditions. (van Leeuwen et al. (2019))
  • A classification framework for CSCL interaction-management systems distinguishes mirroring, metacognitive, and coaching systems: The article proposes a classification framework built on a simple model of coaching for systems that support the management of collaborative interaction. The framework distinguishes between mirroring systems, which display basic actions to collaborators; metacognitive tools, which represent the state of interaction via a set of key indicators; and coaching systems, which offer advice based on an interpretation of those indicators. This framework organizes the reviewed systems along a progression from displaying raw interaction data to offering interpreted advice. (Patrick Jermann et al. (2001))

Claims¶

  • Response time declined across dashboard trials in all conditions, with no statistically significant difference between mirroring, alerting, and advising conditions [+W]
  • Dashboard support type did not influence detection of CSCL events but did influence teachers' interpretation of the situation [+W]
  • Mirroring-condition teachers relied more on indicator views while alerting and advising teachers opened more group overviews [+W]
  • Mirroring dashboards improved teacher situational awareness while alerting-and-guiding dashboards reduced teacher workload and frustration [+W]
  • Teachers reported the highest confidence in their situational awareness in the alerting & guiding dashboard condition [+W]
  • The alerting & guiding dashboard did not increase teacher coregulation more than the mirroring dashboard (H3 not supported); mirroring increased intervention rates (non-significant) while guiding-condition interventions were rare and mostly coregulation strategies [+W]
  • Guiding dashboard support enabled teachers to perform more orchestration actions, more targeted interactions, and more class announcements than mirroring support [+W]
