# Value-added modeling

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
Value-added measures quantify a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains. The brief defines the approach: "'Value added' is a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains." It reports that newly emerging research uses these measures to shed light on disadvantaged students' access to effective teaching, and that variation in how researchers present such evidence complicates practitioner use.

- **Value-added measures of teacher effectiveness**: Value-added measures quantify a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains. The brief defines the approach: "'Value added' is a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains." It reports that newly emerging research uses these measures to shed light on disadvantaged students' access to effective teaching, and that variation in how researchers present such evidence complicates practitioner use. - **Value-added methods as the measure of teacher quality for studying mobility**: The article measures teacher quality using value-added methods, defined in its framing as quality "measured using value-added methods," and focuses on "the variation in these effects across the effectiveness distribution." This makes the effectiveness distribution — the spread of teachers' value-added effects — the organizing axis for its mobility analysis, allowing comparison of mobility behavior for teachers at different points in the quality distribution. (Dan Goldhaber et al. (2011))
- **Value-added methods as a framework for estimating educator effectiveness, presented in technical and nontechnical terms**: The report treats value-added methods as an organizing framework for measuring educator effectiveness, presenting "an overview of value-added methods in technical and nontechnical terms". The framework underlies the model used in Oklahoma's pilot evaluation system to estimate teacher and school effectiveness from instruction during the 2012-2013 school year. The page provides only a summary-level description of this framework. (Elias Walsh et al. (2014))
- **Value-added modeling as a framework for measuring educator effectiveness**: Value-added modeling is a statistical framework for estimating the contribution that schools and teachers make to student achievement growth, operationalized here for use in a district educator evaluation system. The report frames its purpose as describing "the value-added models that are used as part of the BRIDGE teacher and principal evaluations," applying the framework at both school and teacher levels within one district's evaluation program. (Alexandra Resch (2015))
- **Value-added models as a method for separating teachers' contributions to student achievement from other factors**: Value-added models are statistical models used in Oklahoma's evaluation system to compute educator results. As the brief explains, "The results are calculated using value-added models, which separate teachers' contributions to student achievement from other factors." The approach underlies the results distributed to teachers and administrators in spring 2016, and the brief describes updates to these models for that release. (Albert Y. Liu et al. (2016))
- **Value-added models (VAM) as a framework for teacher and principal evaluation**: The report treats teacher and principal value-added models (VAM) as an organizing framework for educator evaluation, summarizing "research findings and implementation practices for teacher and principal value-added models (VAM)". VAM is presented as a measurement approach whose development requires decisions about data, modeling, and implementation. The report organizes the literature and practice examples around this framework for a state pilot project. (Lipscomb et al. (2010))
- **Value added as a measure of teacher effectiveness**: The brief defines value added as a way of quantifying teaching quality: "'Value added' is a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains." It frames emerging research on disadvantaged students' access to effective teaching as based on these value-added measures, while noting that researchers have presented such evidence in varied ways, making it hard for practitioners to interpret. (Jeffrey Max (2014))
- **Value-added measurement of teacher performance as the basis for defining highest-performing teachers**: The brief defines value-added in ideal terms as "the contribution of the teacher to student achievement growth, holding constant the factors outside the teacher's control." It estimates value-added as average test-score growth for each teacher relative to the district average, with statistical controls for student background and entering achievement, using up to three years of data per teacher for precision. The top 20 percent of teachers within each district, subject, and grade span are labeled highest performing. The authors note active debate about high-stakes use but observe these measures resemble indicators districts use in Race to the Top, the Teacher Incentive Fund, DC's IMPACT syste (Steven Glazerman et al. (2011))
