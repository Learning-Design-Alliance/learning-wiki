Systemic Functional Linguistic Theme typology¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article adopts Halliday's Systemic Functional Grammar definition of Theme as "point of departure of the message", recognized because it "is put in the first position", followed by the Rheme. Following SFG it distinguishes three Theme types in terms of textual metafunction — topical, textual (continuatives, conjunctions, conjunctive adjuncts), and interpersonal (vocatives, modal adjuncts, mood-markings) — and two topical types in terms of thematic markedness: unmarked (Theme mapped onto subject in declaratives) and marked Themes, further analyzed as spatial, temporal, manner, cause and contingency subtypes. The study uses this typology as its analytical framework for coding 120 essays at T-unit level.

Claims¶

  • After instruction, the experimental group used proportionally fewer marked Themes and more unmarked Themes, while the control group showed no change in marked/unmarked proportions [+M]
  • After instruction, the experimental group used proportionally fewer mood-marking Themes and more modal adjuncts, and both groups used fewer interpersonal Themes overall [+M]
