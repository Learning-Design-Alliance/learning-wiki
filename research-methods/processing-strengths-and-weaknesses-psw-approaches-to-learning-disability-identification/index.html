Processing strengths and weaknesses (PSW) approaches to learning-disability identification¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 7 claims (1 mixed, 6 against) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

PSW methods operationalize a cognitive discrepancy approach to LD identification based on identifying an intraindividual pattern of processing strengths and weaknesses. The XBA approach "utilizes a series of normative comparisons to identify deficient and normal academic and cognitive abilities," requiring an achievement deficit, a normal cognitive profile, and a theoretically related cognitive deficit. The C/DM instead "calculates difference scores across a number of cognitive and achievement tests and compares these difference scores against computed critical values," requiring concordance between achievement and cognitive deficits and discordance with a cognitive strength. Both are aligned with CHC theory in this study.

Claims¶

  • The XBA and C/DM PSW methods show chance-level agreement (kappa = -.10) in identifying LD among fourth graders with reading comprehension deficits [-M]
  • XBA LD status does not predict posttest reading outcomes beyond pretest performance in decoding, fluency, or comprehension [-M]
  • Simulation evidence cited in the article indicates PSW methods have consistently low positive predictive values despite high negative predictive values [-W]
  • Simulations indicate PSW cognitive assessment would improve classification of response by only about 5-10 students per 1,000 in the best case [-W]
  • A few XBA inclusionary criteria modestly predict word reading only, with very small eta squared values (η2 range .008 to .018) [-W]
  • C/DM LD status likewise does not predict posttest reading outcomes beyond pretest performance [-W]
  • For the C/DM, only short-term memory deficit status significantly predicted posttest word reading [~W]
