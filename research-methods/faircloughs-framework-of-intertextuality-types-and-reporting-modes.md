# Fairclough's framework of intertextuality types and reporting modes

## Description
The article adopts Fairclough's (1992) critical-discourse-analysis model, which distinguishes "manifest intertextuality" from "interdiscursivity or constitutive intertextuality", plus sequential, embedded, and mixed types, and four reporting modes (direct, indirect, free indirect, narrative report of speech act). The study operationalizes the model to classify vocabulary, structures, genres, reporting modes, and intertextuality types in ten reading passages and to structure the experimental instruction.
