# Continuous misconception-strength operationalization

## Description
The article defines a misconception as holding a mental model that appears to explain a phenomenon but is flawed and unscientific, distinct from lacking knowledge. It then operationalizes misconception strength as a continuous spectrum rather than a binary: 'the percentage of the student population that chose the most popular wrong answer (therefore the most distracting wrong answer) among all wrong answers of a misconception-driven multiple-choice item.' This continuous definition, replacing an earlier binary one, allowed the study to model misconception strength as a item-level covariate and moderator of the teacher-knowledge effect.
