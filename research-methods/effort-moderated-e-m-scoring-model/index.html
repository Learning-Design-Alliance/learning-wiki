Effort-moderated (E-M) scoring model¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The effort-moderated (E-M) model, attributed to Wise and DeMars (2006), is a scoring approach in which "rapid guesses are identified and excluded during scoring." It is "intended to estimate the score a disengaged test taker would have received had they been fully engaged." The presentation evaluates and seeks to improve this model using retest data from MAP Growth assessments.

Claims¶

  • E-M scoring partially accounts for observed RIT score differences between disengaged first tests and engaged retests [+M]
  • Effort-moderated scoring estimates disengaged test takers' performance by excluding disengaged responses and scoring the remaining responses [+W]
  • E-M scoring assumes the remaining responses are unaffected by disengagement, and this assumption can fail under partial engagement [-W]
  • E-M scoring leaves residual score distortion, suggesting some disengaged test responses are nonrapid rather than rapid guesses [-W]
