Self-referential analytic assessment framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An evaluation paradigm for analytic essay scoring that shifts from normative (inter-learner ranking) to within-learner interpretation of analytic profiles. "Self-referential analytic assessment focuses on intra-learner variability, identifying aspects where a student performs significantly better or worse than their average proficiency." Standardised analytic scores are averaged within a learner, and deviations of each aspect from that average define a dual binary classification task for positive and negative feedback, using a one-standard-deviation threshold. The article positions this as a stricter, more pedagogically relevant test of analytic assessment capability and the first such framework for L2 AES.

Claims¶

  • Rank-based metrics can be misleading for analytic assessment: systems prompted for holistic scoring achieve strong, sometimes stronger, correlations with analytic scores than when prompted for those aspects [+M]
  • In a self-referential framework, LLMs, particularly GPT-4.1, tend to outperform a single operational human rater at identifying relative weaknesses (negative feedback) across several Language and Content aspects [+M]
  • Human operational raters remain stronger than LLMs at identifying relative strengths (positive feedback) within learner profiles [+M]
