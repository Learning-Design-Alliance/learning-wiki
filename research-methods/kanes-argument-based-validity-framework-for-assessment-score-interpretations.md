## Description
The article applies Messick construct-validity framework and Kane's argument-based approach to interpret LLM examination scores, examining "evidence concerning scoring, generalization, and extrapolation, together with construct-irrelevant variance and construct underrepresentation". Table 1 maps four Kane inferences to evidence: scoring is supported for recorded answers against the official key; generalization is limited to these items and configurations; extrapolation shows that "Clinical competence and equivalence with human score interpretations are untested"; and the study does not validate clinical decisions or autonomous educational use. This framework structures the article's central caution that high model accuracy does not by itself establish professional competence.

- **Kane validity framework applied to LLM examination scores: scoring is supported, but extrapolation to clinical competence and decision-making remain untested**: The article applies Messick construct-validity framework and Kane's argument-based approach to interpret LLM examination scores, examining "evidence concerning scoring, generalization, and extrapolation, together with construct-irrelevant variance and construct underrepresentation". Table 1 maps four Kane inferences to evidence: scoring is supported for recorded answers against the official key; generalization is limited to these items and configurations; extrapolation shows that "Clinical competence and equivalence with human score interpretations are untested"; and the study does not validate clinical decisions or autonomous educational use. This framework structures the article's central caution that high model accuracy does not by itself establish professional competence. (Clemente García-Hidalgo et al. (2026))
- **Kane's Validity Framework as the organizing structure for validating simulation-based assessments**: The Kane Validity Framework is an argument-based approach that conceptualizes validation as a chain of inferences. The article states it "structures the validation process around four key inferences scoring, generalization, extrapolation, and implications each of which must be supported by empirical evidence." The study applies it to high-fidelity simulation-based assessments in Ghanaian nursing education, mapping reliability evidence to scoring, simulation–OSCE correlation to generalization, practicum prediction to extrapolation, and pass/fail consistency to implication. (Ntumi Simon (2025))
- **Kane's validity argument framework for interpreting test scores**: Kane's framework treats validation as a sequence: first determining the use and interpretation of assessment scores, then deriving assumptions from that use, then testing the weakest assumptions by gathering evidence for four inferences in order - scoring, generalization, extrapolation and implication. The article uses it to structure validation of the SJT, noting that unlike other frameworks it "captures evidence related to the measured construct and evidence related to job prediction performance" and specifies the order for collecting validity evidence. In this study only the scoring and generalization inferences were tested. (Obi Uche Shalom et al. (2024))
- **Kane's validation framework applied to admissions test use (interpretation-use argument with extrapolation and implication inferences)**: The study positions itself within contemporary assessment validation, in which "validation entails two steps": articulating key claims in an interpretation-use argument, then developing a validity argument to test them. Inferences move through scoring, generalization, extrapolation, and implication stages, directing collection of Messick's validity evidence types. This study prioritized extrapolation (associations between Casper and OSCE/fieldwork performance) and implication (demographic consequences of weighting Casper) inferences, leaving scoring and generalization to the test developer's manual. (Roduta Roberts Mary et al. (2024))
- **Kane's four-domain validation framework applied via interpretation-use arguments**: Kane's framework describes how to generate interpretation-use arguments (IUAs) that make the reasoning behind score interpretations explicit. It includes four domains: "scoring, generalization, extrapolation, and implication". The article applies it to a POCUS assessment tool, testing the scoring-domain IUA that expert raters' scores will agree when rating heart, aorta, and abdomen ultrasound performance, as a foundation for later validity domains. (Sheppard Gillian et al. (2023))
