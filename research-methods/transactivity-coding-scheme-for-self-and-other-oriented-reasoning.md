# Transactivity coding scheme for self- and other-oriented reasoning

## Description
The article operationalizes transactivity as reasoning made explicit that builds on prior reasoning in a discussion. As printed, "our operationalization of transactivity is defined as the process of building on an idea expressed earlier in a conversation using a reasoning statement." Transacts are self-oriented when they operate on the speaker's own prior reasoning and other-oriented when they operate on a partner's reasoning; other-oriented transacts are treated as particularly central to group knowledge integration. Coding proceeds in steps distinguishing reasoning from non-reasoning statements, externalizations from transactive contributions, and self- from other-oriented transacts.

- **Transactivity construct: 18 types of transactive moves along three dimensions**: Transactivity is a construct from a Piagetian framework formalized as 18 types of transactive moves that characterize each student's conversational turn as an explicit reasoning display connecting with previously articulated reasoning. The article draws on three dimensions for motivating experimental studies: self-oriented versus other-oriented (whether the contribution operates on the speaker's own or a partner's reasoning), representational versus transformational (whether the original idea is restated or transformed), and consensus-oriented versus conflict-oriented. The authors propose these dimensions create a logical ordering of demand that applies to corresponding APT facilitation moves. (Adamson et al. (2014))
