# Two-dimensional taxonomy of instructional video styles: human embodiment and instructional media

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a qualitative taxonomy organizing video lectures along two orthogonal dimensions: human embodiment (from wide shots of instructor and audience to screen capture of a pen tip, including robots and animated characters) and instructional media (from slides and animations to physical objects). The author states: "there are two dimensions that determine the presentation style: 1) human embodiment, 2) instructional media", with nominal values ranging "from the digital (or artiﬁcial) to the physical". It extends Santos-Espino's speaker-centric/board-centric dichotomy by treating speakers and boards as complementary rather than competing, and visualizes existing and potential styles on a scatter plot.
