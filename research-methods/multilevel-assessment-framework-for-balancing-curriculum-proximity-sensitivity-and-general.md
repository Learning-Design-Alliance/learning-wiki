# Multilevel assessment framework for balancing curriculum proximity, sensitivity, and generalizability

## Description
Multilevel assessment, introduced by Ruiz-Primo and colleagues, negotiates location bias by "measuring students at a variety of locations with respect to the curriculum of instruction": assessments close to the curriculum trade generalizability for sensitivity, while distal assessments sacrifice sensitivity for generalizability. This article is the first to apply the framework to an affective construct, interest, by positioning three subscales (science, healthcare, vision care) at different distances from a 2-wk intervention. The authors extend the cognition–observation–interpretation triangle to an "affection–observation–interpretation" (AOI) triangle for affective conceptual change.
