# Pedagogical evaluation framework for student–LLM interaction

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article adapts Lee et al. (2023)'s human-LLM interaction evaluation framework to EFL writing education, organizing evaluation along "targets (§ 3.2.1), perspectives (§ 3.2.2), and criteria (§ 3.2.3)". Targets distinguish output (the LLM's generated feedback) from process (students' essay development, comprehension, and progress). Perspectives incorporate both students and teachers, since immediate correct answers may not be pedagogically optimal. Criteria comprise quality (level of detail, accuracy, relevance, helpfulness), characteristics (five feedback-type dimensions such as negative-positive and indirect-direct), and learning outcomes (essay quality improvement and understanding).

- **Evaluate LLM-based educational applications by focusing on learner–LLM dialogue dynamics, moving beyond solely single-turn evaluations or learning gains**: The article underscores the need to evaluate educational LLMs by attending to the dialogue processes between learners and LLMs, which existing evaluations focused on technical performance or learning outcomes often overlook. It notes that "superior single-turn performance does not necessarily lead to better multi-turn interactions", and that prior work showed patterns of dialogue acts correlate with student learning gains. Dialogue dynamics and pedagogical strategies are therefore proposed as focal points for evaluation methods. (Liqun He et al. (2024))
