# CFIR five-domain framework for organizing e-learning implementation determinants

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)

## Description
The review uses the updated Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) to code and organize implementation determinants. Determinants were "mapped across CFIR’s five domains (Innovation, Outer setting, Inner setting, Individuals, and Implementation process)", coded one at a time to the domain, subdomain, and construct best reflecting the original description, with two researchers coding independently and discrepancies resolved by consensus. In this review it serves as the synthesis structure for facilitators and barriers.

- **Six-domain CFIR-based framework for barriers and facilitators of addiction medicine fellowship implementation**: The article organises its qualitative findings using Damschroder et al.'s Consolidated Framework for Advancing Implementation Science Research (CFIR), mapped onto six domains: "(1) structural, (2) organisational, (3) mentor, (4) learner, (5) patient and (6) community concerns". At the centre is "the learner-mentor-patient triad set in the organisational and structural context", with the outer setting operationalised as structural, community and organisational concerns, the inner setting as learner concerns, and the individuals involved as teachers and patients. The framework is illustrated in Fig. 1 and used to structure the results. (Klimas J. et al. (2017))
- **Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) as the guiding framework for identifying OBLS implementation determinants**: The study employs CFIR to identify factors influencing OBLS implementation success across levels. The article states the framework "encompasses five domains that categorize 39 constructs associated with implementation effectiveness": intervention characteristics, outer setting, inner setting, individual characteristics, and implementation process. Survey items and focus group guides are adapted from CFIR resources to explore barriers and facilitators among rural administrators and healthcare workers. (Cunningham Shayna D. et al. (2026))
