# On-line versus off-line methods for assessing strategy use

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article distinguishes two families of strategy-use assessment. On-line methods, such as thinking aloud, observation, eye-movement registration, and logfile registration, take "measurements taken concurrent to task performance" and code actual behavior against externally defined criteria. Off-line methods, such as questionnaires (e.g., MSLQ) and interviews, ask learners about strategy use before or after performance, so answers rest on reconstruction from memory of past experiences. The article uses this framework to evaluate the five special-issue instruments.

- **Online versus offline measurement as an organising distinction for metacognition assessment**: The article organises metacognition measures by whether they capture metacognition during task performance or via retrospective self-report. "Offline measures are questionnaires that aim to capture an individual's self-reported perception of their own MC ability based on previous learning experiences", such as the MSLQ, MAI and Jr. MAI, whereas "online measures capture an individual's MC via ongoing behaviour and performance as they complete a task", including think-aloud protocols and behaviour observations. The article notes contested boundary cases (confidence judgements, calibration accuracy, JOLs) classified differently by Saraç and Karakelle, Veenman and van Cleef, and Craig et al., and reports poor correspondence between the two measure types in prior studies. (Gemma Muncer et al. (2021))
