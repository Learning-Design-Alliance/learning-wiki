# Kirkpatrick four-level evaluation model with Level 4a/4b distinction

## Description
The review organizes its evidence synthesis around the Kirkpatrick model, "a widely used four-level framework for evaluating the effectiveness of educational interventions, including SBE". Level 1 assesses reaction, Level 2 learning, Level 3 behavior in clinical practice, and Level 4 results; the review adopts the subdivision of Level 4 into 4a (system-level process metrics such as response times and protocol adherence) and 4b (direct patient outcomes such as mortality and complications). This distinction structures its primary outcome coding and thematic synthesis.

- **Kirkpatrick four-level model with the Level 4a/4b distinction as a framework for evaluating simulation-based education**: The review organizes its evidence synthesis around the Kirkpatrick model, "a widely used four-level framework for evaluating the effectiveness of educational interventions, including SBE". Level 1 assesses reaction, Level 2 learning, Level 3 behavior in clinical practice, and Level 4 results; the review adopts the subdivision of Level 4 into 4a (system-level process metrics such as response times and protocol adherence) and 4b (direct patient outcomes such as mortality and complications). This distinction structures its primary outcome coding and thematic synthesis. (Zanin A et al. (2026))
- **Adapted Kirkpatrick Model as organizing framework for medical education outcomes**: The review uses a medical-education adaptation of Kirkpatrick's four-level training-outcome model to categorize the heterogeneous outcomes reported across included studies. The adapted model, per the article, "encompasses the same four levels, with some modification: the learners' satisfaction with program content and delivery (Level 1), change in opinion about the topic (Level 2A), change in knowledge and/or skills (Level 2B), change in behavior (Level 3), and patient outcomes (Level 4)." It served to group studies by outcome type and identify gaps for future research rather than requiring studies to assess all levels. (Clemens Megan et al. (2026))
- **Kirkpatrick model mapping as the organising framework for evaluating technology-enhanced surgical training evidence**: The review uses the Kirkpatrick model of training evaluation as its organising axis, mapping each included study's reported outcomes to a level: level 1 reaction, level 2 learning (simulator scores, kinematic metrics, R-OSATS), level 3 transfer to operative behaviour, and level 4 patient outcomes. It reports that "the available evidence is concentrated at level 2 (learning — simulator scores, kinematic metrics, R-OSATS)". This framework lets the review show that no study reached transfer or patient-level outcomes, bounding what the technology evidence can support. (Shakir T et al. (2026))
- **Kirkpatrick and Miller framework mapping of psychiatry education technology evidence**: The review maps its included studies onto two established evaluation frameworks to locate the evidence's level. According to Kirkpatrick's model, most studies demonstrated effects at Level 2 (learning), with only a few reporting Level 1 (learner satisfaction) and none reaching Levels 3 or 4. On Miller's pyramid, most interventions addressed the 'knows' and 'knows how' levels, while HFSs and immersive VR achieved the 'shows how' level. The review uses this mapping to highlight "the critical gap in evidence for their long-term impact on professional practice and patient care." (Mokhtari Saba et al. (2026))
- **Kirkpatrick's four levels of evidence for evaluating the attributable effect of teaching programs.**: Kirkpatrick's framework outlines four ascending levels of attributable benefit of a teaching program: (1) how well trainees received a program, (2) measured learning, (3) change in behaviour attributable to the program, and (4) outcomes of training such as reduced cost or higher quality of outcomes. The review uses this framework to examine the quality of evidence available for RAS curricula assessment, noting that "The importance of an adequate before-and-after assessment approach with a control group and statistical considerations were highlighted as key to this evaluation." (Kieslich Anna K. et al. (2026))
- **Kirkpatrick's four-level model as an organizing framework for evaluating NTS training outcomes in emergency care.**: The review organises its synthesis of NTS training outcomes using Kirkpatrick's four-level model, covering reaction (Level 1), learning (Level 2), behavioural change (Level 3), and clinical results (Level 4). The authors state that "the outcomes of non-technical skills training were categorized according to Kirkpatrick's four-level model". Because no included study reported participants' reactions, the summary focused on Levels 2–4, "covering learning outcomes, behavioural performance, and clinical results". This mapping clarifies that the most consistent gains occur at the learning and behaviour levels, while evidence at the clinical outcome level remains sparse. (Zhang YuKun et al. (2026))
