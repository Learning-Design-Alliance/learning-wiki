# Latent state-trait model of academic development

## Description
The article describes a model, developed by Steyer (1987), in which children's mathematics achievement at any time is caused by two sets of factors: mathematics skill at the immediately preceding occasion (the MS paths, reflecting content overlap, transfer of learning, and indirect effects via motivation or teacher placement) and an "Unmeasured persistent factor" comprising stable environmental and personal influences. It requires at least three waves of data, can be adapted to experimental designs, and predicts exponential decay of treatment effects alongside high correlational stability. The article uses it to reconcile correlational and experimental estimates of skill-building effects.
