Description¶

The report organizes its comparability evaluation around three criteria drawn from the score-comparability literature: "there are three general categories of criteria adopted in evaluating comparability: (1) content and construct validity, (2) psychometric properties and reliability, and (3) statistical assumption/test administration condition." Claims¶

  • Growth-score differences between CBE and COLO are small, exceeding 0.2 only in Reading Grades K–1 (favoring CBE) and Mathematics Grade 8 (favoring COLO) [+M]
  • Marginal reliabilities of MAP Growth winter scores are comparable across engines and all in the 0.90s, with CBE showing slightly higher precision (lower SEM) [+M]
  • The Altair (engine) main effect is not significant for winter RIT scores in either content area, but an Altair-by-sex interaction is significant in Reading only [+W]
  • CBE shows better engine adaptivity than COLO, especially for extremely low or high achievement students [+W]
  • Item exposure rates are comparable across engines, with most items below 10%, and the CBE Reading item-use rate is lower than COLO's [+W]
  • Test lengths are comparable across engines, but CBE test durations are higher than COLO's in both terms and content areas, more prominently in Reading [+W]
