## Description
The article applies the dose–response framework, borrowed from medicine and pharmacology, to academic intervention: the dose–response curve represents the relation between an intervention's intensity and its effects. Dosage is operationalized following Warren et al. (2007) as "the product of an intervention's session duration (e.g., 30 min), frequency (e.g., three times per week), and length of treatment (e.g., 15 weeks)", quantified as total instructional hours per student. The framework distinguishes a linear dose–response relation, where "the effect of the treatment increases in direct proportion to the amount of treatment delivered", from a nonlinear one that peaks and then plateaus or diminishes. The authors use it to test whether K-3 mathematics interventions show diminishing returns as reading interventions do.
