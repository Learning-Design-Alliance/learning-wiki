## Description
The article characterizes case-oriented PER as a paradigm premised on the assumptions that social actions are guided by the meanings people make of their local environments and that reality is subjectively constructed. Researchers immerse themselves in local context, naturalistically observing interactions, selecting cases of theoretical significance, and connecting case to theory to illustrate, build, or challenge it. As the authors write, "Case-oriented research, in contrast, seeks to refine and develop theory by linking that theory to cases; human action is assumed to be shaped by the meanings that participants make of their local environments." Its aims include demonstrating possibility, clarifying participants' points of view, challenging implicit assumptions, and developing mechanisms.
