Endogenous missingness framework for estimating treatment impacts on classroom quality¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames missing classroom-quality data as a threat that is endogenous to treatment assignment rather than random: because a quality rating can only be collected when a child is in formal care, and Head Start increases formal care enrollment, "missingness" due to being in parental care is associated with randomization. The framework distinguishes three sources of missingness (parental care, unobserved formal settings, unknown care type) and models each as an outcome before estimating quality impacts.

Claims¶

  • Tobit models decompose the Head Start impact into a 48-percentage-point increase in probability of an observed quality score and a 2-point increase in quality conditional on observation [+W]
  • The original Head Start Impact Study threshold-variable method conflates low quality with missing data and discards continuous variance [+W]
  • Random assignment to Head Start increases children's access to formal care and the quality of formal care relative to control [+W]
