Brennan and Resnick's three-dimensional CT framework (concepts, practices, perspectives)¶

Description¶

The article adopts Brennan and Resnick (2012)'s framework as its theoretical scaffold for understanding CT learning and assessment. It classifies CT into three dimensions: "concepts, practices, and perspectives" — concepts are foundational programming ideas (sequences, loops, conditionals, events), practices are how learners apply concepts in problem-solving (testing, debugging, decomposition), and perspectives address how learners view themselves in relation to computing. The framework integrates cognitive, procedural, and dispositional dimensions and directly informed the coding of the learning-outcome moderator.

Accounts¶

  • Brennan and Resnick's three-dimensional CT framework (concepts, practices, perspectives) as the study's organizing scaffold: The article adopts Brennan and Resnick (2012)'s framework as its theoretical scaffold for understanding CT learning and assessment. It classifies CT into three dimensions: "concepts, practices, and perspectives" — concepts are foundational programming ideas (sequences, loops, conditionals, events), practices are how learners apply concepts in problem-solving (testing, debugging, decomposition), and perspectives address how learners view themselves in relation to computing. The framework integrates cognitive, procedural, and dispositional dimensions and directly informed the coding of the learning-outcome moderator. (Suya Liu · Yun Dai · Oi Lam Ng · Zhihui Cai (2025))
  • Three-dimensional operationalisation of computational thinking for early childhood: sequences, action-instruction correspondence and debugging: The study measures computational thinking through three dimensions evaluable with a robotics kit: sequences (sequencing actions to meet a challenge), action-instruction correspondence (relating instructions given to a robot with the action it performs) and debugging (identifying and correcting errors in a programming sequence by trial and error). The article draws on Brennan and Resnick's model, which "is based on three dimensions: computational concepts, practices, and perspectives", and adapts these into the three assessed dimensions. (García-Valcárcel Muñoz-Repiso et al. (2019))
