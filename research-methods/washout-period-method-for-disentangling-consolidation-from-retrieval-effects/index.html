Washout-period method for disentangling consolidation from retrieval effects¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

When assessing whether a post-learning intervention benefits consolidation, the article recommends delaying the first memory test long enough for stimulation aftereffects to dissipate. The 2-h break "was implemented as a washout period in order to separate the effects of stimulation on early consolidation from the lingering effects of such stimulation on retrieval." The rationale draws on reports that alpha-band tACS aftereffects washed out once 70 min had passed, so testing at 120 min provided added caution.

Accounts¶

  • Insert a washout period between post-learning stimulation and memory assessment to attribute benefits to consolidation rather than retrieval: When assessing whether a post-learning intervention benefits consolidation, the article recommends delaying the first memory test long enough for stimulation aftereffects to dissipate. The 2-h break "was implemented as a washout period in order to separate the effects of stimulation on early consolidation from the lingering effects of such stimulation on retrieval." The rationale draws on reports that alpha-band tACS aftereffects washed out once 70 min had passed, so testing at 120 min provided added caution. (Limor Shtoots et al. (2024))
  • Control for reactive inhibition when inferring offline consolidation from post-rest improvements in motor learning studies: Because RI builds during continuous performance and dissipates during breaks, post-rest RT improvements can be illusory learning rather than offline consolidation. The article argues RI "is clearly a necessary factor in understanding motor performance, whereas facilitating consolidation does not appear to be necessary". Studies comparing practice schedules should equate or manipulate break-to-trial ratios before attributing gains to consolidation. (Mohan W. Gupta et al. (2022))

Claims¶

  • Frontal midline theta tACS applied after learning produces significant off-line retrieval gains at all assessment time points relative to beta, parietal theta, and sham conditions [+M]
  • The smaller the ratio of break time to on-task trial time during training, the larger the post-rest RT improvement [+M]
  • Reactive inhibition builds within trials during motor sequence practice, appearing as slower responses later in each trial [+M]
