# Gesture-level video annotation method for robotic surgical skill assessment

## Description
The article builds on a gesture classification system that "deconstructs robotic tissue dissection into basic surgical maneuvers," involving "nine dissection gestures (e.g., hook) and four supporting gestures (e.g., retraction)." Annotators label each gesture's name, duration, and arm, achieving 91.2% inter-rater agreement. The framework positions gestures as "a novel method to objectively evaluate dissection performance and anticipate outcomes," offering more granular, explainable insight than reviewer-scored tools like DART.

- **Surgical gesture classification as an objective, granular framework for evaluating dissection performance**: The article builds on a gesture classification system that "deconstructs robotic tissue dissection into basic surgical maneuvers," involving "nine dissection gestures (e.g., hook) and four supporting gestures (e.g., retraction)." Annotators label each gesture's name, duration, and arm, achieving 91.2% inter-rater agreement. The framework positions gestures as "a novel method to objectively evaluate dissection performance and anticipate outcomes," offering more granular, explainable insight than reviewer-scored tools like DART. (Otiato Maxwell X. et al. (2024))
- **Hierarchical semantic vocabulary decomposing surgical activity into maneuvers and gestures**: The article develops a vocabulary based on a hierarchical segmentation of a surgical task: maneuvers are "circumscribed activity segments that accomplish specific landmarks (e.g., tying a knot)" and are further decomposed into gestures, defined as "atomic segments of activity performed to complete a meaningful portion of the task". The vocabulary comprised seven maneuvers and 30 gestures, with maneuvers shared across tasks and gestures shared across maneuvers. It was generated from transcriptions of an expert surgeon's narration and refined through consultation, and used to annotate 135 dVSS trials. (Vedula S. Swaroop et al. (2016))
