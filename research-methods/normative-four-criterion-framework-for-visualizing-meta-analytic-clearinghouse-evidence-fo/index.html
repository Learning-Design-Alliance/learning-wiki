Normative four-criterion framework for visualizing meta-analytic clearinghouse evidence for decision-making¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that visualizations of clearinghouse meta-analytic data should be judged against four normative goals derived from meta-analytic decision theory: they "ought to visually emphasize: 1. Precise studies over imprecise studies, 2. Summary effects over individual effects, 3. The magnitude of the summary effect, and 4. The uncertainty of the summary effect via a range of plausible values." The framework holds that decision-making should rest on the inverse-variance weighted average effect, judged against a user-defined magnitude threshold and communicated with uncertainty that gives intuition about the range of plausible true effects. It motivates the MARC plot's design choices, such as omitting individual-study confidence intervals to discourage vote-counting.

Claims¶

  • In a pre-registered experiment, visualization type significantly affected education practitioners' accuracy in interpreting meta-analytic evidence, with the MARC plot outperforming bar plots, forest plots, and rainforest plots [+M]
  • The MARC plot offered no advantage over existing visualizations for judging statistical significance of the summary effect or recognizing the summary effect as the best estimate of the true SMD [~M]
  • Education researchers outperformed education practitioners on all four visualizations, with the largest gaps on forest and rainforest plots; differences were significant for FP and RFP but not BP [~W]
