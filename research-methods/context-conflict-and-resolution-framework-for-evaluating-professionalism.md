## Description
The article presents a framework, developed in the author's 2000 review, holding that professionalism evaluation must attend to three elements: the "context" in which a behaviour occurs, "conflict between equally worthy values" such as honesty versus confidentiality, and the individual's "resolution" — how they approach a situation and decide how to act. The article argues existing evaluation methods failed on all three and thus could not fully represent an individual's professionalism, and applies the framework to duty-hour reform, where learners' conflict resolutions may become less visible under enforced compliance.
