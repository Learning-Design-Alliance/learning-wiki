N-gram uptake analysis for human–AI co-writing¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes analyzing textual uptake in AI-supported writing via recurring n-grams between ChatGPT output and the writer's final text, extending Bereiter and Scardamalia's knowledge telling/knowledge transforming distinction to human–AI interaction. The authors write that "Overlaps in n-grams can thus serve as indicators of uptake" and that high verbatim overlap reflects adoption "analogous to knowledge-telling activities" while reformulated use points toward knowledge transforming. The three clusters are interpreted as positions on a spectrum from passive uptake to increasingly active and integrative collaboration, aligned with the ICAP framework.

Claims¶

  • AI-supported argumentative writing yields three distinct uptake clusters (text reproducers, text integrators, text reconstructors) based on recurring n-gram overlap [+M]
  • Text reproducers copied nearly all of their final text from ChatGPT with long verbatim sequences, while text integrators showed fragment-level micro-uptake [+M]
