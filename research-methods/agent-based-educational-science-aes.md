## Description
Agent-based educational science (AES) is a research agenda in which educational theories are formalized as interacting agents and environments, enabling in silico experimentation on developmental processes. The authors frame it as "a coordinated invitation to the research community to reorient its priorities, workflows, data infrastructures, and validation practices around theory-constrained, dynamically updateable, and empirically examinable simulations of learner development." It targets developmental processes that are otherwise slow or infeasible to test empirically.

- **LLM-based web agent that engages with an online learning experience like a student and generates a description of the student experience**: The article creates a web agent built on LLMs that "engages with an online learning experience much like a student would – autonomously navigating the interface and analyzing the website content". It then "generates a comprehensive description of the student experience", which the authors use both to predict student outcomes and to give designers feedback on lesson designs. The agent is positioned as an efficient, low-cost stand-in for early-stage design feedback. (Wang et al. (2025))
- **AI reliance as a complex adaptive system: a micro-founded agent-based model of population-level reliance dynamics**: The article models AI reliance as a population process rather than a one-shot individual judgment. Agents repeatedly choose among solving alone, accepting AI output unverified, or verifying, updating Dirichlet beliefs about AI quality and, when networked, learning from peers through experience-based or opinion-dynamics channels. As the authors put it, "individual learning rules, peer observation, and feedback exposure jointly determine whether a population remains calibrated or enters a self-reinforcing state of unverified reliance." The model is used as a theoretical tool for isolating mechanisms and deriving qualitative comparative statics, not as a point forecast. (Ahana Biswas (2026))
