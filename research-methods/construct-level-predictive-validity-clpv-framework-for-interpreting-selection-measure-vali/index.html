Construct-level predictive validity (CLPV) framework for interpreting selection-measure validity¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article develops and applies the construct-level predictive validity framework following Hunter, Schmidt and Le (2006): the estimate of the "true predictor-outcome correlation" between the underlying construct measured by a selection test and the underlying knowledge measured in later examinations, correcting for restriction of range, measurement unreliability and right-censorship. It argues that raw correlations within selected entrants are of "little real interest" because selectors need to know how all applicants would have performed. The framework is operationalized via an MCMC-adapted HSL method that yields standard errors and confidence intervals, and is applied in a meta-regression of 57 coefficients from six UK cohorts.

Claims¶

  • Construct-level predictive validities (mean .450) are substantially higher than raw predictor-outcome correlations (mean .171) across 57 predictor-outcome pairs in medical student selection [+M]
  • Raw predictor-outcome correlations in medical selection are small (mean .171), requiring very large samples (~290) for significance, explaining why selection measures appear weakly valid in single-cohort studies [+M]
