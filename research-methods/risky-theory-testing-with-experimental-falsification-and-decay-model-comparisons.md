# Risky theory testing with experimental, falsification, and decay-model comparisons

## Description
The article argues, following Meehl, that a prediction is risky if it is unlikely to be true assuming the theory is false, and that non-zero regression-adjusted correlations are consistent with many competing theories positing stable personal and environmental characteristics. It recommends experimental manipulation of early skills as the riskiest test, wider use of falsification tests on correlational data, and models making precise predictions about effect decay. It endorses "comparing correlational estimates to the most relevant experimental impacts whenever possible".
