Aptitude-treatment interaction (ATI) framework¶

Description¶

The article organizes its moderation analysis around the aptitude-treatment interaction (ATI) paradigm, which "states that the effectiveness of an intervention can be conceptualized as a result of the complex interplay between the distinct features of the intervention and students' ability and willingness to utilize them". It extends this with a competence-oriented approach following Weinert, treating (meta-)cognitive and motivational dispositions as competence and demographic background and personality traits as precursors, testing nine learner characteristics simultaneously in a multilevel IPD framework.

Accounts¶

  • Aptitude-treatment interaction framework applied to non-interactive teaching via a competence-oriented approach: The article organizes its moderation analysis around the aptitude-treatment interaction (ATI) paradigm, which "states that the effectiveness of an intervention can be conceptualized as a result of the complex interplay between the distinct features of the intervention and students' ability and willingness to utilize them". It extends this with a competence-oriented approach following Weinert, treating (meta-)cognitive and motivational dispositions as competence and demographic background and personality traits as precursors, testing nine learner characteristics simultaneously in a multilevel IPD framework. (Lachner et al. (2025))
  • Aptitude-Treatment Interaction as the framework for matching dialogue format to learner characteristics: The article applies the Aptitude-Treatment Interaction (ATI) framework, in which "learning outcomes are jointly determined by the combination of learner characteristics (aptitude) and instructional method (treatment)". Learner characteristics — the CE and RCE factors of experiential learning style and critical thinking disposition — serve as aptitudes, while the three LLM+TTS dialogue formats (TS, SS, TT) serve as treatments. The framework motivates examining which format is more effective for learners with which style, and grounds the study's recommendation to personalize format selection. (Fumie Watanabe et al. (2026))
  • Aptitude–treatment interaction paradigm as framework for matching instruction to learner characteristics: The article describes the aptitude–treatment interaction (ATI) paradigm as the concept that some learning environments are more or less effective for particular individuals depending on their learning abilities and needs. As printed: "The concept that some learning environments are more or less effective for particular individuals, depending on their learning abilities and needs, is known as 'aptitude– treatment interaction' or 'ATI paradigm'". The 'treatment' refers to the environment available to the learner, while 'aptitude' refers to individual differences forecasting success under a given treatment. (von Stumm et al. (2021))
  • Aptitude–Treatment Interaction (ATI) as a framework for matching instruction to learner variability: The article presents ATI, launched by Cronbach and Snow in the 1970s, as a research approach bringing rigor to studying the intersection of learner and instruction. It recognizes that learners differ cognitively, psychologically, and environmentally, so individuals respond differently to tasks and instruction forms, and "the same learner might respond to the same intervention differently under different environmental or psychological conditions." ATI laid foundations for computer-based adaptive learning by organizing efforts to match treatment to student need under optimal conditions. (David Dockterman (2018))

Claims¶

  • Language proficiency moderates the effect of non-interactive teaching on immediate monitoring accuracy [+M]
  • Physics interest moderates long-term retention: low- and medium-interest students benefit from non-interactive teaching at eight weeks, high-interest students do not [+M]
  • In TTS dialogue-based lessons, the teacher–teacher format significantly moderates the motivation effects of both the CE factor (positive) and the RCE factor (negative) [+M]
  • Interactions between learner characteristics and dialogue format for learning outcomes showed only trends toward significance, not significant effects [~M]
