## Description
Established in the late 1980s by Anderson et al., instructional dimensions were initially used to differentiate inquiry-based from non-inquiry-based instruction. The article uses them as "a measuring tool for the aspects which present a shift when switching from one approach to the other", presenting Table I which contrasts PI and non-PI approaches on subject matter, activity demands, instructional format, grouping, time management, and teacher-learner interaction. PI shows behaviour-oriented personalised demands, small conceptual units, immediate feedback, and direct learner-knowledge interaction.
