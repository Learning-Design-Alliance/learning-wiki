ESSA four-tier evidence hierarchy for intervention selection¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size

Description¶

The guide explains that "ESSA defines four levels of evidence" as elaborated in the U.S. Department of Education's September 2016 nonregulatory guidance. Strong evidence requires a statistically significant effect from at least one well-designed and well-implemented experimental study; moderate evidence rests on a quasi-experimental study; promising evidence rests on a correlational study with statistical controls for selection bias; and the lowest tier, "demonstrates a rationale," requires a rationale based on high-quality research findings or positive evaluation plus ongoing efforts to examine effects. This hierarchy is the evaluative standard used in the guide's evidence-review tools.

Accounts¶

  • ESSA four-tier evidence hierarchy for intervention selection: The guide explains that "ESSA defines four levels of evidence" as elaborated in the U.S. Department of Education's September 2016 nonregulatory guidance. Strong evidence requires a statistically significant effect from at least one well-designed and well-implemented experimental study; moderate evidence rests on a quasi-experimental study; promising evidence rests on a correlational study with statistical controls for selection bias; and the lowest tier, "demonstrates a rationale," requires a rationale based on high-quality research findings or positive evaluation plus ongoing efforts to examine effects. This hierarchy is the evaluative standard used in the guide's evidence-review tools. (Hale et al. (2017))
  • ESSA Tier IV evidence standard requiring a well-defined logic model and planned research: ESSA Tier IV is the lowest of four statutory evidence levels in the US Every Student Succeeds Act framework, relating to a research-informed rationale. Per the What Works Clearinghouse definition quoted in the article: "the product demonstrates a rationale if the study design follows a well-defined logic model and related research or evaluation is planned or underway". The article focuses on Tier IV for early-stage interventions, where empirical impact evidence may not yet exist, and observes that certification providers apply the guidance with considerable flexibility. (Natalia Kucirkova et al. (2026))
