# Miller's pyramid of clinical competence assessment

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Miller's pyramid is a four-level framework ordering assessment targets from factual knowledge to real-world performance: "Knows" (best assessed with written exams), "Knows How" (written or oral exams), "Shows How" (performance-based assessments with standardized simulations or oral exams with standardized scenarios), and "Does" (clinic-based performance assessments). The review uses it to map which assessment formats and construct types are prevalent in physical therapy education and to identify which levels are underrepresented.

- **Miller's pyramid as an organizing framework for knowledge and skill assessment formats**: Miller's pyramid is a four-level framework ordering assessment targets from factual knowledge to real-world performance: "Knows" (best assessed with written exams), "Knows How" (written or oral exams), "Shows How" (performance-based assessments with standardized simulations or oral exams with standardized scenarios), and "Does" (clinic-based performance assessments). The review uses it to map which assessment formats and construct types are prevalent in physical therapy education and to identify which levels are underrepresented. (Tappan Rachel S. et al. (2026))
- **Miller's four competency levels as an organising framework for patient safety assessment**: The review uses Miller's four competency levels — 'knows', 'knows how', 'shows how', and 'does' — as its central organising framework for categorising assessment tools. At each level "the trainee should be able to demonstrate the ability to imitate or replicate a protocol, apply principles in a familiar situation, adapt principles to new situations, and associate new knowledge with previously learned principles". The review maps all 34 identified tools onto these levels and argues that ideally a toolbox would assess each competency at the Miller level fitting the stage of training reached. (Okuyama Ayako et al. (2011))
