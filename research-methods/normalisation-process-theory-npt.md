## Description
The study applies normalisation process theory (NPT) as a theoretical lens for exploring enablers and barriers to integrating IVR simulation into routine practice. NPT "argues that four mechanisms are crucial to determining whether a new intervention becomes successfully integrated into routine practice": coherence, cognitive participation, collective action and reflexive monitoring. The study's five themes (understanding, engagement, strategies in action, appraisal, psychological safety) were mapped onto these NPT domains, with psychological safety spanning all four.

- **Normalisation process theory (NPT) as a four-construct framework for understanding implementation of complex interventions**: Normalisation process theory explains how new practices become embedded (normalised) in routine work. The article uses NPT to structure collection, coding and interpretation of interview and process data, examining what staff do individually and collectively to embed the intervention. It is built around four constructs of social action: "the ways that people make sense of the practice (‘Coherence’)", cognitive participation, collective action, and reflexive monitoring. NPT was originally developed to explain why eHealth initiatives failed to become routine practice, making it apt for this e-learning intervention. (Keenan Julia et al. (2018))
