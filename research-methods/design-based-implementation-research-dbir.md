# Design-Based Implementation Research (DBIR) as an approach for co-developing differentiated instruction practice

> **Theory** · [All theories](index.md)

## Description
DBIR is an emerging research-and-development approach the article positions as the process for co-developing its differentiated instruction practice framework. It retains iterative analysis supporting incremental changes in teaching practice while adding collaborative, pragmatic problem-solving. The article states that "the approach engages four fundamental principles that define its benefits in educational settings": focus on persistent problems of practice from multiple stakeholders' perspectives; improvement through teams committed to iterative collaborative design; systematic inquiry developing both theory and knowledge about classroom learning and implementation; and developing capacity for sustaining change across the whole system, including universities as part of the ecology of supports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Iterative learning and feedback regimes engaging collaborative co-development
- Teams formed from multiple stakeholders, including teachers, researchers and administrators
#### Constraints
- The article notes DBIR is a comparatively new approach with a relatively small body of related literature
- Educational contexts are dynamic and complex rather than linear, requiring boundary systems of project priorities and goals

### Target Learners
- secondary mathematics teachers in regional Australia

### Target Learning Objectives
- implementing and sustaining differentiated instruction in secondary mathematics classrooms
