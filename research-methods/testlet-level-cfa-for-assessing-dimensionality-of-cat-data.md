## Description
Because CAT administrations give different students different items, item-level factor analysis is impractical: for MAP, "typical missing rates are around 98%". The study's method treats goal scores (item clusters/testlets) as observable variables in CFA and MGCFA, since "items in each CAT test event are balanced among goal areas based on content specifications". At the testlet level the local independence assumption still holds, though it might or might not hold at the item level.

## Description

The study's method treats goal scores (item clusters/testlets) as observable variables in CFA and MGCFA, since "items in each CAT test event are balanced among goal areas based on content specifications". ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Items within each goal area must be content homogeneous across persons so goal scores can serve as observable variables
- Test algorithms must survey item pools within goal or strand areas to assure domain coverage
#### Constraints
- Invariance across academic terms was not statistically tested
- The method tests the factor structure at the goal-score level, not the individual item level

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in grades 3 to 9

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading achievement
- Mathematics achievement
