# ACER framework for analyzing mathematical tool use in upper-division physics

## Description
ACER is an analytical framework designed to guide and structure investigations of students' difficulties with sophisticated mathematical tools in upper-division physics. It organizes expert-identified solution elements into four components: "Activation of the tool, Construction of the model, Execution of the mathematics, and Reflection on the result". The framework focuses on key nodes in solutions to reduce noise, is grounded in a resource view of knowledge, and is deliberately usable by instructors without deep backgrounds in learning theories. It targets back-of-the-book or exam-type problems rather than open-ended ones.

- **The ACER framework structures analysis of students' mathematical problem solving into four components: Activation, Construction, Execution, and Reflection**: ACER is an analytical framework designed to guide and structure investigations of students' difficulties with sophisticated mathematical tools in upper-division physics. It organizes expert-identified solution elements into four components: "Activation of the tool, Construction of the model, Execution of the mathematics, and Reflection on the result". The framework focuses on key nodes in solutions to reduce noise, is grounded in a resource view of knowledge, and is deliberately usable by instructors without deep backgrounds in learning theories. It targets back-of-the-book or exam-type problems rather than open-ended ones. (Wilcox et al. (2013))
- **The ACER framework as operationalized for delta function problems in junior-level electrostatics**: ACER (Activation, Construction, Execution, Reflection) is "an analytical framework that characterizes student difficulties with mathematics in upper-division physics by organizing the problem-solving process into four general components." The authors operationalize it for delta function use in expressing volume charge densities by having a content expert document solution steps and refining the outline against student work. The resulting element codes include A1, A2, C1, C2, C3, E1, R1, and R2, which were used to code exam solutions and classify interview moves. (Bethany R. Wilcox et al. (2014))
- **The ACER framework organizes upper-division students' use of mathematics in problem solving into four components**: ACER is an analytical framework developed through a modified task analysis with disciplinary experts to organize upper-division students' use of mathematics on canonical back-of-the-book problems. Expert negotiation "produced the four components of the ACER framework: Activation of the tool - selecting which mathematical tool will be used to facilitate a solution to the problem", followed by Construction of the model, Execution of the mathematics, and Reflection on the results. The authors stress the process is fluid and non-linear and that ACER organizes rather than models student work. (Caballero et al. (2015))
