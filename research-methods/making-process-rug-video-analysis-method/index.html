Making-Process-Rug video-analysis method¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Making-Process-Rug is a visual video-analysis method developed by the authors to trace "social-discursive, materially mediated, individual, and collaborative making processes" across a longitudinal co-invention project. Video is coded in fixed three-minute segments in ELAN using a sociomaterialgetCode template, then converted into layered, color-coded diagrams resembling woven rag rugs that show how verbal and embodied actions intertwine over time. It enables macro-level overview and later zooming to micro-level events such as process organizing, and quantitative comparison of action proportions.

Accounts¶

  • Making-Process-Rug video-analysis method: The Making-Process-Rug is a visual video-analysis method developed by the authors to trace "social-discursive, materially mediated, individual, and collaborative making processes" across a longitudinal co-invention project. Video is coded in fixed three-minute segments in ELAN using a sociomaterialgetCode template, then converted into layered, color-coded diagrams resembling woven rag rugs that show how verbal and embodied actions intertwine over time. It enables macro-level overview and later zooming to micro-level events such as process organizing, and quantitative comparison of action proportions. (Riikonen et al. (2020))
  • Making-Process-Rug video analysis framework for co-invention processes: A video analysis framework for tracing situationally interleaving social and material aspects of maker-centered collaborative learning. It provides "a systematic approach to examine situationally interleaving social and material aspects of knowledge-creating learning from video data" through pre-defined coding schemes, 3-min segment coding in ELAN, and graphical timeline charts (co-invention process rugs) that render patterns across design-process, computing, product-design, and science practices and teacher/tutor engagement visible. (Sini Davies et al. (2024))

Claims¶

  • Successful seventh-grade co-invention teams spent a much larger share of their embodied making actions on model making and experimenting than the unsuccessful team [+M]
  • Embodied design practices of sketching and experimenting co-occurred with the application of product design, computer engineering, and science practices, while model making was more often followed by it [+W]
  • Student teams differed markedly in their application of computer engineering and product design practices and in their share of off-task activity [+W]
