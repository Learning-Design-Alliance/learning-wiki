## Description
The article argues that judging quota policies requires separating the Humanities, the Physical and Mathematical sciences, and the Biological sciences, because in high pass-rate fields 'approval in early courses or the overall graduation rates are making all students appear equally good'. It further stresses that only sufficiently large datasets make systematic trends visible, and that policy effectiveness cannot be judged solely by the demographic composition of incoming classes but must be evaluated through academic trajectories.
