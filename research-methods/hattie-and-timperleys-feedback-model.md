## Description
The article adopts this theoretical framework to analyse the effectiveness of textual feedback. It "categorized effective feedback into three dimensions (i.e., feeding up, feeding back and feeding forward) and four-level focuses (i.e., task, process, self-regulatory, and self )". Feeding up tells students where they are going; feeding back answers how they are going; feeding forward addresses where to go next. The authors chose it because it "has been adopted widely in previous research to analyse textual feedback", unlike Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's principles judged too general.

- **Hattie and Timperley's feedback model: three dimensions (feeding up, feeding back, feeding forward) and four levels (task, process, self-regulation, self)**: The article adopts this theoretical framework to analyse the effectiveness of textual feedback. It "categorized effective feedback into three dimensions (i.e., feeding up, feeding back and feeding forward) and four-level focuses (i.e., task, process, self-regulatory, and self )". Feeding up tells students where they are going; feeding back answers how they are going; feeding forward addresses where to go next. The authors chose it because it "has been adopted widely in previous research to analyse textual feedback", unlike Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's principles judged too general. (Wei Dai et al. (2023))
- **Hattie and Timperley's three-question feedback model**: The article uses Hattie and Timperley's feedback model as its organising framework for what high-quality feedback addresses. The model identifies "three feedback questions, which should be addressed in order to provide high‐quality feedback: 'where am I going', 'how am I going' and 'where to next'". The EFeCT tool was chosen because it aligns with this model, and the study found the three questions were addressed the majority of the time, though 'where to next' was most often omitted. (Lee R. et al. (2026))
- **Feedback model addressing goals, progress and next steps (Hattie and Timperley) applied to CPR skill feedback**: The article uses Hattie and Timperley's feedback model to structure the individual feedback given after the practical test. The model "addresses the following questions: 'where am I going' (the goals), 'how am I going?' (feedback) and 'where to next?' (advice on progress)". In this trial the feedback aimed to reduce discrepancies between the student's present understanding and the goal, and the authors assumed the 'where to next?' question was the most important, while praise for a task seems less effective. (Nord Anette et al. (2017))
