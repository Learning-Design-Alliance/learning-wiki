# Research-and-development process for concept inventories

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes the development process distinguishing good concept inventories from typical exams: "their development involves extensive research and development by experts in physics education research" to ensure questions represent faculty-valued concepts, responses reflect real student thinking, and scores reliably reflect understanding. The typical process gathers student ideas, writes distractors in students' own wording, validates with student interviews and expert review, revises, administers at scale, and revises again.

- **FCI-style design features for research-based conceptual tests**: This pattern lists the design features the article identifies in successful research-based conceptual tests, modeled on the Force Concept Inventory: questions "written in everyday language without jargon or mathematics"; a focus on conceptual understanding rather than standard problems; distracters effective at "eliciting students’ preconceived ideas about the world"; and questions faculty initially believe are too easy so they are startled by poor student performance and motivated to examine their teaching. The article shows several of these are only partially attainable for quantum mechanics, where jargon cannot be avoided and students lack preconceptions. (McKagan et al. (2010))
