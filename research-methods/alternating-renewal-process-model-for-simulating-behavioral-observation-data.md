# Alternating renewal process model for simulating behavioral observation data

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The alternating renewal process is a statistical model for the stream of behavior as perceived during an observation session, used to simulate realistic direct-observation data. It works in two steps: first simulating a behavior stream of episodes separated by inter-response times with randomly generated durations, then calculating a summary measurement according to a specific recording system's rules. The article states "A key beneﬁt of using this model is that it mimics the physical process of observing a behavior stream and recording data as one does so," enabling emulation of distinctive features of real behavioral observation data.

- **Equilibrium alternating renewal process (EARP) model for the behavior stream within observation sessions**: The equilibrium alternating renewal process is the article's statistical model for the behavior stream within a single observation session. In an EARP, 'IETs are identically distributed random quantities, that event durations are also identically distributed, and that all IETs and event durations are mutually independent.' The model underpins the derivation of the expectations of recorded data for each recording procedure and of the estimators of the prevalence odds ratio. (Pustejovsky (2013))
