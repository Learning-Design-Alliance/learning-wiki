# Whole-network versus egocentric social network analysis paradigms

> **Research Method** · [All research methods](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article distinguishes two network paradigms. Whole-network (sociocentric) studies define a population boundary and seek complete accounting of ties among that node set, suiting questions about how a bounded group is structured. Egocentric (person-centered) studies instead focus on a single actor—the ego—and map that person's connected alters, comparing networks across egos; the goal is "to learn about the size, structure, content, or functions of the personal networks of some type of person." Ego networks can be extracted from whole networks or collected directly from egos, and need not overlap. The authors argue the egocentric framing is the one largely missing from PER.
