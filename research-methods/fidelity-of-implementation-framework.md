## Description
FOI, rooted in program evaluation, asserts that outcomes of student-centered practices are determined by how faithfully their critical components are enacted. The article defines FOI as "the extent to which an enacted program is consistent with the intended program model" (Century et al., 2010). A critical component is "a feature or component that is essential to the instructional practice," while variations in how components are enacted are adaptations, which may be productive, neutral, or unproductive for learning. The authors use this lens to explain variability in formative assessment effect sizes.

- **Fidelity of implementation framework: outcomes of instructional practices depend on whether their critical components are present during enactment**: FOI, rooted in program evaluation, asserts that outcomes of student-centered practices are determined by how faithfully their critical components are enacted. The authors use this lens to explain variability in formative assessment effect sizes. (Offerdahl E. G. et al. (2018))
- **Two-construct model of implementation fidelity: adherence plus competence including didacticism**: The article frames implementation fidelity — "the degree to which the treatment/programme was delivered as intended" — via two constructs: adherence, "the extent to which the core content of a programme was delivered as instructed", and competence, the quality of delivery including pacing, communication and interaction. For group formats it adds "didacticism", proxied by the proportion of facilitator speech time, measured objectively with ELAN software. The framework balances rigidity against flexibility and was operationalised into a checklist-based instrument for the SMILE(UK) course. (Wojewodka G et al. (2017))
