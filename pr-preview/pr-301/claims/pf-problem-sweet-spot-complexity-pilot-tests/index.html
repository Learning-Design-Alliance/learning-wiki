Evidence¶

Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel 2020¶

Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel. (2020). When failure fails to be productive: probing the effectiveness of productive failure for learning beyond STEM domains. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09525-2

q2 · i? · causal · r?

First pilot round tested four triads from two 10th-grade classes on the teacher-conference problem to check complexity. Students produced ideas without solving canonically; on average "6.5 solution ideas" were generated, typically including at least one of the four targeted errors.

"On average, the students generated 6.5 solution ideas in their groups and these solution ideas typically included at least one of the four errors, which are listed in Table 3 (see left column)."

Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel 2020 (2)¶

Valentina Nachtigall · Katja Serova · Nikol Rummel. (2020). When failure fails to be productive: probing the effectiveness of productive failure for learning beyond STEM domains. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09525-2

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Second pilot round tested both learning phases with one whole social science class in the out-of-school lab. The authors report the results "revealed that 10th graders were able to generate various solution ideas without solving the problem canonically", with an average of 4.6 ideas per group.

"Specifically, eight student groups developed an average of 4.6 ideas, which again included at least one of the four errors listed in Table 3."
