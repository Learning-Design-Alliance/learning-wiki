Evidence¶

Inge Molenaar & Sanna Järvelä 2014¶

Inge Molenaar & Sanna Järvelä. (2014). Sequential and temporal characteristics of self and socially regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-014-9114-2

q1 · i? · review · r2

Editorial interpretation of the special-issue contributions, identifying gaps rather than reporting a tested result. Most authors also pointed out the need for a more advanced understanding of the methods used, including guidelines for setting parameters and significance levels.

"These papers analysed self-regulated learning at a micro level and a number of authors (Bannert et al. 2014; Kinnebrew et al. 2014 and Molenaar & Chiu ( 2014) signposted the need for more micro level theory to further articulate our understanding of the temporal characteristics of self and socially regulated learning."

Related Claims¶

  • S-SRL strategy use is dynamic over time, contexts and student characteristics, supporting an event-based conceptualization of self and socially regulated learning — related
  • T-pattern analysis detected temporal-structure differences between children with specific language disorder and typically developing children that frequency and sequential lag analyses did not — related
  • During collaborative learning, low and high cognition have remarkably long lasting (6 lags) positive effects on themselves, planning induces low cognition which sparks high cognition, and scaffolding does not alter these sequences — related
  • Statistical discourse analysis of collaborative learning shows metacognitive planning organizes subsequent cognitive activity while relational activities help enact it — related
  • Process models of successful students come closer to self-regulated learning theory expectations than those of unsuccessful students, who do not evaluate their learning — related
  • Scaffolded conditions in Betty's Brain showed scaffolding-induced strategy use within the scaffolding time window although the three conditions did not differ in learning gains — related
