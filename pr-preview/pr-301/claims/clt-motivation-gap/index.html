CLT's theoretical framework hardly considers motivational processes, yet instructional-design interventions framed by CLT (e.g., fostering retrieval practice or elaboration) implicitly assume learners are sufficiently motivated, and CLT is not well-suited to explain such intervention effects. → Axel Grund 2024

Evidence¶

Axel Grund 2024¶

Axel Grund, Stefan Fries, Matthias Nückles, Alexander Renkl, Julian Roelle. (2024). When is Learning “Effortful”? Scrutinizing the Concept of Mental Effort in Cognitively Oriented Research from a Motivational Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09852-7

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Document analysis within the review: the authors note the 1998 CLT theory article mentions motivation only twice and the 2019 update only in reference titles, then argue that germane-load interventions "try to increase effort-by-allocation" via expectancy and value beliefs, which CLT does not theorize. Conceptual argument, no effect size.

"As such interventions do not address factors related to effort-by-complexity, CLT is not well-suited to explain the found intervention effects. Rather, such interventions try to increase effort-by-allocation."
