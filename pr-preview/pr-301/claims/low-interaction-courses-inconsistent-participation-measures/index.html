Evidence¶

Mohammed Saqr 2020¶

Mohammed Saqr, et al. (2020). Capturing the participation and social dimensions of computer-supported collaborative learning through social network analysis: which method and measures matter? International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 15:227–248. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-020-09322-6

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The study plotted centrality measures against course network characteristics (Fig. 8) to explain variability. The course with the fewest interactions (C2, "n= 439") showed insignificant correlations on several simplified and weighted measures; C10 (n=567) and C4 (n=810) similarly showed mixed or negative results.

"C2 had fewer interactions than all other courses (n= 439), as well as the insignificant correlations on simplified indegree, outdegree, and eigenvector centrality. It was also statistically insignificant in the weighted outdegree and Eigen centralities."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Multigraph closeness centrality is the most consistent of the three configurations for correlating closeness with performance, though less robust than degree and eigenvector centralities
  • Betweenness centrality shows the least consistent correlation with performance, being largely insignificant or negative across configurations except in one course
  • Multigraph indegree centrality is consistently and positively correlated with student performance across 12 PBL courses, with stronger correlations than other configurations in eight courses
  • Multigraph outdegree centrality is the most robust participation indicator, with significant positive correlations in all courses, while weighted outdegree was negatively correlated in two courses
