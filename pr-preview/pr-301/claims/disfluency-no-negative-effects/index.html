Evidence¶

Bjork 2016¶

Bjork, R. A., & Yue, C. L. (2016). Commentary: Is disfluency desirable? Metacognition Learning, 11, 133–137. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-016-9156-8

q1 · i? · review · r2

The authors' interpretive claim in the commentary's discussion, offered as an impression rather than a formal meta-analysis. They argue the lack of negative effects, alongside the lack of positive effects, suggests what matters for comprehension and storage happens after perceptual encoding.

"We have not carried out the necessary meta-analysis, but our impression is that the common-sense hypothesis that disfluency has negative effects can be soundly rejected."
