Evidence¶

Sebastian Maier 2026¶

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.20143

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Confirmatory item-level mixed-effects logistic regression (H2a) in the preregistered online experiment, testing the metacognitive feedback main effect on offloading across nine learning items. The article reports "lower odds of answer offloading" with OR = 0.47, 95% CI [0.22, 1.00], p = .026, one-sided.

"participants who received metacognitive feedback had lower odds of answer offloading than those who did not (𝑏=− 0.75,𝑆𝐸= 0.39,𝑂𝑅= 0.47, 95% CI[0.22, 1.00],𝑧=− 1.95,𝑝=. 026)"

Sebastian Maier 2026 (2)¶

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Confirmatory test of H2b on the six-item unaided final test in the same preregistered experiment. The article reports "higher odds of answering a subsequent unaided test item correctly" with OR = 1.51, 95% CI [0.98, 2.33], p = .030; the CI includes 1.0.

"they had higher odds of answering a subsequent unaided test item correctly (𝑏= 0.42,𝑆𝐸= 0.22,𝑂𝑅= 1.51, 95% CI[0.98, 2.33],𝑧= 1.88,𝑝=. 030)"
