Career-centric reverse academic planning: start from a career aspiration and map backward to courses, credentials, and experiences using skills taxonomies¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Instead of choosing a major then exploring careers, students begin with a career aspiration and the system works backward to the courses, experiences, and credentials that reach it. This depends on machine-readable occupational and skills taxonomies — O*NET-SOC classifying roughly one thousand occupations, the ESCO framework, and Lightcast's open skills taxonomy of more than thirty thousand skills — combined with large language models that parse job descriptions, map them to skills, compare them against course learning outcomes, and surface gaps.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Machine-readable skills taxonomies aligned against curriculum
  • Labor market, cost, and debt data for return-on-education estimates

Constraints¶

  • Earnings-maximizing recommendations can devalue socially important but modestly paid fields; the system should inform choices about value, not dictate them by salary alone

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students choosing majors and pathways
  • working and returning learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • career-aligned course sequencing
  • stackable and micro-credential accumulation
  • informed return-on-education decisions

Related Strategies¶

  • Surround students with college-educated mentors and college-going peers to build college aspirations
