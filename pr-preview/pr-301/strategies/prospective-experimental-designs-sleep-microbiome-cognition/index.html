Use prospective and experimental designs with multiple cognitive flexibility measures, objective sleep assessment, and larger samples to clarify sleep-microbiome-cognition relationships¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that future work move beyond cross-sectional correlational designs: "Prospective and experimental studies are needed to confirm these findings and determine whether improving microbiome health may buffer against sleep-related cognitive decline in older adults." The authors specify that future studies should include multiple measures of cognitive flexibility, objective sleep measures such as actigraphy and sleep diaries, screening for sleep-disordered breathing, larger samples permitting multiple-comparison adjustment, and examination of possible mechanisms such as systemic inflammation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Larger samples to accommodate statistical adjustment for multiple comparisons
  • Multiple measures of cognitive flexibility to improve measurement

Constraints¶

  • The current exploratory study did not utilize multiple-comparison adjustment
  • The most relevant taxonomic rank for analyses (e.g., phylum, genus) remains undetermined

Target Learners¶

  • healthy older adults

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive flexibility
  • executive function
