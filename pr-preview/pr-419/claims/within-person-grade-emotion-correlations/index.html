Evidence¶

Reinhard Pekrun 2022¶

Reinhard Pekrun, Herbert W. Marsh, Felix Suessenbach, Anne C. Frenzel, Thomas Goetz. (2022). School grades and students' emotions: Longitudinal models of within-person reciprocal effects. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2022.101626

q3 · i1 · associational · r3

Correlations of within-person centered scores extracted from the RI-CLPMs, PALMA sample (N = 3,425). The article prints the range "r = .12 to .55" with all ps < .001 for enjoyment and pride with grades.

"In addition, enjoyment and pride showed significant positive within-person correlations with grades (range r = .12 to .55; all ps < .001)."

q3 · i1 · associational · r3

Related Claims¶

  • Grades and positive affect show significant positive reciprocal within-person effects, and grades and negative affect show significant negative reciprocal effects — related
  • Within persons, better-than-usual math grades positively predict subsequent enjoyment and pride, and enjoyment and pride positively predict subsequent grades — related
  • Emotions measured early in the semester longitudinally predict cumulative grades and exam scores, with negative deactivating emotions (hopelessness, boredom) showing stronger coefficients than activating negative emotions — related
  • Within persons, grades negatively predict subsequent anger and hopelessness, and anger, anxiety, boredom, and hopelessness negatively predict subsequent grades — related
  • Aggregated state emotions show no superior incremental predictive validity over self-reported traits for academic outcomes; traits corrected for states were only systematically correlated with criteria for enjoyment and somewhat for pride — related
  • Test boredom correlates negatively with math grades but not with the math test score in Study 1 — related
