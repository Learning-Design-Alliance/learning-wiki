Evidence¶

Wong Patrick C. M. 2009¶

Wong Patrick C. M., Jin James Xumin, Gunasekera Geshri M., Abel Rebekah, Lee Edward R., Dhar Sumitrajit. (2009). Aging and Cortical Mechanisms of Speech Perception in Noise. Neuropsychologia 47(3), 693-703. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2008.11.032

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Brain-behavior correlation analysis in the older group (n = 12) for bilateral MTG clusters. Right MTG correlated with accuracy in Quiet (r = .786, p < .001) and SNR 20 (r = .678, p = .008) but not SNR -5 (r = .336, p = .143); left MTG showed a similar pattern (r = .552, p = .031; r = .492, p = .052; r = .328, p = .149).

"For the right MTG [63 −10 −4; 792 cubic-mm], a significant positive correlation was found in the Quiet [r = .786, p < .001] and SNR 20 [r = .678, p = .008] conditions but not the SNR -5 condition [r = .336, p = .143]."

Related Claims¶

  • Activation in frontal and parietal cognitive regions is positively correlated with speech-in-noise behavioral performance in older listeners, supporting a compensatory role — related
  • Group x condition interactions show condition-dependent activation differences in superior temporal, parietal, cingulate, medial frontal, and middle temporal regions — related
  • Older adults show reduced accuracy in severe noise (SNR -5) but perform comparably to younger adults in quiet and moderate noise (SNR 20) — related
  • Older adults show a more diffused cortical network involving frontal and ventral regions, whereas younger adults show a streamlined auditory network during speech-in-noise processing — related
  • Older adults show reduced activation in auditory cortex and increased activation in prefrontal and precuneus regions during speech-in-noise processing — related
