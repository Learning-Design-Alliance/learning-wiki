Evidence¶

Herbert W. Marsh 2026¶

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Decile-stratified multilevel fits (Fig. 4) of the same specification within achievement deciles: "the contextual class-average effect remained negative across every achievement decile", with minor fluctuations attributed to reduced effective sample size within strata.

"In the achievement-decile analyses, the contextual class-average effect remained negative across every achievement decile and showed no systematic attenuation toward zero at the upper end of the achievement distribution (Fig. 4)."

Herbert W. Marsh 2026 (2)¶

Herbert W. Marsh. (2026). Four Decades After the Big-Fish–Little-Pond Effect: Critical Boundary Tests and Integrative Models of Mean-Context, Ordinal-Standing, and Distributional-Scaling in 46 Countries. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10200-0

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Exploratory quantile-regression analyses of MSC across τ = 0.10–0.90 (Fig. 5): "the contextual effect remained negative and broadly stable", with the slight upper-tail attenuation attributed to ceiling compression in a negatively skewed MSC distribution.

"Across quantiles (τ = 0.10–0.90), the contextual effect remained negative and broadly stable, with only modest attenuation at the extreme upper tail of the MSC distribution (Fig. 5)."
