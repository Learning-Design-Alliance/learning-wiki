Edwards 2022¶

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Unpacking the Unique Relationship Between Set for Variability and Word Reading Development: Examining Word- and Child-Level Predictors of Performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Model 2 of the cross-classified generalized random-effects analysis, adding phonological awareness and number of competitors alongside general predictors. The model "explained 55.83% of the child-level variance" with the printed extra increments above general predictors.

"This model explained 55.83% of the child-level variance, 47.79% of the word-level variance, and 76.66% of the school-level variance, an extra 19.22%, 17.36%, and 26.48% respectively above the general predictors."

Related Claims¶

  • The impact of phonological competitors on SfV accuracy is greater for children with low decoding or low word reading skill — related
  • Word-level features predict SfV item difficulty: significant associations with length, order, and number of competitors, but not frequency, concreteness, or surprisal — related
  • Word attack and the word attack × max P→O surprisal interaction add variance beyond phonology, indicating phonology-to-orthography knowledge contributes to SfV performance — related
  • Word identification adds unique variance and moderates SfV performance, with a three-way word attack × word identification × max P→O surprisal interaction — related
  • Set for Variability performance is strongly correlated with word identification, word attack, phonological awareness, and vocabulary in grades 2–5 — related
