Evidence¶

Burton 2023¶

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

q3 · i3 · causal · r3

Experiment 1 solved-versus-not-solved outcome analysis: AS JOS effect η²p = .37; NS JOSs showed the reverse pattern, F(1, 93) = 56.40, p < .001, η²p = .38. "Anagrams that had received an AS JOS were more frequent among solved anagrams than among not-solved anagrams".

"Anagrams that had received an AS JOS were more frequent among solved anagrams than among not-solved anagrams in the solving phase, F(1, 93) = 53.70, MSE = 3.75, p < .001, η2 p = .37"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • S JOSs were discriminating but not predictive of later solving success in Experiments 1 and 2 — reports the opposite
  • In a self-regulated solving phase, S JOSs predicted solving success and AS and NS JOSs predicted effort regulation — related
  • Training improved the predictiveness of AS and NS JOSs but not S JOSs, indicating training increased solving during the JOS task rather than JOS predictiveness — related
  • Solvable judgments of solvability discriminate solvable from unsolvable anagrams even after excluding already-solved judgments — related
  • Training with longer-duration anagrams improved AS JOS discrimination in blocks 1–3 but not S JOS discrimination — related
  • JOS discrimination decreased across blocks as anagram presentation duration was reduced, driven by different mechanisms for AS versus S JOSs — related
