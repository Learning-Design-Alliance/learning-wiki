Evidence¶

Hornstein 2025¶

Hornstein, J., Keller, M. V., Greisel, M., Dresel, M., & Kollar, I. (2025). Enhancing the Peer-Feedback Process Through Instructional Support: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10017-3

q4 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analysis of 18 studies (k = 92 effect sizes) for feedback provision support on quality of feedback provision: g = 0.72, 95% CI [0.32, 1.12], p < 0.01 (Table 4).

"Regarding RQ4, our analyses revealed small to medium-sized positive effects of feedback provision support on the quality of feedback provision and on the quality of feedback reception. For subject-matter-related knowledge, we did not find a significant effect of feedback provision support"

Related Claims¶

  • During feedback provision, both content-specific support (g = 0.48) and generic support (g = 0.55) positively affect peer-feedback processes and outcomes, with no significant difference between them — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • During feedback provision, content-specific support improves feedback provision quality (g = 0.75) but not knowledge, while generic support improves both provision quality (g = 0.70) and subject-matter-related knowledge (g = 0.55) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Feedback provision support has a positive medium-sized effect (g = 0.51) whereas feedback reception support shows no significant effect — related
  • Instructional support improves quality of feedback provision (g = 0.64) and quality of feedback reception (g = 0.49) but not subject-matter-related knowledge — related
  • Neither content-specific nor generic support during feedback reception shows significant effects on feedback reception quality or subject-matter-related knowledge, but the evidence base is too small to interpret — related
  • A feedback provision scaffold of prompts plus a sample solution significantly improves the quality of feedback messages pre-service teachers provide on peers' case analyses — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Neither scaffold significantly improves pre-service teachers' EIR application skills — related
  • Benefits of learning with more than two external representations primarily depend on the provision of appropriate instructional support. — related
