Evidence¶

M. Zee 2016¶

M. Zee, P.F. de Jong, H.M.Y. Koomen. (2016). Teachers' self-efficacy in relation to individual students with a variety of social-emotional behaviors: A multilevel investigation. Journal of Educational Psychology, 108(7), 1013-1027. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000106

q3 · i? · associational · r?

The multilevel analysis tested teaching experience and perceived classroom misbehavior as moderators of behavior-TSE associations. Perceived misbehavior moderated the externalizing-behavior-management link; the article reports it "exacerbated the negative association." No effect sizes are printed.

"teachers' perceived levels of classroom misbehavior exacerbated the negative association between externalizing student behavior and TSE for behavior management."

Related Claims¶

  • Students' externalizing behavior is negatively associated with teachers' self-efficacy for instructional strategies, behavior management, student engagement, and emotional support — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Preliminary evidence suggests perceived problem behavior effects on teaching stress are mediated by teachers' perceptions of relationship conflict — related
  • Perceived student misbehavior is consistently identified as a key source of teacher stress and is positively associated with emotional exhaustion — related
  • Classroom Level Adversity is associated with lower classroom quality, more student externalizing behavior, and poorer teacher well-being (as reported in prior studies cited by the essay) — related
