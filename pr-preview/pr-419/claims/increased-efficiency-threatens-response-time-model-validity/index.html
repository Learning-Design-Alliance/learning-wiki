Evidence¶

Steven Wise 2015¶

Steven Wise. (2015). Response time as an indicator of test taker speed: assumptions meet reality. Measurement: Interdisciplinary Research and Perspectives, 13(3–4), 186–188. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/response-time-as-an-indicator-of-test-taker-speed-assumptions-meet-reality doi:10.1080/15366367.2015.1105062

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Theoretical argument in the commentary: the increasing efficiency effect "appears to (at least sometimes) represent an additional systematic factor influencing response time", violating a core assumption of response-time-based psychometric models. No empirical test or effect size is reported.

"Whatever its nature, however, this increasing efficiency effect appears to (at least sometimes) represent an additional systematic factor influencing response time. And whenever this effect is present to a material degree, it threatens the validity of models that presume that item response time is systematically influenced only by item time intensity and the test taker's ability and working speed."

Related Claims¶

  • Computer-based testing's routine capture of item-level response times has driven increased interest in applications of response time to measuring ability and achievement — related
  • The increased efficiency effect confounds attempts to exploit the speed-ability trade-off central to many response-time models — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Test takers tend to increase their time efficiency during MAP test events, an effect the author observed in data from 2 very different testing programs — related
  • How commonly the increased efficiency effect is present in test data is unclear — related
  • Processing speed measured with response times is better represented as four distinct cognitive processes (planning, attention, simultaneous, successive) than as a unitary speed factor — related
  • Tests may appear to measure an ability while actually measuring an unintended one, such as speed of writing figures or attention — related
