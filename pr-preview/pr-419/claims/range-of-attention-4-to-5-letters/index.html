Evidence¶

Whipple 1910¶

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Tachistoscope experiment with six college students exposed to 5-, 6- and 7-place letter series for about 0.08 sec; scoring credited one unit per correct letter. The article reports the average "lies between 4 and 5 (4.82)".

"the average number of letters that can be grasped in a single exposure lies between 4 and 5 (4.82)—a result in close accord with previous tachistoscopic work."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Longer exposures barely increase apprehension of nonsense material and objects, but materially increase perception of quickly assimilable sense-material such as poetry — related
  • Longer letter series yield somewhat higher average scores because they offer opportunities for occasional high scores via mental grouping — related
  • Individual differences in quick visual apprehension are striking and material-specific, negating the notion of a general ability to apprehend visually — related
  • Practise has a very slight effect on the range of attention, explicable by habituation and grouping tricks rather than any expansion of the range itself — related
  • Systematic practise gives little warrant for markedly improving adults' quick visual perception; the slight poetry gain is attributed to familiarity with the poem — related
