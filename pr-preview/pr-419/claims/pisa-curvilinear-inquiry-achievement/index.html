q2 · i? · review · r3

Correlational analyses of PISA 2015 self-report data reviewed here show the relation is "not linear but curvilinear"; the review notes the pattern was also found in TIMSS data and confirmed in a meta-analysis covering PISA and TIMSS data.

"the relation between the frequency of inquiry learning activities and science achievement scores is not linear but curvilinear, meaning that inquiry activities do have a positive relation with science performance up to a certain level"

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Chen et al. (2017) found from PISA 2015 data that "the highest average score increase was associated with inquiry learning in combination with forms of direct, teacher-based instruction," which they called the sweet spot of instruction.

"Chen et al. (2017), who found on the basis of PISA 2015 data that the highest average score increase was associated with inquiry learning in combination with forms of direct, teacher-based instruction."
