Evidence¶

Gunderson 2018¶

Gunderson, E. A., Sorhagen, N. S., Gripshover, S. J., Dweck, C. S., Goldin-Meadow, S., & Levine, S. C. (2018). Parent Praise to Toddlers Predicts Fourth Grade Academic Achievement via Children's Incremental Mindsets. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000444

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Follow-up path analyses replacing the composite motivational framework score with the 14-item trait beliefs subscore (α = .65). Indirect paths "were significant as well" in both domains with well-fitting models.

"the indirect paths from process praise to trait beliefs to 4th grade achievement were significant as well (math: bias-corrected bootstrap CI95% = .02 to .33; reading comprehension: bias-corrected bootstrap CI95% = 1.52 to 20.25)"

Gunderson 2018 (2)¶

Gunderson, E. A., Sorhagen, N. S., Gripshover, S. J., Dweck, C. S., Goldin-Meadow, S., & Levine, S. C. (2018). Parent Praise to Toddlers Predicts Fourth Grade Academic Achievement via Children's Incremental Mindsets. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000444

q3 · i0 · associational · r2

Parallel path analyses using the 8-item learning goals subscore (α = .69). Direct paths from praise to learning goals and from learning goals to achievement were non-significant; indirect paths also included zero.

"the direct paths from process praise to learning goals were not significant (ps > .10, percentile bootstrap CIs95% included zero), and the direct paths from learning goals to 4th grade achievement were not significant (ps > 0.70, percentile bootstrap CIs95% included zero)"
