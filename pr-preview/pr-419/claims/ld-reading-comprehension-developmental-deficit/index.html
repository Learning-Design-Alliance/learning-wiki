Evidence¶

Quinn 2020¶

Quinn, Jamie M.; Wagner, Richard K.; Petscher, Yaacov; Roberts, Greg; Menzel, Andrew J.; Schatschneider, Christopher. (2020). Differential Co-Development of Vocabulary Knowledge and Reading Comprehension for Students with and without Learning Disabilities. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000382

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Multiple-group LCS model with measurement and parameter constraints, fit to the full sample; the final freed model fit well (RMSEA = .060, CFI = .98) and showed group differences in intercepts, slopes, and autoregressive pathways. The quoted intercept means are descriptive model estimates.

"Typically developing children on average began over a standard deviation higher on reading comprehension (μRNoLD=0.04) than their peers with an LD(μRLD=−1.24)."

Quinn 2020 (2)¶

Quinn, Jamie M.; Wagner, Richard K.; Petscher, Yaacov; Roberts, Greg; Menzel, Andrew J.; Schatschneider, Christopher. (2020). Differential Co-Development of Vocabulary Knowledge and Reading Comprehension for Students with and without Learning Disabilities. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000382

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Authors' interpretation of the multiple-group model and split violin and trajectory plots: average growth lines for the LD group remained far apart from the non-LD group with little overlap in score distributions through fourth grade. The deficit conclusion is the authors' reading of the fitted model, not a separate test.

"Our results indicated that students not only started lower in reading comprehension when they had a school-identified LD, but they also did not catch up to their typically developing peers in reading comprehension by grade 4. This is indicative of a development deficit, as they were not nearing the average reading comprehension skills of their typically developing peers."
