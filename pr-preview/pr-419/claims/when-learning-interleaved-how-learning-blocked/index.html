Evidence¶

Nan Li 2013¶

Nan Li, William W. Cohen, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2013). Problem Order Implications for Learning. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-013-0005-5

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' interpretive summary of the simulation study: negative feedback in interleaved orders serves as negative training examples for when-learning, while the article conjectures that with memory limitations, how-learning would benefit more from blocked orders because blocked sequences keep same-type positive examples within a fixed memory window.

"In summary, the study provides theoretical support for the claim that learning when to apply a skill benefits more from interleaved problem orders."

Related Claims¶

  • SimStudent given interleaved-ordering curricula received more negative feedback in stoichiometry and fraction addition, supporting more effective when-learning — related
  • In SimStudent simulations across three math and science domains, interleaved-order curricula yielded as effective or more effective learning than blocked-order curricula — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In stoichiometry, SimStudent trained on interleaved-ordering curricula achieved significantly higher step scores than with blocked-ordering curricula — related
  • Interleaving benefits low-skilled students more than high-skilled students when learning math problem categories — related
  • Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning for visual and mathematical materials, but not for expository texts or word categories — related
  • Interleaved practice yields better category learning than blocked practice, and students who choose interleaved practice outperform those who choose blocked practice on novel materials — related
