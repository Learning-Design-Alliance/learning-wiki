Evidence¶

Richard E. West 2017¶

Richard E. West, Gregory S. Williams. (2017). 'I don't think that word means what you think it means': A proposed framework for defining learning communities. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-017-9535-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Rovai (2002), that community can exist independently from geography and campuses as long as members exhibit traditional community behaviors, and that "access can be attained without physical presence in the same geographic space". This is the review's attribution, not a new empirical test.

"He argued that access can be attained without physical presence in the same geographic space."

Richard E. West 2017 (2)¶

Richard E. West, Gregory S. Williams. (2017). 'I don't think that word means what you think it means': A proposed framework for defining learning communities. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-017-9535-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's synthesis of access-boundary scholarship concludes that while member access is agreed upon, "the amount of access and the nature of presence needed to qualify as a community are still undefined". The review notes ongoing negotiation between physical and virtual conceptions of presence.

"Researchers agree that community members should have access to each other, but the amount of access and the nature of presence needed to qualify as a community are still undefined."
