Evidence¶

Putwain 2021¶

Putwain, D. W., Wood, P., & Pekrun, R. (2021). Achievement emotions and academic achievement: Reciprocal relations and the moderating influence of academic buoyancy. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000637

q3 · i? · associational · r3

A latent-interaction SEM using the LMS approach in 1,242 primary students found "a statistically significant interaction was shown for Academic Buoyancy × Anxiety" but not for enjoyment or boredom interactions. Probing showed the negative anxiety–performance relation was strongest at high buoyancy and non-significant at low buoyancy; the authors read this as performance benefiting from lower anxiety combined with higher buoyancy, with the benefit of buoyancy declining at higher anxiety, so that buoyancy "protected performance at lower levels of anxiety" rather than at high anxiety. The design is correlational (emotions and buoyancy self-reported, test scores over four waves), so the moderation describes how the relations differ, not a causal effect of buoyancy.

"A statistically significant interaction was shown for Academic Buoyancy × Anxiety, but not Academic Buoyancy × Enjoyment or Academic Buoyancy × Boredom."

Putwain 2021 (2)¶

Putwain, D. W., Wood, P., & Pekrun, R. (2021). Achievement emotions and academic achievement: Reciprocal relations and the moderating influence of academic buoyancy. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000637

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Simple slope probing of the Buoyancy × Anxiety interaction showed the negative anxiety–performance relation strengthened with buoyancy: "At +1SD buoyancy a negative relation was shown between anxiety and performance (B = -3.91, SE = 1.27)".

"At +1SD buoyancy a negative relation was shown between anxiety and performance (B = -3.91, SE = 1.27). This relation was weaker at mean buoyancy (B = -1.38, SE = .34) and at -1SD buoyancy became non-significant (B = 1.16, SE = 1.25)."
