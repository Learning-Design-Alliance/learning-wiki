Evidence¶

Bosch 2017¶

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

q2 · i1 · associational · r2

Partial correlations between scaffolded-phase affect incidence and fadeout score, controlling for gender, SAT, scaffolded score, and hint usage, in the 99-student lab study; the article reports boredom r=−.149 and frustration r=−.218.

"boredom ( r=−.149) and frustration ( r=−.218) were negative correlated with learning while transitions between confusion → frustration (r=.1 03), frus tration → confusion (r=.105), and boredom → engagement (r=.282) were positively corre-lated with learning."

Related Claims¶

  • Confusion and frustration followed incorrect solution submissions and preceded hint requests, while coding was the central activity surrounding all affective states — related
  • Five of six predicted affect transitions were significant and aligned with the affect dynamics model, but boredom transitioned to engagement rather than to frustration — related
  • During novices' first programming lesson, engagement, confusion, frustration, boredom, and curiosity are the dominant affective states, collectively accounting for approximately 73% of affect judgments — related
  • Frustration was marginally significantly negatively correlated with learning outcomes, while boredom was unexpectedly positively correlated — related
  • Frustration during the design process was related to unexpected events, with more unexpected events yielding greater frustration and worse perceived performance — related
