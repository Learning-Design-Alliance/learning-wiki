Evidence¶

Mikami 2015¶

Mikami, A. Y., & Normand, S. (2015). The Importance of Social Contextual Factors in Peer Relationships of Children with ADHD. Curr Dev Disord Rep. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40474-014-0036-0

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports conflicting findings: some studies of children with externalizing behavior or ADHD found friendship buffers rejection effects, while other ADHD-sample work found "only peer rejection, and not friendship, contributed to negative outcomes such as depression".

"other work involving an ADHD sample found that only peer rejection, and not friendship, contributed to negative outcomes such as depression 6 and 8 years later"

Related Claims¶

  • In an East London adolescent cohort, adjusting for friend or family support barely changed the association between being bullied and boys' later depressive symptoms, which the authors read as support not being sufficient protection — related
  • Students perceive peer interaction—companionship, support as a social buffer, and cooperation toward shared goals—as a key element of fun in learning — related
  • Children with ADHD show marked peer rejection and friendship impairment: approximately four times more likely to be rejected, 52–82% scoring over 1 SD above the classroom mean in rejection, and nearly twice as likely to have no classroom friends
  • Peer rejection incrementally predicts subsequent behavioral, emotional, and academic maladjustment in children with ADHD beyond ADHD status itself — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Existing child-focused treatments for ADHD, including the MTA's intensive behavioral management and medication, and clinic-based social skills training, have not improved peers' liking of children with ADHD — related
  • Children with ADHD show medium-to-large peer problems relative to typically developing youth, with effect sizes from d=.72 (no comorbidities) to d=1.25 (comorbid conduct problems) — related
