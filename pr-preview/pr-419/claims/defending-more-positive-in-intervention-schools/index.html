Evidence¶

Laninga-Wijnen 2023¶

Laninga-Wijnen, L., van den Berg, Y. H. M., Garandeau, C. F., Mulder, S., & de Castro, B. O. (2023). Does Being Defended Relate to Decreases in Victimization and Improved Psychosocial Adjustment Among Victims? Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000712

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Preregistered sensitivity analysis running the main models separately for intervention and control schools (Tables S3 and S4). The article reports defending "worked out more positively in intervention schools" with the printed coefficients; no effect sizes were given.

"In general, they suggested that defending worked out more positively in intervention schools. Speciﬁcally, in intervention schools, nondefended victims had lower feelings of belonging ( B = –.247, SE = .094, p = .009) and higher depressive symptoms ( B = .109, SE = .050, p = .031) at T2 than defended victims."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Victims with at least one defender at the start of the school year report higher feelings of belonging at year's end than nondefended victims, but lower belonging than nonvictims — related
  • Defended and nondefended victims did not differ significantly in severity of victimization, self-esteem, or depressive symptoms at the end of the school year — related
  • In exploratory victims-only analyses, defended victims had lower depressive symptoms at T2 than nondefended victims, an effect not found in the full sample — related
  • Classroom descriptive and popularity norms for defending did not moderate the links between being defended and victims' adjustment or victimization — related
  • Victim and defender categories were highly unstable across the school year: only 16.8% of initially nondefended victims remained nondefended victims at T2 — related
