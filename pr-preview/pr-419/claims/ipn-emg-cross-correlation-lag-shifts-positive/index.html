Evidence¶

Parras 2024¶

Parras, G. G., Delgado-García, J. M., López-Ramos, J. C., Gruart, A., & Leal-Campanario, R. (2024). Cerebellar interpositus nucleus exhibits time-dependent errors and predictive responses. npj Science of Learning 9, 12. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00224-y

q3 · i3 · causal · r1

Cross-correlation analysis of averaged rectified EMG and averaged spike count over the 350 ms CS-US period, across conditioning sessions. Printed correlation coefficients r = 0.7063, r = 0.7746, and r = 0.7781 are large correlations (|r| ≥ .37).

"The strongest correlation in the initial conditioning session (r = 0.7063) is found when the EMG has shifted backward 10 ms. In contrast, during the halfway session, the maximum coefﬁcient was observed at lag 0 (r = 0.7746)— that is, during the middle process of the learning, the two signals were strongly related. Importantly, in the ﬁnal session, maximum cross-correlation was found at lag +1( r = 0.7781)."

Related Claims¶

  • Conditioning brings eyelid EMG onset forward by roughly 150 ms, and IPn firing precedes the detected EMG change — related
  • IPn firing decreases in spontaneous and late time windows and increases during CS and CS-US windows as conditioning progresses — related
  • Percentage of conditioned eyelid responses increases across conditioning sessions, from 7% (initial) to 25% (halfway) to 81% (final)
  • On CS-alone trials, the significant neuronal difference at the US time vanishes by the final session, while a genuine eyelid conditioned response emerges during stimulus omission
