Evidence¶

Nye 2014¶

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review reports corpus analyses of roughly 100 hours of non-expert tutoring (research methods/statistics and algebra) that produced this frame; tutors showed little active student learning, targeted error remediation, or affective adaptation. No effect size printed.

"Collaborative reasoning progressed in a 5-step tutoring frame: (1) Tutor poses a question/problem, (2) Student attempts to answer, (3) Tutor provides brief evaluation as feedback, (4) Collaborative interaction to improve the answer, and (5) Tutor checks if student understands"

Nye 2014 (2)¶

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review reports question-rate comparisons from the human tutoring corpus analyses; rates are descriptive per-hour counts with no effect size printed.

"Compared to classroom instruction, students asked many more questions (~26/h vs. 0.1/h) and were asked more questions (104/h vs. 69/h; Graesser and Person 1994)"

Related Claims¶

  • Ma et al. (2014) meta-analysis: step-based ITSs outperformed no tutoring and large-group teacher-led instruction, but showed no differences against small-group or one-to-one human tutoring — related
