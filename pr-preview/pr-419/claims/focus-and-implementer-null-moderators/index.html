Evidence¶

Anna H. Miller 2025¶

Anna H. Miller, Daniel R. Espinas, Daniel McNeish, & Marcia A. Barnes. (2025). Dosage Response in Intensive Mathematics Interventions for Early Elementary Students With or At-Risk for Mathematics Learning Disability. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 91. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10070-y

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Null moderator result from Model 3 of the meta-regression across 164 effect sizes (15 number and operations, 11 word-problem solving interventions). The authors report no differences at the median dosage and no variation with hours of intervention.

"The main effect of instructional focus (word-problem solving as the reference group) on effect sizes was not significant (β = − 0.01; 95% CI [− 0.44, 0.43]), indicating that there were no differences in effect size estimates between the number and operations interventions and the word-problem solving interventions"

Anna H. Miller 2025 (2)¶

Anna H. Miller, Daniel R. Espinas, Daniel McNeish, & Marcia A. Barnes. (2025). Dosage Response in Intensive Mathematics Interventions for Early Elementary Students With or At-Risk for Mathematics Learning Disability. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 91. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10070-y

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Null moderator result from Model 3; 19 treatments (k = 146) were researcher-implemented and five (k = 18) school-staff implemented. The authors report no differences as a function of implementer, and the interaction with dosage was also insignificant.

"The main effect of intervention implementer (school personnel as the reference group) on effect sizes was insignificant (β = 0.02, 95% CI [− 3.5, 3.5]), indicating that there were no differences in effect size estimates as a function of who implemented the intervention"
