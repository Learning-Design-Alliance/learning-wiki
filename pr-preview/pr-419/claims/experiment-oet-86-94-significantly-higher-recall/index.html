Evidence¶

Luke G. Eglington and Philip I. Pavlik Jr 2020¶

Luke G. Eglington and Philip I. Pavlik Jr. (2020). Optimizing practice scheduling requires quantitative tracking of individual item performance. npj Science of Learning 5:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00074-4

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Crowdsourced experiment (Experiment 2, Ns per condition 59, 53, 57, 50, 56, 16) with 22 min practice and a 3-day delayed final test; the Kruskal–Wallis test showed "a signiﬁcant difference across conditions in average performance on the ﬁnal test, H(4) = 16.5, p < 0.01."

"A one-way non-parametric analysis of variance (Kruskal –Wallis H test) indicated a signiﬁcant difference across conditions in average performance on the ﬁnal test, H(4) = 16.5, p < 0.01."
