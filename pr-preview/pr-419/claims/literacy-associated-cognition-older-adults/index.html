Evidence¶

Bennett JS 2012¶

Bennett JS, Boyle PA, James BD, Bennett DA. (2012). Correlates of health and financial literacy in older adults without dementia. BMC Geriatrics. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2318-12-30

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional regression analysis of global cognition (19-test composite) and five domains in 556 older adults without dementia. The 90th- vs 10th-percentile literacy contrast yielded a global cognition difference of "about 1.51 standard deviations higher", robust to income and chronic conditions adjustment.

"a person with the 90 th percentile total literacy score had a global cognition score about 1.51 standard deviations higher than a person with the 10 th percentile total literacy score (Table 2). Results were similar in fully adjusted model."

Related Claims¶

  • Higher total literacy is associated with more frequent participation in cognitive, physical, and social activities in older adults without dementia — related
  • Financial literacy, but not health literacy, is associated with mental health indices in older adults without dementia — related
  • Lower health literacy and weaker cognitive abilities are associated with poorer performance on common healthcare tasks — related
  • The association of total literacy with physical activity is not statistically significant after adjustment for income and chronic medical conditions — related
  • Literacy is associated with functional status in older adults, but after full adjustment total literacy remains associated only with instrumental activities of daily living and mobility disability — related
  • Poorer current cognitive functioning in all domains is associated with low health literacy in older adults — related
  • Participants with low health literacy had poorer cognitive functioning in all domains and were more often (very) low-educated — related
