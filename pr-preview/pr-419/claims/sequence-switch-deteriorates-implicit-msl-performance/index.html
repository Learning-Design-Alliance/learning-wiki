Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025¶

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Within-group repeated-measures comparisons in experiment 1 (bimanual implicit SRTT). Performance "was significantly reduced compared to the end of session 3" for New, Compatible, and Incompatible groups, while the Same group did not change (p = 1.). No effect size printed.

"When the new, compatible, or incompatible sequence was introduced at the start of session 4, the performance was signiﬁcantly reduced compared to the end of session 3 (p = 0.002, p < 0.001, p = 0.001, for the New, Compatible, and Incompatible group, respectively), while the performance in the Same group did not change between the end of session 3 and the start of session 4 (p =1 . ) ."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In experiment 1, novel movement transitions were performed better in the Compatible and Incompatible groups than in the New group, while initially learned transitions were retained better in the Same group. — related
  • In implicit motor sequence learning, sequence-specific performance shows a significant timepoint-by-group interaction in both a 4-day and a 2-day training design. — related
  • After three prior training sessions, implicit learning of sequences compatible in movement transitions (both Compatible and Incompatible groups) is faster than learning a completely new sequence in experiment 1. — related
  • The facilitative learning effect in implicit MSL depends on the amount of previous training: after only one prior session, the Compatible group did not outperform the New group and no novel-transition integration advantage emerged. — related
  • By the end of the final training session, Compatible and Incompatible groups reach the performance level of the Same group, whereas the New group does not, in both experiments. — related
  • Compatible and Incompatible groups performed comparably across learning, indicating that the basis of compatibility in implicit MSL is shared movement transitions rather than ordinal positions. — related
