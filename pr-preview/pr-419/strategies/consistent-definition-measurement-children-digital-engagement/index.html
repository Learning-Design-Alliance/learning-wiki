Adopt more consistent approaches to defining and measuring children's digital engagement in educational research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's central recommendation is that researchers studying children's engagement with digital technology in educational spaces should define and measure engagement more consistently and explicitly. The review states: "This review highlights the need for more consistent approaches in defining and measuring children's digital engagement." Given that most included studies lacked explicit definitions, theories or measures, researchers should state their engagement conceptualisation and select measures aligned to it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers should explicitly define engagement and link it to a theory or framework when studying children's digital engagement

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • children aged 5-8 in primary school

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving how children's digital engagement is conceptualised and measured in research

Claims¶

  • In definitions of children's digital engagement, the most prominent themes were participation (44.4%), effort (38.9%) and interpersonal skills and behaviour (33.3%) [+W]
  • Among studies of young children's digital engagement in educational spaces, engagement was explicitly defined in only 18 of 88 studies (19.3%), theorised in 8 (9.1%), and measured in 31 (35.2%) [+W]
  • Most studies of children's digital engagement provided no explicit theory, definition, or measure of engagement, and only one study provided all three [+W]
  • In measures of children's digital engagement, the predominant themes were enjoyment (45.2%), participation (35.5%) and interest (25.8%) [+W]
  • Questionnaires were the most common measure of children's digital engagement (11 studies), followed by observation (8) and checklists (5) [+W]
  • The review corpus comprised 88 studies from 2661 screened records, mostly in formal school contexts, spanning quantitative, qualitative and mixed methodologies [+W]
