Keep feedback on instrumental actions immediate to preserve goal-directed control¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends attending to the timing of rewards in real-world decision settings. Because even a 5-second feedback delay lowered response rates, revaluation sensitivity and contingency knowledge, the authors argue that reward timing "can weaken the perceived action-outcome link, highlighting the need for interventions that enhance it for long-term goal adherence." They suggest domains such as finance, medicine and retirement savings, where rewards arrive long after actions, may suffer weakened causal attribution of actions to delayed outcomes rather than mere temporal discounting.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Actions must retain a perceivable, timely link to their outcomes for the goal-directed system to compute a strong action-outcome rate correlation.

Constraints¶

  • Only a single 5 s delay was tested, so how other delay durations modulate goal-directed control remains an open question the article states.

Target Learners¶

  • Adult decision-makers in finance, medicine, and retirement savings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term goal adherence through strengthened causal attribution of actions to their delayed outcomes

Affordances¶

  • Rate Correlation Theory Goal Directed Control

Claims¶

  • Delayed feedback lowers response rates in an instrumental task compared with immediate feedback [+W]
  • Delayed feedback reduces sensitivity to outcome revaluation: a Delay × Val interaction in the pooled data [+W]
  • Delayed feedback weakens self-reported action-outcome contingency knowledge [+W]
  • A mixed-effects model accounting for individual differences confirms the Delay × Val interaction in Experiments 1 and 3 and in the pooled data [+W]
