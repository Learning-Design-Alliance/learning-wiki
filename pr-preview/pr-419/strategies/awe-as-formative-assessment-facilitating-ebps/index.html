Position AWE as a formative assessment tool that facilitates evidence-based practices in writing instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames AWE as an "AI-enhanced formative assessment tool" intended "for use as a formative assessment tool" that facilitates multiple evidence-based practices in writing instruction. Its claimed affordances include saving teachers grading time, providing a supportive writing environment, enabling timely, frequent and personalized feedback loops, aiding higher-level feedback, and encouraging revision. Statewide deployment as a state-contracted tool is the article's exemplar of this strategy at scale.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need support materials and training to integrate formative assessment results into classroom instruction, which the developer supplied via manuals, multimedia training, a help line, and an instructional guide

Constraints¶

  • Effects vary with context, including the associated instructional program and the teacher's role in implementation, per the qualitative reviews the article cites

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in writing classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Writing proficiency, revision behavior, and formative feedback use

Claims¶

  • Customized AI writing systems show benefits in writing quality, learning processes, assessment reliability, and feedback literacy [+M]
  • A review of 50 studies reports that AWE tools can improve the organization and overall quality of writing and encourage self-directed learning [+W]
  • AWE tools such as Grammarly, Quillbot and DeepL Write significantly improve writing precision, fluency and grammatical proficiency through immediate and customized feedback [+W]
  • Overreliance on AWE may lower learners' critical thinking and creativity [-W]
  • AWE feedback has limited depth and questionable accuracy for evaluating complex writing aspects [-W]
  • One year of Utah Compose exposure raised students' SAGE ELA scale scores by 9.14 points (ES = 0.12) versus no exposure [+W]
  • Cumulative Utah Compose effects on ELA scores diminished each year and peaked after three years of implementation [~W]
  • The fourth year of Utah Compose exposure showed no statistically significant additional effect on ELA scores [-W]
