Embed ethical safeguards — informed consent, moral decision-making frameworks, explainable AI — when deploying AI agents in MOOC research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 associational), q1–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that VLC research follow the Belmont Report principles: "VLCs should not violate learners 'personal autonomy, bene ﬁts brought by the VLCs should outweigh risks, and the bene ﬁts and risks should be distributed fairly among learners." It proposes mechanisms to obtain informed consent, emerging moral decision-making frameworks to reduce risk of inappropriate social engineering, and explainable AI frameworks to articulate recommendation rationales and build trust.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Mechanisms to obtain informed consent from learners in MOOC platforms using VLCs
  • Game theoretic modelling of adversarial behaviours to understand whether learners would exploit limiting considerations to game the VLCs

Constraints¶

  • Smart interventions based on learning skill analytics might be perceived as violating learners' personal autonomy
  • Limiting what VLCs can do to comply with ethical guidelines may not be the whole solution

Target Learners¶

  • MOOC learners participating in AI-mediated education research

Target Learning Goals¶

  • protecting learner autonomy and trust while applying AI-driven interventions in MOOCs

Claims¶

  • AI models trained on MOOC completion data risk bias toward high-performing learners, and machine-learned assessment reflects interaction rather than learning [+W]
  • Students' propensity to consent to learning analytics and their institutional trust differ for underrepresented minorities [~W]
  • Institutional trust, data-collection concern, and comfort with instructor data use are the key factors in students' decisions to consent to learning analytics [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Deploy agentic AI in education with transparency about agentic decisions, human oversight and institutional responsibility
  • Address core ethical challenges—data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and transparency—when deploying AI-driven personalized learning
  • Use explainability tools (SHAP/LIME) and human monitoring for fair, transparent AI-driven personalized learning
  • Pursue open AIED-in-MOOCs research directions: sensing-based affective modeling for embodied adaptive learning and MOOC data visualization for stakeholder feedback
