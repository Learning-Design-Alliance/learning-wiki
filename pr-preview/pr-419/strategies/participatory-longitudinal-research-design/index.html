Combine participatory research with longitudinal, technology-supported data collection to study sustainability and scalability¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends research approaches that place participants and stakeholders at the front of research and evaluation of their own context, combined with longitudinal designs. Participatory approaches promote a 'bottom-up' and collaborative approach to innovation, while longitudinal design is needed because sustainability change extends from contemplation through the life of an innovation into scaling. New technologies such as automated video coding can reduce the time and resource costs of longitudinal research.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research must be aligned with teaching and learning goals
  • Stakeholders must be involved in research design and data collection so captured data represent what they wish to change

Constraints¶

  • The article notes many traditional research methods cannot capture change processes in technology-enhanced teaching and learning

Claims¶

  • The article asserts that potential impact and social change rise with increasing stakeholder participation [+W]
  • Leadership support and stakeholder connectivity support sustainability of innovations [~M]
  • Australian case: classroom observation data collection was sustainable and scalable, but resulting changes in teachers' technology integration were not scaled [~W]
  • Netherlands case: design-based TPACK interventions improved student teachers' TPACK and were sustained, but scaling was delayed by leadership decision structures [~W]
  • United States case: a grassroots network sustained and scaled active-learning innovations through leadership and networks rather than research, but remained at second-order change [~W]
