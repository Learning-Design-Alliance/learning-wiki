Equip teachers with technology and training, and conduct large-scale quantitative studies, to advance platform-data use for adaptive instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From the review's implications, the field needs large-scale quantitative studies that systematically verify the initial exploratory findings, and schools should make use of platform data more common. As the review states, "teachers must be equipped with the necessary technology, but also be trained in the required skills" so that platform data can contribute to adaptive instructional design considering learners' needs and individual characteristics.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must be equipped with the necessary technology and trained in the required skills for using data from digital learning platforms

Constraints¶

  • The review notes studies should also examine the extent to which data use depends on platform functionalities, and be conducted in countries with few contributions so far

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers and their heterogeneous student groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Adaptive instructional design based on digital learning data

Claims¶

  • Research on teachers' use of digital learning platform data for instructional design is young and predominantly exploratory, based on a systematic review of 11 studies [+W]
  • A 4-year public institution deployed personalized learning software at scale in College Algebra and used large-scale platform data for continuous improvement rather than acquisition decisions [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Prioritize process-data instrumentation, open collaboration, and dissemination in learning platforms
