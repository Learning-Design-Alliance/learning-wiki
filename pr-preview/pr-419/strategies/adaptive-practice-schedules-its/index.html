Intelligent tutoring systems can adapt practice schedules based on prior knowledge and target knowledge type.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that intelligent tutoring systems might adapt practice schedules by initially selecting a schedule appropriate for the student's prior knowledge level and monitoring acquisition to adapt the schedule accordingly. The system might also "initially prioritize on a particularly important type of knowledge, such as conceptual knowledge, to select the appropriate practice schedule." This strategy leverages the hypothesized interaction between practice schedule, prior knowledge, and target knowledge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The tutoring system must be able to monitor the student's acquisition of knowledge throughout the learning process.

Constraints¶

  • The effectiveness of a given practice schedule depends on the learner's level of prior knowledge and the type of target knowledge, which the authors note are speculative interpretations.

Target Learners¶

  • Students using intelligent tutoring systems for mathematics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Robust learning of conceptual and procedural knowledge of fractions

Claims¶

  • The effect of practice schedules on learning outcomes depends on students' prior knowledge. [+W]
  • The effect of practice schedules depends on the type of target knowledge, with increasingly interleaved practice most effective for area models. [+W]
  • Interleaving multiple graphical representations of fractions gave a qualified advantage over blocked practice, mainly on delayed conceptual transfer and for low-prior-knowledge students [+W]
