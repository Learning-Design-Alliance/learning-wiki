Use survey evidence on student AI task boundaries to ground evidence-based school policy discussions rather than speculation about AI-driven cheating¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools and educators use findings about which academic tasks students consider appropriate for AI chatbots to inform policy and professional development. The authors state they hope the information will encourage "meaningful discussions in professional development and policy committees, shifting the focus of conversations about speculation toward evidence-based decision-making regarding the proper integration of AI into practice." This strategy treats student-reported boundaries as an input to AI integration decisions rather than assuming AI uniformly increases cheating.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administering a behavioral self-report survey of student AI use and allowability perceptions, following the McCabe-style approach of asking about specific behaviors rather than self-identification as cheaters

Constraints¶

  • Cross-year comparisons are descriptive rather than inferential because different school samples were surveyed

Target Learners¶

  • high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic integrity awareness
  • digital and AI literacy

Claims¶

  • The most common reported AI chatbot use for school tasks is explaining concepts (81.63%), followed by idea generation (68.65%) and text summarization (59.21%) [+W]
  • Students strongly oppose allowing AI chatbots to complete entire assignments, with 82.73% saying it should never be allowed, while supporting AI for concept explanation and brainstorming [+W]
  • Self-reported use of AI or digital devices as unauthorized aids on assessments or assignments (item C41) rose to 24.27% in the second year, much higher than the prior year's reported 6.44% and 15.20% [+W]
  • In the second year of generative AI chatbot availability, 72.06% of high school students report engaging in at least one academically dishonest behavior in the prior month, comparable to historical baselines [+W]
  • More than 90% of students report knowing about or having used an AI chatbot, a substantial increase in exposure compared to the prior year [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teach the pedagogical rationale behind AI policies rather than only stating rules
  • Move beyond generic AI policies to method-based ethical policies and process-oriented assessment
