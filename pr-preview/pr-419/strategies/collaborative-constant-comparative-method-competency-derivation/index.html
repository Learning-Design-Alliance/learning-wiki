Use collaborative Constant Comparative Method for Naturalistic Inquiry to derive competency frameworks from practitioner data¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article demonstrates a strategy for deriving competency frameworks from practitioner interview data using a collaborative adaptation of the Constant Comparative Method for Naturalistic Inquiry. The authors “adapted the Constant Comparative Method for Naturalistic Inquiry (Lincoln & Guba, 1985) to be followed collaboratively as a group,” physically sorting transcript excerpts on index cards to identify themes, sub-themes, and categories iteratively. This approach allowed codes and themes to arise from the data rather than imposing an existing model.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A team of researchers working together at the same location over multiple sessions, with lengthy, ongoing, interactive engagement with the data.

Constraints¶

  • The tactile coding approach required printing transcripts and manually cutting and sorting excerpts, which may not scale to large datasets.

Target Learners¶

  • Education and social science researchers
  • Graduate students learning qualitative methods

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Qualitative data analysis skills
  • Competency framework development

Claims¶

  • Competency emphases vary by role and context: IDs focus on ID models, curriculum design, and content creation, while ETs focus on technology evaluation, integration, and support [+W]
  • ID/ET professionals report that cross-disciplinary skills and dispositions are crucial and under-emphasized in existing competency lists [+W]
  • Dispositions such as positivity, lifelong learning orientation, and persistence are reported as essential for ID/ET practice [+W]
  • Interpersonal skills, including communication, active listening, and gaining buy-in, are heavily emphasized by ID/ET professionals [+W]
