Measure HF-HRV to target math anxiety interventions to the students most likely to benefit¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using a physiological index of emotion regulation capacity, resting high-frequency heart rate variability, to decide which math-anxious students should receive interventions aimed at reducing in-the-moment anxiety. As the authors put it, "measuring the HF-HRV of math-anxious people could allow for better targeting of math anxiety interventions, thereby increasing their overall efficacy and allowing for more optimal allocation of limited educational resources." Interventions such as expressive writing that decouple math anxiety from anticipatory state anxiety are expected to benefit low-HF-HRV students most, while high-HF-HRV students likely need interventions targeting other mechanisms.
