Instructors can implement QBI by embedding targeted quizzes at strategic lecture points without new technology¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article positions QBI as a configuration rather than a platform: instructors reposition existing formative questioning inside the lecture sequence. It states that "instructors can promptly implement QBI by incorporating targeted quizzes at strategic points in their lessons, thereby enhancing learner engagement and enabling adaptive explanations as needed". The study emphasises that QBI introduces no new technological product or app; it links sequencing, inquiry progression, and adaptive feedback inside a unified instructional cycle.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Quizzes must be timed to conceptual transitions rather than appended after lecture completion
  • Instructor clarification must follow peer discussion to correct misconceptions in real time

Constraints¶

  • The article notes gains would not occur simply from changing tools, but from integrating questioning and feedback that define QBI

Target Learners¶

  • higher education instructors teaching synchronous online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • student engagement

Claims¶

  • Technology acceptance did not differ between Mentimeter and AhaSlides QBI conditions [+M]
  • QBI conditions showed significantly higher learning engagement, perceived interaction, and course satisfaction than the control, with large effect sizes and no differences between platforms [+M]
  • QBI students scored significantly higher on weekly formative quizzes than control students, with no difference between the two QBI platforms [+M]
  • Interviewed students attributed QBI's attentional gains to structured unpredictability from incrementally placed quiz prompts [+W]
