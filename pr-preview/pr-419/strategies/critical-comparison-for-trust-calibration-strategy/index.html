Add opportunities for critical comparison of AI outputs to Socratic GenAI activities to enable trust calibration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because neither Socratic condition changed trust or perceived competence, the authors recommend that future designs let learners compare or critique AI outputs: "Future studies should investigate whether incorporating opportunities for critical comparison within these approaches could lead to measurable changes." They note prior research shows such contrastive experiences are central to trust calibration, and their design offered no way of comparison to identify genAI limitations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities must include contrastive experiences in which learners can compare or critique genAI outputs against alternatives

Constraints¶

  • Untested in this study; offered as a direction for future research rather than a validated effect

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • teachers in professional development

Target Learning Goals¶

  • trust calibration toward AI
  • critical evaluation of AI outputs

Claims¶

  • Socratic GenAI dialogue leaves faith and trust in technology largely unchanged [~M]
  • A student maintained critical distance from GenAI outputs through a paradoxical trust in their untrustworthiness, deriving practical value while staying sceptical [+W]
  • Reviewed literature reports risks of overreliance, AI errors in complex tasks, and students' uncritical acceptance of AI-generated outputs [+M]
  • Students frequently accepted misleading AI recommendations (overreliance) while rarely rejecting correct ones (underreliance) [+M]
  • Students exhibited high trust in GenAI but limited critical evaluation of its outputs [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Support learners to evaluate AI outputs by verifying sources, reporting errors, and overriding recommendations
  • Iteratively add prompts requiring comparison of AI outputs with psychological theories and structured reflective prompts to counter overreliance on AI
