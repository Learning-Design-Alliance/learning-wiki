Use spatial audio guides to externalize audio explanations to exhibit locations in exhibitions, while treating learning benefits as not yet established¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article discusses the use of spatial audio guides in museums and exhibitions, where audio explanations can be assigned a spatial position so that sound appears to emanate from an exhibit rather than from inside the head. The authors note that with spatial audio, "visitors become active recipients of the audio explanations because both intensity and extent of the acoustically presented information can be influenced by head movements". The strategy is implementable via audio augmented reality with position tracking, but the two experiments found no reliable learning advantage tied to exhibit-located audio, so adoption should rest on experiential rather than learning grounds for now.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Position tracking of visitors and automatic playback in defined audio areas, as implemented with headphones, a tracker and room base stations
  • A hearing training or familiarization so visitors can localize the spatial audio sources

Constraints¶

  • Experiment 2 found no memory or integration differences between audio-location conditions, so the learning benefit of exhibit-located versus other externalized audio locations is not established
  • Externalization ratings in the normal audio condition were distributed across all answer options despite a hearing training

Target Learners¶

  • museum and exhibition visitors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • memory of audio-explained exhibit content
  • text-picture integration of audio explanations with exhibits

Affordances¶

  • Mavcp Audio Visual Cognitive Processing

Claims¶

  • In Experiment 1, memory of audio explanation details about inventions was significantly better with exhibit-located spatial audio than with headphone-located normal audio [+W]
  • In Experiment 1, exploratory measures of judgement of learning and interest in further information showed no significant differences between spatial and normal audio conditions [-W]
  • In Experiment 2, no significant differences between close, distant I and distant II audio-location conditions emerged for memory of inventors, memory of inventions, or text-picture integration [-W]
  • In Experiment 1, text-picture integration did not differ significantly between spatial and normal audio guides [-W]
  • In Experiment 1, perceived externalization differed significantly between spatial and normal audio conditions, confirming the manipulation was reflected in auditory perception [~W]
  • In Experiment 2, externalization, perceived direction and plausibility of audio-room congruency did not differ significantly between the three audio-location conditions [~W]
