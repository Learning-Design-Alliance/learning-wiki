Investigate stakeholder privacy requirements and define transparent rules for learner-data use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

As AIED systems become commercial lifelong learning companions collecting data over years, the paper urges investigation of stakeholder privacy needs, stating that "investigation of best practices of the typically involved stakeholders (e.g., students, parents, teachers, administrators, researchers, system providers) and their privacy related requirements will be important." It further argues that if learner data is used to fund free systems, clear rules should be defined, implemented, controlled, and made transparent.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Differentiated privacy regulations across countries must be considered, and stakeholder-specific requirements investigated

Constraints¶

  • Commercial profitability under strict privacy policies is an open problem; advertisement-funded and data-funded models both carry risks the paper describes via the dystopian scenario

Target Learners¶

  • students, parents, teachers, administrators, researchers, and system providers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • safe and trustworthy use of learning data in adaptive educational systems

Claims¶

  • Algorithmic bias and data privacy are central ethical risks of AI in adult education [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutions implementing learning analytics should spend focused time developing privacy policies and procedures, with stakeholder input including students, before expanding analytics use
  • Provide in-depth privacy training and transparent communication about learning analytics processes to build user understanding and student trust
  • Involve key stakeholders directly in defining privacy and designing LA systems
  • Address core ethical challenges—data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and transparency—when deploying AI-driven personalized learning
  • Six collective-responsibility strategies for AIED researchers facing commercial misuse
