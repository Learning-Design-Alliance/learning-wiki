Design auditory-cognitive trainings with natural, relatable speech material and multisensory presentation to target far transfer to real-world listening¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that training designs combine sensory and cognitive challenge using naturalistic speech and multisensory environments, because such designs produced the only significant transfer to a generalized speech-in-noise task. As the authors put it, "We therefore highly recommend including natural and relatable speech material and multisensory speech presentation in future training studies". They also advise evaluating the degree of immersion experienced, investigating maintenance of effects with longitudinal designs, and establishing the optimal degree of challenge to avoid overstraining listeners.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Speech material reflecting real-world listening demands and a multisensory environment so that processing capacities must be distributed across sensory and cognitive demand

Constraints¶

  • The reported improvement was short-term only, with no evidence on maintenance of the effect; a critical point of increased challenge leading to overstraining remains unexplored

Target Learners¶

  • older adults, particularly those with more pronounced hearing loss or lower auditory working memory capacity

Target Learning Goals¶

  • far transfer of training to speech-in-noise perception in real-world listening situations

Affordances¶

  • Integrated Perceptual Cognitive System Theory

Claims¶

  • Lower auditory working memory capacity moderates the training benefit: participants 1 SD below the mean improve significantly more after immersive than auditory training [+W]
  • More pronounced hearing loss moderates the training benefit: participants with PTA 1 SD above the mean improve significantly more after immersive than auditory training [+W]
  • Speech-in-noise perception improves significantly after a short-term immersive auditory-cognitive training, but not after an audio-only training session [+W]
  • Immersive presentation keeps comprehension performance stable across a training session while auditory-only performance decreases toward the end [+W]
  • Age, general cognition (MoCA), visual working memory, and hearing loss showed no significant association with the extent of training-induced change, and the three-way modality-by-AWM-by-hearing-loss interaction was not significant [~W]
  • Older adults show larger multisensory gains for simple stimuli but multisensory deficits for complex speech stimuli [~W]
