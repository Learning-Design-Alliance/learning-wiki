Use review-derived criteria to evaluate commercial learning games when educators must select quickly¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Lee's response proposes extending academic learning-game design knowledge to commercial games, which are more accessible to educators under time pressure. The editorial states Lee "argues that Ke's work could serve the generation of a set of criteria for evaluating commercial games" and presents "a more comprehensible list of characteristics" to guide that evaluation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A set of evaluation criteria derived from research-based game design themes

Constraints¶

  • Aimed at situations of limited time and pressure where educators choose commercial rather than research-developed games

Target Learners¶

  • educators selecting games for their classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • selecting and evaluating quality learning games

Claims¶

  • Ke's (2016) systematic review synthesized five major themes as guideposts for designing research-based learning games [+W]
  • Commercial games are attractive and accessible to game-based learning advocates but their pedagogical value is often not documented [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Apply lessons from games research through a gamification lens when a quick shift to online teaching is necessary
  • Adopt policies to guide administrators in selecting and purchasing effective educational games
  • Build a repository of cross-platform, customizable, pedagogically evaluated game-based learning applications to support educators
