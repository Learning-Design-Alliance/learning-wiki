Partner with start-up-phase entrepreneurs so students can propose digital innovations in businesses open to change¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A strategy refined through the DBR iterations: after students found early industry partners disengaged, the course shifted to partnering with "entrepreneurial businesses that contribute to their community" in the start-up phase, which is "more open to change". Students propose initiatives supporting digital innovations in these businesses, with a strong emphasis on social awareness and ethical business practices. This strategy responds directly to student feedback that consultative projects with disinterested companies were not real enough.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Lecturers must identify suitable companies and build relations beforehand

Constraints¶

  • Engagement with industry participants required continuous interventions and tweaking of project results

Target Learners¶

  • Information Systems students working on real client projects

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Digital innovation implementation
  • Understanding business needs
  • Social awareness and ethical business practices

Claims¶

  • In iteration 3, when student teams partnered with start-up entrepreneurs, industry partners rated student conduct highest of the three iterations on 7 of 10 items, but innovation usefulness highest on only 3 of 10 [+W]
  • Students reported insufficient engagement with industry partners and projects not real enough in iteration 2 [+W]
  • Community projects need conceptual framing to avoid narrowing the learning object [+W]
