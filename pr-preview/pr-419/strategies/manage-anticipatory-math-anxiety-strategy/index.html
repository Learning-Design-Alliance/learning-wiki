Manage pre-task expectation anxiety with cognitive restructuring or relaxation alongside gradual exposure to mathematics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because state math anxiety is highest before a math task and declines during and after it, the article recommends that interventions not rely on repeated exposure alone. It argues exposure therapy "might be enhanced if it incorporates strategies beyond repeated exposure to mathematical tasks", since repeated exposure alone "would fail to address the critical role of anticipation-related mechanisms of anxiety". Comprehensive interventions should pair gradual exposure and mathematics training with techniques that manage pre-task expectation anxiety.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires addressing anticipation-related mechanisms, not only repeated exposure to mathematical tasks.

Constraints¶

  • The article states the relative effectiveness of different intervention components, such as reducing anticipatory anxiety versus facilitating habituation, should be empirically tested in future studies and lies beyond the scope of the current study.

Target Learners¶

  • students with high trait math anxiety
  • adult mathematics learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reducing anticipatory math anxiety
  • improving engagement with mathematical tasks

Claims¶

  • State math anxiety decreases across repeated measurement points during the arithmetic task, consistent with habituation [+W]
  • State math anxiety decreases linearly from pre- to post-task, with a stronger decrease for individuals with higher trait math anxiety [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use multiple-choice or game-based task formats and repeated exposure to reduce math anxiety, especially for high trait-anxious learners
