Use pretrained transformer-based language models (BERT) to boost classification performance for language-related tasks in educational contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 4 studies (4 design), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends applying pretrained language models such as BERT to educational text classification tasks. The authors state "our study indicates that pretrained language models such as BERT can boost performance for language-related tasks in educational contexts such as classification." This strategy involves fine-tuning a pretrained model on domain-specific labeled data rather than training from scratch, leveraging transfer learning from massive language corpora.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Labeled training data from the target educational domain
  • Sufficient training data (BERT outperforms other models at about 20 to 30% of the training data used in this study)

Constraints¶

  • Performance was demonstrated on German-language reflective writing from preservice physics teachers; generalization to other domains or languages is not established in this article

Target Learners¶

  • preservice teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • automated analysis of reflective writing for scalable feedback

Claims¶

  • Attribution analysis identifies interpretable input features for BERT's classification decisions, including subjunctive verbs in alternatives and appraisal words in evaluation [+W]
  • Reducing BERT to a single encoder layer (BERT (1)) decreases classification performance by .05 points in weighted F1 average compared to the full 12-layer model [+W]
  • BERT outperforms other models once trained on about 20 to 30% of the training data, with all performance curves flattening as training size increases [+W]
  • Fine-tuned BERT outperforms FFNN and LSTM deep learning architectures for classifying segments of written reflections, with weighted F1 average of .82 [+W]
  • Pretraining a multilingual transformer on the large English ASAP dataset and fine-tuning on small target-language data outperforms all monolingual and crosslingual baselines [+W]
  • M-BERT with machine translation is the best crosslingual method, outperforming logistic regression with MT and M-BERT zero-shot [+W]
  • Monolingual English BERT shows only a slight advantage over multilingual BERT for English content scoring [+W]
  • BERT was the best unimodal text classifier for Key Stage classification with an F1 score of 0.75 [+W]
  • Fine-tuned transformer DL models outperform feature-based SVM models for science readability classification across all three languages and configurations [+W]
