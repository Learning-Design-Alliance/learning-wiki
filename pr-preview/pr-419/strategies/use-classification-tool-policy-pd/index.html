Use the classification tool to shape AI-in-education guidelines, professional development, and teacher adaptation of ChatGPT activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends institutional uptake of the tool: "policy makers can use this tool for guidelines for AI in education, and run professional development for integrating ChatGPT as GenAI in education." Teachers are advised to modify the activities, since many are not typical teaching practices, and researchers can use the list to design intervention studies on other GenAI tools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to the consensus-derived classification tool of 20 activities and its needs/phase mappings.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes many activities are not typical or less common teaching practices, so teachers would need to modify them for their own teaching.

Target Learners¶

  • School students taught by teachers adopting ChatGPT-based activities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Integration of ChatGPT-supported SRL into teaching practice
  • Institutional guidelines for AI in education

Claims¶

  • Most of the 20 ChatGPT learning activities (11 of 20) better satisfy competence, fewer satisfy autonomy, and the fewest satisfy relatedness. [+W]
  • Among the 20 ChatGPT learning activities, performance and self-reflection are each better supported by eight activities, and forethought by four. [+W]
  • Competence is the only SDT need that ChatGPT learning activities satisfy across all three SRL phases; relatedness and autonomy are not adequately satisfied at every phase. [+W]
  • A three-round Delphi process with 36 Hong Kong school teachers reached consensus on 20 ChatGPT-based learning activities that satisfy SDT needs and foster SRL phases. [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
  • Universities should provide vivid guidelines and education on how and where ChatGPT may be used for learning
  • Provide accessible guidelines, training materials, or workshops to help students understand responsible GenAI use
