Inform and encourage students to use study apps for retrieval practice through explicit instruction and training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because one-third of surveyed students never used the optional study app, the authors recommend that "more should be done to inform students about the utility of the app and to encourage them to use the study app for retrieval practice, for example, through explicit instruction and training." They also suggest integrating app use as a course study activity to benefit students poor at managing their time, and developing tailored interventions such as nudging disengaged users through app notifications.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must communicate the utility of the app and provide explicit instruction and training on retrieval practice.

Constraints¶

  • The app was positioned as optional in the course, so students needing SRL support may not have chosen to use it.

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in higher education, including disengaged and non-user profiles

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increased engagement with retrieval practice
  • improved exam performance

Claims¶

  • One-third of surveyed students never used the optional mobile study app at all [+W]
  • Active and utilitarian app users obtained higher exam grades than non-users, while disengaged users did not differ from non-users [+W]
  • Latent profile analysis of mobile study app log data identifies three distinct student engagement profiles: disengaged, utilitarian, and active [+W]
  • The study's applicability is limited by its optional app positioning and single-course, single-university setting [~W]
