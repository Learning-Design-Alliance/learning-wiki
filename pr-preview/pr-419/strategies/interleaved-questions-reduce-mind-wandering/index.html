Combat mind wandering by interleaving questions and embedded activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests correcting mind wandering by reasserting missed information, directing attention to display areas, asking content-specific questions, or having learners complete mini-activities. It notes that "This form of interleaved questions and embedded activities can reduce mind wandering (Szpunar et al. 2013) as can asking learners to generate self-explanations". Strategies share goals of capturing attention, enabling reflection, and correcting comprehension deficits.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Detection of mind wandering episodes to trigger the interleaved questions or activities.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • learners reading or studying with technology-based environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • comprehension and sustained attention during learning

Claims¶

  • GazeTutor's attention-aware reorientation improved deep reasoning learning gains for high aptitude learners [+W]
  • Preliminary results suggest a real-time mind-wandering intervention may correct comprehension deficits when mind-wandering probability is high [+W]
  • Providing elaborate explanations and summary interactives instead of just scores increases students' perceptions of engagement and understanding and reduces mind wandering [+W]
  • Questions interpolated between video lecture segments improve subsequent test performance and reduce mind wandering [+M]
