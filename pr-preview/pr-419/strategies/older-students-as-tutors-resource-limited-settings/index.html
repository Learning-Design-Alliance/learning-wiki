Use older students as tutors in resource-limited settings to enhance academic outcomes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends deploying older students as cross-age tutors, particularly where budgets are constrained. Because moderator analyses found tutor type did not significantly moderate effects, older students offer "economic advantages" and "feasibility for schools, allowing them to maximize resources without compromising the quality of tutoring." The authors state such a strategy "could be particularly beneficial for schools with limited budgets, making cross-age tutoring a viable option for enhancing student learning outcomes while managing financial constraints."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutoring programs structured as school-based dyads or small groups with an older tutor and younger tutee in fixed, non-reciprocal roles

Constraints¶

  • None of the included studies conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis comparing peer tutoring with adult tutoring, so the economic advantage is asserted rather than measured

Target Learners¶

  • Older students in grades 5 through 12 as tutors
  • Younger tutees, typically in grades 1 to 3

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading and mathematics achievement

Claims¶

  • Cross-age tutoring produces a small-to-moderate positive effect on academic outcomes for both tutors and tutees [+W]
  • Removing outliers does not meaningfully change cross-age tutoring meta-analysis results [+W]
