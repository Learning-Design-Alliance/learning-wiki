Safety surveillance and usability evaluation as prerequisites for VR research with autistic learners¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that usability evaluation "should be a prerequisite for primary research studies on the effectiveness of interventions since low usability would condition the results", and that VR sessions with autistic learners include safety surveillance: closely observing and monitoring for symptoms such as dizziness, immediately interrupting sessions when any appear, and follow-up observations of younger groups. Precautions in this study included exclusion criteria for seizures, migraines or vertigo, limiting sessions to 3–5 activities, and post-session safety questionnaires after every session.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers trained to run limited-length sessions and to stop immediately on any sign of indisposition
  • Post-session safety questionnaires after every VR session

Constraints¶

  • Findings come from a small sample (21 students) in three schools that were not homogeneous, limiting statistical power

Target Learners¶

  • Autistic students, especially younger learners and those with intellectual disability

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Safe, usable deployment of immersive VR learning tools in educational settings

Claims¶

  • No significant safety issues arose in VR-IWS use; one young participant reported minor dizziness in one session only [+W]
  • Students rated the VR-IWS highly usable, with a mean SUS score of 85.36 [+W]
  • A first evaluation of 3B confirms its conformance to critical usability requirements including safety and comfort, believability, simplicity, acceptability, extensibility, affordability, and mobility [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Apply three design principles for self-guided embodied training: feedback as calibration reference, modality granularity matched to behaviour's temporal structure, and autonomous interpretability as a deployment prerequisite
