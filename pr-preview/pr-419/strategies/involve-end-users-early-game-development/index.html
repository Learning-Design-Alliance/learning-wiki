Involve end-users and teachers early in educational game development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors recommend that academics should not base game design on theoretical principles alone. Collaborating more closely with educators, gaming professionals, and students might result in a better outcome and a more entertaining experience for end-users. They believe involving end-users and their teachers much earlier in development could have prevented issues such as historical references that were more exciting for the authors than for the players.

Related Strategies¶

  • Plan user administration and curriculum fit carefully for educational games
  • Center co-design with teachers and students throughout AI tool R&D, using human-in-the-loop workflows as a primary risk mitigation
  • Involve key stakeholders directly in defining privacy and designing LA systems
  • Mitigate authoring tool investment risk with early-and-often user feedback and a theoretical understanding of users and tasks
