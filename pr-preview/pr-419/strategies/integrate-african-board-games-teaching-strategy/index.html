Integrate classic African board games into teaching mathematics, STEM skills, and AI topics, with teacher training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's authors recommend leveraging classic African board games as affordable, culturally relevant vehicles for teaching. Studies reviewed argue for classroom use: the review reports arguments that I'tche be used "in classrooms to facilitate mathematical learning", that Oware can demystify mathematics, and that these games can increase interest in STEM among digitally divided populations. Because reviewed studies found teachers lacked skills to integrate the games, the strategy includes training teachers to embed the games in instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • teacher training, since reviewed studies (Magonziwa 2017; Sibaya 2019) found teachers lacked the skills to integrate classic African games into teaching

Constraints¶

  • the review notes more empirical studies are needed on the link between gameplay and learning of specific subjects such as mathematics, science, and engineering

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary students in African contexts
  • pre-service teachers
  • digitally divided populations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematics
  • problem-solving
  • computational thinking
  • STEM skills

Affordances¶

  • Sociocultural Learning Theory Board Games

Claims¶

  • Playing classic African board games such as I'tche, Oware, Omweso, Ayo, and Tsoro facilitates mathematical learning [+W]
  • Classic African board games serve as settings for exploring and learning artificial intelligence techniques [+W]
  • Classic African board games function as learning spaces where multidomain learning occurs [+W]
  • Songo gameplay develops computational thinking, including the culturally informed practice the review names songoputation [+W]

Related Strategies¶
