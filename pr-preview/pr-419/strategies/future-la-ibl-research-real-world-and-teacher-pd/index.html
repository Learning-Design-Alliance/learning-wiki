Direct future LA-in-IBL research toward real-world pedagogical demands, non-science disciplines, and teacher professional development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its identified gaps, the review recommends that future work develop and implement LA tools aligned with authentic classroom complexity. It states that "more research is required to explore how LA tools can address the practical demands of inquiry activities in real-world contexts", that research "should also explore how LA can facilitate IBL in non-science disciplines to broaden its application across diverse inquiry contexts", and that "more attention should be given to how LA can support teacher professional development" through iterative cycles of learning, practice, and reflection in authentic settings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • LA tools must be pedagogically grounded and usable in authentic classroom settings, as the review emphasises citing Rodríguez-Triana et al. (2021).

Constraints¶

  • The review notes the field remains underdeveloped, with significant gaps requiring attention, so these directions are proposals rather than tested recommendations.

Target Learners¶

  • teachers
  • K-12 students
  • university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inquiry activity design
  • classroom orchestration of inquiry
  • teacher reflective practice

Claims¶

  • Five reviewed studies reported positive teacher-related impacts of LA support, including enhanced inquiry activity design, improved classroom practice, and increased teacher acceptance of LA tools [+W]
  • Four reviewed studies examined student-related impacts of LA-supported IBL on learning perception, activity engagement, content knowledge acquisition, and productive discourse [+W]
