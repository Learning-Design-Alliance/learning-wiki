Support Wikipedia assignments with prior instructor experience, Wikimedia tools, and structured scaffolding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's best practices for implementing Wikipedia assignments include having prior experience with Wikipedia and successfully carrying out the assigned tasks oneself, dedicating more class time to the assignment, enforcing clear and strict deadlines, providing structured guidance for topic selection, enlisting Wikipedia volunteers, and integrating support tools like the Dashboard. Educators also found the assignment "works better as a group, collaborative project". Support outside class commonly used email (91%) and office hours (77.5%).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must become proficient in Wikipedia community policies and MediaWiki basics to avoid disruptive deletion of student contributions

Constraints¶

  • Awareness and utilization of many Wikimedia support tools remains low, with many respondents unaware of their existence

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • instructors new to Wikipedia assignments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic and digital literacies
  • collaborative knowledge construction

Claims¶

  • Most educators report positive teaching experiences with Wikipedia assignments and intend to continue using them [+M]
  • No significant gender or age bias found among educators using Wikipedia assignments, unlike the Wikipedia editing community [+M]
  • Educators most commonly assign students to expand or create Wikipedia articles worth about a quarter of the course grade [~M]
  • The main challenge of Wikipedia assignments is the MediaWiki learning curve, making them time-consuming [~M]
  • Wikipedia assignments required minimal adaptation during the COVID-19 transition to distance learning [+M]
  • Adoption of Wikipedia assignments shows a global digital divide, concentrated in English-speaking countries [~M]
