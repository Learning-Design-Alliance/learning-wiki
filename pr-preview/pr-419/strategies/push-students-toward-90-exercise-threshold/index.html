Push students to attempt exercises up to the ~90-exercise threshold via reminders or push notifications¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend leveraging the observed grade-exercise relationship by encouraging students to attempt more distinct exercises during formative assessment. They state the result "suggests pushing students to try exercises, in our case, until approaching this threshold", implementable "both during lectures and/or through the system, e.g., with reminders or push notifications".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A formative assessment system that logs the number of distinct exercises each student attempts, so progress toward the threshold can be monitored.

Constraints¶

  • The threshold is specific to this study's data science course and cohort; the article notes that beyond ~97 exercises the shrinking student groups reduce the observable grade difference.

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education students in data science courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Statistical analysis and R programming skills practiced through formative assessment exercises

Claims¶

  • Students who attempted more than 90 different exercises achieved significantly higher final grades than students who attempted fewer [+W]
  • Attempting a new exercise on average increases estimated student ability during formative assessment [+W]
