Study learning dynamics through intensive, long-term, fine-grained repeated observation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that research into the dynamics of complex systems be conducted through 'intensive, long-term, ﬁne-grained, and often-tedious examinations of agents and processes, usually with high-frequency repeated measures or observations'. Such research proceeds piecemeal, often needing many individual or small-sample studies before a critical mass of data is achieved, and the researcher may become part of the process under study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Acceptance that researcher participation is inevitable and a necessary element of inquiry in studies of social systems
  • Willingness to conduct studies more difficult to carry out than questionnaire-based probe-oriented studies

Constraints¶

  • Research along linear-simplicity lines is typically seen as easier and as leading to more powerful and generalizable results, creating pressure against this approach

Target Learners¶

  • educational researchers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • gaining insight into the dynamics of learning processes in practice

Related Strategies¶

  • Future creativity research should study moment-to-moment interactions and mechanisms connecting levels of creativity
  • Develop a theoretically grounded data-granularity structure and stronger evaluation baselines, including meta-analysis-derived baselines
  • Decompose aggregate models of group learning using fine-grained behavioural observations
