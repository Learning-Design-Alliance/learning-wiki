Interleaving¶

Principle · All principles
Evidence · 52 claims (22 for, 28 mixed, 2 against) · 31 studies (15 causal, 6 review, 4 quant-synthesis, 4 theoretical, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), q1–q4 · 16 of 31 report an effect size · 49 claims rest on one study

Conditional relationship¶

This page owns the decision of whether practice mixes kinds or keeps them apart. When a learner must later decide which category an example belongs to, or which procedure a problem calls for, and the candidate categories or problem types resemble one another, practising with examples or problems of different kinds mixed together (interleaved) is expected to produce better later classification and problem choice (near-transfer, delayed-retention) than practising one kind at a time (blocked), even though practice itself usually goes worse when mixed. Interleaving gives the learner repeated chances to compare kinds and to choose, which blocking removes: in a block, the kind of the next item is already known.

The relationship is conditional on four things, each from the evidence below:

  • How alike the categories are. The benefit grows with similarity between categories and shrinks with similarity within a category. Where the kinds are easy to tell apart, there is little for mixing to teach.
  • The material. Across 59 studies the benefit was largest for paintings and naturalistic photographs, smaller for mathematical tasks, absent for expository texts and reversed for word lists, where blocking did better.
  • The goal. The evidence is about discriminating kinds and choosing among them. It is not evidence that mixing helps a learner master a single new procedure, learn one text, or memorise words.
  • What the learner sees during practice. Mixed practice feels harder and looks worse while it happens; learners in one study judged blocking more effective after interleaving had served them better. A design judged on practice performance or on learners' preference will choose blocking.

The meta-analysis found no reliable moderation by retention interval, by retention against transfer test, or by whether examples were shown together or one after another; finding no moderation is not evidence that these never matter. It found larger effects in samples of university students than in other samples, and a second systematic review found no high-school study among its 26. The one school study in the wiki is a classroom experiment with 126 seventh-graders in mathematics, which found the benefit at 1 day (d = 0.42) and larger at 30 days (d = 0.79). Carrying the size of the effect to other ages, other subjects or adults outside education is untested in the wiki. In the few studies a third meta-analysis could split by ability, interleaved practice had negative effects for high-ability students, so who gains is also not settled.

How this sits beside its neighbours: Spaced Learning holds when practice of the same thing recurs; interleaving changes what sits next to what, and in most mixed schedules it also spaces each kind. A systematic review reports that the benefit survives when spacing is held constant, and one set of experiments found that what matters is that different kinds sit next to each other: putting unrelated material between items removed the advantage over blocking. Sequencing holds the wider order of a unit and already carries the blocked-then-mixed choice as one row; Retrieval Practice holds whether practice asks for recall.

Default design, while the dose is untested¶

The meta-analysis establishes the direction and its moderators, not a dose. This default is a labelled proposal: the order of steps follows the evidence, and the numbers (how many blocked items, how much mixing, how many sessions) are untested choices.

  1. Check the condition first. List the categories or problem types the learner will have to tell apart. Ask: would a competent person confuse them? If the kinds are not confusable, keep practice blocked and stop here: there is little evidence that mixing dissimilar kinds helps.
  2. Introduce each kind on its own with two or three worked examples or items, so each is recognisable before it has to be distinguished (the blocked start is a design choice, see Evidence and qualifications).
  3. Mix from the first practice set after each kind has been met: in a set of 12 items covering three kinds, no two consecutive items of the same kind more than occasionally, in an order the learner cannot predict. Keep the mixed items back to back: in one set of experiments, putting unrelated material between items of different kinds removed the advantage, while spreading each kind further apart with other kinds in between helped.
  4. Ask for the choice explicitly: before solving or labelling, the learner names the kind or the procedure, and gets feedback on the choice as well as on the answer. Do not leave a definition of each kind on screen during the mixed set: in one experiment summarised by a review, a definition on screen removed the benefit of interleaving.
  5. Across sessions, keep mixing earlier kinds into later practice (week 1 kinds A–C mixed; week 2 adds D and keeps A–C in the mix), so the mixed sets also space each kind. The one classroom test in the wiki ran mixed assignments over about three months. Plan at least one delayed, mixed check (a week or more later; that classroom study found the advantage larger at 30 days than at 1) before judging the design.
  6. Judge it on the delayed, mixed check, not on practice accuracy and not on learners' ratings of which practice felt better.

Fitting the design to a situation¶

Three facts change the decision most. Ask for them when the brief omits them: are the kinds the learner must tell apart actually confusable? (if not, interleaving has little to work with); what is the material? (visual categories, mathematical problem types, texts, word lists); and what will the learner have to do later: choose among kinds, or reproduce one?

Observation, state and explanation¶

These rows are proposals, untested with learners as diagnostics. A check is itself practice and changes what it measures, so record each one.

Evidence and qualifications¶

  • Interleaving improves discrimination [+S]: three studies. Kornell & Bjork (2008) found learners classified new paintings better after interleaved study of artists, while most believed blocking had helped them more; Rohrer & Taylor (2007) found shuffled mathematics practice gave far better delayed performance despite worse practice; Brunmair & Richter (2019) found the overall benefit depends on category similarity.
  • Interleaved practice produces a moderate overall benefit for inductive category learning [+M]: g = 0.42 across 238 effect sizes in 158 samples (Brunmair & Richter 2019). The same finding is recorded on Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning [~M]; the two claim pages are candidates for a claim merge.
  • Between-category similarity raises and within-category similarity lowers the interleaving effect [~M]: the condition this page is built on.
  • Interleaving benefits are small for mathematical tasks and absent or reversed for expository texts and words [~M]: g = 0.34 for mathematical tasks, no significant effect for expository texts, g = −0.39 for words. The limiting claim: interleaving is not a general improvement to any practice.
  • Interleaving shows positive effects for all visual stimulus types [+M]: g = 0.67 for paintings, 0.35 for naturalistic photographs.
  • Several candidate moderators show no reliable effects [~M]: no moderation by retention interval, retention against transfer test, simultaneous against successive presentation, or publication status. Not detecting a moderator does not show it has no effect.
  • Interleaving effects are larger in pure university student samples [~M]: the effect sizes above may overstate the benefit for other learners.
  • Spaced and interleaved homework practice improves learning and retention [+W]: a practice brief, not a new study; it carries the idea into homework design.
  • Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic (2015), the one classroom experiment in the wiki: 126 seventh-graders practised graph and slope problems for about three months, each student getting some types interleaved and some blocked, then took an unannounced test of new problems. Interleaved practice produced higher test scores [+M] (partial η² = .165); d = 0.42 at 1 day [+M] and d = 0.79 at 30 days [+M], with the schedule-by-delay interaction not significant (p = .09), so a growing benefit is suggested, not shown; by problem type [~M], the slope-problem effect at 1 day (d = 0.43) was not significant. Problems were mixed with reviews of earlier types, so spacing is not separated here.
  • Firth, Rivers & Boyle (2021), a systematic review of 26 studies with a meta-analysis of 17: interleaving improved classification of new examples [+M] (effects up to 0.66) and of studied examples [+M] (up to g = 0.65), reported as maxima, not pooled means; the benefit persists when spacing is held constant and at delays up to ten days [+W]; it is largest where differences are subtle [~M] and weaker or reversed where a clear verbal rule defines each kind [~W]; and no included study used high-school learners [~W].
  • Birnbaum, Kornell, Bjork & Bjork (2013), experiments with undergraduates and online adults learning bird and butterfly species: interleaving beat blocking only when items were contiguous [~M]; unrelated questions placed between interleaved items impaired learning [~M]; with order held interleaved, a longer lag between same-kind items helped [+M] (d = 0.75); and about two thirds judged blocking more effective [~M]. Together these make juxtaposition of kinds, not spacing alone, the working part, and they are why step 3 keeps mixed items back to back.
  • In the few studies that split by ability, learning techniques helped low-ability students and not high-ability students [~W]: Donoghue & Hattie (2021), with negative effects for interleaved practice in the high-ability group; only 6 studies of high-ability students across all ten techniques, so this is a caution, not a finding about interleaving alone.
  • Interleaved practice improves delayed posttest performance compared with blocked practice [+W]: one experiment (132 participants, an Italian verb-conjugation task) in which interleaving reduced the time and trials needed on a test two days later (η² = 0.09); the first wiki evidence of interleaving helping in language material, and it is grammar rules, not word lists.

Taken together: mixing kinds in practice reliably helps learners tell confusable kinds apart, most for visual categories and in one classroom study for mathematics; it does nothing or harm for texts and word lists. The benefit seems to come from comparing kinds side by side, and survives spacing being held constant, but inserting unrelated material between items removes it. No claim in the wiki tests a dose (how much mixing, after how many blocked items), and only one study in the wiki was run in a school.

The evidence by question¶

  • Should examples or problems of confusable categories be met in blocks or interleaved, and for which materials? Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning for visual and mathematical materials, but not for expository texts or word categories [~M]
  • How alike must the categories be for mixing to help, and when does blocking do better? Interleaving effects are stronger when material is more similar between categories, less similar within categories, and more complex [~M]
  • Does it work in a school classroom, over weeks, on an ordinary test? Interleaved practice produces higher mathematics test scores than blocked practice in a classroom experiment [+M]: one study, seventh-grade mathematics.
  • Is the benefit just spacing in disguise? The interleaving benefit is not caused by spacing [+W] and spacing and interleaving are subadditive [~M]: the review and the experiments agree that comparing kinds side by side does work spacing does not.
  • Does it help with new examples, not only the practised ones? Interleaving benefits transfer to novel items from a learned category [+M]

Further evidence, not yet read against this model¶

  • Learners prefer massed study despite its inferior outcomes [~M]: the same misjudgement as Kornell & Bjork's learners, for spacing; it bears on why interleaving needs to be built into materials rather than left to learners.
  • After training, d.tech teachers adopted low-stakes practice tests and spaced, interleaved practice [+W]: adoption by teachers, not a learning outcome.
  • Accuracy in the mixed block decreases across trials while PES also decreases, independent of intelligence [~W] — read 2026-10-19 (not about this page): a "mixed block" of an executive-function task in kindergarten children, where accuracy fell with fatigue; it is not mixed practice of kinds to be learned.
  • High and low CI practice do not differ significantly in overall relatively permanent gains (Δ pre-retention) [-W] — attached 2026-10-21 from Achraf Ammar et al. (2024), which proposed "Do not assume high contextual interference (random) practice guarantees a longer-term performance advantage in PE and sports"; tests this page's relationship.
  • Interleaved homework yields higher surprise criterial test scores than blocked homework in undergraduate physics, with larger effects in Stage 2 [+M] — attached 2026-10-26 from Joshua Samani et al. (2021), which proposed "Replace conventionally blocked homework with interleaved homework when assignments contain multiple isomorphic problems per topic"; tests this page's relationship.
  • Interleaving benefits were not detected on high-stakes midterm exams taken after cramming [~M] — attached 2026-10-26 from Joshua Samani et al. (2021), which proposed "Replace conventionally blocked homework with interleaved homework when assignments contain multiple isomorphic problems per topic"; tests this page's relationship.
  • Controlling for prior knowledge and demographics, interleaving benefited all students on the final exam (β = 0.21, p < 0.001) [~M] — attached 2026-10-27 from Steve Bennoun et al. (2026), which proposed "Reorder homework sets so problems on each topic are distributed across multiple assignments — a low-cost restructuring that requires no extra time, technology, or curricular change"; tests this page's relationship.
  • On the final exam, interleaving reduced gaps for URM students, first-generation students, students with lower mother's education, and students with weaker high school mathematics preparation [~M] — attached 2026-10-27 from Steve Bennoun et al. (2026), which proposed "Reorder homework sets so problems on each topic are distributed across multiple assignments — a low-cost restructuring that requires no extra time, technology, or curricular change"; tests this page's relationship.
  • Interleaving multiple graphical representations of fractions gave a qualified advantage over blocked practice, mainly on delayed conceptual transfer and for low-prior-knowledge students [+W] — attached 2026-10-28 from Rau et al. (2014), which proposed "Instructional materials should provide interleaved practice with multiple graphical representations to promote robust learning, especially for conceptual knowledge."; tests this page's relationship.

Objective and learner-valued goal¶

The designer's objective is usually correct choice among kinds at a later time, without being told which kind it is. Ask separately what the learner wants: a learner preparing for an exam of mixed questions has a goal that interleaving serves directly, while one who wants to feel fluent today may resist mixed practice because it feels worse. Record that preference as an observation. Do not overrule it silently, and do not read it as evidence that blocking works better.

What would revise this model?¶

The expectation should weaken if comparisons with comparable learners find no advantage for mixing on a delayed, mixed test where the kinds are confusable, or if the advantage disappears in further studies that hold spacing constant. If new syntheses find benefits for expository texts or words, the material rows above should change. If the blocked-then-mixed start loses to mixing from the first item for novices, step 2 of the default design should go. If school studies in subjects other than mathematics find no benefit, or studies of high-ability or advanced learners confirm a cost, the learner rows should narrow where interleaving is recommended.

Claims¶

  • Interleaving benefits low-skilled students more than high-skilled students when learning math problem categories [~W]
  • The discriminative-contrast hypothesis treats spacing and interleaving as distinct effects, with interleaving benefits more likely for similar than dissimilar interleaved tasks [~W]
  • All 67 reviewed interleaving studies demonstrated the interleaving effect using very similar interleaved concepts [~W]
  • Interleaving is more effective for materials with a low degree of discriminability [~W]
  • Interleaved practice outperformed blocked practice across eight STEM classrooms [+W]
  • Low (blocked) and high (random/serial) contextual interference practice do not differ significantly in acute skill gains (Δ pre-post) in PE and sports contexts [~W]
  • Interleaved practice yields better category learning than blocked practice, and students who choose interleaved practice outperform those who choose blocked practice on novel materials [+M]
  • In Experiment 1, learners interleaved more only when high utility value of distinguishing was combined with instruction that differences matter, with the two factors interacting rather than showing main effects [~W]
  • Combining utility value of distinguishing with conditional knowledge fosters interleaving of confusable categories (Abel et al., 2024) [+W]
  • In Experiment 1, mental effort was negatively related to perceived effectiveness and (via perceived effectiveness) willingness to use interleaved studying, with a more influential role for interleaved than blocked studying [-W]
  • In Experiment 2, the interleaving benefit on actual test outcomes was not statistically significant, unlike in Experiment 1 [~W]
  • Interleaving—presenting different concepts in alternation rather than repeating the same concept—improves students' discrimination among similar concepts, terms, and principles [+W]
  • Evidence for lasting learning is mixed at 8-week delays: only interleaving, not spacing or retrieval practice in drawing contexts, was supported [+W]
  • Interleaving benefits on criterial tests cannot be attributed to increased study time [+W]
  • Students perform worse on interleaved homework than blocked homework and misjudge its learning value [~W]
  • Stimulation at PMd during repetitive practice fails to induce the early (6-h) time-dependent consolidation typical of interleaved practice [~M]
  • On raw exam scores, interleaved homework produced no significant midterm difference between sections but a significant final exam advantage for the interleaved section [+W]
  • Interleaving disproportionately benefited students with below-average or up-to-0.96-SD-above-average midterm scores, though ceiling effects may contribute [~W]

Related Principles¶

  • Spaced Learning — when practice of the same thing recurs; usually confounded with interleaving
  • Sequencing — the wider order of a unit; carries blocked-then-mixed as one choice
  • Retrieval Practice — practice that asks for recall
  • Worked Examples — how each kind is introduced before mixing

Examples¶

  • Interleaving and Interleave practice types are the strategies that carry this principle into an activity.
  • Include a small block of problems of a new skill at the start of an assignment, then interleave the rest
