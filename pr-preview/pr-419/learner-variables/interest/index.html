Interest¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 226 claims (161 show a difference, 39 mixed, 26 no difference) · 153 studies (61 causal, 41 associational, 20 review, 13 design, 7 quant-synthesis, 6 theoretical, 5 qualitative), q1–q4 · 43 of 153 report an effect size · 220 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Interest is a learner's attraction to a topic. Research separates individual interest, a lasting disposition a learner brings, from situational interest, which material or activity sparks in the moment and which can grow into individual interest over time. Interest is measured by self-report, choice and persistence. It predicts attention, depth of processing and course choice, it moderates the effect of materials (seductive details catch interest without helping learning), and it is an outcome of designs that show relevance or give learners choice. It is a narrower case of motivation.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [~W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Enjoyment of mathematics r = .31; attitudes toward mathematics .12. Claim [+W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Relevance interventions and choice can raise situational interest; whether it lasts depends on later experiences.
  • Interesting but irrelevant additions can distract from what is to be learned.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with little prior interest in a required subject.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Engagement, persistence, course and career choice.

The evidence by question¶

  • Does seeing a task as interesting, important or useful raise the effort learners invest in it? Learners invest more effort in tasks they perceive as interesting, personally important, or useful to their future goals. [+M]
  • Does setting problems in contexts drawn from learners' interests raise engagement and performance? An IES practice-guide presentation recommends personalized problem contexts, asserting without cited studies that students engage more actively and perform better in them [+W]
  • Does making material more interesting with details that do not carry the target cost learning? Interesting but irrelevant details can impair comprehension and transfer, with effects that vary with the material and the learner [+S]
  • Do rewards and praise undermine voluntary engagement? Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [+S]
