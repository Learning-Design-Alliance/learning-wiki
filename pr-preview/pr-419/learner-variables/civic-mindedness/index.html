Civic Mindedness¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 43 claims (29 show a difference, 8 mixed, 6 no difference) · 19 studies (7 associational, 5 causal, 3 theoretical, 2 review, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), q1–q4 · 4 of 19 report an effect size · 41 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Civic mindedness is a learner's orientation toward their community: seeing themselves as someone who can contribute, being informed about and attentive to the needs and goals of others (including needs that differ from their own), and joining with others to pursue shared aims. It includes thinking critically about civic content and social movements and about the democratic systems that govern communities, including the inequities those systems can produce and how they might be addressed. Prosocial behaviour, acting with others in mind, is described as one of its foundations. It is measured with sets of civic-outcome instruments for elementary classrooms (knowledge, thinking, dispositions and participation) and with critical-consciousness scales for older adolescents and adults.

The LVN record describes it from pre-kindergarten through high school. Young children begin to notice others' needs and to take responsibility at home and school; in adolescence, critical consciousness (the confidence and ability to reflect on and act on social issues) is described as a source of empowerment that can support academic engagement, especially for learners from groups that have been historically and systematically excluded.

It is related to social awareness and relationship skills and collaboration, and to belonging: learners who feel they belong may be more likely to take civic action.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Matters for civic education, service learning, project work on community issues and discussion of current events.
  • Caregivers who discuss politics and inequity and model civic action can support adolescents' critical consciousness.
  • Cultures differ in how much children are expected to help others, which shapes how civic identity develops.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners from pre-kindergarten through high school.
  • Adolescents, for whom critical consciousness can support engagement and resist the effects of negative stereotypes.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Civic knowledge and understanding of democratic systems.
  • Prosocial behaviour and regard for others.
  • Civic participation and collective action.
  • Critical reflection on social issues.

Measures¶

  • Civic Outcomes for Elementary School (Chi et al., 2006): instruments for civic knowledge, thinking, dispositions and participation in elementary classrooms.
  • Short Critical Consciousness Scale (Diemer et al., 2022): 13-item scale of critical reflection and critical action for older adolescents and adults.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Adverse Experiences: experiences such as physical abuse are linked to less prosocial behaviour.
  • Collaboration: working with peers can foster regard for others, and civic action usually needs collaboration.
  • Communication Skills: understanding others' needs and speaking up about civic concerns both rest on communication.
  • Affect Regulation: acting prosocially and positive emotion are described as reinforcing each other.
  • Home Learning Environment: responsibilities at home and caregivers' discussion and modelling of civic action support civic development.
  • Identity: civic identity develops within a cultural context, and cultures differ in how much early helping they expect.
  • Motivation: wanting to act with others in mind is part of civic mindedness, and civic action can itself raise motivation.
  • Safety: an unsafe neighbourhood can make some civic activity harder while prompting other forms, such as movements for safety.
  • Belonging: learners who feel they belong may be more likely to take civic action.
  • Sleep: enough good sleep is associated with more prosocial behaviour.
  • Social Awareness and Relationship Skills: prosocial learners tend to have better peer relationships.
  • Social Supports: learners who feel well supported tend to show more empathy and to help others in their community.
