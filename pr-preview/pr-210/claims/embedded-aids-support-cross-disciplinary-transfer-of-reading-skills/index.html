Related Claims¶

  • Textual material treated with embedded aids produced 10-15% higher reading comprehension scores than conventional text, statistically significant at the .01 level, in a science-material study with approximately 200 tenth graders across three schools. — related
  • Using embedded aids required students to take about 7% longer, on average, to complete an assigned chapter, and comprehension scores reflected this additional effort. — related
  • Students and teachers respond favorably to embedded aids: approximately 90% of students reported no difficulty understanding or using the aids, and over 75% wanted similar aids throughout the book. — related
  • Prompting learners to self-explain improves understanding and problem solving on immediate tests by a moderate average amount, with little evidence yet on delayed or classroom gains. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Questioning Strategies Improve Learning — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Students do not appear to have great difficulty adjusting to a more 'busy' page when assistance and enrichment units are woven into textual material. — related
