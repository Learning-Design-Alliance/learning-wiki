Evidence¶

Dana L. Kirkwood-Watts 2022¶

Dana L. Kirkwood-Watts, Emily K. Bremers, Emily A. Robinson, Kathleen R. Brazeal, and Brian A. Couch. (2022). Revisiting Clickers: In-Class Questions Followed by At-Home Reflections Are Associated with Higher Student Performance on Related Exam Questions. Journal of Microbiology and Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1128/jmbe.00038-22

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Generalized linear mixed model with a significant V1*V2 interaction (Table 4: estimate 0.535, P < 0.001) followed by post hoc analysis of vote pathways on isomorphic exam questions; the correct-correct pattern beat all three other named patterns, which were statistically equivalent.

"We found that answering both votes cor- rectly (i.e., correct-correct) was associated with a student scoring signiﬁcantly higher on the corresponding exam question com- pared to all other vote patterns (i.e., incorrect-correct, incor- rect-incorrect, correct-incorrect)"

Dana L. Kirkwood-Watts 2022 (2)¶

Dana L. Kirkwood-Watts, Emily K. Bremers, Emily A. Robinson, Kathleen R. Brazeal, and Brian A. Couch. (2022). Revisiting Clickers: In-Class Questions Followed by At-Home Reflections Are Associated with Higher Student Performance on Related Exam Questions. Journal of Microbiology and Biology Education. https://doi.org/10.1128/jmbe.00038-22

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Post hoc contrast from the same vote-pattern model on isomorphic exam questions; the article interprets that gains from peer discussion "could not be recapitulated at later time points" for students who switched to correct answers.

"students who changed from an incorrect to a correct clicker vote did not perform any better than students who were incorrect on both votes, suggesting that the improved performance they experienced as a result of peer discussion could not be recapitulated at later time points."
