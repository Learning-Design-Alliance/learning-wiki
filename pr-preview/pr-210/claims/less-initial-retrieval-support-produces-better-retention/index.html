The chapter reports Carpenter and DeLosh (2006, Experiment 1), with eight-item lists and no feedback: "whereas subjects correctly recognized 89% of the words, they freely recalled 69% of them", yet free recall enhanced retention more.

"For instance, whereas subjects correctly recognized 89% of the words, they freely recalled 69% of them, yet initial free recall consistently enhanced retention more than initial recognition did."

"On a ﬁnal cued recall test, retrieval practice effects were larger with weak associates than they were with strong associates."
