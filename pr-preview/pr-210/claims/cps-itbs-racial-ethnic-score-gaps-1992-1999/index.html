John Q. Easton 2000¶

John Q. Easton, Todd Rosenkranz, Anthony S. Bryk, Brian A. Jacob, Stuart Luppescu, Melissa Roderick. (2000). Annual CPS Test Trend Review, 1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Annual+CPS+Test+Trend+Review+1999

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive disaggregation of mean ITBS grade equivalents by race/ethnicity and age, 1992-1999 (Part 2, Figure 3, Tables C and D). The brief reports "Asian and white students score consistently higher than Latino and African-American students." No effect size is printed.

"In both reading and math, Asian and white students score consistently higher than Latino and African-American students. Asian and white students tend to be above the national average grade level; Latino and African-American students tend to be below grade level."

John Q. Easton, Todd Rosenkranz, Anthony S. Bryk, Brian A. Jacob, Stuart Luppescu, Melissa Roderick. (2000). Annual CPS Test Trend Review, 1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Annual+CPS+Test+Trend+Review+1999

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive comparison of racial/ethnic trends in Figure 3 (Part 2). The brief states "Asian students score significantly higher in math than white students"; no test statistic or effect size is printed.

"The trends of white and Asian students are similar to each other in reading and math, but Asian students score significantly higher in math than white students."

John Q. Easton 2000 (3)¶

John Q. Easton, Todd Rosenkranz, Anthony S. Bryk, Brian A. Jacob, Stuart Luppescu, Melissa Roderick. (2000). Annual CPS Test Trend Review, 1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Annual+CPS+Test+Trend+Review+1999

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive trend comparison by race/ethnicity, 1992-1999 (Part 2). The brief reports "slightly smaller improvements" for African-American and Latino students with recent acceleration, especially among twelve to fourteen year olds. No effect size is printed.

"In general, African-American and Latino students' scores made slightly smaller improvements than white and Asian students in the period 1992 to 1999, though in more recent years, the improvements for these students have accelerated, especially for the twelve to fourteen year old students."
