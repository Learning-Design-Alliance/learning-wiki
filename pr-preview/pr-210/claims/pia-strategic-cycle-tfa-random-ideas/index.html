Evidence¶

Belle Dang 2024¶

Belle Dang, Andy Nguyen and Sanna Järvelä. (2024). Deliberative Interactions for Socially Shared Regulation in Collaborative Learning: An AI-Driven Learning Analytics Study. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(3). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8393

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Process-mining analysis of deliberation sequences showed PIA groups more often moved from generating options to agreeing and implementing (60% vs. 55% in TFA), and engaged in fewer random idea attempts (f = 80 vs. 144).

"exhibits a higher proportion of reaching agreement and implementing their generated options (f Generate options → Agree and implement = 60%) than that of the TFA type (fGenerate options → Agree and implement = 55%)."

Belle Dang 2024 (2)¶

Belle Dang, Andy Nguyen and Sanna Järvelä. (2024). Deliberative Interactions for Socially Shared Regulation in Collaborative Learning: An AI-Driven Learning Analytics Study. Journal of Learning Analytics, 11(3). https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2024.8393

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Process maps revealed TFA groups engaged in repeated metacognitive self-loops (f = 469, 100% self-transition) and rarely established strategy (f = 8 vs. PIA's 32). The authors interpret this as "a maladaptive pattern of regulation."

"This pattern, marked by a lack of strategic problem-solving ( f PIA’s Establish strategy = 32, f TFA’s Establish strategy = 8) and random idea attempts, also reflect s a maladaptive pattern of regulation."
