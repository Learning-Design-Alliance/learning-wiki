Evidence¶

Ahlquist 2020¶

Ahlquist, E.-M. T., & Gynther, P. (2020). Teaching in the Montessori Classroom: Investigating Variation Theory and Embodiment as a Foundation of Teachers' Development. Journal of Montessori Research, 6(1). https://journals.ku.dk/jmr

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Observation of the teacher's initial polygon presentation to 14 children, documented in field notes. The authors report children mostly received information and that "no reconstructing, reorienting, or generative moves were used by the teacher."

"the children who participated in them mostly received information from the teacher (i.e., instructional moves), rather than opportunities to express how, or to what extent, they understood the content. Thus, in the first observed presentation, no reconstructing, reorienting, or generative moves were used by the teacher."

Ahlquist 2020 (2)¶

Ahlquist, E.-M. T., & Gynther, P. (2020). Teaching in the Montessori Classroom: Investigating Variation Theory and Embodiment as a Foundation of Teachers' Development. Journal of Montessori Research, 6(1). https://journals.ku.dk/jmr

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Observation of the subsequent quadrilateral presentation to five children. The authors note the teacher "began the presentation with a generative move" and also used contrast, confirming, reconstructing, and reorienting moves during the dialogue.

"This time, she began the presentation with a generative move by asking the children to describe the sides and angles of the triangle she held in her hand."
