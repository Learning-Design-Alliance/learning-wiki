PAIR-C scaffolding shows mixed evidence for deep understanding and reduced misconceptions in emergent-phenomena instruction¶

Claim · All claims Evidence · 1 study · causal r2 · q2 · i1 small · n=50 (24 PAIR-C, 26 control)

Explicitly scaffolding the contrast between emergent (decentralized, collective) and sequential (individualistic, cumulative) causal structure — the PAIR-C framework — produced a statistically significant advantage on near-transfer tasks, a non-significant trend on deep-understanding questions, and no far transfer, in one quasi-experimental study.

Evidence¶

Su, Chi & Nagashima 2026¶

Su, M., Chi, M. T. H., & Nagashima, T. (2026). Applying the PAIR-C Framework to foster deep understanding and address misconceptions in science education. Journal of the Learning Sciences, 35(1), 130-175. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2025.2546471

q2 · quasi-experiment with a control condition, pre/post design, no random individual assignment · i1 · one significant near-transfer effect; deep-understanding and misconception-reduction effects were trends that did not reach conventional significance · n=50 (24 PAIR-C, 26 control) · causal · r2

Pre-service teachers learned natural selection using NetLogo agent-based simulations (Peppered Moths, Rock Pocket Mice), either with explicit PAIR-C-dimension scaffolding (annotations, prompts, and feedback framed around the five dimensions) or with traditional instruction focused on differential survival and reproduction using the same simulations. Both groups were assessed pre/post on shallow questions, deep (inter-level causal) questions, near-transfer questions (same concept, different context), far-transfer questions (an unrelated emergent phenomenon), and open-ended misconception coding (nine misconception categories, two raters, Kappa=0.843). The PAIR-C group significantly outperformed control on near-transfer gains. On deep-understanding questions and misconception reduction, PAIR-C showed a larger improvement than control, but the group-by-time interaction in each case did not clear the conventional p<.05 threshold (p=.059, p=.060, p=.095 respectively). Neither group achieved far transfer to an unrelated emergent phenomenon (geese flight formation).

Discussion¶

This is a single-domain (natural selection), single-population (pre-service teachers, not K-12 students) quasi-experiment with a modest sample (n=50). Only the near-transfer result cleared conventional statistical significance; the deep-understanding and misconception-reduction results are trends, not confirmed effects, and should not be reported as "PAIR-C significantly improves deep understanding" without that qualification. The complete absence of far transfer in both groups suggests that naming and contrasting the five dimensions within one domain is not, by itself, sufficient for learners to recognize the same causal structure in an unfamiliar emergent phenomenon — the authors suggest explicit cross-domain analogical comparison (per Jacobson et al., 2020) may be needed for that. Persistence of gains beyond the immediate posttest was not measured.
