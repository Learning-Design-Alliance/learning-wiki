Evidence¶

Watts 1991¶

Watts, Mike; Bentley, Di. (1991). An Instructional 'Mali-Trap': Anthropomorphic and Animistic Thought in Constructivist Science Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED336269

q2 · i? · review · r1

The authors' argumentative review of post-Piaget research on children's explanatory responses, including collected student conception bibliographies, leads them to argue that animistic explanations "not diminish with age". They report that several research papers describe such explanations well beyond the stages Piaget charted.

"There are reasons for arguing that not only do animistic explanations not diminish with age, but that there are strong reasons why they would survive intact into adulthood."

  • Teachers routinely use animistic and anthropomorphic explanations: 36% of 43 observed lessons overall and 78% of observed secondary lessons — related
  • Classroom pedagogical interventions significantly reduce teleologic and anthropomorphic language, with curricular exposure but not age as a significant factor — reports the opposite
