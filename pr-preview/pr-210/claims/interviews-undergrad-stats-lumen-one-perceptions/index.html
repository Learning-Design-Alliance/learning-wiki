Voices of Undergraduate Students Taking Introductory Statistics Courses: Interview Protocol 2025¶

Voices of Undergraduate Students Taking Introductory Statistics Courses: Interview Protocol. (2025). Digital Promise / Achieving the Dream. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/05ccd902-1e80-4c94-9cd9-f706a98d0cc0

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

The protocol's introduction describes its purpose: interviews with undergraduate Statistics students informed the research team about student thoughts on "a new Statistics courseware they were using." Participation was voluntary, recorded, and reported anonymously.

"This document is the interview protocol conducted with undergraduate Statistics students, as a part of a study Digital Promise conducted. These interviews informed the research team about the students thoughts on a new Statistics courseware they were using."

Related Claims¶

  • Dedicated sense-of-belonging courseware features were underused: most students were unaware of the Lumen Community and vaguely remembered the Introduce Yourself module — related
  • About half of students reported instructors helped bridge courseware guidance and their understanding through outreach, availability, and responsiveness — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Undergraduate statistics students most frequently used and valued the courseware's ungraded formative practice features, the Readiness Check and Self Check — related
  • Real-world and racially diverse examples in courseware content helped students retain, comprehend, and relate to statistics — related
  • Instructors in their second or subsequent term using Lumen One made greater use of six of seven instructor-facing courseware components than first-term users. — related
  • BIPOC students perceived statistics as a neutral subject and did not discern explicit racial or cultural inclusion in the courseware, generally viewing that neutrality as appropriate — related
  • Course modality moderated student experience: instructor responsiveness was more prevalent in face-to-face classes and online students reported more frustration — related
  • BIPOC solution providers embed culturally responsive pedagogy features in both their R&D processes and their end products — related
  • Most instructors using adaptive courseware believed it improved student learning and would use it again — related
