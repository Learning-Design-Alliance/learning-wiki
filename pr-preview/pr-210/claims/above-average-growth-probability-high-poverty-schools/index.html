Evidence¶

Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa 2013¶

Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa. (2013). A Level Playing Field? How College Readiness Standards Change the Accountability Game. Northwest Evaluation Association. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/a-level-playing-field-college-readiness-standards-whitepaper/

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Growth analysis of the high-poverty school cohorts reports that such students would have "a 45% probability of achieving above-average growth in mathematics and a 50% chance" in reading, indicating growth in high-poverty schools was largely on par with wealthier counterparts. These probabilities are descriptive percentages, not effect sizes.

"students attending one of the high-poverty schools in the sample would have a 45% probability of achieving above-average growth in mathematics and a 50% chance of above average growth in reading."

Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa 2013 (2)¶

Michael Dahlin and Beth Tarasawa. (2013). A Level Playing Field? How College Readiness Standards Change the Accountability Game. Northwest Evaluation Association. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/a-level-playing-field-college-readiness-standards-whitepaper/

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Based on the observed between-school growth distribution, the report projects that "75th percentile growth would be required of high-poverty schools to eliminate the current gap in college readiness rates", a benchmark the foreword notes many high-poverty schools meet or exceed.

"Evidence from the current study suggests that 75th percentile growth would be required of high-poverty schools to eliminate the current gap in college readiness rates between high- and low-poverty schools."
