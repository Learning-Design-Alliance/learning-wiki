Evidence¶

Greene Nolan 2023¶

Greene Nolan, H. L., & Vang, M. C. (2023). Automated essay scoring in middle school writing: Understanding key predictors of students' growth and comparing artificial intelligence- and teacher-generated scores and feedback. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/187

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Hierarchical linear models (students nested in teachers) on Topeka AES scores estimated four outcomes—growth within first and second prompts, transfer across baselines, and growth from first baseline to second final—as a function of baseline score, finding "the highest growth" for the lowest starters.

"students with lower baseline scores experienced, on average, the highest growth, whereas students who started with mid-range or higher scores were predicted to have limited or no significant change across essay attempts."

Greene Nolan 2023 (2)¶

Greene Nolan, H. L., & Vang, M. C. (2023). Automated essay scoring in middle school writing: Understanding key predictors of students' growth and comparing artificial intelligence- and teacher-generated scores and feedback. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/187

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Executive-summary analysis of the same score dataset reports that lowest-band students, despite the largest gains, ended "around 8–9 points" on the 16-point sum scale, below the 12-point expectations level.

"students who began at that lowest level of 4 points still did not meet expectations (12 points) by the final draft—ending, on average, around 8–9 points."
