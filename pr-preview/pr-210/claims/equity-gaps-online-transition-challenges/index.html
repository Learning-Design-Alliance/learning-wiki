Evidence¶

Means 2020¶

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Group comparisons in the survey contrasted Hispanic, Black, and non-Hispanic White students on major-problem ratings for five challenges. Hispanic students reported a greater number of challenges; Black students' percentages generally fell between the other groups and did not differ statistically from either.

"Fitting the course in with home/family responsibilities, for example, was a major problem for 27 percent of Hispanic students, compared to just 12 percent of non-Hispanic White students."

Means 2020 (2)¶

Means, B., and Neisler, J., with Langer Research Associates. (2020). Suddenly Online: A National Survey of Undergraduates During the COVID-19 Pandemic. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/32aee7c4-5363-4d50-bc9c-a338e7dfac7e

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Survey analysis compared frequencies of connectivity and hardware/software problems by race/ethnicity, household income, and community type. Hardware/software issues also varied by income: 4 percent of high-income versus 11 percent of low-income students reported them often or very often.

"Hispanic students were more likely to experience internet connectivity problems often or very often (23 percent) compared to non-Hispanic White students (12 percent), with the frequency for Black students falling in between (17 percent)."
