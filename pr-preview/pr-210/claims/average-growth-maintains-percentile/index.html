q1 · i? · design · r1

Explanatory statement from the guide's FAQ on goal difficulty, illustrating that average growth preserves relative standing. The guide explains that "growth must exceed the average" for percentile gains, offering the 30th-percentile example as illustration rather than tested evidence.

"if a student who scores at the 30th percentile in the fall makes average growth, the result is maintenance of their status at the 30th achievement percentile for spring. To gain ground and see positive increases in a student's achievement percentile, growth must exceed the average."

Related Claims¶

  • The published norms allow normative interpretation of both status and fall-to-spring growth, e.g., an Algebra 1 student at the 55th fall percentile has an expected gain of 8 RIT with growth SD of 9, and an 11-RIT gain places the student at the 62nd growth percentile. — related
  • Under the 2025 norms, higher math growth expectations lower conditional growth percentiles for higher-growth students — related
