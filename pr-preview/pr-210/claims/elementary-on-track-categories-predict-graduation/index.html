In a sample of 93,617 Chicago students in grades 3-8 (SY2010-11), On-Track students (GPA 3.0+, attendance 90%+) had higher four-year graduation rates and were more likely to graduate with a 3.0+ GPA than Intensive, Academic, or Attendance Support peers. → UChicago Consortium on School Research 2024

UChicago Consortium on School Research 2024¶

UChicago Consortium on School Research. (2024). Family playbook: How can we understand students' progress in school? Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/events-and-resources

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Consortium Elementary On-Track analysis (Seeskin, Massion, & Usher, 2022) of 93,617 CPS students in grades 3-8 from SY2010-11; the printed figure shows, for example, 72% of On-Track students versus 34% of Intensive Support students graduated with a 3.0+ GPA.

"Students with 90+% attendance and a GPA of 3.0+ in grades 3–8 were more likely to graduate high school and have college-ready qualifications than their peers"

Related Claims¶

  • Students in higher CPS EOT categories had progressively higher rates of high school graduation and college enrollment — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Elementary students in the On-Track category graduated high school at more than twice the rate and enrolled in college at more than four times the rate of students in the Intensive Support category — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Attendance Support students maintained a 3.0 GPA despite low attendance but graduated high school at 71 percent, below the district average, and fewer than half attended college — related
  • Academic Support students, who attended regularly but earned below a 3.0 GPA, made up nearly 40 percent of students and saw academic struggles persist beyond the middle grades — related
  • More than one-half of Intensive Support students did not graduate high school in four years and more than 80 percent did not enroll directly in college — related
