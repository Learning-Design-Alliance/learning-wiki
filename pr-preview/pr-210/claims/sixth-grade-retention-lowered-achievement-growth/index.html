Evidence¶

Nagaoka 2004¶

Nagaoka, J., & Roderick, M. (2004). Ending Social Promotion: The Effects of Retention. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/ending-social-promotion-effects-retention

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Regression-discontinuity-style comparisons around the sixth-grade cutoff and a selection-controlled statistical model indicate retained sixth graders' growth was "6 percent lower than that of their low-achieving counterparts who were promoted," an effect remaining two years after the decision. The report prints no standardized effect size.

"Estimates from a statistical model that controls for selection effects (Comparison 3) suggests that achievement growth of retained sixth graders was 6 percent lower than that of their low-achieving counterparts who were promoted."
