Designing for child¶

Age · under 12 · All situations

75 rows from 71 pages say what to change when the brief's age is child; 46 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Positive Self-talk¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The goal is academic writing or problem solving, child or adolescent Teach self-instructions as part of the strategy (its steps become the cues) with goal setting and self-monitoring, over several sessions Strategy Instruction Improves Writing Quality [+M] (grades 4–10; self-instruction bundled with three other components)
Learners are young children (child, early primary) Let them talk aloud while working; the adult models the cue first and the child repeats it, then whispers it; do not treat talking aloud as off-task Thought and language become increasingly interdependent in the first few years, with private speech evolving into inner speech as self-regulation [+W] (a theoretical overview; no study)

Text-to-Speech¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child early readers building fluency Use speech or read-along for access to stories and content above their reading level, not as the main fluency practice; keep the child reading the words aloud themselves in separate practice, and check comprehension after read-along rather than assuming it Repeated listening improves oral reading fluency in second-grade students, with Listening Only outgaining Reading While Listening and Reading Only [~M] (one seven-week study of 75 second graders, reported second-hand in a review; reading while listening had the smallest fluency gain and an average comprehension loss; no statistical test printed)
Young children with an e-book or app, possibly in two languages (from the wording) Choose narration with a highlight that tracks the words, in the language whose print the child should look at; ask two or three story questions after, since attention to print did not reliably become comprehension Audio narration with finger-tracking animation directs bilingual preschoolers' attention to the target-language print in dual-language e-books, including the nondominant language [+M]; Multimedia features improved story comprehension in dual-language e-books (marginal trend) but not in single-language e-books [~M] (one eye-tracking study, bilingual preschoolers)

Ask Experts¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Keep the consultation one-to-one or with two or three children, short (10 minutes), on one problem, with the expert moving up the question–hint–model ladder and back down after each success Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (fourth and fifth graders, long division, 8 per condition, one-to-one)

Authentic Assessment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary school) Keep the task short and concrete with one product, one audience the children know of (another class, families), criteria as two or three "I can" statements, and teacher-led feedback rather than peer scoring. Authentic audiences [+M]: second-graders wrote better for an external audience (one study, abstract only); the rest is untested

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children or young adolescents (child, adolescent) Keep the "why" prompts but allow spoken answers; use two concrete cases rather than abstract descriptions; one relation per session. Self-explanation prompts improve learning from worked examples [+M] (pooled across levels of education, and one study with eighth graders; the adaptations themselves are untested)

Collaborative Evaluation¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (under 12) Use two objects at a time, two or three criteria phrased as questions with picture or worked anchors, and a short spoken comparison in pairs; the teacher gives the reference each time. Peer assessment [+M]: the meta-analysis includes primary students and found no reliable difference by education level; the configuration is untested

Concept Attainment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (under 12) and the target is a procedure (e.g. a fair test) Prefer direct instruction of the procedure with examples. Keep concept attainment for categories with visible attributes (animals, shapes). Guided against unguided discovery [~S]: 112 third- and fourth-graders learning the control-of-variables procedure; not a concept-attainment lesson

Constructive Alignment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children or adolescents (pre-k-12) (from the wording) Write outcomes as short "I can…" statements the teacher shares and checks with learners; keep frequent low-stakes checkpoints with a revision step. Assessment for learning [+M] for formative checkpoints (K–12 syntheses); the "I can" wording is untested; the earlier page described the pattern as developed in higher education

Cultural & Life Experiences Connections¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children (child, K-2) Build assessment tasks from practices families describe in interviews, as play with cards and objects, and ask children to justify their choices Assessment tasks built on family-identified funds of knowledge elicited computational thinking from K-2 children in a small design-based study that made no comparison with standard assessment [+M] (13 children, two tasks, no comparison with a standard task; resource-intensive)

Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Teach each convention by modelling and short practice ("now we think for three seconds, then whisper to your partner"); prefer choral and whiteboard response; talk with families about how questions and help work at home Wait time: Reviews of classroom studies, read from their abstracts, associate longer teacher wait time with longer, higher-cognitive-level student responses, with no effect size recorded [+M] (reviews across grade levels, not by culture). Family conversation: untested

Dual Coding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Supply pictures and flow diagrams with the text; have children label or complete them rather than draw freely Supplied: text with graphics [+M] (effect held across grade levels). Drawing: drawing in STEM [~M] (benefit larger in middle and high school than in primary)

Engagement¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Keep the arrival routine, short work blocks, teacher-held progress chart; avoid tangible rewards for taking part Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [~S] (one meta-analysis reports rewards more detrimental for children than college students); block lengths untested

Epistemic Cognition¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child learners (primary school) Frame questions as "how does it happen?" and have learners compare a mechanistic and a non-mechanistic explanation of a familiar phenomenon, giving a reason; keep content to what they already know, since they reject explanations that conflict with their knowledge. Do not expect web-source evaluation routines to transfer down. Elementary students prefer mechanistic explanations [+W]: grades 2–6, observed in interviews, no instruction tested

Error Analysis¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child (primary school) Keep correction overt (show the correct answer and how, not only right/wrong); use constructed peer errors after instruction rather than problem-first failure, which reversed for second to fifth graders. productive failure [~M]; overt correction for young children [~W] (second-hand, 1962 study)

Experience with Languaging Activities (ELAs) guided by a sequence of experience-based questions¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are in grades 6–8, or young child writers Keep writing short (one or two sentences per step), answer steps 2 and 3 orally, and write only step 4. Writing to learn [~S]: effects were reduced in grades 6–8 and with longer assignments (a moderator in one meta-analysis, read from its abstract)

Experiential Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children, or school-age Use short, concrete hands-on tasks and connect each to its symbol or written form in the same session; tell them what to try, not only to explore Hands-on learning improves achievement [+M] (mathematics, kindergarten to college); Unassisted discovery produces less learning than explicit instruction, while discovery enhanced with guidance outperforms other instruction [~S] (third and fourth graders)

Experiential Learning Cycle¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children (under 12) Shorter loops (experience 10–15 minutes, debrief 5 minutes); concrete materials linked to the written or symbolic form in the same session; tell them what to try rather than only to explore; the teacher states the rule. Hands-on learning [+M] (mathematics manipulatives, kindergarten to college); guided against unassisted discovery [~S] (third and fourth graders learning a procedure); loop lengths untested

Explicit Instruction: Vocabulary¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (pre-K to primary), or are weaker readers Keep the set small (3–4 words per session), teach orally with pictures and actions, read texts aloud with the words in them; expect taught-word gains, not general reading gains Vocabulary Instruction Improves Comprehension [+S] (pre-K–12; students with reading difficulties gained most on custom measures); doses untested

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Shorter cycles with the adult reading each attempt; praise the strategy or the work, never ability; small groups so attempts can be seen Praise: Praise for intelligence, rather than for effort, after success undermines children's motivation and performance after a later failure [-M] (fifth graders). Cycle length: untested

Flexible Grouping¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Fewer formats, taught as routines with visual role cards; similar-need groups with the teacher for 10–15 minutes; other groups do practice they can check themselves Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (fourth and fifth graders, one-to-one); format and timing untested

Game-based Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are preschool or early-primary children Use a simple game whose board or move is the representation (e.g. a numbered linear track); an adult plays alongside naming the numbers or quantities; four sessions of 15–20 min over two weeks Board-game study [+M]; guided play [~W] (guided play, not games specifically)

Gamification¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children (under 12) Prefer verbal, informational feedback and effort-focused badges to tangible prizes; avoid prizes for completion; no public individual ranking Rewards claim [-S] (tangible rewards more detrimental for children than college students, lab); praise claim [~M] (fifth graders)

Graphic Organizers¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) reading stories, including learners with learning disabilities Use a story map (setting, characters, problem, goal, events, resolution), taught by modelling, leading and then testing; withdraw the map once accuracy holds Story Mapping Improves Comprehension [+M] (third and fourth graders; 22 and 6 students; no standardized effect size)

Guided Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age), or adult / professionals Child: keep the cue–hint–demonstration ladder with one-to-one or small-group checking, and short rounds. Adult or professional: ask what they already do, and move guided items onto tasks from their own work as soon as the step is stable Child: Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (fourth and fifth graders, long division, 8 per condition). Adult: untested proposal

Include parents, teachers, or mentors in any self-regulation intervention and support their own skills and coaching capacity¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (under 12), or have autism or another developmental condition Train the parent to deliver the practice at home, with coaching and rehearsal; expect larger effects where the parent delivers practice than from information Parent-mediated intervention [+M] (children with autism, ages 1–6 and school age); parent coaching [+M] (ages 0–7). Both outside this page's 14–25 range

Instructor Accessibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Accessibility means routines in the room: the teacher circulates on a set route, learners have a help signal (a card or cup) so they need not call out, and the teacher holds brief informal talks with each pupil over a fortnight Positive teacher–student relationships are associated with greater student engagement and, more weakly, with achievement [~M] (correlational meta-analyses, preschool to high school; negative relationships mattered more in primary school); the routines are untested

Modeling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children, and the target is play, social or motor behaviour Model by acting out with materials in small groups, over several short sessions (the study used eight), and let children re-enact straight after Modeling training improved imaginative play in disadvantaged kindergartners, with gains in imaginativeness, verbal communication, spontaneity, and attention span persisting two months [+W] (80 kindergartners, a 1973 report read second-hand; modelling combined with direct teaching)

Motivation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) No tangible rewards for merely taking part; praise the strategy or effort, not the child's cleverness, especially before harder work; the teacher keeps the progress chart Rewards [~S] (one meta-analysis found rewards more detrimental for children than college students); praise for intelligence [~M] (fifth graders, nonverbal problems)

Multimodal Instruction¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary school) readers Pair text with a relevant graphic, picture or flow diagram. Do not sort children by a style inventory. Dual coding [+M]: the meta-analysis effect held across grade levels (elementary to adult); learning-styles matching [-M]: no style × modality interaction with 5th graders

Multiple Methods of Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children (child, pre-school or early primary) Observe the goal in play or with objects and pictures, with a short interview ("show me", "why there?"), alongside any written task; record what each child did Preschool children demonstrate behavioral knowledge of classification, one-to-one correspondence, and seriation during everyday free play with everyday materials [+W] (one observed play episode, two children); Assessment tasks built on family-identified funds of knowledge elicited computational thinking from K-2 children in a small design-based study that made no comparison with standard assessment [+W] (13 K–2 children, design study, no comparison with a standard test)

Note-taking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary) or adolescent with little note-taking experience Guided notes with few, well-cued blanks; pauses every 5–8 minutes; teacher models notes on the board for several sessions, not one; review in class Guided Notes Improve Note Accuracy [+M] (meta-analysis reports the benefit "particularly for school-age students", abstract only); timings untested

Observation/Shadowing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are teacher candidates learning to observe children or classrooms (from the wording) Run three or four workshops on authentic classroom video (a simplified simulation first if they are new to observing), each with a group observation assignment covering description, analysis and suggested support, and score it In the initial skill acquisition phase, authentic video yielded higher overall observation assignment scores than virtual simulation [+M]; Analytical competence showed a leap-plateau trajectory across sequential authentic-video workshops, improving mainly between Workshops 2 and 3 [+M] (one design-based study, 8 groups, fixed order)

Phonics¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child, kindergarten or first grade, beginning to read Run the full default: daily 20–30 minute lessons through the first school year, with phonemic-awareness work in the first term and decodable text from the first week Systematic Phonics Improves Word Reading [+S] (kindergarten to grade 6; largest effects in kindergarten and grades 1–2)
Learners with low confidence after years of failure (older children, adults) (from the wording) Begin with a correspondence they will master in the first session; show progress on their own weekly chart; avoid reading aloud in front of peers until the text is fully decodable for them untested proposal
A performance for a real audience (reading to younger children, a class reading, reading aloud at work) (from the wording) Choose or write a text within the learner's taught code, rehearse it with corrections until accurate, and keep the audience performance after accuracy, not as the practice untested proposal

Pre-reading Questioning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Readers are primary-age children (child) Ask two questions aloud and record answers for them; add one question about how they will check they understood ("What will you do if a part doesn't make sense?"), not only questions about the topic Advance organizers aided learning …, but activating prior topic knowledge did not improve primary pupils' text comprehension in one experiment [~M] (88 primary pupils: metacognitive activation helped, topic activation did not)

Procedural Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Shorter rounds (3–5 items), concrete materials or a physical model of each step, one-to-one or small-group checking during supported attempts Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (fourth and fifth graders, long division, one-to-one); round size untested

Process-based Writing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child in the early primary grades Shorter texts; plan orally or with drawings; co-write with a partner in structured pairs (the more able writer helping the less able); revise one thing per piece Collaborative Writing Improves Text Quality [+M] (grades 4–12, and one randomised study of 10–11-year-olds that found larger gains in later individual writing); younger children untested

Self-monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent with, or at risk for, attention difficulties or disabilities, and the problem is staying on task Self-recording of on-task behaviour at short cued intervals (an app, a vibrating timer or a tone), after a training session; record the behaviour, not the learning, and check separately whether work completed changes Self Monitoring Improves On Task Behavior [+M] (single-case studies, grades 4–12, on-task behaviour only)

Sequencing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children in the early primary grades (from the wording) Teach first, then practise; do not put problem solving before instruction Productive Failure Improves Conceptual Learning [~S] (the meta-analysis's advantage reversed for second to fifth graders)

Social Interdependence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (upper primary), or at risk of failure Use pairs with scripted tutor and tutee roles, switching each session; team score = sum of both members' daily individual quiz scores Group rewards combined with individual accountability [+M] (64 at-risk 4th and 5th graders, maths computation; reward plus structure best; effect figures second-hand)

Social Presence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent No public photos or personal profiles; introductions through a class activity the teacher runs (what I like, what I am good at); presence comes mainly from the teacher's relationship with each pupil, built in short regular contacts Teacher–student relationships [+M] (associations; negative relationships mattered more in primary school); the routines are untested

Strengths-based Approach¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Collect strengths through families and play-based tasks built from home practices; name strengths aloud as what the child did, never as "smart"; keep the self-record oral or pictorial Assessment tasks built on family-identified funds of knowledge elicited computational thinking from K-2 children in a small design-based study that made no comparison with standard assessment [+M] (K-2, 13 children, no comparison); Praise for intelligence, rather than for effort, after success undermines children's motivation and performance after a later failure [-S] (fifth graders, intelligence praise)

Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (under 12) Two criteria phrased as questions, with a picture or worked anchor; review in structured pairs, spoken first and then one written suggestion; the teacher models with a class draft each time; revise in the same or the next lesson. Collaborative writing [+S]: in one small randomised study, 10–11-year-olds writing in structured pairs gained more in their individual writing; that was co-writing, not peer review. Peer assessment [+M] found no difference by education level

Supporting Students with Intellectual Disabilities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The student is a child in primary school Keep steps short (5–10 minutes of focused practice), practise daily inside routines, name what the student did after each success, and use pictures or objects for each step Praise for intelligence rather than effort [+M] (fifth graders, general population) for the feedback; the rest untested

Wise Feedback Across Racial and Ethnic Difference¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Give the three parts spoken, short and about the work; praise the strategy or effort, never "smart"; the assurance in plain words ("I know you can get this part right; let's try it again now") and an immediate second attempt Praise for intelligence, rather than for effort, after success undermines children's motivation and performance after a later failure [-M] (fifth graders; tests the praise, not the framing)

Blended Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (pre-k-12) Run the online part in school time under supervision (rotation between stations or groups) rather than at home, and keep online segments short; give the teacher a view of who completed what. untested proposal; the recorded synthesis is mostly higher education and professional training

Cognitive Flexibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Age child (under 12) Use two cases at a time, side by side, with the one dimension that differs named by the teacher; no free navigation among cases. untested proposal; no claim here tests this relationship with children

Criterion- and Norm-Referenced Testing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children (child) in screening or readiness testing Do not use one sitting for a placement; use an individually administered criterion-referenced check with pictures and spoken instructions over two or three occasions; re-check within the year untested proposal

Debriefing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children (primary age) Five minutes, oral, teacher-led, three questions (what happened, what was tricky, what we will do next time), straight after the activity; one class change written on the board and read out next time untested proposal

Deliberate Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) The teacher chooses the component and writes the goal in the child's words; drills of five to ten minutes; a visible chart of attempts that met the standard; whole-task attempt at the end untested proposal

Develop Understanding¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (under 12) Keep explanation prompts short and oral or pointing-based; use concrete, visible cases; check each child's prediction individually rather than by show of hands. Untested proposal; the self-explanation meta-analysis spans levels of education but the claim page does not report results by age

Digital Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Use short sessions (10 minutes or less), supervised by an adult in the room; give feedback that needs little reading; avoid open typed answers untested proposal

Effective Classroom Management Plan Criteria¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are a young child (preschool or early primary) Fewer rules (three), shown with pictures; routines rehearsed physically and daily for longer (four to six weeks); immediate, frequent praise; redirection by proximity and a quiet prompt, not warnings; no self-recording yet untested proposal; the meta-analysis covers K–12 without an age breakdown

Elaboration Theory¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent Make the epitome concrete (a picture, a story or a model of the whole), keep levels to one lesson each, and have the teacher lead the synthesis aloud before asking learners to arrange items. untested proposal; the study tested college and advanced high-school physics students, untested with children

Epistemic Games¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or young adolescent Choose a practice whose decisions are visible and concrete (planning a park, running a shop); shorten sessions to 30–40 minutes; keep an adult in the mentor role; check the content separately. Untested proposal

Evaluating Sources¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child learners (primary age) Use a curated set of sources and a single question ("who made this, and what do others say about them?") asked with the teacher; do not expect the full routine Untested; no claim here tests source-evaluation teaching with children

Flipped Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Do not set pre-class video as homework; run the exposure in class at a station or as a short whole-class video, then move to guided practice with the teacher circulating ("faux-flipped") untested; Contingent scaffolding… [~M] (fourth and fifth graders, one-to-one tutoring) supports the circulating teacher, not the flip

Functional Behavior Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learner is a young child (preschool or early primary) Rely on adult observation and family interview, not the child's report; use a picture card or gesture as the replacement response; keep consequences immediate and the reinforcer frequent; check sleep, hunger and illness first untested proposal; the only functional-analysis claim here is one 4-year-old in a feeding programme (escape and attention [+W])

Goal Setting & Monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Teacher sets the goal with the child, one proximal sub-goal at a time, made visible on a chart; monitor with a simple tally or check-in rather than a written plan untested proposal; the self-monitoring evidence is from students in grades 4–12 with or at risk for disabilities and measures on-task behaviour, see self-monitoring [~M]

Holistic Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent Make the whole concrete (a picture, story or physical model of the whole), keep each part to one lesson, and have the teacher lead the placement aloud before learners do it untested proposal; the hierarchy studies used college and advanced high-school students

Journaling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child (primary school) Entries of a sentence or two, or a drawing with a label, at the end of a lesson; one specific question at a time; the teacher reads and replies orally to a few each day. untested proposal; the writing-to-learn synthesis covered elementary to college but reports no age-specific result except smaller effects in grades 6–8 (Writing To Learn Improves Understanding [~M])

Knowledge Organization¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Use story-like or picture-card organizations of two levels, sorted physically; for narrative content, organize around goal, events and outcome untested for organization design; narrative recall is better when stories follow a familiar story structure (Narrative memory is superior when content follows stereotypical story grammar structure, and goal information is critical to comprehension [+W], second-hand review, narratives only)

Learning by Producing (multimedia production as learning)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) One medium (narrated pictures, a poster with audio), roles named with pictures, the teacher scribes the storyboard, sessions of 30–40 minutes, and an audience they know (another class, families). Untested; the audience comparison with second graders (authentic audiences [+M]) concerned writing, not media

Memory Consolidation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children (child, adolescent) Keep recall items short and low-stakes, read aloud where reading is weak, and space returns across days in class time rather than homework untested; the spacing evidence comes mostly from young adults and is thin for children's long-term retention (Spaced Practice Improves Retention [~S])

Reflection¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child (primary school) Reflection is oral or drawn, led by the teacher, one specific question at a time, immediately after the task; no written journal. untested proposal; the school-based meta-analysis in structured reflection [+M] used written tasks and the claim page does not report ages

Standardized Test Fairness and Bias¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children (child) taking a screening or readiness test Do not use one sitting for a placement; observe over several occasions; use pictures and spoken instructions; re-screen within the year untested proposal

Structured Academic Controversy¶
