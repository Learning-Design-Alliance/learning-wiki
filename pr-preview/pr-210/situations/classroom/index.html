Designing for classroom¶

Setting · school or university class with a teacher · All situations

57 rows from 55 pages say what to change when the brief's setting is classroom; 21 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Authentic Assessment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Large class (more than about 60) or little assessor time (from the wording) Use peer assessment with the rubric for the draft; teacher feedback on a sample plus whole-class feedback on common problems; double-score only a sample of finals and products near the threshold. Peer assessment [+M]; structured criteria not shown to beat freeform peer review [~M], so do not expect the rubric alone to lift the peer effect

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with a teacher designing their own tasks Before teaching, check each planned task against step 4 (does the output go beyond the material?), with a colleague if possible; expect tasks planned as interactive to come out merely active. Teachers had minimal success designing Constructive and Interactive activities [~M] and Students learned more in Constructive than Active activities [+M]

Collaborative Evaluation¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
A large class, or no teacher reaches each group (from the wording) Replace group consensus with the mean of four or five independent ratings per object, and give the reference to the whole class afterwards; discuss only the objects where the mean departs most from it. Peer judgment accuracy [~M]: with a well-specified rubric, averaged ratings by AP English students correlated with expert scores about as well as teachers' did. Correlational, one subject

Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, a large class (from the wording) Use all-student response (cards, whiteboards, polls) for most questions and a simple tally of who answers publicly; correct conduct with low-profile moves first Wait time in classrooms: Questioning Strategies Improve Learning [+M]. Tally and response modes: untested

Effective Classroom Management Plan Criteria¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The whole class is disruptive (from the wording) Start with steps 1 and 2: re-teach every routine for two weeks, with daily rehearsal, before adding any new consequence; tally disruptions before and after Universal classroom management [+M] and less disruption in all 12 studies [+M] (K–12 classrooms, against usual practice)
One teacher with a large group (a lecture, a large class, no assistant) (from the wording) Rely on prevention and routines more than on individual redirection: a visible attention signal, short timed tasks with a clear start and end, a routine for asking for help without the teacher, and peer roles for materials; respond to the group, then to individuals after class untested proposal

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and a large group Collect every learner's attempt at once (mini-whiteboards, a poll, a short written item), give whole-class information on the commonest error, have everyone do one matched item, then go to those who still fail; use peers with a rubric for drafts; budget class time for the loop Trained teachers' loop time [+M] (secondary mathematics, time allocation only, no learning outcome); rubrics and peer feedback [~M] (rubrics changed peer comments, one study found less reflective feedback)

Flipped Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group (100+) (from the wording) Use the opening check as a poll, pairs or small groups for application, and whole-class feedback on the commonest error, then go to groups still stuck; trained peer teachers or assistants can extend the circulation Active learning improves exam performance [+S] (held across class sizes, largest in small classes); Peer-teaching flipped classroom students show higher calculus conceptual understanding… [+M] (two intact classes; the procedure is not described on the claim page)

Fostering Communities of Learning (FCL)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many groups (30+ learners) (from the wording) Use role cards and question stems so groups can run without the teacher; the teacher circulates on a fixed rota and checks each research group once before jigsaw; use written exit checks to decide which group to visit. Contingent scaffolding [~M]: evidence mostly from one-to-one tutoring; its claim page notes contingency across a whole class is hard. The rota is untested

Functional Behavior Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The whole class is disruptive, not one learner (from the wording) Do not start individual assessments; put universal management in place first (taught routines, clear expectations, frequent positive contact), then reassess which learners still stand out Universal classroom management [~M] (12-study meta-analysis, K–12 classrooms; universal practices without functional information)
One teacher with a full class, no support staff (from the wording) Use a tally sheet with three columns (time, activity just before, what followed) during the two lessons where the behavior is worst, for one week; test one trigger change in the second week untested proposal

Gamification¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Use team scores from individual quizzes (step 5), updated once a week on a class board; the teacher reviews the board, not every point Group rewards on individual learning [+M] (school studies, four weeks or more, not gamified)

Graphic Organizers¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with a large group Have pairs build the organizer together; collect a few, project two that differ, and discuss which fits the content better and why Teacher-student interactive semantic mapping produced higher EFL reading comprehension posttest scores than teacher-initiated and student-mediated semantic mapping [+M] (EFL university students); Collaborative concept mapping enhances learning more than individual concept mapping [+W] (second-hand)

Include parents, teachers, or mentors in any self-regulation intervention and support their own skills and coaching capacity¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Setting is a school classroom and the problem is conduct, exclusions or suspensions Add a short exercise on the teachers' stance towards discipline (relationships first, the student's perspective) before or alongside the student programme; track exclusions by group of students Empathic discipline [+M] (middle-school mathematics teachers; overall effect small and marginal in the preregistered replication, larger for students at high risk)

Multiple Methods of Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large group, or scarce marker time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor who checks in briefly) (from the wording) Keep one quick common method for everyone; use the second method only for learners near a decision or out of line with their other work; use trained peer review for the second reading of products; a 2-minute oral check per learner at most Peer assessment improves academic performance by a small average amount relative to no assessment or to teacher assessment [+M] (meta-analysis of 54 studies; about performance, not the accuracy of peer judgements); the targeting rule is untested

Note-taking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large lecture group (from the wording) Guided-notes outline for everyone; two or three pauses per hour with a restatement written to a neighbour or a poll; collect a random sample of notes after the first week to check for errors and address the commonest one in the next lecture Guided Notes Improve Note Accuracy [+M] (one quasi-experiment with two intact undergraduate classes); pauses and sampling untested

Online Course Design (Community of Inquiry)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
online-self-paced with rolling enrolment, or one teacher for hundreds of learners (from the wording) Replace teacher feedback on each piece with automatic feedback on self-checks, a weekly teacher summary of common errors, and peer review against a rubric; keep the week-1 if-then plan; give the teacher's time to learners flagged by a missed week. Implementation intentions [+S] (the two field experiments were in large online courses); the rest is untested

Phonics¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a whole class of 25–30 (from the wording) Introduce the new correspondence to the whole class in 10 minutes with choral responses and every child showing a written answer at once (whiteboards); then small groups by placement for decodable reading while others practise independently; weekly one-to-one checks rotated across the class Phonemic Awareness Training Improves Reading [~M] (for phonemic-awareness training, small groups beat whole-class and individual delivery; carried to phonics); the rotation is untested

Process-based Writing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large class or many sections (from the wording) Peers give the first response with criteria; the teacher reads a sample of peer comments and responds to each writer on two or three priorities only; rotate 5-minute conferences so each writer gets one per composition; do not grade drafts Peer Feedback Improves Writing [+M] (peer vs teacher feedback not reliably different); Students report teacher feedback on writing is insufficient [~W] (interviews; teachers' load)

Social Presence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Face-to-face classroom Presence cues are already there; spend effort on names, on the teacher's warmth and expectations, and on routines that let quiet pupils be heard (pairs before plenary), not on profiles Positive teacher–student relationships are associated with greater student engagement and, more weakly, with achievement [+M] (correlational meta-analyses, preschool to high school, plus a learner-centred synthesis; not a test of any routine)

Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large class, or no teacher reaches each pair (from the wording) Two to three reviewers per draft at random; the teacher reads comments only where an author flags a disagreement, and gives one class-wide note on the commonest problem; use averaged ratings rather than single reviews. Peer feedback on writing [+M]: number of peers did not moderate the effect; peer accuracy [~M]: averaged ratings, one subject, correlational

Summative Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher marks for a large group, or marking time is scarce (volunteers, a busy shift, one tutor) (from the wording) Machine-score the knowledge items; judge a smaller set of performance tasks with a short rubric; double-mark a sample of about one in ten, not all; return results by outcome with one comment each. A computerised adaptive test can shorten a mastery test while reaching nearly the same decision An adaptive mastery testing strategy reduces test length by 30% to 81% while reaching the same mastery decision as the conventional test for 96% of trainees [+W] (a military-training simulation); the marking plan is untested

Supporting Students with Intellectual Disabilities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, a full general class, the student needs intermittent support (from the wording) Build the student's steps into the class task (same activity, smaller step, picture checklist), seat a trained peer partner, and check the student's attempt first when circulating Peer tutoring improves achievement [+M] (tutee ability level not a moderator, mathematics); the rest untested

Ask Experts¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one expert and a large group (office hours, drop-in) Collect question cards in advance, triage them, spend open time on the commonest stalls with a think-aloud, and book short individual slots for learners who have not come; do not count attendance as evidence of benefit untested proposal; drop-in tutoring visits were usually unrelated to course grades in one observational study (Tutoring center visits usually showed no significant difference in individual course performance, and sometimes tutored students performed worse [~M], college tutoring centre, self-selected visitors)

Criterion- and Norm-Referenced Testing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, or facilitator time is scarce (volunteers, a short weekly session) (from the wording) Learners self-score short checks against an answer key with worked reasons; the teacher spends contact time only on near-cut learners and the skill most learners have not mastered untested proposal

Deliberate Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Work on the component most of the class misses; pairs score each other's attempts on that one rubric line; the teacher scans every whole-task attempt at the end and gives whole-class feedback on the commonest error untested proposal

Develop Understanding¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Collect step 1 predictions and step 4 checks from every learner at once (cards, mini-whiteboards, a poll); address the commonest wrong idea with the whole class, then go to those still wrong. Untested proposal; contingent support [~M] is shown one-to-one, and its classroom-scale effect is not

Digital Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and a large group, devices available Use the software for individual practice and hints while the teacher watches the class's results live and goes to those who are stuck; use class time for what the software cannot do (discussion of reasons, whole tasks) untested proposal; contingent-support evidence is one-to-one (Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M])

Dual Coding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Large class, one teacher (from the wording) Collect a sample of learner drawings each session, show one correct and one common error to the class, then have everyone compare their own with the model untested proposal

Engagement¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Make participation visible all at once (everyone writes or shows a response); use spot checks of on-task behaviour across the room; greet at the door even if briefly untested at scale; the greeting claim is from middle-school classrooms (greetings [+M])

Epistemic Games¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher or practitioner for many learners (30 or more) (from the wording) Run in teams of four with differentiated roles (departments); the teacher plays one mentor character for the whole class at set points, and teams check decisions against a written checklist from the epistemography between visits. Untested proposal; the earlier page's role differentiation

Experience with Languaging Activities (ELAs) guided by a sequence of experience-based questions¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Have learners write steps 4 and 5; the facilitator reads a sample of five step 4 answers and responds to the whole group, asking one learner why a correct answer is correct; pairs check each other's answer against a model answer. Untested; the "why did you choose this correct answer" move is the source's (strategy page)

Explicit Instruction: Vocabulary¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group, or scarce facilitator time (a volunteer, one short check-in a week) (from the wording) Teach the set to everyone at once in 10 minutes; let spaced review run on cards or an app; in the check-in, look at one thing only: a written or spoken use of two or three of the week's words untested proposal; the review part rests on Supplemental computer-based spaced repetition… [+M]

Flexible Grouping¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, many learners (30 or more), or a class of about 50 (from the wording) One similar-need group with the teacher per segment while the rest work in mixed groups with roles and self-checkable tasks; rotate which group meets the teacher across the week; whole-class reteach when more than half share a gap untested proposal; contingent support is shown one-to-one only (Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M])

Goal Setting & Monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Large class, one teacher (from the wording) Use one shared goal template and peer or self check-ins against the criterion; teacher reviews goals only for learners whose progress stalls untested proposal

Guided Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Have every learner show each attempt at once (mini-whiteboards, cards, a quick poll) so the teacher can scan them all; give whole-class feedback on the commonest error, then go to those who still fail. With older learners, pairs can also check each other against a worked answer; with young children, or when one misconception is widespread, the teacher's scan is safer than peer checking. Collect one unaided item from everyone before independent work untested proposal; contingent-support evidence is one-to-one and its classroom-scale effect is not shown (Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M])

Holistic Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group (from the wording) Have every learner show their placement at once (cards, mini-whiteboards, a poll), address the commonest misplacement to the whole group, then go to those still misplacing untested proposal

Journaling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a mentor who checks in briefly) (from the wording) Do not promise individual replies. Read a rotating sample (for example five entries a week), give one whole-group reply on the commonest confusion, and use peer reading in pairs with one question each; keep the learner's own reread of their last next step every time. untested proposal; the reason for keeping some use of entries is diary alone [-M]

Knowledge Organization¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and a large group One shared organization on the board; pairs sort printed cards into it; the teacher checks only top-level placement across the room, then everyone rebuilds individually from memory untested at class scale; pairs building maps together is reported to beat individuals (An ICAP theory article reports, second-hand and without describing the design, a study in which concept mapping with a peer enhanced learning more than concept mapping alone [+W], one study reported second-hand)

Learning by Producing (multimedia production as learning)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many teams, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Spend the facilitator's minutes on two checks only: the blueprint's content (5 minutes per team) and the individual explanation before presentation. Teams peer-review blueprints against the criteria list first; use templates so tool questions do not reach the facilitator. Untested proposal; the blueprint check is the step most directly tied to the content-quality concern [~M]

Memory Consolidation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher or tutor for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift) (from the wording) Use self-marked recall (learners check against an answer card) and look only at the items many missed; spend facilitator time on re-explaining those, not on marking untested proposal

Modeling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group (from the wording) Give one live model to all; at each pause have every learner show a prediction at once (cards, mini-whiteboards, a poll); then in pairs, one learner narrates the next item while the other checks against the modelled decision list; re-model only for the error most pairs show untested proposal; the explanation step is carried from Prompting learners to self-explain… [+S], and the large field study under Constructive learning beats active and passive learning [~M] found only the most interactive activities ahead of passive ones in real classes

Motivation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Spend the scarce minutes on one question about the likely low component, and on the learners whose behaviour dropped; let relevance sentences and progress records be written by learners without the facilitator untested proposal

Positive Self-talk¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and many learners Model once for the whole class; learners write their own cue cards and rehearse in pairs; the teacher prompts "What's your cue?" only for learners who stall Untested proposal

Pre-reading Questioning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large class (from the wording) Collect every learner's guesses at once (cards, mini-whiteboards, a poll), scan for the commonest confident wrong prediction, and spend the after-reading check on that one, with the text's refutation untested proposal; the confront-the-misconception move is from Instruction that confronts science misconceptions … produces conceptual change more often than standard exposition [~M] (science and reading syntheses, abstracts only)

Procedural Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and a large group Every learner shows each attempt at once (mini-whiteboards, cards, a poll); whole-class feedback on the commonest error, then the teacher goes to those still failing; pairs check each other against a worked answer for routine steps untested proposal; contingent-support evidence is one-to-one (contingent scaffolding [~M])

Self-monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and many learners Run whole-class checkpoints on a common signal; every learner records on a one-line card; the teacher compares the cards of four or five learners per checkpoint with their work, rotating so each learner is checked at least weekly; whole-class feedback on the commonest mismatch Untested; the behaviour evidence is from individual students in single-case designs

Sequencing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners (from the wording) Give the prerequisite check and the stage-end items to everyone at once (cards, a quick poll), keep one main order for the class, and give a short branch (back to a prerequisite, or ahead to mixed practice) to the learners the check sorts out untested proposal

Social Emotional Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large class (30+) and little time (from the wording) Run the skill lesson whole-class with paired rehearsal and cue cards; use a 2-minute written check-in instead of a sharing circle; coach a few learners each day in rotation. Untested proposal

Standardized Test Fairness and Bias¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher or tutor prepares many learners, or facilitator time is scarce (volunteers, a short weekly session) (from the wording) Use one full practice sitting under real conditions, self-marked against an answer key with worked reasons, and spend the scarce contact time only on the three items most learners missed; move demographic forms after the test, which needs no time untested proposal

Strengths-based Approach¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Replace individual conferences with a written two-line strengths-and-next-target comment on one piece of work per learner per fortnight, and a learner self-record checked at the same time; use the low-stakes task in step 1 for everyone in the first week untested proposal

Supporting Gifted and Talented Students¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, a full mixed class, several students ahead at different points (from the wording) Use one pre-check and two or three tiers of replacement work the teacher can run at once; check in with the ahead group together for 5 minutes twice a week rather than individually untested proposal

Text-to-Speech¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large class (from the wording) Make speech available to all rather than assigned to a few; at each section break use a whole-class check (a quick poll or one written line) so the teacher sees who is lost, and go to those learners untested proposal

Transfer of Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Pairs compare the two cases on one sheet and write what is the same; the teacher collects answers and states the principle from them; every learner does one unaided transfer item on paper untested; the comparison evidence includes classroom experiments in the meta-analysis but no class-size result

Universal Design For Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Offer choice at the level of the whole task (two versions of one activity) rather than per learner; one shared rubric displayed for all formats; check-ins on who is stuck untested proposal

Validity, Reliability, and Bias in Classroom Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher marks for a large group (from the wording) Use selected-response or short-answer items for breadth; reserve judgement-scored tasks for the objectives that need them; mark one question across all scripts at a time rather than one script at a time; re-mark the first ten scripts at the end to check for drift untested proposal

Wise Feedback Across Racial and Ethnic Difference¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Write one framing sentence once and reuse it, but add one clause that names something in this learner's work; cap the critique at two points; a fixed time budget of about 2–3 minutes per piece: 30 seconds for the framing, 1–2 minutes for two critiques with next steps, 30 seconds for the revision date untested proposal; a template with no personal clause is expected to read as generic
