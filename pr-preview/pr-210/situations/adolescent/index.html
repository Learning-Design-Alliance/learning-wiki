Designing for adolescent¶

Age · 12 to 17 · All situations

29 rows from 29 pages say what to change when the brief's age is adolescent; 20 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children or young adolescents (child, adolescent) Keep the "why" prompts but allow spoken answers; use two concrete cases rather than abstract descriptions; one relation per session. Self-explanation prompts improve learning from worked examples [+M] (pooled across levels of education, and one study with eighth graders; the adaptations themselves are untested)

Cognitive Flexibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Age adolescent, goal a mathematics procedure Have learners compare alternative solution methods side by side; expect gains in choosing methods, and add a separate item asking why each method works. Comparing cases side by side improves learning and transfer by a moderate average amount, though in one algebra study it raised procedural knowledge and flexibility but not conceptual knowledge [~M]; tested with seventh-graders, procedural flexibility rose, conceptual knowledge did not

Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent Name conventions openly and invite learners to say where their norms differ; avoid public correction in front of peers; watch for learners who define themselves against school (step 10) Correction: empathic discipline [+M] (US middle-school maths teachers). Rest untested

Dual Coding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent or adult, goal is a principle in science or mathematics Add learner drawing of the process or system, with a model to compare against Drawing in STEM [+M] (STEM only, abstract read)

Effective Classroom Management Plan Criteria¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescents who meet many teachers a day Agree the rules with the class in the first week; give reasons for every procedure; greet each learner at the door; correct in private, never in front of peers; add self-recording for learners who want it Greetings at the door [+M] (middle-school classes); self-monitoring [+M] (grades 4–12, students with or at risk for disabilities); the rest untested

Engagement¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent, with disruptive or off-task behaviour Add the greeting routine at every lesson; for individual learners, cued self-recording of on-task behaviour in work blocks Positive Greetings At The Door Improve Engagement [+M] (middle school); Self Monitoring Improves On Task Behavior [+M] (students with or at risk for disabilities; carried to others untested)

Epistemic Cognition¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adolescent learners, web or news sources, a school course Embed six 50-minute lessons on lateral reading in an existing subject (government, history), with teacher preparation; practise on live sites. Civic Online Reasoning Instruction Improves Evaluation [+M]: high-school field study, matched controls

Evaluating Sources¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adolescent learners in a school subject (government, history, science) Embed six or so 50-minute lessons in the subject's own topics, with teacher preparation beforehand; practise on live sites about the unit's questions Civic Online Reasoning Instruction Improves Evaluation [+M] (high-school field study with matched controls, and eleventh graders without a control)

Functional Behavior Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learner is an adolescent (about grade 4 and up) Interview the learner as a main source of trigger information; agree the replacement with them; add self-recording of on-task behavior from week 4 or so Self-monitoring of on-task behavior [+M] (single-case studies, grades 4–12, students with or at risk for disabilities; behavior, not achievement)

Motivation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent and say the subject is pointless Value first: the relevance-writing exercise at each unit start, two or three options of topic within the unit Task value [+M] (reviews; the writing exercise reported second-hand)

Note-taking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary) or adolescent with little note-taking experience Guided notes with few, well-cued blanks; pauses every 5–8 minutes; teacher models notes on the board for several sessions, not one; review in class Guided Notes Improve Note Accuracy [+M] (meta-analysis reports the benefit "particularly for school-age students", abstract only); timings untested

Online Course Design (Community of Inquiry)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent in a web-based school course Plan every interaction and the community-building explicitly; schedule live contact; keep the weekly structure strict and contact families or a school mentor after a missed week. Online community required deliberate, planned effort [+W] (interviews with high-school e-teachers); the actions are untested

Phonics¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Older struggling readers (grade 2 and above, or adolescent) Diagnose the gaps rather than restart the sequence; teach multisyllable decoding and word parts (prefixes, roots, suffixes); pair every phonics lesson with fluency and comprehension work; expect decoding gains before comprehension gains Systematic Phonics Improves Word Reading [~S] (older readers with difficulties: decoding effects remained, comprehension and spelling effects not reliably above zero); Morphological Instruction Improves Literacy [+M] (largest gains on decoding, particular benefit for less-able readers, preschool to grade 8)

Positive Self-talk¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The goal is academic writing or problem solving, child or adolescent Teach self-instructions as part of the strategy (its steps become the cues) with goal setting and self-monitoring, over several sessions Strategy Instruction Improves Writing Quality [+M] (grades 4–10; self-instruction bundled with three other components)

Self-monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent with, or at risk for, attention difficulties or disabilities, and the problem is staying on task Self-recording of on-task behaviour at short cued intervals (an app, a vibrating timer or a tone), after a training session; record the behaviour, not the learning, and check separately whether work completed changes Self Monitoring Improves On Task Behavior [+M] (single-case studies, grades 4–12, on-task behaviour only)

Social Emotional Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent Frame skills around fairness, identity, friendship and real stakes; let learners choose scenarios and lead discussion; avoid scripts that feel young; offer mindfulness or breathing practice as one strategy among several. Autonomy support [~M], carried from intrinsic motivation to SEL; mindfulness programmes [+W] and self-regulation interventions with high schoolers and young adults [+W], one practice brief's review, no effect sizes

Social Interdependence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent (early secondary) Use cooperative goal structures as the default arrangement for multi-week units, with individual checks feeding a team score Cooperative learning improves achievement [+S] (meta-analysis of 148 studies of early adolescents, direction only; Learning Together method d = 0.70–0.91 from an unpublished report)

Social Presence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent No public photos or personal profiles; introductions through a class activity the teacher runs (what I like, what I am good at); presence comes mainly from the teacher's relationship with each pupil, built in short regular contacts Teacher–student relationships [+M] (associations; negative relationships mattered more in primary school); the routines are untested

Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent secondary-school writers Anonymous drafts, four or five reviewers per essay with a well-specified rubric, and the average of their ratings shown to the author beside the comments; teacher reads flagged disagreements. Peer accuracy [~M]: AP English students' averaged rubric ratings correlated with expert scores about as well as teachers' did, in lower- as in higher-performing schools; correlational

Supporting Students with Intellectual Disabilities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The student is an adolescent or young adult near transition (work, independent living) Move practice into community and workplace tasks; teach the student to set and review their own goals weekly and to lead their own planning meeting, rehearsed beforehand Self-determination instruction [+M] and student-led IEP meetings [+M] (high-school students with intellectual or learning disabilities); post-school outcomes not measured

Elaboration Theory¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent Make the epitome concrete (a picture, a story or a model of the whole), keep levels to one lesson each, and have the teacher lead the synthesis aloud before asking learners to arrange items. untested proposal; the study tested college and advanced high-school physics students, untested with children

Epistemic Games¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or young adolescent Choose a practice whose decisions are visible and concrete (planning a park, running a shop); shorten sessions to 30–40 minutes; keep an adult in the mentor role; check the content separately. Untested proposal

Holistic Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or adolescent Make the whole concrete (a picture, story or physical model of the whole), keep each part to one lesson, and have the teacher lead the placement aloud before learners do it untested proposal; the hierarchy studies used college and advanced high-school students

Include parents, teachers, or mentors in any self-regulation intervention and support their own skills and coaching capacity¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent, 11–15 Put more of the component into the adults (teachers and carers cue in the moment, structure the setting); keep the youth's practice short and frequent; give emotion regulation a large share untested here; from the brief's statement that reward and emotion systems mature before control systems at these ages (adolescent brain imbalance [+W], the brief's summary of research, q2)

Memory Consolidation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are children (child, adolescent) Keep recall items short and low-stakes, read aloud where reading is weak, and space returns across days in class time rather than homework untested; the spacing evidence comes mostly from young adults and is thin for children's long-term retention (Spaced Practice Improves Retention [~S])

Observation/Shadowing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
School pupils (adolescent) job shadowing for career exploration Set the purpose as finding out what the work involves; give three questions to ask the host (what a typical day holds, what they decide, how they learned it); a short write-up or talk to peers afterwards; do not assess performance untested proposal

Structured Academic Controversy¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Age adolescent or adult in civics, ethics, history or policy Use the default sequence. For adults, ask for the learner-valued goal explicitly, since adults often arrive with a held view; collect the starting position privately. untested; from the earlier page (target learners); untested proposal for the adult change

Supporting Gifted and Talented Students¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The student is an adolescent in secondary school, ahead in one subject Subject acceleration in that subject (the next course, online or with an older class), with the student staying with their year group for other subjects untested proposal; the earlier page's constraint that repeated grade-skipping opens large maturity gaps
