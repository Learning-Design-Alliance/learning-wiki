Conceptual change theory distinguishes misconceptions from uncertainty, implying different teaching approaches¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article applies conceptual change theory to teacher knowledge of dyslexia, arguing that the instructional response depends on the learner's status: "Research on conceptual change suggests a different teaching approach is needed if an individual holds a misconception rather than an uncertainty." The Dyslexia Knowledge Questionnaire-2 items were therefore designed to classify each dyslexia concept as held as a scientific conception, a misconception, or an uncertainty. This framework organizes the study's diagnosis of which concepts need misconception-targeted versus uncertainty-filling instruction.
