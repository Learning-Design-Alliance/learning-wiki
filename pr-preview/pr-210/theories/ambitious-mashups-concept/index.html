Ambitious mashups: cyberlearning projects combine novel technologies, theories, methods, and equity goals in frontier-oriented integrations¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report's central organizing concept: cyberlearning projects are mashups that 'combined many elements in a novel integration, to explore a promising approach to a particular learning goal or context'. They are 'pushing for frontiers' rather than integrating established components, and they study 'what happens when learners inhabit' the designs. The output is understanding of a technology, theory, method, or equity approach rather than a scalable solution.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Projects bring together computer science expertise, learning sciences theories and methods, and a commitment to investigating equity

Constraints¶

  • The output is not usually intended to be a scalable near-term solution but understanding applicable as future technologies arrive

Target Learners¶

  • Learners of all ages across formal, informal, and workplace settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Big learning goals in STEM and beyond, including opening difficult subjects to more students

Claims¶

  • Collaborative learning appeared in 36 funded projects, embodied learning in 15, and identity theories in 9 [+W]
  • Design-based research was part of 22 funded projects, learning analytics part of 34, and multimodal analytics in roughly a dozen [+W]
