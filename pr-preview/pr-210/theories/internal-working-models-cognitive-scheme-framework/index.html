Attachment internal working models as cognitive schemes linking early relationships to flexibility and adjustment¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article frames attachment through Bowlby's internal working models: early caregiving relationships form mental representations of ego and others that guide later close relationships. It links these models to cognitive schemes via Rothbard and Shaver's four connected elements, which are "autobiographic memory", "beliefs and attitude", "aims and motives" and "behaviour strategies", and uses Bartholomew and Horowitz's four attachment styles (secure, obsessed, afraid, negligent) as the measurement framework in the study.

Claims¶

  • Direction Of Predictor Flexibility Relationships [+M]
  • Hostility and afraid attachment do not meaningfully predict cognitive flexibility, while the other variables do [+W]
  • Irrational beliefs, obsessive attachment and anxiety are the strongest stepwise predictors of cognitive flexibility, together explaining 30% of variance [+W]
  • Attachment styles, irrational beliefs and psychological symptoms jointly explain approximately 41% of the variance in university students' cognitive flexibility [+W]
