Reading Rope (Strand Model of Skilled Reading): word recognition and language comprehension as multifaceted strands¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Scarborough's 2001 Strand Model, or Reading Rope, elaborates the Simple View by treating each component as multifaceted: the word recognition strand encompasses phonological awareness, decoding, and sight recognition, while the language comprehension strand includes background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures, verbal reasoning, and literacy knowledge. The paper organizes Coach's microintervention inventory by these critical elements, stating the microinterventions are "aligned to the reading rope."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction must develop each strand's subskills with practice so word recognition becomes automatic and language comprehension increasingly strategic

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • early and emerging readers in grades pre-K to 5

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • phonological awareness
  • decoding
  • sight recognition
  • vocabulary
  • background knowledge
  • verbal reasoning

Claims¶

  • Word recognition fluency significantly predicted reading comprehension in grades 1-3, with rate becoming the stronger predictor by third grade [+W]
  • L2 oral language proficiency predicts reading comprehension more strongly than decoding skills [+W]
