Framework of the dimensions of scale-up explaining why effect sizes might decrease as scale increases¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article develops a framework of the dimensions of scale-up, an explanatory account of why intervention effect sizes might shrink as interventions move from small studies to broader adoption. The authors state: "we develop a framework of the dimensions of scale-up to explain why effect sizes might decrease as scale increases." They then use this framework to organize a comparison of causal estimates from the present state-level study with those from earlier, smaller studies of Pre-K Mathematics, and report the observed decline as consistent with it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires causal estimates from studies at multiple points along the scale-up sequence (pilot, efficacy, effectiveness, scale-up) so that effect sizes can be compared across scale.

Constraints¶

  • As presented, the framework is used interpretively to explain an observed decline in effect sizes; the available text does not print a test of the framework's dimensions.

Target Learners¶

  • Low-income children of pre-K age in California pre-K programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Early mathematics ability

Claims¶

  • Pre K Math Effect Sizes Decreased Over Scale Up [+M]
