Three-premises framework for viewing ACE within the national VET system¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report imposes order on wide-ranging views about ACE's role by working from three premises that form "the lens or framework through whichwe have written this Report": ACE is a provider; ACE providers are community-based providers distinct from public and private providers; and the ACE sector is a network of community-based ACE providers. The framework's purpose is described as pragmatic and context specific, providing common boundaries around the analysis of ACE's role in the national VET system.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The framework is explicitly pragmatic and context specific to the Australian national VET system of the mid-1990s

Constraints¶

  • The report acknowledges it takes the existing objectives of the national VET system as given, running the risk of presenting the national VET system in its current form as immutable

Target Learners¶

  • adult learners in Australian community-based education settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • vocational education and training outcomes within nationally agreed frameworks

Claims¶

  • ACE providers are increasingly delivering recognised outcomes in the national VET system, with around 180 registered providers and 1.5-2% of national VET course activity [+W]
  • ACE's main VET contribution is General Education and Training (around 20% of provision), with around 10% in non-industry-specific occupational training and little in industry-specific training [+W]
  • ACE providers deliver VET to equity target groups and to rural and remote communities where other VET provision is limited or non-existent [+W]
  • General Adult Education remains the heartland of the ACE sector, forming no less than 70% of ACE provision [+W]
