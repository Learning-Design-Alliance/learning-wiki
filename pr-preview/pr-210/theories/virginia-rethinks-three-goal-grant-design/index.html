Virginia RETHINKS three-goal grant design: student outcomes, workforce connections, and college efficiencies¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Virginia RETHINKS Health Sciences Education initiative organized a $24 million TAACCCT grant across all 23 Virginia community colleges around three broad goals: improving student outcomes, building connections to the workforce, and improving college efficiencies. Strategies included redesigning curriculum in developmental education and health-related courses, in-person counseling and mentoring, and technology platforms for students, faculty, and staff. The report evaluates implementation of each strategy.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A multi-college system-wide grant structure coordinating curriculum redesign, advising, and technology platforms

Constraints¶

  • Implementation was shaped by grant funding, with sustainability of strategies in question after the grant period

Target Learners¶

  • community college students preparing for health care careers, including dislocated and low-skilled workers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • preparation for jobs in health care industries

Claims¶

  • Career coaches strengthened collaboration between colleges and the workforce development system, but sustaining these connections requires additional resources after the grant period [+W]
  • College-specific barriers related to resources, time, and local labor market conditions limited implementation of the statewide E-HLTH career studies certificate [~W]
  • Colleges adopted new technology platforms to increase efficiencies, but face challenges sustaining their use after the grant ends [~W]

Examples¶
