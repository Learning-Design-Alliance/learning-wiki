CHSRI theory of action: small schools improve outcomes via student support relationships and teacher professional community¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

CHSRI posited that small, intimate schools would improve student outcomes through two mechanisms: students who felt personally and academically supported would attend more regularly and learn more, and teachers in collaborative, innovation-conducive environments would build professional communities that spurred instructional improvement. Prior CCSR work found the relational elements were present—students reported better relationships with adults and teachers reported more collegiality, innovation, and trust—but "these surveys did not indicate a consistent pattern of instructional strength," and teachers reported teaching no differently than before.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Small school size intended to enhance meaningful teacher collaboration and stronger student-teacher relationships, per the initiative's theory

Constraints¶

  • The relational and professional-community elements were present but were not strong enough to change instruction or raise test scores, per the report's synthesis of prior survey and interview work

Target Learners¶

  • Low-performing high school students in under-served Chicago neighborhoods

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Attendance and persistence to graduation
  • Academic achievement and college readiness

Claims¶

  • Chsri Act Scores No Different Low [+M]
  • Chsri Higher Four Year Graduation Rates [+M]
  • CHSRI freshmen earned slightly higher core GPAs than similar students, though averages remained below a C [+M]
  • CHSRI freshmen were absent about 10 fewer days per year than similar students at similar non-CHSRI schools, a statistically significant gap in every year studied [+M]
  • The CHSRI graduation advantage was concentrated among students least likely to graduate, exceeding six percentage points in the lowest stratum versus about two in the highest [+M]
  • Outcome differences between CHSRI new-start and redesigned schools were not statistically significant after accounting for incoming student differences [~M]
  • Classrooms in high-performing CHSRI schools showed positive, mutually respectful student-teacher relationships with emotional and academic support [+M]
  • Positive teacher–student relationships are associated with greater student engagement and, more weakly, with achievement [+M]
