Canale and Swain's integrative framework of communicative competence synthesizes grammatical, sociolinguistic, and discourse knowledge¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The packet presents Canale & Swain's (1980) framework as an integrative theory in which communicative competence is a synthesis of grammatical knowledge, knowledge of how language performs communicative functions in social contexts, and knowledge of how utterances and functions combine according to principles of discourse. The framework is depicted in Figure 1 with grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic components, and is described as integrative because it covers speaking, reading, and writing rather than a subset of skill areas.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction adopting the framework requires teachers to develop students' grammatical competence as well as sociolinguistic and strategic competencies

Constraints¶

  • The packet notes the framework focuses on speaking, reading, and writing rather than a subset of these skill areas

Target Learners¶

  • bilingual education teachers in training
  • L1 and L2 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • communicative competence across grammatical, sociolinguistic, and strategic components

Examples¶

  • Teacher training packets operationalize communicative competence theory through workshop activities, pre/posttests, and guided discussion rather than expert knowledge alone
  • Adopting a communicative approach requires teachers to take a dual role facilitating both natural acquisition and learning
