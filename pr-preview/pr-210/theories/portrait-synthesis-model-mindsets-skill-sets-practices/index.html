Portrait of a Graduate Synthesis Model: Mindsets, Skill Sets, and Practices¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A taxonomy derived from the authors' analysis of 69 district Portraits, organizing graduate attributes into three levels: "Mindsets are overarching ways of thinking or acting that support learners to develop and apply Skill Sets" (e.g., Curiosity, Perseverance); Skill Sets are "Broadly framed abilities that are essential for a person's lifelong success" (e.g., Analyze to Understand; Care For and Contribute to Society); and Practices are "Actionable subcategories of Skill Sets that learners can demonstrate across academic and professional contexts" (e.g., Evaluate Appropriate and Accurate Information). The model synthesizes attributes identified by districts nationwide into six Skill Sets with sub-categorized Practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts adopt or adapt the model to their own community-designed Portrait, since each community customizes the attributes

Constraints¶

  • The six Skill Sets are broadly defined and do not directly correlate to the current assessment and credentialing paradigm of standardized tests and course grades

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Cross-disciplinary transferable skills including analysis, collaboration, communication, creation, civic contribution, and self-awareness

Claims¶

  • Six Skill Sets Capture 90 Percent Portrait Attributes [+M]
  • Fewer than one fifth of districts with Portraits have taken the step of identifying actionable Practices within their Skill Sets [+M]
