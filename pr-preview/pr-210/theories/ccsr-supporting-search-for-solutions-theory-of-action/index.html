CCSR theory of action: research builds capacity by supporting the search for solutions¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

CCSR's theory of action holds that the proper role of research in urban school reform is not to hand districts technical answers but to build system capacity by informing an ongoing, interactive search for solutions. Drawing on Lindblom, the report argues that 'The proper aim of applied social science is not to find the one best technical solution to a problem (and then advocate for it) but rather to inform the existing competition of ideas.' The model assumes findings are assimilated slowly, through repeated interaction between researchers and practitioners, and that researchers must shift 'from outside expert to interactive participant in building knowledge of what matters for students' success.'

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Long-term ongoing relationships between researchers and the district rather than one-off studies
  • Mechanisms that let practitioners process and assimilate findings over time, such as stakeholder engagement and accessible reports

Constraints¶

  • The report states research impacts on policy take time and are often indirect, so specific studies can seldom be directly linked to changes in practitioners' work

Target Learners¶

  • District leaders, principals, teachers, and reform organizations in urban school systems

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Building educator and district capacity to use evidence, manage decentralized decision making, and improve schools

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Individualized school reports (ISRs) tracking schools on core research-linked indicators
  • CCSR steering committee as multi-partisan stakeholder advisory structure
  • CCSR longitudinal data archive on Chicago Public Schools
  • No-surprises pre-release briefings with district leadership before research reports are made public
