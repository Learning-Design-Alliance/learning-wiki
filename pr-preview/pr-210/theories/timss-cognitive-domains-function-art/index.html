TIMSS cognitive domains (Knowing, Applying, Reasoning) as an analytical lens for function art evaluation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study repurposes the TIMSS cognitive framework—originally designed for standardized test construction—as an interpretive lens for analyzing student cognition in open-ended, creative function art tasks. Knowing encompasses recall and retrieval of function types and graph features; Applying covers parameter adjustment and implementation of graphing techniques; Reasoning captures integration of multiple representations and evaluation of alternative strategies. The authors acknowledge the framework "has not been formally validated for this context" and recommend further validation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clear operational criteria for classifying artwork into cognitive domains
  • Inter-rater reliability procedures to ensure consistent application

Constraints¶

  • Originally developed for structured, test-based assessments rather than open-ended creative tasks
  • Not formally validated for function art contexts

Target Learners¶

  • Grades 8-12 mathematics students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Mathematical function understanding
  • Transformation and symmetry reasoning
  • Creative mathematical expression

Claims¶

  • Three distinct learner profiles emerge from function art activities: Repetitivists, Simplists, and Multifunctionists [+W]
  • Multifunctionist students use a broader range of function types than Simplist and Repetitivist students [+W]
  • Cluster membership is significantly associated with grade level but not gender [+W]
  • Repetitivist group included significantly more lower-grade students while Multifunctionist group included more upper-grade students [+W]
