Two-category taxonomy of alternative student growth measures: alternative assessment-based value-added models and student learning objectives¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report organizes alternative ways to measure student growth in grades and subjects not covered by state assessments into two broad categories. The first, "alternative assessment–based value-added models," uses statistical methods on end-of-course or commercially available tests. The second, "student learning objectives," are customized goals set by a teacher or teacher team at the start of the year with principal approval, and "do not involve statistical modeling." The taxonomy structures the report's comparison of uses, benefits, and challenges.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State assessments typically cover only grades 3–8 and one high school grade, motivating alternatives for uncovered grades and subjects

Constraints¶

  • Student learning objectives do not involve statistical modeling, distinguishing them from value-added approaches

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in grades and subjects not covered by annual state assessments

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Measuring student achievement growth for teacher evaluation in non-tested grades and subjects
