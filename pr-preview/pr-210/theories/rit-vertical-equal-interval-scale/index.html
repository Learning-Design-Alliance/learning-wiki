RIT vertical equal-interval scale grounded in item response theory¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The RIT scale is the report's central measurement framework: an equal-interval vertical scale spanning grades K–12, derived from item response theory and the Rasch model. The report explains that "The RIT scale has two key characteristics. It is a vertical scale with equal-interval units," and that "the equal-interval characteristic of the RIT scale allows the measurement of growth over two or more time points (i.e., learning) by calculating the difference in scores." IRT places item difficulty and person ability on the same continuous scale, enabling adaptive testing and score comparability across item sets.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Items must be calibrated to the RIT scale, and scoring uses maximum likelihood estimation with fencing for extreme response patterns

Constraints¶

  • The scale spans grades K–12 in Math and Reading but grades 2–12 in Language Usage and Science

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Comparing achievement across grades and terms and measuring growth as score change

Related Theories¶

  • Item Response Theory as a framework for scale development
  • MAP Growth norms taxonomy: ten norms per subject, level, and grade, spanning achievement, within-year growth, and between-year growth
  • Unidimensional Rasch model scaling of MAP scores on the RIT scale

Examples¶

  • MAP Growth interim computer adaptive assessment system
  • NWEA MAP Growth vertically scaled assessment as a common comparison measure
  • Use the Learning Continuum to locate skills by difficulty and guide formative assessment and scaffolding decisions
  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment and longitudinal achievement database
  • MAP Growth Course-Specific assessments with documented psychometric quality evidence
  • MAP Growth: computer-adaptive, vertically scaled interim assessment on the RIT scale
  • Spanish MAP Growth Reading assessment: an adaptive K–8 reading assessment on a RIT scale linked to the English version
