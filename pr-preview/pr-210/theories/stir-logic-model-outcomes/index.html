STIR logic model linking intervention activities to short-, medium-, and long-term outcomes¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The partnership organizes its theory of change as a logic model moving from "Implement STIR intervention" through "Refine" and "Assess usability/feasibility ... across multiple cohorts" to studying long-term outcomes. Short-term outcomes are educator and leader understanding of evidence-based practices and data use; medium-term outcomes are their incorporation of those practices "within the context of a tiered delivery system"; long-term outcomes are increased students scoring at or above benchmarks. A diagnostic and implementation monitoring tool provides formative feedback across stages.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Iterative cohorts for refinement and usability/feasibility assessment
  • A diagnostic and implementation monitoring tool for formative feedback

Constraints¶

  • Long-term outcomes are anticipated, not yet measured in this document

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten and grade 1 students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Early literacy decoding skills
  • Educator capacity for evidence-based literacy instruction

Claims¶

  • The STIR partnership anticipates that schools will report more students at or above early literacy benchmarks relative to prior years [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • LEAD logic model chain from leader and teacher knowledge to student literacy and math outcomes
  • SWELL partnership logic model linking teacher professional learning to English learner literacy outcomes
  • TFT partnership logic model linking administrator, teacher, and student outcomes across three time horizons

Examples¶

  • Use a diagnostic and implementation monitoring tool to give educators formative feedback on their instructional practices
  • Disseminate partnership information through blog posts, videos, and infographics to support acceptability and feasibility
  • Build school leaders' instructional leadership through five PLC sessions on school-wide data systems
  • Train educators through a Summer Institute plus PLC and coaching cycles on data use and instructional routines
