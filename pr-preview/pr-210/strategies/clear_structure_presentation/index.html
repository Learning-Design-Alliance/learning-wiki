Clear Structure Presentation¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 8 studies (4 causal, 3 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 1 of 8 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Clear structure presentation organizes content into an explicit, visible hierarchy before and during instruction: outlines, section headings, advance organizers, signposting language, and typographic signaling cues. The learner is never left to infer how ideas relate; the organization is made perceptible so that working memory is spent on comprehension rather than on reconstructing the author's logic.

Design Implications¶

Signaling — headings, previews, pointer words, and emphasis — reliably improves selective attention and recall by directing learners to the organizing structure of material Emphasis and relevance cues direct learner attention to structurally important content. [+S]. Structuring also reduces extraneous load: when the outline is given, learners need not hold unorganized fragments in working memory while searching for relationships Chunking content into organized units reduces working memory load. [+M]. The strongest implementations pair a global organizer (an outline or advance organizer presented up front) with local signals (headings, transitions, highlighting) so the same structure is reinforced at both scales.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A coherent underlying structure identified before presentation — the designer must know the hierarchy, not just the content
  • An advance organizer or outline presented before the detail (Advance Organizers)
  • Consistent heading levels, section lengths, and typographic conventions throughout the material
  • Signaling cues (bold, callouts, "three reasons…" framing) that map onto the actual structure rather than decorating it

Constraints¶

  • Signaling helps novices but can be redundant or even harmful for learners with high prior knowledge, who process the structure anyway Guidance that helps novices becomes less effective as expertise grows. [~M]
  • Over-signaling — highlighting too much — eliminates the contrast that makes emphasis useful; when everything is marked, nothing is Emphasis and relevance cues direct learner attention to structurally important content. [-M]
  • A visible structure imposed on genuinely non-hierarchical or ill-structured content can mislead learners into forcing false organization onto the material [-W]
  • Structure cues do not substitute for the connections themselves; headings without coherent prose between them produce an outline illusion [-M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Advance organizers — abstract, comparative frameworks presented before the material (Advance Organizers)
  • Signaling in text — headings, previews, summaries, pointer words, typographic emphasis
  • Signaling in multimedia — on-screen headings, voice emphasis, and highlighted key steps in narrated animation Media features such as headings and emphasis affect what learners recall and retain. [+M]
  • Outlining tools — learner-facing document outlines and navigation panes that let learners re-orient at any point (Document Outline Tool)

Target Learners¶

  • Novices, who lack the schemas to infer structure from unorganized content and benefit most from externally supplied organization Chunking content into organized units reduces working memory load. [+M]
  • Learners with limited working memory capacity or reading difficulties, for whom explicit structure reduces navigational and integrative load
  • Less beneficial for advanced learners, who may find detailed signposting redundant Guidance that helps novices becomes less effective as expertise grows. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Recall and retention of organized bodies of factual or conceptual content
  • Building accurate schemas of how a domain's ideas relate hierarchically
  • Efficient navigation and re-orientation in long-form or reference material

Instructions¶

  1. Analyze the content and commit to an explicit hierarchy — what are the top-level ideas, and what is subordinate to what?
  2. Present an advance organizer or outline before the detail (Advance Organizers), framing what learners are about to encounter and how it connects to prior knowledge.
  3. Chunk the content into sections that each map to one node of the hierarchy (Chunking).
  4. Signal the structure locally: consistent headings, preview sentences, transitions, and sparing emphasis on the few structurally critical points (Headings and Highlight Strategy).
  5. Close sections with brief summaries that restate position in the hierarchy, and keep the full outline visible (sidebar, agenda slide) throughout.

Related Strategies¶

  • Chunking — the unit-sizing decision that makes a structure usable in working memory
  • Advance Organizers — the "before" half of structure presentation
  • Headings and Highlight Strategy — the "during" half: local signaling within the material
  • Document Outline Tool — persistent structural navigation for long documents

Examples¶

  • Khan Academy — unit → lesson → exercise hierarchy with persistent progress navigation, so learners always know where they are in the structure.
  • Mayer's multimedia signaling research — narrated animations with on-screen section headings and highlighted key steps produce better transfer than identical animations without signals (Mautone & Mayer, 2001).
  • Textbook chapter openers — the standard outline-plus-learning-objectives opener is a mass implementation of this strategy; its persistence reflects consistent evidence for signaling effects.
