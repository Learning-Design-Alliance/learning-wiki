Pursue SCT-L2 research on L2 as a psychological tool, the teaching-learning dialectic, Dynamic Assessment, and comparative theory work¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Lantolf names four promising research avenues for sociocultural theory in second language development: whether L2 users can mediate their thinking through the L2 as a psychological tool; the teaching-learning dialectic (obuchenie) pursued through Concept-Based Language Instruction; Dynamic Assessment, including group-level and technology-based implementations; and in-depth comparative theoretical discussion across SLD theories. He advises young researchers to "spend time reading Vygotsky" and to join the SCT-L2 Working-Group meetings where junior and senior scholars present work in progress and receive feedback.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Direct engagement with Vygotsky's original writings and participation in scholarly communities such as the SCT-L2 Working-Group

Constraints¶

  • Lantolf notes most journals privilege empirical over theoretical research, limiting comparative theory discussion

Target Learners¶

  • junior and senior second language development researchers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • advancing sociocultural theory research on L2 development, teaching-learning dialectics, and Dynamic Assessment
