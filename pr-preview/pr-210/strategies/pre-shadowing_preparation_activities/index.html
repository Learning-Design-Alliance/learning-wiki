Pre-Shadowing Preparation Activities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 8 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 3 causal, 1 review), q2–q4 · 3 of 8 report an effect size

Description¶

Pre-shadowing preparation is a set of activities completed before an educator shadows a student for a day. It typically includes: (1) writing focused learning goals for the shadow day; (2) selecting a student whose experience can illuminate those goals; (3) coordinating consent and logistics with the student, family, and staff; and (4) explicitly listing one's assumptions so the day can be approached with "beginner's eyes." Preparation converts an unstructured visit into a purposeful observation.

Design Implications¶

Preparation works because it directs limited attention: a full school day contains far more sensory and social information than an observer can process, so advance goals function as an advance organizer that filters what gets noticed Advance organizers improve learning. [+M]. Surfacing assumptions before observation reduces confirmation bias — observers who have articulated a prior belief are better positioned to notice disconfirming evidence rather than silently filtering it Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Approximately one hour of preparation time
  • Templates or worksheets for goal setting and assumption listing
  • Consent and coordination with the student, parents/guardians, and staff
  • A debrief plan for after the shadow day (Individual Reflection or structured Check-In)

Constraints¶

  • Coordination can fail or stall; an unwilling student or family undermines the experience [-W]
  • Overly narrow goals can tunnel vision the observer, causing them to miss unanticipated but important phenomena [~M]
  • Assumption-questioning exercises done superficially (listing assumptions without genuinely holding them loosely) can paradoxically strengthen confirmation bias [~W]
  • Ethical risk: shadowing a minor requires genuine informed consent, not just administrative clearance [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Goal focus varies: engagement, college and career readiness, experiences of English Learners, transitions between classes, or social dynamics
  • Some programs (e.g., Shadow a Student Challenge) pair the educator with a random student; others use deliberate selection to target a population of concern
  • Preparation can be individual or done in a cohort with shared goal-setting and post-shadow Class Discussion

Target Learners¶

  • Educators and school leaders participating in student shadowing to gain insight into student experience
  • Especially valuable for administrators who have been out of the classroom long enough that their mental model of a student's day is outdated Activation improves learning. [+M]
  • Teachers preparing to shadow in a different grade level, department, or school context

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Empathic understanding of the student experience (Building Empathy)
  • Perspective-taking and bias awareness
  • Generating grounded, actionable insights for school improvement

Instructions¶

  1. Set learning goals. Write 1–3 focused questions or goals for the day (e.g., "How does an EL student experience unstructured time?") — State Objectives
  2. Select a student. Choose a student whose schedule, grade level, and experiences can answer those goals; consider students whose perspective differs from your default assumptions
  3. Coordinate. Obtain student assent and parent consent; brief the student's teachers and relevant staff so the day runs normally
  4. Question assumptions. List what you currently believe about this student's experience and commit to testing each belief rather than confirming it — Cognitive Conflict
  5. Plan the debrief. Schedule immediate post-shadow Individual Reflection while observations are fresh

Related Strategies¶

  • Empathy Walks — the broader family of structured observation experiences this prepares for
  • Activating Prior Knowledge — the assumption-listing step is a deliberate activation-and-suspension of prior knowledge

Related Elements¶

  • State Objectives — the goal-setting step
  • Individual Reflection — the post-shadow counterpart that completes the cycle
  • Cognitive Conflict — assumption questioning creates the disequilibrium that observation can then resolve
  • Check-In — coordination touchpoints with the shadowed student

Examples¶

  • Shadow a Student Challenge (School Retool) — a national campaign in which school leaders shadow a student for a full day; its preparation materials include goal-setting prompts and assumption exercises
  • Empathy interviews and shadowing in design-thinking school redesign (Stanford d.school K12 lab work) — preparation includes framing "How might we…" questions before observation
