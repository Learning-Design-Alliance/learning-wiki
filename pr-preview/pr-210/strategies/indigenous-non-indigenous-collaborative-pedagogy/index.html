Indigenous–non-Indigenous academic collaboration grounded in trust, collegiality and un-learning of Western epistemic authority¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The paper presents collaboration between Indigenous and non-Indigenous academics as effective pedagogy in practice for reinstituting Indigenous knowledges. It holds that effective interchange requires "a fundamental re-thinking of the w ays in which Western knowledge is institutionally validated and hierarchised", that for non-Indigenous academics "un-learning is crucial", and that collaboration demands "trust and collegiality and respect for both Indigenous and non-Indigenous ways of knowing" — without wholesale deference to all Indigenous viewpoints.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Trust, collegiality and mutual respect between collaborators; willingness of non-Indigenous academics to un-learning Western epistemic assumptions

Constraints¶

  • Nakata is cited warning of the dangers of appropriation and distortion of Indigenous knowledge by non-Indigenous academics, balanced with the view that non-Indigenous people need to be part of the dialogue

Target Learners¶

  • University academics and students in Indigenous Studies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Incorporating Indigenous standpoints and complementary knowledge sets into teaching and research practice

Affordances¶

  • Foucauldian Episteme Indigenous Knowledges Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Cultivation of Multiple Ways of Knowing and Making Meaning
