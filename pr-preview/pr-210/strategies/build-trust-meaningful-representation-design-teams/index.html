Form design teams by building trust and relationships and prioritizing meaningful representation before project kick-off.¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

This strategy governs the first activity phase, forming design teams. It has two parts: build trust and relationships, by establishing shared understanding and goals around equity and meeting community members before project kick-off; and prioritize meaningful representation, by providing "a wide range of stakeholders: students, alumni, teachers, administrators, parents, community partners", resisting rushing the process, and positioning youth and adults as equally valuable design partners.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Establish shared understanding and goals around equity before the project begins
  • Meet community members before project kick-off
  • Provide a wide range of stakeholders and resist rushing the process

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • high school students, alumni, teachers, administrators, parents, and community partners in equity-focused high school communities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • designing alumni surveys that serve each school community's own evaluation purposes

Related Strategies¶

  • Anticipate barriers to data use by building in strategies for future data use from initial conversations, and trust the process by going at the community's pace.
  • In co-design meetings, position community members as experts and create space for challenging ideas, including adapting survey questions to community feedback.

  • Conduct research in partnership with educators on equity problems educators prioritize
  • Multi-stakeholder analytics partnership using improvement science with strict data governance
  • Build personal connections and find common ground in research-practice partnerships
