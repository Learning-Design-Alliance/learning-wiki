Least-to-Most Prompting Hierarchy¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

A prompt is a hint or supplementary instructional stimulus that helps a learner make a correct response. Behavioral-science practice identifies three categories of prompt, ordered from least to most intrusive:

  • Verbal prompts — cues, reminders, or instructions delivered verbally (e.g., "Leave a space between words"; "First adjust the object lens").
  • Gestural prompts — a demonstration or gesture that models the step for the learner (e.g., pointing to a microscope's fine adjustment knob and making a turning gesture).
  • Physical prompts — hand-over-hand guidance for learners who lack the fine motor control to follow a verbal or gestural prompt alone (e.g., guiding a child's hand while forming a letter).

Least-to-most prompting is the practice of starting with the least intrusive prompt that might work (verbal) and escalating to gestural or physical only if needed, rather than defaulting to a more intrusive prompt. The rationale is fading: verbal prompts are far easier to withdraw than physical ones, so a learner who becomes dependent on physical prompts will find it harder to demonstrate the skill independently of the teacher.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A clearly defined target skill or response, so the teacher can judge whether a given prompt level is sufficient
  • Willingness to attempt the least intrusive prompt first, even when a more intrusive one might feel faster in the moment

Constraints¶

  • Starting with (or over-relying on) physical prompts risks creating prompt-dependence that is difficult to fade later
  • Prompts are a support for acquiring stimulus control over a correct response, not a permanent substitute for independent performance — the whole rationale for ordering prompts from least to most intrusive is to make eventual fading easier

Target Learners¶

  • Learners acquiring a new procedural or motor skill who are prone to errors without support
  • Learners with fine-motor-control limitations that make gestural or verbal prompting alone insufficient, for whom physical prompting may be necessary at first

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Errorless or near-errorless initial skill acquisition, followed by independent performance once prompts are faded

Instructions¶

  1. Identify the target response and anticipate where the learner is likely to need support.
  2. Attempt a verbal prompt first (a cue, reminder, or instruction).
  3. If the verbal prompt is insufficient, escalate to a gestural prompt (demonstration or gesture).
  4. If the gestural prompt is insufficient, escalate to a physical prompt (hand-over-hand guidance).
  5. As the learner succeeds at a given level, drop back to a less intrusive prompt level on subsequent attempts, working toward no prompt at all.

Related Strategies¶

  • Behavioral Feedback for Correct and Incorrect Responses — prompts precede the learner's response; feedback follows it

Examples¶

  • A teacher first says "Don't forget the fine adjustment!" while a student uses a microscope (verbal), only pointing and gesturing at the knob (gestural) if the verbal reminder alone doesn't produce the correct response, and only physically guiding the student's hand (physical) as a last resort.
