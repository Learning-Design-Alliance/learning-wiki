Good Tutee-Tutor Contact¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 6 studies (4 review, 2 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 2 of 6 report an effect size

Description¶

Good tutee-tutor contact means frequent, scheduled, and personally engaged interaction between tutor and tutee — knowing the tutee by name, asking about their well-being, and treating them as an individual with a life beyond the session. Contact is not merely logistical; it builds the relational foundation on which motivation, honest disclosure of confusion, and persistence through difficulty depend. Chickering and Gamson (1987) identify frequent student-faculty contact as the first of their seven principles of good practice, citing its motivational power.

Design Implications¶

Contact quality predicts both motivation and achievement: learner-centered teacher-student relationships show moderate positive associations with participation, motivation, and achievement across a large meta-analysis Learner-centered relationships improve participation, motivation, and achievement. [+S]. Perceived approachability and support from instructors predict tutees' intrinsic motivation and perceived choice Teacher support predicts intrinsic motivation. [+M]. In tutoring contexts, contact serves a second function beyond rapport: tutees who feel known are more willing to expose confusion, which makes Coaching and Feedback cycles more diagnostic and effective.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Regularly scheduled sessions with continuity — the same tutor and tutee pair over time, not rotating staff
  • Tutor willingness and skill to engage personally: learning names, asking about well-being, remembering prior conversations
  • Institutional structures that make contact feasible: session length, frequency, and low tutor caseloads

Constraints¶

  • High tutor-to-tutee ratios make individualized contact impractical; attempts at personal connection under heavy caseloads become superficial and can feel performative [-M]
  • Contact without pedagogical substance — warmth without challenge or structure — does not improve learning outcomes and may consume session time [~M]
  • Tutors without interpersonal training can misjudge boundaries; over-familiarity or inconsistent availability damages trust more than neutral professionalism [-W]
  • Benefits attenuate when contact is tutor-initiated only; tutees who never take ownership of the relationship remain dependent [~W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • In-person vs. mediated: video or chat-based tutoring can sustain contact, but purely asynchronous text exchange weakens the relational cues that carry rapport [-W]
  • Group tutoring: contact shifts to knowing each member individually within a small group, trading depth for reach [~M]
  • Peer tutoring programs (e.g., Supplemental Instruction) build contact through shared status, which lowers the disclosure barrier for struggling students [+W]

Target Learners¶

  • Tutees in higher education and adult learning programs, where voluntary attendance makes motivation the binding constraint Task value increases motivation and engagement. [+M]
  • At-risk or first-generation students, for whom a single trusted academic relationship predicts persistence Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence. [+M]
  • Students with low prior confidence, who avoid help-seeking unless the tutor is demonstrably approachable [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Affective and motivational goals: sustained engagement, help-seeking, persistence through difficulty
  • Metacognitive goals: honest self-assessment of understanding, which requires a relationship safe enough for disclosure
  • Indirectly supports all cognitive goals by increasing session attendance and participation

Instructions¶

  1. Open every session with a brief personal check-in — name use, follow-up on something the tutee mentioned previously (see Check-Ins).
  2. Establish session continuity: agree on goals and review them at the start of each meeting, so contact accumulates around visible progress.
  3. Model engagement through Active Listening — paraphrase the tutee's confusion before correcting it, so disclosure is rewarded rather than penalized.
  4. Use Coaching during the session: guide rather than tell, adjusting support as the tutee's competence grows.
  5. Close with specific, actionable feedback and an explicit invitation to return, ending on a note of progress Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S].

Related Strategies¶

  • Active Listening — the conversational skill that converts contact time into trust
  • Achievable Micro-Goals — gives each contact a visible success, reinforcing the relationship with progress
  • Action Planning — structures what tutees commit to between contacts

Related Elements¶

  • Coaching — the instructional mode that depends on the rapport contact establishes
  • Collaboration — reframes tutoring from delivery to joint work, which contact enables
  • Class Discussion — group settings where tutor contact must extend to each participant

Patterns That Use This Strategy¶

  • Cognitive Apprenticeship — the coaching and scaffolding phases presuppose a sustained learner-expert relationship
  • Collaborative Learning — contact norms generalize to peer relationships within groups
  • Formative Assessment — honest tutee disclosure, built through contact, is the raw material of formative feedback

Examples¶

  • Supplemental Instruction (University of Missouri–Kansas City) — https://learningcenter.umkc.edu/supplemental-instruction/ — peer leaders attend the same lectures as participants and hold regular collaborative sessions; the shared-course relationship sustains contact and normalizes help-seeking.
  • Writing center tutoring (e.g., Purdue OWL's on-campus Writing Lab) — https://owl.purdue.edu — scheduled one-to-one conferences where tutors open with conversation about the writer's goals before addressing the text, treating the writer as the primary agent.
  • A mathematics tutor opens each session by asking how the tutee's week went and references a struggle from the previous session ("Last time fractions tripped us up — let's see if today's practice changed that"), signaling that the relationship persists between meetings.
