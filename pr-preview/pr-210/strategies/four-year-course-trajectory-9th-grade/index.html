Develop a four-year course trajectory with each 9th grader leading to a college-ready curriculum¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Recommendation 1 advises high schools to offer a college-ready curriculum as the default, communicate course requirements to students and families before high school, and plan coursework with each 9th grader. The panel recommends that "at a minimum, all students should pass Algebra I by the end of their 9th-grade year," and that counselors build individualized plans that are living documents referred to by teachers, counselors, and provided to parents.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • High schools must offer the relevant courses as a default and ensure students understand what constitutes a college-ready curriculum by 9th grade

Constraints¶

  • Low level of evidence: none of the studies examining the impact of offering a college-ready curriculum met WWC standards

Target Learners¶

  • High school students, particularly low-income and first-generation students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic preparation for college-level work without remediation

Related Strategies¶

  • Use assessment measures throughout high school to inform students of college readiness and assist them in overcoming deficiencies
  • Provide hands-on assistance with college entry steps (entrance exams, searches, visits, applications) and financial aid, beginning before 12th grade
  • Surround students with college-educated mentors and college-going peers to build college aspirations
