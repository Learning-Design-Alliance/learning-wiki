Use school admissions lotteries as randomized experiments to estimate long-term civic outcomes¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's design strategy is to exploit oversubscribed school admissions lotteries as a source of randomization: it matches "records from Democracy Prep's admissions lotteries to a national database of registration and voting" and uses "the admissions lotteries to conduct a randomized experimental analysis" of adult civic participation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A school whose admissions are determined by lottery, and access to an external database of the long-term outcomes of interest (here, registration and voting records)

Constraints¶

  • Applies to cohorts old enough for the outcome to be observed; the analysis here covers students from the network's early years who could vote in the 2016 election

Target Learners¶

  • Students admitted or denied via school admissions lotteries

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring effects of schooling on adult civic participation outcomes

Related Strategies¶

  • Document the lotteries schools of choice conduct and their waiting-list admission procedures
  • Use school lotteries as a research design for estimating impacts of schools of choice on student outcomes
