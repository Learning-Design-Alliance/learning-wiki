Prewriting Activities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 8 studies (4 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 8 report an effect size

Description¶

Prewriting activities are structured tasks completed before drafting — brainstorming, listing, word webs, graphic organizers, outlining, freewriting, and vocabulary activation — that help writers generate, organize, and connect ideas. They reduce the planning burden during composition by externalizing content and structure in advance. Prewriting can be done individually or collaboratively, and is a core component of process approaches to writing instruction.

Design Implications¶

Prewriting reduces cognitive load during drafting by offloading idea generation and organization into a separate phase, freeing working memory for transcription and sentence construction Chunking reduces working memory load. [+M]. Meta-analytic evidence identifies prewriting/planning instruction among the most effective writing interventions, with particularly strong effects for weaker writers Graham & Perin's meta-analysis of adolescent writing instruction. [+S]. Experimental work shows that outline strategies improve text quality compared with drafting directly, especially when planning reduces attentional overload Kellogg's outline-strategy experiments. [+S]. Activating topic knowledge and vocabulary before writing also improves the content of compositions about that topic Activation improves learning. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A clear writing task and audience so generated ideas have a purpose
  • One or more concrete planning tools: graphic organizers, word webs, outlines, personal glossaries
  • Time allocated as a distinct phase — prewriting compressed into minutes before drafting tends to be skipped or done superficially
  • Modeling of the planning process by the instructor (Think-Aloud) so students see how experts generate and select ideas

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness depends on genuine engagement; filling in an organizer mechanically without elaboration yields little benefit [-M]
  • Overly rigid organizers can constrain idea generation for open-ended or creative tasks [~M]
  • Novice writers often do not spontaneously transfer planning strategies to new tasks without explicit instruction and continued support [-M]
  • Time-intensive; may crowd out drafting and revision time if over-scheduled [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Individual (freewriting, listing, outlining) vs. collaborative (group brainstorming, shared concept maps — Collaborative Learning)
  • Structured (graphic organizers, formal outlines) vs. unstructured (freewriting, incubation)
  • Oral prewriting (discussion, Class Discussion) as a precursor for writers who struggle to generate text
  • Digital tools (concept-mapping software, collaborative documents) supporting revision of plans

Target Learners¶

  • Low-achieving writers, who benefit most — prewriting compensates for weak prior knowledge and vocabulary by building content before drafting [+S]
  • Novice writers still managing transcription demands, for whom planning offloads working memory [+M]
  • All writers benefit, but skilled writers may plan mentally and find formal organizers redundant [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Idea generation and content development for compositions
  • Text organization and rhetorical planning (audience, purpose, structure)
  • Vocabulary development and activation of background knowledge
  • Self-regulated writing: planning as the first phase of a recursive writing process

Instructions¶

  1. Activate prior knowledge and vocabulary on the topic through discussion, reading, or Activation activities.
  2. Model idea generation with a Think-Aloud: generate candidates, evaluate them against purpose and audience, and select.
  3. Have students externalize ideas using a chosen tool — word web, list, Concept Map, or outline — individually or in pairs.
  4. Organize ideas into a plan: sequence, group, and connect them to the intended structure.
  5. Draft from the plan, then return to it during revision; treat the plan as revisable, not a contract.

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — builds the content base that prewriting organizes
  • Collaborative Writing — prewriting is often the most productive phase for group work Collaborative writing improves text quality. [+M]
  • Graphic Organizers — the most common prewriting tool format

Examples¶

  • Writing Next recommendations — Graham & Perin (2007) identify prewriting among eleven elements of effective adolescent writing instruction, with an effect size of d ≈ 0.32 when taught explicitly and paired with modeling.
  • Kellogg's outlining studies — college writers instructed to produce outlines before drafting produced higher-quality essays than those drafting directly, because outlining reduced attentional overload during composition.
  • Writer's Workshop (elementary) — a published process-writing model in which prewriting (brainstorming, sketching, discussion) is a distinct, teacher-modeled phase before drafting and conferencing.
  • KWL charts — a common prereading/prewriting organizer (Know–Want–Learn) that activates topic knowledge and vocabulary before students write about a text.
