Panel protocol: open with community cultural resources, then moderated discussion connecting community assets to computing education¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article describes a panel structure in which "participants will first explore Appalachian culture and perspectives through resources such as the The Appalachian Retelling Project," followed by an introduction of context and panelists, a moderated discussion at the intersection of community cultural wealth and K-12 CS/CT education, and finally audience participation inviting attendees to share how the questions relate to their work and generate new ideas on behalf of the Appalachian CS/CT community and beyond.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to authentic community cultural resources such as The Appalachian Retelling Project, and panelists representing administrators, teachers, parents, and community organizations

Constraints¶

  • Designed for a conference panel format in the Kentucky Appalachia context

Target Learners¶

  • Educators and community stakeholders in rural computing education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding culturally responsive approaches to CS/CT education

Affordances¶

  • Community Cultural Wealth Framework Yosso

Related Strategies¶

  • Use named community strengths — kinship, hopes and dreams for the future, grit and resistance to injustice — as scaffolds in K-8 CS/CT instruction
  • Connect computer science learning to Appalachian ingenuity and community identity to move it from novelty to change
  • Connect computational thinking to local maker and problem-solver heritage to teach it responsively to local culture
  • Create Culture Kits
  • Cultural Responsive Practice
