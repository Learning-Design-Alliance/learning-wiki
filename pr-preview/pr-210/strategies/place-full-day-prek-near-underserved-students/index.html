Districts expanding pre-k should prioritize placing full-day pre-k classrooms close to where historically underserved students live¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's authors recommend that when district leaders expand pre-k and decide where to locate classrooms, they "have an opportunity to prioritize policies and funding that provide more full-day pre-k classrooms close to where historically underserved students live." This follows from the finding that intentional placement of full-day, school-based pre-k classrooms in neighborhoods that typically lack them made a difference for student outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts must have authority over the number and geographic allocation of full-day, school-based pre-k classrooms, as Chicago did when it increased classrooms and reallocated them citywide beginning in 2013.

Constraints¶

  • The Chicago study does not compare outcomes between students who attended pre-k and those who did not, so the recommendation rests on policy-variation relationships rather than an attendance contrast.

Target Learners¶

  • three- and four-year-old children entering pre-k, particularly high-priority groups such as Black students and students in the lowest-income group

Target Learning Goals¶

  • kindergarten entry skills
  • second-grade math and reading achievement
  • academic grades
