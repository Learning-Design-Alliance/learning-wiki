Fishbowl¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 9 studies (3 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 theoretical, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 1 of 9 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Two learners sit facing each other in the center of the room; the remaining learners sit in a circle around them. The two central learners have a conversation based on a pre-determined topic, often deliberately practicing specific discussion skills the class is working on (asking follow-up questions, paraphrasing, elaborating on another person's point). Learners on the outside observe, take notes, or perform another discussion-related task assigned by the teacher. Variations include relay-style swapping between inner and outer circles, outer-circle "coaching" from the sidelines, and outer-circle scoring of the conversation against a rubric followed by a structured debrief.

Design Implications¶

The fishbowl makes discussion itself the object of study: the inner circle models conversational moves while the outer circle analyzes them, converting tacit participation norms into observable, discussable behavior Modeling expert performance supports observational learning. [+M]. Structured observation with a specific analytic task (rubric, tally of moves, note-taking guide) outperforms unstructured watching, because it directs attention and prompts self-explanation of what made a move effective Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+S]. The debrief is where much of the learning consolidates — outer-circle observations become explicit criteria learners can apply to their own future participation Self-monitoring improves self-regulation. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A physical space where learners can form a circle around a central area
  • A pre-determined topic and clearly named target discussion skills
  • Observation guides or rubrics so outer-circle learners have an analytic task, not passive spectating
  • A structured debrief in which observers give specific, respectful feedback (Coaching)

Constraints¶

  • Can be intimidating for shy or reluctant speakers placed in the inner circle; participation structures that allow entry through the outer circle first reduce this threat [-M]
  • Requires careful facilitation to prevent dominance by a few learners and to keep feedback respectful [-M]
  • Time-consuming relative to whole-class discussion; the observation and debrief cycle can consume an entire session for a conversation involving only a few speakers [-W]
  • Observers without a concrete analytic task learn little from watching alone Example-based sequences outperform problem-only practice for novices, and fading support is argued to aid transfer [-S]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Relay fishbowl: outer-circle learners periodically swap into the inner circle, keeping the conversation going while broadening participation
  • Coaching variation: outer-circle members whisper or pass coaching prompts to inner speakers mid-discussion
  • Rubric variation: observers score the conversation against a discussion-quality rubric, then report out in the debrief
  • Content fishbowl vs. skill fishbowl: the inner conversation can target subject-matter reasoning (e.g., interpreting a text) or explicit communication skills (paraphrasing, questioning)

Target Learners¶

  • Learners of all ages and subject areas, particularly those developing discussion and communication skills
  • Classes working on group dynamics, where observing peers makes participation norms visible Modeling expert performance supports observational learning. [+M]
  • Less suitable, without adaptation, for learners with high social anxiety about speaking in front of a group [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Communication skills: questioning, paraphrasing, building on others' points Accountable talk moves improve the quality of peer dialogue. [+M]
  • Active listening and observation: attending to others' reasoning rather than waiting to speak
  • Metacognitive awareness: recognizing and naming effective conversational moves Self-monitoring improves self-regulation. [+M]
  • Peer feedback and coaching: giving specific, criterion-referenced feedback

Instructions¶

  1. Select a topic and name the specific discussion skills being practiced; prepare observation guides or a rubric.
  2. Arrange seating: two (or three to five) learners in the inner circle, the rest surrounding them.
  3. Brief the outer circle on their analytic task — tallying moves, noting paraphrases, or scoring against the rubric (Individual Reflection).
  4. Run the inner-circle conversation while the teacher facilitates minimally, intervening only to protect norms (Modeling).
  5. Optionally swap or coach mid-discussion to broaden participation (Peer Discussion).
  6. Debrief: outer-circle observers give specific feedback; the class names effective moves for future discussions (Coaching).

Related Strategies¶

  • Socratic seminar — a related structured discussion format; the fishbowl adds an explicit observation layer
  • Think-pair-share — lower-stakes precursor that builds the same conversational skills before public performance
  • Peer feedback protocols — the debrief phase enacts structured peer critique

Related Elements¶

  • Peer Discussion — the inner-circle conversation is the core activity
  • Modeling — inner speakers model discussion skills for observers
  • Coaching — outer-circle feedback and sideline prompts enact coaching
  • Individual Reflection — observation notes and debrief reflection consolidate learning

Patterns That Use This Strategy¶

  • Cognitive Apprenticeship — the fishbowl serves as the modeling phase for discussion skills, with outer-circle coaching as the coaching phase
  • Discussion-Based Learning — the fishbowl is a structured variant that makes discussion quality observable

Examples¶

  • EL Education (formerly Expeditionary Learning) protocols — the fishbowl is a named protocol in EL Education's structure of protocols for student-led discussion, used across K-12 expeditionary curricula. https://eleducation.org
  • Face to Face Advisement / discussion-skills curricula — middle-school advisory programs use fishbowls to teach listening and paraphrasing skills, with observers tracking how often speakers build on prior points.
  • Teacher education methods courses — pre-service teachers observe a fishbowl of peers leading a discussion, then debrief facilitation moves, making the strategy itself a professional-development tool.
