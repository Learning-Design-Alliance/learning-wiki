Explore differentiated preservice preparation, contextualized in-service learning, and incentives to retain principals in lower-retention environments¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends that state and local education leaders consider retention strategies for the high-turnover environments the study identified. It suggests that "preservice preparation could be differentiated based on the administrators' upcoming placements, in-service professional learning opportunities might be better contextualized, or incentives could be offered to encourage effective leaders to remain in certain (traditionally lower-retention) environments." Leaders are advised to check whether such strategies exist where turnover is highest.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State and local education leaders able to monitor school outcomes and direct resources to high-turnover contexts

Constraints¶

  • The study did not examine causes of retention and mobility, so strategy selection would benefit from in-depth exploration of the environments where turnover is highest

Target Learners¶

  • School principals in Arizona, Nevada, and Utah traditional public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Principal retention and leadership continuity in high-poverty, minority-enrollment, and lower-performing schools
