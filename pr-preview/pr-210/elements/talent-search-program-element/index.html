Talent Search program: federal TRIO services supporting high-school completion and college access for low-income, first-generation students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Talent Search is a federally funded TRIO program (under the Higher Education Act of 1965) that "aims to help low-income and first-generation college students ... complete high school and gain access to college" through academic support and college-access services. Services include test-taking and study-skills assistance, academic advising, tutoring, career development, college campus visits, and financial aid application assistance. It serves both middle and high school students, about 380,000 students a year through more than 400 projects, at an average federal cost of $376 per participant in fiscal year 2005.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Projects must primarily serve students who are low income and whose parents did not complete a four-year college degree

Constraints¶

  • According to a 2000 survey of project directors, nearly half of Talent Search participants received 10 or fewer hours of services a year

Target Learners¶

  • low-income middle and high school students
  • first-generation college students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • high school completion
  • college enrollment and financial aid access

Claims¶

  • Across the two qualifying studies, Talent Search shows an average improvement index of +17 percentile points on completing school [+W]
  • Talent Search participants in Florida completed high school or GED at a significantly higher rate than matched comparison students (84% vs. 70%) [+W]
  • Program dosage was low for many participants: nearly half of Talent Search participants received 10 or fewer hours of services a year [~W]
  • The WWC rated Talent Search as having potentially positive effects on completing school, capped below positive because both studies were quasi-experimental [+W]
  • Talent Search participants in Texas completed high school or GED at a significantly higher rate than matched comparison students (86% vs. 77%) [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Bottom Line college advising program (Access and Success components)
  • Completing-school outcome measure: diploma or GED receipt within five years of entering ninth grade, from statewide databases
