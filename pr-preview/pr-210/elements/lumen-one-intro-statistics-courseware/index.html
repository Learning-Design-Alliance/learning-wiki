Lumen One Introduction to Statistics courseware¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 12 claims (10 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Lumen One Introduction to Statistics is an online courseware product designed to replace traditional textbooks in gateway statistics courses. Content is "provided online as short segments of text with extensive graphics and video interspersed with interactive exercises." It includes student-facing components (Readiness Checks, Study Plans, Learn It text, Apply It activities, Fresh Take videos) and instructor-facing supports (a Faculty Engagement Center, automated messages, time-sensitive intervention flags, question banks). The courseware embeds diverse identities and real-world statistics applications, and automatically executes 13 of 27 surveyed EBT practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor assignment of core components (Study Plans, Self-Check quizzes) for most or all modules
  • At least 20% of student grade coming from Lumen One for full fidelity

Constraints¶

  • Fresh Take videos had lower assignment rates (53% of instructors); intended as optional supplementary resources

Target Learners¶

  • College students in introductory statistics, including those from historically marginalized groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Introductory statistics conceptual understanding and skills

Affordances¶

  • Courseware Initiative Theory Of Change

Claims¶

  • The number of evidence-based teaching practices enacted in courseware-using statistics classes is strongly associated with class-level affective engagement (r = 0.61). [+W]
  • EBT practice enactment is positively associated with residualized course grades (r = 0.316). [+W]
  • EBT practice enactment is positively associated with residualized student learning on the BASIC assessment (r = 0.315). [+W]
  • Meeting four or more implementation fidelity criteria is associated with course grades about 0.24 points higher than expected, a 0.58 grade-point swing versus meeting only two criteria. [+W]
  • Instructors in their second or subsequent term using Lumen One made greater use of six of seven instructor-facing courseware components than first-term users. [+W]
  • Three fidelity components—completing Apply It problems, following up on Time-Sensitive Interventions, and having Automated Messages on—differentiate classes with better-vs-poorer-than-expected grades. [+W]
  • Digital Promise and Achieving the Dream interviewed undergraduate statistics students about their Lumen One courseware experience [+W]
  • Course modality moderated student experience: instructor responsiveness was more prevalent in face-to-face classes and online students reported more frustration [~W]
  • About half of students reported instructors helped bridge courseware guidance and their understanding through outreach, availability, and responsiveness [+W]
  • Dedicated sense-of-belonging courseware features were underused: most students were unaware of the Lumen Community and vaguely remembered the Introduce Yourself module [-W]
  • Undergraduate statistics students most frequently used and valued the courseware's ungraded formative practice features, the Readiness Check and Self Check [+W]
  • Real-world and racially diverse examples in courseware content helped students retain, comprehend, and relate to statistics [+W]
