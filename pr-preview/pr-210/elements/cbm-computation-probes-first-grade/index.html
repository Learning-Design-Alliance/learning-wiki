CBM Computation progress-monitoring probes for first-grade math¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Curriculum-based measurement (CBM) Computation probes are brief standardized math tests used throughout the RTI process. Each probe consists of "25 math computation problems representing the year-long, first-grade math computation curriculum," with problem types constant across forms and facts and positions randomized. Students complete the probe in 2 minutes and are scored by number of correct digits, which the authors say is "a more sensitive index of student change" than whole-problem scoring.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Alternate test forms assessing end-of-year performance, administered weekly or bi-weekly, with digit-correct scoring following right-to-left scoring of addition and subtraction answers

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • First-grade students in general education and intervention settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Math computation fluency
  • Monitoring responsiveness to math intervention

Claims¶

  • CBM slope cutoffs of .25 (primary) and .50 (secondary) digits per week define adequate first-grade responsiveness [+W]
  • RTI identifies learning disabilities via dual discrepancy in level and rate of performance [+W]
  • Student self-graphing of CBM scores is reported as motivating during secondary prevention [+W]
  • Three options exist for setting IEP goals: end-of-year benchmarks, intra-individual framework, and national norms [+W]

Examples¶

  • Apply goal-line decision rules to CBM data to adjust IEP goals or revise instruction
  • Use CRA-based small-group math tutoring with scripts, manipulatives, and computer fact practice as secondary prevention
  • Timed Practice With Progress Monitoring
  • Response To Intervention
  • Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)
