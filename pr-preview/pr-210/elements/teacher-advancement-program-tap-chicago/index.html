Teacher Advancement Program (TAP): performance pay, professional development, and advancement opportunities tied to student achievement growth¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) is a school-level teacher incentive model implemented in Chicago Public Schools as a Teacher Incentive Fund grantee. As the report describes it, "The TAP model offers teachers performance pay, professional development, and advancement opportunities based in part on their students' achievement growth." The program bundles financial incentives with career-advancement structures and embedded professional development, and its incentive component is tied to measured student achievement growth rather than to status measures alone.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implementation within a school district context, here Chicago Public Schools, under a Teacher Incentive Fund grant

Constraints¶

  • Incentives are based only "in part" on students' achievement growth, not solely on growth measures

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student achievement growth in participating TAP schools

Claims¶

  • Chicago TAP did not fully implement its performance-based pay or value-added components as intended [~M]
  • Chicago TAP increased the amount of mentoring, promotion opportunities, and compensation in participating schools relative to non-TAP schools [+M]
  • Chicago TAP did not raise student math or reading scores overall during the four-year rollout in Chicago Public Schools [-M]
  • Teachers in TAP schools reported significantly more mentoring and support than peers in similar non-TAP schools [+W]
  • TAP produced no measurable impacts on student test scores through March of the start-up year [-W]
