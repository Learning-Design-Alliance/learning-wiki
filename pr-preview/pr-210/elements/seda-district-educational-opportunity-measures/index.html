SEDA measures of district educational opportunity¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

SEDA is described by the brief as a data resource providing "a unique measure of educational opportunity across the United States". It produces both achievement scores and growth estimates at the district level, and this brief reports validation of those measures against a common assessment. The validation research supports the achievement scores while revealing differences in the growth estimates.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Validation against a common assessment is used to check the measures, per the brief's title

Constraints¶

  • The brief notes differences in growth estimates, indicating the growth measures are less settled than the achievement scores

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students across United States school districts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • District-level educational opportunity and achievement growth measurement

Claims¶

  • Validation research via a common assessment supports SEDA achievement scores [+W]
  • SEDA growth estimates show differences from common-assessment-based estimates [~W]
  • SEDA and MAP Growth district growth estimates in mathematics correlate at .90 (precision-adjusted) [+W]
  • SEDA and MAP Growth district growth estimates in ELA correlate at .82 (precision-adjusted) [+W]
  • Small systematic discrepancies imply SEDA growth estimates may not generalize to MAP Growth-based estimates in some states [~W]
  • Discrepancies between SEDA and MAP Growth ELA growth estimates are slightly more pronounced in high-socioeconomic-status districts [~W]
  • SEDA growth estimates show a high degree of congruence with MAP Growth-based estimates overall [+W]
