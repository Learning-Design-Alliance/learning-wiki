Arthur book-based educational television program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Arthur is a book-based educational television program based on Marc Brown's storybooks about an 8-year-old aardvark, designed for children ages 4–8. "Each show is 30 minutes in length and includes two stories involving characters dealing with moral issues." It is broadcast daily on PBS stations, and its materials are available as free online downloads from the PBS Kids website. It was used as a listening comprehension and language development intervention for English language learning students, though it is not specifically designed for them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools using the program need access to televisions; episodes can also be obtained from bookstores, video stores, or public libraries.

Constraints¶

  • Arthur is not specifically designed for English language learners but, according to the developer, can be used with these students.

Target Learners¶

  • children ages 4–8
  • preschool and kindergarten students
  • English language learning kindergarten students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • listening comprehension
  • language development
  • narrative skill development

Claims¶

  • The average improvement index for Arthur in English language development is +11 percentile points, ranging from –5 to +17 across findings [+W]
  • Mean clause length showed a small negative difference favoring the comparison group, not statistically significant [-W]
  • Watching Arthur improves English narrative skill development in kindergarten English language learners more than an alternative educational program (potentially positive effects) [+W]
  • The WWC rated Arthur as having potentially positive effects rather than positive effects because only one study met evidence standards and it lacked a strong design [+W]
  • The supporting evidence is a randomized controlled trial in which 108 kindergarten English learners watched 54 episodes of Arthur or Between the Lions over one school year [+W]
  • The medium of a story shapes children's apprehension: television highlights action, picture books figurative language, radio sound effects [+W]
