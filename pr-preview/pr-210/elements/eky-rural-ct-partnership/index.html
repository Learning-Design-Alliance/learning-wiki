Expanding rural computing education partnership across Eastern KY districts, industry, and national organizations¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The project is described as "an ever-expanding partnership" organized by Floyd County Schools (about 5,500 students, 8 K-8 schools), Pikeville Independent School District (approximately 1,200 students), Eastern KY software development start-up Bit Source, Throughlines Edu in Pittsburgh, PA, and Digital Promise. Funded by the National Science Foundation, it pairs a first three-year project, "Tough as Nails, Nimble Fingers: Developing a K-8 Coding Pathway for Kentucky Appalachia", with the current "Drawing on Kinship" project.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Partner organizations spanning local districts, local industry, and external research organizations
  • External funding (NSF)

Constraints¶

  • Partnership is specific to Eastern KY districts and organizations

Target Learners¶

  • K-8 students in Floyd County and Pikeville Independent districts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Computational thinking aligned with KY technology and computer science standards

Claims¶

  • A research-practice partnership is creating a K-8 computational thinking pathway in two rural Kentucky districts by applying South Fayette School District's model and training 75+ teachers over three years [+W]
  • The partnership's research plan studies pathway creation, grade-band competencies, and whether a competency-based PD framework assists instructors in demonstrating teaching proficiency [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Computing Pathways Research Practice Partnership with three districts
  • Two NSF CSforAll research-practice partnerships designing a K-8 Computational Thinking pathway in Kentucky Appalachia
  • Tough As Nails, Nimble Fingers (TAN) virtual computing PD project
