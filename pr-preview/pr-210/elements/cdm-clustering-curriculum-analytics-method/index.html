Curriculum analytics method combining CDM attainment estimation with hierarchical clustering of learner profiles¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A two-step curriculum analytics approach that maps existing assessments to professional standards and applies a generalized cognitive diagnostic model to assessment grades to produce attainment probabilities (0–1) per standard, then uses hierarchical agglomerative clustering to group learners into interpretable profiles. The article states: "The model's output provides probabilities (on a scale of 0 to 1) indicating the extent to which the latent constructs (skills or standards) are attained." It was applied to an Australian ITE program using the APST framework and does not alter current assessment practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A validated curriculum mapping between assessments and the professional standards, plus learners' assessment grades, as model inputs

Constraints¶

  • Standards 5–7 were excluded because their assessments were pass/fail with little variability; the method requires re-running if the curriculum mapping changes

Target Learners¶

  • Pre-service teachers in higher education professional degree programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Attainment of externally regulated professional teaching standards

Claims¶

  • CDM-estimated attainment of professional teaching standards varies non-uniformly across standards, with Standard 1 highest (0.86) and Standard 4 lowest (0.50) [+W]
  • Hierarchical clustering of CDM attainment probabilities identifies five learner groups with distinct patterns of professional standards attainment [+W]
  • Mean program GPA differs significantly across the five standards-attainment learner groups [+W]
  • GPA fails to distinguish three pairs of learner groups that differ in standards-attainment patterns [+W]
  • Standard 1 (knowing students) was attained relatively easily by all groups except the Novice group, while Standard 4 (supportive and safe environments) proved most challenging [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Use standards-based learner profiles to deliver targeted support and curriculum-level interventions in professional degree programs
