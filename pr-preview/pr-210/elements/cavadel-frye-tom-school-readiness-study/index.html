Short-term longitudinal study of theory of mind and school readiness in 120 low-income children¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A short-term longitudinal study of "120 low-income preschool and kindergarten children" examining "relations among theory of mind, the understanding of teaching, and learning behaviors and their collective role in children's literacy and numeracy skills at school entry." It was published in Developmental Psychology, vol. 53, issue 12, and situates findings within theory of mind and sociocognitive development in school readiness.

Claims¶

  • Low-income children show theory of mind development differences compared with typically studied higher-income samples [+W]
  • A combination of sociocognitive variables predicts concurrent academic outcomes at school entry [+W]
  • Theory of mind predicts concurrent literacy and numeracy outcomes at school entry among low-income children [+W]
  • Children's understanding of teaching predicts changes in literacy scores over time [+W]
