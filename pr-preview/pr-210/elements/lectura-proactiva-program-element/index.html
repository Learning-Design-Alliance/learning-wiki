Lectura Proactiva: one-to-small-group Spanish reading intervention accelerating the transition to reading in English¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Lectura Proactiva, distributed as Intervenciones tempranas de la lectura, is “a one-to-small group reading intervention aimed at providing Spanish-speaking early readers the skills and strategies to accelerate the transition to reading in English.” Instruction begins with “alphabetic principles, reading connected text, and reading for meaning,” then moves to “decoding multisyllabic words, fluency, and comprehension skills.” Students meet daily for 50 minutes in small groups of three to five with a bilingual certified teacher.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Delivered by bilingual certified teachers
  • Delivered as a supplement to regular reading instruction

Constraints¶

  • Positive outcomes were demonstrated in urban Texas schools with high populations of EL learners

Target Learners¶

  • Spanish-speaking first-grade students scoring below the 25th percentile on literacy measures

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Spanish-to-English reading transition skills: alphabetic principles, decoding multisyllabic words, fluency, and comprehension

Claims¶

  • Lectura Proactiva's evidence base comes from randomized studies of Spanish-speaking first graders below the 25th percentile in urban Texas schools [+W]
  • Lectura Proactiva raises literacy outcomes for Spanish-speaking first-grade struggling readers, with an average effect size of +0.49 across two studies [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Bilingual Cooperative Integrated Reading and Composition (BCIRC) program
  • Imagine Español: personalized Spanish language and literacy program for grades Pre-K–6

Examples¶

  • Schedule daily 50-minute small-group sessions of three to five students with a bilingual certified teacher
