Wisconsin's Model Academic Standards for Information and Technology Literacy (Bulletin No. 9002, 1998)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A state standards publication issued by the Wisconsin Department of Public Instruction in 1998. It states that "Academic standards specify what students should know and be able to do, what they might be asked to do to give evidence of standards, and how well they must perform." Each of its four content standards is followed by performance standards by the end of grade 4, grade 8, and grade 12, and each performance standard includes indicators detailing how students demonstrate proficiency. The document was drafted by a task force of educators, parents, and business people with public engagement through forums, focus groups, and discussion drafts.

Context¶

  • Districts adopting the model integrate it into local grade-by-grade curriculum

  • Adoption is voluntary; the standards are a model to be met or exceeded, not intended to limit local districts

Target Learners¶

  • PK-12 students, including students with disabilities, LEP students, and accelerated learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Information and technology literacy across the PK-12 curriculum

Claims¶

  • The task force states the performance standards require district commitment to technological infrastructure, technical support, and ongoing teacher training. [+W]
  • Adopting Wisconsin's Model Academic Standards is voluntary, and districts may use them as guides for local curriculum. [+W]
  • The Wisconsin information and technology literacy standards are organized into four content standards: media and technology, information and inquiry, independent learning, and the learning community. [+W]
  • The Wisconsin standards call for shared applications of knowledge in five general categories to be developed in every class across the curriculum. [+W]
