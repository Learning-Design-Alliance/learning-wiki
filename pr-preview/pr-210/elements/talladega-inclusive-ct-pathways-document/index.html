Talladega Inclusive CT Pathways document and competency map¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 design), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A K-12 pathway document developed by Talladega County Schools to expand computer science and computational thinking access, built on the Rigorous Curriculum Design template because it "was very similar to Talladega's content pacing guides used at all grade levels." The accompanying competency map aligns grade-level content to five CT competencies, maps to Alabama DLCS standards, and was refined through teacher focus-group feedback, classroom piloting, and professional development sessions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Development time with official release time from classroom duties; the planning team logged 65 hours of working sessions

Constraints¶

  • The district acknowledged that disparities in computing participation and achievement persisted despite the pathway

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in a rural district, with an equity focus on students from low socioeconomic households and female students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Computational thinking and computer science competencies across K-12 grade levels

Claims¶

  • A district produced an inclusive computational thinking pathway document through a participatory, teacher-represented development process spanning multiple school years. [+W]
  • Computing participation and achievement disparities persisted in the district despite development of an inclusive CT pathway. [-W]
  • Talladega County Schools narrowed a broad CT framework to five core competencies embedded across the curriculum [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • CT Pathways Toolkit for district design of K-12 CS/CT pathways
  • Computing Pathways Research Practice Partnership of districts designing K-12 CS/CT pathways
  • IPSD Inclusive CT Pathway competency map of six computational thinking competencies

Examples¶

  • Make the computational thinking pathway visible to the wider community through a public website
  • Show teachers concrete examples of computational thinking in practice before asking them to design CT lessons
  • Select teacher leaders as \"experts down the hall\" to represent grade bands and coach peers
  • Add a grade-by-grade Key Vocabulary column to give teachers a common CT lexicon and progress marker
