SpellRead™ small-group literacy program with three instructional phases¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 9 studies (5 causal, 4 quant-synthesis), q3 · 5 of 9 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

SpellRead™, formerly SpellRead Phonological Auditory Training®, is a small-group literacy program for struggling readers in grades 2–12 distributed through PCI Education. It "consists of 105 lessons implemented in three distinct phases that interweave phonemics, phonetics, and instruction in language-based reading and writing". Phase A (50 lessons) trains students to hear and manipulate the 44 sounds of English, Phase B (30 lessons) covers secondary vowel spelling, consonant blends, and two-syllable decoding, and Phase C (25 lessons) covers words of three or more syllables, clusters, and verb forms. The program takes five to nine months and is delivered in small groups of five students with one instructor in 60–90 minute classes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Delivered with small groups of five students and one instructor in 60–90 minute classes
  • Grade-tailored SpellRead™ Libraries containing accompanying readers and trade books

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated evidence base is limited to two studies of fifth- and sixth-grade struggling readers, rated by the WWC as a small extent of evidence

Target Learners¶

  • Struggling readers in grades 2–12, including adolescents in grades 5–6

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Decoding and phonemic awareness (alphabetics)
  • Reading fluency
  • Reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • In the Rashotte, MacPhee, and Torgesen (2001) RCT, SpellRead™ produced statistically significant positive effects on alphabetics and reading fluency for fifth- and sixth-grade struggling readers after eight weeks [+M]
  • One year after the intervention ended, SpellRead™ effects were not statistically significant on the supplemental Torgesen et al. (2007) outcomes [-W]
  • SpellRead™ shows potentially positive effects on alphabetics for adolescent readers, with an average improvement index of +23 across two studies [+W]
  • SpellRead™ shows potentially positive effects on comprehension for adolescent readers, with an average improvement index of +11 [+W]
  • SpellRead™ shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency for adolescent readers, with an average improvement index of +14 [+W]
  • In the Torgesen et al. (2006) RCT, SpellRead™ showed indeterminate effects for fifth graders, with no statistically significant or substantively important effects in any domain after six months [-M]
  • One-year follow-up and LiPS-vs-RWT supplemental comparisons showed mostly non-significant differences, with some follow-up alphabetics effects remaining significant [~W]
