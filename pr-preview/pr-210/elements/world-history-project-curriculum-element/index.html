World History Project curriculum¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

World History Project is a free, online, year-long high school world history curriculum published by OER Project, including "units with readings, videos, and historical thinking skill activities." It launched in November 2019 and is accessed by thousands of U.S. teachers; for 2022-23, 4,706 teachers logged in at least 20 times. It aims to be truly global in authorship and content, provides spiraling supports for historical thinking skill development, extensive pedagogical guidance, and a cohesive curriculum with customization options.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need time to develop a holistic understanding of the curriculum structure and support during initial adoption phases

Constraints¶

  • As of the 2022-23 school year the report identified areas needing improvement in usability, engagement, and accessibility

Target Learners¶

  • ninth or 10th grade on-level or honors world history students in U.S. public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • historical thinking skills including argumentation, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing

Claims¶

  • World History Project students show comparable competency to comparison students in the other five historical thinking skills [+M]
  • World History Project does not engage students more than business-as-usual curricula on first implementation, with somewhat negative perception trends [-M]
  • Teachers find World History Project overwhelming in their initial adoption year and make substantial modifications across three main categories [-M]
  • World History Project adoption increases learning opportunities for historical thinking in summative activities relative to business-as-usual curricula [+M]
  • Students learning from World History Project demonstrate more understanding of continuity and change over time in history than comparison students [+M]
  • World History Project assignments scored higher on most learning opportunity rubric dimensions than other assignments [+M]
