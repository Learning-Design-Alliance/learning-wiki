Reading Mastery: a Direct Instruction reading curriculum for grades K–6 in three versions¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Reading Mastery is "one of several curriculum components that constitute the Direct Instruction curriculum from SRA/McGraw-Hill," designed to provide systematic reading instruction to students in grades K–6. It can serve as an intervention for struggling readers, a supplement to a core reading program, or a stand-alone program. It is available in three versions: Reading Mastery Classic (pre-K–3), Reading Mastery Plus (K–6), and Reading Mastery Signature Edition (K–5), the last with Reading, Oral Language/Language Arts, and Literature strands. Students are grouped by reading level based on program placement tests, and the program includes a continuous monitoring component.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students are grouped with other students at a similar reading level, based on program placement tests
  • The program typically spans one academic year

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary-age children above, at, or below grade level in reading performance
  • English language learners
  • Special education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Letter sounds, segmenting and blending words, vocabulary, and comprehension strategies
  • Phonemic awareness, phonics, word analysis, fluency, spelling, decoding, and word recognition
  • Oral language, communication, and writing skills

Claims¶

  • Reading Mastery shows no discernible effects on reading comprehension for adolescent learners, with an average improvement index of –7 percentile points [-W]
  • Reading Mastery has potentially positive effects on reading fluency for adolescent learners, with a WWC improvement index of +19 percentile points [+M]
  • The evidence base for Reading Mastery with adolescent learners is small: only 2 of 175 reviewed studies met WWC evidence standards [~W]
  • Supplemental Reading Mastery instruction showed substantively important immediate effects on four of five reading measures for English language learners, none statistically significant [~M]
  • The WWC-computed average effect size for Reading Mastery on ELL reading achievement is 0.76, corresponding to an improvement index of +28 percentile points [+M]
  • One year after the intervention, Reading Mastery showed a statistically significant effect on word attack for English language learners, with positive but non-significant differences on other reading measures [+M]
  • The WWC rates Reading Mastery as having potentially positive effects on the reading achievement of English language learners [+M]
