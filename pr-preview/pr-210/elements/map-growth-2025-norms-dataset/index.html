2025 MAP Growth Achievement and Growth Norms dataset and norms tables¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The 2025 MAP Growth Norms are a released set of national achievement and growth norms for U.S. public school students, derived from "a comprehensive dataset from 30% of all US public schools,makingthemoneofthelargestnormingsamplesevercreated". The release includes student and school achievement norms and percentiles for Mathematics and Reading (K–12), Language Usage (grades 2–11), and Science (grades 2–10), plus growth norms tables (Appendices A–C), built from Survey with Goals adaptive test events meeting reliability and growth-event eligibility criteria.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Eligible test events must meet reliability standards, including event duration of at least 6 minutes, RIT scores within scale bounds, and standard error of measure between 1.0 and 5.5

Constraints¶

  • MAP Spanish Reading test events are excluded because they are on a different measurement scale and have separate user norms

Target Learners¶

  • U.S. public school students in grades K–12, including students using assistive technologies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Evaluating student and school achievement and growth; individualizing instruction and goal setting

Affordances¶

  • Map Growth Norms Taxonomy Ten Norms

Claims¶

  • EISA-based MAP Growth testing produced slightly lower fall Mathematics scores but higher winter and spring Mathematics scores than traditional MAP Growth, and equipercentile linking was used to align the two versions for norming [+M]
  • The 2025 MAP Growth norms were derived from one of the largest norming samples ever created, spanning 116,225,831 test events from 13,804,182 students in 29,720 schools across 7,000 districts over six testing terms [+M]
  • The 2025 MAP Growth norming sample showed no systematic differences from the U.S. public school target population on selected school-level correlates, with no standardized mean difference reaching the 0.2 threshold [+M]
  • Across 90 grade-subject-level conditions, the three-piece piecewise model showed the lowest deviance for 64% of student-level and 78% of school-level models, outperforming both compound polynomial candidates [+M]
  • The 2025 MAP Growth norms adopted a two-year, two-level piecewise mixed-effects design because two consecutive years of data are sufficient for within-year and adjacent-year growth norms [+M]
