Playworks program (full-time recess coaches in low-income schools)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Playworks is an initiative of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation that "places full-time coaches in low-income schools to provide opportunities for organized play during recess and throughout the school day." The evaluation covered implementation and impacts across 25 schools using random assignment, conducted by Mathematica and the John W. Gardner Center at Stanford University.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Full-time coaches placed in low-income schools to organize play during recess and throughout the school day

Constraints¶

  • Findings reported here are selected results from the first cohort of schools only

Target Learners¶

  • Students in low-income elementary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Social behavior and inclusion during recess
  • Attention and readiness for classroom learning

Claims¶

  • Teachers in Playworks schools reported significantly better student behavior at recess and readiness for class [+W]
  • Playworks made transitions from recess to classroom learning less difficult, per teacher reports [+W]
  • Playworks improved teachers' perceptions of students' safety and feeling included during recess in a randomized experiment [+W]
  • Most teachers, students, and principals reported positive perceptions of the Playworks program [+W]
  • Teachers in Playworks schools reported less bullying and exclusionary behavior during recess than control-school teachers [+W]
  • Teachers in Playworks schools were more likely to report that students enjoyed adult-organized recess activities [+W]
  • In a randomized controlled trial, children in Playworks schools spent more time in vigorous physical activity at recess than students at non-Playworks schools, and more teachers in Playworks schools reported students engaged in intense recess activity [+W]
