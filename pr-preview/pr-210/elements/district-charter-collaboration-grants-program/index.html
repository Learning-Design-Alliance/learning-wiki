District-Charter Collaboration Grants program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A grant program of the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation awarded in November 2012 to seven cities, "with the goal of fostering cooperation between the public and charter school sectors." Mathematica evaluated the seven grantees from 2013 to 2016 under the Evaluation of the District-Charter Collaboration Grants project, and this issue brief summarizes that evaluation's key findings, including educator grantees' reports that collaborative work on classroom observation, operations alignment, and curriculum development supported their professional development.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Funding awarded by the Gates Foundation to city grantees to foster cooperation between the public and charter school sectors

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated in seven cities receiving the November 2012 grants, over a 2013-2016 time frame

Target Learners¶

  • Educators in public and charter school sectors in the seven grantee cities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Educator professional development through cross-sector collaboration

Claims¶

  • Educator grantees in the District-Charter Collaboration Grants cited collaborative work on classroom observation, operations alignment, and curriculum development as particularly helpful to their professional development [+W]
  • In schoolwide grant-activity cities, about half of surveyed educators collaborated with educators from the opposite sector [+W]
  • In cities with individually targeted grant activities, more than three-quarters of grant participants collaborated across sectors [+W]
  • Respondents viewed local foundations, businesses, and community groups as promoting cross-sector collaboration, while competition, political divisions, and teachers' unions may hinder it [+W]
  • Nearly all respondents said inadequate school time and opportunities for collaboration made cross-sector collaboration more difficult [-W]
  • Cross-sector leadership programs were widely viewed as the most successful collaboration grant activities, while limited grant time period and scope and lack of goal clarity hindered implementation [+W]
  • Leaders at city, central office, and school levels are perceived to play a key role in promoting cross-sector collaboration [+W]

Examples¶

  • Use cross-sector collaborative tasks (classroom observation, operations alignment, curriculum development) as vehicles for educator professional development
