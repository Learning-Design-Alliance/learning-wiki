League of Innovative Schools membership network¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A Digital Promise membership network of forward-thinking school districts, described as "a coalition of 86 districts across 33 states, serving more than 3 .3 million students", including urban, suburban, and rural districts. Members engage in cross-district collaboration, cross-sector partnerships with research institutions and education-focused companies, and sharing of best practices. Related Digital Promise offerings to member districts include Verizon Innovative Learning Schools (8.6 percent of districts), educator micro-credentials (50 percent of surveyed districts), ed-tech research pilots (16 percent), and Learning Studios (26 percent).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts apply for membership and are admitted based on evaluation scores and network balance

Constraints¶

  • Large, urban districts were underrepresented in the survey respondent sample

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 district leaders, teachers, and students in member districts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Innovative learning and leadership practices
  • technology-supported teaching and learning

Affordances¶

  • League Innovation Intermediary Theory Change

Claims¶

  • 81.3 percent of surveyed League district leaders reported that League membership increased their capacity to innovate [+W]
  • 81.3 percent of surveyed leaders reported their students benefited from League membership [+W]
  • League district leaders rated professional development highly and reported extensive peer networking and best-practice sharing [+W]
  • Longer League membership significantly predicted leaders' reported increases in capacity to innovate [+W]
  • Longer League membership significantly predicted leaders' reported increases in programs for students [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Recruit practitioners for research engagement by leaning into existing networks, using open calls and targeted invitations
  • Join national innovation networks and build internal leadership academies to advance responsible district AI adoption
