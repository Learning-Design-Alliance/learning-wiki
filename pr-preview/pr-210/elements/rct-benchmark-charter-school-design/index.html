RCT-benchmark design using student-level charter school data with standardized achievement test outcomes¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's design uses student-level data drawn from a large-scale randomized controlled trial of charter schools as a benchmark against which nonexperimental regression and propensity score estimators are tested for bias. The key outcome measures are standardized achievement tests. The article states it addresses prior studies' limitations by "using student-level data based on a large-scale RCT of charter schools for which standardized achievement tests are the key outcome measure."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An RCT with randomized treatment assignment to serve as the unbiased benchmark against which nonexperimental estimators are compared

Constraints¶

  • Applies to charter school evaluation settings with standardized achievement test outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 charter school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate estimation of charter schools' effects on standardized achievement

Claims¶

  • Using baseline data strongly predictive of outcome measures considerably reduces bias in nonexperimental estimates of charter school achievement effects [+M]
  • Some bias may remain in nonexperimental estimates even when rich pre-intervention data are used [~M]
  • Prior studies finding nonexperimental estimators biased examined weaker evaluation designs than RCT-benchmark studies [~M]
  • Nonexperimental methods incorporating pre-treatment outcome measures can replicate experimental ITT impact estimates when control-group crossover occurs [~M]
