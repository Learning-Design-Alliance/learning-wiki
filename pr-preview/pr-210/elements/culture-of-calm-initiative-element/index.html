Culture of Calm initiative: a multi-strategy school-climate program delivered through vendors and school staff in 47 Chicago high schools¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Culture of Calm initiative was a Chicago Public Schools safety program begun in late 2010 in 47 high schools, intended to improve school climate through "Leadership and staff commitment; Behavior frameworks; Staff development; Student development; Community engagement; and Performance management." Schools received funds for professional development, mentoring, case management, and curriculum, often delivered by vendor community organizations, with a designated coordinator overseeing efforts. Six pilot schools received more resources and central-office consulting support.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A designated CoC coordinator to oversee school and vendor efforts, and principal buy-in for implementation

Constraints¶

  • CoC schools served neighborhoods with higher unemployment, poverty, and crime, and had slightly lower incoming test scores than comparison schools

Target Learners¶

  • high school students in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved school climate and safety supporting student learning

Claims¶

  • Student reports of academic personalism increased dramatically in Culture of Calm schools relative to non-CoC schools (d = 1.1), though the substantive meaning is uncertain [+W]
  • Time constraints, student attendance, and slow central-office approval of Statements of Work were the major implementation barriers reported by coordinators, principals, and vendors [~W]
  • Teacher-Principal Trust improved only marginally significantly, and Student-Teacher Trust, Program Coherence, Quality Professional Development, and Teacher-Parent Trust showed no significant differential change [~W]
  • Pilot CoC schools did not improve faster than non-pilot CoC schools or non-CoC schools on any measure [-W]
  • Student reports of safety did not improve faster in Culture of Calm schools than in other similar high schools [-W]
  • Teachers' reports of disorder and crime decreased at a faster rate in Culture of Calm schools than in non-CoC schools between 2009 and 2011 (d = 0.4) [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Secure principal buy-in and an in-school ally before launching external vendor programs in schools
