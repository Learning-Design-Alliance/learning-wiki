PROMISE federal demonstration for youth ages 14-16 receiving Supplemental Security Income¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (1 for, 5 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

PROMISE (Promoting Readiness of Minors in Supplemental Security Income) is described as "a federal initiative that aimed to improve the long-term self-sufficiency of youth ages 14-16 receiving Supplemental Security Income", a means-tested benefit for people with very low incomes. The evaluation covered 2013-2022 and was prepared for the Social Security Administration. This report provides "a more detailed picture of PROMISE youth's employment, education, training, and living arrangements" and benchmarks outcomes against similarly aged youth.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Targets youth ages 14-16 receiving Supplemental Security Income, a means-tested benefit intended for people with very low incomes.

Constraints¶

  • The initiative was evaluated in the context of the federal PROMISE grants program administered for the Social Security Administration.

Target Learners¶

  • Youth ages 14-16 receiving Supplemental Security Income

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improve long-term self-sufficiency through education, training, and employment outcomes

Claims¶

  • Youth who were less education-oriented and more work-oriented may have been more likely to enroll in the PROMISE demonstration [~W]
  • Treatment and control groups showed few differences in job characteristics, work search activities, and perceived barriers to employment [~M]
  • PROMISE treatment group youth had higher employment rates than control group youth [+M]
  • PROMISE treatment group youth had lower educational attainment than control group youth [-M]
  • PROMISE treatment and control group youth enrolled in education and training at similar rates five years after random assignment [~M]
  • PROMISE youth experienced worse education and employment outcomes than ACS youth with and without disabilities [-W]
  • Many SSI youth received transition services without PROMISE, but there was substantial room for PROMISE to improve service use [~M]
  • Identifying SSI youth for outreach is challenging under the current transition system [~M]
