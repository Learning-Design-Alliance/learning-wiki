English cognate constructions¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Cognate constructions are sentences in which the object and verb share meaning, such as 'sing a song,' 'drink a drink,' and 'think a thought.' The paper defines them as those "in which theobject bolo the same meaning as the verb," noting that cognate verbs govern a class of objects whose generic noun is based on the same morpheme as the verb. They serve as the underlying source for dissimilated sentences in English.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A generic cognate noun based on the same morpheme as the verb

Target Learners¶

  • linguistics students
  • students of English grammar

Target Learning Goals¶

  • recognizing and analyzing cognate verb-object constructions

Claims¶

  • Dissimilation is stated as a two-step rule: delete the generic cognate noun, then replace the verb with a pro-verb [+W]
  • Subject case category governs pro-verb selection and passivization in dissimilated sentences [+W]
  • Spanish and French show the same dissimilation pattern for reflexive-type verbs as English [+W]
