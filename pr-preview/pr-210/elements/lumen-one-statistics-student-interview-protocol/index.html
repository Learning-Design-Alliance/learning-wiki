Semi-structured interview protocol for undergraduate statistics students using Lumen One courseware¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A semi-structured interview instrument conducted with undergraduate Statistics students as part of a Digital Promise study of the Lumen One courseware. The protocol opens with consent and disclosures stating the session will be recorded and that reports will refer to participants anonymously, then moves through a guided tour, course content, learning behaviors, belonging, and wrap-up ratings on 0-10 scales. Its questions probe perceived usefulness, difficulty, inclusivity of resources, and helpfulness of feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interviewer uses a checklist of key topics during the student-guided tour, prompting any topics the student does not cover
  • Session is recorded and consent obtained before starting

Constraints¶

  • Participation is voluntary and identities are not shared with instructors or colleges
  • Findings refer to participants anonymously

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate students taking introductory statistics courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Evaluate student perceptions of digital statistics courseware and its support for learning

Claims¶

  • Digital Promise and Achieving the Dream interviewed undergraduate statistics students about their Lumen One courseware experience [+W]
  • Dedicated sense-of-belonging courseware features were underused: most students were unaware of the Lumen Community and vaguely remembered the Introduce Yourself module [+W]
  • Undergraduate statistics students most frequently used and valued the courseware's ungraded formative practice features, the Readiness Check and Self Check [+W]
  • Real-world and racially diverse examples in courseware content helped students retain, comprehend, and relate to statistics [+W]
  • About half of students reported instructors helped bridge courseware guidance and their understanding through outreach, availability, and responsiveness [+W]
  • Instructors in their second or subsequent term using Lumen One made greater use of six of seven instructor-facing courseware components than first-term users. [+W]
  • BIPOC students perceived statistics as a neutral subject and did not discern explicit racial or cultural inclusion in the courseware, generally viewing that neutrality as appropriate [+W]
  • Course modality moderated student experience: instructor responsiveness was more prevalent in face-to-face classes and online students reported more frustration [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Lumen One Introduction to Statistics courseware

Examples¶

  • Hypothetical fallback questions for unexperienced course features
  • Student-guided screen-share tour to elicit courseware perceptions
