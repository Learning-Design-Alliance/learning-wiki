Research-practice partnership dataset linking seven cohorts of test takers' demographic and English proficiency data to seven years of EL and SPED participation¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Through a research-practice partnership, the study links "student demographic data and initial English proficiency assessment data across seven cohorts of test takers" and observes "EL and SPED programmatic participation for these students over seven years." This linked longitudinal dataset is the evidentiary basis for the study's regression discontinuity and regression comparisons of EL status effects on special education placement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires a research-practice partnership providing access to student demographic records, initial English proficiency assessment data, and multi-year EL and SPED programmatic participation records.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students classified as English Learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable identification of students for special education services

Claims¶

  • EL status had either a null effect or led to slight under-identification in SPED placement [+W]
  • EL status led to under-identification for Spanish speakers but proportionate representation for Mandarin/Cantonese and other-language speakers [+W]
  • Regression discontinuity estimates of EL status effects on SPED placement consistently differ substantively from regression analysis results [+W]
  • Under-identification of SPED placement occurred two years after EL classification [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • NWEA dataset of 186,139 ever-ELs and never-EL peers in 1,520 schools
  • Years-as-EL student-level dataset from a California district (N = 41,343)
