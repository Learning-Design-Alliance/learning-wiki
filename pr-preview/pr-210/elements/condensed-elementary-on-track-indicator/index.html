Condensed Elementary On-Track (Condensed EOT) indicator¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Condensed EOT is a simplified version of CPS's five-level Elementary On-Track indicator that places students in grades 3-8 into four categories using two thresholds: "At least a 3.0 GPA" and "At least 90% attendance." The four categories are On-Track, Academic Support, Attendance Support, and Intensive Support. The report uses it to connect elementary grades and attendance to later outcomes and offers it for identifying and matching students with interventions and monitoring progress over time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students need end-of-year core GPA (math, reading, social studies, and science) and end-of-year attendance rates to determine placement
  • Practitioners are recommended to use the framework at least quarterly to identify and intervene with students

Constraints¶

  • Students who attend charter schools are not included in the analysis since course performance or GPA information for charters was unavailable
  • The framework is best suited for conversations among educators and adults who work with educators, not directly with students themselves

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 3-8 in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Identifying students who need additional academic or attendance support
  • Monitoring student progress and designing systems of student support

Claims¶

  • Academic Support students, who attended regularly but earned below a 3.0 GPA, made up nearly 40 percent of students and saw academic struggles persist beyond the middle grades [+W]
  • Attendance Support students maintained a 3.0 GPA despite low attendance but graduated high school at 71 percent, below the district average, and fewer than half attended college [+W]
  • More than one-half of Intensive Support students did not graduate high school in four years and more than 80 percent did not enroll directly in college [+W]
  • The share of students in the On-Track category increased by 20 percentage points between 2009 and 2019 while the Intensive Support share decreased from 12 to 8 percent [+W]
  • Elementary students in the On-Track category graduated high school at more than twice the rate and enrolled in college at more than four times the rate of students in the Intensive Support category [+W]
  • Black and Latinx students were less likely than their peers to be in the On-Track category, and within each race/ethnicity group boys were less likely to be on-track than girls [+W]
  • Students in higher CPS EOT categories had progressively higher rates of high school graduation and college enrollment [+W]
