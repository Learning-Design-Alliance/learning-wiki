Elevate survey for classroom-level student experience data¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Elevate is a student experience survey co-developed by the Consortium team and PERTS, introduced beginning in 2020 to a selection of CPS schools and classrooms, typically partnered with a school support organization with expertise in data use. It asks students about the same classroom learning conditions found on Cultivate but is administered by individual teachers to their own students, with classroom-level data shared with the teacher whose classroom is surveyed, who may then provide access to others. This contrasts with Cultivate's schoolwide model, making Elevate suited to teacher-level inquiry into learning conditions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administered by individual teachers to their own students
  • Typically used in schools or classrooms partnered with a school support organization with data-use expertise

Constraints¶

  • Classroom-level data goes to the individual teacher rather than being aggregated schoolwide

Target Learners¶

  • Students in participating CPS schools and classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving classroom learning conditions through teacher-level data use

Claims¶

  • Engaging students as partners in analyzing and discussing student experience data creates authentic engagement and signals responsiveness to students [+W]
  • Educators need a shift in adult mindsets—from evaluative/accountability data use toward improvement-oriented use—for student experience data to improve practice [+W]
  • Better coordination, communication, and coherence across professional learning and supports are needed to sustain centering student experience [+W]
  • Schools running four or more planned continuous improvement cycles show marked improvements in learning conditions, while ad hoc or two-cycle implementations do not [+W]
