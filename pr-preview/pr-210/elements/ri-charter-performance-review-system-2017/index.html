Rhode Island Charter School Performance Review System (2017 updated framework)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Charter School Performance Review System is Rhode Island's framework defining processes for approving, renewing, and revoking charter schools, first created in 2010 and updated in 2016–2017. The updated version established "data-driven and transparent reviews" with authorization decisions founded on objective, standardized criteria, stricter accountability described by officials as "tough" and "complex but clear," annual performance dashboards, stakeholder engagement, and a more supportive customer-service role for RIDE. Development was led by RIDE in partnership with local stakeholders and aligned with National Association of Charter School Authorizers best practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Objective measures, transparency, and freedom from political influences so schools have clear performance indicators to monitor progress and prepare for renewal decisions
  • Stakeholder feedback from parents, community members, advocacy groups, and school leaders to ensure buy-in

Constraints¶

  • High staff turnover has left RIDE understaffed, limiting its ability to provide the level of support schools need

Target Learners¶

  • Rhode Island charter school students and families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Charter school quality assurance and continuous improvement

Claims¶

  • Officials raised equity concerns that closing low-performing charter schools may not better serve students when alternative district schools perform even worse [+W]
  • Officials report the new framework's transparency and annual dashboards reduced surprise and stress and enabled focus on continuous improvement [+W]
  • Demand for Rhode Island charter seats surged, with 30,000 applications submitted for 2,500 available seats in 2024–2025 [+W]
  • Key officials report that Rhode Island's new charter performance framework reduced political interference through standardized, transparent data and consistent processes [+W]
  • Key officials describe a shift from a punitive top-down accountability approach to a collaborative, improvement-focused model that fostered trust and communication [+W]
  • National studies cited in the report found Rhode Island charter students outperformed traditional public school peers in reading and math [+W]
  • Charter attendance in Rhode Island is associated with positive, significant one-year growth gains in reading (0.16 SD) and math (0.15 SD) relative to matched TPS controls [+W]
  • A larger share of Rhode Island charter schools outperform local options (43% reading, 57% math) than the national rates found in the 2013 CREDO Study (25% reading, 29% math) [+M]
  • Charter learning gains in each of the three growth periods do not differ statistically from TPS peers [+M]
