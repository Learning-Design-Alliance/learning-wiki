PROMISE programs: coordinated federal-state-local service initiatives for youth with disabilities on SSI¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

PROMISE programs are state-level initiatives designed, in the article's words, to "improve connections among federal programs at the state and local levels" for "youth with disabilities and their families with accessing resources that can improve their educational and employment outcomes." The evaluation covers six PROMISE programs, assessed for impacts on service receipt, education, employment, expectations, health insurance coverage, income, self-determination, and public assistance participation.

Claims¶

  • PROMISE's primary innovations over usual services were intensive case management, facilitating early work experiences, and a family focus [+W]
  • The PROMISE evaluation estimated program impacts on service receipt, education, employment, and related outcomes at 18 months after enrollment [+W]
  • The PROMISE interim report estimated impacts across seven outcome domains for youth and families [+W]
  • Many SSI youth received transition services without PROMISE, but there was substantial room for PROMISE to improve service use [+W]
  • PROMISE youth experienced worse education and employment outcomes than ACS youth with and without disabilities [-W]
