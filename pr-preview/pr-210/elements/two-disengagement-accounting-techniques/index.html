Two techniques for accounting for test disengagement: sample removal and rapid-guess score adjustment¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article examines two techniques for accounting for test disengagement: "removing unengaged test takers from the sample" and "adjusting test scores to remove rapidly guessed items". These operate on observed scores and samples rather than on the test itself, and are evaluated for their effect on estimates of school contributions to growth, achievement gaps, and summer learning loss.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires computer-based tests that produce item response time metadata usable to identify likely disengaged responses

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students taking computer-based achievement tests

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate estimation of school contributions to student growth, achievement gaps, and summer learning loss

Claims¶

  • Accounting for test disengagement had minimal impact on estimates of school growth contributions, achievement gaps, and summer learning loss [+W]
  • Removing disengaged examinees from the sample will likely induce bias in estimates of educational effectiveness [-W]
  • Summer learning loss estimates changed significantly under filtering but not effort-moderated scoring [+M]
  • E-M scoring leaves residual score distortion, suggesting some disengaged test responses are nonrapid rather than rapid guesses [+M]
  • Filtered students had much lower effort-moderated achievement, suggesting motivation filtering may bias mean estimates [+M]
  • Rapid guessing had mostly minimal effects on estimates of school contributions to student growth in this sample [+M]
