Experiment embedded in the 2006 National Survey of Student Engagement comparing numerically and vaguely quantified frequency reports¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's data source is an experiment embedded in the 2006 National Survey of Student Engagement, a large-scale survey of student engagement. The experiment collected behavioral frequency information in two formats: "numerically or vaguely quantified frequency reports," allowing comparison of the measurement models each format supports. It serves as the empirical basis for the working paper's findings on measurement-model structure.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Behavioral frequency information must be collectable in both numerically and vaguely quantified report formats within the same survey context.

Constraints¶

  • Evidence is limited to the 2006 National Survey of Student Engagement context as described in the available text.

Target Learners¶

  • Postsecondary students surveyed in the National Survey of Student Engagement

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate measurement of student behavioral frequency reports for engagement research

Claims¶

  • The format used to quantify behavioral frequency reports directly influences substantive findings [+W]
  • Measurement models based on vaguely quantified behavioral frequency reports differ in structure from those based on numerically quantified reports [+W]
  • Differential interpretation of vague quantifiers is best represented as variability between latent interpretation classes [+W]
  • Latent variable models can be used to detect individual variability in the interpretation of vague quantifiers of behavioral frequency [+W]
