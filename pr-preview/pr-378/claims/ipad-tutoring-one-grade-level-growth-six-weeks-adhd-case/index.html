Evidence¶

McClanahan 2012¶

McClanahan, B., Williams, K., Kennedy, E., & Tate, S. (2012). A Breakthrough for Josh: How Use of an iPad Facilitated Reading Improvement. TechTrends, 56(3). https://link.springer.com/journal/11528

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Single-student action research case: pre/post comparison of a different form of the same informal reading inventory. Second grade moved from instructional (word recognition 96%, comprehension 75%) to independent (100%/100%); third grade became the new instructional level (98%/85%).

Related Claims¶

  • The iPad helped a student with ADHD focus attention and become more metacognitive about his reading — related
  • In the Rashotte, MacPhee, and Torgesen (2001) RCT, SpellRead™ produced statistically significant positive effects on alphabetics and reading fluency for fifth- and sixth-grade struggling readers after eight weeks — related
  • In the Torgesen et al. (2006) RCT, SpellRead™ showed indeterminate effects for fifth graders, with no statistically significant or substantively important effects in any domain after six months — related
  • During COVID, a third-grade volume-of-reading strategy showed greater reading growth than a control group without the strategy — related
  • Four remedial reading interventions combined improved phonemic decoding, word reading accuracy and fluency, and reading comprehension for third-grade struggling readers one year after the interventions ended, though not on all measures — related
  • For fifth-grade struggling readers, the four interventions combined improved phonemic decoding on one measure but produced a small reduction in oral reading fluency one year after the interventions — related
  • The interventions narrowed some reading gaps, reducing the Word Attack gap by about two-thirds for third graders and one-half for fifth graders — related
  • Younger struggling readers benefited more: the interventions generally helped third-grade cohort students more than fifth-grade cohort students — related
  • Failure Free Reading, the only word-level-plus-comprehension program, showed impacts for third graders but no impacts for fifth graders — related
