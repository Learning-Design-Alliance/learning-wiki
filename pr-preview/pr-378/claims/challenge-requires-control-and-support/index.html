Stevens 2014¶

Stevens, W. D., Allensworth, E. M., Pareja, A. S., de la Torre, M., Gwynne, J. A., Rosenkranz, T., & Sebastian, J. (2014). Free to Fail or On-Track to College: An Introduction to the UChicago CCSR Research Series. UChicago CCSR Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/free-fail-or-track-college

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The Instruction Study used district-wide student and teacher survey reports of specific classes with statistical models adjusted for background and prior achievement to relate instructional elements to grades and EPAS test gains. It found instruction "needs to be challenging for students to show learning gains."

"The report shows that classroom instruction needs to be challenging for students to show learning gains."

Stevens 2014 (2)¶

Stevens, W. D., Allensworth, E. M., Pareja, A. S., de la Torre, M., Gwynne, J. A., Rosenkranz, T., & Sebastian, J. (2014). Free to Fail or On-Track to College: An Introduction to the UChicago CCSR Research Series. UChicago CCSR Research Brief. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/free-fail-or-track-college

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The same statistical modeling of classroom instruction showed challenge alone is insufficient: "Increasing challenge does not lead to more learning unless teachers can maintain high levels of classroom control," and grades decline with higher challenge unless support increases. The brief warns teachers adopting harder standards risk higher failure and disengagement without monitoring and support.

"Increasing challenge does not lead to more learning unless teachers can maintain high levels of classroom control. Furthermore, grades decline with higher challenge unless teachers increase the level of support they give students."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students show the highest learning gains in classes that are both challenging and where students actually do work aligned with teachers' expectations; grades are higher in challenging classrooms with high support than in easier classrooms with little support — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Self-determination theory holds that autonomy support raises intrinsic motivation, and a meta-analysis finds expected tangible rewards undermine it — related
  • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. — related
  • Imbalanced challenge and support causes novice library learners to give up or remain dependent (authors' asserted relationship) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Learning pitched at the edge of students' current abilities — the ZPD — is more effective than learning that is too easy or too frustrating — related
  • Introducing more challenging work without sufficient supports or attention to students' emotions and learning strategies can lead to frustration, withdrawal, disruption, and less learning — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Teachers treat resilience under challenge as a teachable skill that current routines and dashboards do little to support — related
