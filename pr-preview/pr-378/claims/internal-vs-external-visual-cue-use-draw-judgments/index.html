Evidence¶

Jaeger 2024¶

Jaeger, A. J., & Fiorella, L. (2024). Metacognitive effects of instructional visuals: the role of cue use and judgment type. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09370-x

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Study 1b with 47 undergraduates reading biology texts. Among the 18 who reported visual cues for draw judgments, text-only participants favored internal mental imagery while image-condition participants favored recall of external images, "X2(1) = 68.10, p = 0.004".

"participants in the text-only condition being more likely to report basing their judgments on their ability to generate an internal mental image (n = 8 out of 9) while those in the text-with-image condition were more likely to report basing their judgment on their ability to recall the image they saw or images they have seen in textbooks before (n = 7 out of 9), X2(1) = 68.10, p = 0.004."

Jaeger 2024 (2)¶

Jaeger, A. J., & Fiorella, L. (2024). Metacognitive effects of instructional visuals: the role of cue use and judgment type. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09370-x

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Study 2 with 48 undergraduates reading chemistry texts replicated the internal-external pattern among the 23 reporting visual cues for draw judgments, "X2(1) = 19.33, p < 0.001".

"participants in the text-only condition were more likely to report basing their judgments on their ability to generate an internal mental image (n = 11 out of 12), whereas those in the text-with-image condition were more likely to report basing their judgment on their ability to recall the provided image they saw or images they have seen in textbooks before (n = 11 out of 11), X2(1) = 19.33, p < 0.001."
