Evidence¶

Scheiter 2020¶

Scheiter, K., Ackerman, R., & Hoogerheide, V. (2020). Looking at Mental Effort Appraisals through a Metacognitive Lens: Are they Biased? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09555-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports that in several studies where students worked on multiple task items varying in complexity within participants, "effort ratings to be higher for complex tasks than for simpler ones." This sensitivity to complexity serves as a proxy for the resolution measure used in metacognitive research, though the degree of resolution is not quantified via within-participant correlations in CLT studies.

"Several studies have found effort ratings to be higher for complex tasks than for simpler ones (e.g., Paas et al. 1994; Schmeck et al. 2015; van Gog et al. 2012)."
