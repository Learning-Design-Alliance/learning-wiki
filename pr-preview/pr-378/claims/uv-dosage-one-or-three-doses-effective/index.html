Evidence¶

Canning 2018¶

Canning, E. A., Harackiewicz, J. M., Priniski, S. J., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., & Hyde, J. S. (2018). Improving Performance and Retention in Introductory Biology with a Utility-Value Intervention. Journal of educational psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000244

q3 · i0 · causal · r3

Posthoc LSD comparisons probing the significant three-way interaction in the fully crossed 2×2×2 design on course grade and continuation. The article reports "both three doses as well as a single dose of UV (at any time point) promoted performance" while two doses did not; the three-way interaction on grades was β = .09, p = .006 — a small effect. 88.16% of three-dose students, 85.92% of early single-dose, and 84.51% of late single-dose students continued versus 69.51% of controls.

"In general, both three doses as well as a single dose of UV (at any time point) promoted performance, compared to the control condition (see Figure 2). Although, the condition in which students received the full dosage of UV had the highest overall mean, it was not more effective than any of the three conditions in which students received a single dose of UV."
