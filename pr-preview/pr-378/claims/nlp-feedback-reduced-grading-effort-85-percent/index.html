Narrative review attribution in Section 2.2: the paper reports Bernius et al.'s use of NLP-based models for feedback on textual student answers in large courses, with the printed figure that "grading effort could be reduced by up to 85%"; no effect size is printed.

"Bernius et al. [39] used NLP-based models to generate feedback for textual student answers in large courses, where grading effort could be reduced by up to 85% with a high precision and an improved quality perceived by the students."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • A technical review reports a 70% cut in grading time and a 15% rise in learner satisfaction from NLP-based automated grading on one MOOC platform, with no comparison design described — related
  • AI-supported co-produced feedback achieved comparable quality and statistically significant learning gains with reduced production time and workload — related
  • GPT models achieved high polarity agreement with instructors only for positive feedback on the Topic aspect, and could not reliably assess the other rubric aspects — reports the opposite
  • NLP models in LA studies typically reach moderate agreement (mean Cohen's kappa 0.54) and mean accuracy 0.79, with deep learning models outperforming others in most studies from 2021 onward — related
  • Transformer-based NLP models achieve automated essay scoring with correlations to human grading exceeding 0.85 — related
  • Pre-service teachers who received NLP-based adaptive feedback were better able to justify their diagnoses than those who received static feedback (reported from Sailer et al.) — related
