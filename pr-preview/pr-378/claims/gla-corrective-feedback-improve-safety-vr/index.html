Evidence¶

Valdemar Stenberdt 2025¶

Valdemar Stenberdt, Ban Mouid Shiwalia, Guido Makransky. (2025). Re-Experiencing vs. Self-Explaining: Comparing Generative Learning Activities in VR. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10096-2

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Pre-registered between-subjects experiment with 161 high school students learning pipetting in immersive VR; the safety analysis included 152 participants. Post-hoc t-tests on the mixed ANOVA showed both groups "significantly improved from after the lesson to after the GLA" with large effects (re-experiencing d = −1.15; self-explaining d = 1.18), surviving Bonferroni correction.

"the re-experienc - ing group significantly improved from after the lesson to after the GLA, p < 0.001, d = −1.15, 95% CI = [−1.50, −0.80], and that the self-explaining group also improved significantly from after the lesson to after the GLA, p < 0.001, d = 1.18"

Valdemar Stenberdt 2025 (2)¶

Valdemar Stenberdt, Ban Mouid Shiwalia, Guido Makransky. (2025). Re-Experiencing vs. Self-Explaining: Comparing Generative Learning Activities in VR. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10096-2

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Same safety analysis in the VR pipetting experiment: the mixed ANOVA showed "no significant differences in how well the two groups remembered safety instructions" (ηp2 = 0.001); the article also reports no Condition × Time interaction.

"However, there were no significant differences in how well the two groups remembered safety instructions, F(1, 150) = 0.160, p = 0.690, ηp2 = 0.001"
