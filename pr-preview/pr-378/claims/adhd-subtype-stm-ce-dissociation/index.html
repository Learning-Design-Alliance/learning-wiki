Evidence¶

Dovis 2015¶

Dovis, S., Van der Oord, S., Wiers, R.W., & Prins, P.J.M. (2015). ADHD subtype differences in reinforcement sensitivity and visuospatial working memory. Journal of Clinical Child and Adolescent Psychology, 44(5), 859-874. https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2014.895940

q3 · i? · causal · r?

This is a controlled experimental comparison reported in the study of 27 children with ADHD-I, 70 children with ADHD-C, and 40 typically developing controls aged 9-12. The article states that "In children with ADHD-C, both STM and the CE seem impaired, and together with motivational impairments, give rise to their deficits in visuospatial WM." In contrast, for ADHD-I the article reports that "STM performance of children with ADHD-I was, in contrast to that of children with ADHD-C, not different from controls." No effect sizes are printed in the supplied text, so impact is null.

Related Claims¶

  • STM and WM performance is worse in ADHD-C than in ADHD-I, while CE-related performance does not differ between subtypes — related
  • Both ADHD subtypes show equally pronounced motivational deficits that impair visuospatial STM and WM performance — related
  • High reinforcement improves STM and WM performance in both ADHD subtypes but not in controls — related
  • Affect recognition deficits were not found unanimously across ADHD groups, suggesting a nuanced subtype-specific pattern — related
  • Diagnostic validity analyses yield results similar to the prevalence findings for WM, STM, and motivational impairments — related
  • Impairment on WM, STM, and/or motivation is associated with more inattention symptoms, medication use, and lower IQ scores — related
  • Memory and motivation represent independent neuropsychological domains in children with ADHD — related
  • Visuospatial working memory and short-term memory impairments are most prevalent in children with ADHD-C relative to ADHD-I and typically developing controls — related
  • In the ADHD-I restrictive subtype, visuospatial WM impairments are more prevalent than in controls but STM impairments are not — related
