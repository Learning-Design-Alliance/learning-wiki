Evidence¶

Torbeyns 2015¶

Torbeyns, J., Schneider, M., Xin, Z., & Siegler, R. S. (2015). Bridging the Gap: Fraction Understanding is Central to Mathematics Achievement in Students from Three Different Continents. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.03.002

q3 · i3 · associational · r1

A Country × Grade ANOVA on four fraction division trials in the 187-student study showed a large main effect of Country with printed effect size partial eta² = .48. The article separately reports means of 91% (China), 71% (Belgium), and 22% (U.S.) correct.

"For the fraction division task, a parallel Country × Grade ANOVA showed main effects of Country, F(2, 186) = 82.01, p < .01, partial eta² = .48, and Grade, F(1, 186) = 13.95, p < .01, partial eta² = .07."

Torbeyns 2015 (2)¶

Torbeyns, J., Schneider, M., Xin, Z., & Siegler, R. S. (2015). Bridging the Gap: Fraction Understanding is Central to Mathematics Achievement in Students from Three Different Continents. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.03.002

q3 · i2 · associational · r1

A parallel Country × Grade ANOVA on fraction addition in the 187-student study showed a significant "Country × Grade interaction", F(2, 186) = 7.07, p < .01, with printed effect size partial eta² = .07.

"A parallel Country × Grade ANOVA on fraction addition revealed main effects of Country, F(2, 186) = 86.74, p < .01, partial eta² = .49, and Grade, F(1, 186) = 13.95, p < .01, partial eta² = .07, and also a Country × Grade interaction, F(2, 186) = 7.07, p < .01, partial eta² = .07."
