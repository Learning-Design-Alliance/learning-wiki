Evidence¶

Bert De Smedt 2010¶

Bert De Smedt, Bart Boets. (2010). Phonological processing and arithmetic fact retrieval: Evidence from developmental dyslexia. Neuropsychologia, 48, 3973–3981. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2010.10.018

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Repeated-measures ANOVA (operation within-subject, group between-subject) on reaction times in the 25 dyslexia vs 25 control study. The group main effect was also significant (F(1, 48) = 24.31, p < .01, ηp² = .34), and group differences remained when speed of digit identification was covaried (F(1, 47) = 9.46, p < .01, ηp² = .17).

"There was also a signiﬁcant Group× Operation interaction ( F(1, 48) = 10.55, p < .01, /DC1p2 = .18), suggesting that the difference between multiplication and subtraction was larger in participants with dyslexia than in controls."

Related Claims¶

  • Simple subtraction in adults relies heavily on strategy use, which disadvantages participants under limited working memory resources — related
  • Adults use a mix of retrieval and procedural strategies in single-digit arithmetic, with retrieval more frequent in multiplication and procedures more frequent in subtraction — related
  • Adults with dyslexia execute both retrieval and procedural arithmetic strategies more slowly than controls — related
  • Adults with developmental dyslexia are slower than controls at speeded digit identification — related
  • Adults with developmental dyslexia use arithmetic fact retrieval less frequently than matched controls during single-digit multiplication and subtraction — related
  • Children with DD show slower reaction times than TD children during arithmetic problem solving, with no operation-specific RT effect — related
  • Multiplication employs fact retrieval processes to a larger extent than addition and subtraction, though the regressive difference is slight — related
  • Individual records revealed contrasting specific disabilities within the same pupil across arithmetic operations — related
