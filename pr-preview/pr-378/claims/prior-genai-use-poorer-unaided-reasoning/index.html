Evidence¶

Dawid Ratajczyk 2026¶

Dawid Ratajczyk, Agata Dymarska, Adam Matłoka, Michał Tomczyk, Małgorzata Wiącek. (2026). Thinking With AI, Reasoning Without It: Cognitive Offloading to Generative AI Weakens Effort Regulation. Preprint. https://osf.io/59qtm/ doi:10.31234/osf.io/83jum_v1

q3 · i1 · causal · r3

Exploratory item-level mixed-effects logistic regression on Stage 2 accuracy in the preregistered lab experiment. The quoted estimate shows prior genAI users had OR = 0.68, approximately 32% lower odds of a correct response in the no-incentive condition.

"Within the no-incentive reference condition, prior genAI access was associated with lower odds of answering a Stage 2 question correctly (𝑏= −0.392,𝑆𝐸= 0.195,𝑧= −2.01,𝑝=.045,𝑂𝑅= 0.68)."

Dawid Ratajczyk 2026 (2)¶

Dawid Ratajczyk, Agata Dymarska, Adam Matłoka, Michał Tomczyk, Małgorzata Wiącek. (2026). Thinking With AI, Reasoning Without It: Cognitive Offloading to Generative AI Weakens Effort Regulation. Preprint. https://osf.io/59qtm/

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Preregistered 2×2 ANOVA on Stage 2 network-reasoning accuracy in the same lab experiment. The quoted tests show no significant genAI, incentive, or interaction effects, so H1a, H1b and H4 were not supported. The lower odds of a correct answer appear only in the exploratory item-level analysis, within the no-incentive condition; the genAI × incentive interaction was not significant there either, and the authors say this conditional effect "should not be interpreted as evidence that the effect of genAI differed between incentive conditions". GenAI access was assigned; the study does not separate those who used the tool from those who had it.

"There was no significant main effect of prior genAI access (𝐹(1,153) = 1.84,𝑝=.177,𝜂 2 𝑝 =.011), no significant main effect of incentives (𝐹(1,153) = 1.14,𝑝=.287,𝜂 2 𝑝 =.007), and no significant interaction between prior genAI access and incentives (𝐹(1,153) = 2.22,𝑝=.138,𝜂 2 𝑝 =.014)."
