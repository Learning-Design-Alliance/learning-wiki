Evidence¶

Reinhard Pekrun 2024¶

Reinhard Pekrun. (2024). Overcoming Fragmentation in Motivation Science: Why, When, and How Should We Integrate Theories? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09846-5

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The commentary reports, describing Noetel et al. (2023), a longitudinal study of student-perceived teacher behavior and engagement in physical education. The review states that "behaviors derived from multiple theories were predictive," supporting integration; no effect size is printed in the quoted text.

"The empirical validity of the model was tested in a longitudinal study of the predictive power of student-perceived teacher behavior on students’ engagement in physical education. The results show that behaviors derived from multiple theories were predictive, thus, supporting the value of integrating propositions."

Michael Noetel 2023¶

Michael Noetel, Philip Parker, Theresa Dicke, Mark R. Beauchamp, Nikos Ntoumanis, Ryan M. Hulteen, et al. (2023). Prediction Versus Explanation in Educational Psychology: a Cross-Theoretical Approach to Using Teacher Behaviour to Predict Student Engagement in Physical Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09786-6

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-theoretical predictive analysis in which behaviours from self-determination theory, achievement goal theory, growth mindset theory, and transformational leadership theory competed simultaneously in elastic-net models. The article reports the influential behaviours "did not neatly fit within any single motivational theory".

"The influential behaviours did not neatly fit within any single motivational theory. These findings support arguments for integrating different theoretical approaches, and suggest practitioners may want to consider multiple theories when designing interventions."

Ronnel B. King 2024¶

Ronnel B. King, Luke K. Fryer. (2024). Hybridizing Motivational Strains: How Integrative Models Are Crucial for Advancing Motivation Science. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09850-9

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The essay's summary of the special issue's only empirical paper, Noetel et al. (2023), which used predictive modeling techniques to examine which theory could best explain changes in students' engagement. The essay reports the cross-theoretical finding but no effect sizes are given here. This is an essay reviewing the special issue; it gives neither the number of behaviours nor the sample, which come from Noetel et al. (2023) itself (its entry above).

"They test four major theories—self-determi- nation theory, achievement goal theory, growth mindset theory, and transforma- tional leadership theory—to determine the most critical teacher behaviors that predict changes in students' engagement. Their findings about the most critical teacher behaviors did not fit neatly within one theoretical model, demonstrating the value of integration."
