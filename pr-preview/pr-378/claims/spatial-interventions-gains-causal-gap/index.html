Evidence¶

Jeffrey Buckley 2018¶

Jeffrey Buckley, Niall Seery, Donal Canty. (2018). A Heuristic Framework of Spatial Ability: a Review and Synthesis of Spatial Factor Literature to Support its Translation into STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9432-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports that factor-structure research "has proved to be beneficial to the design of interventions which have resulted in increased STEM educational performance and retention", citing Sorby and colleagues' intervention studies.

"This work has proved to be beneficial to the design of interventions which have resulted in increased STEM educational performance and retention"

Jeffrey Buckley 2018 (2)¶

Jeffrey Buckley, Niall Seery, Donal Canty. (2018). A Heuristic Framework of Spatial Ability: a Review and Synthesis of Spatial Factor Literature to Support its Translation into STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9432-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review states that despite intervention gains, "research studies categorically illustrating a causal effect remain outstanding", so the mechanism of the interventions' success is not scientifically known. Spatial ability is also hard to observe in practice.

"However, research studies categorically illustrating a causal effect remain outstanding (Ramey and Uttal 2017; Stieff and Uttal 2015), and therefore it is not scientifically known how or why these interventions are achieving the results they are."
