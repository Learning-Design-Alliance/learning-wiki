Evidence¶

Haigh Sarah M. 2018¶

Haigh Sarah M., Walsh Jennifer A., Mazefsky Carla A., Minshew Nancy J., Eack Shaun M. (2018). Processing Speed is Impaired in Adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder, and Relates to Social Communication Abilities. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3515-z

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

Case-control comparison of 76 verbal adults with ASD and 64 healthy controls, full-scale IQ matched, using ANCOVA on the MATRICS-based composite of Trail Making A, Symbol Coding, and Category Fluency z-scores. The article reports a large effect size, "d = 1.02" (F(1, 134) = 32.36, p < .001).

"The ASD group showed significantly lower processing speed composite scores compared to the control group after controlling for age, race, and education level, F(1, 134) = 32.36, p < .001, d = 1.02"

Related Claims¶

  • The processing speed deficit in adults with ASD appears on each of the three standardized subtests: Trail Making A, Symbol Coding, and Category Fluency — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Slower processing speed in adults with ASD is associated with greater impairment on ADOS Communication and Reciprocal Social Interaction scores — related
  • Processing speed in adults with ASD shows no significant association with SAS-II social adjustment, and only a near-significant positive association with GAF functioning — related
  • The article attributes the discrepancy with inspection-time studies to methodology, arguing standardized measures are sensitive enough to detect processing speed deficits in ASD — related
