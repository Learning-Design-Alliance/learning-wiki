Evidence¶

Sunae Kim 2025¶

Sunae Kim, Paul L Harris, Benjamin Néher. (2025). Curiosity can influence metacognitive processes. Metacognition and Learning (2025) 20:39. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09444-y

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Experiment 1 (65 Hungarian adults, 40 general-knowledge trials with two answer options, Qualtrics). Mixed-effects regression with participants as random effect showed higher confidence on correct trials, "F(1, 2591) = 273, p <.001" with β = 0.69; means were 73.7 (correct) vs 51.5 (incorrect).

"Participants' confidence level was higher for correct than for incorrect answers F(1, 2591) = 273, p <.001 (b = 20.22, SE = 1.22, β = 0.69)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Pre-monitoring and retrospective confidence judgments are highly consistent, with most agreement at the highest scale level regardless of correctness — related
  • Adults opt out of answering more on trials they later answer incorrectly, and curiosity is higher on opted-out trials (metacognitive control) — related
  • The literature is mixed on age differences in the accuracy of retrospective confidence judgments during recognition memory tests, with older adults sometimes producing more high-confidence false alarms and poorer resolution with deceptive lures — related
  • Second-order metacognitive judgments of subjective uncertainty show a small high-confidence error/correct difference, smaller than curiosity ratings show — related
  • Providing a standard answer to compare against improves the accuracy of students' retrospective monitoring judgments — related
  • Heuristic cues — response time, familiarity, problem complexity, and context reinstating — guide opting-out decisions — related
