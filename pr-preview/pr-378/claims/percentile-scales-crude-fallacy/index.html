The article argues that scales built on percentile differences are crude and fallacious, and that raw percentile values from different tests cannot be equated, translated or combined. → Wells 1915 q1 i? Adjacent percentile intervals differ in size, unlike equal-unit scales: the gap between the 0th and 10th percentiles is not equal to the gap between the 50th and 60th. → Wells 1915 (2)

A theoretical argument (type e) about scaling practice, offered without data. The article adds that the significance of, for example, the 80th percentile varies with the variability and dispersion of different traits.

"Scales devised upon a basis of percentile differences are bound to be very crude, besides embodying a fundamental fallacy."
