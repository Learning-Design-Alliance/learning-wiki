Descriptive disaggregation of the fall 2015 entering cohort by race and ethnicity, all sectors (Figure 2). Race/ethnicity data covered 79 percent of the cohort. The report also states Black students had the lowest persistence rate at 66.9 percent with 54.5 percent retained.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Of students who started college in fall 2015, 73.4 percent persisted at any U.S. institution and 61.1 percent were retained at their starting institution in fall 2016
  • In four-year private institutions, white and Asian students had nearly identical persistence rates (89.8 and 89.6 percent), but Asian students were more likely to continue at their starting institution (82.5 percent)
  • In two-year public institutions, white students had a lower retention rate (50.2 percent) than Hispanic students (55.1 percent) because a higher share of white students transferred to four-year institutions
  • First-year persistence declines with age at entry: 78.2 percent for students 20 and under, 57.8 percent for ages over 20 to 24, and 52.7 percent for students over 24
  • Sector persistence rates for fall 2015 entrants: 81.7 percent at four-year public, 84.9 percent at four-year private, 62.7 percent at two-year public, and 55.7 percent at four-year for-profit institutions
  • Latinx, Black, and Asian students participated at higher rates than White peers; household size explained Latinx, Black, and Asian differences — related
