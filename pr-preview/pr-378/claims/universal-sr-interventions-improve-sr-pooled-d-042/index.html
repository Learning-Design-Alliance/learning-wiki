Evidence¶

Pandey A 2018¶

Pandey A, Hale D, Das S, Goddings A-L, Blakemore S-J, Viner RM. (2018). Effectiveness of Universal Self-regulation–Based Interventions in Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6059379/

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analysis of 19 studies reporting SR outcomes based on task performance scores, pooled under a random-effects model. The review states the overall effect was statistically significant and favored the intervention, with "pooled effect size d = 0.42; 95% CI, 0.32-0.53".

"We found that the overall effect was statistically significant and favored the intervention (pooled effect size d = 0.42; 95% CI, 0.32-0.53)."

Related Claims¶

  • Exercise-based interventions improved self-regulation in 4 of 6 interventions (67%), significant on conventional tests of statistical significance. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Some interventions brought more improvements in self-regulation measures of those with lower baseline scores, offering promise for resource-constrained settings. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Heterogeneity in self-regulation outcomes was partly accounted for by assessment method, with restriction to task performance scores reducing I2 from 77% to 39.3%. — related
  • Social and personal skills interventions had a higher mean effect size (d = 0.64; 95% CI, 0.42-0.86) than the overall pooled estimate (d = 0.42; 95% CI, 0.32-0.53). — related
  • Interventions were effective across all child and adolescent age groups, with no particular age group in which interventions were most effective. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Family-based interventions improved self-regulation in 5 of 9 studies (56%) and had longer follow-up durations than other intervention types. — related
  • Mindfulness and yoga interventions improved self-regulation in 4 of 8 interventions (50%), mostly applied within the adolescent age group. — related
  • Mindfulness training improves EFs most in 7–9 year-olds with initially poorer EFs, and yoga improves EFs where physical training alone does not — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Self Talk Improves Learning And Performance — related
  • Mindfulness interventions show comparatively robust efficacy for preschool SR/EF, especially for outcomes requiring integrated mobilisation of EFs and SR — related
  • Most preschool SR/EF interventions report at least one significant outcome, but only about half of evaluated effects are significant — related
