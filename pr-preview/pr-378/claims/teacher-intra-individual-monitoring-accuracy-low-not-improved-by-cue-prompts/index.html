Evidence¶

Jan A. A. Engelen 2018¶

Jan A. A. Engelen, Gino Camp, Janneke van de Pol, Anique B. H. de Bruin. (2018). Teachers' monitoring of students' text comprehension: can students' keywords and summaries improve teachers' judgment accuracy? Metacognition and Learning 13:287–307. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9187-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 with 255 sixth-graders and 14 teachers: teachers gave text-by-text JOLs, seeing each student's keywords or summaries when present; monitoring accuracy was the intra-individual gamma correlation. The grand mean was "−.016, 95% CI [ −.113 .082]", and cue prompt had no significant effect, F(2, 26) = 2.586, p = .095.

"The grand mean of the model was −.016, 95% CI [ −.113 .082], suggesting that teachers' monitoring accuracy was not better than chance. The effect of cue prompt was not significant, F(2, 26) = 2.586,p = .095, suggesting that teachers' monitoring accuracy did not differ between cue-prompt conditions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The quality of sixth-graders' keywords is not diagnostic of intra-individual variation in test performance (Experiment 1).
  • Sixth-graders' own monitoring accuracy is low and does not differ between no-cue, keyword, and summary cue-prompt conditions (Experiment 1).
  • Teachers' inter-individual judgment accuracy (predicting differences between students) is well above chance (.528) even though their intra-individual accuracy is not (Experiment 1).
  • Teachers show stronger regulation than students: both select lower-JOL texts for restudy, but the JOL–restudy correlation is significantly stronger for teachers (Experiment 1). — related
  • Test performance differs by cue-prompt condition in Experiment 1, with higher scores in the no-cue condition than the summary condition. — related
  • The keyword-quality–JOL correlation increases after comparison with expert keywords, indicating learners exploit keyword quality as a cue only when given external standards — related
  • Organizational cues during a lecture have a large effect on understanding of lecture structure and memory for details — related
