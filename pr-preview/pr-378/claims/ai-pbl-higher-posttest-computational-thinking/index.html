Evidence¶

Ayanwale 2026¶

Ayanwale, M. A., & Omeh, C. B. (2026). AI-supported problem-based learning for enhancing computational thinking skills in STEM education. Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans 7 (2026) 100263. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2026.100263

q2 · i3 · causal · r2

One-way ANCOVA in a quasi-experimental pre-test–post-test non-equivalent groups study of 103 first-year undergraduates (51 AI-PBL, 52 lecture control), controlling Pre_CT_Total and gender. The adjusted marginal mean for the experimental group (M = 3.81, SE = 0.08) exceeded the control group (M = 2.40, SE = 0.08), "partial η2 = .681".

"The results revealed a statistically significant main effect of instructional group, F (1, 70) = 149.23, p < .001, partial η2 = .681, indicating a large effect size"

Ayanwale 2026 (2)¶

Ayanwale, M. A., & Omeh, C. B. (2026). AI-supported problem-based learning for enhancing computational thinking skills in STEM education. Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans 7 (2026) 100263. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2026.100263

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Simple main effects follow-up from the same ANCOVA study, reported in the Results section, showing the instructional benefit was "consistent across genders"; no effect sizes are printed for these follow-up tests.

"Further analysis using simple main effects showed that this group difference was statistically significant for both female students, F = 105.50, p < .001, and male students, F = 54.79, p < .001"
