Evidence¶

Wolf L.K. 2015¶

Wolf L.K., Bazargani N., Kilford E.J., Dumontheil I., Blakemore S.-J. (2015). The audience effect in adolescence depends on who's looking over your shoulder. Journal of Adolescence, 43, 5–14. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adolescence.2015.05.003

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Mixed-design ANOVA on accuracy data from 24 younger adolescents, 20 older adolescents and 18 adult females performing a relational reasoning task under alone, friend-present and experimenter-present conditions. The three-way interaction was significant (ηp2 = 0.09); "The adult group showed neither a main effect of social condition nor an interaction between social condition and task-level".

"There was a significant three-way interaction between social condition, task-level and age group, (F(4,118) = 2.91, p = 0.024, ηp2 = 0.09), which was followed up by separate social condition by task-level repeated-measures ANOVAs for each age group. The adult group showed neither a main effect of social condition nor an interaction between social condition and task-level (ps > 0.4, Fig. 2)."

  • Younger adolescents (10.6–14.2 years) show lower relational reasoning accuracy when observed by a friend than by an experimenter only in low-relational trials — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Across all age groups, relational reasoning accuracy is lower in the friend-present condition than in the experimenter-present condition overall — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The study found no evidence of age differences in relational reasoning performance, possibly due to design choices maximising sensitivity to audience effects — related
