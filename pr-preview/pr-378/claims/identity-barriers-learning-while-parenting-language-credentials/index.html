Evidence¶

Jacobs 2024¶

Jacobs, B., Babineau, K., Parker, D. (2024). From Childcare to Educare: Inspiring Change in Early Childhood Education for Rural Tennessee. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/222

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Thematic analysis of provider focus groups on the 'Learning While Parenting' theme found 'The lack of childcare support can contribute to higher dropout rates', alongside added expense, opportunity costs, accessibility limits, and emotional toll.

"The lack of childcare support can contribute to higher dropout rates, as participants may be forced to choose between their educational goals and their responsibilities as parents and caregivers."

Jacobs 2024 (2)¶

Jacobs, B., Babineau, K., Parker, D. (2024). From Childcare to Educare: Inspiring Change in Early Childhood Education for Rural Tennessee. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/222

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Focus-group thematic analysis found 'Many immigrant participants find that their foreign degrees and qualifications are not considered equivalent to U.S. standards', producing added time and financial costs; language barriers also hindered networking and certification exams.

"Many immigrant participants find that their foreign degrees and qualifications are not considered equivalent to U.S. standards. This can require them to retake courses or obtain additional certifications to meet TECTA program requirements."
