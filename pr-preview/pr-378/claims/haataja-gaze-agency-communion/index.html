Evidence¶

Nückles 2021¶

Nückles, M. (2021). Investigating Visual Perception in Teaching and Learning with Advanced Eye-Tracking Methodologies: Rewards and Challenges of an Innovative Research Paradigm. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09567-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Naturalistic multiple-person mobile eye-tracking study by Haataja et al. triangulating gaze behavior with continuous ratings of agency and communion during mathematics problem-solving; both teachers and students used gaze to take initiative.

"Teachers especially used gazes to express high agency, for example, in directing students'attention to specific aspects of the learning material."

Nückles 2021 (2)¶

Nückles, M. (2021). Investigating Visual Perception in Teaching and Learning with Advanced Eye-Tracking Methodologies: Rewards and Challenges of an Innovative Research Paradigm. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09567-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Same Haataja et al. naturalistic eye-tracking study; gaze expressing communion encouraged student initiative. The author notes the relative contribution of non-verbal gaze to relationship regulation remains an open question.

"with “friendly eye-contacts,” teachers also expressed communion and thereby encouraged students to take initiative in the problem-solving process."

  • Teachers' gaze scanpaths are more similar within an individual than between individuals, in both attentional and communicative gaze — related
  • Teacher scanpaths are most dissimilar across combined expertise-and-culture sub-groups, significantly for attentional gaze and marginally for communicative gaze — related

  • Expert teachers begin gaze sequences with student fixation and return to students throughout, whereas Hong Kong experts intersperse student scans that UK experts never use — related
  • Gaze-based assistance produced a significantly higher follow-up fixation rate on the spoken secondary AOI than the baseline — related
  • Only a minority of teacher educators interpreted a critical classroom-management incident as theoretically expected, despite marked eye-tracking differences — related
