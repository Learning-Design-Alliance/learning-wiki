Evidence¶

Kroesbergen 2023¶

Kroesbergen, E. H.; Huijsmans, M. D. E.; Friso-van den Bos, I. (2023). A Meta-Analysis on the Differences in Mathematical and Cognitive Skills Between Individuals With and Without Mathematical Learning Disabilities. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543221132773

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analytic unconditional model of 427 number-sense effect sizes from 82 studies. The pooled MLD deficit was "Hedges's g = 0.88", significant at p < .001; trim-and-fill adjusted it to g = 0.79.

"It showed that the weighted mean difference of Hedges's g = 0.88 was large and significant, with p < .001."

Kroesbergen 2023 (2)¶

Kroesbergen, E. H.; Huijsmans, M. D. E.; Friso-van den Bos, I. (2023). A Meta-Analysis on the Differences in Mathematical and Cognitive Skills Between Individuals With and Without Mathematical Learning Disabilities. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543221132773

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Moderator analysis of number-sense task type within the meta-analysis. Mapping tasks (reference category, intercept B = 1.00) yielded larger effect sizes than symbolic (B = –0.42, p < .001) and nonsymbolic (B = –0.58, p < .001) tasks; numeracy did not differ significantly from mapping (p < .080).

"A comparison between models with different ref- erence categories revealed that numeracy and mapping tasks produced the larg- est group differences, indexed as Hedges's g effect sizes."
