EERs can support the three basic psychological needs of self-determination theory: autonomy through multiple solution pathways and self-set sub-goals, competence through progress made visible by unlocking puzzles, and relatedness through collaborative pursuit of the common escape goal. → Vorderobermeier 2024

Evidence¶

Vorderobermeier 2024¶

Vorderobermeier, A., Abel, J., & Sailer, M. (2024). Theoretical foundations and approaches in research on educational escape rooms: A systematic review. Educational Research Review, 44, 100625. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100625

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review synthesizes how Abdul Rahim et al. (2022) and Cantwell et al. (2022) apply self-determination theory to EERs, describing how EER implementation "can support and enhance these needs, thus fostering intrinsic motivation". No effect sizes are reported.

"Autonomy in EERs is encouraged by players’ freedom in choosing how to solve given puzzles. To facilitate this, multiple pathways should be created in order to reach the final goal of the game."
