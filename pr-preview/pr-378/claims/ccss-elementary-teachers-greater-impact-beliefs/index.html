Evidence¶

Julia A. Gwynne and Jennifer R. Cowhy with R. Steven Quispe 2018¶

Julia A. Gwynne and Jennifer R. Cowhy with R. Steven Quispe. (2018). Getting Ready for the Common Core State Standards: A 2016 Update. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/getting-ready-common-core-state-standards-experiences-cps-teachers-and-administrators

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Spring 2016 My Voice, My School survey of CPS teachers, analyzed separately for ELA (n=4,098) and math (n=3,477) response groups. "just over 80 percent of elementary teachers" reported great impact on what they teach, versus around 45 percent of high school teachers; student-achievement impact was reported by just over 60 percent of elementary versus around 30 percent of high school teachers.

"In 2016, just over 80 percent of elementary teachers in both the ELA and math response groups felt that the CCSS would have a great deal of impact on what they teach"

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers reported feeling more familiar with and more prepared to teach the CCSS in 2016 than in 2015, with elementary and ELA-group teachers higher than high school and math-group teachers — related
  • Despite very different district implementation strategies for ELA and math, 2016 survey responses showed few differences between teachers responsible for each set of standards — related
  • Elementary teachers were more likely than high school teachers to report that standards-related professional development had extensive impact on their teaching practices — related
  • Teachers in 2016 were far less likely than administrators in 2015 to report that unaligned assessments, insufficient collaboration time, or inadequate PD were major barriers to CCSS implementation — related
  • Around half of teachers reported meeting in grade-level teams at least weekly and observing colleagues' classrooms at least quarterly to implement the CCSS — related
