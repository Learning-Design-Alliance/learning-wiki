Evidence¶

Elisabeth Graf 2024¶

Elisabeth Graf, Johanna L. Donath, Elouise Botes, Martin Voracek, Thomas Goetz. (2024). The Associations Between Discrete Emotions and Political Learning: A Cross-Disciplinary Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09893-y

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r?

Publication-bias assessment using an adaptation of Egger's regression test suitable for dependent effect sizes, plus funnel plots and publication-status moderators. The article "found significant, negative effects in the model of negative-activating emotions of cross-sectional designs, indicating funnel-plot asymmetry", and the same pattern for experimental positive-activating and anxiety models.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Anxiety shows no significant overall association with political learning in either cross-sectional or experimental designs — related
  • Funnel-plot asymmetry ran contrary to small-study effects because larger-sample studies reported larger effect sizes, likely reflecting longer training programs — related
  • Publication bias is evident for Negative Behaviour and Anxiety/Stress across all RCTs and for Mindfulness in active-control RCTs, suggesting overestimation of MBI effects — related
  • Publication-bias evidence differs by subset: significant funnel-plot asymmetry for course-embedded spacing studies but no strong evidence of bias for isolated-material studies. — related
  • Funnel plot asymmetry for openness, extraversion, and neuroticism appears attributable to reasons other than publication bias — related
  • Publication bias is present in the embodied vocabulary learning literature, suggesting pooled effects may overestimate the true benefit — related
  • Negative-activating emotions show small positive associations with political learning in both cross-sectional and experimental designs — related
  • Positive-activating emotions show a small positive association with political learning in cross-sectional designs, but not in experimental designs — related
  • Publication bias evidence is mixed: marginal asymmetry for tutees but no missing studies, and no bias for tutors — related
