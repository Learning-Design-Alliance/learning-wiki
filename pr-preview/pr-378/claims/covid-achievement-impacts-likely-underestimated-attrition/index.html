Evidence¶

Angela Johnson 2020¶

Angela Johnson, Megan Kuhfeld. (2020). Fall 2019 to fall 2020 MAP Growth attrition analysis. NWEA Research. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/fall-2019-to-fall-2020-map-growth-attrition-analysis/

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The authors' inference from the attrition analysis: since lower-achieving and minoritized students were more likely to be missing, observed fall 2020 results overstate the achievement of the full population, so COVID-19 impacts are "likely underestimated".

"The systematic differences between attriters and students who were tested in fall 2020 mean that the impacts of COVID-19 on student achievement are likely underestimated."

Related Claims¶

  • Systematic demographic differences in available fall 2020 assessment data suggest COVID-19 impacts on the most vulnerable students may be underestimated — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Students who did not retest in fall 2020 were disproportionately minoritized and lower-achieving compared with students who retested — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Approximately one in four students who tested the prior year were missing from fall 2020 data, disproportionately Black or Latinx students in higher-poverty schools, so prior estimates likely understated pandemic impacts — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Attrition was concentrated in schools with higher concentrations of racial/ethnic minorities and socioeconomically disadvantaged students — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The dashboard's cross-sectional view compares same-grade students across terms using the full sample of testers, but attrition is not random — related
  • Teacher attrition is approximately 55% higher in high-poverty schools than in lower-poverty schools — related
  • Longitudinal growth observed for current ELs likely underestimates total-cohort growth because students who reach English proficiency exit and are no longer assessed — related
  • Year 3 and Year 4 student achievement growth in mathematics and reading showed no significant differences between 2019 and 2020 cohorts in NSW primary schools — related
  • Longitudinal subject attrition produces systematic baseline differences among continuing, dropout, and non-participating students — related
  • Sensitivity analyses restricting to students assessed in all 15 terms reproduced the full-sample between-group comparisons — related
  • The dashboard's cohort view restricts results to students who consistently tested across selected terms, and those outcomes may be positively biased — related
  • COVID-19 school closures caused approximately 70% attrition on the primary outcome, limiting the trial's power to detect effects — related
