Zabolotna 2025¶

Zabolotna, K., Nøhr, L., Iwata, M., Spikol, D., Malmberg, J., & Järvenoja, H. (2025). How does collaborative task design shape collaborative knowledge construction and group-level regulation of learning? A study of secondary school students' interactions in two varied tasks. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09442-3

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Mann–Whitney U comparison of video-coded cognitive regulation episodes across the two tasks. The article reports "a significant difference between how it occurred in two different tasks (U = 67.0, p < 0.001)" favoring SimSketch.

"Similar results were obtained for group-level regulation of cognition—students engaged in this type of regulation more in the physics task than in the robotics one. A Mann–Whitney U test once again indicated a significant difference between how it occurred in two different tasks (U = 67.0, p < 0.001)."

Related Claims¶

  • Emotion–motivation regulation is the code most often connected to other codes in both tasks, linking to negotiation in SimSketch but to share/compare in Simua
  • Collaborative knowledge construction episodes are significantly longer in the conceptual physics task but almost three times more frequent in the robotics task — related
  • Cognitive regulation led to negotiation in the physics task, whereas in the robotics task negotiation more often preceded cognitive regulation — related
  • The robotics task prompted longer group-level regulation of emotion–motivation than the conceptual physics task — related
  • Edge weights between CKC–regulation codes differ significantly between the tasks only for the share/compare and disagree phases — related
