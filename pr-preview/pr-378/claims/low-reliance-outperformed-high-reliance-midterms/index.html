Evidence¶

Minju Park 2026¶

Minju Park, Ivan Orozco Vasquez, and Cristina Conati. (2026). Characterizing Students' LLM Usage Behaviors and Their Association with Learning in Critical Thinking Tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04534 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.04534

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive comparison of midterm scores between the High-Reliance group (7 students using LLMs in 50-100% of submissions) and the Low-Reliance group (16 students, 5-33% of submissions). Because group sizes were highly imbalanced, the authors "did not conduct statistical tests" and interpreted only the descriptive trends in Figure 7.

"On average, Low-Reliance group consistently outperformed the High-Reliance group across all three midterms, with a stable performance gap of approximately 7-8% throughout the term."

  • Students who used LLMs on assignments scored significantly lower than non-users on the first midterm (large effect size), but this gap narrowed and was non-significant on the later midterms — related
  • Individual trajectories suggest heavy LLM use focused on support other than full answer generation can coexist with improving scores, whereas reliance on LLM-generated summaries was associated with declining scores — related
  • Students whose LLM use was mostly student-driven support scored higher on Midterm 1 than LLM-driven users (about 10%), but the two groups converged on later midterms — related
  • In an unrestricted university course, only a minority of students (23 of 68, 34%) reported using LLMs, averaging 12% of discussion-point and 11% of critical-summary submissions — related
  • LLM usage varied across assignments and tasks, increasing for later assignments (especially discussion points), while Low-Reliance students concentrated use early and reduced it after Midterm 2 — related
  • Across the student body as a whole, no clear statistical trend was confirmed between AI usage levels and code comprehension — related
  • Students who implemented code without AI (usage level 0) maintained consistently high code comprehension, while level-3 AI users showed very little comprehension in the Mid period — related
