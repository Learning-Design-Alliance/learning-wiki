Culturally informed research agenda: re-evaluate motivation constructs within cultural socialization, test cultural moderation, and develop alternative East Asian frameworks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes directions for future motivation research grounded in its cultural analysis. Researchers should 're-evaluate the nature of widely used motivation constructs within the context of particular cultural socialization, to explicitly test cultural moderation in motivational dynamics, and to develop alternative concepts and frameworks that more accurately represent East Asian students' motivation.' The goal is refining and expanding motivation theories so their explanatory power broadens across diverse contexts.

Claims¶

  • The adverse effects of an external locus of causality on anxiety are less pronounced in collectivistic than individualistic samples [+W]
  • Extrinsic motivation to please teachers is positively related to intrinsic motivation among Asian American students but negatively among Caucasian students [+W]
  • Other-made choices produce similar or better motivation and performance than self-made choices for East Asian youth, contrary to Western choice findings [+W]
  • Parental pressure frequently predicts positive academic outcomes among East Asian children, including stronger mastery goals and higher self-efficacy [+W]
  • A sense of relatedness moderates East Asian learners' internalization of others' choices, with benefits of other-made choices diminishing without belongingness [+W]
