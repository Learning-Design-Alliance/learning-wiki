Minimise efficacy burden and foster trust elements such as data security and privacy to support AI adoption¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities, policymakers and Big Tech support successful AI adoption among Gen Z students by reducing the demands placed on learners' self-efficacy and by building trust. It states they "should minimise the burden on efficacy and foster elements of trust, such as data security and privacy". This follows directly from the finding that self-efficacy and perceived trust predict behavioural intention.
