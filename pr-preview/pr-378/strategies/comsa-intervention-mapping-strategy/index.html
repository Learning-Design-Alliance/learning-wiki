Using the CoMSA to formalize assumptions about how interventions map onto learning curve changes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The CoMSA offers a strategy for researchers to formalize assumptions about how educational interventions (motivational, cognitive, metacognitive) translate into changes on the learning curve. Researchers can hypothesize whether an intervention shifts the norm of study, willingness to study, slope, or metacognitive parameters, then simulate to predict where and when students benefit. This enables nuanced predictions about intervention effectiveness across different student and classroom contexts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit specification of which learning curve parameter an intervention is hypothesized to affect
  • Computational resources for simulation

Constraints¶

  • The model does not make theoretical commitments regarding how changes to the shape of the learning curve or motivation of the learner occur

Target Learners¶

  • Educational researchers designing interventions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Predicting intervention effects on achievement

Claims¶

  • The relationship between prior knowledge and knowledge gains shows context-dependent patterns in the CoMSA simulation [+W]
  • Not every hour of study time is equally productive; the benefit of an additional hour depends on the student's position on their learning curve [+W]
  • Simulated correlations between time-on-task and achievement vary substantially across contexts and can be negative even when time-on-task causally increases achievement [+W]
  • Simulated time-on-task achievement correlations resemble empirical distributions from K-12 classroom studies without any fitting procedure [+W]
  • Formal theory offers advantages over common sense for guiding implementation, but empirical evidence of effectiveness is lacking [+W]
