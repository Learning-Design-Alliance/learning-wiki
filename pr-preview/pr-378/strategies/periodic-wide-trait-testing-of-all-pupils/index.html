Teachers should periodically test all pupils in a wide variety of mental and physical traits to gain intimate knowledge of individual qualities and weaknesses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "every teacher should, at intervals, test all the pupils in his grade in as great a variety of traits, mental and physical, as possible." Such testing yields "intimate knowledge of and correct insight into the individual qualities and weaknesses" of pupils, explaining difficulties and removing obstacles that would otherwise remain mysteries. The article's own battery shows this can be done without extensive apparatus.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tests conducted at intervals on all pupils in the grade
  • A variety of simple mental and physical measures chosen from an available list

Constraints¶

  • Trait curves have only relative value under the particular study's arbitrary rank system

Target Learners¶

  • grammar-grade pupils in a single teacher's classroom

Target Learning Goals¶

  • diagnosing individual strengths and weaknesses to adapt grading, special interests, and educational processes to individual needs

Claims¶

  • Children in the same school grade show extreme ranges of ability in every physical and mental trait examined [+W]
  • Individual pupils' abilities vary widely across traits, showing little evidence of correlation of abilities [+W]
  • Seventh- and eighth-grade pupils overlap completely in ability, with no line of demarcation between grades [+W]
  • Ability distributions in grade-school children are non-normal, with ability clustering in the extreme ranks rather than the median [+W]
  • Group averages are misleading when applied to individual pupils because of extensive overlapping in weight and height across grades [+W]
  • Physiological age, as evidenced by the advent of pubescence or first menstruation, shows wide variations and low correlations with other traits [+W]
  • Physiologically accelerated children are mentally accelerated: height correlates with mental age +0.53 for 49 girls with age held constant [+W]
  • An extra year of school life produced no discernible improvement in the traits tested, with possible deterioration in addition and spelling [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use mental tests as supplementary evidence of pupils' abilities in dealing with the apparently bright and the apparently stupid
  • Detect suspected mental debility in children via a four-part test battery plus medical examination
