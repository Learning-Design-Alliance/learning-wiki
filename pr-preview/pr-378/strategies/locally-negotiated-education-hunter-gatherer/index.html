Distance from broad global solutions and design education around specific local needs, letting communities decide the purpose school serves¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's implications section recommends that to reimagine education for hunter-gatherer communities, educators and policymakers "must distance ourselves from broad global solutions and focus on specific local needs." It argues that until stigma is reduced and economic opportunities associated with schooling improve, attendance mostly does not fulfil its promise, and that individuals and communities should decide for themselves the purpose school serves and whether children should be compelled to attend.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recognition of local knowledge systems and community self-determination in education policy and school design.

Constraints¶

  • The review states that simply providing access to education does not straightforwardly lead to economic, social, and gender equality, and that historically embedded structural and cultural barriers play a critical role in continued educational exclusion.

Target Learners¶

  • Hunter-gatherer children and communities in the Global South

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Relevant, appropriate, and accessible education aligned with community-defined purposes

Affordances¶

  • Hunter Gatherer Schooling Challenges Framework

Claims¶

  • The direct and indirect costs of schooling are often too high for hunter-gatherer families, constituting an economic barrier to attendance [+W]
  • In hunter-gatherer communities, children themselves generally decide whether to attend school, reflecting the cultural value of childhood autonomy [+W]
  • The incongruence between stationary schools and mobile hunter-gatherer lifestyles, including distant, poorly maintained schools and abusive hostels, blocks school access [+W]
  • When hunter-gatherer languages are not used in schools, children often drop out because they do not understand the language of instruction [+W]
  • Schooling disrupts hunter-gatherer values, languages, and traditional knowledge, contributing to language loss and erosion of traditional knowledge transmission [+W]
  • Hunter-gatherer communities also view schooling positively, as a means of asserting rights, socioeconomic empowerment, and new social relationships [~W]
  • Social barriers—teachers from dominant groups, poor teacher-parent relations, and stigma and discrimination from teachers and peers—undermine hunter-gatherer children's schooling [+W]
  • Most students reported a high degree of agency in deciding which high school to attend, though agency was constrained or absent for some [~W]
