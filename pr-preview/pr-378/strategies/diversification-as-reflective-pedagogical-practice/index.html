Treat curriculum diversification as reflective pedagogical practice rather than content inclusion¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article concludes that diversifying the philosophy curriculum is best pursued through reflective pedagogical practice rather than simply adding diverse content. The authors state that "diversification is best understood as a reflective pedagogical practice rather than simple content inclusion," and suggest "exploring aesthetic approaches to evaluating transformative education." Teachers should therefore design experiences that help students engage responsibly with difference and reflect on how their pedagogy shapes curricular decisions.

Claims¶

  • Exposure to diverse philosophical perspectives is viewed by teachers as key to developing critical reflection [+W]
  • Teachers see diversification as deliberate design of experiences for engaging with difference, not curriculum expansion [+W]
  • University philosophy teachers prioritize fostering students' intellectual growth over transmitting content [+W]
  • Philosophy teachers are divided on transformative education, with some associating it with indoctrination [~W]
