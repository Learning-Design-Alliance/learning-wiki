Redesign course projects around collaboration, open-ended problems, shared knowledge objects, technology mediation, and as-needed mentoring¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article reports five design principles used to redesign the course: collaborative projects involving social interaction replaced individual assignments; open-ended and complex problems requiring inquiry were introduced; shared knowledge objects were requested as part of envisioned solutions; technology mediation via an online collaboration application replaced a course management system; and interactive mentoring and supervision sessions on an as-needed basis replaced lectures. The participating teacher was involved in the re-design, and students were encouraged to organize and manage their own projects using skills from prior research courses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher involvement in redesign
  • An introductory workshop introducing small-group projects, ill-structured problems, object-oriented collaboration, and online technology
  • Access to external clients

Constraints¶

  • Implemented and studied within one design-based research iteration in a single higher-education course

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • engagement in collaborative knowledge production
  • addressing open-ended, ill-structured problems

Claims¶

  • Groups performing generative epistemic actions more frequently produced higher-quality knowledge objects than a division-of-labor group [+W]
  • Group interaction profiles differed: Groups D and E favored generative actions while Group B's interaction was predominantly regulative [+W]
  • Productive interactions in long-term small-group collaboration take different forms, with discourse-based and object-oriented patterns the most relevant [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pedagogical scenarios that organise learning around knowledge creation with shared objects
  • Use open-ended, modeling-focused collaborative tasks with embedded scaffolds to support group disciplinary engagement
