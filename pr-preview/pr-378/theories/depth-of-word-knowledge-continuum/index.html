Depth of word knowledge continuum (association, comprehension, generation)¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article describes word knowledge as existing on a continuum of depth, with levels described as minimal, partial, and full, and, following Stahl, as association, comprehension, and generation. Verbal association connects an unknown word to a definition; partial concept knowledge permits limited use and limited meanings of multiple-meaning words; full concept knowledge includes using a word in new instances, inferring meaning from similar words, and discriminating among multiple meanings. The article uses this framework to argue that instruction must teach concepts, not just definitions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction that moves students beyond verbal association toward full concept knowledge, e.g., by connecting new information to previous knowledge

Constraints¶

  • The framework is presented in the article's background synthesis of prior vocabulary research rather than tested directly in this study

Target Learners¶

  • secondary students, including students with learning disabilities

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • deep conceptual knowledge of content-area vocabulary
