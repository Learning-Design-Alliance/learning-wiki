Instructors integrating generative AI into writing-intensive courses should have students critically understand, access, prompt, vet and integrate AI-produced content¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Drawing on Tate et al.'s study of an AI-facilitated writing tool in writing-intensive STEM courses, the review recommends enabling critical student use of AI. Students should be challenged "to understand, access, prompt, vet and integrate AI-produced content critically". The review also reports the study's best practices for instructors: setting clear learning goals, defining AI's role in the writing process, iterating curriculum design and reflecting on implementation outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors set clear learning goals and define AI's role in the writing process, per the best practices the review reports

Constraints¶

  • The strategy is reported for writing-intensive STEM courses as studied by Tate et al.; the review attributes it rather than testing it directly

Target Learners¶

  • students in writing-intensive STEM courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation and integration of AI-produced writing content

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate AI feedback as a supportive resource within process-oriented drafting cycles, with explicit guidance on critical evaluation
  • Embed AI in guided accept/reject/justify workflows, teach digital critical thinking explicitly, and integrate institutional authorship standards
  • Train teachers in prompt engineering, evaluating AI-generated responses, ethical and privacy considerations, and integrating AI outputs into pedagogical decision-making
  • Scaffolded AI integration with teacher training and reflective tasks to reduce over-reliance
  • Ground AI integration in higher education on core principles of human agency, academic freedom, transparency, ethics, inclusivity, and critical thinking
