Algebraic Thinking¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 39 claims (23 show a difference, 11 mixed, 5 no difference) · 27 studies (15 causal, 4 associational, 2 review, 2 qualitative, 2 theoretical, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), q1–q4 · 6 of 27 report an effect size · 36 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Algebraic thinking is the ability to generalise, represent, justify and reason with abstract mathematical structures and relationships: noticing that a pattern holds for all numbers, writing it with symbols, and arguing why it holds. The Learner Variability Navigator stresses that it is not only a secondary-school subject: in the primary grades it deepens understanding of arithmetic, for example by making the properties of operations explicit, and it helps students connect separate parts of their early mathematics. It is assessed with written early-algebra assessments and with researcher-designed measures that separate conceptual knowledge, procedural knowledge and flexibility in solving equations.

LVN follows it from grade 3 to grade 10. It links forward to geometric reasoning and statistical reasoning, where equations and functions describe shapes and data, and back to operations, number sense and fraction knowledge in proportional reasoning. It draws on working memory and general cognitive ability, and on reading comprehension for word problems.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Early algebra can be woven into arithmetic in the primary grades through generalising and justifying patterns.
  • Comparing multiple solution strategies for the same equation supports flexibility and performance.
  • Word problems add reading and language demands to the algebra itself.

Target Learners¶

  • Upper-primary and lower-secondary learners moving from arithmetic to symbolic algebra.
  • Learners with lower working memory or high mathematics anxiety, for whom algebraic word problems are harder.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Generalising and representing relationships symbolically; equation solving.
  • Understanding functions and their use in geometry and data.

Measures¶

  • Early algebra assessment (Blanton et al., 2015): Written assessment to measure students' understanding of core algebraic concepts and practices.
  • Contrasting Cases Algebra Assessment (e.g., Star et al., 2015): A researcher-designed measure of Algebraic Thinking that focuses on conceptual knowledge, procedural knowledge, and flexibility in problem solving.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • General Cognitive Ability: fluid reasoning relates to solving equations that call for algebraic thinking.
  • Working Memory: higher working memory goes with better performance on algebraic word problems.
  • Geometric Reasoning: shapes can be described by equations, so algebraic manipulation becomes a tool for geometry.
  • Communication Skills: reading comprehension and understanding of algebra's symbols and syntax relate to algebra performance.
  • Mathematical Flexibility: learning several strategies for equations builds flexibility and better algebra performance.
  • Proportional Reasoning: fraction knowledge relates to equation knowledge and predicts improvement in solving equations.
  • Statistical Reasoning: functions describe relationships in data, such as a linear trend.
  • Socioeconomic Status: average algebra scores differ by area income, a gap reflecting resources and opportunity, not a cause.
  • Spatial Ability: stronger spatial skills go with better performance on algebraic tasks.
  • Number Sense: understanding the properties of numbers is a foundation for algebra.
  • Operations and Place Value: algebraic thinking helps learners notice arithmetic relationships and the properties of operations.
  • Executive Function: cognitive flexibility relates to calculation and applied word problems that call for algebra.
