Evidence¶

Peter Seban 2025¶

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Tukey HSD post-hoc comparisons from the two-way ANOVA on the one-week delayed test. Both experimental approaches significantly beat single reading: MTR vs 1R at "p < 0.001" and RR vs 1R at "p = 0.039", each with printed 95% CIs. No standardised effect size is printed for these pairwise contrasts.

"For differences between MTR group and control group, it was p < 0.001, 95% C.I. = [− 14.25, − 2.95] and for differences between RR group and control group, it was p = 0.039, 95% C.I. = [− 11.48, − 0.18]."

Peter Seban 2025 (2)¶

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Same post-hoc analysis reported in the Results section: on the one-day delayed test the article found "nonsignificant differences in the means between the reading approaches and the control group"; equivalence was not tested.

"we found nonsignificant differences in the means between the reading approaches and the control group on the one-day delayed test, while on the one-week delayed test, differences between the experimental groups and the control group were significant."
