Evidence¶

Patricia D. Simon 2026¶

Patricia D. Simon, Lily Min Zeng, Luke K. Fryer. (2026). A systematic review of student engagement research in adaptive learning platforms. Computers and Education Open. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100360

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing included studies, that W-Pal lesson activity "decreased over time, which they attributed to student fatigue"; it also lists technical issues, boredom with long modules, limited content, and lack of teacher support as disengagement factors.

"Behavioral engagement (i.e., lesson activity as measured by the average completion percentage of each module) in the W-Pal decreased over time, which they attributed to student fatigue [50]."

Related Claims¶

  • Included studies suggest engagement increases when adaptive platform activities are tailored to learners' personal needs and emotional state — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Technical issues (login scanning, internet connectivity, projector setup, and glitches) frustrated students and created a significant barrier to playlist implementation — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Interviewed teachers attributed disengagement to technical, institutional, and social-relational environmental constraints rather than capability deficits — related
  • During observed playlist sessions, students were mostly on task and concentrating, though off-task behavior rose when the digital notebook was repeated — related
  • No significant impact of frustration or boredom (or anxiousness, discouragement, distractedness) on practice performance was found — related
  • Generalizable detectors of students' affective states in DLPs remain an unsolved problem — related
  • Frustration at not understanding a concept is perceived to motivate exploration and persistence, while frustration attributed to teachers or curriculum impairs motivation — related
