Evidence¶

Rovers 2019¶

Rovers, S. F. E., Clarebout, G., Savelberg, H. H. C. M., de Bruin, A. B. H., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2019). Granularity matters: comparing different ways of measuring self-regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09188-6

q2 · i3 · review · r2

Review reports Zhou and Winne (2012), in which goal orientations were self-reported via the AGQ and inferred in gStudy from hyperlink clicks and tag use while studying a hypnosis article; large divergences (d = 1.39–3.94) were found.

"For all goal orientations, there were significant differences between students'self-reports of their goal orientations and the traces collected in gStudy, with effect sizes ranging between d= 1.39 and d= 3.94"

Rovers 2019 (2)¶

Rovers, S. F. E., Clarebout, G., Savelberg, H. H. C. M., de Bruin, A. B. H., & van Merriënboer, J. J. G. (2019). Granularity matters: comparing different ways of measuring self-regulated learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09188-6

q2 · i1 · review · r3

Same review-reported study: traced goal orientations significantly related to posttest reading achievement, with correlation coefficients of r = .17 and r = .23, whereas self-reported goals did not; achievement was operationalized as text recall and comprehension.

"A significant correlation with reading achievement posttest performance was found for traced goal orientations (correlation coefficients ranging between rτ = .17 and rτ = .23), but not for self-reports"
