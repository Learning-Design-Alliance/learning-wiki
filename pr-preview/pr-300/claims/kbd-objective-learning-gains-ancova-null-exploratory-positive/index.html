Evidence¶

Stephan Vonschallen 2026¶

Stephan Vonschallen, Karim Kaufmann, Dominique Oberle, Friederike Eyssel, Theresa Schmiedel. (2026). Teachy Mini: Development and Preliminary Evaluation of a Knowledge-Based Generative Social Robot for Higher Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22345 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.22345

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA on the 7-item posttest with pretest score as covariate; the overall model was significant (F(2, 21) = 4.506, p = .024, 23.4% variance explained), but the condition effect was "not significant".

"The KBD group showed a mean learning gain of 0.75 points (SD= 1.54), compared to 0 points (SD= 1.54) in the control group. However, this effect was not significant,B= 0.447,t= 0.967,p= .344,95% CI ={−0.514, 1.407}."

Stephan Vonschallen 2026 (2)¶

Stephan Vonschallen, Karim Kaufmann, Dominique Oberle, Friederike Eyssel, Theresa Schmiedel. (2026). Teachy Mini: Development and Preliminary Evaluation of a Knowledge-Based Generative Social Robot for Higher Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22345

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Post-hoc exploratory linear regressions adding gender, age, education, prior robot experience, baseline competence, general motivation, group learning preference, and robot attitudes as covariates; the KBD coefficient was "positive and statistically significant".

"Further, after accounting for these covariates, the coefficient for the KBD condition was positive and statistically significant (B= 1.714,t= 2.355,p< .05,CI= [0.142, 3.285])."

Stephan Vonschallen 2026 (3)¶

Stephan Vonschallen, Karim Kaufmann, Dominique Oberle, Friederike Eyssel, Theresa Schmiedel. (2026). Teachy Mini: Development and Preliminary Evaluation of a Knowledge-Based Generative Social Robot for Higher Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22345

q2 · i? · causal · r2

In the same exploratory regressions, "Only learning preference had a significant impact on objective learning effectiveness", favoring group-preferring learners; an unadjusted moderator finding from a small sample.

"Only learning preference had a significant impact on objective learning effectiveness, such that participants who preferred learning in groups compared to learning alone had significantly greater learning gains (B= 0.667,t= 2.251,p= .042,CI= [0.027, 1.308])."
