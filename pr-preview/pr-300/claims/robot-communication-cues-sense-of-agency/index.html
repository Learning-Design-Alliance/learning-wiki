Evidence¶

Glawe 2025¶

Glawe, F., Schmeckel, T., Brauner, P., & Ziefle, M. (2025). Human Autonomy and Sense of Agency in Human-Robot Interaction: A Systematic Literature Review. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22271 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2509.22271

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports an experiment by Yu et al. on movement-path communication by delivery robots in urban spaces; participants felt pressured to comply with the robot's prediction of their own path, lowering their sense of agency.

"displaying a robot's predicted movement path increased sense of agency, whereas displaying the predicted path of the approaching human decreased it [80]"

Glawe 2025 (2)¶

Glawe, F., Schmeckel, T., Brauner, P., & Ziefle, M. (2025). Human Autonomy and Sense of Agency in Human-Robot Interaction: A Systematic Literature Review. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22271. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2509.22271

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Lombardi et al.'s experiment in which the robot's gaze direction mattered for sense of agency; the article attributes the effect to eye contact hypothesised to trigger self-awareness via self-referential processing.

"sense of agency was significantly lower for an averted gaze than for direct gaze (eye contact) [72]"

Glawe 2025 (3)¶

Glawe, F., Schmeckel, T., Brauner, P., & Ziefle, M. (2025). Human Autonomy and Sense of Agency in Human-Robot Interaction: A Systematic Literature Review. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22271. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2509.22271

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports two experiments examining robot facial expression valence; because valence was randomised, participants could not predict outcomes, which the authors align with prior findings on predictable versus randomised emotional outcomes.

"positive or negative facial expression of a robot was examined, but no effect on sense of agency was found [65, 72]"
