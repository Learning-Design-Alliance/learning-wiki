Jonathan A. Karr Jr. 2026¶

Jonathan A. Karr Jr., Grigorii Khvatskii, Ting Hua, and Nitesh V. Chawla. (2026). Why AI Detection Fails for Academic Integrity. Proceedings of the ACM AI Leadership Summit (AILS '26). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11256 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.11256

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Controlled scoring study of 642 OpenAlex abstracts and Gemini 3 Flash rewrites with Pangram 3.2 and GPTZero at τ=0.50. The article reports light-edit flag rates of "Pangram 64.4% (2013–15) and 80.1% (2023–25); GPTZero 37.6% and 48.5%" under human-source labeling.

"Refine (abstract only)flag rates equal1 −FNR in Table 3 (same scores, opposite proxy labels; Appendix B): Pangram 64.4% (2013–15) and 80.1% (2023–25); GPTZero 37.6% and 48.5%."

Related Claims¶

  • After humanization with Undetectable AI, fewer than 4% of AI-labeled rewrites remain detectable (FNR > 96%) for Pangram and GPTZero — related
  • A detector can achieve high aggregate accuracy while producing materially different over-detection rates across author groups — related
  • Applied to 1,163 real master's theses, Pangram flagged 529 (45.5%) as containing AI-generated content, mostly at low-to-moderate levels — related
