q3 · i? · associational · r3

Same multivariable model; maternal-reported SDQ total difficulties was 1.74 points higher (95% CI: 0.21, 3.27) for children sleeping <9 hours/day at 5-7 years versus the ≥10 hours/day reference. Higher scores indicate more difficulties.

"Children with sleep duration <9 hours/day at 5ŌĆō7 years also had worse maternal-reported scores: (BRIEF 2.90; 95% CI: 0.16, 5.63; SDQ total difficulties 1.74 points; 95% CI: 0.21, 3.27)"

Related Claims¶

  • In bivariate analyses, children sleeping <9 hours/day at ages 5-7 had higher mother- and teacher-reported BRIEF and SDQ scores than those sleeping ≥10 hours/day — related
  • Infants who slept 11 to <12 hours/day had worse teacher-reported but not mother-reported neurobehavioral outcomes at age 7 — related
  • Infants who slept <11 hours/day showed no association with mother- or teacher-reported neurobehavioral outcomes at age 7 after full adjustment — related
  • Children who slept <10 hours/day at ages 3-4 years had worse maternal-reported executive function and behavioral difficulties at age 7 than children with age-appropriate sleep — related
  • Teacher-reported neurobehavioral outcomes at age 7 were consistent with maternal reports for insufficient sleep at ages 3-4 and 5-7 years — related
  • Short sleep duration (<9 hours) at follow-up is associated with impaired academic achievement, while baseline sleep duration did not significantly predict later school performance — related
