Evidence¶

Mona Holmqvist 2011¶

Mona Holmqvist. (2011). Teachers' learning in a learning study. Instr Sci 39:497–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-010-9138-1

q2 · i? · design · r3

Learning study A (possessive pronouns, year 5): A2 used contrast between dependent and independent pronouns built on pupils' pre-understanding. Delayed post-test averages were A1 2.97, A2 2.94, A3 2.82, but A2 retained most relative to its post-test drop.

"The result showed how the pupils in group A2, who had not been introduced to the systematic structure (the tables with personal and possessive pronouns), had developed most knowledge in a longterm perspective."

Mona Holmqvist 2011 (2)¶

Mona Holmqvist. (2011). Teachers' learning in a learning study. Instr Sci 39:497–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-010-9138-1

q2 · i? · design · r3

Learning study B (to be in present tense): the author's interpretation that B2's contrast-based instruction produced more sustainable knowledge, despite B3 being most successful in the short term (post-test 16.5 vs 12.4 in B2).

"It seems as the instruction used in lesson two, where the pupils were offered to discern singular in contrast to plural, and did not know the words am/are/is in advance, developed a more sustainable knowledge."

Mona Holmqvist 2011 (3)¶

Mona Holmqvist. (2011). Teachers' learning in a learning study. Instr Sci 39:497–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-010-9138-1

q2 · i? · design · r3

Learning study C (to have): C1 used the contrast between am/are/is and have/has during the entire lesson; its delayed post-test (19.1) exceeded C2 (15.5) and C3 (16.8). The author links this to generative learning, a kind of transfer.

"The results show that learning in a long-term perspective was highest in C1, where the contrast between am/are/is and have/has was introduced."
