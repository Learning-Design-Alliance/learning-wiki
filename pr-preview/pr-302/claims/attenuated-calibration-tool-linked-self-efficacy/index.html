Evidence¶

Liu 2026¶

Liu, D., Fan, G., & Pan, L. (2026). Tool, tutor, or crutch?: A grounded theory of cognitive scaffolding and offloading in AI-assisted programming education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-025-00592-w

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Dyadic interview analysis of the AI-user group: 14 of 24 students described pride in writing perfect prompts, with self-efficacy tethered to managing the tool rather than reasoning from first principles. A contrasting case (Peng) used prompts as scaffolds for reasoning with test-first edits.

"A recurring theme in the interviews with more than half of the AI-user group (14 of 24) was a developing pride in their “prompt craft.”"

Liu 2026 (2)¶

Liu, D., Fan, G., & Pan, L. (2026). Tool, tutor, or crutch?: A grounded theory of cognitive scaffolding and offloading in AI-assisted programming education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-025-00592-w

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Comparative analysis of pre- and post-semester concept maps scored by two raters, triangulated with interview statements of dissonance between high project grades and perceived knowledge (e.g., fear of the AI-off final exam). The authors interpret these summaries "cautiously as converging, descriptive evidence rather than inferential tests".

"Figure 3 visually contrasts the average gains in concept map complexity, with the human-pair theoretical contrast group showing larger increases in cross-links and hierarchical organization."
