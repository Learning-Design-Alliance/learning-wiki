Assess social and group flow in real time through non-intrusive observation instead of interrupting learners with self-report surveys¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

To minimise disruption to flow and reduce recall bias from retrospective self-report, the study used a non-intrusive, real-time observational approach: two authors independently coded behaviours, verbal expressions and facial expressions against Walker's 7-point Joy–Sadness Display Scale, and used Armstrong's observable synchronisation indicators (leaning in, echoing gestures, completing others' sentences) to mark group flow. Interviews replaying video episodes triangulated the observations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Two independent coders and repeat coding to establish inter-rater and intra-rater reliability (here kappa 0.73 and 0.80); post-task interviews to check researcher interpretation against student perspectives

Constraints¶

  • Observational interpretation may be subject to researcher bias and misinterpretation, which the article addresses via interview triangulation

Target Learners¶

  • School students in collaborative technology-supported learning activities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Assessing quality of collaborative learning processes

Affordances¶

  • Cscl Quality Dual Spaces Group Flow Lens

Claims¶

  • Social flow and group flow were sustained and even strengthened as task challenge increased in the game's algebraic stages [+W]
  • In groups with pre-established positive collaborative relationships, NGI clusters aligned with high positive emotions and frequent group synchronisation during collaborative DGBL [+W]
  • NGI functioned as a mechanism for emotional coordination, restoring individual members' affective states through shared laughter [+W]
  • NGI did not appear to divert students from learning objectives; attention momentarily shifted but quickly returned to gameplay [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use two researchers as independent instruments for interpretation instead of one researcher confirming the other's reliability
