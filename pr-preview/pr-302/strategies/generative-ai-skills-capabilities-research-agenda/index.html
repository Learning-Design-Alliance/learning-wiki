Research agenda for generative AI in teaching and learning: identify needed skills, resources and capabilities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Across the expert contributions the article identifies questions for further research in three thematic areas, including teaching, learning, and scholarly research. A concrete strand asks, from a skills view, "which skills, resources and capabilities (both technical and non-technical) are necessary within organisations to better understand the limitations of ChatGPT within different contexts of use, and promote ethical decision-making". It further asks what role government policies, training providers, higher education and technology developers should play in developing these skills among a workforce whose jobs may be made redundant by robotic process automation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multi-disciplinary research examining ethical concerns surrounding black-box algorithms

Constraints¶

  • The agenda is a set of proposed research questions, not tested findings

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education students, educators, researchers and the human workforce

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding limitations of generative AI in different contexts of use
  • Ethical decision-making with generative AI

Claims¶

  • The skills required in a world powered by ChatGPT and related technologies will be different from those demanded previously [+W]
  • Generative AI platforms cannot yet independently lead meaningful innovation; at best they augment human intelligence for innovation [+W]
  • The review identifies three pathways of AI-empowered STEM education: technological leap to generative AI, integration into instructional systems, and value shift toward competency cultivation [+M]
  • AI's key contribution in STEM education is intelligent scaffolding that lowers the threshold for understanding knowledge, driving a shift from knowledge transmission to capability development [+W]
  • AI-supported design learning demands integrative capabilities, judgement, and learner agency rather than a single new skill [+W]
