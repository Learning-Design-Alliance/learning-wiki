Design adaptive teachable agents that promote sustained knowledge-building responses so learners of any prior knowledge can benefit¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's design recommendation is to build teachable agents that pose persistent, contextually relevant follow-up questions adapted to the tutor's responses, "scaffolding tutors' elaborations and prompting reflection". Because %KBR predicted post-test scores and was not predicted by prior knowledge, such systems "may help all learners––regardless of prior knowledge––improve their conceptual and procedural knowledge." The strategy targets the knowledge-telling default by mirroring the tutor's confusions and eliciting elaboration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The agent must sustain productive dialog with persistent, contextually relevant follow-up questions that adapt to the tutor's responses over time
  • Follow-up questions should target conceptual elements of an expected elaborated response that the tutor has not yet conveyed

Constraints¶

  • The observed relationships are correlational, not causal; the authors plan a randomized controlled trial before stronger design claims

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students with low prior knowledge in the taught domain
  • middle school students generally (grades 6–8)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • conceptual knowledge
  • procedural skill
  • knowledge-building over knowledge-telling
