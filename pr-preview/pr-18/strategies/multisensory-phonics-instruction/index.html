    Content plan:

    Description: Multisensory phonics instruction teaches letter-sound correspondences and decoding through simultaneous engagement of visual, auditory, and kinesthetic/tactile channels — e.g., tracing letters in sand while saying the sound. Rooted in Orton-Gillingham. Evidence: systematic explicit phonics is strongly supported; the multisensory component per se is less clearly the active ingredient.

    Design Implications: The strong evidence is for systematic, explicit phonics instruction [Ehri 2001]. The multisensory overlay may support attention and letter formation but evidence for modality-specific benefits is weak; benefits likely derive from added engagement, redundancy, and motor encoding rather than "matching" to learning styles [~W]. Avoid conflating with VAK learning styles [X].

    Requirements: - Systematic scope and sequence of grapheme-phoneme correspondences - Explicit instruction with modeling and immediate practice - Simultaneous VAKT activities (sand tracing, arm tapping, letter tiles) - Decodable text practice

    Constraints: - Multisensory activities can consume instructional time without adding learning beyond the phonics content itself [~W] - Evidence for the multisensory component specifically is weak; benefits come from systematic explicit phonics [~M] - VAK modality-matching (teaching to a learner's preferred modality) is discredited [X] - Excessive sensory embellishment can add extraneous load [coherence principle claim] [-M] - Less effective if not paired with connected-text reading; phonics alone without application

    Implementation Variability: - Orton-Gillingham, Wilson Reading System, Lindamood, Spalding, Jolly Phonics (UK), structured literacy - Intensity variations: Tier 2/3 small group vs whole class

    Target Learners: - Beginning readers K-2 [automatic word recognition claim] - Struggling readers / dyslexia — structured literacy [Galuschka] - Not particularly more effective for dyslexia than other systematic phonics? Galuschka found phonics instruction effective; no superiority of any specific approach.

    Target Learning Goals: - Alphabetic principle / grapheme-phoneme correspondence - Automatic word recognition → comprehension resources [claim link] - Decoding accuracy and fluency

    Instructions: 1. Follow a systematic scope and sequence... 2. Model the letter-sound with explicit instruction / demonstration 3. Add simultaneous multisensory routine: see it, say it, trace it 4. Practice with letter tiles / dictation 5. Apply in decodable text 6. Assess and reteach
