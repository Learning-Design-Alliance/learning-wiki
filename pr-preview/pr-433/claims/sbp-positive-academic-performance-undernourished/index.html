Adolphus, K., Lawton, C. L., and Dye, L. (2013). The effects of breakfast on behavior and academic performance in children and adolescents. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00425

Related Claims¶

  • Quality of habitual breakfast, in terms of variety of food groups and adequate energy, is positively related to school performance
  • Increased frequency of habitual breakfast is consistently positively associated with academic performance in children and adolescents — related
  • Regular breakfast consumption is associated with increased odds of high academic achievement in Norwegian adolescent girls and boys, most strongly in girls — related
  • Robust RCTs of school breakfast programs failed to find behavioral benefits, with low and variable attendance and a shift from at-home to at-school consumption limiting impact — related
  • Breakfast consumption shows a mainly positive effect on on-task behavior in the classroom in children — related
  • A low glycaemic load breakfast increases time on-task in young children, but the effect is short-lived and specific to the first observation period — related
  • A binding SBP mandate increases milk and fruit consumption and decreases soda consumption, but its effects on attendance are imprecise, suggesting the achievement effect operates through improved nutrition — related
  • A binding state mandate makes schools 33 percentage points more likely to offer the SBP, and SBP availability increases math, reading, and science achievement in ECLS-K data — related
