Evidence¶

Allensworth 2018¶

Allensworth, E.M., Nagaoka, J., & Johnson, D.W. (2018). High school graduation and college readiness indicator systems: What we know, what we need to know. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://ncs.uchicago.edu/freshman-on-track-toolkit

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's design analysis of setting-level indicators states that "summative indicators do not allow for quick feedback on practices" and that waiting a year to see whether new practices worked is not efficient. This is an analytical argument, not a measured comparison; no effect size is printed.

"However, summative indicators do not allow for quick feedback on practices. It is not efficient to wait a year to see if new school practices had the intended result."

Allensworth 2018 (2)¶

Allensworth, E.M., Nagaoka, J., & Johnson, D.W. (2018). High school graduation and college readiness indicator systems: What we know, what we need to know. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://ncs.uchicago.edu/freshman-on-track-toolkit

q2 · i? · review · r2

As a formative alternative, the review describes schools setting attendance goals and monitoring them "weekly or monthly to determine whether new practices are showing improvements in those indicators," since attendance is strongly related to on-track rates and available on an ongoing basis. Descriptive recommendation; no effect size is printed.

"Schools can set goals around attendance, and monitor them weekly or monthly to determine whether new practices are showing improvements in those indicators."
