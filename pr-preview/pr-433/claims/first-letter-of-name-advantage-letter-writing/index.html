Evidence¶

Puranik Cynthia S. 2014¶

Puranik Cynthia S., Petscher Yaacov, Lonigan Christopher J. (2014). Learning to Write Letters: Examination of Student and Letter Factors. Journal of experimental child psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.07.009

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Model 5 fixed effects for 5-year-olds. The first-letter-of-name dummy was significant with probability rising "from 50% to 83%"; no standardized effect size is printed.

"however, when the letter written was the first letter in the child’s name, the log odds increased by 1.55, translating into a probability increase from 50% to 83%, t(2645) = 4.01, p < .001."

Puranik Cynthia S. 2014 (2)¶

Puranik Cynthia S., Petscher Yaacov, Lonigan Christopher J. (2014). Learning to Write Letters: Examination of Student and Letter Factors. Journal of experimental child psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2014.07.009

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Model 5 fixed effects for 4-year-olds show both first-letter and later-letter name effects were significant, with probabilities rising from 7% to 30% and 7% to 12% respectively.

"when the letter written was also the first letter in the child’s name, a significantly higher probability was estimated, predicted probability = .30, t(2271) = 4.71, p < .001, translating into a probability increase from 7% to 30%, and when the letter appeared later in the child’s name, a significant effect was observed, predicted probability = .12, t(2271) = 2.89, p < .01, translating into a probability increase from 7% to 12%."
