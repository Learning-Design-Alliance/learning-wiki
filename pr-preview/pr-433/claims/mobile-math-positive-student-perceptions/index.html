Evidence¶

Fabian 2018¶

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Group interviews with the experimental group (minus two unavailable students) in this mixed-methods study found most students positive; students found tablets fun (n = 11), made learning easier (n = 5), useful (n = 3) and better than usual lessons (n = 3).

"Of that sample, 80% gave positive feedback, 15% gave mixed feedback and just 5% gave negative feedback."

Fabian 2018 (2)¶

Fabian, K., Topping, K. J., & Barron, I. G. (2018). Using mobile technologies for mathematics: effects on student attitudes and achievement. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9580-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive analysis of end-activity evaluation semantic-differential ratings showed "most of the activity ratings fall within the positive ratings with a range between 2.6 and 4.8", indicating majority positive evaluation of the activities.

"From the graph, it can be observed that most of the activity ratings fall within the positive ratings with a range between 2.6 and 4.8."
