Evidence¶

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis 2019¶

Casey Inez Canfield & Baruch Fischhoff & Alex Davis. (2019). Better beware: comparing metacognition for phishing and legitimate emails. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-019-09197-5

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Calibration-curve analysis (Fig. 2b) of pooled judgments across both experiments, phishing emails separately. Proportion correct was barely related to confidence except for expressions of certainty (100%).

"Participants were consistently overconfident for phishing emails, with their judged probability of being correct higher than their actual probability, except for the few times when they said that they were guessing (50%). For example, they correctly identified only 56% of the phishing messages when they were 90 –99% confident."
