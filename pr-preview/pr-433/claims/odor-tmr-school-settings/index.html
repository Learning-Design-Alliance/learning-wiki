Evidence¶

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann 2024¶

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann. (2024). An update on recent advances in targeted memory reactivation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8

q2 · i2 · causal · r?

The review attributes to Neumann and colleagues a 6th-grade study in which students studied German-English vocabulary with an incense stick at their desk and bedside. The printed effect sizes are "d = 0.6–1.2" in the TMR groups.

"Effect sizes in the TMR groups were quite large (d = 0.6–1.2), indicating that TMR does not only work in a lab environment but also in a regular school setting."

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann 2024 (2)¶

Julia Carbone & Susanne Diekelmann. (2024). An update on recent advances in targeted memory reactivation during sleep. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00244-8

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes to Vidal and colleagues a secondary-school history lesson paired with an odor dispenser at learning and the same vs a different odor during sleep at home. "memory performance of the history class was signi ﬁcantly improved"; no effect size printed.

"When students were presented with the same odor during the night at home, compared to a different odor in the control condition, memory performance of the history class was signi ﬁcantly improved."
