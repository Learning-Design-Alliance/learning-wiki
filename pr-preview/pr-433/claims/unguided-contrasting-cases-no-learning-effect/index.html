Evidence¶

Loibl 2020¶

Loibl, K., Tillema, M., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2020). The effect of contrasting cases during problem solving prior to and after instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09504-7

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANCOVA on quality of the best solution attempt in the 2×2 experiment (N = 181). Contrasting cases showed no significant effect on quality, and the conceptual posttest contrast PScc-I vs. PS-I was "not significant (p = 0.60)"; the overall contrasting-cases effect on conceptual understanding was F(1, 176) = 2.65, p = 0.11, η2 = 0.02. The authors note that non-significant results can only be interpreted with caution, so this is an absence of a detected effect, not evidence that contrasting cases cannot help.

"In contrast to Hypothesis 3c, the effect of contrasting cases, F(1, 176) = 0.53, p = 0.47, η2 < 0.01, and the interaction effect, F(1, 176) = 2.21, p = 0.14, η2 = 0.01, were not significant"
