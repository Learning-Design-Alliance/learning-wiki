The report offers this as an authors' argument for the educative value of human-scored performance assessment, listing benefits including collaboration across classrooms, schools, and districts; it presents no empirical test of these benefits.

"Participating in scoring activities can: allow teachers to participate in facilitated professional learning connecting standards to expectations on the assessment; provide opportunities to practice analyzing student work; encourage teachers to collaborate with colleagues across classrooms, schools, and districts;"
