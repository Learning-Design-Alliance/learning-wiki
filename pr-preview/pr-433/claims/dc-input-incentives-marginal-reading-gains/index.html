Evidence¶

Roland G. Fryer 2010¶

Roland G. Fryer, Jr. (2010). Financial Incentives and Student Achievement: Evidence from Randomized Trials. NBER Working Paper No. 15898. http://www.nber.org/papers/w15898

q3 · i? · causal · r2

School-level randomized trial in Washington, DC: 34 schools signed up, 17 treated, students in grades 6–8 paid $2 per point across five input metrics. The article reports a "marginally signiﬁ cant" reading effect and a non-significant math effect with controls (.152 (.092) and .114 (.106) SD).

"paying middle school students for various inputs to the educational production function such as attendance and behavior has a positive, though marginally signiﬁ cant, eﬀect on reading scores and a slightly smaller (not signiﬁ cant) eﬀect on math scores"
