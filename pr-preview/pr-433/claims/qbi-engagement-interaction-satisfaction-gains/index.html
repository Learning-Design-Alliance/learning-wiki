Evidence¶

Zamzami Zainuddin 2026¶

Zamzami Zainuddin, Michael Sailer, Shurui Bai, Madziatul Churiyah, Agung Winarno. (2026). Quiz-based inquiry: embedding incrementally sequenced questions to enhance engagement and learning in synchronous online lectures. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10614-1

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

One-way ANOVA on questionnaire scales (5-point Likert) across the three conditions (N = 90); Table 9 reports η² of 0.18 (engagement), 0.24 (interaction), and 0.17 (satisfaction), all above the article's stated large-effect threshold of 0.14.

"The results of the one-way ANOVA revealed statistically significant differences between all variables tested (p < .05), with large effect size, as shown in Table 9. The differences in learning engagement [F (2, 87) = 9.62, p < 0.05], student interaction [F (2, 87) = 13.854, p < 0.05], and learning satisfaction [F (2, 87) = 9.22, p < 0.05]."

Zamzami Zainuddin 2026 (2)¶

Zamzami Zainuddin, Michael Sailer, Shurui Bai, Madziatul Churiyah, Agung Winarno. (2026). Quiz-based inquiry: embedding incrementally sequenced questions to enhance engagement and learning in synchronous online lectures. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10614-1

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Tukey's HSD post hoc comparisons: each experimental group differed from control on engagement, interaction, and satisfaction (ps < .05), while experimental group I vs II showed no difference (e.g., p = .999 for engagement, p = .984 for interaction and satisfaction).

"Perceived interaction also showed substantial differences between experimental group II and the control group (p < .05), but not between the experimental groups (p = .984). Learning satisfaction revealed a significant difference between experimental group II and the control group (p < .05), with the two experimental groups showing no statistical difference (p = .984)."
