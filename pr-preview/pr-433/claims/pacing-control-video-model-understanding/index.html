Evidence¶

Wouters 2007¶

Wouters, P., Tabbers, H. K., & Paas, F. (2007). Interactivity in Video-based Models. Educational Psychology Review, 19, 327–342. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9045-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports a study by Schwan and Riempp (2004) in which learners saw a video about nautical knotting and could accelerate, decelerate, stop or repeat the video; the review attributes the finding that pacing control "resulted in a better understanding of the underlying processes and less practice time was needed."

"This control of pacing was heavily used, especially with increasing knot difficulty. The more difficult the knots became, the more use of control options was made, which resulted in a better understanding of the underlying processes and less practice time was needed."
