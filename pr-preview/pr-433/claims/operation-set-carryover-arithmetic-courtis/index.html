Evidence¶

Zirbes 1919¶

Zirbes, L. (1919). A Problem for Careful Research: How Does the Ability to Make Rapid Mental Adjustments Affect Test Scores and Pupil Achievements? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22How+Does+the+Ability+to+Make+Rapid+Mental+Adjustments+Affect+Test+Scores+and+Pupil+Achievements%22+Zirbes

q1 · i? · qualitative · r?

Error analysis of Courtis practice tests in grades IV, V and VI found process-carryover mistakes, notably on Test A—Lesson Thirteen; errors 'were most frequent in the first few examples of each kind'. Some pupils later caught themselves.

"The subtraction examples which follow addition examples were worked as addition. The multiplication examples which follow the subtraction were worked either as addition or an subtraction."

Zirbes 1919 (2)¶

Zirbes, L. (1919). A Problem for Careful Research: How Does the Ability to Make Rapid Mental Adjustments Affect Test Scores and Pupil Achievements? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22How+Does+the+Ability+to+Make+Rapid+Mental+Adjustments+Affect+Test+Scores+and+Pupil+Achievements%22+Zirbes

q1 · i? · causal · r?

Classroom intervention: a specially framed lesson mixed examples from Lessons 1-12 with exaggerated emphasis on shifting processes. Afterwards, according to the article, 'Test A was then repeated with success at first trial.'

"Pupils took longer to work this lesson or did not finish in the prescribed time. The practice effects noted were increased accuracy and increased speed."
