Related Claims¶

  • Older adults show lower JOL resolution only when recollection-based responses are contrasted with pooled familiarity and new responses, an outcome attributed to age-related decreases in strong recollective experiences at test — related
  • FOK resolution for general-knowledge information is similar for younger and older adults, whereas the literature argues for a deficit in FOK resolution for episodic memory tasks in older adults — related
  • Feeling-of-knowing resolution for episodic memory depends on underlying memory quality: repeated presentations raised FOK resolution for both age groups, with a larger benefit for older adults, and FOK resolution was at least as good for older adults tested with a 48-hour delay as for younger adults — related
  • Older adults show higher retrospective metacognitive accuracy than young adults in the selection-only task, especially on homogeneous unit-digit problems, but comparable accuracy in the selection-execution task — related
  • Eliciting confidence ratings leads to poorer metacognitive monitoring in older adults — related
  • Pre-monitoring and retrospective confidence judgments are highly consistent, with most agreement at the highest scale level regardless of correctness — related
  • Children's recognition choices overwhelmingly follow the alternative given the highest pre-monitoring judgment — related
  • Young and older adults make accurate retrospective confidence judgments but not prospective ease-of-selection judgments about their arithmetic strategy selections — related
  • Adults' confidence judgments track answer accuracy: confidence is higher on correct than incorrect general-knowledge trials (metacognitive monitoring) — related
  • Children's confidence in endorsing statements indicates they were not confused by hybrid statements — related
  • Students feel quite confident about their monitoring and regulation judgments even before self-scoring — related
  • Semantic integration of memorized words induces false memory: false-alarm rates are higher to semantically related lure probes than to unseen new probes — related
  • Older age is associated with poorer face recognition: lower d′, higher false alarm rate, and longer response time — related
