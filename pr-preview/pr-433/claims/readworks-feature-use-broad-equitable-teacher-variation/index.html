Evidence¶

Tare 2019¶

Tare, M. & Shell, A. R. (2019). Designing for learner variability: Examining the impact of research-based edtech in the classroom. Washington, DC: Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/82839c44-adb1-400c-b422-9bfbbca65985 doi:10.51388/20.500.12265/81

q2 · i? · design · r2

Feature-level log analysis in the district study (82 teachers made assignments; 1,857 students completed assignments; 1-to-1 student-to-device). Analysis focused on use of features collectively rather than individually.

"We found that 92 percent of students had used at least one of the LVP-supported product features over the course of the study and that 75 percent of assignments were completed with feature use. The most commonly used feature was the audio, followed by the split screen, which saw increased use over the first few months of the school year."

Tare 2019 (2)¶

Tare, M. & Shell, A. R. (2019). Designing for learner variability: Examining the impact of research-based edtech in the classroom. Washington, DC: Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/82839c44-adb1-400c-b422-9bfbbca65985. doi:10.51388/20.500.12265/81

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Null comparison of feature use across student characteristic groups in the district log data. The report concludes it was not the case that only students labeled as needing additional support used the features, suggesting students with varying strengths and challenges potentially benefited.

"we also examined whether any individual differences in student characteristic data related to differential use of the features (e.g., lower STAR scores, active special education status, lower income); however, we did not find any differences in feature use between the student groups"

Tare 2019 (3)¶

Tare, M. & Shell, A. R. (2019). Designing for learner variability: Examining the impact of research-based edtech in the classroom. Washington, DC: Digital Promise Global. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/82839c44-adb1-400c-b422-9bfbbca65985. doi:10.51388/20.500.12265/81

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Mixed-effects linear model of rate of feature use found teacher was a significant variance component (χ² = 368.03, df = 1, p < .001), with feature use varying from teacher to teacher by around +/-17.5%. The report says this is consistent with teachers playing an important role in students' edtech experience.

"The average rate of whether or not a student tried a particular feature varied significantly from teacher to teacher"
