Evidence¶

Sophie von Stumm 2022¶

Sophie von Stumm, Sophie Nicole Cave and Paul Wakeling. (2022). Persistent association between family socioeconomic status and primary school performance in Britain over 95 years. npj Science of Learning (2022) 7:4. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00120-3

q3 · i2 · associational · r3

Robustness re-analysis restricted to cohorts with more than 1000 participants (k = 11, N = 89,552) born 1946–2011, using the same adjusted meta-regression models shown in Fig. 2 panel (b) and Fig. 3 panel (b), mirroring the full-sample stability result. The printed correlation effect size averaged 0.30 [0.25–0.36], varying minimally from 0.30 to 0.31.

"After adjusting for cohort-specific confounders, family SES correlated on average 0.30 [0.25 –0.36] with children’s school performance (Fig. 2, panel (b); full model details are in the SI). The associations varied minimally from 0.30 to 0.31 across cohorts."
