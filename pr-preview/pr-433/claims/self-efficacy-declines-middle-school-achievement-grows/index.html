Soland 2019¶

Soland, J. (2019). English Language Learners, Self-efficacy, and the Achievement Gap: Understanding the Relationship between Academic and Social-emotional Growth (The Collaborative for Student Growth at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/english-language-learners-self-efficacy-and-the-achievement-gap-understanding-the-relationship-between-academic-and-social-emotional-growth/

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Descriptive plots of mean self-efficacy, math, and reading scores by year and ELL status (Figure 2) from a single-grade middle school cohort in a large urban California district. The article reports "a steady decline in self-efficacy and incline in achievement between 5th and 8th grade"; no effect size for the decline is printed.

"Before turning to subgroup differences, one should note the steady decline in self-efficacy and incline in achievement between 5th and 8th grade. Thus, self-efficacy consistently declines for all students even while achievement grows steadily."

Related Claims¶

  • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
