Evidence¶

Fred Paas & John Sweller 2011¶

Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2011). An Evolutionary Upgrade of Cognitive Load Theory: Using the Human Motor System and Collaboration to Support the Learning of Complex Cognitive Tasks. Educ Psychol Rev (2012) 24:27–45. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-011-9179-2

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The article's own boundary-condition argument, citing Leahy and Sweller (2011): because spoken information is transitory, it must be low in element interactivity to be held in working memory, so the audiovisual advantage is limited to spoken text of low content and complexity.

"The advantage of using the biologically primary knowledge associated with listening and looking simultaneously may disappear when dealing with lengthy, complex, spoken text. Under those circumstances, the modality effect may disappear or even reverse, with written text being superior to spoken text (Leahy and Sweller 2011)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Transient information effect: lengthy spoken text can reverse the modality effect, while shorter spoken text restores the conventional modality advantage — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Presentation pacing moderates the modality effect: large under system-paced conditions and not significant under self-paced conditions, where it may disappear or reverse — related
  • Presenting words as spoken narration rather than on-screen text alongside graphics improves learning — related
  • Element interactivity and expertise reversal effects: cognitive load effects diminish and may reverse as expertise increases because known elements chunk into single elements — related
  • The review reports that the modality effect reverses with expertise: audio-visual presentations helped inexperienced learners, but after further training a diagram-only condition showed a substantial advantage — related
  • Working-memory limitations can be circumvented by schema construction, automation, and multiple presentation modalities — related
