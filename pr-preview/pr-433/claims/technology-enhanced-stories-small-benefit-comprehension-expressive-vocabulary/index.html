Evidence¶

Takacs 2015¶

Takacs, Z. K., Swart, E. K., & Bus, A. G. (2015). Benefits and Pitfalls of Multimedia and Interactive Features in Technology-Enhanced Storybooks: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 85(4), 698-739. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314566989

q3 · i0 · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-analytic synthesis of 43 experimental or quasi-experimental studies contrasting technology-enhanced stories with traditional story presentations, using a random effects model. The pooled effect for story comprehension was g+ = 0.17, 95% CI [0.01, 0.34], p = .04 (Table 2); the printed effect size g+ = 0.17 falls below d = 0.2.

"A small but significant additional benefit of technology was found for story comprehension (g+ = 0.17) and expressive vocabulary (g+ = 0.20), based on data from 2,147 children in 43 studies."

Takacs 2015 (2)¶

Takacs, Z. K., Swart, E. K., & Bus, A. G. (2015). Benefits and Pitfalls of Multimedia and Interactive Features in Technology-Enhanced Storybooks: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 85(4), 698-739. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314566989

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

Same meta-analytic synthesis (43 studies, 2,147 children, random effects model). The pooled effect for expressive vocabulary was g+ = 0.20, 95% CI [0.01, 0.39], p = .04 (Table 2); the printed effect size g+ = 0.20 is small.

"A small but significant additional benefit of technology was found for story comprehension (g+ = 0.17) and expressive vocabulary (g+ = 0.20), based on data from 2,147 children in 43 studies."
