q2 · i? · design · r2

Log analysis of the Aalto CS1 deployment on the safe_integer Parsons problem. Among students with incorrect attempts, "50% of students with incorrect attempts substituted return for raise ValueError", which the authors interpret as treating the error as a silent exit rather than an exceptional event.

"At Aalto University, this pattern was prominent insafe_integer (P2), where 50% of students with incorrect attempts substituted return for raise ValueError , treating a parse failure as a silent exit rather than an exceptional event."

Related Claims¶

  • Exception-type confusion was the dominant difficulty pattern in SafeDivide at the University of Houston (90% of incorrect attempts) and persisted across harder problems in both deployments — related
  • Deploying a trace-capturing Parsons tool in two university courses surfaced recurring difficulty patterns including exception-type misidentification, return-for-throw confusion, and incorrect control-flow ordering — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Control-flow ordering errors appeared across all UH problems (43–52% of incorrect attempts) but were only a secondary pattern at Aalto, with the conceptual barrier apparently shifting with problem structure — related
  • State revisits within solving sessions were rarer in the Pulla deployments (6–12% of UH sessions, 0–3% at Aalto) than the 21–33% previously reported for similar Parsons tasks — related
  • Student specification comments are predominantly natural language, except Return statements which show a high proportion of Strict-Code expressions — related
