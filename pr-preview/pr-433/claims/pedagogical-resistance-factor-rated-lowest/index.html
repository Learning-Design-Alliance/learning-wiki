Evidence¶

Hadad 2020¶

Hadad, S., Meishar-Tal, H., & Blau, I. (2020). The parents' tale: Why parents resist the educational use of smartphones at schools?. Computers & Education, 157, 103984. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103984

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

Repeated-measures ANOVA on the Resistance Factors Questionnaire (n=220); Bonferroni comparisons showed the "pedagogical factor (M=2.83) was rated significantly lower than social (M=3.57), economic (M=3.45) and environmental resistance factors", with pη2=.232.

"Analysis of variance with repeated measures showed that a significant difference was found between the four resistance factors F (3,216) =73.80, p=.000, pη2=.232."

Hadad 2020 (2)¶

Hadad, S., Meishar-Tal, H., & Blau, I. (2020). The parents' tale: Why parents resist the educational use of smartphones at schools?. Computers & Education, 157, 103984. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103984

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Separate repeated-measures ANOVAs per resistance group (Table 4): non-resistance F(3,231)=41.81, passive F(3,195)=18.09, active F(3,225)=28.14, all p=.000; among "passive and active resistance groups, social resistance overcame environmental resistance". No effect sizes beyond pη2 printed in table.

"Pedagogical resistance factor was found to be rated the lowest at all parental resistance levels. In addition, among parents who reported non -resistance, environmental resistance factor was rated lower compared to social and economic resistance factors."
