Xingliang Chen & Antonija Mitrovic & Moffat Mathews 2019¶

Xingliang Chen & Antonija Mitrovic & Moffat Mathews. (2019). Investigating the Effect of Agency on Learning from Worked Examples, Erroneous Examples and Problem Solving. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education (2019) 29:396–424. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00179-x

Generalized mixed model with normalized learning gain as response, agency group and prior-knowledge level (LPK/HPK) as between-subject factors, in the same 40-participant SQL-Tutor study. The article reports the level main effect, F(1,35) = 5.87, p = .021, with effect size ƞ2 = .144.

Related Claims¶

  • HPK students improved significantly only in the Low-Agency condition, with a large post-test advantage over HPK peers in High-Agency (d = .95) — related
  • All training policies narrowed the prior-knowledge achievement gap from pretest to posttest, with BKT achieving the largest reduction (77.1%) — related
  • High-ability students' learning behaves as a dominant α-process even at low pretest scores, while average-ability students show more γ-process behavior — related
  • Under a dominant α-process, the normalized gain contains no pretest-score term and is uncorrelated with pretest score if α is uncorrelated with pretest score — reports the opposite
  • When the γ-process (associative interaction of correct and incorrect knowledge) is considered, the normalized gain correlates positively with pretest score — reports the opposite
  • Pre-instruction LCTSR scores correlate with normalized learning gains, most strongly for theoretical content (TUG-K r=0.59) and more weakly for descriptive content (DIRECT r=0.50) — related
  • High- and Low-Agency conditions in SQL-Tutor do not differ in learning outcomes, though both improve significantly from pre- to post-test — related
  • Students with low prior knowledge show significantly higher proportional learning gains than students with high prior knowledge when learning with MetaTutor — related
