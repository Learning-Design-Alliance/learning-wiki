Evidence¶

Minkang Kim 2021¶

Minkang Kim, Jean Decety, Ling Wu, Soohyun Baek and Derek Sankey. (2021). Neural computations in children's third-party interventions are modulated by their parents' moral values. npj Science of Learning 6:38. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00116-5

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Repeated-measures ANOVA on EEG data from 42 children with usable ERP data, with Group (Protester vs. Non-Protester) as between-subject and CMST Condition (Help vs. Harm) as within-subject variables. The article reports a "signi ﬁcant Group × Condition interaction effect" with f = 0.39; no standardised effect size for the interaction itself is printed.

"However, between Protesters (N = 15) and Non-Protesters ( N = 27), there was signi ﬁcant Group × Condition interaction effect, F(1, 40) = 6.063; p = 0.018, power = 0.694, f = 0.39"

Minkang Kim 2021 (2)¶

Minkang Kim, Jean Decety, Ling Wu, Soohyun Baek and Derek Sankey. (2021). Neural computations in children's third-party interventions are modulated by their parents' moral values. npj Science of Learning 6:38. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00116-5

q3 · i2 · associational · r2

Post-hoc comparison within the Non-Protest group of the same ERP analysis. The article reports the harm-vs-help P2 difference "with a med-ium effect size (power = 0.826, d = 0.568)"; in the Protest group the difference was not significant (d = 0.11).

"In the Non-Protest group, the mean P2 amplitude in response to viewing harming scenes ( M = 2.358) was smaller than the mean amplitude of viewing helping scenes ( M = 3.494), t(27) = 4.133, p < 0.001 < 0.05/2, with a med-ium effect size (power = 0.826, d = 0.568)"
