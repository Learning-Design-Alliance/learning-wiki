Evidence¶

Congdon Eliza L. 2017¶

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Next-day follow-up of the randomized three-condition experiment (24 children per condition) testing basic problems after a binomial mixed-effects regression; the simultaneous condition beat both sequential conditions, with no difference between the sequential conditions (β = 0.50, p = .80).

"Here, unlike performance at the immediate test, there was a significant effect of condition (χ2 (2) = 42.29, p < .001), with children in the S G condition performing better than children in both the S S condition (β = 17.76, z = 6.17, p < .001) and the S G condition (β = 17.26, z = 5.98, p < .001)."

Congdon Eliza L. 2017 (2)¶

Congdon Eliza L., Novack Miriam A., Brooks Neon, Hemani-Lopez Naureen, O'Keefe Lucy, Goldin-Meadow Susan. (2017). Better together: Simultaneous presentation of speech and gesture in math instruction supports generalization and retention. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2017.03.005

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same experiment, generalization problems at next-day follow-up: significant condition effect, with the simultaneous speech-with-gesture group outperforming both sequential groups; the two sequential groups did not differ (β = 0.72, p = .73).

"Performance on the generalization problems on the next day follow-up showed a similar pattern as the basic problems. We again found a significant effect of condition (χ2 (2) = 31.72, p < .001), with children in the S G condition performing better than children in both the S S condition (β = 16.27, z = 5.56, p < .001) and the S G condition (β = 15.54, z = 4.87, p < .001)."
