Descriptive comparison across entering cohorts in the Clearinghouse data. The report computes the average persistence-retention gap and states "nearly one in eight students who start college in any fall term, transfer to a different institution by the following fall."

"For the included years, the overall persistence rate is about 1 3 percentage points higher, on average, than the retention rate. Thus, nearly one in eight students who start college in any fall term, transfer to a different institution by the following fall."

Related Claims¶

  • In four-year private institutions, white and Asian students had nearly identical persistence rates (89.8 and 89.6 percent), but Asian students were more likely to continue at their starting institution (82.5 percent)
  • Sector persistence rates for fall 2015 entrants: 81.7 percent at four-year public, 84.9 percent at four-year private, 62.7 percent at two-year public, and 55.7 percent at four-year for-profit institutions — related
  • In two-year public institutions, white students had a lower retention rate (50.2 percent) than Hispanic students (55.1 percent) because a higher share of white students transferred to four-year institutions — related
  • Of students who started college in fall 2015, 73.4 percent persisted at any U.S. institution and 61.1 percent were retained at their starting institution in fall 2016 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
