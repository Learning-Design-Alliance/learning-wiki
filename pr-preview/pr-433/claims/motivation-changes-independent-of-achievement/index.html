Evidence¶

Lehikoinen 2025¶

Lehikoinen, H., Väisänen, P., Havu-Nuutinen, S., Lappalainen, K., & Niemivirta, M. (2025). Developmental relations between mathematics self-concept, interest, and achievement: A comparison of solo- and co-taught classes. Instructional Science, 53, 239–261. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09678-4

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Conditional parallel process model (N = 146) with teaching condition, prior test performance, prior grade, and gender as predictors. Only prior teacher-rated grade predicted the onset of self-concept (β = 0.61, p < .001); the article reports changes were "independent of previous achievement".

"Contrary to our expectation, teaching condition was not predictive of changes in self-concept and interest."

Lehikoinen 2025 (2)¶

Lehikoinen, H., Väisänen, P., Havu-Nuutinen, S., Lappalainen, K., & Niemivirta, M. (2025). Developmental relations between mathematics self-concept, interest, and achievement: A comparison of solo- and co-taught classes. Instructional Science, 53, 239–261. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09678-4

q2 · i? · causal · r1

In the full model with end-of-year test performance and teacher-rated grade as outcomes, neither levels nor slopes of self-concept and interest significantly predicted later mathematics performance. Table 4 prints the standardised path coefficients, all nonsignificant: self-concept level β = 0.14 (test) and 0.17 (grade), self-concept slope β = −0.01 and 0.16, interest level β = −0.04 and 0.00, interest slope β = 0.04 and −0.11 (all p > .18).

"against our assumptions, neither the initial levels nor changes in self-concept and interest predicted later mathematics performance"
