Evidence¶

Sowmya Vajjala 2016¶

Sowmya Vajjala. (2016). Automated assessment of non-native learner essays: Investigating the role of linguistic features. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00729

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Comparison of ReliefF top-10 feature lists across the two corpora. The two datasets share "only with respect to the lexical diversity features (TTR and CTTR)"; co-reference chain features were best for FCE but not TOEFL11.

"It is interesting to note that compared to the best features on TOEFL11S UBSET , there is an overlap only with respect to the lexical diversity features (TTR and CTTR)."

Sowmya Vajjala 2016 (2)¶

Sowmya Vajjala. (2016). Automated assessment of non-native learner essays: Investigating the role of linguistic features. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00729

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Error features also diverge: percentage of spelling errors was most predictive for TOEFL11SUBSET, while non-spelling and all errors were most predictive for FCE.

"With respect to the error features, while percentage of spelling errors was the most predictive feature or TOEFL11S UBSET , non-spelling errors and all errors were the most predictive for FCE."

  • SHAP analysis identifies Hapax Ratio as the most influential stylometric feature in Random Forest predictions, followed by NSR, Noun Ratio, Adverb Ratio, and TTR — related

  • Removing native language (L1) sharply degrades discourse-feature scoring on TOEFL11 but not on FCE — related
