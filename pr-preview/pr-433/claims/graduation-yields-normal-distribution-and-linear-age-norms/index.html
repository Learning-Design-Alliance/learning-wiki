Evidence¶

Otis 1918¶

Otis, A. S. (1918). An absolute point scale for the group measurements of intelligence. Part I. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 9(5), 239-261. https://books.google.com/books/about/the_journal_of_educational_psychology.html

q2 · i? · design · r2

A graphical graduation procedure in which preliminary point values were assigned to scores by rank (the 30th individual given 40 points, the 90th given 60) and median point values across the ten test distributions were read at selected rank positions; the printed result is that the values "indicate that the distribution of abilities of the 121 pupils approximately normal".

"It should be stated that these values indicate that the distribution of abilities of the 121 pupils approximately normal."

Otis 1918 (2)¶

Otis, A. S. (1918). An absolute point scale for the group measurements of intelligence. Part I. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 9(5), 239-261. https://books.google.com/books/about/the_journal_of_educational_psychology.html

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Age norms were found by averaging total point scores of pupils within overlapping age windows and plotted in Fig. 2. The author reports that, unexpectedly, "the points representing the norms for ages 10 to 14 lay in almost a perfectly straight line", despite the omission of the fifth and seventh grades from the group tested.

"To our surprise, the points representing the norms for ages 10 to 14 lay in almost a perfectly straight line, which suggests that they are fairly reliable, at least, for the school population tested."
