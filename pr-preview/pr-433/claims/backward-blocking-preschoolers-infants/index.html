Evidence¶

Gopnik 2012¶

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Sobel et al. (2004): children saw A alone activate the machine, then A and B together; because A alone sufficed, children judged B less likely to be a blicket. The article notes this inference is probabilistic and hard for associationist theories to explain.

"Sobel et al. (2004) found that preschool children would also make correct causal inferences from more complex statistical patterns, particularly “backward blocking”."

Gopnik 2012 (2)¶

Gopnik, A., & Wellman, H. M. (2012). Reconstructing constructivism: Causal models, Bayesian learning mechanisms and the theory theory. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1085-1108. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028044

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review attributes to Sobel and Kirkham (2007) backward blocking at 18 months and, in an anticipatory looking task, apparent covariation-based causal inference even in 9-month-olds.

"Sobel and Kirkham (2007) found that children as young as 18 months old also showed similar capacities for backward blocking."
