Evidence¶

Matthew L. Bernacki 2023¶

Matthew L. Bernacki, Megan Claire Cogliano, Shelbi L. Kuhlmann, Jenifer Utz, Christy Strong, Jonathan C. Hilpert, Jeffrey A. Greene. (2023). Relations between undergraduates’ self-regulated learning skill mastery during digital training and biology performance. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09356-9

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Study 1 path analysis of n = 49 treatment-condition students who completed all three training modules, scored by rubric for skill mastery. The authors report that "Behavioral and environmental regulation knowledge predicted midterm and final exam grades; knowledge of SRL processes did not." No effect size is printed for this path model finding.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Completing the Science of Learning to Learn training conferred an effect of about one-third of a letter grade (d ~ 0.3) on subsequent quizzes and exams in the original randomized study
  • Declarative and conceptual levels of learning-skill mastery predicted biology exam performance, but application-level knowledge did not
  • Skill-mastery predictions of biology exam performance were sustained in a redesigned video-based multimedia training prioritizing learning efficiency — related
  • Training activity scores in cognitive, metacognitive, and behavioral/environmental regulation each significantly related to subsequent exam performance after controlling for prior knowledge — related
