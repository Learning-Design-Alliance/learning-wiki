Evidence¶

Jochem E. J. Aben 2024¶

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative thematic analysis of think-aloud and interview data from twelve students. While "ten of the twelve students (all, except John and Anthony) strongly argued in the interviews that they were not affected" during provision, six students spontaneously referenced the recipient's perceived language skills or effort, e.g., noting the peer "has in general, let's say, a higher level of writing skills".

"half the students (Mary, James, Steven, Sarah, Daniel, Anthony) showed awareness of the feedback recipient, without being prompted by one of the research-assistants."

Jochem E. J. Aben 2024 (2)¶

Jochem E. J. Aben, Mayra Mascareño Lara, Anneke C. Timmermans, Filitsa Dingyloudi, Jan-Willem Strijbos. (2024). The impact of interpersonal perceptions on the process of dealing with errors while providing and processing peer-feedback on writing. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09660-0

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Quantitative content analysis of think-aloud transcripts coded for effects of interpersonal relationship: only Mary once made a remark coded as perceived effort and Thomas once a remark coded as friendship; effects surfaced mainly in interviews and off-task utterances.

"Almost no effects of inter- personal perceptions were visible in the think-aloud data."
