Evidence¶

John Chick 2026¶

John Chick, Laura Morello and Stephanie Staffey. (2026). From fear to innovation: a case study of transformative faculty development for ethical AI integration in higher education. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00225-x

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Pre/post survey comparison of ten institute participants. The article reports that high-confidence responses increased by 60 percentage points, "from 0% to 60%", with 90% at moderate/high confidence post-institute; no effect size or significance test is printed.

"Confidence in helping students use AI was flipped from pre to post: while participants responded overwhelmingly with low confidence or unsure before the institute (80%), most participants responded with moderate/high confidence after the institute (90%)"

Related Claims¶

  • Entering faculty were characterized by uncertainty rather than opposition: mean AI perception was 3.22 on a five-point scale and mean self-rated fluency was 1.89 on a six-point scale — related
  • Faculty concerns centered on academic integrity (80%) and student over-reliance on AI (70%), and their most requested development topic was AI ethics and academic integrity (70%) — related
  • Faculty transformation followed four themes: fear shifting to curiosity, desire for ethical clarity, inclusive design as equity amplifier, and evolution from gatekeepers to guides — related
  • Participants' perceptions of AI in higher education shifted from 20% negative and 40% neutral pre-institute to 0% negative and 90% positive post-institute — related
  • Retrospective pre/post confidence rose on all four course learning objectives with large rank-biserial effects (N=159) — related
  • Paired pre/post AI readiness scores rose descriptively from 82.5% to 89.9% (mean gain 7.4 percentage points) among seven co-creation clients, treated as descriptive context only — related
  • AI-supported lab manual design increased students' confidence in troubleshooting and data analysis — related
  • The simulation increased teacher candidates' confidence and self-efficacy for addressing sensitive topics, but did not alleviate concerns about parent and administrator backlash — related
