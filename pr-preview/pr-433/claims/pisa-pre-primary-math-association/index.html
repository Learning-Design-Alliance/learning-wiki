Evidence¶

L.M. Richter et al 2021¶

L.M. Richter et al. (2021). Measuring and forecasting progress in education: what about early childhood? npj Science of Learning 6:27. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00106-7

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Cross-sectional multivariate regression of 2018 PISA data for 430,264 adolescents in 73 middle- and high-income countries, controlling for child sex and age, age of school entry, parental schooling and household socioeconomic status. The article reports "0.10 SD (95% CI 0.09, 0.11) higher mathematics test scores" for 1 year and 0.22 SD (95% CI 0.21, 0.23) for 2 or more years; no standardised effect size statistic is printed for this finding.

"Students who participated in 1 year of pre-primary programmes had on average 0.10 SD (95% CI 0.09, 0.11) higher mathematics test scores at age 15 compared to students who participated in <1 year or no pre-primary programmes"
