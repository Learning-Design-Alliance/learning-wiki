Clark 2017¶

Clark, Melissa A.; Isenberg, Eric; Liu, Albert Y.; Makowsky, Libby; Zukiewicz, Marykate. (2017). Impacts of the Teach For America Investing in Innovation Scale-Up. Princeton, NJ: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/impacts-of-the-teach-for-america-investing-in-innovation-scaleup

q3 · i? · causal · r?

The report's key findings state that TFA corps members hired in the first two years of the scale-up period were "as effective as other teachers in the same high-poverty schools in both reading and math." No effect size for the overall comparison is printed in the supplied text.

"The study found that, on average, TFA corps members hired in the first two years of the scale-up period were as effective as other teachers in the same high-poverty schools in both reading and math."

  • TFA secondary math teachers increase student math achievement by 0.07 standard deviations over one school year relative to other math teachers in the same schools — related
  • TFA shows a small positive effect on reading for early elementary students (PreK to grade 2) but not in math — related
  • An elementary-school RCT found no statistically significant difference between TFA and non-TFA teachers in mathematics or reading achievement — related
  • Under the i3 scale-up grant, TFA planned to grow its corps by 80 percent and aimed for a majority of its teachers to be rated highly effective by the 2014-2015 school year — related
  • During the first two years of the i3 scale-up, TFA expanded its corps by 25 percent but fell short of annual targets for increasing the number of teachers and teacher effectiveness — related
  • The authors propose creating America's Teacher Corps through federal legislation to recognize highly effective K-12 public school teachers — related
  • No statistically significant TFA impacts were found for upper elementary grades (3 through 5) in reading or math or for other examined subgroups — related
