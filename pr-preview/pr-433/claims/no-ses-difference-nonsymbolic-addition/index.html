Evidence¶

Gilmore Camilla K. 2010¶

Gilmore Camilla K., McCarthy Shannon E., Spelke Elizabeth S. (2010). Non-symbolic arithmetic abilities and achievement in the first year of formal schooling in mathematics. Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.02.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Experiment 1 compared 41 low- to middle-SES kindergarten children (19 low-SES, 22 middle-SES by free/reduced lunch status) with a new group of 21 children from a higher socioeconomic community on the same mathematics achievement test.

"the group of children drawn from the wealthier community performed better on the mathematics achievement test than their less advantaged peers, (F(1,58) = 5.45, p = .023, with age as covariate; Table 1, top), consistent with the widespread gap in mathematics achievement between wealthy and poor children"

Gilmore Camilla K. 2010 (2)¶

Gilmore Camilla K., McCarthy Shannon E., Spelke Elizabeth S. (2010). Non-symbolic arithmetic abilities and achievement in the first year of formal schooling in mathematics. Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.02.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Experiment 2 compared 62 children from a lower-middle socioeconomic school with 21 children from diverse public and private schools in the high socioeconomic community, tested in a single summer session on both tasks.

"children in the more advantaged community performed better on the test of mathematics achievement (t(76.9) = 2.06 p =.043; ; Table 1, bottom), but not significantly better than their less advantaged peers on the test of non-symbolic addition (t(81) =1.60, p = .114)"
