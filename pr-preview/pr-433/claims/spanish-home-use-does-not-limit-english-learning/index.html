q2 · i? · review · r2

The brief reports, citing Hammer et al. (2020), aggregate results from three studies: children "who knew more Spanish when they entered the educational system were also more likely to have higher skills in English at the end of the school year." No effect sizes are printed.

"Aggregate results from three studies found that young Spanish–English bilinguals who knew more Spanish when they entered the educational system were also more likely to have higher skills in English at the end of the school year— Spanish use at home in no way limited children’s abilities to learn English (Hammer et al., 2020)."
