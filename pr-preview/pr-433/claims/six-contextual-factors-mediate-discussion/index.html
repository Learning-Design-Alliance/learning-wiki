Evidence¶

Janneke M. Frambach 2014¶

Janneke M. Frambach, Erik W. Driessen, Philip Beh and Cees P.M. van der Vleuten. (2014). Quiet or questioning? Students' discussion behaviors in student-centered education across cultures. Studies in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2012.754865

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative comparative case study across three medical schools; the results section enumerates six contextual factors mediating cultural influences, with Table 4 detailing each factor's inhibiting and enhancing elements, e.g. a 'Dominant tutor or inactive tutor' versus an 'Active tutor who stimulates and explains'.

"six other contextual factors were also found to either inhibit or enhance discussion behaviors: (1) the nature of students'prior school education, (2) the scope of PBL implementation, (3) students'personal characteristics, (4) language of instruction, (5) tutor behavior, and (6) the assessment system."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The assessment system determined whether an exam-directed focus inhibited or enhanced discussion behaviors
  • Four cultural factors inhibit students from speaking up, asking questions and challenging others in PBL discussions, with stronger impact in non-Western schools
  • Student-centered education is feasible in different cultural contexts, but processes and outcomes are likely to differ across them
  • Tutor behavior had a major impact on students' discussion behaviors, with dominant or passive tutors both failing to stimulate lively discussion — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The scope of PBL implementation shaped discussion quality: hybrid lecture-based implementation reduced uncertainty but yielded less critical discussion than full PBL — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Students' discussion skills increased from first to third year in all three institutions, but at different rates across cultures — related
