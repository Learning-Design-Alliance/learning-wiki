Evidence¶

Stormark KM 2019¶

Stormark KM, Fosse HE, Pallesen S, Hysing M (2019) The association between sleep problems and academic performance in primary school-aged children: Findings from a Norwegian longitudinal population-based study. PLoS ONE 14(11): e0224139. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224139

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Parallel multiple mediation analysis (PROCESS macro, 5,000 bootstrap samples) with internalizing and externalizing SDQ subscales as mediators. The "significant relative direct effect in Persistent DIMS group (log odds ratio of 0.7076, p< .001)" was absent in the Concurrent group.

"there was still an overall significant direct relative effect of DIMS on poor school performance (χ(3) = 14,26 p< .001), due to a significant relative direct effect in Persistent DIMS group (log odds ratio of 0.7076, p< .001), while not in the Concurrent DIMS group (log odds ratio of 0.2240, p = .23)"

Related Claims¶

  • Sleep education at school improves academic performance in children — related
  • Children with poor academic performance show more sleep problems, delayed pubertal development, poorer family economy, lower parental education and more mental health problems than average-to-good performers — related
  • Persistent sleep problems (DIMS at both ages 7–9 and 11–13) increase the risk of poor academic performance even after adjusting for mental health, gender, pubertal status and SES — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Concurrent sleep problems at age 11–13 do not increase the risk of poor academic performance after controlling for mental health problems — related
  • Transitory sleep problems (DIMS at age 7–9 only) do not increase the risk of later poor academic performance — related
