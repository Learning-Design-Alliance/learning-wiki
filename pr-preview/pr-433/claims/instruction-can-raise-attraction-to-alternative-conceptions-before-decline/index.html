Evidence¶

Sadler 1998¶

Sadler, P. M. (1998). Psychometric models of student conceptions in science: Reconciling qualitative studies and distractor-driven assessment instruments. Journal of Research in Science Teaching, 35(3), 265–296. doi:10.1002/(SICI)1098-2736(199803)35:3<265::AID-TEA3>3.0.CO;2-P

q2 · pre/post psychometric study · i? · no effect size reported in the abstract · n=1,250 students, grades 8–12 · associational · r2

Multiple-choice astronomy items whose distractors captured alternative conceptions identified in qualitative research were given to 1,250 students in grades 8 to 12 at the start and end of their introductory astronomy courses. Psychometric modelling placed the appeal of the various ideas on a single scale and showed the rise-then-fall profile for attractive distractors, as well as the ages at which particular conceptions are most prevalent. There is no comparison group; the rise is a pattern across the ability scale and the pre/post administrations, not a manipulated effect. Coded from the published abstract; the full text was not read.

Discussion¶

A rise in the number of students choosing an alternative-conception distractor after a unit is not, on this evidence, necessarily a sign that teaching failed: it may mark learners moving from no idea to a partial one. That reading fits the knowledge-in-pieces view of misconceptions as steps in a refinement. For misconception probes it means a single pre/post comparison of distractor choices can mislead.
