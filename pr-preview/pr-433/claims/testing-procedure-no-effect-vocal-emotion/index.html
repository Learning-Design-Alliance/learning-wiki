Evidence¶

Grosbras 2018¶

Grosbras, M.-H., Ross, P. D. & Belin, P. (2018). Categorical emotion recognition from voice improves during childhood and adolescence. Scientific Reports 8, 14791. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-32868-3

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Mixed-model comparison in the children-adolescents sample (133 paper-tested, 92 computer-tested). The article reports non-significant tests only, with no effect size printed, so impact is null; equivalence was not formally tested.

"There was no effect of testing proce- dure [Z-Wald = 0.2334; p = 0.81) nor interaction with the other factors (with age, Z = −0.179, p = 0.85; with sex: z = −0.19, p = 0.85)."

Related Claims¶

  • Female children and adolescents recognize vocal emotions more accurately than males, with no interaction between sex and age — related
  • Adult-level vocal emotion recognition performance is reached between 14 and 15 years of age, with a non-linear trajectory of faster childhood improvement followed by a late-adolescent plateau — related
  • The ability to identify basic emotions from non-linguistic vocal bursts improves significantly with age from 5 to 17 years — related
  • Confusion patterns between vocal emotions are broadly stable across development: children miscategorize anger as fear, adolescents as sadness — related
  • Happiness is the easiest vocal emotion to recognize and anger the hardest, with anger and fear showing the greatest age-related improvement — related
  • Children's average vocal emotion recognition performance is significantly below the adult group's average — related
