Evidence¶

Klatte M 2013¶

Klatte M, Bergström K and Lachmann T (2013) Does noise affect learning? A short review on noise effects on cognitive performance in children. Front. Psychol. 4:578. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00578

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports psychoacoustic studies showing children's speech identification in stationary noise requires "5–7 dB higher" SNRs than adults, with adult-like performance at about 6 years; with fluctuating maskers adult-like performance is usually not reached before age 10.

"With stationary noise makers, signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) have to be 5–7 dB higher for young children when compared to adults in order to achieve comparable levels of identiﬁcation of speech or nonspeech signals, with adult-like performance reached at about 6 years of age"
