Evidence¶

Nye 2014¶

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

q2 · i2 · review · r2

Review reports evaluations of AutoTutor 1.1 and 2.0 in computer literacy against textbook-studying controls; the two versions performed comparably. Printed effect size: approximately 0.5 σ.

"Despite these differences, both versions performed comparably to each other and both showed learning gains of approximately 0.5 σ over controls who studied relevant textbook chapters for an equivalent amount of time"

Nye 2014 (2)¶

Nye, B. D., Graesser, A. C., & Hu, X. (2014). AutoTutor and Family: A Review of 17 Years of Natural Language Tutoring. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0029-5

q2 · i2 · review · r2

Review reports the test-type dependence of the early computer-literacy evaluations' effect sizes, foreshadowing higher gains for deep reasoning. Printed effect sizes: 0.15 σ, 0.28 σ, 0.64 σ.

"These effect sizes were highly dependent on the type of evaluation test question, with gains of 0.15 σ for shallow questions, 0.28 σ for deep questions, and 0.64 σ for cloze questions"
