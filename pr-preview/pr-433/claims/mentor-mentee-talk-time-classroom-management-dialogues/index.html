Evidence¶

Sempowicz 2011¶

Sempowicz, T., & Hudson, P. (2011). Analysing mentoring dialogues for developing a preservice teacher's classroom management practices. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 36(8). https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ajte/vol36/iss8/1 doi:10.14221/ajte.2011v36n8.4

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Talk-time analysis of five video-recorded dialogue sessions (Table 1) during the 20-day practicum. The mentor spoke more than the mentee in video sessions, though the gap reduced in subsequent sessions as participants became accustomed to recording.

"In the majority of the video episodes, the mentor's talking time ranged from 4 to 14:11 minutes while the mentee's talking time ranged from 1:10 to 4:20 minutes (Table 1)."

Sempowicz 2011 (2)¶

Sempowicz, T., & Hudson, P. (2011). Analysing mentoring dialogues for developing a preservice teacher's classroom management practices. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 36(8). https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ajte/vol36/iss8/1. doi:10.14221/ajte.2011v36n8.4

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Subset analysis of classroom-management talk in the five video sessions (Table 1). The mentor spoke more about classroom management techniques than the mentee in all but one session; audio dialogues showed more attentive listening by the mentor in 7 of 8 dialogues.

"Further analysis showed that the talk about classroom management for both the mentor and mentee ranged between 3% and 39% of the total talk time across the five sessions (Table 1)."
