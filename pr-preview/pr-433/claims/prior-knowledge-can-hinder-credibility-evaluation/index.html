Evidence¶

Carita Kiili 2026¶

Carita Kiili, Ivar Bråten, and Julie Coiro. (2026). The framework of prior knowledge in online credibility evaluation. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2026.2717081

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review reports, citing Stadtler and Bromme (2014), that "Readers may attend selectively to information that aligns with their existing prior knowledge," which can reduce attention to source features or hinder revision of inaccurate knowledge. No effect size is printed.

"Readers may attend selectively to information that aligns with their existing prior knowledge (Stadtler & Bromme, 2014), which can reduce their attention to source features (Bråten et al., 2018) or hinder revision of inaccurate knowledge (Ecker et al., 2022)."

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review asserts, citing Kruger and Dunning (1999), that "strong reliance on one’s prior knowledge can also lead to overcon - fidence and reduced criticality." The article offers this as a cited caution without further evidence; no effect size is printed.

"Finally, strong reliance on one’s prior knowledge can also lead to overcon - fidence and reduced criticality (Kruger & Dunning, 1999)."
