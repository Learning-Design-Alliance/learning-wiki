q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports two Finnish adolescent case-control studies (Saarikivi et al., 2016; Putkinen et al., 2021): no significant EF differences, but "the completion time for the tests was signiﬁcantly faster in the music-trained groups."

"the studies by Putniken and Saarkivi failed to identify any improvement in inhibitory control measurements among those with prior music training. Nevertheless, the completion time for the tests was signiﬁcantly faster in the music-trained groups"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Music training RCTs show improved verbal working memory (backward digit span), but not visuospatial or forward-span tasks
  • Only one identified study found improved visuospatial working memory after music training
  • The review concludes music training has a beneficial effect on core executive function performance, primarily in inhibitory control and to a lesser extent in working memory and cognitive flexibility — related
  • Music training is associated with improved inhibitory control in children, though the largest studies found no executive function differences — related
  • Music training effects on fluency are largely null, with one case-control study reporting improved verbal fluency — related
