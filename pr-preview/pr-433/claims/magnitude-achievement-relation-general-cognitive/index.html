Evidence¶

Torbeyns 2015¶

Torbeyns, J., Schneider, M., Xin, Z., & Siegler, R. S. (2015). Bridging the Gap: Fraction Understanding is Central to Mathematics Achievement in Students from Three Different Continents. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2014.03.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The study's correlational and hierarchical regression analyses across Belgian, Chinese, and U.S. 6th and 8th graders supported Hypothesis 2: the article concludes the strong relation between fraction magnitude understanding and mathematics achievement "can be interpreted as a general cognitive characteristic of all students".

"Moreover, in all three countries, fraction magnitude understanding was the best predictor of mathematics achievement scores, even after controlling for variation in fraction arithmetic skill. We did not observe this pattern of results in the oldest group of Chinese students, presumably due to ceiling effects in their knowledge of fraction magnitudes."
