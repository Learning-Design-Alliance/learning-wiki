Evidence¶

Blair 2016¶

Blair, Clancy; McKinnon, Rachel D.; the Family Life Project Investigators. (2016). Moderating effects of executive functions and the teacher–child relationship on the development of mathematics ability in kindergarten. Learning and Instruction, 41, 85–93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.10.001

q2 · i? · associational · r3

The authors' interpretation in the Discussion (§4.2) of the measure-discrepancy finding: unlike Applied Problems, the ECLS-K Math battery routes children to distinct ability levels and covers more domains, giving lower stability and more room to detect small effects.

"Our findings, however, indicate that studies that rely exclusively on the Applied Problems subtest, a widely used measure of mathematics ability in children, are likely to miss small but meaningful influences on math achievement."
