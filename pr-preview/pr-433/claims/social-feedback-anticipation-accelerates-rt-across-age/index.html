Evidence¶

Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck 2023¶

Alexandra Sobczak and Nico Bunzeck. (2023). Effects of positive and negative social feedback on motivation, evaluative learning, and socio-emotional processing. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00178-7

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Mixed-design experiment (SID-Photo and SID-Video studies; young n = 101, older n = 107 as stated in the introduction) using a 2 × 2 × 3 permutation F-test on z-transformed RT. Post-hoc tests showed "a signi ﬁcant difference between the positive vs and negative vs neutral (p = 0.0001) but not positive vs negative condition"; a Bayesian ANOVA favored the age + condition model (BF10 = 2.44 × 1013).

"The 2 × 2 × 3 mixed design permutation F-test showed signi ﬁcant main effects of age (p = 0.0001, Fig. 3a) and condition ( p = 0.0001; Fig. 3b) but no signi ﬁcant main effect of study and no signi ﬁcant interactions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Baseline simple response times were faster in the SID-Video study than the SID-Photo study and faster in young than older adults, with no study × age interaction — related
  • Cues predicting positive social feedback acquire positive implicit valence (evaluative learning) in both young and older adults, while negative cues do not differ from neutral — related
  • Reaction times are faster for predictable targets, but the RT benefit of prediction diminishes with progressing age — related
  • Explicit cue valence ratings show an age × condition interaction, with smaller positive-vs-neutral and positive-vs-negative rating differences in older adults; implicit measures show no such age effect — related
  • Explicit cue valence correlates with feedback video valence only in the positive condition (rs = 0.4389), indicating specific transfer of emotional valence from positive feedback to predictive cues — related
