Evidence¶

Mukherjee 2023¶

Mukherjee, S., Rohles, B., Distler, V., Lenzini, G., & Koenig, V. (2023). The effects of privacy-non-invasive interventions on cheating prevention and user experience in unproctored online assessments: An empirical study. Computers and Education, 207, 104925. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104925

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The article reports, citing Freiburger et al. (2017) and Nagin & Pogarsky (2003), that "the likelihood of being caught has been found to deter students from cheating more effectively than merely stating the severity of punishment." The study's own qualitative data supports the role of perceived surveillance.

"The decision to cheat was influenced by the extent to which interventions conveyed that cheating is wrong and also by test-takers' perception of getting caught if they cheated."
