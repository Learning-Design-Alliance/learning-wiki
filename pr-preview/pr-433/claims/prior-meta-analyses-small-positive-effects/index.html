Evidence¶

Verhoeven 2022¶

Verhoeven, L.T.W.; Voeten, M.J.M.; Segers, P.C.J. (2022). Computer-assisted word reading intervention effects throughout the primary grades: A meta-analysis. Educational Research Review, 37, article 100486. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100486

q2 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r?

The review reports, citing Blok et al. (2002), an overall positive effect size of "0.19 over 42 different experimental programs" for computer-assisted reading instruction in primary education; higher effects when English was the instruction language.

"Blok, Oostdam, Otter, and Overmaat (2002) were the first to analyze the effects of computer-assisted reading instruction in primary education. They reported an overall positive effect size of 0.19 over 42 different experimental programs."

Verhoeven 2022 (2)¶

Verhoeven, L.T.W.; Voeten, M.J.M.; Segers, P.C.J. (2022). Computer-assisted word reading intervention effects throughout the primary grades: A meta-analysis. Educational Research Review, 37, article 100486. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2022.100486

q2 · i0 · quant-synthesis · r?

The review reports, citing Cheung and Slavin (2011), that across 85 rigor-screened studies educational technology showed "a positive but small effect size (0.16) in comparison to traditional methods", with 0.28 for comprehensive reading interventions.

"Finally, Cheung and Slavin (2011) reported the results of a meta-analysis on the effectiveness of educational technology for enhancing reading achievement while taking into account methodological rigor of the studies. For a total of 85 studies, they found a positive but small effect size (0.16) in comparison to traditional methods."
