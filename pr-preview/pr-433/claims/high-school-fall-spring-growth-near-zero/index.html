Related Claims¶

  • Within-year (fall-to-spring) achievement gains decelerate linearly as students move through school, at roughly 1.4 to 4 RIT per year depending on subject and centering grade — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Within-school-year achievement growth is often not linear, particularly in reading — related
  • Mean foundational-skills scores rise from fall to spring with the largest gains in kindergarten and grade 1, and growth flattens by grades 2-3 — related
  • Mathematics growth in Grades 1-3 is nonlinear with deceleration, so more growth occurs in the first half of the school year, and average growth rates decline across grade levels — related
  • Aspirational Growth defaults to 60th-percentile growth (met by about 40% of similar students) and is selectable from the 55th to 95th percentile in 5-percentile increments — related
  • The published norms allow normative interpretation of both status and fall-to-spring growth, e.g., an Algebra 1 student at the 55th fall percentile has an expected gain of 8 RIT with growth SD of 9, and an 11-RIT gain places the student at the 62nd growth percentile. — related
