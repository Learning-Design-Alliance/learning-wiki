Evidence¶

Wanli Xing 2025¶

Wanli Xing, Nia Nixon, Scott Crossley, Paul Denny, Andrew Lan, John Stamper, Zhou Yu. (2025). The Use of Large Language Models in Education. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00457-x

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The editorial reports, citing Henkel et al. (2024), an empirical study using the ROARs dataset in which "LLMs could achieve grading accuracy comparable to human experts" on short-answer reading comprehension items. No statistics or effect sizes are printed in the editorial.

"explore the applications of LLMs in grading short-answer reading comprehension questions demonstrating that LLMs could achieve grading accuracy comparable to human experts, emphasizing the potential of LLMs to facilitate the grading of formative assessments"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Newer LLM generations such as GPT-4 generally outperform GPT-3.5 in short-answer grading accuracy, but this advantage is not consistent across all tasks and domains — related
  • LLMs perform worse on complex, open-ended, or subjective assignments than on short, clearly formulated answers — related
  • Prompt quality and detailed scoring rubrics significantly influence the accuracy and consistency of LLM-generated grades — related
  • Out-of-the-box LLMs agree with human essay grades far less than human raters agree with each other — reports the opposite
  • LLM grading performance relative to human graders is mixed: comparable in 12 reviewed cases, worse in 11, with the strongest alignment on short, straightforward tasks — related
  • Hint-based prompts raise ChatGPT scoring agreement with human experts to 98–100%, while no-hint prompts are unstable (Q2 precision 0.42) — related
