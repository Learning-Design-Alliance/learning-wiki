Related Claims¶

  • School-based sleep education improves children's sleep duration and quality — related
  • Sleep education at school improves cognitive functioning in children — related
  • Children with poor academic performance show more sleep problems, delayed pubertal development, poorer family economy, lower parental education and more mental health problems than average-to-good performers — related
  • Concurrent sleep problems at age 11–13 do not increase the risk of poor academic performance after controlling for mental health problems — related
  • Persistent sleep problems (DIMS at both ages 7–9 and 11–13) increase the risk of poor academic performance even after adjusting for mental health, gender, pubertal status and SES — related
  • The association between sleep problems and poor school performance is partly mediated by children's internalizing and externalizing mental health problems, with a direct effect remaining only for persistent DIMS — related
  • Transitory sleep problems (DIMS at age 7–9 only) do not increase the risk of later poor academic performance — related
  • Short sleep duration (<9 hours) at follow-up is associated with impaired academic achievement, while baseline sleep duration did not significantly predict later school performance — related
