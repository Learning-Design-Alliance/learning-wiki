Evidence¶

de Bruin 2025¶

de Bruin, A. B. H., Janssen, E. M., Waldeyer, J., & Stebner, F. (2025). Cognitive Load and Challenges in Self-regulation: An Introduction and Reflection on the Topical Collection. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10042-2

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Experimental field study with 10-year-old students by Wirth et al. (2025), comparing direct and combined direct-and-indirect strategy training against a control condition. The editorial reports "preliminary evidence for far metacognitive transfer" of effort regulation, with no significant difference between training conditions and small effect sizes.

"the results provide preliminary evidence for far metacognitive transfer: students in the training conditions showed improved metacognitive regulation of effort, a cognitive strategy that had not been taught in the training."

Related Claims¶

  • Training in metacognitive regulation combined with a cognitive learning strategy transfers to better metacognitive regulation of mental effort (far transfer), with small effect sizes — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Hybrid training fosters far transfer of metacognitive skills to a new cognitive strategy, though one hybrid condition did not beat control — related
  • Metacognitive regulation of learning imposes additional cognitive load (metacognitive load) on top of the load of actual learning — related
  • Theoretical expectation: hybrid metacognitive skill training benefits content knowledge acquisition more in near than in far transfer because far transfer is more cognitively demanding — related
  • Hybrid training improves fifth-graders' application of metacognitive skills in near-transfer tasks compared to non-hybrid and control training — related
  • Cognitive strategy application in a far-transfer task after hybrid training depends on the specific strategy: text highlighting transferred, conducting experiments did not versus non-hybrid training — related
  • The four aggregated non-hybrid training groups did not differ on the dependent variables of the analyses — related
  • Before metacognitive skill training, fifth graders possessed none of the metacognitive skills later trained, so metacognitive skills are task-specific at first and become domain-general only through transfer — related
