Evidence¶

Chang Zhu 2010¶

Chang Zhu, Martin Valcke and Tammy Schellens. (2010). A cross-cultural study of teacher perspectives on teacher roles and adoption of online collaborative learning in higher education. European Journal of Teacher Education. https://doi.org/10.1080/02619761003631849

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Interview-based classification of adopters among 60 Chinese and 30 Flemish teachers (Table 6). The article reports the printed adoption percentages for e-learning and for online collaborative learning in each group, noting Ghent University's university-wide Minerva platform.

"From the results, we see only about 8% of the Chinese faculty adopted an online collaborative learning mode; however, 27% of the Flemish faculty adopted this innovative mode. The general adoption of e-learning by the Flemish faculty reaches about 95%; however, the general adoption of e-learning is only about 30% among the Chinese faculty."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Chinese teachers emphasize competition among students more than Flemish teachers, while perceived power distance and collaboration are similar across the two groups
  • Chinese university teachers stress the expert, authority and model teacher roles to a larger extent than Flemish teachers, with no group difference in facilitator or delegator roles
  • Perceived power distance correlates with expert and authority roles, collaboration with facilitator and delegator roles, and competition correlates with all five teacher roles — related
  • Teachers' adoption of online collaborative learning is associated with constructivist beliefs, facilitator/delegator roles, perceived need for innovation, and institutional conditions — related
