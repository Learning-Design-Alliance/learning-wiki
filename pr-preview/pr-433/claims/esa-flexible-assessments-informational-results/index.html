Evidence¶

Berry 2024¶

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

q2 · i? · review · r2

Analysis of the 15 ESA state statutes finds "14 of the ESA programs" require some assessment. Arkansas, Florida, Indiana, New Hampshire, South Carolina, and West Virginia let families choose between state and norm-referenced exams, and several states allow IEP-based exemptions or alternative assessments for students with disabilities.

"The state statutes of 14 of the ESA programs have some sort of requirement for recipients to take a state or national norm-referenced assessment to provide families with information on student learning."

Berry 2024 (2)¶

Berry, W. & Chait, R. (2024). Education savings accounts and accountability: A landscape analysis across states. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663763

q2 · i? · review · r2

Statute analysis reports that assessment requirements are designed to inform parents and the public rather than impose consequences, and that "only one state, West Virginia," sets explicit benchmarks (scoring at least within or above the 4th stanine or demonstrating growth). Florida, Iowa, South Carolina, and Tennessee require schools to report results to parents.

"There is only one state, West Virginia, that has explicit benchmarks that recipients must meet as a requirement of use"
