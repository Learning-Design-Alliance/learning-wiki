Evidence¶

Ludvigsen 2016¶

Ludvigsen, S. (2016). CSCL: connecting the social, emotional and cognitive dimensions. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 11, 115–121. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-016-9236-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The editorial reports Dillenbourg et al.'s five experiments engaging participants in collaborative problem solving, with tasks varying in importance and in how difficult it is to build accurate models; the finding partly contradicts the starting assumption.

"The quality of the modeling emerges through the task and is sensitive to the awareness of tools and to media richness in the environment."

Ludvigsen 2016 (2)¶

Ludvigsen, S. (2016). CSCL: connecting the social, emotional and cognitive dimensions. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 11, 115–121. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-016-9236-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The editorial reports this symmetry result from the same five experiments by Dillenbourg et al., noting explicitly that it held in several but not all of the experiments.

"In several, but not all of the experiments, the symmetry of mutual models in the dyad seems to be crucial."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Providing combined cognitive and behavioral group awareness information positively affects cognitive and behavioral learning processes and outcomes in social media, per the editorial's report of Ollasch et al., but its emotional effects were not consistently positive.
  • In most of the reported collaborative experiments, the accuracy of the partner model A built about B was correlated with the accuracy of the model B built about A — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Symmetry of partner models does not rule out a contribution of individual modelling abilities to mutual modelling quality — related
  • The five mutual-modelling experiments varied how important it was to build an accurate model (task) and how difficult it was to build one (setting) — related
  • The quality of mutual models appears to depend more on the quality of interactions than on individual representational abilities — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Collaboration does not emerge spontaneously just by placing students together — related
