Evidence¶

Winch 1911¶

Winch, W. H. (1911). Mental adaptation during the school day as measured by arithmetical reasoning.—Part I. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Mental+adaptation+during+the+school+day%22+Winch+1911

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The article's general summary states the exception among the schools studied: "the earlier working group improves more rapidly than the one working later" in the very poor neighborhood school. The detailed experimental section for this school appears in the concluded part not supplied here.

"In one case, that of a school in a very poor neighborhood, the earlier working group improves more rapidly than the one working later."

Winch 1911 (2)¶

Winch, W. H. (1911). Mental adaptation during the school day as measured by arithmetical reasoning.—Part I. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Mental+adaptation+during+the+school+day%22+Winch+1911

q1 · i? · causal · r1

The author's offered explanation (type e) for the exception: the poor-school boys' employment before school is said to make "their general mental adaptation ... at its height earlier in the morning than that of children in other schools." This is interpretation, not a tested result.

"About half the boys in this class "go to work" very early in the morning before commencing school, and nearly all of them do some work before school begins."
