Related Claims¶

  • After one year of DLP coaching, coached teachers reported more frequent and more powerful technology use than non-participating colleagues — related
  • DLP teachers reported significant increases in using technology for both content and pedagogy, but no significant difference from non-DLP teachers in basic technology skills — related
  • DLP teachers reported more confidence than non-DLP teachers in using technology to develop students' six 21st-century skill categories — related
  • Teachers coached for more than one cycle reported more powerful technology use and greater confidence, though a breadth-versus-depth tradeoff remains open — related
  • Baseline survey found only about 35% of teachers felt all their students were comfortable with technology, while 85% wanted more technology-training — related
  • Teachers described an ideal technology coach as prepared, patient, observant, and using current research-based methods — related
  • Coached teachers report increases in how often students and teachers use technology, compared to non-coached peers — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Argument structure training increased students' self-reported confidence with the Toulmin model — related
  • Teachers who report coaching as a partnership report more improvement in classroom challenges and higher technology-use confidence; voluntary participation and non-evaluative support correlate with improvement in classroom challenges — related
  • More time with coaches was significantly associated with better teaching outcomes and higher teacher self-reported technological proficiency — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • DLP participants reported more frequent meaningful coaching activities than other coaching programs, but fewer opportunities to observe other teachers — related
