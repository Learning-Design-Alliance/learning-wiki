Evidence¶

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur 2026¶

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur. (2026). From Body to Word: A Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Embodied Vocabulary Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10194-9

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r3

Between-subject meta-regression of movement-meaning congruency. Congruent movements (g = 0.74) outperformed non-congruent movements (g = 0.38), a significant moderation (F = 6.52, p = .01).

"movement-meaning congruency significantly moderated vocabulary learning outcomes ( F = 6.52, p = .01), with congruent movements ( g = 0.74, 95% CI [0.52, 0.97]) yielding larger effects than non-congruent movements ( g = 0.38, 95% CI [0.10, 0.67])."

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur 2026 (2)¶

Xiaoxuan Li · Julia Chatain · Fan Wang · Manu Kapur. (2026). From Body to Word: A Three-Level Meta-Analysis of Embodied Vocabulary Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10194-9

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r3

Within-subject meta-regression of movement-meaning congruency. Congruent movements (g = 0.41) outperformed non-congruent movements (g = −0.17), significant at F = 22.66, p < .001.

"movement-meaning congruency was identified as a significant moderator ( F = 22.66, p < .001), with congruent movements ( g = 0.41, 95% CI [0.25, 0.57]) associated with larger learning gains compared with non-congruent movements ( g = −0.17, 95% CI [−0.41, 0.07])."
