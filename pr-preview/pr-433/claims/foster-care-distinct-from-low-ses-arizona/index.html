Evidence¶

Barrat 2015¶

Barrat, V. X., Berliner, B., & Felida, N. J. (2015). Arizona's Invisible Achievement Gap: Education Outcomes of Students in Foster Care in the State's Public Schools. San Francisco: WestEd. http://www.azed.gov/research-evaluation/files/2013/11/2013-a-f-technical-manual.pdf

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of linked Arizona Department of Education and Department of Child Safety administrative data for 1,108,795 K–12 students, including 10,770 in foster care and 535,681 low-SES students, in 2012/13. The report finds students in foster care were "classified with a disability at twice the rate of both comparison groups" (23 percent vs. 12 and 11 percent).

"Students in foster care were more likely than low-SES students to be African American or White, but less likely than low-SES students to be Hispanic or to be designated as English language learners. They were also classified with a disability at twice the rate of both comparison groups"
