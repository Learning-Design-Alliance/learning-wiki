Evidence¶

Yao 2026¶

Yao, X., Huber, J.F., Li, Z., Findik, Y., Nuerk, H.-C. & Artemenko, C. (2026). The dynamics of state math anxiety vary by paradigm and timing during arithmetic. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00398-z

q3 · i? · causal · r2

LMM (Model E) on SMA ratings at six mid-task measurement points (breaks at the midpoint of each paradigm block) in the same study. The time effect was significant (p < .001), "by an estimate of -0.11", with paradigm included as a random effect.

"The main effect of time indicates that for each repetition of the arithmetic task, state math anxiety decreases over time by an estimate of -0.11."

Yao 2026 (2)¶

Yao, X., Huber, J.F., Li, Z., Findik, Y., Nuerk, H.-C. & Artemenko, C. (2026). The dynamics of state math anxiety vary by paradigm and timing during arithmetic. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00398-z

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same LMM (Model E) testing the time × trait math anxiety interaction across the six mid-task points. The interaction was significant (p < .001), "with an estimate of -0.07", which the authors interpret as adaptation or emotion regulation over task progress.

"The interaction of time and trait math anxiety with an estimate of -0.07 indicates that, as time progresses, individuals with higher trait math anxiety tend to experience a decrease in state math anxiety at a slightly faster rate compared to those with lower trait math anxiety."
