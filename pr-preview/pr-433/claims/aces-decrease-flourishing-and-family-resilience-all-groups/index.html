Evidence¶

Goldstein 2021¶

Goldstein, E., Topitzes, J., Miller-Cribbs, J. & Brown, R. L. (2021). Influence of race/ethnicity and income on the link between adverse childhood experiences and child flourishing. Pediatric Research. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-020-01188-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional secondary analysis of the 2016-17 NSCH (n = 44,686 children ages 6-17) using a moderated-mediation probit model with weighted least squares estimates. The article reports that "ACEs are consequential for all children regardless of their background"; no standardized effect size is printed.

"As ACEs increased, the estimated probability of all indicators of ﬂourishing outcomes and family resilience decreased across race/ ethnicity and FPL, reinforcing the notion that ACEs are consequential for all children regardless of their background."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Moderation by race/ethnicity: White children show significantly greater flourishing decrements as ACEs increase, while Black and Hispanic children show steeper declines in family resilience
  • ACE exposure is unevenly distributed: children below 400% FPL and Black and Hispanic children have higher prevalence of two or more ACEs — related
  • Family resilience partially mediates the association between ACEs and child flourishing, with mediation proportions varying by race/ethnicity and income — related
  • The moderated-mediation effect of family resilience on the ACEs-curiosity-in-learning relationship is significantly greater for Black than for White children — related
  • Above-FPL children improved more in staying calm and in control as family resilience increased, but no significant FPL moderated-mediation effects emerged — related
