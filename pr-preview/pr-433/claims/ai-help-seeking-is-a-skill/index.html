The review reports Hou et al.'s ACE 2024 study combining a survey of 47 computing students with eight interviews: "GenAI had not displaced traditional help resources", with preliminary evidence that effective AI help-seeking is itself a skill unevenly distributed.

"Hou et al. [2024a] found that GenAI had not displaced traditional help resources; preferences varied with the task and with perceived quality, latency, and trustworthiness."

The review reports Sheese et al.'s 12-week deployment with 52 students and more than 2,500 queries: most sought immediate assignment help, fewer pursued conceptual understanding, and "students often supplied minimal contextual information".

"A complementary 12-week deployment of an LLM-powered programming assistant with 52 students captured more than 2,500 queries: most sought immediate help with programming assignments, fewer pursued broader conceptual understanding, and students often supplied minimal contextual information"

Related Claims¶

  • Post-training GenAI use patterns were unevenly distributed: efficient collaborators were the largest profile (47.5%), followed by over-revisers (26.2%) and mixed users (21.2%) — related
  • Students shifted AI usage toward debugging and concept explanation over the semester, with 31 of 89 reporting more debugging use — related
  • Student-agent interaction produced far more help-seeking but a non-uniform cognitive distribution dominated by lower-order engagement (54.52%), unlike the balanced distribution of student-instructor interaction — related
  • More than half of participants' ChatGPT queries sought knowledge or advice, with methodological knowledge and classroom management the largest prompt categories
  • HelpCoach led to greater knowledge retention one week later than pre-task help-seeking training alone — related
  • Not requesting any hints in at least one problem is related to greater wheel-spinning, consistent with help avoidance — related
  • The Coach's hint budget was managed poorly: students split into early-spenders and savers, and within the Coach condition only those with a deliberate hint strategy scored higher — related
  • Students' critical examination of AI-generated outputs increased across learning cycles, though unevenly, with some participants continuing to seek confirmation from AI — related
  • Undergraduate computing students report help-seeking avoidance and anxiety, with 62.5% sometimes avoiding asking for help even when needed and 75% reporting anxiety when not understanding a topic — related
  • In 38% of help episodes students never asked for help, and these episodes usually ended poorly — related
