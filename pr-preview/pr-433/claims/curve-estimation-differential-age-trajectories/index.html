Safa A. Elgamal 2011¶

Safa A. Elgamal, MBBCh, PhD, Eric A. Roy, PhD, Michael T. Sharratt, PhD. (2011). Age and Verbal Fluency: The Mediating Effect of Speed of Processing. Canadian Geriatrics Journal, Volume 14, Issue 3. https://doi.org/10.5770/cgj.v14i3.17

Curve estimation analysis of the cross-sectional data relating age to each cognitive measure. The results section states processing speed "remains unchanged until about the age of 30 years, after which it declines linearly" and that category fluency and IQ are "best represented by a quadratic curve"; letter fluency plotting "did not show any change with age" (Figure 1).

"Plotting the data using curve estimation showed that processing speed remains unchanged until about the age of 30 years, after which it declines linearly. However, the changes in category fluency and in IQ are nonlinear and best represented by a quadratic curve."

Related Claims¶

  • Age correlates negatively with processing speed and category fluency and positively with IQ, but not with letter fluency — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Young adults outperform older adults on category fluency and processing speed, while older adults outperform young adults on premorbid IQ (NART) — related
  • Category fluency declines with age and remains significantly impaired after controlling for processing speed and IQ — related
  • Letter fluency shows no age-group difference in ANOVA, but older adults outperform young adults once processing speed and IQ are controlled — related
  • Processing speed has a significant effect on both letter and category fluency in older adults, while IQ does not — related
  • Decline in letter fluency beyond normal age expectations warrants further evaluation for neurological or mental disorder — related
  • Cross-sectional age relations show monotonic decline in fluid cognition (age-reasoning correlation about −0.40) but stability followed by decline for acquired knowledge — related
  • When relevant knowledge can be drawn upon, age trends in performance can reverse, with older adults outperforming younger adults on crossword puzzles — related
