Evidence¶

Dominguez 2021¶

Dominguez, X., Rood, E., Kamdar, D., Leones, T., & Huynh, K. (2021). Splash and Bubbles for Parents App: Field Study Report [Project Report]. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/119

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Analysis of pre/post parent interviews and case examples of two bilingual families showing self-reported gains in confidence engaging in science talk. The report states "Families who explored the app most often and more deeply also reported stronger self-reported changes in joint media engagement and science," while families confused about the app's purpose "do not seem to have tried out the strategies or tools." This is an interview pattern, not a test of whether more use caused the changes.

Related Claims¶

  • Nearly all participating families reported using the Splash and Bubbles for Parents app and found it helpful for supporting children's science learning — related
  • Most families used the Activities section and rated it highly helpful, with many completing two or more activities — related
  • Sync feature uptake declined between survey and interview, with non-users citing unawareness, technical difficulties, or single-device viewing — related
  • Families who used the app's features more extensively reported stronger changes in joint media engagement and science — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Over 70% of participants in the field and station studies rated the app a 4 or 5 for supporting children's science learning — related
  • The usefulness of app components varied by child's age: conversation starters were too advanced for some younger children but helpful for older children — related
