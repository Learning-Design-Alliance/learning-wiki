Narrative synthesis of achievement-outcome studies (theme five); the review reports Swanson's (2013) comparison of high versus low humour classes, cautioning that teachers may not have accurately reported humour use due to demand characteristics.

"Swanson (2013) measured students' Spanish test scores for high humour classes compared to low, finding high humour to be associated with higher overall scores."

  • Affiliative humour is associated with positive classroom climate, enjoyment and willingness to participate among adolescent students — related
  • Aggressive humour has mixed effects on adolescent students' educational experiences, with reported increases in anger, boredom and resistance but some students reporting sarcasm improved rapport — related
  • Observed teacher humour differs from self-reports: sarcasm and mocking accounted for nearly two-thirds of observed teacher humour although teachers and students reported aggressive humour as least common — related
