Evidence¶

Sidi Yael 2023¶

Sidi Yael, Shamir-Inbal Tamar, Eshet-Alkalai Yoram. (2023). From face-to-face to online: Teachers' perceived experiences in online distance teaching during the Covid-19 pandemic. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104831

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative phase: semi-structured interviews with 20 fifth- and sixth-grade teachers, coded bottom-up per grounded theory, with ~25% of statements double-coded to agreement. The analysis yielded "473 statements which were categorized into five main categories" covering social, emotional, cognitive, pedagogical, and system support.

"The analysis resulted in the identification of 473 statements which were categorized into five main categories. Each category was divided into subcategories according to the thematic analysis."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • System support was only marginally present in teachers' accounts, and most teachers who raised it felt they did not receive proper support. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Most pedagogical-category statements were positive: teachers perceived online distance teaching as an opportunity to diversify teaching methods and maintain routine. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Emotional experiences in online distance teaching were dominated by negatives — frustration, sadness, and detachment driven by lack of student feedback and visual contact. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Cognitive experiences of online teaching were mixed: 53% of statements reported workload and stimulus overload while 47% expressed a sense of self-efficacy. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
