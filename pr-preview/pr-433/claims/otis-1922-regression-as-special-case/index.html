Evidence¶

Arthur S. Otis 1922¶

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Derivation setting rXX = +1.00 so √rYY = rXY, reducing Equation 53 to the regression equation for finding the score corresponding to or normal for any age. The article states the correspondence formula

"This, of course, is the regular regression equation, showing that to find the score corresponding to (or normal for) any age, we may use the line of regression, that is, the line passing through the central tendencies of the arrays of scores for the several ages."

Arthur S. Otis 1922 (2)¶

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

An explicit scope restriction printed as a footnote to the second proof: the relation-line formula is for correspondence between true scores, while prediction of most-probable associated scores requires the regression equation.

"This formula is not to be uBed for prediction or for estimating true values in one scale from obtained values in another. For these purposes the regression equation should be used."

Related Claims¶

  • The mean of the array of scores on one scale associated with a given score on another fallible measure tends to be nearer the overall mean than the truly corresponding value is, so the regression line does not show true correspondence — related
  • For two tests of the same trait with unequal reliabilities, the line of true correspondence is y = (σY/σX)√(rYY/rXX)·x, measured from the respective means — related
  • The standard deviation of true scores equals the square root of the reliability coefficient times the standard deviation of obtained scores — related
