Preschool musicality is associated with school-age communication abilities through genes related to rhythmicity. npj Science of Learning 10:39. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00329-y

q2 · i2 · associational · r2

Data-driven multivariate modelling (EFA in one random half, CFA in the other) of 25 ALSPAC measures yielded a correlated 5-factor model with good fit (CFI = 0.90, TLI = 0.88, RMSEA = 0.049, SRMR = 0.034); the article reports "associations of F musicality with F cognition (r = 0.33(SE = 0.02)) and F speech (r = 0.50(SE = 0.02))".

"Focusing on meaningful factor correlations ( | r | >0.3), we observed associations of F musicality with F cognition (r = 0.33(SE = 0.02)) and F speech (r = 0.50(SE = 0.02)) but not with factors underlying more social aspects of communication (Fpragmaticand FSCDC)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Preschool and school-age musicality measures predict school-age communication and social communication, explaining up to 6% of phenotypic variance, while infant nursery-rhyme measures explain less than 1% — related
  • The association between preschool musicality and school-age speech-related communication is partially accounted for by polygenic scores for rhythmicity — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Genetic variation shared with rhythmicity does not account for the association between preschool musicality and school-age verbal cognition — related
  • Polygenic scores for rhythmicity associate with school-age speech-related communication and verbal-cognition outcomes, with two associations passing multiple-testing correction and four at the nominal level
  • Polygenic scores for adult rhythmicity validate ALSPAC preschool and school-age musicality measures, extending genetic associations with rhythmicity to an earlier developmental window
