Evidence¶

Xin Sun 2023¶

Xin Sun, Owen Norton and Shaylene E. Nancekivell. (2023). Beware the myth: learning styles affect parents', children's, and teachers' thinking about children's academic potential. npj Science of Learning 8:46. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00190-x

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Experiment 1 parent sample (N = 94, recruited via Prolific) rated the same two described students. Parents rated visual learners "signiﬁcantly smarter than hands-on learners", d = 0.68.

"parents rated visual learners (M(SD)Visual = 3.02 (0.49)) as signiﬁcantly smarter than hands-on learners ( M(SD)Hands-on= 2.55 (0.74), Fig. 1right panel), t = 4.66, padjusted< 0.001, d = 0.68"

Xin Sun 2023 (2)¶

Xin Sun, Owen Norton and Shaylene E. Nancekivell. (2023). Beware the myth: learning styles affect parents', children's, and teachers' thinking about children's academic potential. npj Science of Learning 8:46. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00190-x

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Same parent sample rated sportiness. Parents "viewed hands-on learners ... as signiﬁcantly sportier than visual learners", d = 1.24, indicating the intelligence judgment is not a global halo effect.

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Learning-Style Preferences — predictor: learners reporting different styles differed here; not a test of matching instruction to style

Related Claims¶

  • Children aged 6–12 rate students described as visual learners as smarter than students described as hands-on learners — related
  • In forced choice, parents and teachers pick the visual learner as smarter and the hands-on learner as sportier, with teachers showing a larger effect — related
  • Hands-on learners are predicted higher grades in art, gym, and music, and are picked as working harder, replicating the smarter judgment — related
  • Parents and teachers predict visual learners earn higher grades in core subjects and hands-on learners higher grades in non-core subjects (pre-registered) — related
