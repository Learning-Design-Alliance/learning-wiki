Evidence¶

Quintas 2020¶

Quintas, A., Bustamante, J.-C., Pradas, F., & Castellar, C. (2020). Psychological effects of gamified didactics with exergames in Physical Education at primary schools: results from a natural experiment. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103874

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Simple ANOVA follow-up of the Time x Treatment interaction on the time transformation dimension of the Dispositional Flow Scale-2, showing significant reduction in the control group with η p2=.046 and significant increase in the experimental group with η p2=.053 (medium effects).

"Specifically, the time transformation of the control group significantly reduced with time (F(1)=8,28, p=.005, η p2=.046), while it significantly increased in the experimental group (F(1)=12.02, p=.001, η p2=.053)."

Quintas 2020 (2)¶

Quintas, A., Bustamante, J.-C., Pradas, F., & Castellar, C. (2020). Psychological effects of gamified didactics with exergames in Physical Education at primary schools: results from a natural experiment. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103874

q2 · i1 · causal · r2

Simple ANOVA follow-up on the autotelic experience dimension, part of the reported Time x Treatment interaction on autotelic experience (F(1)=12.83, p=.000, ηp2=.033, a small effect), showing reduction only in the control group.

"Specifically, t he control group 's autotelic experience significantly reduced wi th time (F(1)=9.16, p=.003, η p2=.051), but it did no t significantly increase in the experimental group."

Quintas 2020 (3)¶

Quintas, A., Bustamante, J.-C., Pradas, F., & Castellar, C. (2020). Psychological effects of gamified didactics with exergames in Physical Education at primary schools: results from a natural experiment. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103874

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Factorial ANOVA on total dispositional flow scores in the same natural experiment; the article reports no interaction effect or main effects, so H4 was not supported. Equivalence was not tested.

"H4 predicted than with time, the gamified exergaming intervention would improve dispositional flow in students more than the non-gamified and non-exergaming intervention. No interaction effect or main effects on dispositional flow took place."
