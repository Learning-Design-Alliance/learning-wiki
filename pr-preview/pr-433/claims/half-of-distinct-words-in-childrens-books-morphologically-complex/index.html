Evidence¶

Korochkina 2025¶

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Corpus analysis of the CYP-LEX database of 105,694 distinct words from 1200 popular British children's books, classified via the MorphoLex database. Table 1 shows morphologically complex words at 45%, 48%, and 51% across the 7–9, 10–12, and 13+ age bands; the article states they "comprise about half of the distinct words in children's books".

"These data make clear that morphologically complex words comprise about half of the distinct words in children's books."

Korochkina 2025 (2)¶

Korochkina, M. & Rastle, K. (2025). Morphology in children's books, and what it means for learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00313-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Same CYP-LEX corpus analysis, comparing age bands on words unique to older bands: "61% (N = 6276)" of words new at 10–12 and "68% (N = 6317)" of words new at 13+ are morphologically complex, supporting the claim that most new words encountered in reading are new morphemic combinations.

"61% (N = 6276) of the words that occur in the 10–12 but not 7–9 age band are morphologically complex, and 68% (N = 6317) of the words that occur in the 13+ but not 10–12 age band are morphologically complex."
