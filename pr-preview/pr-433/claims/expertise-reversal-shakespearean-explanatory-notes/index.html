Evidence¶

Slava Kalyuga 2010¶

Slava Kalyuga, Alexander Renkl. (2010). Expertise reversal effect and its instructional implications: introduction to the special issue. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9102-0

q3 · i? · review · r2

Experiments with Grade 10 novices and Shakespearean experts reading extracts with integrated Modern-English explanatory notes. Novices in the explanatory condition "reported a lower cognitive load and performed better in a comprehension test than the control group," while experts showed the reverse; verbal protocols indicated the explanations became redundant for high-knowledge readers.

"In Experiment 2, the same material was presented to a group of Shakespearean experts, and a reverse effect occurred: the control group outperformed the experimental group."
