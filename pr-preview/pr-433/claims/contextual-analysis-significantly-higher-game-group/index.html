Evidence¶

Shu-Wei Liu 2025¶

Shu-Wei Liu, Hung-Yu Chan, & Huei-Tse Hou. (2025). Role-playing monument exploration: an online educational game with a role-playing mechanism and multi-dimensional scaffolding for monument tours. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10480-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Mann–Whitney U test on the eight-dimension collaborative cognitive experience questionnaire (α = 0.819): only contextual analysis differed significantly, with the control group (M = 3.22) significantly lower than the experimental group (M = 4.38). All other dimensions were non-significant.

"The results show that there was a significant difference between the two groups in terms of contextual analysis (Z = − 2.574, p = 0.010), with the control group having a significantly lower score, indicating that the control group considered that the analysis of the contextual background was less helpful than the experimental group"

Shu-Wei Liu 2025 (2)¶

Shu-Wei Liu, Hung-Yu Chan, & Huei-Tse Hou. (2025). Role-playing monument exploration: an online educational game with a role-playing mechanism and multi-dimensional scaffolding for monument tours. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10480-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Qualitative analysis of open-ended questionnaire responses: control-group learners reported peer collaboration focused on correctness rather than historical context, while experimental-group learners reported that role-playing explanations increased immersion and helped them analyze the situation in the game.

"learners reported that during peer collaboration, their ability to assist each other in analyzing the context was diminished, leading them to primarily concentrate on the correctness of the task"
