Evidence¶

Wendt 2019¶

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Hierarchical linear modeling of 1,132 treatment and 1,132 matched comparison grade-level clusters across 16 states, adjusting for baseline achievement, demographics, and nesting of grades within schools. The analysis found "0.17 of a standard deviation for proficiency rates and 0.13 for scale scores," both reported as p = .01 (as rounded in the table) and significant after BH correction. Both effect sizes are below 0.2 SD.

"The magnitude (or effect size) of the difference was 0.17 of a standard deviation for proficiency rates and 0.13 for scale scores."

Wendt 2019 (2)¶

Wendt, S., Rice, J., & Nakamoto, J. (2019). A cross-state evaluation of MIND Research Institute's ST Math program and math performance. San Francisco, CA: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED601907

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Separate multiple regressions by grade level (392, 366 and 374 grade-level clusters in each group, matched one-to-many and weighted to equal group sizes) adjusting for baseline achievement and school demographics. The report states grade 3 proficiency rates were "0.13 of a standard deviation higher" than comparison clusters; Exhibit 3 prints effect sizes for every grade-level outcome: proficiency 0.13, 0.23 and 0.15 and scale scores 0.12, 0.17 and 0.10 for grades 3, 4 and 5. All but grade 4 proficiency are below 0.2 SD.

"students in clusters of grade level 3 provided with the ST Math program had z-score proficiency rates in math that were, on average, 0.13 of a standard deviation higher compared to students in clusters of grade level 3 not provided with the ST Math program"
