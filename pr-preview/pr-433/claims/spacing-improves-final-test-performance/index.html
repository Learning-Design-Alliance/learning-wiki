Evidence¶

Carpenter 2012¶

Carpenter, S.K., Cepeda, N.J., Rohrer, D., Kang, S.H.K., & Pashler, H. (2012). Using Spacing to Enhance Diverse Forms of Learning: Review of Recent Research and Implications for Instruction. Educational Psychology Review, 24, 369–378. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-012-9205-z

q2 · i? · review · r?

The review reports, as an established finding of research on human learning, that "performance on final tests of learning is improved" when study sessions are "spaced" apart rather than massed. No effect size is printed in the available text.

"This research has shown that performance on final tests of learning is improved if multiple study sessions are separated—i.e., "spaced" apart—in time rather than massed in immediate succession."
