Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • By age 5.25, executive tasks load significantly and consistently on a distinct EC latent factor — related
  • At age 3, executive task performance does not form a coherent latent EC factor independent of processing speed — related
  • Latent EC at age 3 does not predict mathematics achievement, but EC at ages 3.75, 4.5, and 5.25 shows independent prospective links with mathematics — related
  • Executive task accuracy and processing speed improve dramatically with age across the preschool period, with no cohort effect from repeated testing — related
  • A unitary EC factor is preferred over separate working memory and inhibitory control factors at age 5.25 even after accounting for processing speed and language — related
  • The four effortful control subscales load onto a single latent factor in preschoolers — related
  • EF and ToM each show substantial longitudinal stability from 3 to 5 years — related
  • In a meta-analytic database of ten studies, the latent EF factor model was preferred in seven samples, with two favoring the components model and one equal — related
  • Language proficiency predicts residual variance in executive performance not explained by processing speed at all preschool age points — related
