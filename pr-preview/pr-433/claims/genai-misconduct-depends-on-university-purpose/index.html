Conceptual argument in the introduction of this primarily conceptual and analytic article. The authors state that "how we characterise academic integrity, academic misconduct, or the relationship between the two becomes secondary" to the question of the university's purpose; they offer no empirical evidence for the logical claim itself.

"the question of whether students' use of GenAI in higher education constitutes academic misconduct depends, fundamentally, on what we take the purpose of higher education to be"

Related Claims¶

  • Undergraduate students hold mixed views on whether using GenAI for coursework constitutes plagiarism, ranging from not misconduct to serious misconduct — related
  • University administrations endorse contradictory positions on GenAI, encouraging faculty integration while treating AI-generated work as misconduct — related
  • GenAI undermines academic integrity systemically rather than as individual dishonesty, because it optimises plausibility over truth and erodes the conditions for integrity — related
  • The article interprets the ethics gap as students viewing ethics through coursework practices and instructors treating it as an integrity question shaped by lack of institutional clarity — related
  • Student perceptions of AI use in academic work are neutral and range from viewing AI as valid learning assistance to viewing it as cheating, depending on task type, transparency, and assessment design — related
  • Academic integrity was the panel's most frequently cited GenAI policy concern, requiring clear definitions of misconduct and enforceable consequences — related
  • Students express profound confusion about whether GenAI-assisted coursework is original work and whether they can claim authorship — related
