Evidence¶

Gabay 2025¶

Gabay, Y., Jacob, L., Mansour, A. & Hertz, U. (2025). Computational markers show specific deficits for dyslexia and ADHD in complex learning settings. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00323-4

q3 · i2 · associational · r2

Mixed-factorial analysis of percentage of games learned in the ADHD study (N = 21 with ADHD, 32 controls), with task as within-subject and group as between-subject factor. The ADHD group was "significantly poorer" than controls, F(1, 51) = 4.57, p = 0.037, ηp² = 0.08.

"Performance was signiﬁcantly poorer, F (1, 51) = 4.57,p = 0.037, ηp²= 0.08, in the ADHD group (M = 0.62, S.E. = 0.03) than in the control group (M = 0.71, S.E. = 0.02)."

Related Claims¶

  • The group-by-task interaction is not significant for either ADHD or dyslexia comparisons, though simple t-tests show task-specific group differences — related
  • Adults with dyslexia show heightened decay rates than controls in complex learning settings, indicating quicker discounting of learned associations — related
  • Adults with ADHD show reduced reliance on Bayesian inference strategies (lower alpha) than controls in complex learning settings — related
  • Meta-analyses establish that ADHD is associated with poorer performance on tasks measuring inhibition, working memory, planning, and vigilance in children, with overall similar impairment patterns in adults and a strong association with reaction time variability — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Performance is substantially better on the Explicit-Assist task than on the No-Explicit-Assist task in both the ADHD and dyslexia comparisons — related
