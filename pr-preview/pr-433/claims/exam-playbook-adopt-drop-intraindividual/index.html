Evidence¶

Chen 2022¶

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

q3 · i0 · associational · r2

Stratified matching analysis (13 classes), matching on first-exam performance, entrance scores, gender, race, and first-generation status, found adopters "performed an average of 1.75 percentage points ([0.69, 2.81], d = 0.12, p = .001) better".

"students who adopted the Exam Playbook performed an average of 1.75 percentage points ([0.69, 2.81], d = 0.12, p = .001) better on the second exam, compared to those who never used it"

Chen 2022 (2)¶

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

q3 · i0 · associational · r2

In the parallel stratified matching analysis on first-exam users, droppers "performed an average of 1.88 percentage points ([0.64, 3.11], d = 0.14, p = .003) worse" than continuers.

"students who dropped the Exam Playbook performed an average of 1.88 percentage points ([0.64, 3.11], d = 0.14, p = .003) worse, compared to those who kept using it"
