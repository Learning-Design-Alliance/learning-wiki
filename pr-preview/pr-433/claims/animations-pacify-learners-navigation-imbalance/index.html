Evidence¶

Guttormsen Schär 2007¶

Guttormsen Schär, S., & Zimmermann, P. G. (2007). Investigating Means to Reduce Cognitive Load from Animations: Applying Differentiated Measures of Knowledge Representation. Journal of Research on Technology in Education, 40(1), 64–78. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826063 doi:10.1080/15391523.2007.10782497

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Logged click data from the experiment showed the AV group used far fewer navigation actions than the PV and PT groups, although only nine forward clicks were needed in the picture settings; the authors link this to Salomon's invested mental effort account and suggest animations induce a passive learning mode.

"The mean number of navigation activities for the AV group was 8.54, 34.11 for PV and 34.92 for PT ."

Related Claims¶

  • Letting learners pace instruction helped on complex material in one experiment, while learner control in general showed almost no effect across 18 studies — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it

  • Segmenting multimedia instruction into learner-paced segments improves retention and transfer performance with small-to-medium effects — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it

  • Learner flow control over animations is rarely used, and the authors report no correlation between its use and knowledge-test results although the printed Spearman r is 0.464 — related
