Evidence¶

Daniel J. Mitchell 2018¶

Daniel J. Mitchell, Cam-CAN (Cambridge Centre for Ageing and Neuroscience), Rhodri Cusack. (2018). Visual Short-Term Memory Through the Lifespan: Preserved Benefits of Context and Metacognition. Psychology and Aging. https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000265

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Correlational analysis of metacognitive awareness (confidence-error partial correlation) against age in the Cam-CAN sample, run with and without sensorimotor and VSTM-performance adjustment. The decline was minimal after adjusting for VSTM performance.

"metacognitive judgments were found to become less accurately predictive of performance with age (without adjusting for performance: r = −0.23, p < .001; after adjusting for sensorimotor performance: r = −0.14, p < .001; after adjusting for VSTM (RMSE) performance: r = −0.08, p < .05), but on average remained well above chance across the age range"

Related Claims¶

  • Better metacognitive awareness of trial-to-trial VSTM performance is associated with lower error, higher precision, and fewer misbinding errors, but not with item capacity, and this association is invariant across age — related
  • At low memory loads, older adults report less uncertainty than younger adults despite making larger errors, suggesting an overconfidence bias — related
  • VSTM performance declines with advancing adult age in raw error, item capacity, precision, and misbinding, even after adjusting for sensorimotor performance — related
  • Aging does not materially reduce the relative accuracy (resolution) of judgments of learning during encoding, with cross-sectional evidence of no age declines and even age-related increases in aggregate JOL resolution across ages 18 to 81 — related
