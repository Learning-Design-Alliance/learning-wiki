Related Claims¶

  • Synthesized studies of OER in college courses found academic outcomes comparable to traditional textbooks and positive perceptions among students and instructors — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Students achieve the same or better learning outcomes when using OER compared to commercial textbooks, while saving significant amounts of money. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Across 2002-2018, OER efficacy research consistently shows that OER does not harm student learning, with a small positive impact. — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In direct comparisons of OER and commercial textbooks, a strong majority of participants report OER are as good or better. — related
  • Open textbooks appear to offer financial benefits to students without decreasing learning outcomes in higher education — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In studies comparing ratings from separate groups of OER users and CT users, results were mixed: two studies favored CT, two favored OER, and one showed similar ratings. — related
  • Students using OER save significant amounts of money compared to purchasing commercial textbooks. — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • OER efficacy studies are frequently confounded by simultaneous changes in pedagogy or delivery method. — related
