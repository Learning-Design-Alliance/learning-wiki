Alshehri OAO 2026¶

Alshehri OAO, Lamouchi A, Abdellatif MS, Al-Dosari MNA, Mekheimer M and Nemt-allah MA (2026). From tool to scaffold: structured human–AI collaboration and its effects on academic writing and digital critical thinking among Saudi EFL learners. Front. Psychol. 17:1830103. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1830103

q1 · i? · causal · r2

Qualitative thematic analysis of experimental-group reflections (Theme 3, verification as digital critical practice, evidenced in 81% of reflections per Table 5). Theme 2 additionally documented hallucinated citations and cultural or tonal mismatches students identified and repaired.

"Students described cross-checking AI claims via Google Scholar, library databases, reputable news outlets, and official websites, retaining only content that could be externally supported."

Alshehri OAO 2026 (2)¶

Alshehri OAO, Lamouchi A, Abdellatif MS, Al-Dosari MNA, Mekheimer M and Nemt-allah MA (2026). From tool to scaffold: structured human–AI collaboration and its effects on academic writing and digital critical thinking among Saudi EFL learners. Front. Psychol. 17:1830103. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1830103

q1 · i? · qualitative · r?

Qualitative thematic analysis of the same reflections (Theme 4, metacognitive regulation and ethical self-positioning, 100% of reflections). Students positioned AI as a tool, emphasized authorship maintenance, plagiarism avoidance, and alignment with course expectations.

"Looking forward, students outlined specific self-regulation strategies: drafting independently before using AI, restricting AI to defined functions such as grammar checks and vocabulary refinement, setting time limits on AI interaction, and verifying sources immediately rather than near submission."
