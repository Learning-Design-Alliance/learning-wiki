Evidence¶

Hsiao 2022¶

Hsiao, J. H., An, J., Hui, V. K. S., Zheng, Y. & Chan, A. B. (2022). Understanding the role of eye movement consistency in face recognition and autism through integrating deep neural networks and hidden Markov models. npj Science of Learning 7:28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00139-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Simulation result: among 80 partially trained DNN+HMM models, entropy (a consistency measure) correlated negatively with accuracy; the article prints "p < 0.001 R2 = 0.205" but no standardised effect size.

"The entropy of partially trained models was negatively correlated with accuracy, p < 0.001 R2 = 0.205"

Hsiao 2022 (2)¶

Hsiao, J. H., An, J., Hui, V. K. S., Zheng, Y. & Chan, A. B. (2022). Understanding the role of eye movement consistency in face recognition and autism through integrating deep neural networks and hidden Markov models. npj Science of Learning 7:28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00139-6

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Simulation: at epoch 100, eyes- and nose-focused representative patterns showed no accuracy difference (d = 0.16, MA = 0.464 vs MB = 0.457); equivalence was not tested. The article also reports "the AB scale was not correlated with accuracy".

"the patterns A and B of partially trained models did not differ in accuracy on the validation data, t(78) = 0.72, p = 0.475, d = 0.16, MA = 0.464, MB = 0.457"

Related Claims¶

  • In typically developing children aged 6–11, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement consistency but not by eye movement pattern — related
  • Children's eye movement consistency is associated with selective attention and executive function, whereas eye movement pattern relates to working memory — related
  • A less eyes-focused pattern and lower eye movement consistency jointly predict poorer recognition beyond age and cognitive abilities — related
  • A more eyes-focused scanning pattern and higher eye movement consistency are each correlated with better face recognition performance — related
  • In adults, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement pattern, with eye movement consistency adding no additional variance — related
  • In well-trained DNN+HMM face-recognition models, an eyes-focused eye movement pattern yields higher recognition accuracy than a nose-focused pattern — related
