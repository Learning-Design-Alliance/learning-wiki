Evidence¶

Berwid Olga G. 2012¶

Berwid Olga G., Halperin Jeffrey M. (2012). Emerging Support for a Role of Exercise in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Intervention Planning. Current Psychiatry Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-012-0297-4

q3 · i? · review · r2

Randomized controlled trial by Davis et al. of a 13-week exercise program (20 or 40 min/day) versus control in 171 sedentary, overweight 7- to 11-year-old children, assessing cognition with the CAS and achievement with the Woodcock-Johnson Tests of Achievement III.

"A significant dose-response benefit of exercise on executive functioning (i.e., Planning from the CAS) and math scores emerged. No effects were detected on other scales of the CAS or on reading scores."

Berwid Olga G. 2012 (2)¶

Berwid Olga G., Halperin Jeffrey M. (2012). Emerging Support for a Role of Exercise in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Intervention Planning. Current Psychiatry Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11920-012-0297-4

q2 · i? · causal · r1

fMRI pilot study within the Davis et al. trial with 20 children scanned at baseline (control n = 9, exercise n = 11) and post-test (control n = 9, exercise n = 10) while completing an anti-saccade task.

"Those participating in the exercise program showed evidence of increased bilateral prefrontal activity and reduced bilateral posterior parietal activity compared with controls."
