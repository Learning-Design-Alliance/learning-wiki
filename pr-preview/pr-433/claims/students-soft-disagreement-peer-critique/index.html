Evidence¶

Zhou 2025¶

Zhou, J., Hmelo-Silver, C. E., Ryan, Z., Stiso, C., Murphy, D., Danish, J., Chinn, C. A., & Duncan, R. G. (2025). Disagreeing softly: Supporting students in managing disagreement in peer critique. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09438-z

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Conversation-analytic coding of gallery walk breakout-room video in a 5th-grade virtual science club, unit of analysis a dissenting turn. Frequency analysis showed "a substantially higher number of soft forms (64%) compared to direct forms (36%)"; hedging was the most frequent form (n = 56).

"A total of 185 instances of disagreement forms were identified across 156 dissenting turns, with some turns containing 2 forms. Students employed a substantially higher number of soft forms (64%) compared to direct forms (36%)."

Related Claims¶

  • Nearly all peer critique disagreements were resolved, mostly through concession, with co-construction appearing only in soft and mixed disagreements — related
  • Mixed and exclusively soft disagreement episodes featured more extended turns and more perspectives than exclusively direct episodes — related
