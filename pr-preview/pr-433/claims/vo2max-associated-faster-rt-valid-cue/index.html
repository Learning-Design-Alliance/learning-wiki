Related Claims¶

  • Reaction times differ significantly across all three cue conditions, with fastest RT for valid cues and slowest for no-cue stimuli — related
  • Higher cardiorespiratory fitness is associated with faster reaction times for stimuli following invalid cues in healthy male high-school students — related
  • Cardiorespiratory fitness is not associated with reaction times for uncued stimuli in healthy male high-school students — related
  • A significant main effect of stimuli category exists on reaction time in the Posner cue task — related
  • Later time-point of cognitive testing is associated with faster reaction times, with significantly slower RT in the morning — related
  • Cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max) is the physical fitness measure most strongly correlated with cognitive functioning in adolescents — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Neither muscle mass nor weekly training frequency is associated with reaction time across cue conditions in healthy male high-school students — related
  • N-back response time increases and accuracy decreases with cognitive load, and Posner cue conditions differ in speed and accuracy trade-offs — related
