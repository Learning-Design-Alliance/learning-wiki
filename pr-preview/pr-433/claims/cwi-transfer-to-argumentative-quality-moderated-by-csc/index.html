Evidence¶

Anouk ten Peze 2024¶

Anouk ten Peze, Tanja Janssen, Gert Rijlaarsdam, Daphne van Weijen. (2024). Instruction in creative and argumentative writing: transfer and crossover effects on writing process and text quality. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-023-09647-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The abstract of the switching-replications quasi-experiment with four Grade 10 classes reports the panel 1 contrast between the creative writing instruction (CWI) and argumentative writing instruction (AWI) conditions: "This pattern indicates a transfer effect of creative writing instruction on argumentative text quality." The results section reporting the underlying statistics was not available in the supplied text.

"The effects varied per panel. The first panel showed that CWI had an effect on creative text quality compared to AWI, while AWI had no effect on argumentative text quality, compared to CWI. This pattern indicates a transfer effect of creative writing instruction on argumentative text quality."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students' creative writing speed decreased in panel 1 except for students with relatively high Creative Self-Concept, and increased in panel 2
  • Students in both panels were generally positive about the creative writing unit's instructional design, with only two items scoring below neutral and a group difference on one scale — related
  • Implementation fidelity of the creative writing unit was high: absentee rates below about 3% and completion of crucial divergent-thinking and writing assignments between 88% and 94% — related
  • AP participation did not significantly improve self-concept as measured by the Piers-Harris Self-Concept Scale — related
