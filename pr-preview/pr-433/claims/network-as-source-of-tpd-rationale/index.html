Evidence¶

Coppe 2026¶

Coppe, T., Thomas, L., Strijbos, J.-W., & Vanderlinde, R. (2026). The connection between the social network perspective and teacher professional development: A systematic literature review. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2026.100818

q2 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review synthesis of 51 empirical studies (2000–2024) identified via ERIC, Scopus, and PsycINFO. The thematic analysis found the source rationale dominant: "viewing the teacher network as a source of professional development , seen in 32 studies". Studies in this rationale assume more interaction opportunities are better and that isolation from the network core limits TPD.

"The most commonly offered rationale for connecting the social network perspective and TPD was viewing the teacher network as a source of professional development , seen in 32 studies"

Related Claims¶

  • A second rationale treats the network as a catalyst for enactment and sustainability of reforms with TPD (15 studies, mostly US) — related
  • Two further rationales treat the network as purposefully (re)shaped by TPD initiatives or as a latent school capacity (7 studies each) — related
  • The four rationales differ along two dimensions: the network's functional role (independent vs. dependent variable) and its ontological stance (active vs. passive) — related
  • Most reviewed studies used a sociocentric network approach (36 of 51), while 12 used an egocentric approach and 3 combined both — related
  • Included studies conceptualized TPD in ten distinct themes, most often as improvement of instructional practices (20 studies) — related
  • Maintaining access to TPD materials and community was highly valued by faculty in a pandemic-redesigned program — related
  • Prior TPD research focused on teacher impact, leaving student-centered evaluation of TPD for OBL unexamined — related
