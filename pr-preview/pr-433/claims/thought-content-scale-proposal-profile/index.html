J. Carleton Bell 1919¶

J. Carleton Bell. (1919). Composition Scales for Thought Content [Editorial]. The Journal of Educational Psychology, Volume X, September Number. https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Design proposal in the editorial: ten topics of child interest given to a sufficiently large number of children might yield "evenly distributed variations of thought content" amenable to scale divisions. It is a hypothetical plan, not a carried-out study.

"If we should choose ten topics in which children are interested and on which some children think well, and should give each of them to a sufficiently large number of children, we might get such evenly distributed variations of thought content as would lend themselves to the divisions of a scale."

J. Carleton Bell 1919 (2)¶

J. Carleton Bell. (1919). Composition Scales for Thought Content [Editorial]. The Journal of Educational Psychology, Volume X, September Number. https://www.jstor.org/journal/jeducpsych

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

The editorial extends the proposal to diagnosis: a series of exercises across the chosen lines would let a teacher construct what it calls a "fairly accurate 'profile' of his thinking power" for an individual pupil. No such profile is actually constructed in the article.

"In dealing with any given pupil, John Smith, we could determine by a series of exercises his ability to think along each of these lines, and thus could construct a fairly accurate 'profile' of his thinking power."
