Evidence¶

Darmawansah 2025¶

Darmawansah, D., Hwang, G.-J., Lin, C.-J., & Febiyani, F. (2025). An artificial intelligence-supported GFCA learning model to enhance L2 students' role-play performance, English speaking and interaction mindset. Education Tech Research Dev (2025) 73:1451–1479. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10453-6

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Mann–Whitney test on post-test pronunciation scores from the AI speaking app log profile (15 read sentences) in the 45-student quasi-experiment; pronunciation favored AI-GFCA with "r = 0.28".

"students in the AI-GFCA group (Mdn = 63) had better pronunciation scores than students in the AI-C group (Mdn = 55) with U = 168.00, z = − 1.934, p = 0.05, and r = 0.28."

Darmawansah 2025 (2)¶

Darmawansah, D., Hwang, G.-J., Lin, C.-J., & Febiyani, F. (2025). An artificial intelligence-supported GFCA learning model to enhance L2 students' role-play performance, English speaking and interaction mindset. Education Tech Research Dev (2025) 73:1451–1479. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10453-6

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Null Mann–Whitney result for the intonation variable of the AI speaking assessment; no statistically significant group difference was detected and equivalence was not tested.

"In contrast, the intonation scores of the AI-GFCA group (Mdn = 65) and the AI-C group (Mdn = 49.5) did not show a significant difference (U = 175.50, z = − 1.761)."
