Evidence¶

Graesser 2016¶

Graesser, A. C. (2016). Conversations with AutoTutor Help Students Learn. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0086-4

q3 · i? · review · r2

The article summarizes multiple AutoTutor studies comparing learning to reading text for equivalent time, reporting gains "between 0.3 sigma (Nye et al. 2014) and 0.8 (Graesser et al. 2008)". Effect sizes are described as substantially higher against pre-test and no-study controls.

"reports covering multiple studies have reported average learning gains that vary between 0.3 sigma (Nye et al. 2014) and 0.8 (Graesser et al. 2008) when compared to reading text for an equivalent amount of time"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The review reports that AutoTutor improves learning by nearly one letter grade compared with reading a textbook for an equivalent time or with a pretest — related
  • AutoTutor's learning gains are virtually equivalent to those of 1-to-1 human tutoring in direct comparisons — related
  • AutoTutor achieved learning gains comparable to human tutors in physics and computer literacy — related
  • The talking head contributes little to AutoTutor's learning gains; the content of the conversation matters — related
  • AutoTutor cannot handle most student questions, so student question frequency extinguishes quickly in tutoring sessions — related
  • AutoTutor produces learning gains averaging about 0.8 standard deviations over static-textbook controls, and bystanders cannot distinguish its tutoring turns from a human tutor's — related
  • Early AutoTutor versions gained about 0.5 σ over textbook controls, with gains depending on test type (cloze 0.64 σ, deep 0.28 σ, shallow 0.15 σ) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Reinforcement learning used for micro-adaptation showed potential learning gains of up to 1 sigma over poorly-optimized dialog or no dialog (per the article, citing Chi et al. 2014) — related
  • In WHY2 physics studies, AutoTutor outperformed read-textbook and no-materials controls and performed comparably to WHY2/Atlas and non-expert human tutors — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
