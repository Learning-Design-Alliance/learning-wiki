Related Claims¶

  • Extraneous cognitive load arising from limited scope and high distance in the rule-based game pushed the facilitator toward first- and second-order interventions instead of the third-order interventions the learning goal required — related
  • In a rule-based simulation game with narrow scope and high distance, facilitator interventions were predominantly first- and second-order and participant learning was mainly first- and second-order — related
  • Participants in the rule-based simulation game perceived it as demanding, and learning and applying its procedures and rules hindered investment in their learning goals — related
  • Cognitive Overload Degrades Learning — related
  • In an open simulation game with wide scope and low distance, second-order interventions effectively elicited second-order responses — related
