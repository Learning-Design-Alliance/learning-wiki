Evidence¶

Matt Dennis 2016¶

Matt Dennis, Judith Masthoff, Chris Mellish. (2016). Adapting Progress Feedback and Emotional Support to Learner Personality. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0059-7

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Crowdsourced online experiment (Amazon Mechanical Turk) with a 6x2 between-subjects design; participants gave progress feedback and emotional support to a fictional learner with high or low conscientiousness at one of six scores. The authors report that "The score had a significant effect on all of the different types of emotional support used and slant employed."

"The score had a significant effect on all of the different types of emotional support used and slant employed. Hypothesis H1 (the type of emotional support given will depend on the score the learner achieved) is thus confirmed."

Matt Dennis 2016 (2)¶

Matt Dennis, Judith Masthoff, Chris Mellish. (2016). Adapting Progress Feedback and Emotional Support to Learner Personality. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0059-7

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Post-hoc pairwise comparison (Bonferroni corrected) of score effects in the same conscientiousness experiment. The authors report "Praise is only used for passing scores, which supports H1a", though praise was seldom used for the passing scores of 55% and 70%.

"For Praise, a pairwise comparison yields three subsets. Praise is only used for passing scores, which supports H1a."
