Evidence¶

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee 2022¶

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee, Zachary C. K. Hawes and Kelly S. Mix. (2022). Spatial thinking as the missing piece in mathematics curricula. npj Science of Learning 7:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00128-9

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The meta-analysis moderator analysis found spatial training effects on mathematics "increased with age"; the authors offer two possible explanations, including older children's greater proficiency with spatial representations on novel problems. No effect size is printed.

"We also found that spatial training effects on mathematics increased with age, perhaps because older children are grappling with higher-level mathematics content that may benefit more from spatial visualization skills"

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee 2022 (2)¶

Katie A. Gilligan-Lee, Zachary C. K. Hawes and Kelly S. Mix. (2022). Spatial thinking as the missing piece in mathematics curricula. npj Science of Learning 7:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00128-9

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The meta-analysis found training duration "had no significant effect" on transfer to mathematics, suggesting even short exposure may reach a minimum threshold in spatial skill. This is a null moderator finding; no effect size is printed.

"other moderators such as duration of training had no significant effect on transfer of spatial training gains to mathematics, suggesting that even short exposure to spatial intervention may enable children to reach a minimum threshold in spatial skill"
