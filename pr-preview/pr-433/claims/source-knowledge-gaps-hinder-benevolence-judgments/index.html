Evidence¶

Carita Kiili 2026¶

Carita Kiili, Ivar Bråten, and Julie Coiro. (2026). The framework of prior knowledge in online credibility evaluation. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2026.2717081

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review reports, citing Abel et al. (2024), an experiment in which lower and upper secondary students judged fictitious Twitter profiles with manipulated expertise and benevolence; "Making inferences about benevolence was particularly difficult for lower secondary school students." No effect size is printed.

"In their study, lower and upper secondary school students were asked to judge the trustworthiness of fictitious Twitter profiles. The profiles’ expertise and benevolence were manipulated, requiring students to infer benevolence based on the profiles’ affiliation. Making inferences about benevolence was particularly difficult for lower secondary school students."

Carita Kiili 2026 (2)¶

Carita Kiili, Ivar Bråten, and Julie Coiro. (2026). The framework of prior knowledge in online credibility evaluation. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2026.2717081

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review reports, citing Wertgen and Richter (2023), a reading-time finding: posts whose "source message contradicted the expected argument position" took longer to read than aligned messages, suggesting readers use source knowledge about specific organizations in validation. No effect size is printed.

"reading short social media posts where the source message contradicted the expected argument position (e.g., a WWF message opposing climate change) took longer than reading messages aligned with the expected argument position."
