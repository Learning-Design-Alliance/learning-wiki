Evidence¶

Nizamani 2026¶

Nizamani, S. B., Lee, Y., Donekal Chandrashekar, N., Ellis, M., & Ramakrishnan, N. (2026). Understanding Student Use of Large Language Models Across Computer Science Subfields. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01158 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2610.01158

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive analysis of post-assignment reflection surveys from 211 students who completed all seven assignments in a within-subject course design. The results section reports that "Algorithms (89.6%) and Web Development (80.6%) showing the highest usage, and Software Engineering the lowest (15.2%)"; Networking and Databases showed moderate adoption (58.3%).

"LLM adoption varied substantially by assignment, with Algorithms (89.6%) and Web Development (80.6%) showing the highest usage, and Software Engineering the lowest (15.2%)."

Related Claims¶

  • Prompt-count distributions differ significantly across assignments, with moderate prompting most common and extended prompting (10+) rare except in Algorithms (18.0%) — related
  • Students predominantly conceptualize the LLM as an Assistant rather than Peer or Expert, and role distributions differ significantly across assignments — related
  • Verification is nearly universal among LLM users, and most students combine multiple verification strategies rather than relying on a single one — related
