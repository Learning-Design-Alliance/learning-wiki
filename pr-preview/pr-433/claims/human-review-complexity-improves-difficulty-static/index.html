Evidence¶

Antonio Laverghetta Jr. 2024¶

Antonio Laverghetta Jr., Simone Luchini, Averie Linell, Roni Reiter-Palmon and Roger Beaty. (2024). The creative psychometric item generator: a framework for item generation and validation using large language models. CREAI 2024: Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Creativity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00202 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2409.00202

q2 · i? · design · r?

Human content review in which five annotators with prior creativity-rating experience rated CPIG items on five-point complexity and difficulty scales, blinded to round, using a missing-data approach (Results §4.4, Fig. 6). Findings are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

"For complexity, we see a definite improvement from round five, with a much larger fraction ofitems achieving the ideal complexity level than was present in round one. Trends are more staticfor difficulty as the distributions are quite similar to each other, especially at the ideal difficultylevel."

Antonio Laverghetta Jr. 2024 (2)¶

Antonio Laverghetta Jr., Simone Luchini, Averie Linell, Roni Reiter-Palmon and Roger Beaty. (2024). The creative psychometric item generator: a framework for item generation and validation using large language models. CREAI 2024: Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Creativity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00202

q2 · i? · design · r?

Reliability analysis of the same five-annotator content review (Results §4.4). The article prints ICCs of 0.52 (complexity) and 0.49 (difficulty) for absolute agreement on average ratings and attributes the modest agreement to the subjectivity of rating creativity.

"We obtained intraclass correlations of0.52 for complexity and0.49 for difficulty, for absoluteagreement on the average ratings, indicating a modest rater agreement."
