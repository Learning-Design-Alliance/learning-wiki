Evidence¶

Thakore 2024¶

Thakore, K., Das, T., Jahan, S., & Sweller, N. (2024). From Hands to Mind: How Gesture, Emotional Valence, and Individual Differences Impact Narrative Recall. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 111. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09948-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Online experiment with 100 undergraduates reading three emotive narratives aloud, half instructed to gesture. In the linear mixed model predicting free recall, "the spontaneous condition performed better than the instructed gesture condition, χ2(1) = 11.84, p < 0.001"; no effect size was printed.

"There was a significant main effect of the gesture condition (see Table 1 for descriptive statistics), such that the spontaneous condition performed better than the instructed gesture condition, χ2(1) = 11.84, p < 0.001."

Related Claims¶

  • Gesture instructions impaired narrative recall only for participants with average or above-average verbal memory, not those below average (Study 1) — related

  • In Study 2, spontaneous gesturing produced better specific recall than instructed gesturing, and negative narratives were recalled better than positive — related
  • Gesture instructions hindered specific recall for participants with lower propensity to gesture (Study 2) — related
  • Propensity to gesture and verbal memory positively predicted free recall, and the two were uncorrelated (Study 2) — related
  • Emotional valence consistently influences narrative recall, with negative narratives most memorable, while instructed gesturing shows mixed effectiveness — related
