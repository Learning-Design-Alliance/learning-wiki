Evidence¶

Arneson 2024¶

Arneson, A., Hinze-Pifer, R., Franklin, K., & Johnson, D.W. (2024). Removing police officers from Chicago schools: Trends and outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/removing-police-officers-from-Chicago-schools

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Descriptive categorization of CPS high schools into three equal-sized groups by 2022-23 suspension rate (Figure 7). Similar patterns held for behavioral and high-level infraction rates.

"of the 29 high schools with the lowest suspension rates in 2022–23, eight had an SRO presence (1-2 SROs). For the 29 high schools with the highest suspension rates, 22 had an SRO presence."

Arneson 2024 (2)¶

Arneson, A., Hinze-Pifer, R., Franklin, K., & Johnson, D.W. (2024). Removing police officers from Chicago schools: Trends and outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/removing-police-officers-from-Chicago-schools

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of school enrollment and SRO status (Figure 7); only seven of the smallest 29 high schools had no SRO presence. Schools serving the lowest proportions of free/reduced-price lunch eligible students were most likely to have removed SROs before 2020.

"In 2022–23, 19 of the 29 high schools with the highest student enrollment had no SRO presence."
