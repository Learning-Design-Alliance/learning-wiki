Evidence¶

Ricker Timothy J. 2014¶

Ricker Timothy J., Cowan Nelson. (2014). Differences between Presentation Methods in Working Memory Procedures: A Matter of Working Memory Consolidation. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034301

q3 · i? · causal · r2

General Discussion statement summarizing the four experiments: duration-based forgetting appeared in every experiment under both presentation methods, and the authors argue it cannot be explained by retroactive interference accounts because no interfering events occurred during retention.

"Significant forgetting was always observed as a function of the retention-interval duration, regardless of presentation method."

Ricker Timothy J. 2014 (2)¶

Ricker Timothy J., Cowan Nelson. (2014). Differences between Presentation Methods in Working Memory Procedures: A Matter of Working Memory Consolidation. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034301

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Cross-experiment synthesis in the General Discussion comparing mean items forgotten between 1 and 12 s: conditions with long consolidation times (.19-.31) versus short consolidation times (Expt. 1 simultaneous =.39, Expt. 2 simultaneous =.67, Expt. 3 sequential=.41, simultaneous=.45).

"Long consolidation times always led to much slower rates of time-based forgetting (mean items forgotten between 1 and 12s; Expt. 1 sequential =.19, Expt. 2 sequential =.22, Expt. 4 sequential=.22, simultaneous=.31) than did short consolidation times"
