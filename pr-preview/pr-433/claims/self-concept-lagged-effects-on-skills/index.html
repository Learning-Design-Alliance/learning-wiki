RI-CCLPM analysis of ProFAN mathematics data (N = 9,142). Self-concept "predicted future skill gains in the form of lagged effects" (β2 = [.111, .159], p < .001) across approximately 8-month semester intervals; the contemporaneous path was fixed to zero. No standardized effect size is printed for this path in this section.

"Mathematics self-concept, in turn, predicted future skill gains in the form of lagged effects (β2 =[ .111,. 159], p<. 001), whereas contemporaneous effects were nonsignificant and fixed to zero for parsimony."

Related Claims¶

  • Reciprocal effects are robust to time-invariant confounders, but the French lagged self-concept effect did not survive the lag2 carryover stress test — related
  • LCSM on the same simulated PALMA-like data shows negative cross-lagged effects, contradicting the reciprocal effects model — reports the opposite
  • In German secondary-school mathematics, effects of self-concept on achievement are lagged (lag1) whereas effects of achievement on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0) — related
  • STARTS model shows a positive effect of prior achievement on subsequent self-concept but no effect of prior self-concept on subsequent achievement — reports the opposite
  • Academic skills predict contemporaneous (within-semester) changes in academic self-concept in both mathematics and French — related
  • The temporal asymmetry in effect sizes: contemporaneous skill effects on self-concept are moderate (d = 0.33 in French) while alternative cross-effects are null or negligible — related
  • The meaning of theoretical arrows (causal vs predictive, timescale, functional form, magnitude) is unclear, and cross-lagged effect signs can change with assessment intervals — related
