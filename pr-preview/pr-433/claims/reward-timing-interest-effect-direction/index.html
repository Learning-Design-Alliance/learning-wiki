Evidence¶

Zhongyu Hu & Jiongjiong Yang 2025¶

Zhongyu Hu & Jiongjiong Yang. (2025). Timing of reward cues alters the interaction between reward and interest on memory over time. npj Science of Learning 10:82. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00374-7

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

ANOVA on the interest effect (accuracy difference between high- and low-interest conditions) across experiments, with a printed effect size of ηp2 = 0.12. At 1 day with pre-clip cues, "the interest effect was significantly weaker under reward than no-reward conditions"; with post-clip cues it was significantly stronger under reward (p = 0.002); neither pattern appeared at 10 min.

"results showed a signi ﬁcant three-way interaction among experiment, reward, and delay on the interest effect (F (1, 81) = 11.52,p = 0.001, ηp 2 = 0.12)"

Learner Variables¶

  • Interest — moderator: an instructional effect differs with it
  • Long-Term Memory — outcome: instruction changes it
  • Motivation — moderator: an instructional effect differs with it

Related Claims¶

  • Rewards attenuated forgetting for low-interest clips when cued before stimuli but for high-interest clips when cued after, per the forgetting-rate analysis — related
  • When reward cues follow stimuli, rewards improve memory for high-interest clips at 1 day, increasing the interest effect (additive effect) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • When reward cues precede stimuli, rewards improve memory for low-interest clips at 1 day, decreasing the interest effect (undermining effect) — related
  • The timing of reward cues moderates the reward-by-interest interaction on memory over time (four-way interaction), with effects emerging only after consolidation — related
  • Reward cues and delay conditions did not influence participants' interest ratings, confirming the independence of the reward manipulation from interest — related
