Evidence¶

Mok 2022¶

Mok, S. Y., Rupp, D., & Holzberger, D. (2022). What kind of individual support activities in interventions foster pre-service and beginning teachers' self-efficacy? A meta-analysis. Preprint submitted for peer-review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100552

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r?

Meta-regression model 1, controlling for mastery experience, across the meta-analytic sample. The moderator for feedback on lesson plans showed "a large effect (g = 1.033" with p = .056, slightly missing the .05 significance level.

"providing feedback on a lesson plan was the only moderator of the overall effect on teacher self-efficacy that was very close to the significance level of .05 and it showed a large effect (g = 1.033, SE = .273, p = .056, CI[-.013, .821], Table 3a)."

Mok 2022 (2)¶

Mok, S. Y., Rupp, D., & Holzberger, D. (2022). What kind of individual support activities in interventions foster pre-service and beginning teachers' self-efficacy? A meta-analysis. Preprint submitted for peer-review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100552

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r?

Meta-regression model 2 additionally controlled for research design, teacher self-efficacy measurement, and teacher group. The moderator effect was "slightly reduced" to g = .849 with p = .082, still above the .05 threshold.

"providing feedback on lesson plans remained a moderator of the overall effect on teacher self-efficacy –again close to the significance level of .05– and was slightly reduced when controlling for the methodological variables, but still indicated a large effect (g = .849, SE = .785, p = .082, CI[-.057, .736]; Table 3b)."
