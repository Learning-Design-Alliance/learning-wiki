Evidence¶

trust-mixed¶

Seth Bernstein and Naaz Sibia. 2026. Flawed but Memorable: Student Critical Reception of Interest-Personalized GenAI Analogies in Computing Education. Koli Calling '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06095 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.06095

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

RQ3 interview accounts in the ten-participant qualitative study. The article reports the pattern was not uniform: P1 became less critical of tailored analogies while P7 trusted them less and shared the bare minimum about herself.

"P1 reported exactly the reduction in scrutiny our framework anticipated, saying that knowing the analogy was tailored to them left them "less critical about it because I could trust it more." But P7 reported the opposite association between personal relevance and trust, treating heavy personalization as a reason for suspicion rather than comfort"

expertise-scrutiny¶

Seth Bernstein and Naaz Sibia. 2026. Flawed but Memorable: Student Critical Reception of Interest-Personalized GenAI Analogies in Computing Education. Koli Calling '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06095

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

The article reports that domain expertise, not personal interest, consistently tracked scrutiny, quoting P9 that knowing the concepts let her tell when an analogy went wrong without increasing trust. The authors treat this as an emergent qualitative interpretation, not a causal effect.

"What tracked scrutiny consistently, across participants, was domain expertise rather than personal interest per se: P9 noted that their ability to audit an analogy came from prior knowledge of the target concept, not affection for the source domain"
