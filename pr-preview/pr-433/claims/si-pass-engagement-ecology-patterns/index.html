Evidence¶

Puttaert 2025¶

Puttaert, N., Dachet, D., Baye, A., Quertemont, E., Leduc, L., Nyssen, A.-S., & Verpoorten, D. (2025). Effects of SI-PASS on a high-risk course – A randomized controlled trial. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102042. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102042

q3 · i? · causal · r2

RCT comparison of the number of online exercises completed between experimental and control groups using LMS learning analytics. "No statistical difference was found in engagement with the online exercises" between the two randomly assigned groups.

"No statistical difference was found in engagement with the online exercises (number of online exercises completed, irrespective of the accuracy of the answers) between the groups (t(182) = .48, p = .63)."

Puttaert 2025 (2)¶

Puttaert, N., Dachet, D., Baye, A., Quertemont, E., Leduc, L., Nyssen, A.-S., & Verpoorten, D. (2025). Effects of SI-PASS on a high-risk course – A randomized controlled trial. Learning and Instruction, 95, 102042. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.102042

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Correlation matrix computed for SI-PASS participants only, relating meeting attendance to online exercise interaction. The correlation was significant at "r(90) = .33, p = .001", a printed effect size of r = .33, suggesting students engaged with one support tend to engage with the other.

"Yet, a correlation matrix calculated for SI-PASS students only reveal a significant relationship between their attendance at meetings and their interaction with online exercises (r(90) = .33, p = .001)"
