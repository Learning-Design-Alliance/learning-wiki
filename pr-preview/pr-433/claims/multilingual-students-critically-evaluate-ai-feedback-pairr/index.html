Evidence¶

MacArthur M 2025¶

MacArthur M, Minnillo S, Sperber L, Whithaus C and Stillman N (2025) GIFT-AI: teaching the game and leveling the field: Peer and AI Review + Reflection in a business writing course. Front. Commun. 10:1615752. https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2025.1615752

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

Case-study analysis of three multilingual international students (Pengxi, Haoyu, Thuy) in the Business Writing course, drawing on their reflections and the instructor's observations. The authors state this pattern of agreement is consistent with their larger sample and see evidence the model can encourage students to maintain their voice.

"Reassuringly, all three critically evaluated AI feedback. For example, Haoyu disagreed with ChatGPT's suggestion to add more reasons and solutions to his internal memo, justified why he did not adopt that suggestion, and commented that "the peer review will be more helpful than the chat. ""

Related Claims¶

  • Business Writing students valued AI feedback for higher-order and sentence-level comments but found it sometimes too general, while appreciating peers' contextual knowledge — related

  • After PAIRR, most students prefer combined peer and AI feedback over either source alone — related
  • Synchronous AI suggestions shape subsequent writing, offering new ideas but risking influence over the writer's voice and argument — related
  • Students encountered usability obstacles: text-recognition errors, feedback-generation delays, and overly complex vocabulary — related
  • AI and peer feedback differed in complementary ways: AI gave general, rubric-driven, actionable feedback while peers gave more specific, contextual feedback — related

  • Comparative evaluation of AI output taught students that AI is not infallible and reinforced critical reading — related
