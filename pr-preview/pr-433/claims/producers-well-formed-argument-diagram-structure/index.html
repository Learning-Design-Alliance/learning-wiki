Evidence¶

Marije van Amelsvoort 2016¶

Marije van Amelsvoort, Alfons Maes. (2016). Show me your opinion. Perceptual cues in creating and reading argument diagrams. Instructional Science 44:335–357. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9378-9

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Production study with 20 first-year bachelor students drawing argument diagrams on a whiteboard from pre-given for/against argument cards. A binomial test showed "the proportion of wellformedness of .80 was higher than the expected .50, p = .012"; no effect size was printed.

"As expected, Table 1 shows that most of the diagrams have a basic geometry: circles/ovals (as in Fig. 4a) and squares/rectangles (as in Fig. 4b) comprised 80 % of all diagrams. We performed binomial tests for all the main categories, testing the occurrence of perceptual cues against chance. A binomial test indicated that the proportion of wellformedness of .80 was higher than the expected .50, p = .012."
