Evidence¶

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies 2012¶

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. (2012). WWC Intervention Report: Adolescent Literacy — Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Findings table in Appendix C for the Fuchs et al. (1997) cluster-randomized study of 12 schools and 120 students. The intervention group mean was 6.02 (1.79) versus 5.18 (1.66) for comparison, a mean difference of 0.84, effect size 0.48, improvement index +19, with the original study reporting p < 0.05.

"CRAB: Questions Correct Average age: 9.8 years 12 schools/ 120 students 6.02 (1.79) 5.18 (1.66) 0.84 0.48 +19 < 0.05"

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies 2012 (2)¶

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. (2012). WWC Intervention Report: Adolescent Literacy — Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

q3 · i? · causal · r2

WWC re-analysis of the same study, correcting for clustering of students within schools. The note states the adjusted effect "was not statistically significant (p = 0.12)", so significance differs from the original study's report; equivalence was not tested.

"When adjusted for clustering, the WWC-calculated effect on the Questions Correct score of the CRAB was not statistically significant (p = 0.12)."

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies 2012 (3)¶

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. (2012). WWC Intervention Report: Adolescent Literacy — Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/

q3 · i? · review · r2

The Effectiveness Summary section states the WWC rating rationale: one study showed substantively important positive effects and no studies showed negative effects, yielding a rating of "potentially positive effects, with a small extent of evidence."

"Thus, for the comprehension domain, one study showed substantively important positive effects. This results in a rating of potentially positive effects, with a small extent of evidence."

Related Claims¶

  • Peer-assisted instruction and small-group instruction by adults have large, robust short-term effect sizes for K-6 struggling students — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • The evidence base for PALS in the Adolescent Literacy review rests on a single randomized trial of 120 students across 12 schools, rated as meeting standards with reservations — related
  • PALS effects on the learning-disability subsets analyzed separately were not statistically significant, while the full-sample effect was significant in one study (Fuchs et al., 1997) and not in the other (Saenz et al., 2005) — related
  • PALS shows no discernible effects on mathematics for students with learning disabilities — related
  • PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading comprehension for students with learning disabilities (average effect size 0.75, not statistically significant) — related
  • PALS shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency for students with learning disabilities, with a substantively important but not statistically significant WWC-calculated effect size of 0.41 in Saenz et al. (2005) — related
  • Clustering effects in education random-assignment trials vary by design but are typically large, requiring large school samples — related
  • Disciplinary Literacy Instruction Improves Comprehension — related
  • The WWC rates The Expert Mathematician as having potentially positive effects on middle school mathematics achievement — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Earlier meta-analyses report small positive effects for computer-assisted literacy instruction (0.19; 0.16) — related
