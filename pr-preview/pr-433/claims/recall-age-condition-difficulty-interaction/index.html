Evidence¶

Gönül 2021¶

Gönül, G., Tsalas, N., Paulus, M. (2021). The effect of time pressure on metacognitive control: developmental changes in self-regulation and efficiency during learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09262-y

q3 · i? · causal · r2

GLMM on number of recalled items in the study time allocation experiment (183 participants). Main effects of condition, F(1, 720) = 73.917, p < 0.001, and difficulty, F(1, 720) = 152.745, p < 0.001, were also reported.

"Finally, there was also a significant three-way interaction between age group, condition and difficulty, F(2, 720) = 3.892, p = 0.021."

Gönül 2021 (2)¶

Gönül, G., Tsalas, N., Paulus, M. (2021). The effect of time pressure on metacognitive control: developmental changes in self-regulation and efficiency during learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09262-y

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The article's follow-up of the three-way interaction: in the timed condition with difficult items, adults recalled significantly more items than 10-year-olds, t(720) = 4.994, p < 0.001, and 14-year-olds, t(720) = 3.242, p < 0.001.

"Careful examination of the interaction effects reveal that results mostly based on the dramatic difference between adults and 10-year-olds in the most challenging condition: timed condition with the difficult items."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Judgments of learning are higher for easy than difficult items at all ages and increase with age, confirming the difficulty manipulation aligned with subjective difficulty — related
  • Mild time pressure increases learning efficiency: recall per mean second of study time is higher in the timed than the untimed condition for all age groups — related
  • Learning efficiency increases with age, with adults and 14-year-olds more efficient than 10-year-olds, and no age-by-condition interaction — related
  • Study time allocation becomes more strategic with age: older learners allocate relatively more time to difficult items, and the easy-difficult allocation gap is larger in the untimed condition — related
