Evidence¶

Lucero 2024¶

Lucero, M., Montanero, M., & van Boxtel, C. (2024). Semiempty collaborative concept mapping in history education: students' engagement in historical reasoning and coconstruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09659-7

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Chi-square analysis of coded interaction utterances from 10 randomly chosen dyads (5 per condition) in two Spanish history classes. Causal explanation reached 49.9% of concept-mapping utterances versus 8.9% in summary writing; the authors report students "engaged more in argumentation and causal explanation". No effect size printed.

"Chi-square analyses revealed significant differences between the summary writing and the semiempty concept mapping condition (χ 2 = 420.31, p < .01). Compared to the summary writing condition, students in the semiempty concept mapping condition engaged more in argumentation and causal explanation"

Related Claims¶

  • Summary writing produced no significant comprehension improvement and the most disputational talk, and did not significantly differ from completing a map in learning outcomes — related
  • Summary-writing dyads produce more utterances overall, but most reflect mechanical dictation and copying rather than historical reasoning — related
  • Semiempty concept mapping dyads use substantive historical and metahistorical concepts more often than summary-writing dyads — related
  • Interaction during semiempty concept mapping shows higher transactivity: less elicitation and more externalization and integration than summary writing — related
  • Concept-mapping dyads produce significantly more re-elaborations (corrections and precisions) of partners' ideas, an indicator of collaborative historical reasoning — related
  • Evaluative questions elicit more historical reasoning than explanatory questions — related
  • Teacher-guided whole-class discussions show more contextualization, explanatory questions, and abstract concepts than student dyad discussions — related
