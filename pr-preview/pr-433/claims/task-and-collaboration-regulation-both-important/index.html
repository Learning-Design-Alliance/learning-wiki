Evidence¶

Nadira Saab 2012¶

Nadira Saab. (2012). Team regulation, regulation of social activities or co-regulation: Different labels for effective regulation of learning in CSCL. Metacognition Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9085-5

q1 · i? · review · r2

The article asserts, as a conceptual statement introducing the special issue, that "Both regulation of task performance and regulation of the collaboration process are important for effective CSCL," because learners must coordinate their collaboration to reach a common goal. The article offers this as a framing argument rather than a tested result.

Related Claims¶

  • Regulation at the dyadic and/or group level is positively related to group performance in CSCL — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Regulation of social activities positively predicts group performance in CSCL inquiry tasks — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Collaborative activities in CSCL chat cluster into four factors: information discussion, regulation of task-related activities, regulation of social activities, and social activities — related
  • Instruction combining communication rules (RIDE) and a hypothesis tool (CHT) increases team regulation in computer-supported collaborative inquiry, and team regulation relates to learning outcomes only in that combined condition — related
  • Secondary students in CSCL devote much of their communication to regulating task performance, especially planning the task and monitoring task progress — related
  • Performing social activities negatively predicts group performance in CSCL inquiry tasks — related
  • In CSCL history inquiry, regulation of social activities predicts group performance while social activities are negatively related to group performance — related
  • Game-structured forced collaboration prevented free riding and made continuous information sharing the most important learning outcome — related
