Randomized comparison for second-cohort fifth graders taught by teachers in the study both years found a positive, statistically significant ReadAbout impact on the social studies reading comprehension assessment with "effect size: 0.22", statistically significantly larger than the first-cohort impact for the same teachers.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Student subgroup impact differences were rarely statistically significant: six of 288 differences in the second cohort and eight of 360 subgroup analyses in the follow-up year. — related
  • ReadAbout had a positive and significant impact in the study's second year among teachers with one year of experience using the intervention — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Project CRISS, ReadAbout, and Read for Real showed no significant impacts on reading comprehension in the study's first year — related
  • The four supplemental reading comprehension curricula had no statistically significant impacts on students one year after the end of their implementation (Cohort 1 follow-up in sixth grade). — related
  • Impacts of the curricula were not statistically significantly larger after schools had one year of experience using them (Cohort 2 fifth graders). — reports the opposite
  • Teacher-practices subgroup differences were essentially null: none were statistically significant for the second cohort, and five of 60 were significant in the follow-up year. — reports the opposite
  • After one school year of implementation (first cohort), the curricula produced no statistically significant positive impacts, and four statistically significant negative impacts were observed. — related
  • A national evaluation design was produced to assess supplemental reading comprehension programs for fifth-grade students — related
