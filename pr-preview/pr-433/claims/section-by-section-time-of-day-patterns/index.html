Analysis of Table VI (third series, infants): marks compared section by section from the highest to the lowest preliminary-mark bands for the 9.40 A.M. and 10.25 A.M. groups; the article states the final-test differences favour the later-working sections. No effect size is printed.

W. H. Winch 1911 (2)¶

W. H. Winch. (1911). Mental Adaptation During the School Day as Measured by Arithmetical Reasoning.—Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mental+Adaptation+During+the+School+Day+as+Measured+by+Arithmetical+Reasoning

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Analysis of Table X (fifth series, boys): each preliminary-mark section of the 9.40 A.M. group advanced more than the corresponding section of the 11.30 A.M. group. The article attributes this reversal to the many boys who began paid work at 6.30 A.M. No effect size is printed.

"In the case of every section we find the children working their final tests earlier in the morning have made a greater advance on their preliminary work than those working later."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In a Standard I infants class, the group working arithmetical reasoning tests at 10.25 A.M. improved more on preliminary records than the group working at 9.40 A.M. — related
  • In a boys' school where three-fourths of pupils rose early for work, the earlier-working group (9.40 A.M.) improved more (about 14 per cent) than the later-working group (11.30 A.M., 9 per cent). — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In a London girls' school class, the group working problematic arithmetic later in the morning improved about 5 or 6 per cent more than the early group — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Improvement by practice continues in both morning and afternoon groups, but the morning group improves faster across weeks — reports the opposite
  • In a Standard VII girls' class, the later-working group (11.25 A.M.) improved 21 per cent on preliminary records versus 15 per cent for the earlier-working group (10 A.M.). — related
  • The equal-group tests showed high test-retest and between-group correlations, supporting the reliability of the arithmetical-reasoning marking method. — related
  • In a London boys' school class, the group working problematic arithmetic later in the morning improved about 5 per cent more than the group working it early — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In the lowest mnemonic section, afternoon workers improved more than morning workers, contrary to the suggestion that the more strongly endowed do better in the afternoon — related
