Evidence¶

Benjamin Mawdsley 2026¶

Benjamin Mawdsley, Tom Quilter, Richard Turner, Sarah Jackson, and Paul Edwards. (2026). Archetypes or ability? Clustering for modelling student mathematical competence. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26063 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.26063

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Comparison of LogRegs (single average-grade feature) and LogRegm (19 per-question features) in the predictive evaluation; Table 1 shows accuracy 0.769±0.005 versus 0.778±0.005 with overlapping error bars. The improvement is described as "quite small".

"Despite adding many more features, the performance improvement between a single feature logistic regression and a multi question performance is quite small, supporting the conclusion from [20] that the dominant factor in predicting performance is overall mathematical competence."

Related Claims¶

  • Cluster profiles of student mathematics performance are dominated by overall ability, with most clusters being linear rescalings of average exam performance — related
  • Explainable models including logistic regression, kNN and a Bernoulli Mixture Model all reach around 78% accuracy in predicting question-level pass/fail, with well-calibrated probabilities — related
  • Ablation of feature-vector models: time-window features add no predictive power to logistic regression but boost a feedforward network, and total count features substantially boost performance on all datasets — related
  • Supplementary nonlinear models yield only modest AUC gains over the calibrated logistic baseline — related
  • Prior mathematical accuracy is the dominant propensity factor: over half of January accuracy variance and under one-fifth of fluency variance predicted June performance — related
