Evidence¶

Lisa Bardach 2024¶

Lisa Bardach, Aki Schumacher, Ulrich Trautwein, Enkelejda Kasneci, Maike Tibus, Franz Wortha, Peter Gerjets & Tobias Appel. (2024). Taking another look at intelligence and personality using an eye-tracking approach. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00252-8

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Same leave-one-participant-out cross-validation approach as for CFT prediction, applied to predicting Big Five traits from scanpath similarity in 182 adults. The model "explained no variance in the personality traits"; restricting to performance-relevant items yielded the same result.

"However, the model explained no variance in the personality traits, indicating that the personality traits were not signiﬁcantly linked to gaze pat-terns while participants solved intelligence test items."

Lisa Bardach 2024 (2)¶

Lisa Bardach, Aki Schumacher, Ulrich Trautwein, Enkelejda Kasneci, Maike Tibus, Franz Wortha, Peter Gerjets & Tobias Appel. (2024). Taking another look at intelligence and personality using an eye-tracking approach. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00252-8

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Facet-level supplementary analyses of gaze-personality links in the same sample; for performance-relevant gaze, Assertiveness (4.27%), Self-Consciousness (1.10%), Aesthetics (1.79%), and Fantasy (1.76%) each exceeded 1% explained variance.

"Focusing on gaze patterns indicative of higher intelligence test performance, we identiﬁed four facets for which more than 1% of the variance could be explained: Asser-tiveness (4.27%), Self-Consciousness (1.10%), Aesthetics (Openness facet, 1.79%), and Fantasy (Openness facet, 1.76%)."

Related Claims¶

  • Behavioral (gaze) data outperformed Big Five questionnaire data in predicting intelligence test performance, but the article cautions against dismissing self-report measures — related
  • Gaze patterns recorded during a timed intelligence test explain 35.91% of variance in intelligence test scores — related
  • Big Five personality traits explain 3.18% of variance in fluid intelligence test scores, with Openness and Agreeableness the only meaningful contributors — related
  • Facet-level personality measures explain more than twice the variance of trait-level measures in intelligence test scores (7.67% vs 3.18%) — related
  • Combining gaze-based predictions with personality explains 37.50% of intelligence test variance, with significant unique contributions from both; with facets, 38.02% — related
  • Of the Big Five personality traits, only conscientiousness (r = .22) and openness (r = .13) correlate appreciably with mathematics achievement — related
  • Gaze patterns on a timed intelligence test partially reflect constructive matching and response elimination strategies, with constructive-matching-like patterns more successful — related
  • Conscientiousness shows the strongest links to SRL and robustly predicts academic performance even after accounting for cognitive ability — related
