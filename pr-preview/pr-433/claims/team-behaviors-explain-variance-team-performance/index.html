Evidence¶

Robert A. Sottilare 2018¶

Robert A. Sottilare, C. Shawn Burke, Eduardo Salas, Anne M. Sinatra, Joan H. Johnston, Stephen B. Gilbert. (2018). Designing Adaptive Instruction for Teams: a Meta-Analysis. Int J Artif Intell Educ 28:225–264. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0146-z

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Random-effects meta-analysis (Hunter and Schmidt guidelines) of correlations from approximately 300 primary studies on small interdependent teams; Table 2 reports action processes rho = .65 (42% variance) and OCBs rho = .55 (30%), both statistically significant. The article reports "team behaviors explain up to 42% of the variance in team performance."

"According to our meta-analytic findings, team behaviors explain up to 42% of the variance in team performance. Considering the importance of distinguishing specific behaviors, we highlight action processes and organizational citizenship behaviors (OCBs) as the most important."

Related Claims¶

  • Team attitudes predict team performance, with collective efficacy (20%) and psychological safety (17%) explaining the most variance
  • Team behaviors account for 7–36% of the variance in team learning, with communication (25%) and reflexivity (15%) the main antecedents
  • Team cognition predicts team performance: transactive memory systems (20%) and shared mental models (10%) explain significant variance, and situational awareness shows the largest relationship but rests on a small sample
  • Attitudes reflective of cooperation account for 37% of the variance in team learning, with psychological safety, cohesion, and trust prominent but understudied
  • Self-report Likert measures of team constructs are subject to social desirability bias, availability heuristics, and scale-point interpretation problems, favoring behavioral markers as a measurement approach
  • Meta-analysis finds task-focused leadership accounts for about 10% of variance in team effectiveness perceptions and empowerment behaviors nearly 30% of variance in team learning — related
