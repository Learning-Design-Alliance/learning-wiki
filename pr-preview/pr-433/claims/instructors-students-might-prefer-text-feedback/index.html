Evidence¶

Lowenthal 2021¶

Lowenthal, P. R. (2021). Video feedback: is it worth the effort? A response to Borup et al. Education Tech Research Dev, 69, 127-131. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09872-4

q2 · i? · theoretical · r?

The abstract reports, as a finding from prior research it responds to, that "instructors and students might prefer text-based feedback" despite video's benefits. The article offers no new preference data of its own and prints no effect size.

"However, research also suggests that despite its benefits, instructors and students might prefer text-based feedback."

Related Claims¶

  • In Borup et al.'s (2015) blended-course study, students and instructors perceived text and video feedback as equally valuable on quality and delivery, but valued text's efficiency over video's affective benefits — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Borup et al. (2015) found video feedback more convenient for student support and text-based feedback more convenient for exploratory feedback — related
  • Asynchronous video communication and feedback enhance social presence and helped sustain engagement and connections between socially isolated faculty and students during the COVID-19 shift online — reports the opposite
  • Borup et al.'s work juxtaposed two different modalities of learning and feedback — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Teacher candidates and instructors hold perceptions of feedback in blended learning environments, as examined by Borup et al. (2015) — related
