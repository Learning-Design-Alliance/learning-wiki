Evidence¶

Rubinsten O 2010¶

Rubinsten O, Tannock R. (2010). Mathematics anxiety in children with developmental dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions 6:46. http://www.behavioralandbrainfunctions.com/content/6/1/46

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Omnibus repeated-measures analysis of the 2 × 4 × 4 factorial priming experiment (group between-participants; primes and targets within block) in 12 DD and 11 control children. The three-way interaction "F(9,189) = 10.4, p < 0.001" was the primary result.

"Of primary relevance to the aims of the current study was the significant interaction between group, type of prime and arithmetic operation, F(9,189) = 10.4, p < 0.001"

Rubinsten O 2010 (2)¶

Rubinsten O, Tannock R. (2010). Mathematics anxiety in children with developmental dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions 6:46. http://www.behavioralandbrainfunctions.com/content/6/1/46

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Same analysis. Main effects of group (DD M = 977.8 ms vs Control M = 844 ms; F(1,21) = 2541, p < 0.001), prime type (F(3,63) = 31, p < 0.001) and arithmetic operation (F(3,63) = 43.2, p < 0.001) all "reached significance".

"The main effects of group (DD: M = 977.8 ms, S.D. = 29.8 ms; Control: M = 844 ms, S.D. = 20.1 ms); F(1,21) = 2541, p < 0.001, type of prime (positive primes: M = 920.7 ms, S.D. = 89.9 ms; neutral: M = 912.3 ms, S.D. = 69.3 ms; negative: M = 912 ms, S.D. = 61.41 ms; math: M = 909 ms, S.D. = 63.24 ms; F(3,63) = 31, p < 0.001)"

Related Claims¶

  • Negative priming effects are generally larger in mathematics than in language or science, particularly in arithmetic tasks — related
  • Math-related word primes facilitate arithmetic processing in children with DD but inhibit it in typically-developing controls
  • Children with developmental dyscalculia respond faster to simple arithmetic targets after negative than positive affective primes, across all four arithmetic operations — related
  • Math anxiety in DD is specific to the math context and occurs in the absence of generalized anxiety — related
  • Children with DD show slower reaction times than TD children during arithmetic problem solving, with no operation-specific RT effect — related
  • Typically-developing children show the standard affective priming pattern, responding faster to arithmetic targets after positive than negative primes — related
