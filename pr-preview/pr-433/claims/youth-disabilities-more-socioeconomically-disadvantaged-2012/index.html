Key finding from the National Longitudinal Transition Study 2012 surveys of secondary-school youth with disabilities and their parents, stating that these youth "are more socioeconomically disadvantaged" than other students. The brief adds they were more likely to live in low-income households and with parents receiving federal food benefits.

"Compared with other students in 2012, youth with disabilities are more socioeconomically disadvantaged and less likely to have experiences and expectations that are associated with success after high school."
