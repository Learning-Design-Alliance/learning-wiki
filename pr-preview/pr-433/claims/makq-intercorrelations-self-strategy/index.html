Evidence¶

Mulvihill 2025¶

Mulvihill, A., Matthews, N., Chidley, K., Macmahon, S., Armitage, K. L., Sherlock, D., Dux, P. E., & Carroll, A. (2025). The meta-attention knowledge questionnaire (MAKQ): a new instrument for investigating meta-attention in a young adolescent sample. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09433-1

q2 · i3 · design · r2

Pearson correlations among the three MAKQ dimensions in Study One (N = 293), all statistically significant. The authors interpret that the components "likely reflect related, but partially independent features"; r = .56, .26, .18 as printed. The article applies no threshold; the i3 code is this wiki's reading of r = .56 on its impact scale, not the authors'.

"Meta-attention monitoring was moderately correlated with meta-attention control (r =.56, p <.001). Meta-attention strategy knowledge was weakly correlated with meta-attention control ( r =.26, p <.001) and meta-attention monitoring ( r =.18, p =.002)."

Related Claims¶

  • MAKQ dimensions show convergent validity with parent-rated attention and executive functioning (moderate-to-strong negative correlations) — related
  • MAKQ strategy knowledge, but not the self-perception scales, converges with behavioural inhibitory control on the Go-No-Go task — related
  • The MAKQ self-knowledge items show a stable two-factor structure: meta-attention control (15 items) and meta-attention monitoring (5 items) — related
  • All measured graduate attribute scores correlate positively with one another, with the Resourcefulness Scale correlating most weakly
  • Self-report factors correlate weakly with metacognitive behavior while within-factor subcomponent correlations are larger, per studies reviewed for the meta-analysis
  • Metacognitive awareness is weakly correlated with intelligence and independently predicts performance (review-reported) — related
