Evidence¶

Yuan-Hsuan Lee and Jiun-Yu Wu 2026¶

Yuan-Hsuan Lee and Jiun-Yu Wu. (2026). Differential engagement with generative artificial intelligence in higher education: Gender, motivation, and achievement trajectories. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00615-8

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Inductive–deductive content analysis of all 2,819 chatbot entries (Cohen's kappa = .898) coded ten fine-grained behaviors in three macro-categories; the Initial Inquiry macro-category comprised 70.3% (n = 1,984) of queries, dominated by definition requests and pasted assignment problems.

"Within this category, two behaviors were highly dominant: Definition (Def) queries (42.1%, n = 1,189) and Copy behaviors (24.3%, n = 687). Collectively, these two codes constituted 66.4% of all student-chatbot interactions, establishing the tool's primary role as a resource for definitional knowledge and direct problem framing (fact seeking)."

Yuan-Hsuan Lee and Jiun-Yu Wu 2026 (2)¶

Yuan-Hsuan Lee and Jiun-Yu Wu. (2026). Differential engagement with generative artificial intelligence in higher education: Gender, motivation, and achievement trajectories. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00615-8

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Within the Follow-Up Inquiry macro-category (26.4%, n = 746), the most common behaviors were Repeat (11.6%, n = 326) and Check (7.7%, n = 217), while explicit critique of chatbot answers (Doubt) and extension (Extend) were rare; Other Inquiry was minimal at 3.3% (n = 92).

"Behaviors reflecting explicit self-correction or critique, such as Doubt (3.4%, n = 97) and Extend (3.8%, n = 106), were considerably less frequent."
