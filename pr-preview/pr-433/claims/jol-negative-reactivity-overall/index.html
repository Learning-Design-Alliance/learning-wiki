Evidence¶

Murphy 2024¶

Murphy, D. H., Rhodes, M. G., & Castel, A. D. (2024). Age-related differences in metacognitive reactivity in younger and older adults. Metacognition and Learning, 19, 863–877. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09391-0

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Pre-registered online mixed-factor experiment with 481 younger and older adults studying related and unrelated word pairs with JOL or equal-duration inter-stimulus interval conditions. The JOL group recalled fewer pairs: "M = 0.60, SD = 0.18" vs M = 0.65, F(1, 477) = 8.20, p = .004, ηp2 = 0.02; t-test d = 0.25.

"Participants making JOLs recalled fewer word pairs ( M = 0.60, SD = 0.18) than participants not making JOLs (M = 0.65, SD = 0.19), [F(1, 477) = 8.20, p = .004, ηp2 = 0.02, BF10 = 0.88], exemplifying negative reactivity; a separate t-test corroborated this effect [t(479) = 2.75, p = .006, d = 0.25"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • JOL reactivity differed by age: making JOLs impaired older adults' recall but did not affect younger adults' recall — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • JOL reactivity depended on pair relatedness: negative reactivity for unrelated word pairs but no reactivity for related word pairs — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Exploratory analyses suggest older adults' negative reactivity was specific to unrelated word pairs, with no reactivity for related pairs in either age group — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Making JOLs is not a neutral act: metacognitive ratings reactively affect subsequent learning, so experiments need judgment-free conditions — a broader claim this one bears on
