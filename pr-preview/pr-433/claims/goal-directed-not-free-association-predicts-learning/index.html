Evidence¶

Luchini 2025¶

Luchini, S. A., Kaufman, J. C., Goecke, B., Wilhelm, O., Kenett, Y. N., Lei, D., Benedek, M., van Hell, J. G., & Beaty, R. E. (2025). Creativity supports learning through associative thinking. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00334-1

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Pearson correlations in Study 2 (N = 145) between day 2 LET recall and semantic distance in the two verb generation conditions; the free and goal-directed conditions were themselves positively correlated (r = 0.34, p < 0.001), yet "only goal-directed association showed a signiﬁcant correlation with day 2 learning".

"only goal-directed association showed a signiﬁcant correlation with day 2 learning (r = 0.23, p = 0.005); while free association showed a non-signiﬁcant correlation (r = 0.01, p = 0.89)"

Luchini 2025 (2)¶

Luchini, S. A., Kaufman, J. C., Goecke, B., Wilhelm, O., Kenett, Y. N., Lei, D., Benedek, M., van Hell, J. G., & Beaty, R. E. (2025). Creativity supports learning through associative thinking. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00334-1

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The goal-directed–free semantic distance difference score, computed to use the full verb generation dataset, correlated medium-sized with day 2 learning in Study 2 pairwise correlations, "treating the free association condition as a baseline".

"this difference score showed a medium positive correlation with day 2 learning (r = 0.37, p < 0.001)"

Related Claims¶

  • Associative thinking (semantic distance of generated word associations) predicts paired-associate foreign-language vocabulary learning beyond general cognitive ability in undergraduates — related
  • Vocabulary retention declines from immediate to 24-hour-delayed no-feedback testing, and goal-directed associations are more semantically distant but slower to produce than free associations — related
  • Prior learning of word pairs reduces the semantic distance of goal-directed (but not free) associations, suggesting interference from newly acquired knowledge — related
