Evidence¶

Khosravi 2026¶

Khosravi, H., Gašević, D., Sadiq, S., Yan, L., Lodge, J. M., Tangen, J. M., Denny, P., DiCerbo, K., Buckingham Shum, S., & Baker, R. S. (2026). Building AI Companions that Prioritise Learning over Performance. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04816 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.04816

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The paper reports, citing prior work, that hybrid LLM-plus-knowledge-tracing architectures outperform either alone; LLMs are described as less effective at inferring overall skill mastery. No effect size is printed.

"hybrid approaches that combine LLMs (for content interpretation and response analysis) with traditional knowledge-tracing algorithms (for longitudinal integration) have been shown to outperform either method alone"

Related Claims¶

  • LLMKT outperforms existing knowledge tracing methods at predicting student turn correctness in the CoMTA and MathDial tutoring dialogue datasets, and generally outperforms DKT-Sem. — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it

  • Knowledge tracing performance on tutoring dialogues is relatively low, with a maximum of around 76% AUC, which the authors take to show dialogueKT is a challenging task.

  • Emerging evidence indicates generative AI tutors currently struggle to accurately infer student knowledge at the outset of an interaction or as the activity progresses — related
  • First-year EAP students prefer a hybrid feedback model combining AI-generated comments with instructor feedback over either source alone — related
  • DKT-Sem, a DKT variant using semantic text embeddings, performs better than existing KT methods on tutoring dialogues, with a smaller margin on the larger MathDial dataset. — related
  • Longer student histories do not improve LLM knowledge tracing: FKT AUC-ROC stays near 0.5-0.6 whether models see 50 or 400 prior interactions — related
