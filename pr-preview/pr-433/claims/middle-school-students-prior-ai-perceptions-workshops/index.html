Evidence¶

Randi Williams 2023¶

Randi Williams, Safinah Ali, Nisha Devasia, Daniella DiPaola, Jenna Hong, Stephen P. Kaputsos, Brian Jordan, Cynthia Breazeal. (2023). AI + Ethics Curricula for Middle School Youth: Lessons Learned from Three Project-Based Curricula. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00298-y

q2 · i? · design · r2

Pre-test questionnaire administered via Google Form on the first day across all three workshops. Students were highly AI-aware: "90.5% (n = 74 total responses) of students had heard of AI before", with near-universal use of YouTube, Google Search, and Netflix. Percentages are descriptive; no inferential test is reported.

"Across all three curricula, 90.5% (n = 74 total responses) of students had heard of AI before and an overwhelming majority of students had interacted with AI integrated technologies such as YouTube (100%, n = 45), Google Search (95.6%, n = 43), and Netflix (88.9%, n = 40) before"

Randi Williams 2023 (2)¶

Randi Williams, Safinah Ali, Nisha Devasia, Daniella DiPaola, Jenna Hong, Stephen P. Kaputsos, Brian Jordan, Cynthia Breazeal. (2023). AI + Ethics Curricula for Middle School Youth: Lessons Learned from Three Project-Based Curricula. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00298-y

q2 · i? · design · r2

Same pre-test questionnaire. When asked which technologies use AI, "Students were least sure whether Video Calls (21.6% said "Not sure this uses AI", n = 37)" along with game consoles, filters, and printers. The authors note students erred on the side of calling items AI. Descriptive percentages only.

"Students were least sure whether Video Calls (21.6% said "Not sure this uses AI", n = 37), Game Consoles (13.64%, n = 44), Snapchat Filters (13.62%, n = 44), and Wireless Printers (13.51%, n = 37) used AI."

Randi Williams 2023 (3)¶

Randi Williams, Safinah Ali, Nisha Devasia, Daniella DiPaola, Jenna Hong, Stephen P. Kaputsos, Brian Jordan, Cynthia Breazeal. (2023). AI + Ethics Curricula for Middle School Youth: Lessons Learned from Three Project-Based Curricula. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-022-00298-y

q2 · i? · design · r2

Self-perception items on the pre-test. Only a minority (17.1%, n = 35) said they were smarter than AI, while "81.1% (n = 37) reporting that they believed the relationship between themselves and AI was symbiotic". Percentages are descriptive; no test reported.

"Students seemed to believe in the collaborative potential of AI, however, with 81.1% (n = 37) reporting that they believed the relationship between themselves and AI was symbiotic, such that humans and AI could both have control of technology"
