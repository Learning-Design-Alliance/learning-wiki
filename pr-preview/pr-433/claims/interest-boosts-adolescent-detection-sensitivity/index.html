Evidence¶

Banerjee Snigdha 2015¶

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Behavioral ANOVA in the cued spatial attention task of 24 male adolescents (mean age 15.07). The authors report these findings were "driven by higher d-prime scores for high versus low interest stimuli, and higher d-prime scores in the right hemifield." No effect size printed.

"For d-prime, there was a significant main effect of motivation (F(1,23) = 12.27, p = .002), a significant main effect of stimulus location (F(1,23) = 14.26, p = .001), and a significant motivation by stimulus location interaction (F(1,23) = 17.63, p < .001)."

Related Claims¶

  • Motivation modulates anticipatory attentional processing: high-interest stimuli produced larger LDAP-period amplitude over right parieto-occipital cortex — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Sensory evoked responses to the cue stimuli did not differ between high- and low-interest blocks, arguing against block-level arousal differences — related
  • Motivation did not interact with visuospatial attention effects on post-stimulus visual evoked potentials to imperative stimuli — related
  • Adolescents show adult-like anticipatory broadband ERP attention components (ADAN, LDAP, BRN) contralateral to the attended location — related
  • Processing of affective stimuli is prioritized and interferes with target processing during the attentional blink interval in early adolescence — related
  • Exploratory analysis reveals sustained greater anticipatory activation for high versus low motivation conditions from 700–1100 ms post-cue over parieto-occipital scalp — related
