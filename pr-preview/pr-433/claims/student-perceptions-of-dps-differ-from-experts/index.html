Evidence¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman 2026¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman, Daqing Hou, and Maliha Noushin Raida. (2026). LLM-Generated Design Problems for Assessing Higher-Order Thinking in Project-Based Learning. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.11032. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11032 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.11032

q2 · i? · design · r2

Descriptive statistics of Task B student ratings on 5-point Likert scales in three course deployments showed Cognitive Complexity highest at M=4.35 (87% agreement) and Feasibility lowest at M=3.63, with 14% disagreeing.

"Cognitive Complexityreceived the highest mean rating (𝑀= 4.35), with 87% agreeing that the prompts engaged HOT.Scenario Qualitywas also rated highly ( 𝑀= 4.21), with 88% finding the scenarios realistic and meaningful."

Ahmad D. Suleiman 2026 (2)¶

Ahmad D. Suleiman, Daqing Hou, and Maliha Noushin Raida. (2026). LLM-Generated Design Problems for Assessing Higher-Order Thinking in Project-Based Learning. arXiv preprint arXiv:2607.11032. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11032

q2 · i1 · design · r2

Weighted Cohen's κ and paired t-tests comparing student and expert ratings on 39 shared DPs found "no agreement overall (𝜅=− 0.11)"; students rated Scenario Quality higher (d=0.33) and Feasibility lower (d=0.74) than experts.

"Comparing student and expert ratings on the 39 shared DPs, there was no agreement overall (𝜅=− 0.11), indicating that the two groups often judged the same DPs differently."
