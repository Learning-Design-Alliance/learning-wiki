Evidence¶

dos Santos Kawata KH 2021¶

dos Santos Kawata KH, Ueno Y, Hashimoto R, Yoshino S, Ohta K, Nishida A, Ando S, Nakatani H, Kasai K and Koike S (2021) Development of Metacognition in Adolescence: The Congruency-Based Metacognition Scale. Front. Psychol. 11:565231. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.565231

q2 · i? · associational · r2

A one-way ANOVA compared CMS scores across the three surveys. CMS metacognition differed significantly between surveys [F(2, 630) = 15.4, p < 0.01], but CMS self-judgment accuracy did not [F(2, 630) = 1.4, p = 0.24].

"A one-way ANOV A revealed a signiﬁcant diﬀerence between surveys in the CMS metacognition [F(2, 630)= 15.4, p < 0.01] but not in CMS self-judgment accuracy [F(2, 630)= 1.4, p= 0.24]."

dos Santos Kawata KH 2021 (2)¶

dos Santos Kawata KH, Ueno Y, Hashimoto R, Yoshino S, Ohta K, Nishida A, Ando S, Nakatani H, Kasai K and Koike S (2021) Development of Metacognition in Adolescence: The Congruency-Based Metacognition Scale. Front. Psychol. 11:565231. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.565231

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Bonferroni post-hoc tests showed survey 3 (mean = 4.7, SD = 2.6) had a significantly higher CMS metacognition score (lower metacognitive ability) than surveys 1 (mean = 3.5, SD = 2.1) and 2 (mean = 3.5, SD = 2.1).

"For the CMS metacognition, a Bonferroni post-hoc test showed that survey 3 (mean= 4.7, SD= 2.6) presented with a signiﬁcantly higher score (i.e., lower metacognitive ability) compared to surveys 1 (mean= 3.5, SD= 2.1) and 2 (mean= 3.5, SD= 2.1)."
