Evidence¶

Palladino 2025¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Pearson correlation analysis across the measures at both administration points (Table 3). At T0, children with higher vocabulary showed higher confidence, better discrimination between correct and incorrect responses, and better control of their test performance; at T1, vocabulary was associated with confidence and metacognitive control but not with relative monitoring accuracy.

"English vocabulary at T0 was positively correlated with confidence judgements and relative monitoring accuracy at T0 and with metacognitive control at both T0 and T1."

Palladino 2025 (2)¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The correlation analysis partly confirmed Hypothesis 3: at T0, vocabulary and emotions did not correlate, but by T1 only the negative emotions related to vocabulary, contrary to the expectation that all three emotions would relate.

"However, boredom and anxiety at T1 were both negatively correlated with English vocabulary at both T0 and T1. On the other hand, enjoyment did not correlate with English vocabulary performance at either time point."
