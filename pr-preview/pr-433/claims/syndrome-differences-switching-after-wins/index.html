Hallman 2026¶

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning and arousal responses in Down, Fragile X, and Williams syndromes. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00438-2

q3 · i? · associational · r?

Bayesian GLMM results in the Results section, with TD and loss as reference categories. The model interaction shows "DS were more likely to change response after wins" with b = 1.47 and BF > 150, and "WS were less likely" with b = -0.87, BF = 12.79.

"DS were more likely to change response after wins (b = 1.47, 95% CI = [0.74, 2.18], BF > 150, pd = 1), whereas WS were less likely (b = -0.87, 95% CI = [ −1.51, −0.23], BF = 12.79, pd =1 )."

Hallman 2026 (2)¶

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning and arousal responses in Down, Fragile X, and Williams syndromes. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00438-2

q3 · i? · associational · r?

Follow-up pairwise comparisons (Fig. 1) between syndromes after wins, reported in the Results section. The article additionally reports "FXS individuals were more likely to change response after wins than WS individuals (OR: 2.34, 95% HPD [1.03, 4.16])."

"The results showed that after positive feedback (win), DS individuals were more likely to change their response than both FXS individuals (OR: 2.8, 95% HPD [1.08, 5.41]) and WS individuals (OR: 6.59, 95% HPD [2.93, 11.96]), as indicated by the HPD interval not covering 1."
