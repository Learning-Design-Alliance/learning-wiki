Evidence¶

Göldi 2019¶

Göldi, M. & Rasch, B. (2019). Effects of targeted memory reactivation during sleep at home depend on sleep disturbances and habituation. npj Science of Learning 4:5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0044-2

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Repeated-measures ANOVA (cueing × night) in the full sample of 66; the cueing × night interaction was significant with "F(2,130) = 4.01, P = 0.03; η2 = 0.06", and night 3 showed cued advantage at t(65) = 2.31, P = 0.024.

"we observed a signi ﬁcant interaction between these factors ( F(2,130) = 4.01, P = 0.03; η2 = 0.06), con ﬁrming that TMR had differential effects on memory for the three nights. In the whole sample, cued words signi ﬁcantly pro ﬁted from TMR during sleep only in night 3 and were signi ﬁcantly better remembered (99.95 ± 1.96%) than uncued words (96.29 ± 1.53%; t(65) = 2.31, P = 0.024, Fig. 2b)."

Göldi 2019 (2)¶

Göldi, M. & Rasch, B. (2019). Effects of targeted memory reactivation during sleep at home depend on sleep disturbances and habituation. npj Science of Learning 4:5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0044-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Gain/loss analysis of the same night-wise design: cued words showed "4.18 ± 0.37" gains vs 3.42 ± 0.32 for uncued in night 3, t(65) = 2.28, P = 0.026; lost words showed no significant effects (all P > 0.60).

"Post hoc comparisons revealed that TMR during sleep resulted in an increase in the number of gains for cued words (4.18 ± 0.37) than uncued words in night 3 (3.42 ± 0.32; t(65) = 2.28, P = 0.026"
