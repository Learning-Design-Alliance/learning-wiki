Evidence¶

Nan Li 2013¶

Nan Li, William W. Cohen, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2013). Problem Order Implications for Learning. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-013-0005-5

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Controlled simulation study in which SimStudent was trained on 12 curricula per domain (six blocked, six interleaved) built from real-student problems, then tested on held-out testing problems; learning curves are shown in Figs. 5–7 and the text states "in all three domains, the interleaved-ordering curricula yielded as effective or more effective learning".

"As we can see in the graph, in all three domains, the interleaved-ordering curricula yielded as effective or more effective learning than the blocked-ordering curricula."

Related Claims¶

  • In equation solving, SimStudent trained on interleaved-ordering curricula achieved significantly higher step scores than with blocked-ordering curricula — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In fraction addition, there was no significant difference in step scores between interleaved- and blocked-ordering curricula — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • SimStudent given interleaved-ordering curricula received more negative feedback in stoichiometry and fraction addition, supporting more effective when-learning — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • SimStudent produced only human-like fraction-addition errors under both problem orders, with no difference in error types between orders — related
  • In stoichiometry, SimStudent trained on interleaved-ordering curricula achieved significantly higher step scores than with blocked-ordering curricula — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Learning when to apply a skill benefits more from interleaved problem orders, while learning how to apply a skill benefits more from blocked problem orders — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In an experimenter-controlled task, interleaved practice yields better classification performance than blocked practice (d = 1.05) — related
  • Interleaved practice outperformed blocked practice across eight STEM classrooms — related
