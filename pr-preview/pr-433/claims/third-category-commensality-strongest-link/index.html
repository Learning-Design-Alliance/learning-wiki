Evidence¶

Jinxi Wen 2025¶

Jinxi Wen, Wenjing Lyu, Jin Liu & Li Yang. (2025). Commensality and academic performance: measuring peer acceptance among college students. npj Science of Learning 10:69. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00357-8

q2 · i0 · associational · r2

Observational regression analysis of multiple peer categories (Table 2, N = 2485 first semester; N = 3007 second semester). Third-category commensality: β = 0.076, p = 0.002 first semester; β = 0.134, p < 0.001 second semester.

"an increase in the commensality value for third-category peers by 3.15 (equivalent to approximately three shared meals with one such peer or one shared meal with three such peers) is linked to an increase of ~9.70 points in the student’s total score by the end of the semester"

Jinxi Wen 2025 (2)¶

Jinxi Wen, Wenjing Lyu, Jin Liu & Li Yang. (2025). Commensality and academic performance: measuring peer acceptance among college students. npj Science of Learning 10:69. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00357-8

q2 · i? · associational · r2

F-test comparison of the four commensality variables in the first-semester model: "Roommate commensality and the commensality diversity index (F(1, 2262) = 4.29, p = 0.0384)" among significant pairwise differences; the three peer categories did not differ significantly.

"Based on the standardized regression coefﬁcients and F-test results, we conclude that in the short term, the third-category commensality value (β = 0.076) has the strongest association with semester average scores"
