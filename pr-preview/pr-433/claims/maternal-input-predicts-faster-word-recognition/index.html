Evidence¶

Hurtado Nereyda 2008¶

Hurtado Nereyda, Marchman Virginia A., Fernald Anne. (2008). Does input influence uptake? Links between maternal talk, processing speed and vocabulary size in Spanish-learning children. Developmental Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2898277/

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Median-split analysis of the same 27-dyad longitudinal sample: mothers divided by word tokens produced at 18 months, children's RT measured with the looking-while-listening eye-tracking procedure at 24 months. Children of more talkative mothers were "significantly faster mean RTs, than did children with less talkative mothers, t(25) = 3.5, p < .01"; Table 3 shows maternal talk measures correlated with 24-month RT, accounting for 18–26% of variance.

"As shown in Figures 1 and 2, those children whose mothers produced more words shifted from the distracter to the target picture sooner, resulting in significantly faster mean RTs, than did children with less talkative mothers, t(25) = 3.5, p < .01."
