Evidence¶

Mohammad Hadra 2026¶

Mohammad Hadra, Karleen Cambridge and Mostefa Mesbah. (2026). Evaluating the accuracy and reliability of AI content detectors in academic contexts. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00213-1

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Fisher's Exact Test (one-sided) comparing correct classification of 48 professional texts (100% accuracy for Originality) against 48 EFL texts (91.6%). The article reports "a borderline statistically significant trend (one-sided p = 0.058)" that does not reach conventional significance.

"Fisher’s Exact Test indicated a borderline statistically significant trend (one-sided p = 0.058) toward higher accuracy for professional writing. Although the result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance, the directional trend aligns with emerging literature documenting potential detector bias against non-native or less proficient writers."

Mohammad Hadra 2026 (2)¶

Mohammad Hadra, Karleen Cambridge and Mostefa Mesbah. (2026). Evaluating the accuracy and reliability of AI content detectors in academic contexts. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00213-1

q2 · i? · design · r2

Fisher's Exact Test comparing Turnitin accuracy on 48 EFL texts (91.6%) versus 48 professional texts (93.8%); the article reports "no statistically significant difference (Fisher’s Exact Test, p = 0.50)". Accuracy was used as the primary metric because all texts in this subset were human-authored.

"Turnitin demonstrated comparable accuracy for both EFL student writing and professional writing, with no statistically significant difference (Fisher’s Exact Test, p = 0.50)."
