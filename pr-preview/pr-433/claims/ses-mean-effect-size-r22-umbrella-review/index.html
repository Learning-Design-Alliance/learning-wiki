Evidence¶

Tan 2024¶

Tan, C. Y. (2024). Socioeconomic Status and Student Learning: Insights from an Umbrella Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09929-3

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r?

A second-order three-level meta-analysis of 21 effect sizes from 15 first-order meta-analyses, using the metafor package in R, reports a mean SES effect size of "r = .22". The author deems this large against educational benchmarks but notes confounding with other demographics.

"It yields a mean SES effect size of r = .22 (deemed as large with reference to educational benchmarks, expected students’ achievement gains, and percentile points in achievement tests) but SES effects may be confounded with other demographics."
