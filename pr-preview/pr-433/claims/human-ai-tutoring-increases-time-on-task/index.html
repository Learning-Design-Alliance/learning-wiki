Evidence¶

Ashish Gurung 2026¶

Ashish Gurung, Ge Gao, Jordan Gutterman, Danielle R. Thomas, Shivang Gupta, Lee Branstetter, Emma Brunskill, Vincent Aleven, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student Needs. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.11155

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Within-subject DiD analysis of IXL logs over the 10 school weeks preceding each MAP test. Human-AI tutoring increased "student time on task in both the IK bandwidth subsample (25%; +1.32 hours, p< .001)" and the full sample (31%; +1.61 hours, p<.001).

"we find that human-AI tutoring associated with significant improvement in student time on task in both the IK bandwidth subsample (25%; +1.32 hours, p< .001) and the full sample (31%; +1.61 hours, p<.001)."

Ashish Gurung 2026 (2)¶

Ashish Gurung, Ge Gao, Jordan Gutterman, Danielle R. Thomas, Shivang Gupta, Lee Branstetter, Emma Brunskill, Vincent Aleven, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Improving Hybrid Human-AI Tutoring by Differentiating Human Tutor Roles Based on Student Needs. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11155

q2 · i? · causal · r2

DiD comparison of proactive versus reactive groups on IXL time on task (Table 5). The article reports no "significant difference in time on task between proactive and reactive tutoring" in either sample; equivalence was not tested.

"we did not find a significant difference in time on task between proactive and reactive tutoring in either the IK bandwidth subsample or the full sample, i.e., time on task did not differ reliably between the two tutoring approaches."
