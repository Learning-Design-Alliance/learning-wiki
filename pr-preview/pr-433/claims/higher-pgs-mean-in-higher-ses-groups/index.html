Between-family analysis of PGS means across four SES groups in 2335 children with genome-wide SNP data; mean PGS increased with SES (Δ−2LL = 81.3, p < 0.001; Fig. 4).

"In higher SES groups, children had, on average, a higher PGS, meaning that they inherited more alleles with a positive effect on educational attainment ( Δ−2LL = 81.3, Δdf = 3, p < 0.001)."

Related Claims¶

  • The strength of the association between polygenic score and educational achievement does not differ across SES groups — related
  • SES differences in educational achievement persist after accounting for polygenic score differences between children — related
  • Educational attainment polygenic scores are positively associated with childhood visual acuity, while myopia polygenic scores show a negative association — related
  • Students in state non-selective schools have lower EduYears polygenic scores than students in grammar (d = 0.41) and private (d = 0.37) schools, with no significant difference between grammar and private schools — related
  • A positive G×E interaction between the education PGS and SES indicates a favorable SES environment enhances the effect of offspring genome — related
  • Including SES and offspring genome in the same model reduces the education PGS effect by more than 20% and SES effects by about 10–15%, implying 20–30% of parental SES effects are genomic — related
  • Higher education polygenic scores predict placement into more advanced 9th-grade math courses, robust to family SES, school SES, and school fixed effects — related
  • Education polygenic scores predict year-to-year math enrollment above and beyond prior-year math GPA, family SES, and school SES — related
  • Higher education polygenic scores predict persistence in mathematics coursetaking across four years of secondary school, robust to SES covariates, prior tracking, and school fixed effects — related
  • EduYears polygenic scores predict achievement increasingly with age (about 4% at KS1 to 10% at GCSE) and show largely stable effects, with less than 1% age-specific prediction — related
  • A polygenic score for educational attainment positively predicts reading and speaking skills in early childhood, later scholastic achievement, and adult economic security — related
