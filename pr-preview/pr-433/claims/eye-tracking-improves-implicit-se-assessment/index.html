Evidence¶

Christina Merten and Cristina Conati 2005¶

Christina Merten and Cristina Conati. (2005). Eye-Tracking to Model and Adapt to User Meta-cognition in Intelligent Learning Environments. https://www.cs.ubc.ca

q2 · i? · design · r2

Evaluation on log files from a new study of 18 university students (109 self-explained and 68 non-self-explained exploration cases), with thresholds from ROC curves over training data. "model 3 yields a ROC curve with greater area than that of model 2"; Table 3 shows combined accuracy rising from 63.9% to 73.0%, with the increase "more substantial for specificity".

"As shown in Figure 6, model 3 yields a ROC curve with greater area than that of model 2. This difference in area is statistically significant to the z > 1.96 level"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In an initial eye-tracking study, time on action predicted self-explanation (16-second threshold) and gaze shifts were most reliable at detecting its absence
  • Customizing knowledge-node priors from pretest scores improves model accuracy, but tailoring SE-tendency priors yields no significant improvement
  • Time and gaze-shift evidence are complementary predictors of self-explanation: time alone yields higher sensitivity, gaze shifts alone higher specificity, and the combination the best accuracy
  • More accurate assessment of self-explanation significantly improves the model's assessment of student learning through exploration — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Leave-one-out cross-validation over 36 students confirms significant accuracy and stability gains from eye-tracking in both self-explanation and exploration assessment — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Eye-tracker attention patterns significantly improved a student model's ability to capture self-explanation versus a time-based predictor — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
