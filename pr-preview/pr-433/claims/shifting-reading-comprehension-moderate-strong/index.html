Qualitative synthesis of 14 shifting-reading comprehension studies: "Most studies showed moderate to strong links", with one exception reporting r = .15 using the general Multiple Classification Card Sorting Test and GORT; no pooled effect size is printed.

"Fourteen studies examined the relationship between shifting and reading comprehension. Most studies showed moderate to strong links, except one study reported a small association ( r=.15; Bock et al., 2018)"

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Pattern across six studies reporting large associations (Barber et al., 2020; Cartwright, 2002; Cartwright et al., 2010, 2019, 2020; Niedo et al., 2014): large effects occurred "particularly when the shifting task required higher-order conceptual or verbal switching" with literal comprehension measures.

"The large effects were found across a variety of shifting tasks and reading comprehension assessments, particularly when the shifting task required higher-order conceptual or verbal switching (i.e., reading-specific shifting task), and the measure of comprehension focused on literal comprehension (e.g., WRMT Passage Comprehension or WJ Passage Comprehension)."
