Evidence¶

Habibullah 2026¶

Habibullah, A., Alshoibi, Y., Alshiekh, M., Khan, S., & Khan, N. (2026). Where LLM graders succeed and break: Evidence from two computer-science exams. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29333 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.29333

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Full-cohort configuration-grid evaluation on the CV exam (570 dual-graded students, 35-point base scale), comparing each model's strict and neutral runs identical bar the persona sentence. The quote fragment "14 of the 17 config-matched pairs out of the graded band" states the headline result; three models stop grading outright.

"Prepending a short “strict grader” preamble to the otherwise-best prompt takes 14 of the 17 config-matched pairs out of the graded band, all six families represented, MAE rising×1.69 to×7.11."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Six closed-model configurations grade at or below the human inter-grader floor of 2.61/35 on the CV exam, with the best reaching MAE 1.64 — related
  • The strict-persona vulnerability replicates on an independent machine-learning exam, but its direction is exam-specific: seven of 17 models improve because the persona cancels their over-marking — related
  • The strict-persona collapse is attributed to the preamble's two credit-withholding policy sentences, not its harsh tone, with S2 alone causing refusal in two models — related
  • One LoRA adapter fine-tuned on the two exams' pooled ~3,900 dual-graded examples brings five small open models to parity or better with a human grader and makes them insensitive to harsh personas — related
  • Grading skill transfers across exams: an adapter fine-tuned on one exam improves grading on the unseen other, though transfer stops short of in-domain performance — related
  • LLM grading performance relative to human graders is mixed: comparable in 12 reviewed cases, worse in 11, with the strongest alignment on short, straightforward tasks — related
