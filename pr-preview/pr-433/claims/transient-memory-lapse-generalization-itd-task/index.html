Evidence¶

Beverly A. Wright & Ruijing Ning 2024¶

Beverly A. Wright & Ruijing Ning. (2024). A transient memory lapse in humans 1–3h after training. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00272-4

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Different groups of ILD-trained listeners were tested on untrained ITD discrimination at 30 min (n=38), 1 h (n=8), or 10 h (n=15), against naïve controls; LMER main effect of time F(3,116) = 13.021, p < 0.0001; the article prints medium pairwise effect sizes "30 min vs. 1 h: t(116) = 3.16, p = 0.004, d = 0.73" and "10 h vs. 1 h: t(118) = 2.52, p = 0.02, d = 0.66".

"Parallel to the trained task, the magnitude of improvement was greater 30 min and 10 h after training than 1 h after training (30 min: n = 38, improvement relative to mean naïve performance: 29.89 µs; 10 h: n = 15, 27.41 µs; 1 h: n = 8,−1.50 µs), and did not differ between the 30-min and 10-h time points (LMER post-hoc: 30 min vs. 1 h: t(116) = 3.16, p = 0.004, d = 0.73; 10 h vs. 1 h: t(118) = 2.52, p = 0.02, d = 0.66"

Related Claims¶

  • For the untrained ITD task, the 1–3 h lapse manifested as uniform performance at naïve levels with stable within-session performance — related
  • On the trained ILD-discrimination task, learning magnitude is smaller 1–3 h after training than 30 min or 6–10 h after training, documenting a transient memory lapse — related
  • The transient decrease in ILD learning 1–3 h after training was most prominent in listeners with higher initial ILD-discrimination thresholds — related
