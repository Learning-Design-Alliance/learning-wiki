Kiili 2025¶

Kiili, C., Kiili, K., Räikkönen, E., & Coiro, J. (2025). Explicit Video-Based Instruction Enhanced Students' Online Credibility Evaluation Skills: Did Storifying Instruction Matter?. Computers and Education, 229, Article 105252. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2025.105252

Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparison within the questioning ANCOVA found no difference between the two intervention formats ("MDiff = 0.07, p = 1.00"); no effect size was printed, so the null indicates no detected difference, not measured equivalence.

"The intervention groups performed equally well (MDiff = 0.07, p = 1.00; 95% CI = [ 1.82, 0.31])."

Related Claims¶

  • At the individual level, most students showed no reliable change in credibility evaluation skills, while 9–18% showed reliable positive change depending on the skill — related
  • Explicit video-based instruction improved sixth graders' skills to question the credibility of less credible online texts relative to control, with a large condition-level effect — related
  • Explicit video-based instruction nearly doubled students' relevant justifications of overall credibility evaluations relative to control — related
  • Explicit video-based instruction did not improve sixth graders' skills to confirm the credibility of more credible online texts, nor increase skepticism toward them — related
  • Basic reading skills were associated with change in confirming credibility but not with change in questioning or justifying credibility — related
  • The intervention improved students' sourcing in credibility judgments and in written products relative to controls — related
