Evidence¶

Spiegler O and Leyendecker B 2017¶

Spiegler O and Leyendecker B (2017) Balanced Cultural Identities Promote Cognitive Flexibility among Immigrant Children. Front. Psychol. 8:1579. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01579

q3 · i? · associational · r2

In the same moderation analyses of 225 children, identity measures showed no independent or interactive relation with congruent-trial (working memory) or incongruent-trial (inhibition) accuracy; Table 1 reports non-significant coefficients for both identity terms in those models.

"Children's Turkish and German identity were neither independently nor interactively related to performance in the congruent trials and incongruent trials ( Table 1 )."

Related Claims¶

  • Incongruent items in school-topic tasks are generally associated with longer response times and lower accuracy, patterns consistent with inhibitory control recruitment — related
  • Turkish and German identity interactively predicted accuracy on mixed Dot Task trials requiring cognitive flexibility among immigrant-origin children
  • Children's age did not moderate the link between bicultural identity and cognitive flexibility — related
  • The identity interaction on mixed-trial accuracy held marginally when controlling for language abilities, which themselves did not predict performance — related
  • Dot Task accuracy decreased from congruent to incongruent to mixed trials, with condition accuracies positively correlated — related
  • The link between Turkish identity and mixed-trial accuracy was negative when German identity was low and marginally positive when German identity was high — related
