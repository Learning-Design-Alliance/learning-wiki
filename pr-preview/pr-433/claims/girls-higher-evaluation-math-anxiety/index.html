Evidence¶

Geary 2019¶

Geary, D. C., Hoard, M. K., Nugent, L., Chu, F., Scofield, J. E., & Hibbard, D. F. (2019). Sex Differences in Mathematics Anxiety and Attitudes: Concurrent and Longitudinal Relations to Mathematical Competence. Journal of Educational Psychology, 111(8), 1447-1461. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000355

q3 · i1 · associational · r2

Cross-sectional analysis of the overall sixth-grade sample (n = 1,091; 545 boys) with school as a level-2 random effect, replicated in two subsamples. The summary reports higher "mathematics anxiety for evaluations (ds = −.30 to −.34)" in girls and no substantive difference (ds = −.14) for learning anxiety.

"Girls had consistently better English attitudes (ds = −.32 to −.45), as well as higher mathematics anxiety for evaluations (ds = −.30 to −.34) than did boys, whereas boys had consistently better mathematics attitudes (d = .20 to .28) than did girls."
