Evidence¶

Brian Mathias 2022¶

Brian Mathias, Christian Andrä, Anika Schwager, Manuela Macedonia, Katharina von Kriegstein. (2022). Twelve- and Fourteen-Year-Old School Children Differentially Benefit from Sensorimotor- and Multisensory-Enriched Vocabulary Training. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09648-z

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Mixed effects modeling of composite scores by word type in the 75-child intervention. Picture-enriched learning beat non-enriched for concrete (d = 0.62) and abstract words (d = 0.87); gesture-enriched learning beat non-enriched for concrete (d = 1.00) and abstract words (d = 1.15).

"The mixed effects model indicated no significant interaction between learning condition and word type variables (χ2 (2, N = 75) = 1.08, p = 0.58, v = 0.08)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Concrete L2 words are learned better than abstract words overall, but this concreteness advantage appears only in 14-year-olds — related
  • Gesture enrichment specifically aided translation of abstract words in Experiment 1, but this word-type specificity did not replicate in Experiment 2 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Gesture-enriched learning improves 8-year-olds' L2 vocabulary translation and free recall compared with non-enriched learning, persisting up to 6 months — related
  • Both gesture and picture enrichment enhanced children's test performance compared with non-enriched learning, with benefits persisting up to 6 months for concrete and abstract words — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Children's L2 translation and free recall scores were higher for concrete than abstract nouns across experiments — related
  • Gesture and picture enrichment produced similar benefits on children's L2 vocabulary learning, contrary to adult findings favoring gestures — related
  • Enrichment benefits on children's L2 vocabulary persist up to 6 months post-learning without diminishing relative to non-enriched learning — related
  • For very young children, more abstract vocabulary terms such as afloat, haste, mellow, noticeable, outward and skill are relatively more difficult, while more concrete terms such as dungeon, envelope, eyelash, nerve, plumbing, pork and snip are learned earlier. — related
