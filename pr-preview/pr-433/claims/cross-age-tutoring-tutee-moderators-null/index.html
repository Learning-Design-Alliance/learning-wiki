Evidence¶

Chang 2025¶

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-regression with RVE for tutees across four moderators. All Wald test p-values exceeded 0.05, indicating no significant moderation of tutee outcomes by sessions, tutor type, or risk status.

"Results indicate that intervention effects did not depend on the number of tutoring sessions (b = − 0.00, SE = 0.00, p > 0.05), whether the tutor was an older student or an adult volunteer (b = 0.09, SE = 0.17, p > 0.05) or whether tutees were at-risk for learning difficulties or typically developing (b = 0.07, SE = 0.17, p > 0.05)."

Chang 2025 (2)¶

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Subject-area moderator for tutees was non-significant (b = −0.19, SE = 0.16, p > 0.05), indicating similar effects for reading and math outcomes.

"Additionally, effect sizes did not differ based on whether the outcome was a measure of reading or math (b = − 0.19, SE = 0.16, p > 0.05)."
