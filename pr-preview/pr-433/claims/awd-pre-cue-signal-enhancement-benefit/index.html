Evidence¶

Moores 2011¶

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Three-factor ANOVA and pairwise cue comparisons for the AwD group in the attention orientation analysis (set size eight crowded vs sixteen displays). Pre-cued accuracy exceeded both post-cued (p<.001) and uncued (p<.001) conditions; post-cued vs uncued was not significant (p=.076).

"Pair-wise comparisons for the cue type measure indicated significant differences between pre-cued and post-cued (p<.001) and between pre-cued and uncued (p<.001) conditions. The difference between post-cued and uncued conditions was not significant statistically (p=.076)."

Moores 2011 (2)¶

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Pairwise cue comparisons for the control group in the same attention orientation analysis. Only the pre-cued vs uncued contrast reached significance (p<.01); pre-cued vs post-cued and post-cued vs uncued did not differ significantly.

"Pair-wise comparisons for cue type indicated significant differences between the pre-cued and uncued (p<.01) conditions only."
