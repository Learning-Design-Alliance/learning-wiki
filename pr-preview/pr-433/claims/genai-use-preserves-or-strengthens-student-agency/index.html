Interview findings from the qualitative study. One participant reported, "I feel like I've gained a sense of control over my research process," noting the AI helped navigate literature while final decisions remained the participant's own.

"The students described feeling more empowered and skilled thanks to GenAI, rather than subservient or irrelevant. They attributed an increase in their critical thinking and writing quality to the AI collaboration."

Related Claims¶

  • Graduate students experienced GenAI-assisted research as supporting originality through new ideas and perspectives — related
  • Graduate students viewed personalization and critical modification of AI-generated content as necessary for maintaining authorship — related
  • GenAI's effects on higher-order thinking are mixed: support for reflection and idea expansion alongside cognitive offloading and no overall innovativeness gain — related
  • In interviews with 25 engineering students at one Swedish university, students described GenAI as acceptable when it stimulates reflection and directly copying its outputs as cheating, and one used its fallibility as a prompt to double-check — related
  • Students exercise ethical agency in GenAI use through human-AI collaboration strategies, personal ethical frameworks, strategic calibration of use, and resistance to over-reliance — related
  • The educational value of GAI is contingent on pedagogical integration, not on the technology itself — related
  • A student maintained critical distance from GenAI outputs through a paradoxical trust in their untrustworthiness, deriving practical value while staying sceptical — related
