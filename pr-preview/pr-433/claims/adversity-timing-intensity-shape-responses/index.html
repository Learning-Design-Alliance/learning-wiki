Narrative review synthesis of studies on adversity intensity. The review reports "Children with high scores on the Life Stress Interview (LSI), which quantifies the intensity of children’s stress exposure, have smaller amygdala and hippocampal volumes than children exposed to less intense levels of early life stress." No effect sizes are printed.

Related Claims¶

  • Early life stress produces structural and functional alterations in prefrontal-hippocampal-amygdala circuits in animals and humans, with reduced hippocampal volume among the most reliable human findings — related
  • Children's perception and interpretation of stressors, rather than event type alone, shapes neurobiological stress responses — related
  • Threat and deprivation show overlapping effects on PFC-amygdala connectivity and hippocampal volume — related
  • Chronic HPA axis activation via CRH and glucocorticoids is proposed to partially mediate the neural effects of early life stress — related
  • Hippocampal volume relates to episodic memory differently across development: adults show head/body associations, children show tail associations — related
  • Early life stress disrupts dopaminergic reward circuitry in animals and humans, with altered reward learning and links to depressive and anxiety symptoms — related
  • Unpredictability and lack of contingent caregiver responding in childhood is associated with increased adolescent externalizing behaviors and altered prefrontal-hippocampal-amygdala circuits — related
  • The probability of school failure increases as a function of the timing and length of children's exposure to relational adversity — related
  • Lower SES adolescents show greater stress-related increase in serotonin transporter gene methylation, associated with greater amygdala reactivity and depression symptoms — related
  • Parenting quality and life stress fully mediate the relationship between SES and children's hippocampal volume — related
  • Exposure to adverse early circumstances affects the developing brain in ways that increase risk for later problems — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Children exposed to early adversity are at increased risk for a broad range of developmental difficulties affecting both cognitive and emotional adjustment — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Parental presence and support buffer children's neurobiological stress responses, mitigating adversity effects when support is high — related
  • Lower-SES adolescents have smaller hippocampal and DLPFC volumes but do not differ in caudate volumes — related
  • Threat-related psychopathology associations appear more consistently mediated by stress response systems than deprivation-related ones — related
  • Cumulative stress predicts adult outcomes beyond specific stressor types in longitudinal studies — related
  • Poverty predicts children's cortisol and executive function, partially mediated by parental sensitive caregiving — related
  • Each additional period of exposure to social and economic adversity accumulates to produce greater effects on educational and cognitive outcomes, though effects may plateau at a threshold — related
  • Variations in maternal care causally alter DNA methylation at the glucocorticoid receptor promoter with lasting effects on stress reactivity, as the review reports from Meaney and colleagues — related
