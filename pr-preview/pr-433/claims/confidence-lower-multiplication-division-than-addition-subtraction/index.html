Evidence¶

Lortie-Forgues 2017¶

Lortie-Forgues, Hugues and Siegler, Robert S (2017) Conceptual knowledge of decimal arithmetic. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 374-391. ISSN: 0022-0620. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000148

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Study 1 repeated-measures ANOVA of 5-point confidence ratings yielded a main effect of operation (F(3, 159) = 20.31, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.28); "Confidence in direction of effect judgments was lower for division" than multiplication and lower for multiplication than addition or subtraction.

"Confidence in direction of effect judgments was lower for division (M = 3.97, SD = 1.01) than for multiplication (M = 4.37, SD = 0.72; t(54) = 4.24, p < 0.001), and lower for multiplication than for addition"

Lortie-Forgues 2017 (2)¶

Lortie-Forgues, Hugues and Siegler, Robert S (2017) Conceptual knowledge of decimal arithmetic. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 374-391. ISSN: 0022-0620. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000148

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Subgroup analysis of the 49% of Study 1 participants whose judgments always followed whole number direction of effect showed "Confidence ratings did not differ between problems with numbers above and below one", consistent with the operation knowledge hypothesis rather than strong conviction.

"Confidence ratings did not differ between problems with numbers above and below one (for problems above one, M = 4.44, SD = 0.58; for problems below one, M = 4.46, SD = 0.58; t(26) = 0.42, p = 0.7)."
