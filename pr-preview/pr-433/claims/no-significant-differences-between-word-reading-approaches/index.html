Julia J. Yi 2026¶

Julia J. Yi, A. Angelique Aitken, Emily Sharp, Adiba Nusrat, Micheal Sandbank. (2026). What Works for Teaching Word Reading to Adolescents? A Meta-Analytic Investigation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10124-9

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r?

Meta-regression moderator analysis across the 37 interventions, which were coded for seven word-reading approaches plus integrated/multi. The article reports "No significant differences emerged between intervention approaches" and a trend toward null effects for whole word recognition.

"No significant differences emerged between intervention approaches, though trends suggested that interventions using whole word recognition were associated with null effects."

Related Claims¶

  • Phonics instruction has a small but statistically confirmed effect for children with reading disabilities, while Orton-Gillingham structured-literacy programs show positive but non-significant effects — related
  • Systematic phonics outperforms unsystematic or no-phonics instruction on children's reading overall, but its advantage over whole-word teaching on novel words is untested in children and contested — related
  • Prior reviews report positive word-reading intervention effects for adolescents but with a wide range (0-1.31) due to broad outcome conceptualization — related
  • Morphological instruction improves word-level literacy outcomes (decoding, spelling, vocabulary, morphological knowledge) but shows no reliable effect on reading comprehension — related
  • Word study intervention shows a moderate overall effect on word reading and comprehension for older struggling readers — related
