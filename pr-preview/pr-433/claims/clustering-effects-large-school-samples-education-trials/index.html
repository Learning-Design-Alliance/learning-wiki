Evidence¶

Peter Z. Schochet 2008¶

Peter Z. Schochet. (2008). Statistical Power for Random Assignment Evaluations of Education Programs. Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics, vol. 33, no. 1. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/journal-article-statistical-power-for-random-assignment-evaluations-of-education-programs

q2 · i? · theoretical · r?

The article's abstract reports its central empirical conclusion: clustering effects are "typically large" across designs, and consequently "large school samples are required for education trials". No effect size or sample count is printed in the available text.

"Clustering effects vary by design but are typically large. As a result, large school samples are required for education trials, and many evaluations will have sufficient power to detect precise impacts only for relatively large subgroups of sites."

Peter Z. Schochet 2005¶

Peter Z. Schochet. (2005). Statistical Power for Random Assignment Evaluations of Education Programs. Princeton, NJ: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/statistical-power-for-random-assignment-evaluations-of-education-programs

q2 · i? · theoretical · r?

The paper's empirical analysis of statistical power for education experiments finds that clustering effects are typically large across designs. The author reports that "large school samples are required" as a consequence, limiting the rigor of most impact studies to broad questions.

"Clustering effects vary by design but are typically large; consequently, large school samples are required, suggesting that most impact studies will only be able to address broad questions rigorously."
