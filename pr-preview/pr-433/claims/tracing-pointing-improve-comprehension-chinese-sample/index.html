Evidence¶

Yining Wang 2025¶

Yining Wang, Kexin Han, Paul Ginns. (2025). Tracing and Pointing Support Multimedia Learning: A Cross-Cultural Replication. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10005-7

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Randomized experiment with 84 native Chinese-speaking adults studying a 12-page paper-based heart lesson for 25 minutes, then completing a 20-item comprehension test. The tracing and pointing condition outperformed controls, "d = 0.55 [95% CI 0.11, ∞]", one-tailed p = 0.007.

"Participants in the tracing and pointing condition answered significantly more comprehension test questions correctly (M = 13.31, SD = 3.38) than participants in the non-tracing and pointing condition (M = 11.36, SD = 3.75), t(82) = 2.50, p = 0.007, d = 0.55 [95% CI 0.11, ∞], supporting H5."

Related Claims¶

  • The reviewed tracing studies consistently confirm learning benefits of tracing across retention, comprehension, and transfer measures, though cognitive-load hypotheses were not confirmed in all studies — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Pointing and tracing gestures improved retention and comprehension over no gesturing (review attributes this to Macken & Ginns, 2014) — related
  • In a three-group eye-tracking experiment, the pointing group significantly outperformed both the tracing group and the no-touch group on comprehension performance — related
  • Terminology test advantage of tracing and pointing is not robust to adjustment for prior knowledge — related
  • Gesturing during learning improves retention and reduces cognitive load — related
  • Pointing during multimedia learning leads to superior comprehension and increased attentional focus compared with tracing or no hand movements — related
  • Mimicking tracing and pointing gestures on a touchscreen during timed multimedia worked examples hindered adolescent learners' geometry learning — reports the opposite
