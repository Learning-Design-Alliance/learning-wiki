Evidence¶

Na'ilah Suad Nasir 2008¶

Na'ilah Suad Nasir, Victoria Hand and Edd V. Taylor. (2008). Culture and Mathematics in School: Boundaries between "Cultural" and "Domain" Knowledge in the Mathematics Classroom and Beyond. Review of Research in Education, Vol. 32. https://doi.org/10.3102/0091732X07308962

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing studies of grocery shopping (Lave et al., 1984), dairy case work (Scribner), street vending in Brazil (Carraher et al.; Saxe), and basketball (Nasir), that individuals "tended to score higher on tasks more closely linked to their everyday practices." This is the review's narrative synthesis of second-hand studies, not a pooled estimate.

"Such studies have also found that when asked the same questions in an everyday and a school format, individuals tended to score higher on tasks more closely linked to their everyday practices, though schooled people do sometimes bring their school knowledge to bear to solve out-of-school problems."
