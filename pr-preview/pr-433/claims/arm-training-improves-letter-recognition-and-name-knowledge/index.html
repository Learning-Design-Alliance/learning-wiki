Evidence¶

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024¶

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

q4 · i1 · causal · r3

Ordinal regression on letter recognition (12-point circling task) in the RCT, after combining the non-significant pencil-and-paper and arm-body groups; the arm training group showed "OR = 1.908, d = .356".

"Combining the two latter groups resulted in a more parsimonious and significant model [chi2(1) = 4.4, p = .037], according to which the arm training group was almost twice more likely to score high on letter recognition on post-tests than the other two groups (b = 0.646, OR = 1.908, d = .356, p = .038)."

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro 2024 (2)¶

Fernando Núñez-Regueiro, Natacha Boissicat, Fanny Gimbert, Céline Pobel-Burtin, Marie-Caroline Croset, Marie-Line Bosse, and Cécile Nurra. (2024). Involving the Body to Improve Letter Knowledge and Script: An Experimental Study in French Kindergarten. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09923-9

q4 · i1 · causal · r2

Ordinal regression on letter-name knowledge (6-point naming task) in the same RCT; the article adds that pencil-and-paper training was borderline-significantly negative (p = .069) on this outcome.

"effects on letter-name knowledge were positive and significant for the arm training group (b = 0.614, OR = 1.629, d = .269, p = .030)"
