Evidence¶

Blair 2024¶

Blair, K. P., Banes, L. C., & Martin, L. (2024). Fostering noticing of classroom discussion features through analysis of contrasting cases. Instructional Science, 52:417–452. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09661-z

q2 · i2 · causal · r1

Study 1 follow-up t-test on student-focused codes; the article calls it "a marginal increase" (p = .096, d = 0.43).

"Follow-up analysis found a marginal increase in the total number of student-focused codes from pre-to-post (t(16) = -1.77, p = .096, Cohen’s d = 0.43), consistent with one of our hypothesized patterns of shifts."

Related Claims¶

  • Preservice teachers became more likely to notice the absence of students building on each other's ideas and marginally more likely to notice open-ended questions
  • Teacher valence codes showed a nominal non-significant interaction: presence codes decreased while absence/missed-opportunity codes increased
  • After a dialogic-focused professional development intervention, mathematics teachers noticed student dialogue significantly more, in both attending (83 to 170 cases) and responding (24 to 71 cases)
  • Individual trajectories varied: 9 of 14 teachers increased dialogic noticing per episode, while two teachers slightly decreased, which the authors attribute to limits of the nondirective intervention
  • The intervention significantly increased teachers' noticing of six dialogic attributes — persuasive interaction, one leads and another is led, agreement and collective attention, students have equal status, dialogic gap, and monologue — but not of dialogue-expansion attributes
  • A contrasting cases activity shifted the focus of preservice teachers' video noticing from teacher-focused to student-focused codes (significant time × subject-focus interaction) — related
