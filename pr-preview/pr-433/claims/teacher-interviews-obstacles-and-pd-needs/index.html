Evidence¶

Nikolai Maurer 2026¶

Nikolai Maurer, Mathea Brückner, Christoph Thyssen, Sebastian Becker-Genschow, Johannes Huwer. (2026). Science educators' AI literacy and AI usage in teaching: Implications for post-qualification programs. Computers and Education Open 10. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100376

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Semi-structured post-questionnaire interviews with n = 21 teachers, analyzed by inductive consensus coding; obstacle counts are descriptive frequencies with no effect size. Teachers also cited lack of teacher competencies and costs (n = 7 each).

"Regarding the obstacles teachers perceive in using AI, the most frequently cited issues fell into the categories of insufficient "output quality" of AI and concerns about a decline in students' cognitive engagement when AI is used as a tool in the classroom ("lack of cognitive ability") (n = 9 each)."

Nikolai Maurer 2026 (2)¶

Nikolai Maurer, Mathea Brückner, Christoph Thyssen, Sebastian Becker-Genschow, Johannes Huwer. (2026). Science educators' AI literacy and AI usage in teaching: Implications for post-qualification programs. Computers and Education Open 10. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100376

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Same interview study: coded responses on desired professional-development topics, with subject-specific examples (n = 11) ranked first and general use (n = 7) second; needs for time savings (n = 6) and legal frameworks (n = 5) were also expressed.

"Regarding professional development, most teachers explicitly emphasized the importance of subject-specific examples for the application of AI ("specific examples of use") (n = 11), followed by an overview of general application possibilities for AI ("general use") (n = 7) as the two most relevant topics."
