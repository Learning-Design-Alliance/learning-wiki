Evidence¶

Moll K. 2015¶

Moll K., Snowling M. J., Göbel S. M., Hulme C. (2015). Early language and executive skills predict variations in number and arithmetic skills in children at family-risk of dyslexia and typically developing controls. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.03.004

q3 · i? · associational · r2

In the final longitudinal path model, counting and number knowledge at t2 predicted arithmetic efficiency at t3, "account[ing] for 46% (FR) and 40% (TD) of the variance in arithmetic at t3"; phonological awareness was not a predictor. Arithmetic was measured with timed one-minute addition and subtraction tests.

"both counting skills and number knowledge at t2 (but not phonological awareness) predict formal arithmetic skills at t3. These two variables together account for 46% (FR) and 40% (TD) of the variance in arithmetic at t3."
