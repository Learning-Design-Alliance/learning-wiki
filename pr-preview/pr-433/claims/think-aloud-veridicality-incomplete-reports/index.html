Evidence¶

Hu 2017¶

Hu, Jingjing; Gao, Xuesong (Andy). (2017). Using think-aloud protocol in self-regulated reading research. Educational Research Review, v.22, pp. 181-193. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.09.004

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Scott (2008) with fourth-grade students across four assessment methods, that think-aloud showed more veridicality in accessing self-regulated reading processes than the other three methods.

"Results indicated that the participants in the think-aloud group reported a larger number and a broader range of self-regulated reading strategies, particularly metacognitive reading strategies."

Hu 2017 (2)¶

Hu, Jingjing; Gao, Xuesong (Andy). (2017). Using think-aloud protocol in self-regulated reading research. Educational Research Review, v.22, pp. 181-193. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.09.004

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Branch (2001), that participants unfamiliar with the task could not think aloud; Barkaoui (2011) found adults reported being unable to verbalize all thoughts because some were automatic or too complex.

"Findings showed that some participants had difficulties in verbalizing their metacognition during reading."
