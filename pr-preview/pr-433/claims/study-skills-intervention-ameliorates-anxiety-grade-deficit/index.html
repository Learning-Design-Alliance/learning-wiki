Evidence¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer 2023¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer. (2023). Strategies for remediating the impact of math anxiety on high school math performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00188-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Linear mixed model of second-semester grades with random effects for participants, course subject, teacher, and previous math performance (N = 224 combined). The model also showed a significant main effect of math anxiety (χ2(1) = 8.42, p = 0.004; β = −3.87, t(160.89) = −2.90) and "a significant interaction between the AAI-Math scores and intervention group".

"While there was no signiﬁcant main effect of group on grades, χ2(1) = 0.003, p = 0.95, there was a signiﬁcant interaction between the AAI-Math scores and intervention group for math grades, χ2(1) = 6.73, p = 0.010 (Fig. 1, Right)."

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer 2023 (2)¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer. (2023). Strategies for remediating the impact of math anxiety on high school math performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00188-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Descriptive contrast reported for the interaction from the same LMM analysis of second-semester grades: at the 4th quartile of anxiety scores, SS students scored "approximately half a letter grade higher" than ER students, a difference of 6.29 grade points.

"On average, for students highest in math anxiety, students in the Study Skills group had grades that were approximately half a letter grade higher compared to their peers that were randomly assigned to the Emotion Regulation group (6.29 grade points, calculated as a difference score between groups in the 4th quartile of anxiety scores)."

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer 2023 (3)¶

Rachel G. Pizzie and David J. M. Kraemer. (2023). Strategies for remediating the impact of math anxiety on high school math performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00188-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The same interaction analysis indicated a reversal at low anxiety: "the Emotion Regulation Intervention was associated with better math performance" for students lower in math anxiety. The authors offer the quoted explanation speculatively, as an untested interpretation.

"For students lower in math anxiety, these results suggest that the Emotion Regulation Intervention was associated with better math performance – possibly because these students may have already been using their own effective study strategies."

Related Claims¶

  • The math-anxiety grade effects were specific to math anxiety: trait anxiety and test anxiety showed no interactions with intervention group or quarter — related
  • Math anxiety is associated with decreased grade performance in high school math classes before any intervention is introduced — related
