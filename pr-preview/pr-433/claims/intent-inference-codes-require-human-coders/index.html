Liu 2026¶

Liu, A., Esbenshade, L., Xiao, M., Tian, V., Zhang, Z., He, K., & Sun, M. (2026). Agreement Is Not Quality: Blind Expert Verification of Human and LLM Qualitative Coding When Human Consensus Is Not Ground Truth. Preprint, no identifier printed in text. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28890 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.28890

q2 · i? · design · r2

Per-code endorsement analysis of contested code applications in the blind verification protocol. These are codes, in the article’s words, where "LLMs over-apply and the verifier rejects their application", and they "require recognizing pedagogical intent not explicitly stated in the text".

"Tiered Scaffolding (human endorsed 86%, LLM 27%), Generate Summative Assessments (75% vs. 21%), and Multimedia Use for Instruction (71% vs. 35%) represent codes where LLMs over-apply and the verifier rejects their application."

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Division-of-labor classification of the 72-code instrument: the Human-required category contains 15 codes. The article states these codes "require recognizing pedagogical intent from context", and that until models develop richer pedagogical reasoning they cannot be automated without quality loss.

"These codes show human endorsement two to five times higher than LLM endorsement, for example Tiered Scaffolding (86% vs. 27%) and Generate Summative Assessments (75% vs. 21%). They require recognizing pedagogical intent from context, a capability current models do not reliably demonstrate."
