Evidence¶

Gankin 2026¶

Gankin, K., Venkatesh, R., Khokhlovich, E., & Vyshedskiy, A. (2026). Receptive and expressive language phenotyping in over 62,000 autistic individuals. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00445-3

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Heatmap-based participant clustering of all 62,414 participants against the 26 linguistic items (Figure 7). The remaining clusters were Single-Sentence + Modifier (25.4%), Nonspeaking + Command (30.5%), and Single-Word + Modifier (22.3%), mapped in the article's own terms.

"The leftmost cluster of participants shows the predominant blue color (indicating competency) across all six clusters of linguistic items and is therefore termed the Multi-Sentence-expression/Syntactic-comprehension Phenotype (marked on Figure 7 as “Multi-Sentence + Syntactic;” 9.7% of participants, Table 8)."

Gankin 2026 (2)¶

Gankin, K., Venkatesh, R., Khokhlovich, E., & Vyshedskiy, A. (2026). Receptive and expressive language phenotyping in over 62,000 autistic individuals. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00445-3

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Chi-square test on Table 9 counts: the Multi-Sentence + Syntactic Phenotype contained more mild ASD (80.4% vs 4.0% severe), while Nonspeaking + Command contained more severe ASD (34.3%). Significant per the printed statistic; no effect size printed.

"A Chi-square test of independence revealed a significant association between receptive-expressive phenotypes and ASD severity distribution, χ2 (6, N = 62,414) = 8121.71, p < 0.0001."
