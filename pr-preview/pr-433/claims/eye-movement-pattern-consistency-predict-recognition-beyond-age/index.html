Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In adults, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement pattern, with eye movement consistency adding no additional variance — reports the opposite
  • Older age is associated with lower eye movement consistency in face recognition, reflected in higher overall entropy and C3 entropy — related
  • Older age is associated with poorer face recognition: lower d′, higher false alarm rate, and longer response time — related
  • Older age is marginally associated with a greater response bias toward affirming faces as seen, independent of eye movement behavior — related
  • Lower eye movement consistency (higher C3 entropy) is associated with poorer selective attention and inhibitory control — related
  • C3 entropy partially mediates the relationship between age and face recognition performance — related
  • In typically developing children aged 6–11, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement consistency but not by eye movement pattern — reports the opposite
  • Children's eye movement consistency is associated with selective attention and executive function, whereas eye movement pattern relates to working memory — a broader claim this one bears on
  • A more eyes-focused scanning pattern and higher eye movement consistency are each correlated with better face recognition performance — related
  • In partially trained DNN+HMM models simulating children, eye movement consistency (lower entropy) predicts recognition accuracy but eye movement pattern does not — related
