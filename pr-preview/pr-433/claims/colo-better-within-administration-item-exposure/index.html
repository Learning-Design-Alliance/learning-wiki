q2 · i? · design · r2

Item exposure rates within an administration were computed for each item in the pool (Tables 4.7–4.15) for both engines. The report attributes the difference to the randomesque procedure "implemented on COLO but not on the CBE."

"Within the same administration, more items in the item bank were administered and less items were overexposed by COLO than by the CBE. This is likely due to the randomesque procedure implemented on COLO but not on the CBE to control item exposure."

Related Claims¶

  • Across administrations, the CBE may re-administer an item to the same student before the longitudinal exposure window elapses when the item bank cannot fulfill test requirements, which COLO does not allow — related
  • Adaptivity and score reliability of both engines depend on item-pool depth; the CBE often performed better than COLO for extreme low or high achievers when item banks were shallow — related
  • Item exposure rates are comparable across engines, with most items below 10%, and the CBE Reading item-use rate is lower than COLO's — related
  • Test lengths are comparable across engines, but CBE test durations are higher than COLO's in both terms and content areas, more prominently in Reading — related
  • CBE shows better engine adaptivity than COLO, especially for extremely low or high achievement students — related
  • Differences in test-taking engagement across content categories were primarily due to differences in the reading load of items — related
