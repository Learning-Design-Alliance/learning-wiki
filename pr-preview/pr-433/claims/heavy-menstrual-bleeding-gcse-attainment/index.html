Evidence¶

Sawyer 2025¶

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Confounder-adjusted linear regression of GCSE total point score (range 0–540) in ALSPAC (N = 2698). The −5.65 coefficient is a raw score difference; no standardised effect size is printed.

"Participants reporting heavy or prolonged bleeding had a lower total GCSE score ( −5.65; 95% CI: −10.10, −1.20) compared with those who did not report heavy or prolonged bleeding, after adjusting for confounders (equivalent to roughly a 1 grade reduction, i.e., moving from an A to a B)."

Sawyer 2025 (2)¶

Sawyer, G., Fraser, A., Lawlor, D. A., Sharp, G. C. & Howe, L. D. (2025). Associations of adolescent menstrual symptoms with school absences and educational attainment: analysis of a prospective cohort study. npj Science of Learning 10:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00338-x

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Logistic regression for the binary attainment outcome (five A*-C including Maths and English), reported with Fig. 1. Unadjusted analyses supported the same conclusions.

"heavy or prolonged bleeding (OR 0.73; 95% CI: 0.71, 0.75: absolute risk difference−5.9%) and menstrual pain (OR 0.84; 95 CI: 0.81, 0.86: absolute risk difference −2.2%) were associated with a reduced likelihood of achieving ﬁve A*-C GCSEs including Maths and English"
