Evidence¶

H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari 2018¶

H. Moriah Sokolowski and Daniel Ansari. (2018). Understanding the effects of education through the lens of biology. npj Science of Learning 3:17. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0032-y

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing a recent meta-analysis, that "mindset interventions have only a weak effect on later academic achievement"; the authors attribute this to the nurture assumption that individual differences lack a biological basis. No effect size is printed.

"However, a recent meta-analysis examining the effectiveness of mindset interventions on academic achievement revealed that mindset interventions have only a weak effect on later academic achievement."

Related Claims¶

  • Mind-set interventions show weak overall effects on achievement, with some support that low-SES or academically at-risk students might benefit — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • GMT as a standalone intervention shows small average effects that approach zero when publication bias is controlled, per the review's account of Macnamara and Burgoyne's meta-analysis — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Prior meta-analyses report mixed effects of growth mindset interventions on achievement, with estimates ranging from a non-significant d = 0.05 to d = 0.14 — related
  • Growth-mindset interventions have weak average effects on achievement, including a negligible GPA gain (d = 0.11) for lower-achieving US ninth-graders in one preregistered national trial — related
