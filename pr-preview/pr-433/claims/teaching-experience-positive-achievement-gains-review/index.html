Evidence¶

Kini 2016¶

Kini, T., & Podolsky, A. (2016). Does Teaching Experience Increase Teacher Effectiveness? A Review of the Research. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/our-work/publications-resources/does-teaching-experience-increase-teacher-effectiveness-review-research doi:10.54300/625.642

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review's synthesis of 30 studies since 2003 examining teaching experience and student achievement on standardized tests reports that "28 found that teaching experience is positively and significantly associated with teacher effectiveness." No effect size is printed for this synthesis count.

"Of these 30 studies, 28 found that teaching experience is positively and significantly associated with teacher effectiveness."

q3 · i? · review · r2

Among the roughly two-thirds of reviewed studies using longitudinal datasets with teacher fixed effects, the preferred within-teacher method, the review reports "18 out of 18 found that teaching experience is positively associated with teacher effectiveness." No effect size is printed for this count.

"Of these studies, 18 out of 18 found that teaching experience is positively associated with teacher effectiveness."

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers continue to improve in their second decade of teaching, though fewer studies find improvement after 15 years — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • A Florida study found experience effects in elementary and middle school math and reading up to 0.16 SD for teachers with 15–24 years, but no returns to experience for high school teachers — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In a North Carolina high school study with teacher fixed effects, achievement returns to experience rose up to 20 years, from about 0.06 SD at 1–2 years to about 0.27 SD beyond 27 years — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • On average, more effective early-career teachers remain in the teaching profession and stay in their initial schools — related
  • In a North Carolina middle school study, a teacher with 12 years of experience raised scores 0.08 SD in ELA and 0.18 SD in math versus a novice teacher, with returns persisting through 21–27 years — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
