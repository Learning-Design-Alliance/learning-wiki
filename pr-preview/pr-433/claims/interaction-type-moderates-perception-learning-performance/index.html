Evidence¶

Hauert 2026¶

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

q4 · i? · causal · r2

Stepwise multiple regression in the randomized trial (n = 66) testing the Step 3 interaction term for perception of learning by interaction type on performance; the article reports "F (1, 62) = 8.84, p = .004, ΔR2 = .123, b = -9.50".

"The results supported a moderator effect of interaction type on the relationship between perception of learning and performance, F (1, 62) = 8.84, p = .004, ΔR2 = .123, b = -9.50."

Hauert 2026 (2)¶

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

q4 · i? · causal · r2

Subgroup regression analyses exploring each interaction group separately. In the learner–content group the relationship was positive (F(1,31) = 7.13, p = .012, R2 = .187, b = 7.01); in the learner–learner group there was no predictive relationship, F(1,31) = 1.82, p = .187, R2 = .06.

"In the learner–content interaction group, the relationship between perception of learning and performance was statistically significant and positive, F(1,31) = 7.13, p = .012, R2 = .187, b = 7.01."
