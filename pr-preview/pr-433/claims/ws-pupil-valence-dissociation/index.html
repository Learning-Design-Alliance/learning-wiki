Evidence¶

Hallman 2026¶

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning and arousal responses in Down, Fragile X, and Williams syndromes. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00438-2

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Follow-up pairwise comparisons of pupil dilation (Fig. 2) in the Results section of the Bayesian LMM. The article concludes "feedback valence may haveno reliable effect on pupil dilation" in WS, whereas TD showed loss-driven dilation.

"For the TD group, pupil dilation were larger following negative feedback (losses) compared to positive feedback (wins) (b = 0.37, 95% HPD [0.23, 0.5]). In contrast, the WS group showed no reliable difference in pupil response between negative and positive feedback (b = 0.03, 95% HPD [−0.19, 0.24])."

Hallman 2026 (2)¶

Hallman, A. E. Z., Willfors, C., Frick, M. A., Nordgren, A. & Kleberg, J. L. (2026). Comparing feedback learning and arousal responses in Down, Fragile X, and Williams syndromes. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00438-2

q3 · i? · causal · r?

The syndrome-by-feedback interaction in the pupil LMM (Table 4), Results section. The interaction was significant for WS but not for the FXS or DS groups, for which the evidence remained inconclusive.

"For WS, there was moderate evidence for an interaction effect between syndrome and feedback (b = 0.34, 95% CI [0.09, 0.59], BF = 6.66, pd =1 ) , indicating that feedback valence inﬂuenced pupil responses differently in WS compared to TD. This effect was notevident for the FXS or DS groups."
