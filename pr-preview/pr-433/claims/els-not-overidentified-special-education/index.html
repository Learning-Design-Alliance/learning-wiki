Evidence¶

de la Torre 2019¶

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Descriptive comparison of IEP identification rates across elementary grades (Table 1 shows 7 vs 5 percent in kindergarten, 11 vs 10 in third, 17 vs 15 in eighth). The report notes began-as-EL students with IEPs were more often identified with learning disabilities (75 vs 65 percent). No effect size is printed.

"Our results do not support this theory: over the course of their academic careers, continuously-enrolled CPS students who began as ELs were identified as needing IEPs at similar rates to students who were never classified as ELs."

Related Claims¶

  • Students who began as ELs and demonstrated proficiency by eighth grade had academic outcomes similar to or higher than students never classified as ELs — related
  • Students who began kindergarten as English Learners had similar achievement and growth, and higher attendance, compared to students never classified as ELs — related
  • More than half of students who began as ELs passed the ACCESS proficiency exam by third grade and three-fourths by fifth grade; students who had not passed by fifth grade were unlikely to pass before high school — related
  • ELs who did not reach proficiency by eighth grade showed continued growth but started first grade with much lower ACCESS scores, and rising grade-level thresholds meant they never met the proficiency cut — related
  • GPAs were very similar for students who did and did not enter CPS as ELs, with a reading-grade gap that closed by seventh grade and similar Freshman OnTrack rates — related
  • TEP's student population was similar to other neighborhood schools: high poverty, mostly Hispanic, with similar rates of special education services — related
  • New programs increased training for leading special student populations, but sufficiency across all contexts remains debated — related
  • The study gives explicit consideration to cultural differences and similarities in learning, including English learners and learners with learning disabilities — related
