Evidence¶

Amante 2026¶

Amante, C., Lucero, M., & Montanero, M. (2026). Learning with concept maps: the effect of activity structure and the type of task. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09764-1

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Two-way ANOVA on 226 undergraduates randomly assigned to 12 conditions compared three activity structures. The I+C condition outperformed the individual condition in comprehension ("F (2, 223) = 3.84, p =.023, η2 = 0.03") and both other structures in recall (η2 = 0.12).

"The difference was significant with the individual work condition in terms of the level of understanding the contents, F (2, 223) = 3.84, p =.023, η2 = 0.03, and with both conditions in the recall variable F (2, 223) = 5.86, p =.004, η2 = 0.12."

Related Claims¶

  • Self-constructing a complete concept map produces better delayed recall than completing, sorting a map, or writing a summary, with the largest effect in the I+C structure — related
  • Summary writing produced no significant comprehension improvement and the most disputational talk, and did not significantly differ from completing a map in learning outcomes — related
  • Task type and activity structure interact on recall: self-constructed maps help mainly when individually prepared and then discussed in pairs — related
  • In one reviewed study, students who first collaborate and then complete an activity individually in the VRL are more successful than students who complete an individual task first and then collaborate — reports the opposite
  • Self-constructed collaborative concept maps trigger more deep exploratory talk and less non-dialogic talk than completing or sorting maps, which yield mostly superficial exploratory talk — related
  • Technology type, collaboration level, and intervention duration show no significant differences in concept-mapping effects — reports the opposite
  • An individual preparation phase yields significantly more integration-oriented and conflict-oriented consensus building and less externalisation and non-task talk in the social dimension — related
