Evidence¶

Ritter 2007¶

Ritter, S., Anderson, J. R., Koedinger, K. R., & Corbett, A. (2007). Cognitive Tutor: Applied research in mathematics education. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 14(2), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03194060

q3 · i? · causal · r1

A study in Moore, Oklahoma in which teachers taught some classes with Cognitive Tutor and some with their previous textbook. Cognitive Tutor students "scored higher on a standardized test," received higher grades, and reported greater confidence and usefulness beliefs; the article prints no effect size for these individual outcomes.

"The result was that the Cognitive Tutor students scored higher on a standardized test (the ETS Algebra I End-of-Course Assessment), received higher grades, reported greater confidence in their mathematical abilities, and were more likely to believe that mathematics would be useful to them outside of school."
