Hao 2025¶

Hao, Z.; Cao, J.; Li, R.; Yu, J.; Liu, Z.; Zhang, Y. (2025). Mapping Student-AI Interaction Dynamics in Multi-Agent Learning Environments: Supporting Personalised Learning and Reducing Performance Gaps. https://osf.io/preprints/eduRxiv

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Behavioral proportion comparison between the top-30% (n = 33) and bottom-30% (n = 38) pre-test groups in the MAIC course. The article reports significantly more regulatory behaviors among high prior knowledge students and more question-raising among low prior knowledge students.

"students with higher prior knowledge demonstrated significantly more regulatory behaviors, including monitoring and regulating the learning process (12.9% vs. 11.2%), managing AI peers (7.5% vs. 4.5%), and sharing emotions (7.4% vs. 2.6%). In contrast, students with lower prior knowledge were more likely to engage in question-raising behaviors (55.3% vs. 45.2%)"

Hao, Z.; Cao, J.; Li, R.; Yu, J.; Liu, Z.; Zhang, Y. (2025). Mapping Student-AI Interaction Dynamics in Multi-Agent Learning Environments: Supporting Personalised Learning and Reducing Performance Gaps. https://osf.io/preprints/eduRxiv

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Group-level LSA of behavioral transitions (Figures 7a and 7b) found more significant sequences for the high prior knowledge group, centered on SB5 regulation, whereas the low group's significant sequences centered on SB4 negotiating and confirming.

"the group with higher prior knowledge exhibited a greater number of significant behavioral sequences compared to the low prior knowledge group. Specifically, in the interactions between high prior knowledge students and AI agents, the monitoring and regulating process (SB5) plays a more central role"
