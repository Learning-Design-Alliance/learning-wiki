Evidence¶

Xia Zhichao 2018¶

Xia Zhichao, Zhang Linjun, Hoeft Fumiko, Gu Bin, Gong Gaolang, Shu Hua. (2018). Neural Correlates of Oral Word Reading, Silent Reading Comprehension, and Cognitive Subcomponents. International Journal of Behavioral Development. https://doi.org/10.1177/0165025417727872

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Whole-brain vertex-wise regression (n = 21) in the Results section, with Random Field Theory correction (p-cluster < 0.05). Clusters were in the "right superior temporal gyrus", left inferior temporal gyrus, left superior temporal gyrus and left supramarginal gyrus; no regions showed a significant negative correlation.

"Significant positive correlations between CT and word reading efficiency were found in four brain areas after controlling for age, gender and performance IQ (Table 3, Figure 1A)."

Related Claims¶

  • For sentence comprehension regions, LPHP thickness is correlated with morphological awareness and RV1 thickness with orthography-semantic mapping — related
  • The authors hypothesize that brain-reading cortical thickness correlations are positive in early reading acquisition and negative at later, consolidated stages — related
  • The word-reading and comprehension cortical regions are specific to their respective reading level: cross-level correlations were nonsignificant after FDR correction — related
  • In Chinese children aged 10-12, silent sentence reading comprehension is positively correlated with cortical thickness in the left parahippocampus and right calcarine fissure — related
  • Reading-defined regions uniquely predict distinct cognitive subcomponents: LSTG for phonetic representation, LSMG for phonological awareness, and LITG for orthography-phonology mapping — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Word reading efficiency and sentence comprehension show distinct behavioral subcomponent profiles: word reading correlates with phonological subcomponents, comprehension with morphological awareness and orthography-semantic mapping — related
  • Encoding L2 words by viewing videos of social situations engages more native-like neural responses than textual translation, and in a second study improved behavioral performance over non-social videos — related
  • In six cortical parcels, individual neural scores reflecting category separability significantly predicted behavioral concept-knowledge scores in novices after one hour of instruction — related
  • VR-based interactive L2 vocabulary learning shows cortical-thickness correlations in right IPL across training and with delayed retention, unlike non-VR learning — related
