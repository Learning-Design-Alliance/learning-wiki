Evidence¶

Tamara van Gog & Nikol Rummel 2010¶

Tamara van Gog & Nikol Rummel. (2010). Example-Based Learning: Integrating Cognitive and Social-Cognitive Research Perspectives. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9134-7

q2 · i? · review · r3

A narrative review synthesizing worked-example experiments, mostly with highly structured tasks in domains such as algebra, statistics, geometry, and physics. The review attributes the effect to worked examples preventing weak means-ends analysis, stating that better outcomes are "often reached with less investment of time and effort during acquisition".

"Research on studying worked examples has consistently shown that for novice learners, instruction that relies more heavily on studying worked examples than on problem solving is more effective for learning, as well as more efficient in that better learning outcomes are often reached with less investment of time and effort during acquisition"

Tamara van Gog & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2010¶

Tamara van Gog & Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2010). Cognitive Load Theory: Advances in Research on Worked Examples, Animations, and Cognitive Load Measurement. Educ Psychol Rev (2010) 22:375–378. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9145-4

q2 · i? · review · r1

Narrative review reporting the accumulated worked-example research, citing Cooper and Sweller 1987, Paas 1992, Sweller and Cooper 1985 among others. The review states worked examples are "more effective in facilitating learning and transfer compared to conventional problem solving"; no effect sizes are printed.

"An impressive body of research over the last few decades has shown that for novice learners, worked examples are more effective in facilitating learning and transfer compared to conventional problem solving, and are often more efficient in their requirements of time or mental effort"
