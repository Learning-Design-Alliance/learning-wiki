Evidence¶

Yunhee Kim 2026¶

Yunhee Kim, Doyeon Chae, Jaehwan Jang, Kyeong Taek Oh, Byeong-Sun Park & Min-gu Kim. (2026). Does tapping in sync reflect cognitive control? Links between sensorimotor synchronization, social context, and executive function in early childhood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00443-5

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

Extended LMM (REML) on log-transformed DTW values including the 50 bpm bimanual task; the pairwise reduction during joint tapping was significant (∆M = -0.05, SE = .02, p = .005), described as "a modest but reliable synchronization benefit across task types".

"The main effect of Social Context was significant, F (1, 1656) = 7.81, p = .005, ηp2 = .005, such that synchronization variability was lower during Joint tapping (M = 2.31, SE = .27, 95% CI [1.14, 3.48]) than during Solo tapping condition"

Related Claims¶

  • Hand asymmetries in synchronization vary by task: left-hand variability is greater in sequential unimanual tasks but slightly reversed in the 50 bpm bimanual task — related
  • A co-tapping partner reduces synchronization variability significantly more at 150 bpm metronome (near children's SMT) than in music or slow bimanual tasks — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Rhythmic context dominates synchronization variability: music tapping is far less synchronized than metronome tapping, while the 50 bpm bimanual task does not differ from 150 bpm — related
  • Slow 50 bpm bimanual tapping in the solo condition is selectively associated with inhibitory control, working memory, and overall EF, though associations do not survive FDR correction — related
