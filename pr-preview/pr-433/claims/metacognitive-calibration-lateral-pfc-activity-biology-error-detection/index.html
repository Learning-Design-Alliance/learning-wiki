Evidence¶

Mei Grace Behrendt 2024¶

Mei Grace Behrendt, Carrie Clark, McKenna Elliott & Joseph Dauer. (2024). Relation of life sciences students' metacognitive monitoring to neural activity during biology error detection. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00231-z

q2 · i? · associational · r2

fMRI study of 50 undergraduates from an introductory life sciences course performing a biology model error detection task. In group models, phi and metacognitive efficiency were linked to a right middle frontal cluster spanning BA 8 and 9; the article reports the cluster "spanned BA 8 and 9". No effect size printed.

"Controlling for accuracy on the model evaluation task, students' ϕ values were positively associated with and their level of activity in the right middle frontal gyrus for the Error > No error model contrast"

Related Claims¶

  • Confidence bias is not linked to course grades or task accuracy, but lower confidence bias is associated with more left inferior frontal gyrus activity during error evaluation — related
  • Neural contrasts: confident trials recruit widespread fronto-parietal activation; confident-error trials recruit medial frontal and bilateral inferior prefrontal/insular regions; overconfidence differences are confined to the lingual gyrus — related
  • Metacognitive measures show minimal correlations with course grades except meta-d', while task accuracy correlates with grades — related
  • Students' confidence distinguishes accurate from inaccurate model-evaluation trials, with a large printed effect size (d = 1.09), though students remain often confident on inaccurate trials — related
