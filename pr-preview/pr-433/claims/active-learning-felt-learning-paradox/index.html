Evidence¶

Favero 2025¶

Favero, L., Pérez-Ortiz, J. A., Käser, T., & Oliver, N. (2025). Do AI tutors empower or enslave learners? Toward a critical use of AI in education. Applications of Generative AI to support teaching and learning in Higher Education, co-located with AIED 2025, Palermo, Italy. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06878 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2507.06878

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The paper reports, citing Deslauriers et al., that "students in active learning environments learned more but felt they learned less". This cognitive bias leads students to favor fluent, low-effort activities that give the illusion of learning; no effect size is printed.

"Interestingly, Deslauriers et al.[29] found that students in active learning environments learned more but felt they learned less. Mental effort was misinterpreted as failure, while smooth lectures mistakenly felt more effective, though they were not."

Ken Koedinger 2024¶

Ken Koedinger. (2024). Navigating Ethical Benefits and Risks as AIED Comes of Age. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-023-00350-5

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Commentary assertion citing Deslauriers et al. (2019) via footnote; the article itself reports no data. It attributes to that cited study that students' perceived learning favored passive lectures while actual learning favored active learning, explaining the mismatch by the psychological aversiveness of failure.

"Physics students at Harvard think passive lectures are better for their learning but they're not 3. Active learning is better for learning."
