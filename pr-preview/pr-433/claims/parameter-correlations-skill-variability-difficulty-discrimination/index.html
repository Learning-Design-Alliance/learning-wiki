Evidence¶

Pelánek 2015¶

Pelánek, R., & Jarušek, P. (2015). Student Modeling Based on Problem Solving Times. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0048-x

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of fitted parameters on real Problem Solving Tutor data; skill estimates were approximately normally distributed with larger variance for educational problems than logic puzzles, and the correlation with per-attempt variability is "in range from r =− 0.2t o r =− 0.4".

"This correlation differs for individual problem types, but typically it is in range from r =− 0.2t o r =− 0.4."

Pelánek 2015 (2)¶

Pelánek, R., & Jarušek, P. (2015). Student Modeling Based on Problem Solving Times. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0048-x

q2 · i? · design · r2

Observational correlation analysis of problem parameters a, b, c fitted on real tutor data: "more difficult problems are more discriminating ( r = 0.17)", with weaker variance correlations (r = 0.09, r = 0.16). The authors note the results indirectly support the log transformation.

"There is a slight correlation between the basic problem difficulty and its discrimination – more difficult problems are more discriminating ( r = 0.17)."
