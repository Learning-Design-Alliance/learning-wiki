Evidence¶

Mark Feng Teng 2026¶

Mark Feng Teng. (2026). Effectiveness of GenAI in enhancing writing performance: a meta-analysis. Asian-Pacific Journal of Second and Foreign Language Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40862-026-00444-2

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Subgroup moderator analysis in the expanded main synthesis (k = 11). Traditional classroom studies (k = 3, g = 1.19, 95% CI [0.73, 1.64]) showed a larger pooled effect than AI teaching studies (k = 8, g = 0.69, 95% CI [0.41, 0.96]); QB = 27.26, p < .001.

"the subgroup analysis initially indicated that the instructional model was a significant source of between-study heterogeneity ( QB = 27.26, p <. 001), with traditional classrooms associated with a larger descriptive effect size (g=1.19) than AI-teaching contexts (g=.69)."

Mark Feng Teng 2026 (2)¶

Mark Feng Teng. (2026). Effectiveness of GenAI in enhancing writing performance: a meta-analysis. Asian-Pacific Journal of Second and Foreign Language Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40862-026-00444-2

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Multivariate robust meta-regression (HC3) controlling for risk-of-bias classification and sample size. The traditional-classroom coefficient was "statistically non-significant (β = 0.74, p = .230)", and the article attributes the initial subgroup difference to confounding rather than pedagogical format.

"the coefficient for the traditional classroom model became statistically non-significant (β = 0.74, p = .230). This suggests that the initial subgroup differences may be partially confounded by study-level methodological quality or small-study effects, rather than the pedagogi -cal format itself."
