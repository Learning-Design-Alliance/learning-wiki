Evidence¶

Sara Coemans 2017¶

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of limitations. The review cites Moletsane and colleagues (2009) on the risk that publicly acknowledging authorship exposes participants to sanctions, and notes researchers hold power to decide how people are interpreted, risking further stigmatization.

"Using arts-based methods poses new ethical challenges in conducting research. Some of these ethical concerns were identified in this review: i.e. anonymity, ownership, representation and emotions."

Sara Coemans 2017 (2)¶

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports a lack of time as a limitation of many studies, that more training was needed to prepare facilitators or participants, and practical costs including equipment such as digital cameras, projectors, color printers and laptops, plus costs of expositions and artist collaboration.

"Using ABR methods may demand long term processes and an increased amount of time, training and resources."
