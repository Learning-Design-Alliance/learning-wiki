Evidence¶

Sapey-Triomphe 2023¶

Sapey-Triomphe, L.-A., Sanchez, G., Hénaff, M.-A., Sonié, S., Schmitz, C., & Mattout, J. (2023). Disentangling sensory precision and prior expectation of change in autism during tactile discrimination. npj Science of Learning, 8:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00207-5

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Trial-to-trial modeling of Exp. II responses estimated the precision ratio per block; autistic participants "had a lower precision ratio than NT" (−0.14 vs 0.63, d = 0.42), with the largest group difference in block 4 (d = 0.86), consistent with a stronger prior of sameness.

"On average, in Exp. II, autistic participants had a lower precision ratio than NT ( −0.14 (± 1.8) in ASD vs. 0.63 (±1.8) in NT, t(97) = 2.1, p = 0.040, d = 0.42, BF = 1.42, Fig. 6f)"

Sapey-Triomphe 2023 (2)¶

Sapey-Triomphe, L.-A., Sanchez, G., Hénaff, M.-A., Sonié, S., Schmitz, C., & Mattout, J. (2023). Disentangling sensory precision and prior expectation of change in autism during tactile discrimination. npj Science of Learning, 8:54. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00207-5

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Model-estimated response bias parameter b in Exp. II context-setting blocks differed between groups (NT: −0.1 ± 0.2; ASD: 0.1 ± 0.2), d = 0.96; in Exp. III the difference was again significant (d = 1.15), with opposite answer tendencies across groups.

"There was a group difference in response bias b (NT: −0.1 ± 0.2; ASD: 0.1 ± 0.2; t(31) = 2.8, p = 0.010, d = 0.96, BF = 5.27)"
