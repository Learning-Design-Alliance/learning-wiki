Evidence¶

Patterson 1909¶

Patterson, T. L. (1909). Pedagogical Suggestions from Memory Tests. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com

q2 · i? · associational · r1

About 700 story-recall tests were made in Worcester grammar schools, both high schools, one Clark University department, and Miss Aiken's School; the 324-word story was scored in 152 terms. The article reports descriptive percentages only. It states the test was only partially successful in determining appeals to senses.

"As a whole, so far as the grades ran parallel, the girls remembered 4 per cent, more of the story than the boys, and no decline of memory power was discovered in the girls. 37 per cent, of the whole story was the average remembered by all grades."

Patterson 1909 (2)¶

Patterson, T. L. (1909). Pedagogical Suggestions from Memory Tests. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Serial-position analysis of the story-recall data: the first third was remembered better than the second by all grades with one exception, and the second better than the last without exception. The article notes the seventh-grade reading condition may reflect rereading the first part.

"The per cent, remembered by all in the three-part division was successively 46, 38 and 27. Practically the same ratio was sustained by all grades."
