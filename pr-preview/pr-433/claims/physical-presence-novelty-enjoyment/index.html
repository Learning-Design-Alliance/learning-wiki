Evidence¶

Ackermann 2025¶

Ackermann, H., Henke, A., Chevalère, J., Yun, H. S., Hafner, V. V., Pinkwart, N., & Lazarides, R. (2025). Physical embodiment and anthropomorphism of AI tutors and their role in student enjoyment and performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00293-z

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Path models on data from 56 students comparing condition A (virtual only) and condition B (physical robot added), with on-task enjoyment self-reported every 10 minutes via experience sampling and split into novelty and working phases. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

"in two out of four path models the coefﬁcient between the condition and on-task enjoyment in the novelty phase was signiﬁcant, while the other two models showed not signiﬁcant associations with p = 0.061 and p = 0.054 (H1a)."

Ackermann 2025 (2)¶

Ackermann, H., Henke, A., Chevalère, J., Yun, H. S., Hafner, V. V., Pinkwart, N., & Lazarides, R. (2025). Physical embodiment and anthropomorphism of AI tutors and their role in student enjoyment and performance. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00293-z

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Same path models as the novelty-phase analysis; the working-phase (second 30 minutes) enjoyment showed no significant condition difference in any of the four models. The authors interpret the fading enjoyment benefit as a possible novelty effect.

"However, learning with or without a physically present robot along with a virtual agent (condition A vs. condition B) and on-task enjoyment during the working phase were not signiﬁcantly related across all models (H1a)."
