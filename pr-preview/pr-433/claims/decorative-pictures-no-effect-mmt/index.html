Evidence¶

Schewior 2024¶

Schewior, L., & Lindner, M. A. (2024). Revisiting Picture Functions in Multimedia Testing: A Systematic Narrative Review and Taxonomy Extension. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09883-0

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of twelve studies comparing text-picture conditions with decorative pictures against text-only controls. None found a significant performance effect, supporting the conclusion that "DPs seemed to neither increase nor decrease performance."

"None of these twelve studies found a significant effect of DPs on performance compared to text-only"

Schewior 2024 (2)¶

Schewior, L., & Lindner, M. A. (2024). Revisiting Picture Functions in Multimedia Testing: A Systematic Narrative Review and Taxonomy Extension. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09883-0

q2 · i? · review · r3

Eye-tracking evidence from multiple studies indicates decorative pictures receive minimal visual attention from test-takers, supporting the interpretation that they demand little cognitive engagement rather than acting as seductive details.

"Findings from this and other eye-tracking studies showed, however, that DPs are rarely (Dewolf et al., 2015) and shortly (Strobel et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022) fixated by test tak ers"
