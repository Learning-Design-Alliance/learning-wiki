McKim 2016¶

McKim, A. J., & Velez, J. J. (2016). An Evaluation of the Self-Efficacy Theory in Agricultural Education. Journal of Agricultural Education, 57(1), 73-90. https://doi.org/10.5032/jae.2016.01073

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Swan (2005) found "17% of the variance in career intent could be attributed to the perceived efficacy of student teachers," and cites Wheeler and Knobloch (2006) on a positive self-efficacy–career commitment relationship and Blackburn and Robinson (2008) on a positive relationship with job satisfaction. Related Claims¶

  • Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Student teachers' teaching efficacy follows a dip trajectory: it rises during on-campus preparation, falls to its lowest point at the midpoint of student teaching, and rebounds by the end — related
  • Posttest self-efficacy accounts for a significant increment in posttest skill variability, but its relation to persistence is mixed in learning settings — related
  • Servant leadership shows the strongest effect on teacher self-efficacy, which in turn predicts life satisfaction — related
  • Vicarious experiences are positively related to agriculture teachers' general teaching efficacy, with observing a first year teacher explaining 11% of variance — related
  • The effect of social persuasion on student teacher efficacy depends on its form: verbal feedback from cooperating teachers is positive while structured communication tools and peer pairing show no benefit — related
  • Mastery experiences may not be the optimal method for initially increasing preservice agriculture teachers' self-efficacy — related
  • Teacher self-efficacy shows positive links with personal accomplishment, job satisfaction, and commitment — related
