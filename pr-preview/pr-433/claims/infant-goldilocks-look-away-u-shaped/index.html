Evidence¶

Kidd 2015¶

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The review reports the authors' infant studies (Kidd, Piantadosi, & Aslin, 2012, 2014), in which 7- and 8-month-olds viewed event sequences of varying complexity measured by an idealized learning model, with look-away marking attentional drift. The U-shaped pattern held across visual displays, auditory stimuli, and within individual infants.

"We found that infants’ probability of looking away was greatest to events of either very low information content (highly predictable) or very high information content (highly surprising)."
