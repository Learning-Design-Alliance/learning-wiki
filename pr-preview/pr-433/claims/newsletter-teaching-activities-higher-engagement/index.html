Pooja K. Agarwal 2024¶

Pooja K. Agarwal. (2024). Personal reflections on science communication and sharing retrieval practice research with teachers. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09839-w

q1 · i? · qualitative · r1

Observational single-case data from the author's own Mailchimp newsletter: an issue featuring the brain-dump teaching activity outperformed prior research-summary issues on open rate. No statistic or effect size is printed beyond the word significant.

"the first newsletter in which I shared a specific teaching activity (free recall reframed as a "brain dump"; Agarwal, 2017) resulted in a significantly higher email "open rate" than previous content"

Pooja K. Agarwal 2024 (2)¶

Pooja K. Agarwal. (2024). Personal reflections on science communication and sharing retrieval practice research with teachers. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09839-w

q1 · i? · design · r2

The author's observational comparison of content types across newsletter issues found engagement metrics highest for issues with practical teaching activities and downloadable materials. Descriptive, with no printed effect size.

"I found that the newsletters with the most engagement (i.e., open rates, click rates, number of website visitors, and sharing on social media) were the ones that included practical teaching activities and downloadable materials"
