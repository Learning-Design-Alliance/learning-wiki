Pearson correlation analyses in the 301-participant study. The article also reports the eyes-focused pattern correlated with better d′ (r(297) = .146, p = .012) and lower false alarm rate (r(297) = -.147, p = .011), but not recognition RT or hit rate.

"higher eye movement consistency, as reflected in lower C3 entropy, was correlated with better recognition d′ (r(297) = -.199, p < .001, padjusted = .004), lower false alarm rate (r(297) = .211, p < .001, padjusted = .002), shorter RT (r(297) = .175, p = .002, padjusted = .019)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In adults, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement pattern, with eye movement consistency adding no additional variance — related
  • Older age is associated with poorer face recognition: lower d′, higher false alarm rate, and longer response time — related
  • Lower eye movement consistency (higher C3 entropy) is associated with poorer selective attention and inhibitory control — related
  • C3 entropy partially mediates the relationship between age and face recognition performance — related
  • In typically developing children aged 6–11, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement consistency but not by eye movement pattern — reports the opposite
  • A less eyes-focused pattern and lower eye movement consistency jointly predict poorer recognition beyond age and cognitive abilities — related
  • In partially trained DNN+HMM models simulating children, eye movement consistency (lower entropy) predicts recognition accuracy but eye movement pattern does not — related
  • In well-trained DNN+HMM face-recognition models, an eyes-focused eye movement pattern yields higher recognition accuracy than a nose-focused pattern — related
  • Higher eye movement consistency is associated with higher ERP decoding accuracy and faster face recognition responses — related
  • Higher eye movement consistency is associated with shorter peak latency of high-alpha decoding accuracy, reflecting more efficient neural representation development — related
