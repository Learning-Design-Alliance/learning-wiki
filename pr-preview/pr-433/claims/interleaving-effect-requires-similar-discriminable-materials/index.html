Evidence¶

Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller 2021¶

Ouhao Chen & Fred Paas & John Sweller. (2021). Spacing and Interleaving Effects Require Distinct Theoretical Bases: a Systematic Review Testing the Cognitive Load and Discriminative-Contrast Hypotheses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09613-w

q2 · i? · review · r2

Systematic review analysis of 67 interleaving studies coded by material similarity. The review reports that "All 67 studies demonstrated the interleaving effect" using shuffling or lagging designs with no rest-from-deliberate-learning. Reported Cohen's d values in Table 2 included values such as 1.21 and .56.

"All 67 studies demonstrated the interleaving effect and used very similar interleaved concepts during learning. They shuffled targeting concepts or inserted lags (i.e., intervening concepts) between two targeted concepts without no mental rest-from-deliberate-learning."

Related Claims¶

  • Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning for visual and mathematical materials, but not for expository texts or word categories — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The discriminative-contrast hypothesis treats spacing and interleaving as distinct effects, with interleaving benefits more likely for similar than dissimilar interleaved tasks — related
  • Interleaving—presenting different concepts in alternation rather than repeating the same concept—improves students' discrimination among similar concepts, terms, and principles — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
