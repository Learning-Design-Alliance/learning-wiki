Evidence¶

Araiza-Alba 2020¶

Araiza-Alba, P., Keane, T., Matthews, B., Simpson, K., Strugnell, G., Chen, W. S., & Kaufman, J. (2020). The potential of 360-degree Virtual Reality videos to teach water-safety skills to children. https://www.lsv.com.au/vr

q3 · i? · causal · r2

In the randomized three-condition study, the 360° VR group lacked instructor interaction by design yet performed comparably on the Water-Safety Knowledge Questionnaire, supporting 360° VR videos as a tool for self-directed learning.

"The results of this study indicated that children in the 360° VR video group (self -directed learning) learned as much as the children in the other two groups who were taught by a lifeguard."

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers perceive 360° VR videos as engaging, feasible, and useful for explaining concepts difficult to demonstrate traditionally — related
  • Children learning water-safety content via 360° VR videos, traditional video, or posters show similar learning gains, with 95% improving knowledge and retaining it at one and eight weeks — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Participants in the 360° VR video condition report higher interest and enjoyment than those learning via traditional video or posters — related
  • Immersive lessons show a small effect size advantage over less immersive technology, but increased presence does not necessarily lead to more learning — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Prior single-exposure IVR studies show mixed cognitive outcomes: affective gains without cognitive benefits (Parong & Mayer) and short-lived retention gains (Christopoulos) — related
