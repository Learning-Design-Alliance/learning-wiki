Evidence¶

Liang 2025¶

Liang, C., Toyokawa, Y., & Ogata, H. (2025). Optimizing group formation with a mixed genetic algorithm: an empirical study in active reading using marker data. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-025-09452-9

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Within-cohort comparison of vocabulary learning gain (15-item pre/post quizzes) between random and algorithm-formed grouping conditions in a Japanese senior high school EFL class. The Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test reached significance (W = 90.5, p < .01), with effect size r = 0.504; experimental mean LG was 0.51 versus control 0.33.

Related Claims¶

  • The mixed algorithm produced groups differing significantly in marker-feature distributions, with shared (overlapping) yellow markers significantly lower than unique yellow markers
  • Process mining of two fitness-typical groups suggests the group with more heterogeneous yellow markers and shared red markers worked more efficiently while both achieved the same summary outcome
  • A text-mining group and representation tool scaffolds students' learning processes and improves outcomes under specific conditions — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Most students reported using the Wordcloud for group discussion, and Wordcloud use showed a marginally non-significant advantage on content comprehension but no difference on vocabulary gain — reports the opposite
  • Group summary scores did not differ significantly between algorithm-formed and randomly formed groups on content, vocabulary, or grammar rubrics — related
