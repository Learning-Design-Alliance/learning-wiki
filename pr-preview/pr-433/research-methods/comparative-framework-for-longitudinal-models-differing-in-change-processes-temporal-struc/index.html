Comparative framework for longitudinal models differing in change processes, temporal structure, and measurement design¶

Research Method · All research methods

Description¶

Beyond responding to the commentary, the authors "present a generalizable framework for comparing longitudinal models that differ in their assumptions about change processes, temporal structure, and measurement design." The framework organizes model comparison around three axes: what change process a model assumes, how it represents temporal structure (e.g., lag0 and lag1 effects), and what measurement design it presumes. It is used in the article to reanalyze the simulated and PALMA data and evaluate the extended REM against alternatives.

Accounts¶

  • Comparative framework for longitudinal models differing in change processes, temporal structure, and measurement design: Beyond responding to the commentary, the authors "present a generalizable framework for comparing longitudinal models that differ in their assumptions about change processes, temporal structure, and measurement design." The framework organizes model comparison around three axes: what change process a model assumes, how it represents temporal structure (e.g., lag0 and lag1 effects), and what measurement design it presumes. It is used in the article to reanalyze the simulated and PALMA data and evaluate the extended REM against alternatives. (Fernando Núñez-Regueiro et al. (2025))
  • Juxtapose results from competing panel models rather than selecting a single best model by goodness-of-fit: The article recommends that longitudinal model evaluation rest on substantive interpretation alongside fit indices, following Muthén and Asparouhov's advice. Because alternative models may be formally equivalent or hard to distinguish by fit, "conclusions should be based on the juxtaposition of different results rather than selecting a single "best" model based on goodness-of-fit" when competing models each support a priori theory-based hypotheses. Researchers should report results from competing models and consider more parsimonious constrained versions when convergence problems arise. (Marsh et al. (2024))
  • Test alternative longitudinal models and juxtapose their results and interpretations before drawing causal conclusions: The article endorses Marsh et al.'s (2024) recommendation that researchers "test alternative models and juxtapose their results and interpretations". When findings from alternative models converge, causal conclusions are corroborated; when they diverge, "caution is advised and causal conclusions appear premature". The authors demonstrate this by fitting RI-CLPM, LCSM, ESDM and STARTS models to the same simulated data and finding contradictory positive, negative, and null effects. (Kimmo Sorjonen et al. (2025))
