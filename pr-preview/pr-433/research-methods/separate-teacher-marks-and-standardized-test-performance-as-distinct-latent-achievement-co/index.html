Separate teacher marks and standardized test performance as distinct latent achievement constructs in longitudinal panel models¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that future investigations on causal ordering include both marks and test performance as distinct latent variables rather than a single achievement indicator, because single-indicator models yield artificially reciprocal patterns. The authors state this complex model was "recommended for future investigations on this issue," and that single-indicator use "may lead to artificially high regression coefficients." This applies to longitudinal panel research on motivation–achievement relations.

Claims¶

  • Models using a single achievement indicator (marks or tests alone) yield a reciprocal self-concept–achievement pattern, unlike integrated models [+M]
  • When marks and tests are modeled as separate latent variables with cross-lagged effects between them, effects of self-concept on achievement completely disappear [+M]
  • In elementary school, achievement predicts later self-concept of ability but prior self-concept does not significantly predict later achievement when achievement is modeled with both marks and tests integrated [+W]
