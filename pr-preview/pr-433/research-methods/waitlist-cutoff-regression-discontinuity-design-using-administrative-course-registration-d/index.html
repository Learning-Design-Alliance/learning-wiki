Waitlist-cutoff regression discontinuity design using administrative course registration data¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study introduces a novel research design that exploits discontinuities in course admissions created by waitlists, applied to administrative course registration data at a community college. It is described as providing "the first causal evidence on this mechanism at a community college" regarding course availability as a channel through which funding levels impact students. The design compares students just below versus just above waitlist cutoffs across a range of bandwidths.

Claims¶

  • Course availability can disrupt community college students' educational trajectories [+W]
  • Course shutouts increased transfer rates to nearby, potentially less-desirable two-year colleges [+W]
  • Community college students shut out of a course section via waitlist cutoffs were 22–28 percent more likely to take zero courses that term [+W]
