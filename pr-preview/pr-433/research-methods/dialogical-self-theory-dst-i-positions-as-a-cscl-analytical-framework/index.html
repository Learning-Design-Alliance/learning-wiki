Dialogical self theory (DST) I-positions as a CSCL analytical framework¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

DST holds that, as the article states, "the self is populated by other inner selves that collectively derive from the world and have their own voice, motives, opinions, and knowledge that become present in what are known as I-positions via dialogue in response to the social situation". The article operationalizes four positions — I–it (objectified technological other), I–me (personal knowledge), I–you (encountered other), I–we (collective identity) — grounded at utterance level to visualize knowledge and identity construction in CSCL.

Claims¶

  • Group A's two most common I-positions were I–it and I–me, while group B's were I–me and I–we, indicating different reliance on interpersonal versus intrapersonal voices [+M]
  • Group B showed a shift toward intrapersonal voices (I–you/I–we) during its later empirical evaluation, while group A remained interpersonal (I–it/I–me) even when investigating, with un-negotiated inputs [+M]
  • In group B, usage of I–we gradually increased and peaked in the final stage during critical evaluation, suggesting intrapersonal collective voice drives critical evaluation of evidence [+M]
