Multivariate Bayesian Causal Forests¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This study adopts a multivariate extension of Bayesian Causal Forests (BCF), a non-parametric causal inference machine learning model built on Bayesian Additive Regression Trees. The article states it "adopts a multivariate extension of BCF developed by McJames et al. (2023), which allows BCF to be applied to multiple outcome variables such as mathematics and science achievement simultaneously". The model estimates baseline achievement (mu) and treatment effects (tau) for four treatment groups covering higher frequencies and longer durations, with a classroom random intercept, capturing interactions and non-linear relationships without assuming linearity.

Claims¶

  • Daily mathematics homework raises eighth-grade mathematics achievement by about 7.36 TIMSS units relative to homework up to one or two times per week [+M]
  • The optimal homework frequency in science is three or four times per week, with daily science homework showing no significant benefit over the baseline [+M]
  • Increasing homework duration beyond fifteen minutes shows no clear benefit for mathematics or science achievement [+M]
  • Students from advantaged socioeconomic backgrounds do not benefit significantly more from homework than their peers in eighth grade [+M]
