Taxonomy of reciprocal effect panel models: CLPM, RI-CLPM, PCPM, and CCLPM with lag0, lag1, and lag2 effects¶

Description¶

The article's methodological contribution is a taxonomy of structural equation models for directional ordering, extending Muthén and Asparouhov's contemporaneous-effects models with latent measurement factors, lag2 paths, random intercepts, and covariate strategies. "We focus on four basic models (Fig. 2): CLPMs (lag1 effects; no RI; no lag0 effects), RI-CLPMs (with lag1 and RI effects, but no lag0 effects), pure contemporaneous panel models with only contemporaneous effects (PCPM; RI and lag0 effects, but no lag1 cross-paths), and reciprocal models (RI-CCLPM; RI, lag0 and lag1 effects)." Lag0 paths are reciprocal paths within each wave; lag2 paths link non-adjacent waves. The models were fit to five annual waves of PALMA data.

  • Contemporaneous-Lagged Reciprocal Effects Model (CL-REM): a timescales continuum refinement of the Reciprocal Effects Model: The CL-REM is the article's proposed refinement of the Reciprocal Effects Model, drawing on the timescales continuum framework to formalize how self-concept and skills influence each other at different speeds. It distinguishes three levels: a "Contemporaneous skill development effect (microlevel timescale)", a "Lagged self-enhancement effect (mesolevel timescale)", and covariance of stable traits at the macrolevel estimated through random intercepts. The model predicts that skills modify self-concept quickly (within a semester) while self-concept shapes skills gradually (across semesters), and that detectability depends on aligning measurement intervals with these timescales. (I Feel Competent, Therefore I Am: Self-concept and Skill Interact at Different Speeds)

Claims¶

  • In German secondary-school mathematics, effects of self-concept on achievement are lagged (lag1) whereas effects of achievement on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0) [+M]
  • Academic skills predict contemporaneous (within-semester) changes in academic self-concept in both mathematics and French [+M]
  • Academic self-concept predicts lagged (across-semester) changes in academic skills in both mathematics and French [+M]
  • The temporal asymmetry in effect sizes: contemporaneous skill effects on self-concept are moderate (d = 0.33 in French) while alternative cross-effects are null or negligible [+M]
