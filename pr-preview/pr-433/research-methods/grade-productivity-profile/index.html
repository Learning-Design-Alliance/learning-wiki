Grade productivity profile¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The guide introduces a value-added framework in which each school grade's productivity profile presents "three inter-related pieces of information for each school grade." Input status is the previous year's matched test scores, output is end-of-year scores for the same stable students, and the gain is the improvement between them. Profiles are built year by year from 1987 to 1996, smoothed with best-fitting trend lines, and used to read school improvement over time.

Accounts¶

  • Grade productivity profile framework: input, output, and learning-gain trends for each school grade: The guide introduces a value-added framework in which each school grade's productivity profile presents "three inter-related pieces of information for each school grade." Input status is the previous year's matched test scores, output is end-of-year scores for the same stable students, and the gain is the improvement between them. Profiles are built year by year from 1987 to 1996, smoothed with best-fitting trend lines, and used to read school improvement over time. (Anthony S. Bryk et al. (1998))
  • Trapezoidal pedagogical production function model of learning: A calibrated model (from Kaffenberger and Pritchett, 2020) in which learning in a grade is a trapezoidal function of a student's initial skill level, defined by width, height, slope and centre parameters. The article explains that "the PPF assumes there is a range of initial skill levels within which children learn and above and below which they do not", and a pace parameter shifts the PPF from one grade to the next. Calibrated to PISA-D countries, it is used here to simulate Covid-19 shock and mitigation scenarios through grade 10. (Kaffenberger (2021))

Claims¶

  • Productivity profiles in adjacent grades are negatively related, so judging a school from selected grades alone can be very misleading [+W]
  • Judging school improvement requires reading learning gain trends and output trends simultaneously, since either trend monitored alone can mislead [+W]
  • Profile trends labeled up or down are determined by statistical criteria, so those labels denote statistically significant trends [+W]
  • CPS elementary school productivity improvements stalled after 1997, with no evidence of significant productivity growth since then [+W]
  • Learning losses accumulate because children fall behind the curriculum and outside the range of the pedagogical production function, making no further gains: 72 percent of in-school children after the unmitigated shock [+W]
  • In the calibrated simulation, a shock reducing grade 3 learning by one-third leaves the cohort a full year of learning behind the no-shock counterfactual by grade 10 [+W]
