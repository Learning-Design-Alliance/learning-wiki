The Statistical–Mechanism Gap Fallacy: statistical contextual indicators are not psychologically transparent mechanisms¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article names and defines the Statistical–Mechanism Gap Fallacy as "the tendency to collapse the gap between statistical indicators and the psychological, social, or cognitive mechanisms they are presumed to represent". Contextual means, ordinal ranks, and dispersion statistics are treated as "partially overlapping statistical representations of socially experienced comparison environments rather than as isolated psychological mechanisms". The framing motivates modeling mean, rank, and dispersion jointly rather than assuming any single statistic uniquely represents the comparison process.
