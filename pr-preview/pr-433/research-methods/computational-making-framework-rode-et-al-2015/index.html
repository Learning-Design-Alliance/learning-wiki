Computational making framework (Rode et al., 2015)¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The framework describes the child as "the artifact creator who makes autonomous judgments and applies nonlinear processes when constructing artifacts" and organizes computational making into five dimensions: aesthetics, creativity, constructing, visualizing multiple representations, and understanding materials. This study adopted it as the analytical lens for coding facilitators' fieldnotes, triangulated with artifacts and video, and reported findings under each of the five dimensions.

Claims¶

  • Constructing and materiality posed the main computational making challenges: frayed conductive thread caused short circuits, and two pairs' projects failed without substantial assistance [+M]
  • Sharing multiple sample e-textile projects and sample code appeared to limit children's creativity, with final artifacts imitating the samples [+M]
