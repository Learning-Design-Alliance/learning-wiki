Interpretable mathematical model (IMM) algorithm for classifying gaze strategies from saccade properties¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The IMM is an algorithm that transforms sequences of saccades on the graph area into classifications of horizontal or vertical scanning. It maps saccades to values based on length (200-pixel cutoff) and slope (0.875 cutoff), removes short saccades, counts runs of consecutive long horizontal or vertical scans, and outputs a binary classification. The authors state "The best model we found relies on the following algorithm, which is based on saccade lengths and angles." Cut-off values were found empirically by testing values close to a slope of 1.

Cut-off values were found empirically by testing values close to a slope of 1. (Boels et al. (2024))
  • Prefer interpretable models over black-box machine learning when strategy identification must be transparent to educational researchers: The article argues that when automated classification must be understood by educational researchers, an interpretable model built directly from the data is preferable to a black-box MLA. The authors note that "the disadvantage of many MLAs is that they are effectively like black boxes that do not reveal how they identify the results from the data," whereas an interpretable model "captures the most important characteristics of the strategy, in a way that can be understood by human beings and that is transparent." They used the MLA only as a baseline for the IMM. (Boels et al. (2024))
