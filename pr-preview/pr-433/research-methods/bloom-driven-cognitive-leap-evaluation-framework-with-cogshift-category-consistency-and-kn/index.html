Bloom-driven cognitive-leap evaluation framework with CogShift, category consistency, and knowledge coverage metrics¶

Description¶

A Bloom's-Taxonomy-driven framework for evaluating LLM-generated educational questions that pivots "from a knowledge-point perspective to a cognitive-leap paradigm". It combines usability filtering (unique, readable, answerable via majority voting across GPT evaluators), category consistency (CatCons), knowledge coverage (KnowCov), and a Cognitive Shift Score (CogShift) that weights mismatches by the ordinal distance between original and generated Bloom levels, classifying shifts as leap, regression, or drift. The framework was applied to 20,700 questions across six LLMs and three domains.

Claims¶

  • Cognitive leap is the most prevalent deviation pattern of LLM-generated questions, and fine-grained prompting amplifies upward shifts while suppressing cognitive regression [+M]
  • Fine-grained prompting improves Bloom-level category consistency and knowledge coverage of generated questions in the computer science domain [+M]
  • The Bloom-driven framework generalizes to social-science questions, with automated rubrics agreeing strongly with experts on usability but only modestly on Bloom level [+M]
  • LLMs exhibit an inherent abstraction bias, consistently generating questions at higher-than-intended cognitive levels regardless of prompting strategy [~W]
  • Higher knowledge-identification accuracy is associated with improved knowledge coverage in LLM-generated questions under Chain-of-Thought prompting [+W]
