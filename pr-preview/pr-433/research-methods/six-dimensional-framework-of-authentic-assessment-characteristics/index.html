Six-dimensional framework of authentic assessment characteristics¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review proposes a six-dimensional framework for authentic assessment (AA) characteristics consisting of authenticity in assessment, cognitive challenges, assessment criteria, feedback, student agency and social collaboration. It extends prior frameworks (Gulikers et al. 2004; Ashford-Rowe et al. 2014; Villarroel et al. 2018) by distinguishing multiple meanings of authenticity — assessment, professional, digital, self and social authenticity — and by adding student agency and social collaboration as design elements. The review states it 'underscores the design of student agency, which enables students to integrate their knowledge, actions and identity across various practices'. The framework was used to code the 37 included studies.

Claims¶

  • Authentic assessment designs in higher education predominantly replicate professional workplace scenarios, with few addressing social authenticity [+W]
  • Social collaboration in authentic assessment is mostly peer- and teacher-based, with few connections to industry or community partners and rare equitable teacher-student collaboration [+W]
  • Student choice is rare in authentic assessment designs while assigned, sometimes graded, self-reflection is more common [+W]
  • Formative feedback dominates authentic assessment designs while sustainable feedback is rare [+W]
  • Students are typically passive users of assessment rubrics in authentic assessment designs [+W]
