Probabilistic peer grading models (PG1, PG2, PG3)¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article introduces a family of Bayesian models in which each submission has a latent true score, each grader a bias (tendency to "inﬂate or deﬂate her assessment") and a reliability (precision of assessments after bias correction). PG1 places priors over these latents; PG2 adds temporal coherence of bias across assignments (consecutive-assignment bias correlation of 0.33); PG3 couples a grader's reliability to her own grade. Inference uses Gibbs sampling or EM, producing posterior belief distributions over grades rather than single scores.

Claims¶

  • Probabilistic peer grading models that estimate grader bias and reliability reduce RMSE against ground-truth grades by 33% and 31% on two HCI course offerings [+M]
  • Estimating each grader's bias alone accounts for 95% of the RMSE reduction, while modeling grader reliability contributes little with only four grades per student [+M]
  • The model's posterior confidence estimates are conservative: over 95% of predictions the model claims 90–95% confidence in are correct [+M]
  • Higher-scoring students tend to be more reliable graders (except the very best), and students are least biased when grading peers with similar scores [+W]
