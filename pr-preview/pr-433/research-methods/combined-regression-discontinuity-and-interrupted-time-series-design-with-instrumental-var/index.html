Combined regression discontinuity and interrupted time-series design with instrumental variables¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

When a threshold-based policy changes conditions for non-targeted students too, a standard regression discontinuity cannot estimate the policy effect, only the enrollment effect. The authors show that "combining the two designs—a regression discontinuity and a time series design we address limitations that are usually inherent in each": the discontinuity provides a within-cohort check while cohort comparisons reveal effects on non-targeted students and on students far from the cutoff, with instrumental variables addressing imperfect compliance.

Accounts¶

  • Combine regression discontinuity with interrupted time series (and instrumental variables) to estimate policy effects when the policy affects both targeted and non-targeted students: When a threshold-based policy changes conditions for non-targeted students too, a standard regression discontinuity cannot estimate the policy effect, only the enrollment effect. (Takako Nomi et al. (2008))

Claims¶

  • Double-dose algebra improved algebra test scores for targeted students just below the cutoff, with a larger post-policy gain than for students just above the cutoff [+M]
  • The double-dose algebra policy adversely affected non-targeted above-norm students: their algebra grades declined and failure rates increased post-policy [+M]
