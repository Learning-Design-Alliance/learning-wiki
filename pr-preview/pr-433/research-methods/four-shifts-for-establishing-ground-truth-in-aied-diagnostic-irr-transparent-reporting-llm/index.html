Four shifts for establishing ground truth in AIED: diagnostic IRR, transparent reporting, LLM risk mitigation, and validity-first evidence¶

Description¶

The article's central framework proposes four practical shifts for establishing ground truth in AIED labeling: (1) treat IRR as "a diagnostic signal to localize disagreement and refine constructs rather than a mechanical acceptance threshold"; (2) require transparent reporting of rater expertise, codebook development, reconciliation procedures, and segmentation rules; (3) mitigate risks in LLM annotation through bias audits and verification workflows; and (4) complement agreement statistics with validity and effectiveness evidence, including uncertainty-aware labeling, criterion-related checks, and close-the-loop evaluations. The framework is grounded in the distinction between noise and bias and illustrated through multimodal tutoring case studies.

Claims¶

  • In classroom video labeling, high raw agreement (often exceeding 90%) co-occurred with low chance-corrected κ due to temporal misalignment [+M]
  • Providing LLM pre-labels can decrease human annotation accuracy when the model is incorrect, due to automation bias [+M]
  • Multimodal LLMs grading handwritten student work achieved κ = 0.90 on arithmetic but κ ≈ 0.47 on interpreting student illustrations [+M]
  • Gemini 2.0 Flash validated as a reliable LLM annotator of feedback focus types [+W]
  • AI-teacher diagnostic agreement was higher in the Diagnostic Review class and varied by issue type, with local language issues best diagnosed [+M]
  • The human-refined framework improved human agreement substantially but produced only modest gains for ICL-based LLM annotation [+W]
  • Favorable assessments of LLM review workflows frequently coexist with reported unmet reliability or performance requirements [+W]
  • Kimi shows reasonable but weaker agreement with human examiners than ChatGPT (weighted kappa 0.571) [~W]
