Bidirectional validity approach¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The bidirectional validity approach examines a test's fairness by working backward from the criterion: examinees are matched on the outcome the test is supposed to predict (here, type of math course taken and grade received in first-year college math), and their test scores are compared. Wainer and Steinberg apply it to the SAT-M with "almost 47,000 men and women" and complement it with a prospective regression analysis of the same data. Differences in test scores among equally performing examinees indicate differences in the test's predictive validity across groups.

  • Internal validity as precondition for game-based team assessment: The article frames validation research as preceding intervention research: before asking whether games train teams effectively, one must establish whether in-game measures relate to in-game team quality. "Internal validity implies that teams with better in-game performance also have better team quality, and vice versa. In-game team performance scores by themselves have very little external value." In-game performance serves as a proxy for team quality demonstrated during play, and unobtrusively logged game analytics (stealth assessment) are the key advantage of digital serious games for training and assessment. (Mayer (2018))
