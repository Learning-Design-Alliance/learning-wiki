Durkin's eight-category scheme for classifying teachers' comprehension-related behavior¶

Description¶

The article develops a coding scheme distinguishing comprehension instruction—"Teacher does/says something to help children understand or work out the meaning of more than a single, isolated word"—from seven related categories: application, assignment, help with assignment, review of instruction, preparation for reading, assessment, and prediction. The definition was built after a literature search found no usable definition, and it deliberately excludes single-word work such as phonics. It was applied identically to reading and social studies periods so time use could be compared.

Accounts¶

  • Durkin's eight-category scheme for classifying teachers' comprehension-related behavior: The article develops a coding scheme distinguishing comprehension instruction—"Teacher does/says something to help children understand or work out the meaning of more than a single, isolated word"—from seven related categories: application, assignment, help with assignment, review of instruction, preparation for reading, assessment, and prediction. The definition was built after a literature search found no usable definition, and it deliberately excludes single-word work such as phonics. It was applied identically to reading and social studies periods so time use could be compared. (Dolores Durkin (1978))
  • Improving comprehension instruction requires first observing and timing what classrooms currently do: The article argues that reform must begin with descriptive data: "it is impossible to improve instruction until what goes on now, and with what frequency, is known." The study therefore observed middle- and upper-grade classrooms specifically to find, describe, and time comprehension instruction, and published its category definitions and directions so other researchers could replicate the observations. (Dolores Durkin (1978))
