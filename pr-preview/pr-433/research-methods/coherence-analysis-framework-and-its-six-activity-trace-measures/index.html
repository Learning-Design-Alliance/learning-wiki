Coherence analysis framework and its six activity-trace measures¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Coherence analysis (CA) is the paper's approach to analyzing learners' problem-solving processes in OELEs by "combining information gleaned from sequences of student actions to produce measures of action coherence." CA assumes that when students encounter information that could improve their solution, they should act on it; edits not justified by recent views are labeled unsupported edits. Six measures are derived from activity traces: edits/annotations per minute, unsupported edit percentage, information viewing percentage, potential generation percentage, used potential percentage, and disengaged percentage.

  • Coherence analysis framework and its six activity-trace measures: Coherence analysis (CA) is the paper's approach to analyzing learners' problem-solving processes in OELEs by "combining information gleaned from sequences of student actions to produce measures of action coherence." CA assumes that when students encounter information that could improve their solution, they should act on it; edits not justified by recent views are labeled unsupported edits. Six measures are derived from activity traces: edits/annotations per minute, unsupported edit percentage, information viewing percentage, potential generation percentage, used potential percentage, and disengaged percentage. (Gautam Biswas et al. (2016))
  • Coherence analysis (CA) as a theory-driven measure of metacognitive strategy use from trace data: Coherence analysis is a method that evaluates metacognitive strategies by examining the sequential alignment of students' learning actions within online learning contexts. The article states CA "examines how learning activities are sequentially connected in a way that indicates metacognitive strategies such as self-reflection and planning". In this study, the authors use CA to quantify coherent reading (reviewing material after a quiz) and coherent quiz (preparatory engagement before a quiz) as metacognitive strategies, counting time devoted to related content within a five-minute window. (HaeJin Lee · Nigel Bosch (2025))

Claims¶

  • Cluster analysis of coherence metrics on 98 students revealed five distinct behavior profiles, with engaged and efficient students showing the highest reading scores and significantly higher science gains than all other clusters. [+M]
  • Confused guessers had significantly lower pre- and post-test skill scores than most other clusters, but their science learning gains were not significantly different from those of Clusters 1, 2, and 4. [+M]
