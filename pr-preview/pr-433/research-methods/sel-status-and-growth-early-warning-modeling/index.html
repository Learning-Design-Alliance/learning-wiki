SEL status-and-growth early-warning modeling¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 11 claims (11 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This framework treats social-emotional learning constructs (academic growth mindset, self-efficacy, self-management) as developmental processes with their own growth trajectories, asking "whether a student's mean SEL score in 6th grade (status) or growth between 6th through 8th grade is more predictive of being off track academically in 9th grade." The article operationalizes it with latent growth curve models, in which "the random intercept reflects the average 6th grade score and the latent growth factor represents the linear change between 6th and 8th grade." Status and growth factors are then used as predictors of 9th-grade off-track indicators.

Accounts¶

  • SEL status-and-growth framework: middle school SEL constructs as developmental trajectories predicting 9th-grade off-track status: This framework treats social-emotional learning constructs (academic growth mindset, self-efficacy, self-management) as developmental processes with their own growth trajectories, asking "whether a student's mean SEL score in 6th grade (status) or growth between 6th through 8th grade is more predictive of being off track academically in 9th grade." The article operationalizes it with latent growth curve models, in which "the random intercept reflects the average 6th grade score and the latent growth factor represents the linear change between 6th and 8th grade." Status and growth factors are then used as predictors of 9th-grade off-track indicators. (Soland et al. (2019))
  • Incorporate social-emotional development trajectories into identification of students not on track for high school success: The article's stated implication is that early-warning identification of academically off-track students should draw on how students develop socially-emotionally, not only on academic indicators. As the summary puts it, “understanding how a student develops social-emotionally can improve identification of students not on track to succeed in high school.” The principle applies in middle school, where SEL level and growth can be measured before the 9th-grade transition. (James Soland (2019))
  • NWEA working paper on SEL trajectories and 9th-grade off-track identification: A NWEA working paper and companion research brief by James Soland and Megan Kuhfeld examining whether middle school SEL skills predict being off-track to graduate high school. The page lists it under topics including “Social-emotional learning” and “High school,” and summarizes that the study examined SEL stability and the relation of 6th-grade SEL level and growth to successful transition to secondary school. (James Soland (2019))
  • SEL trajectory framework distinguishing status from growth as predictors of 9th-grade off-track outcomes: The article organizes its inquiry around a framework contrasting two ways of summarizing a student's social-emotional learning trajectory: initial level versus change over time. It states the study examines "whether a student’s mean SEL score in 6th grade (status) or growth between 6th and 8th grade is more predictive of being off track academically in 9th grade." Growth models are fit for three SEL constructs shown to relate to successful secondary-school transition. The framework treats SEL development as a longitudinal quantity whose status and growth components carry different predictive information for early high school outcomes. (James Soland (2021))

Claims¶

  • Self-management status and growth across middle school predict lower odds of having a GPA below a C and of being suspended in 9th grade, but not chronic absenteeism [+M]
  • Growth in self-management predicts 9th-grade off-track status above and beyond 6th-grade status on that construct [+M]
  • Self-management and self-efficacy show highly collinear growth during middle school, with slopes correlated .95 and intercepts .74 [+M]
  • Students' 6th-grade SEL level and SEL growth from 6th to 8th grade relate to successful transition to secondary school [+M]
  • Growth in self-management between 6th and 8th grade predicts being off track academically in 9th grade above and beyond 6th-grade self-management status [+M]
  • Understanding how a student develops social-emotionally over intermediate school can improve identification of students not on track to succeed in high school [+M]
  • In a large urban district, 6th-grade SEL status is more frequently a significant predictor of being off track academically in 9th grade than SEL growth between 6th and 8th grade [+M]
  • Whether students' SEL skills are stable over time, and whether initial level or change better flags later academic risk, was largely unknown before this study [+W]
  • Successful transition from intermediate to secondary school depends on both academic preparation and social-emotional learning skills suited to a more independent environment [+W]
  • Successfully transitioning from intermediate school to secondary school is pivotal for students to remain on track to graduate [+W]
  • SEL-related constructs are strongly predictive of long-term academic achievement and attainment [+M]
