Prospective value-conflict analysis for educational evaluation¶

Description¶

The article recommends that evaluators apply the value-conflict taxonomy prospectively rather than only retrospectively. As the abstract puts it: "It is suggested to use the taxonomy before starting an evaluation." Working through the six value areas and the goal/means and qualitative/quantitative distinctions beforehand makes latent dilemmas, such as anonymity vs. administrative demands, visible before commitments are made.

Accounts¶

  • Use the taxonomy of value conflicts before starting an evaluation: The article recommends that evaluators apply the value-conflict taxonomy prospectively rather than only retrospectively. As the abstract puts it: "It is suggested to use the taxonomy before starting an evaluation." Working through the six value areas and the goal/means and qualitative/quantitative distinctions beforehand makes latent dilemmas, such as anonymity vs. administrative demands, visible before commitments are made. (Patry et al. (2004))
  • Consider the implicit norms embedded in empirical evaluations of educational inequality: Evaluations of inequality embed norms about which outcomes matter and for whom. The article proposes "consideration of the implicit norms that forms of evaluation set for the pursuit of equality in education," including "consideration of the potential reinforcement of inequality through the normativity of pursuing certain outcomes." Group-level indicators (e.g., explained variance by background) and individual-level learning-gain indicators embody different norms about what equality requires. (Louise Elffers et al. (2024))
