A data-driven taxonomy organizing the meanings of concrete and abstract along a learner–representation–concept dimension¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes a taxonomy of the semantic landscape of concreteness and abstraction in mathematics education, built from qualitative coding of a corpus of 368 papers. It models learning as follows: "To learn a concept ( C ), a learner ( L ) interacts with a representation ( R) of C , designed through mapping." Axes are placed at the interaction level (learner–representation) or the mapping level (representation–concept), each running between a concrete and an abstract end, and each paired with a design question to make the taxonomy usable in research and practice.

Claims¶

  • Mathematics education literature uses the terms concrete and abstract with multiple distinct meanings [+W]
  • A single representation can be abstract along one dimension and concrete along another, and concrete for one learner but abstract for another [+W]
  • Disagreements about concreteness and abstraction in mathematics education may be verbal disputes rooted in word meaning [+W]
