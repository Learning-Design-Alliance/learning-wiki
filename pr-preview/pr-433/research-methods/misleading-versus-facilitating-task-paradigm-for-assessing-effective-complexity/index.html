Misleading-versus-facilitating task paradigm for assessing effective complexity¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article distinguishes misleading tasks, which activate mutually incompatible schemes competing for application (e.g., dual-task paradigms and backward span tasks), from facilitating tasks solvable with habitual automatised schemes, such as standard forward span tasks. Misleading situations "can assess effective complexity reliably — whereas facilitating situations, common in learning/memory paradigms and psychometric-intelligence tasks, do not". Effective complexity is indexed by the number of effortfully activated task-relevant schemes required, and these situations show developmental performance discontinuities subtending stages.
