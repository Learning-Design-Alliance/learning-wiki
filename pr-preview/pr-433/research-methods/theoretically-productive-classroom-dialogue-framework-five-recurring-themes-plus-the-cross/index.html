Theoretically productive classroom dialogue framework: five recurring themes plus the cross-cutting theme of high student contribution¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article defines theoretically productive dialogue as classroom verbal interaction complying with five recurring themes: open questions, elaboration of previous contributions, reasoned discussion of competing viewpoints, linkage and coordination across contributions, and meta-cognitive engagement with dialogue, plus high student contribution. It states that amongst proposals about optimizing outcomes, "five themes recur", and uses the framework to code routine lessons and test whether the themes relate to student outcomes in teacher-student dialogue, not just small-group work.

(Howe et al. (2018))
  • Productive classroom dialogue framework: Alexander's five characteristics, exploratory talk, and accountable talk: The article organises classroom dialogue research around socio-cultural views of learning as socially mediated, and identifies consensus features of productive dialogue. Alexander's characteristics hold that classroom dialogue should be "collective, with participants reaching a shared understanding", "reciprocal", "supportive", "cumulative" and "purposeful". Littleton and Mercer distinguish disputational, cumulative and exploratory student–student talk, with exploratory talk regarded as most educationally effective, and accountable talk encompasses accountability to the learning community, standards of reasoning, and knowledge. (Long et al. (2024))
