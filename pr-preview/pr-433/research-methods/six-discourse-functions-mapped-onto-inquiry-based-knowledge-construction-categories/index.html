Six discourse functions mapped onto inquiry-based knowledge construction categories¶

Research Method · All research methods
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article inductively derived a coding taxonomy: "This resulted in the following six DF: reviewing directions and strategies, sharing and confirming information, brainstorming, empirical evaluation, clarification and elaboration, and submission and reflection." These map onto inquiry-based learning phases — orientation (reviewing directions, sharing information), conceptualization (brainstorming), investigation (empirical evaluation), discussion (clarification and elaboration) and conclusion (submission and reflection) — as a means of segmenting CSCL dialogue by its function for knowledge construction.

Claims¶

  • Group A, who failed the learning task, devoted most dialogues to orientation and conceptualization and asked yes-no questions at the personal level, while group B, who succeeded, distributed dialogue across investigation and discussion [+M]
  • The emergent LLM coding pipeline reaches substantial agreement with expert human coding (Cohen's κ = 0.78, raw agreement 84%) on a stratified 10% sample [+W]
