Criterion-referenced versus norm-referenced tests as two distinct assessment purposes¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The brief organizes its comparison around a distinction between two test purposes. "The ISAT and other tests that are constructed to measure students' performance on specific subject matter content are called Criterion-Referenced Tests (CRTs)," indicating whether students demonstrate pre-determined mastery of state-defined standards. The ITBS, by contrast, "is designed to compare students to a nationally representative group on widely acknowledged basic skills," making it a Norm-Referenced Test scored relative to other students rather than to content material. This framework underpins the brief's analysis of content, scoring, cut scores, and growth measurement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Distinguishing the two types requires knowing a test's intended purpose: measurement against defined content standards versus comparison to a national average.

Constraints¶

  • The brief notes the two purposes are "different but not mutually exclusive," and that the ITBS, spanning all grades on a continuous scale, supports measuring annual growth while the ISAT, given only at benchmark grades without a unitary underlying scale, does not.
