Negotiation of meaning sequences model (Varonis & Gass, revised by Smith) with trigger and resolution parts¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's analytic framework codes chat interaction into negotiation of meaning sequences consisting of "two parts: trigger and resolution". The trigger is "the utterance or portion of an utterance on the part of the speaker that results in some indication of non-understanding on the part of the listener"; the resolution comprises indicator , response , reaction /, confirmation and reconfirmation . Five trigger types were found in this study: lexical, content, syntactic, task complexity, and discourse. Sequences of T-I-R or longer were counted; T-I-only sequences were excluded.

Requirements¶

Constraints¶

  • Sequences consisting of only a trigger and indicator were not included in the data analysis

Target Learners¶

  • non-native speaker dyads in communicative second language tasks

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • quantifying negotiation of meaning as an index of input comprehension work in second language acquisition

  • Subtitled Videos Increase Negotiation Of Meaning Online Tbll [+M]
  • Content-related problems trigger most negotiation routines, and syntactic triggers are absent from text chat where turns are short [+W]
  • Videoconferencing was conducive to more negotiation of meaning than text chat and discussion board, measured as proportion of negotiated turns [+W]
  • Completing meaning-focused tasks through CMC fosters negotiated interactions in all three modes (discussion board, text chat, videoconferencing) [+W]
