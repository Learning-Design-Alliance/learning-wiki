Four-dimension contrast of legacy versus strategic-surprise-ready R&D architecture¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The paper's organizing framework contrasts legacy educational R&D with a strategic-surprise-ready architecture along four dimensions: framing commitments, problem definition, project/talent model, and focal outcomes. An accompanying table elaborates contrasts such as low versus high risk tolerance, bespoke institution-bound research versus platform-enabled network-oriented research, and success measured by publications versus practical utility, adoption, and platform-level telemetry.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Adopting the architecture requires cross-sector performer teams with technical, contextual, and engineering fluency rather than individual principal investigators.

Constraints¶

  • The paper states the architecture departs from long-standing conventions of proposal-vetting, multiyear grants, and publication-based success metrics.
