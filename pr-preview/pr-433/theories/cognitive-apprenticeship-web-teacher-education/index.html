Cognitive apprenticeship model (Collins et al.) as a framework for Web-based teacher education¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article applies the cognitive apprenticeship model, in which the teacher first models expert-level practices, then designs scaffolds supporting learners in applying them, coaches throughout, and encourages articulation and reflection while fading support. The author argues it "may be particularly well-suited for a Web-based environment for educational technology because students must use educational technology to learn about educational technology." In I-TEP it was instantiated through OMS media, tiered scaffolds, email coaching, and blog reflections.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor investment in creating modeling media and providing ongoing coaching and feedback
  • Technology access sufficient to deliver video, animation, email and Web-based tools

Constraints¶

  • The author states the applicability of the findings is context dependent, based on a student population with broadband access at a mid-sized liberal arts university

Target Learners¶

  • Pre-service K–12 (primary and secondary) teacher education students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Integrating technology into future teaching practice
  • Technology problem-solving and troubleshooting skills
