Taxonomy of macro- and micro-skills of listening comprehension¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The paper organizes listening skills into macro-language use, "the learning of elements of language and their potential combinations", and micro-skills that each perform different functions. It reports Rivers and Temperly's seven enumerated listening abilities, Richards' 33 micro-skills (of which ten are listed, from retaining chunks of language to recognizing core conversational vocabulary), Richards' three listening areas (conversational, academic, pleasure listening), and Richards' three levels of processing: propositional identification, interpretation of illocutionary force, and activation of real world knowledge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction should treat listening as a primary skill in its own right rather than a by-product of other practice

Constraints¶

  • The skills are inter-dependent and integrated with each other, not separate

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners from elementary to advanced levels

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Listening comprehension skill development in EFL

Claims¶

  • A 2010 teacher guide reports, citing Postovsky (1974, 1975), that delaying oral production and emphasising aural comprehension early in EFL instruction gives better results than intensive oral practice [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Listening as an active process of constructing meaning from a stream of sounds
  • Unified taxonomy of subskills shared between and exclusive to reading and listening comprehension

Examples¶

  • Four-stage progression for teaching listening skills across proficiency levels
  • Increase listening instruction time and replace audio-lingual pattern drills with listening comprehension materials
