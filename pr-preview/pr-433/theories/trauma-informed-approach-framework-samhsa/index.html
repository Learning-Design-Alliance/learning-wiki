SAMHSA-based framework of trauma-informed approaches: four assumptions, six key principles, and three core domains¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review defines a trauma-informed approach, drawing on SAMHSA and NCTSN, as a program, organization, or system that "realizes the impact of trauma, recognizes the symptoms of trauma, responds by integrating knowledge about trauma policies and practices, and seeks to reduce retraumatization." It is a framework infused across an organization rather than a standalone intervention, incorporating "key trauma principles into the organizational culture." Hanson and Lang's three core domains—workforce/professional development, organizational changes, and practice changes—operationalize it for the review's inclusion criteria.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • At least two of the three key elements must be present: workforce development, trauma-focused services, and organizational environment and practices

Constraints¶

  • The review notes there is no consensus on terminology such as trauma-informed care, trauma-sensitive, and trauma-informed system, which makes implementation and study challenging

Target Learners¶

  • PreK-12 students in school settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Reducing impact of trauma on academic, behavioral, and socioemotional functioning

Related Theories¶

  • Integration of decolonial, socioecological, translingual, trauma-informed, and community-engaged praxis across the handbook

Examples¶

  • Three-tier trauma-informed consultation model implemented in Appalachian Head Start programs
  • HEARTS whole-school trauma-informed program in San Francisco elementary schools
  • Proceed with caution in adopting trauma-informed approaches and begin evaluating them, including descriptive and qualitative research on implementation
  • Initiate trauma-informed planning before the start of the school year
