Go-Lab online teacher community and Inquiry Learning Spaces¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Go-Lab is an online ecosystem and teacher community that supports the creation, sharing, reuse, and refinement of inquiry-based learning spaces and resources, developed and maintained by the Go-Lab Initiative.

Components¶

  • Go-Lab/Graasp Inquiry Learning Spaces with online scaffolding tools: The experimental condition used virtual learning environments from the Go-Lab project, built on the Graasp platform. "The platform involves Inquiry Learning Spaces (ILSs) as learning resources that foster inquiry learning" by following "the inquiry cycle phases of orientation, hypothesis generation, experimentation, conclusion, and discussion". ILSs can include virtual/remote labs, scaffolding apps, and multimedia. In this study teachers developed nine ILSs for force and energy, each covered in about two hours over five weeks, using a hypothesis scratchpad and an experiment design tool as scaffolds. (Hasan Ozgur Kapici (2025))
  • Inquiry Learning Spaces (ILSs) as digital inquiry environments: Inquiry Learning Spaces are online or digital environments where students interact with learning material including labs, apps, and multimedia resources. Material is organized in phases following the Go-Lab inquiry cycle, which includes "orientation, conceptualization, investigation, conclusion, and discussion" phases. Teachers can adapt the number and names of phases, populate them with content, and configure apps to scaffold student inquiry processes such as hypothesis construction and experiment design. (de Jong et al. (2021))

Claims¶

  • Re-used Inquiry Learning Spaces were about four times more likely to be implemented than those created from scratch, and scaffolding increased the implementation rate [+M]
  • Middle school students in inquiry-based computer-based learning environments gain more conceptual understanding of force and energy than peers in regular inquiry-based classrooms [+M]
  • Inquiry skills improved significantly only in the computer-based inquiry group, which outperformed the regular-classroom group at posttest [+M]
