Force and Motion Microworld (FMM)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A suite of three computer simulation programs developed by Tao et al. for exploring force and motion through collaborative predict–observe–explain tasks.

Components¶

Claims¶

  • Computer-supported collaborative learning with simulation programs fostered conceptual change for students who were cognitively engaged and prepared to reflect on and reconstruct their conceptions [+W]
  • Alternative conceptions in force and motion were very difficult to change: of 14 students, 6 showed substantial conceptual change, 1 some change, and 7 no change [~W]
  • High joint on-task engagement with high equality and mutuality of engagement did not necessarily mean cognitive engagement or ensure conceptual change [~W]
  • Peer conflicts did not always produce conceptual change; they appeared to work only for students prepared to reflect on and reconstruct their conceptions [~W]
  • Co-construction of shared understanding needed to be accompanied by students' personal construction and sense making for conceptual change to be stable over time [~W]
