Courseware Initiative¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A Gates Foundation initiative and theory-of-change framework that combines equity-centered digital courseware with instructors' evidence-based teaching practices to improve engagement and achievement, especially for students at risk of failing to earn course credit.

Components¶

  • Courseware Initiative Theory of Change: courseware plus evidence-based teaching practices, not technology alone: The Gates Foundation's theory of change for digitally enabled courseware posits that improved student engagement and achievement result from combining equity-centered courseware with instructor implementation of evidence-based teaching practices. The article states the theory "does not treat learning technology as a 'silver bullet'" and instead includes "instructor implementation practices grounded in empirical research on what constitutes effective instruction" as "a key part of the intervention to be scaled and tested." It targets students from groups historically at risk of failing to earn course credit. (Barbara Means et al. (2026))

Claims¶

  • The number of evidence-based teaching practices enacted in courseware-using statistics classes is strongly associated with class-level affective engagement (r = 0.61). [+M]
  • EBT practice enactment is positively associated with residualized student learning on the BASIC assessment (r = 0.315). [+M]
  • EBT practice enactment is positively associated with residualized course grades (r = 0.316). [+M]
  • Meeting four or more implementation fidelity criteria is associated with course grades about 0.24 points higher than expected, a 0.58 grade-point swing versus meeting only two criteria. [+W]
  • Instructors in their second or subsequent term using Lumen One made greater use of six of seven instructor-facing courseware components than first-term users. [+W]
  • Three fidelity components—completing Apply It problems, following up on Time-Sensitive Interventions, and having Automated Messages on—differentiate classes with better-vs-poorer-than-expected grades. [+W]
