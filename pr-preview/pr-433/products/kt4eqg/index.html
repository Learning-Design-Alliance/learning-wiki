KT4EQG¶

Description¶

KT4EQG is a personalized question-generation system combining knowledge tracing, large-language-model generation, reinforcement learning, and an alignment verifier, developed by Xinyi Gao et al.

Components¶

  • KT4EQG system with released code: KT4EQG is an implemented personalized question generation system combining the KT2 knowledge tracer, a QWEN3-8B-based question generator trained with SFT and GRPO reinforcement learning, and an alignment verifier built on ALL-MINILM-L6-V2. The paper notes "Code available at https://github.com/UCSB-NLP-Chang/KT4EQG". The system was evaluated on XES3G5M and MOOCRADAR across six knowledge modules, with training on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs and the verifier trained on a single RTX 3090 Ti. (Xinyi Gao et al. (2026))

Claims¶

  • Reinforcement learning with a learned alignment reward substantially improves question-concept alignment after supervised fine-tuning, while answerability remains high throughout training [+W]
  • KT4EQG achieves the highest exam score across all practice-round counts, while methods without effective concept selection show only marginal gains as rounds increase [+W]
  • Without alignment training, generated questions collapse to similar content regardless of the intended knowledge concept, whereas KT4EQG questions spread across many verified KCs [+W]
  • Providing baselines with oracle knowledge-concept selection substantially improves their performance, identifying concept selection as a key bottleneck, though baselines with oracle KCs still trail KT4EQG [+W]
  • KT4EQG remains competitive when evaluated with alternative knowledge tracers (BKT and DKT), though its advantage varies across modules [+W]
  • KT4EQG's education-value objective produces an adaptive KC-selection policy: weaker students are given better-mastered concepts and stronger students progressively more challenging ones [+W]
  • KT4EQG achieves the highest exam score across all knowledge modules on both XES3G5M and MOOCRADAR, outperforming all question-generation baselines [+W]
  • Hybrid approaches combining LLMs with traditional knowledge-tracing algorithms outperform either method alone for learner modelling [~W]
