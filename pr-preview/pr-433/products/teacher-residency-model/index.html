Teacher residency model¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 7 studies (3 review, 3 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A teacher-preparation programme model run by partnerships between local education agencies and institutions of higher education, combining credential coursework, a yearlong mentored clinical placement, and financial support for residents.

Components¶

  • Pathways Alliance national definition of teacher residencies: The Pathways Alliance, a coalition of K–12 and higher education organizations, defines teacher residencies as "preparation pathways that are anchored in partnership" with curricula collaboratively designed by local education agencies and teacher preparation programs. Two goals anchor the definition: ensuring aspiring teachers have affordable, high-quality supports while learning to teach, and supporting the instructional and staffing needs of local schools and districts. In yearlong pre-service clinical practice, residents are not teachers of record; they work alongside accomplished mentor teachers experiencing the breadth of teachers' roles across a year. The report uses this definition to guide its review of state policies. (Saunders et al. (2024))
  • Teacher residency pathway: LEA-IHE partnership integrating credentialing coursework with a yearlong placement in an expert mentor teacher's classroom: A teacher residency is an intensive preparation pathway in which "a resident's credentialing coursework is integrated with a yearlong placement in the classroom of an expert mentor teacher." It is developed and operated by a partnership between a local educational agency and an institution of higher education with a state-approved preparation program, and residents typically receive financial support in exchange for a multi-year teaching commitment in the host LEA. The brief treats residencies as a route to well-prepared, retained 1st-year teachers responsive to local hiring needs. (Brannegan et al. (2024))
  • Contextualized preparation model of teacher residencies: The Texas Education Agency framed the TCLAS residency model as "contextualized preparation": teachers are prepared within the district where they will work, learning from district context and culture, engaged in daily practice-based learning in district classrooms, coached daily by a high-quality mentor teacher, and building relationships within the school community during preparation. The report describes this as a departure from licensure systems premised on teachers being equally ready to work at any school in the state. (Wojcikiewicz et al. (2025))
  • Teacher residency model with eight key characteristics of strong programs: A teacher residency is an accelerated certification pathway in which candidates "work as paid apprentices to skilled expert teachers while completing highly integrated coursework" during a full-year clinical placement in a high-need classroom, with tuition and living support repaid through a multi-year service commitment. The report lists eight characteristics of strong residencies drawn from a research review, including strong district-university partnerships, tightly integrated coursework, expert mentor co-teaching, cohorts in partnership teaching schools, and ongoing mentoring for graduates. States including California, Texas, Pennsylvania, Michigan, and Louisiana fund or mandate residency models. (Espinoza et al. (2018))
  • Teacher residency programs as district-preparation partnerships for high-need positions: Teacher residency programs are "partnerships between school districts and teacher preparation programs designed to recruit and prepare a diverse pool of candidates to teach in high-need positions." They are yearlong apprenticeships modeled on medical residencies, providing residents extensive clinical practice under an expert mentor teacher while completing tightly integrated coursework. Districts with residencies reported certainty about the number and quality of teachers they could expect to hire. (Carver-Thomas et al. (2021))
  • Teacher residency model with yearlong clinical apprenticeship, cohort coursework, stipend-for-commitment exchange, and LEA co-construction: The report describes a teacher residency preparation model in which candidates spend a full school year coteaching in a mentor teacher's classroom while taking residency-specific coursework in cohorts, receive a stipend in exchange for a multiyear teaching commitment in the partner district, and are prepared through university-LEA partnership. As printed, "All programs gave residents stipends to help cover their residency-year expenses, in exchange for a commitment to teach in the program's partner LEA(s) for 3 or 4 years following program completion." The model is organized around seven prioritized teaching practices aligned to California's Teacher Performance Expectations and the Danielson Framework for Teaching. (Fitz et al. (2025))
  • Eight key characteristics of strong teacher residency programs: The report enumerates eight characteristics shared by high-quality residencies: strong district/university partnerships; coursework tightly integrated with clinical practice; a full-year residency teaching alongside an expert mentor; recruitment of high-ability, diverse candidates to meet district hiring needs; financial support in exchange for a three- to five-year commitment; cohorts placed in teaching schools; expert mentors who co-teach; and ongoing mentoring for graduates. The report states that "the success of residencies requires attention to each of the defining characteristics of the model, and the integrity of their implementation". (Guha et al. (2016))
  • Common elements of high-quality teacher residencies: Research on graduate-level residency programs with demonstrated strong outcomes identifies a set of common design elements that distinguish residencies from traditional and most alternative preparation pathways. Well-designed residencies "consist of strong partnerships between local education agencies and teacher preparation programs," require a full year of residency alongside an accomplished mentor teacher who is the teacher of record, tightly integrate coursework with classroom practice, place cohorts in teaching schools, offer post-residency mentoring, recruit diverse candidates for district needs, and provide financial support in exchange for a multiyear teaching commitment. (Saunders et al. (2024))
  • Common features of a high-quality teacher residency: The brief enumerates characteristics that research has identified in high-quality teacher residency programs: residencies "consist of strong partnerships between school districts, universities, and sometimes other organizations," tightly integrate coursework with classroom practice, require a full year of residency teaching alongside an accomplished mentor teacher, recruit diverse candidates for specific district needs, provide financial support in exchange for a teaching commitment, place cohorts in teaching schools modeling evidence-based practices, and offer ongoing mentoring for graduates. The brief presents these features as making early childhood-focused residencies part of a multipronged solution for preparing qualified, diverse teachers. (Cathy Yun (2022))
  • Eleven characteristics of high-quality teacher residencies: eight research-based characteristics plus three emerging from the case studies: Across the five case study programs, eleven practices emerged as promoters of success. Eight align with characteristics previously identified by the Learning Policy Institute and Pathways Alliance: strong LEA–EPP partnerships; selectively recruited diverse candidates; a full-year teaching experience alongside an expert mentor teacher; expert mentor teachers who coteach; tightly integrated coursework and clinical practice; cohorts of residents; financial supports for residents; and ongoing mentoring and support for graduates. Three additional characteristics emerged from the current study: "Proactive whole person supports that residents can easily access through a 'go-to person,'" ongoing program capacity development, and an explicit focus on promoting equity for residents and their students. (Yun et al. (2025))

Claims¶

  • A majority of teacher residency candidates report financial hardship during their residency year, including food and housing insecurity [~M]
  • Many programs face partnership alignment, hiring-placement, and administrative capacity challenges that limit financial sustainability [~M]
  • California residency program completers are more likely to identify as candidates of color than completers of other preparation pathways [+M]
  • Residency completers report more intensive clinical experiences with higher support and are more likely to pass teaching performance assessments on their first attempt [+M]
  • Teacher residency program graduates are more racially diverse than other new teachers and much more likely to stay in teaching, especially in sponsoring high-need districts [+M]
  • Teacher residencies and preparation partnerships proved important to district recruitment during the pandemic [+M]
  • Residency-prepared teachers remain in teaching and in their districts at higher rates than other novice teachers, typically 80–95% after 3 years [+M]
  • Residency graduates report strongly positive perceptions of their preparation, and principals tend to perceive them as more effective than other novice teachers [+M]
