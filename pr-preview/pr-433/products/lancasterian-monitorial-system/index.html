Lancasterian (Monitorial) System¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A nineteenth-century mass-schooling programme created by John Lancaster that used student monitors, scripted lessons, learning progressions, and mastery assessments to organize peer-assisted instruction at scale.

Components¶

  • The Lancasterian (Monitorial) System as an early competency-based, self-paced, peer-learning model: The article describes the early-nineteenth-century Monitorial System created by John Lancaster as a scalable instructional organization predating modern personalized learning. Children were placed along learning progressions per domain, with student monitors delivering "scripted lessons to develop skills and assessments to move each student to the next level in the sequence as soon as he demonstrated mastery." The author writes, "Today we might call what Lancaster devised 200 years ago a self-paced, mastery-based, peer-learning instructional system," noting its similarity to current competency-based visions. (David Dockterman (2018))

Claims¶

  • Instructional technology is rejected when it mismatches the dominant pedagogy, as with the blackboard in monitorial schools [~W]
  • Grouping students solely by content competence neglected social and motivational differences, which the monitorial system had to patch ad hoc [~W]
