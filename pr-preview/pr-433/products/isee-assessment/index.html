Description¶

The ISEE Assessment is a named assessment framework for evaluating education against holistic human-flourishing and well-being goals, developed by the ISEE Assessment initiative.

Components¶

  • ISEE Assessment human-flourishing framework built on Aristotle's eudaimonia: The ISEE Assessment's conceptual framework defines the purpose of education as human flourishing rather than economic growth, built on Aristotle's concept of "eudaimonia, loosely translated as 'flourishing.'" It defines flourishing, learning, evaluation and education through transdisciplinary review and consensual discussion, resting on the idea that education must ensure holistic development nurturing both human relationships and academic growth, aligned with Article 26 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. It contrasts this with human capital theory, traditionally considered the primary educational goal for economic growth. (Singh et al. (2025))
  • ISEE Assessment conceptual framework linking contexts, policies and the What, Where, How and When of learning: The ISEE Assessment is guided by a conceptual framework (Fig. 1) that maps inter-linkages within the education system: societal and environmental contexts influence educational policies and practices, which in turn influence what, where, how and when we learn. The article states that "The conceptual framework presented in Fig. 1 captures the key inter-linkages within the education system, which will be assessed, and will guide the assessment." The framework uses learning success benchmarks that feed into human flourishing and the 17 Sustainable Development Goals. (Duraiappah et al. (2021))
  • Four pillars of education as benchmarks for evaluating learning success: The framework adopts UNESCO's four pillars of education — Learning to Know, Learning to Do, Learning to Be, and Learning to Live Together — as benchmarks for evaluating learning success, which the article links to human flourishing and the Sustainable Development Goals. The article states: "Second, we will use the four pillars of education: (i) learning to Know, (ii) learning to Do, (iii) learning to Be, and learning to Live Together19 as benchmarks to analyze how contextual factors have in ﬂuenced and been in ﬂuenced by the educational aims and practices that these pillars encompass". The pillars are used in Box 4 of the conceptual framework. (Duraiappah et al. (2021))

Claims¶

  • Education focused on economic growth has produced upward trends in literacy and credentials alongside downward trends in agency, humanistic values, relationships, and civic engagement, motivating a shift toward human flourishing [+W]
  • Because cognitive, social and emotional processing show no clear demarcation in the brain and dynamically interact with the environment, the ISEE Assessment recommends an integrative CASE (cognitive, academic, social, emotional) approach across learning, teaching and assessment [+W]
