Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 11 claims (9 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 6 studies (5 associational, 1 design), q1–q3 · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A district accountability assessment system prepared for and run by District of Columbia Public Schools to estimate school and teacher value added.

Components¶

  • Value-added models for measuring school and teacher effectiveness in DC Public Schools (IMPACT and TEAM), 2010-2011: This report presents the value-added models used in the District of Columbia Public Schools' IMPACT and TEAM effectiveness systems for the 2010-2011 school year. As the report states, it "presents the value-added models used to measure school and teacher effectiveness in the District of Columbia Public Schools in the 2010-2011 school year." It was prepared for DC Public Schools under the Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers project (time frame 2009-2015). (Eric Isenberg (2011))
  • Urban school district value-added specification comparison (Working Paper 15): A working paper analyzing urban school district data to investigate whether value-added model specifications—track indicators or classroom-characteristic controls—eliminate potential bias from student sorting into academic tracks. It was prepared for District of Columbia Public Schools as part of a "Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers" project (2009-2015). The paper compares specifications by subject and grade level and examines effects on estimate precision and distribution tails. (Ali Protik et al. (2013))
  • Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers (2009–2015 project context): The article is associated with the project "Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers," with a stated time frame of 2009–2015, prepared for District of Columbia Public Schools. It investigates empirical Bayes shrinkage of teacher value-added estimates within this district accountability context, examining how the adjustment relates to teachers of students with hard-to-predict achievement levels. (Mariesa Herrmann et al. (2016))

Claims¶

  • Applying the Full Roster-Plus Method instead of the Full Roster Method produces very small empirical differences in value-added estimates in District of Columbia Public Schools data [+W]
  • Accounting for mean and standard deviation of classroom achievement may reduce bias for middle school math teachers [+W]
  • Including track indicators and classroom achievement controls simultaneously reduces precision of value-added estimates [+W]
  • Alternative value-added specifications produce substantially different teacher estimates but small changes in distribution tails [+W]
  • Including track indicators in value-added models may reduce tracking-related bias for high school reading teachers [+W]
  • Teachers of students with low prior achievement and who receive free lunch tend to have less precise value-added estimates [+W]
  • Shrinkage could differentially affect consequences for teachers of fewer students or students with hard-to-predict achievement [+W]
  • Shrinkage had no statistically significant effect on the relative probability that teachers of hard-to-predict students received consequences [+W]
  • Mathematica designed a method to identify effective schools for a consortium of 99 charter schools in 18 states and the District of Columbia in the project's first year. [+W]
  • Rapid guessing did not appear to substantively affect teacher value-added estimates under the value-added specifications used [~W]
  • Up to 26% of teachers in the bottom value-added quintile under one specification are ranked higher under another specification [-W]
