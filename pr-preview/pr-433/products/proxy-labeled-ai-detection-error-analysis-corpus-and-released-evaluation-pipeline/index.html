Description¶

Karr et al.'s released corpus and code evaluate commercial AI-text-detector errors using published abstracts, model rewrites, and humanized texts.

Components¶

  • Proxy-labeled AI-detection error-analysis corpus and released evaluation pipeline (642 OpenAlex abstracts, three rewrite conditions, two detectors, one humanizer): A controlled evaluation corpus and pipeline for quantifying AI-detector error rates under proxy labels. The authors sampled English abstracts from OpenAlex in chemistry, computer science, political science, and theology for 2013–2015 and 2023–2025; "After requiring 25 to 500 words and PDF access (≈20% loss), we retained 642 abstracts." Each abstract contributed an original plus three Gemini 3 Flash rewrites (refine abstract-only, refine abstract+article, new article-only), scored with Pangram 3.2 and GPTZero before and after Undetectable AI v11 humanization. Analysis code is released at the article's GitHub repository. (Jonathan A. Karr Jr. et al. (2026))

Claims¶

  • After humanization with Undetectable AI, fewer than 4% of AI-labeled rewrites remain detectable (FNR > 96%) for Pangram and GPTZero [+M]
  • Light AI-assisted edits to human-written abstracts are flagged by commercial detectors at 64–80% (Pangram) and 38–49% (GPTZero) rates [+M]
  • No detection threshold simultaneously keeps original flag rates, light-edit capture, and AI-pool false negatives low [+M]
  • Detector flag rates on 2023–2025 original abstracts vary sharply by domain, with non-STEM rates far above STEM [+M]
  • Inter-detector agreement between Pangram and GPTZero collapses after humanization, so a single vendor flag is not a reliable consensus signal [+M]
  • Independent evaluation found commercial AI-text detectors neither sufficiently accurate nor sufficiently reliable for high-stakes use [+W]
  • Elevated detector scores track long-token and Academic Word List density, with numeric and non-alphabetic token ratios correlating negatively [~W]
  • Comparative studies report that AI detection tools do not reliably distinguish human-written from AI-generated text [+W]
  • A detector can achieve high aggregate accuracy while producing materially different over-detection rates across author groups [~W]
  • In a documented exploratory audit, one human-authored manuscript received materially different classifications from five commercial detectors, spanning 0% human to Human Generated [+W]
  • A linear mixed-effects model of detection error showed significant paper type, tool, and interaction effects, with underestimation largest for fully AI-generated papers and Pangram consistently producing the smallest errors [+W]
  • All four detection tools classified 100% of fully human-written papers as true negatives, with false positives nearly absent [~W]
  • Humanization works by systematically suppressing detector-salient surface cues — long-token ratio, AWL density, and lexical diversity — rather than fragmenting syntax [~W]
  • The stylometric detector misclassified 4 of 18 independently authored documents as GPT-assisted (FPR 22.2%) and 2 of 18 GPT-assisted documents (FNR 11.1%), with wide confidence intervals [~W]
