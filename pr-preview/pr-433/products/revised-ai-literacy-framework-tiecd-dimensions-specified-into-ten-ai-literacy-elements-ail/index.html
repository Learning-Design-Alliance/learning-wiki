Revised AI literacy framework: TIECD dimensions specified into ten AI Literacy Elements (AILEs)¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A Niri et al. framework that operationalizes Technology, Impact, Ethics, Collaboration, and Design into ten AI-literacy elements for embedding AI literacy in K–12 STEAM education.

Components¶

  • Revised AI literacy framework: TIECD dimensions specified into ten AI Literacy Elements (AILEs): The review proposes a revised AI literacy framework that preserves the TIECD areas (Technology, Impact, Ethics, Collaboration, Design) while specifying ten operational AI Literacy Elements under them: Fundamental AI Concepts, Computational Thinking, Data Literacy, Social Reasoning, Creative Imagination, Ethical Awareness, Using AI, Creating with AI, Managing AI, and Designing AI. The authors state it advances existing conceptualizations by "establishing data literacy as a distinct and foundational element" and "reconceptualizing collaboration into three operational elements", positioning it as "a theoretically robust and contextually grounded model for embedding AI literacy into K-12 STEAM education". (Niri et al. (2026))
  • Ten AI literacy element categories synthesized inductively from 11 validated AI literacy frameworks: From mapping DigComp 2.2's 73 novel AI examples onto 11 validated AI literacy frameworks, the review inductively consolidated framework dimension labels into ten AI literacy element categories (Table 3): technical knowledge, AI ethics, critical evaluation, usage and application, affect/attitude/impact, cognitive learning, teacher-related TPACK, psychological meta-competencies, creation and higher-order thinking, and behavioral learning. The table notes that "Categories emerged through inductive analysis of dimension labels across 11 validated AI literacy frameworks." The taxonomy shows how AI literacy extends digital literacy constructs such as ethics, critical evaluation, and communication with AI. (Zemin Guo et al. (2026))

Claims¶

  • Technical AI literacy elements dominate STEAM implementations: Fundamental AI Concepts (36 studies), Data Literacy (30) and Computational Thinking (29) far exceed Managing AI (6), Designing AI (8) and Creative Imagination (9) [+M]
  • Ethical reflection is often addressed separately from technical and creative AI activities, showing weak co-occurrence with Social Reasoning (42%), Using AI (41%) and Creating with AI (55%) [+M]
  • DigComp 2.2 added 73 novel AI examples, concentrated in communication and collaboration, problem solving, and information and data literacy [+M]
