Opportunity Culture¶

Description¶

Opportunity Culture is a school redesign and staffing framework developed by Public Impact that uses tiered teaching roles to expand the reach of excellent teachers and strengthen teacher leadership.

Components¶

  • Opportunity Culture defined-role staffing structure (MCLs, MRTs, Elementary Specialists, Team Teachers, Teacher Residents, Reach Associates): Opportunity Culture is "an approach to school design based on a tiered teaching structure within a content area or across one or more grade levels in a school," intended to increase teacher leadership and expand the instructional impact of teachers recognized for strong instruction. Multi-Classroom Leaders lead teacher teams through feedback, coaching, modeling instruction, co-planning, and modeling data analysis, and are accountable for all team student results. Supporting roles include Master Reach Teachers with larger classes aided by technology and interns, Elementary Specialists teaching specific content, Team Teachers, year-long Teacher Residents, and Reach Associates (redesigned paraprofessionals) who supervise students during digital, skills-practice, or project-based learning. (Center on School Turnaround at WestEd (2018))
  • Opportunity Culture school design principles: reach, pay, budget-neutral funding, protected time, and matched authority-accountability: The brief lists five design principles that "characterize the Opportunity Culture school structure": reach more students with excellent teachers and their teams; increase compensation for teachers who extend their impact; utilize regular budgets to increase pay for teachers in leadership roles; provide protected in-school time and clarity about using that time for planning, collaboration, and professional development; and match authority and accountability to each teacher's responsibilities. The pattern operates at whole-school staffing level, redistributing existing teacher positions into tiered roles. (Center on School Turnaround at WestEd (2018))

Claims¶

  • Teachers on Multi-Classroom Leader teams produced learning gains equal to top-quartile teachers in math and nearly that in reading [+M]
