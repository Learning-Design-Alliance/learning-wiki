Otis's absolute grade of intelligence scale with coefficient of brightness¶

Description¶

An intelligence assessment scale proposed by Arthur S. Otis that assigns absolute intelligence grades and age-adjusted brightness coefficients to estimate general intelligence and probable ultimate attainment.

Components¶

  • Otis Group Intelligence Scale Advanced Examination: ten tests, described as the oldest and perhaps most widely used group test: Arthur S. Otis's Advanced Examination is described as "The oldest and perhaps the most widely used of the group tests of intelligence", consisting of 10 tests each containing from 20 to 30 tasks, including following directions, opposites, disarranged sentences, proverbs, arithmetic, geometric figures, analogies, similarities, narrative completion and memory, suitable for grades 5 to 12. The Army Alpha examination is noted as based largely on the Otis tests. (Bell (1920))
  • The Absolute Point Scale framework and the Coefficient of Brightness: ability units defined so equal point increments equal ability increments and zero points mean absence of ability: The framework holds that scores from different tests can be averaged fairly only after transmutation into common absolute units, or points, such that equal increments of ability receive equal increments of points. Otis defines the Coefficient of Brightness as "the quotient that would be obtained by dividing the measure of the absolute amount of mental ability of any individual by the measure of the absolute amount of mental ability which was normal for the age of that individual." The scale's zero is set so that zero points represent "just absence of ability," and the framework was applied in this study by dividing each pupil's total point score by the age norm taken from the curve of Fig. 2. (Otis (1918))
