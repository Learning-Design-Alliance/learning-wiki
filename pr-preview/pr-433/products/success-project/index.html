Success Project¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A school-based college-access and student-success programme implemented by full-time Success coordinators through the 6to16 curriculum, grades-and-attendance monitoring, and high-school application support.

Components¶

  • Success Project theory of action: three levers of social and cultural capital, grades-and-attention focus, and high school application support: The report articulates the program's theory of action, which held that "The Success Project intended to build three lev ers in schools for improving student outcomes": improving students' social and cultural capital via 6to16, increasing school-wide attention to grades and attendance, and getting students to apply to more and better-matched high schools. The levers were to be worked by full-time Success coordinators through three tasks: teaching the curriculum, monitoring grades and attendance, and facilitating applications. The model assumed the combination of levers would raise high school and college graduation chances. (Cowhy et al. (2017))

Claims¶

  • Students' attendance and middle grades on-track rates in Success schools improved during the first two program years, especially in Year 2 [+W]
  • Success schools' student GPAs did not show much improvement in the first two years [~M]
  • After two years, fewer graduates attended career academies and neighborhood high schools and more attended charter schools; these shifts were no different from comparison schools [~W]
  • Students reported improved school-work priorities, peer academic behavior, teacher connection and support, and school connectedness after two years [+W]
