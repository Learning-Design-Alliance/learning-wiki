Illinois principal preparation redesign¶

Product or Programme · All products and programmes
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A statewide Illinois policy and programme redesign for preparing and certifying school principals through selective admissions, competency-based coursework, internships, and performance assessments.

Components¶

  • Illinois Principal Preparation Program Theory of Change with feedback loop: The Illinois principal preparation redesign is organized around a theory of change depicting a pipeline from selection of high-potential candidates, through course work and school-based learning experiences including internships, to performance-based assessments, principal certification, placement in schools, highly effective principals and assistant principals, and schools demonstrating positive change. The theory of change evolved over a 10 year period and "identifies the critical elements of the new policy." A feedback loop involves data gathered and analyzed for external evaluation to inform effective policies and practices for continuous improvement. (Klostermann et al. (2015))

Claims¶

  • Respondents identified five main areas of benefit from the redesigned principal preparation policy, including more rigorous selection and a more extensive competency-based internship [+M]
  • Program representatives and statewide stakeholders were largely positive about the goals of Illinois' redesigned principal preparation policy [+M]
  • Illinois' shift to selective, principal-specific preparation programs caused substantial but anticipated enrollment declines [+M]
  • The new policy strengthened university-district partnerships so principal preparation reflects local school needs [+M]
