Regression discontinuity design applied to NCLB AYP thresholds in Title I schools¶

Element · All elements
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report is an exploratory quasi-experimental study that "use[s] a regression discontinuity design to examine the relationships between certain features of No Child Left Behind accountability and subsequent student achievement in Title I schools in two states and three school districts." It exploits AYP and identification thresholds to estimate sanction effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School-level AYP and identification status data in Title I schools in the studied states and districts

Constraints¶

  • Findings from two states and three cities cannot be generalized to produce a national estimate

Target Learners¶

  • Students in Title I schools in two states and three school districts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student achievement in reading and mathematics under NCLB accountability

Claims¶

  • Schools that missed AYP showed some positive subsequent achievement impacts under NCLB accountability [+M]
  • First-year identification for school improvement showed no positive achievement impacts [+M]
  • Accountability sanction effects were not consistent across years and outcomes [+M]
  • Findings from two states and three cities cannot be generalized to a national estimate of program effects [~M]

Related Elements¶
