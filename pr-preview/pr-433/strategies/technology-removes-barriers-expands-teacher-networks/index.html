Use technology to remove participation barriers and widen teacher networks in specialized-context PD¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends strategically integrating widely available technology into teacher PD so that collaboration is not bounded by geography or schedule. It reports that "the use of technology facilitated the collaboration of teachers across diverse contexts by simultaneously diminishing barriers to participation (for example, finding substitutes) and broadening the pool of teachers participants had the opportunity to interact with." The virtual modality also expanded professional learning communities beyond a single school or district and offered districts affordable, feasible structures for specialized PD.

  • sustained professional growth through collaborative inquiry and reflection

Claims¶

  • Professional learning on emerging technologies is widely available but misaligned with faculty workload, time, and incentives [~W]
  • Participation in the LSVC hybrid PD model was followed by statistically significant self-reported growth in bilingual instructional practices and confidence across experience groups [+W]
  • Teachers across the career span appreciated communicating with peers of different experience levels, with novices emphasizing observation of experienced teachers and veterans valuing newer teachers' fresh ideas [+W]

  • Join national innovation networks and build internal leadership academies to advance responsible district AI adoption
