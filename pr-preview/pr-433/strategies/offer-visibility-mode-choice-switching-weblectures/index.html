Offer students a choice of visibility-modes and allow free switching during a weblecture¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that educational institutions offer students a choice of visibility-mode when watching a weblecture, and allow them to switch freely between modes during the lecture. This follows from the finding that the effect of visibility on reported attention differs between the first and second weblecture. The authors state: "the best strategy for educational institutions will probably be to make it possible for students to watch a weblecture with an option to choose between more than one visibility-mode, and to switch freely between these modes during the weblecture".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A weblecture platform that supports multiple visibility-modes and free switching between them during playback.

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • maintaining attention during weblectures

Claims¶

  • Lecturer visibility has a positive effect on reported attention in weblectures when watching lasts more than 15 minutes [+W]
  • No difference in reported attention between visibility-modes after watching a short weblecture [+W]
  • Students prefer weblectures with a visible lecturer over audio and slides only [+W]
