Embed instructional and relevance prompts at multiple time points across the self-regulated learning process (instruction, reading, writing)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The strategy embeds two kinds of cognitive prompts — pre-actional instructional prompts (a knowledge-integration introduction, text-specific focus questions, and modeling of interrelations between texts) and content-independent relevance prompts on the importance of knowledge integration — into learners' self-regulated learning processes. In the study, "Both types of prompts were embedded in the students’ learning process at three time points: the instructional phase, the reading phase, and the writing phase." Task sheets remained with participants so prompts stayed accessible during the task.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prompts must be provided pre-actionally and repeatedly across the instructional, reading, and writing phases of the learning process
  • Relevance prompts refer to the relevance of knowledge integration in general, not to specific content

Constraints¶

  • The article states: "We found no direct evidence that pre-service teachers were supported in their knowledge integration by pre-actional cognitive prompts in the form of task-supplemental focus questions in combination with repeated content-independent relevance instruction."

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers in university teacher education courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • knowledge integration from multiple text sources across domains
  • transfer-oriented writing from multiple documents

Claims¶

  • Combined instructional and relevance prompts increase pre-service teachers' perceived relevance of knowledge integration over time, while relevance prompts alone and no prompts do not [+W]
  • Combined prompts produce substantially longer engagement with the text material than relevance prompts alone or no prompts [+W]
  • Unprompted pre-service teachers' texts show higher propositional similarity to the source reference model than combined-prompts texts [-W]
  • Unprompted pre-service teachers' texts match the combined source-text reference model more closely on structural matching than prompted groups' texts [-W]
  • The three prompting conditions did not differ on control variables (separative/integrative learning, metacognition, epistemological beliefs, self-efficacy, motivation, text-material perception) [~W]
  • Prompting type shows no significant effect on rated text quality (transfer, validity of conclusions, integration), with low interrater reliability for two criteria [~W]
