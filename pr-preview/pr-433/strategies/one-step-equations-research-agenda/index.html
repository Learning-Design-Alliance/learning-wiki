A five-question research agenda for one-step equation learning and instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article proposes that researchers investigate five questions arising from the classification: whether Type 1 equations are easier than Type 2 to learn; whether equations without a special feature are easier than those with one; the best way to teach equations involving a decimal, a percentage, and a fraction; how to help students learn equations with the pronumeral on the right side; and why solutions involving a decimal or fraction challenge students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research should use the Type 1/Type 2 classification as the framework for designing studies on one-step equations

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • mathematics students learning one-step equations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving equation solving skills across one-step equation types

Related Strategies¶

  • Teach one-step equations in a hierarchical sequence: Type 1 without special features first, then Type 1 with special features, then Type 2 in the same order
