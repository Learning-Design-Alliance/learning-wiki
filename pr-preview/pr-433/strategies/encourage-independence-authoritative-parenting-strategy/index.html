Parenting that encourages age-appropriate independence and uses authoritative practices may support early academic and social-emotional competencies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's findings, the authors conclude that "parenting that aims to encourage age-appropriate independence in young children and that relies on authoritative practices may promote the development of children's early academic and social-emotional competencies, with positive effects on academic achievement in later years." Parenting practices are described as a particularly important malleable factor among low-income families because they may buffer children from socioeconomic challenges. This strategy targets mothers of young Latino children during the transition to formal schooling.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parenting practices that are malleable and can be targeted in interventions supporting academic success

Constraints¶

  • The article reports associations from a longitudinal observational design; causality cannot be inferred
  • The authoritarian/authoritative typology is a Westernized measure whose applicability to Latino families has been questioned

Target Learners¶

  • Young Latino children (pre-k through first grade) from low-income families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Early academic competencies
  • Social-emotional school readiness

Claims¶

  • Authoritative parenting is associated with better social-emotional and academic school readiness in Mexican and Dominican American students [+W]

  • Academic readiness and adaptive behaviors at school entry predict higher academic achievement at the end of first grade [+W]
