Use FOXGLOVE as a testbed for studying feedback uptake, variation, and downstream revision outcomes rather than rated quality alone¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends future uses of FOXGLOVE: controlled studies of how students act on feedback, connecting expert ratings to outcomes such as revision quality, comprehension, and perceived usefulness. It also proposes using the dataset as "a testbed for whether eliciting more varied, reader-specific feedback improves usefulness", since human feedback shows greater variation while models converge on similar spans and phrasing. The authors caution that deployment decisions should rest on downstream student experience and outcomes, not on rated quality alone.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use alongside other measures rather than as a sole criterion, since the schema and rubric encode one particular view of what good feedback is

Constraints¶

  • The finding that LLM feedback receives higher ratings should not be used to justify replacing human instructors, as the ratings do not directly measure what actually helps students revise

Target Learners¶

  • twelfth-grade argumentative essay writers
  • writing instructors and model evaluators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • formative writing feedback design and evaluation
  • connecting feedback quality to revision outcomes

Related Strategies¶
