Manage ethical risks of AI-driven TEFL tools through data protection policies, fairness audits, human oversight, and balanced-use design¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review identifies three primary ethical concerns—data privacy and security, AI bias and fairness, and technology addiction and balanced use—and recommends concrete management strategies for each. For data privacy, it recommends "implementing robust data protection policies, obtaining explicit informed consent, adhering to relevant national and international data protection frameworks." For AI fairness, it recommends transparency in algorithmic decision-making, regular fairness audits, human oversight and appeal mechanisms, and diverse stakeholder involvement in system design. For over-reliance among younger learners, it recommends balanced learning experiences integrating technology with face-to-face interaction, explicit digital citizenship teaching, and monitoring of screen time. The review stresses that many included studies did not explicitly address ethical dimensions, a gap it calls increasingly consequential.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit informed consent procedures, especially parental involvement for younger learners
  • regular fairness audits and human oversight mechanisms for AI systems

Constraints¶

  • The review notes the relatively sparse explicit attention to ethical considerations across the study corpus represents a significant gap in the current TEFL technology literature

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners at all educational levels, with particular attention to younger learners' vulnerability

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Safe, fair, and balanced use of AI-driven and data-intensive technologies in English language education

Claims¶

  • Human-AI relationship concerns persist across all CAI generations while academic integrity and data privacy are emerging ethical concerns [~W]
  • Educator- and institutional-level challenges include curricular misalignment, need for teacher mediation, unequal access, privacy concerns, academic integrity risks, and algorithmic bias [+M]
  • Safeguards in university AI policies are sparse and unevenly covered [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Address core ethical challenges—data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and transparency—when deploying AI-driven personalized learning
  • Deploy agentic AI in education with transparency about agentic decisions, human oversight and institutional responsibility
  • Teachers adopting EAI should address digital literacy and ethical design challenges identified for each AI dimension
  • AI risk management should build students' ability to understand, manage, and respond to AI-related risks, not only reduce perceived risks
  • Balance AI integration in SRL so technological advantages are harnessed without undermining student self-efficacy
