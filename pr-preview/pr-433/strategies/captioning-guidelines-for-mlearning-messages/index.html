Use captioning to provide adaptable mobile messages across contexts, with font and placement guidelines¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends captions as one of the most versatile mechanisms available to the mobile message designer, rendering audio in a format "suitable for almost all users at any time or place". It adapts the Captioned Media Program guidelines (synchronized, equivalent, accessible captions) and broadcast television font and placement standards (Helvetica-like sans serif font, 32-character line, bottom two lines, translucent box) as a starting point for mLearning captioning standards.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Captions should be synchronized with audio, equivalent in content including speaker identification and sound effects, and accessible to those who need or want them

Constraints¶

  • Placement must not interfere with existing images such as maps, illustrations, or speakers' faces; if bottom placement interferes, captions move to the top or elsewhere

Target Learners¶

  • mobile learners in noisy environments, deaf learners, and learners using audio-unavailable contexts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension, listening comprehension, vocabulary acquisition, word recognition, decoding skills and motivation to learn
