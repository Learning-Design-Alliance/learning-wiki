Meet three requirements — specific measurement, specific manipulation and functional validation — using single-cell sequencing and dCas9/TALEN-based locus-specific epigenetic editing to study memory mechanisms¶

Description¶

The authors propose three requirements for definitively linking nucleic-acid mechanisms to memory: "(1) speci ﬁc measurement, (2) speci ﬁc manipulation and (3) functional valida- tion". Specific measurement means cell-type and locus-specific analyses, supported by single-cell sequencing, cell-type-specific epigenetic profiling methods developed by the authors, and click DNA labelling at single-base resolution. For manipulation and validation, dCas9 can site-selectively direct epigenetic modifiers in awake behaving animals, and TALEN has been used to functionally validate the sufficiency of modifications at one locus for modulating behaviour.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Studies should serve as the standard set by techniques for measurement and manipulation evolving, so that relating bulk protein or mRNA levels to behavioural change is no longer acceptable, as the data no longer support the 1:1 relationship of the central dogma

Constraints¶

  • The authors note there is a paucity of data to definitively link all of the pieces of how nucleic acids alter memory and learning

Target Learners¶

  • researchers studying molecular mechanisms of learning and memory in animal models

Target Learning Goals¶

  • establishing causal, locus-specific roles for epigenetic modifications in learning and memory behaviour

Affordances¶

Claims¶

  • Gene-specific DNA methylation and histone modification are associated with memory formation, as the review reports from Sweatt and colleagues' studies [+W]
  • The RNA modification m6A is highly dynamic in the brain and critically involved in fear memory formation, per the authors' recent work cited in the review [+W]
