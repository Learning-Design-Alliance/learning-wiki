Institutional support for skill upgrading and post-secondary navigation to bolster student parents' academic self-efficacy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that institutions support student parents in upgrading academic skills and navigating the post-secondary environment. The authors reason that "Beginning post-secondary study after an extended period of absence from the formal school environment may negatively impact student parents' self-efficacy beliefs with regards to their capacity to do well in school," and that "student parents might benefit from institutional support in upgrading their skills and navigating the post-secondary environment in order to increase self-efficacy beliefs and reduce attrition." This strategy targets the academic self-efficacy pathway identified in the structural model.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional programs that address both academic skill upgrading and orientation to the post-secondary environment

Constraints¶

  • Recommended based on cross-sectional correlational findings; causal impact on self-efficacy or attrition not yet tested

Target Learners¶

  • student parents returning to formal education after an extended absence

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic self-efficacy
  • persistence and retention in post-secondary study

Related Strategies¶

  • Recognize student parents as a distinct population rather than subsuming them within mature or non-traditional learners
