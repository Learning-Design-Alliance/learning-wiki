Weigh legibility gains against speed gains when choosing a penmanship style to teach¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article closes by posing an equating-values question for penmanship instruction: whether a legibility advantage is worth more than a speed advantage. It asks "Is 10 per cent. (9.76 per cent, appearing in vertical writing over medium slant) in legibility more worth striving for in teaching penmanship, than are 3 words ... in speed of writing?" It further asks whether the measured factors can be used to secure both improvements regardless of slant type.

Claims¶

  • Back-hand shows the largest average deviation from the median, indicating the least stable speed, while medium slant shows the least deviation in the one-minute test [+M]
  • The fastest handwriting style, back-hand, shows the largest percentage of cases written with free hand movement [+M]
  • Medium slant is 9.76 per cent less legible than vertical writing, while mixed writing is 4.26 per cent more legible than extreme slant [+M]
  • Extreme slant is fastest in the one-minute test while back-hand is fastest in the five-minute test and vertical writing slowest [+M]
