Strategies

Separate challenging material in time from peak-curiosity moments, or build a sustained curiosity state before presenting less-interesting material¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests that piquing strong curiosity can interfere with encoding of other material presented too close in time, so curiosity-based classroom techniques should avoid placing difficult-to-learn material immediately adjacent to high-curiosity moments. The authors propose that "in real-world applications, curiosity would likely be a more sustained (tonic) state" and that "Waiting some amount of time to pass after a tonic curiosity state might then prevent overshadowing of the target information, whilst retaining the neuromodulatory benefits of the state".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Whether a tonic curiosity state is neurobiologically supported in this way is, per the article, unclear, so this strategy remains a hypothesis for future research

Constraints¶

  • Based on a phasic, trial-by-trial laboratory paradigm tested only at immediate testing
  • The article states a tonic state might prevent overshadowing, not that any tested manipulation did

Target Learners¶

  • classroom learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retention of material that does not spark curiosity on its own

Claims¶

  • Memory for incidental scholastic facts is worse when the facts are paired with pre-determined high- versus low-curiosity trivia questions across three task versions [+W]
  • Scholastic memory differed between task versions, with Early Anticipation outperforming Late Anticipation but not differing from Post Satisfaction [+W]
  • Curiosity enhances learning, including for incidental material encountered during high-curiosity states [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Present problems before explanatory texts to arouse curiosity and situational interest in learners lacking prior knowledge
