Shift responsibility for coping with cultural stressors from youth and families to school-level intervention targets identified by participants¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that interventions combatting cultural stressors target contexts (schools) rather than only equipping youth and parents with coping skills. “A focus on how contexts can be changed to reduce cultural stressors shifts the responsibility away from youth and families by pointing to contextual-level intervention targets.” Participants’ reimagined realities supply these targets: greater Latinx representation, caring and trained teachers, bilingual resources, anti-discrimination rules and allies, and supportive infrastructure.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Incorporate youths’ and parents’ voices and hopes (aspirational capital) when designing contextual-level interventions.

Constraints¶

  • Targets are drawn from participants in six high schools in one Central Texas district with high Latinx and economically disadvantaged enrollment.

Target Learners¶

  • Latinx youth and their families in secondary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reducing cultural stressor experiences at school
  • Promoting student and parent engagement
  • Fostering student well-being and academic thriving

Affordances¶

  • Aspirational Capital Freedom Dreaming Framework

Claims¶

  • Youth and parents reimagined schools with more caring, understanding, and supportive teachers who know students personally and are trained to engage them respectfully [+W]
  • Youth and parents reimagined inclusive, student-engaged learning with bilingual resources, gradual transitions to English coursework, and hands-on activities [+W]
  • Youth reimagined schools with more Latinx teachers who would motivate them, provide role models, and make them feel understood [+W]
  • Latinx youths and parents reimagined ideal school realities centered on inclusive, welcoming, safe schools and schools free of cultural stressors with supportive allies [+W]
  • Youth and parents reimagined schools with more resources and supportive infrastructure, including transportation, everyday-life programs, and activities for economically constrained families [+W]
  • Youth and parents reimagined schools without discrimination, with clear rules for equitable treatment, and with adults and peers who speak up against discrimination and support them [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Tailor m-learning programmes to the cultural and economic realities of the local context
