Institutions should gently promote pre-registration hall and departmental Facebook groups while avoiding aggressive marketing, teaching or pastoral interventions in students' social space.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that, because Facebook helps new students settle in, higher education institutions could act as a catalyst by publicising existing accommodation/hall and departmental Facebook groups to pre-registration students. They pair this with a strong caution: aggressive marketing, teaching or pastoral interventions in a space students regard as 'theirs' are not recommended, and staff may help students recognise employability-related skills developed through informal Facebook learning. The article states that "higher education institutions could sensibly and gently act as a catalyst for this process for pre -registration students by promoting the existence of accommodation/hall and departmental Facebook groups."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional promotion must remain light-touch, since students view Facebook as a social space that is 'theirs' rather than an academic one.

Constraints¶

  • Aggressive marketing, teaching or pastoral interventions via Facebook are explicitly not recommended by the authors.
  • Making such technologies available does not guarantee every individual becomes more socially connected, and face-to-face friendships remain significant.

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduate students transitioning into university

Target Learning Goals¶

  • social integration into university life
  • informal peer learning and employability skill recognition

Claims¶

  • First-year students specifically joined Facebook before university registration to make new face-to-face friends at university while keeping in touch with friends and family from home. [+W]
  • Facebook acted as part of the 'social glue' helping first-year students settle into university life, though most students did not see it as crucial to overcoming loneliness. [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Professors integrating Facebook should leverage its social dimension while providing training and addressing privacy concerns.
