Integrate AI education throughout the K-12 curriculum via innovative approaches to integrative AI-science, AI-math, AI-language arts, and AI-social science education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article calls for "innovative approaches to integrative AI-science, AI-math, AI-language arts, and AI-social science education" so that students become adept at using AI across disciplines. It argues this integration will yield more effective approaches to, for example, "doing science," and will also address the practicalities of school curricula that are already exceedingly full.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An integrated model of K-12 AI education that infuses AI throughout the curriculum

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as a research need rather than a validated approach

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • using AI in science and math
  • using AI in language arts and social science

Claims¶

  • There is not currently a research foundation for K-12 AI education comparable to those for K-12 science and mathematics education [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop research programs across four areas of K-12 AI education: Learning & Teaching, Integration into STEM/Language Arts/Social Science, Professional Development, and Assessment
  • Develop frameworks for assessing students' AI competencies, including new instruments and AI-driven assessment technologies measuring conceptual knowledge, problem-solving practices, and ethical foundations
  • Create AI professional development for K-12 teachers addressing both AI content knowledge and AI pedagogical knowledge, for in-service and pre-service teachers
  • Embed AI literacy across disciplines by identifying intersections with existing content and using case studies, interdisciplinary modules, and collaborative projects
  • Breadcrumb strategy: embed small AI-native skill exercises, nudges, and prompts across multiple courses throughout the four-year curriculum
  • Integrate computational thinking into existing disciplinary teaching across grade bands rather than adding it on
