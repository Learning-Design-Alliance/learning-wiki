Evaluate chatbot-based digital tutors in real field settings rather than only laboratory experiments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors argue that evaluating a chatbot-based digital tutor in the field provides insights into how students actually interact with the technology over several months in a formal educational setting, which laboratory studies cannot capture. They note that prior chatbot research often used laboratory experimental studies, and that a laboratory setting would allow a control group but not insight into the actual learning process of several months.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Informed consent from participating students and alignment of the tutor's capabilities with the course structure, with new learning content published each week

Constraints¶

  • In the reported field study no control groups could be set up due to ethical considerations, since giving only a subset of students access was not an option

Target Learners¶

  • students in large-scale formal educational settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding actual long-term interaction with chatbot-based learning systems during a lecture period

Claims¶

  • In a four-month field study, students used the digital tutor extensively, initiating over 37,000 sessions and solving more than 80,000 formative questions [+W]
  • Qualitative interviews with 23 students support the survey results, indicating the digital tutor supported students' learning processes [+W]
