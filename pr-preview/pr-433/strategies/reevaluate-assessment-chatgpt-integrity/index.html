Reevaluate online assessment security and assessment formats in light of LLM performance on comprehension exams¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article argues that because ChatGPT can generate valid answers to comprehension exam questions within minutes, assumptions that minimally supervised online exams are secure may no longer hold. The authors suggest "Potential solutions include increased proctoring, reduced reliance on essay-based work, and the utilization of alternative assignment formats, such as videos or presentations". They also raise transitioning assessment toward effective, critical use of language models, such as instructing students to identify inaccuracies in ChatGPT-generated content.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recognition that comprehension answers need not be readily accessible online to be compromised

Constraints¶

  • Students are prohibited from using computers during conventional in-person examinations, so the concern applies specifically to computer-based or online exams

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary and higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Protecting the integrity of knowledge assessment in reading comprehension and similar subjects

Claims¶

  • GPT-3.5 passes Dutch VWO English reading comprehension exams with a mean grade of 7.3 with re-prompting, and 6.5 when only its first responses are scored [+M]
  • GPT-4 significantly outperforms the average Dutch student on VWO English reading comprehension, scoring 8.3 (March) and 8.1 (June) without re-prompting [+M]
  • GPT-3.5 performed worst on open one-point exam questions, while GPT-4 improved mainly on multiple-choice items [+W]
  • Bootstrapped self-consistency at temperature 1.0 flags potentially incorrect GPT-4 answers: about half of inconsistent responses were incorrect versus about 5% of consistent ones [+W]
  • Exam questions that GPT-4 answered inconsistently or incorrectly were harder for human examinees, who scored about 63–68% on them versus about 75% on the rest [+W]
  • ChatGPT shows high success on open-ended general chemistry exam questions but lower accuracy at the university level, and unverified copying of its answers is a reported risk [+W]
  • LLMs perform worse on complex, open-ended, or subjective assignments than on short, clearly formulated answers [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use bootstrapped self-consistency of LLM answers as an initial measure of exam item difficulty
  • Intermediate exams that require students to explain their own code to counter blind use of AI tools
  • Two Stage Exams
