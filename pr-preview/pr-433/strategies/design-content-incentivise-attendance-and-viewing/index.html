Design course content with sufficient incentive for both lecture attendance and recording viewing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that if instructors would like students to attend lectures as well as view the recordings, "the course content should be strategically designed so that there is sufficient incentive on both fronts." This addresses the conundrum of students expecting lecture capture to facilitate learning while instructors worry about waning attendance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors reflect on why students should be attending the face-to-face sessions

Constraints¶

  • With academia almost en route to becoming netflixised, the reason why students should be attending the face-to-face sessions needs to be carefully thought through

Target Learners¶

  • university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • attendance at face-to-face lectures and use of recorded lectures

Claims¶

  • Lecture capture has an adverse impact on lecture attendance, the most widely documented drawback across 20 of 71 reviewed articles [+W]
  • Lecture capture works well if and only if students use it as a supplementary resource rather than an alternative to live lectures [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement lecture recording with joint staff-student agreement on how the recording object should be used, and give students guidance on its use
  • Offer course lectures both live and as posted recordings to balance equity of access with schedule and accountability
