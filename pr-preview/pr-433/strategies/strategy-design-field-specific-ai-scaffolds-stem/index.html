Design AI scaffolds for STEM learning by linking learners' goals and interests to real scientific applications of AI¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes designing AI-featured scaffolding activities that connect learners' academic goals and interests to field-specific applications of AI, expanding the Relevance category of the ARCS model. This involves introducing learners to how AI features apply to their field-specific areas and tailoring scaffolding design to learners' prior knowledge and experience with AI. The article emphasizes that scaffolding design must be distinctly connected to learners' existing knowledge and prior experiences to motivate engagement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prior investigation of the learners' knowledge level for topics and experience with AI should proceed before designing scaffoldings.

Constraints¶

  • If scaffolding design cannot be distinctly connected to the learners' existing knowledge and prior experiences, it is difficult to motivate learners to engage with it.

Target Learners¶

  • College engineering students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Critical thinking and problem-solving skills in STEM

Claims¶

  • AI-featured methods significantly increased motivation across all four ARCS categories for computational problem-solving activities [+M]
  • AI-featured methods significantly increased motivation only in Attention and Confidence categories for essay-writing activities [+M]
