Apply real-time question detection to provide feedback on questioning across lesson phases¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests LLM-based question detection could provide real-time feedback during lectures, supporting adaptive teaching. "this could mean supporting science teachers in fostering more interactive classroom interactions by providing real-time feedback on their questioning in dif- ferent in different phases of the lesson as well as in different educational settings." It also envisions automatic question detection enhancing AI-based tutoring and enabling immediate assessment of teacher actions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • LLM technology capable of processing spoken classroom language in real time

Constraints¶

  • Generative LLMs still have a long way to go in effectively modelling productive educational dialogues in authentic classroom settings

Target Learners¶

  • Science teachers
  • Students in science classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Enriching authoritative modes of teaching with dialogic interactions
  • Adaptive learning and adaptive teaching

Claims¶

  • Few-shot chain-of-thought prompting outperforms zero-shot prompting for detecting teacher questions [+W]
  • LLMs struggle with nuanced coding schemes but binary question detection is feasible with simple prompts [+W]
  • GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo in a single-lesson question-detection test [+W]
  • Few-shot LLM approaches outperform standalone supervised ML approaches, yet hand-crafted rules remain relevant [+W]
  • In detecting teacher questions with zero-shot GPT-3.5-turbo, chain-of-thought prompting had slightly higher F1 and precision than Self-Ask but slightly lower recall and balanced accuracy [+W]
