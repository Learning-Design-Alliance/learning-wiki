Design learning tasks with high interdependence to promote student collaboration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the discussion the authors draw an educational implication: "teachers can promote bette r collaboration and mutual understanding among students by designing tasks with high interdependence, ultimately improving learning outcomes and fostering team spirit." The strategy rests on the study's finding that high task interdependence improved cooperative performance for both friend and stranger dyads, and is presented as applicable to work and educational team settings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tasks structured so that participants' success depends on coordinating responses with a partner, as in the HICT

Constraints¶

  • The study used a low-level motor-control key-pressing task; the authors state the generalizability of the ﬁndings may be limited to more complex real-world cooperative situations

Target Learners¶

  • students working in dyads or teams

Target Learning Goals¶

  • collaboration and mutual understanding

Claims¶

  • Task and interpersonal interdependence interact on intra-brain FC in DLPFC.R and SMG.R: strangers show higher FC under HICT, friends show higher FC under LICT [+W]
  • In HICT, friend dyads' key-pressing scores correlate positively with IBS in right DLPFC and right pars triangularis [+W]
  • High interdependent cooperation tasks yield higher key-pressing scores than low interdependent tasks for both friend and stranger dyads [+W]
  • Task and interpersonal interdependence interact on inter-brain synchrony in the right supramarginal gyrus [+W]
