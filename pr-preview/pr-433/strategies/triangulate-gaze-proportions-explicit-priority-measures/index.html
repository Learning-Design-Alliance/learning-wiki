Triangulate implicit gaze-proportion measures of teacher priority with established explicit self-report measures in future research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that future eye-tracking research on teacher priorities supplement the novel implicit gaze-proportion framework with an established explicit approach, such as self-report measures of teacher beliefs and priorities. The authors state that incorporating these preceding explicit measures with their implicit measure "would improve teacher decision-making research substantially."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to both eye-tracking recordings and explicit self-report instruments measuring teacher priorities in the same participants

Constraints¶

  • Stated as a next step for future research; the present study's gaze-proportion measure was not itself triangulated with an explicit measure

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring teacher priorities
  • teacher decision-making research
