Mitigation strategy: AI-literacy curricula with fact-checking modules, comprehensive educator training, and transparent documentation of AI use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The perspective article proposes multifaceted risk-mitigation strategies for responsibly integrating LMFMs in education. Within curricula, AI literacy should be "a core part of the curriculum" for both teachers and students, with modules on fact checking and ethical considerations to develop students' critical evaluation of AI-generated content, plus assessment strategies aligned with learning objectives in LMFM-enhanced environments. Educators need comprehensive training in AI literacy, ethical usage, data privacy, and fact-checking, and researchers should transparently document LMFM utilization and cross-reference AI outputs with established resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training systems that adapt to rapid changes in educational technology, including prolonged phases of continual further qualification.
  • Policies and resource allocation prioritizing equitable access to AI technologies for all students.
  • Engaging parents as part of the educational community to foster long-term acceptance.

Constraints¶

  • The perspective article states regular policy reviews and updates are needed to monitor AI developments for biases, indicating mitigation requires ongoing rather than one-time effort.

Target Learners¶

  • teachers and educators
  • students
  • educational researchers
  • parents as part of the educational community

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation of AI-generated content
  • AI literacy regarding capabilities and limitations of AI and LMFMs
  • ethical and privacy-aware AI use

Affordances¶

  • Lmfm Multimodal Foundation Model Framework

Claims¶

  • A perspective article warns that students risk overreliance on LMFM outputs and that overuse may impede critical thinking, research skills, and independent problem-solving [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Fund AI literacy initiatives integrating AI ethics into curricula and adapting assessments
  • Cultivate learners' critical AI literacy and integrity-focused group practices for GenAI-supported group work
  • Incorporate AI literacy into educational-leadership preparation curricula
  • Embed GenAI literacy and ethical use directly in nursing curricula rather than prohibiting it
  • Embed AI literacy as a credit-bearing module and design adaptive systems with transparent, user-controllable recommendation interfaces
  • Embed AI literacy across disciplines by identifying intersections with existing content and using case studies, interdisciplinary modules, and collaborative projects
  • Integrate AI literacy education as an integral component of first-year curriculum development rather than an add-on
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Design AI literacy tools with transparency in development and establish district policies for vetting AI tools
