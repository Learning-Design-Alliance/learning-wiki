Probe problem solving with a graduated suggestion sequence moving from pure reason to concrete imagery to objective experiment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

A diagnostic teaching and probing technique in which a student attempts a deliberately difficult problem unaided, and the experimenter then offers hints in three ordered tiers: "the first appealing to pure reason, the second to concrete imagery, and the third to immediate sensory experience or objective experiment." Which tier finally works serves as an index of the student's mental processes, and a required objective demonstration counts as a complete failure to solve.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Problems must be difficult enough that subjects cannot grasp the solution at a flash, so they halt, think, and become amenable to the experimenter's questions.

Constraints¶

  • The authors note introspective reports of inexperienced observers are apt to be quite untrustworthy; the method relies on graded suggestion efficacy rather than self-report alone.

Target Learners¶

  • secondary students solving plane-geometry problems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • diagnosing reasoning methods and mode of attack in mathematical problem solving

Claims¶

  • Good students reached a final correct solution in every case while poor students succeeded in only three of eighteen cases, and poor students were helped mainly by objective suggestions [+W]
