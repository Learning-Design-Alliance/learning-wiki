Provide students access to prevalent digital tools to develop fluency and confidence as an integral part of DL education programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that familiarity with commonly used tools contributes to DL performance, the article recommends that schools make prevalent tools available so students develop "the fluency and confidence needed for accomplishing tasks using digital tools" in everyday and context-specific problem-solving scenarios. It further stresses developing capacity to use digital tools students have not encountered, since devices and applications change rapidly.

Context¶
