Four guidelines for effective use of structural assessment in serious games¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes four guidelines to effectively use structural assessment in serious games: (1) Determine the appropriateness of the domain for structural assessment, (2) select an appropriate referent for the target group(s), (3) select the number of concepts needed for structural assessment, and (4) consider the analysis of the graphical knowledge representations to obtain in-depth information about the quality of the knowledge structures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Understanding of the domain characteristics and target group expertise levels

Constraints¶

  • The number of concepts should not be too high to avoid participant fatigue

Target Learners¶

  • Instructional designers and researchers using serious games

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Effective measurement of learning in serious games

Claims¶

  • Coherence scores decreased after game training for both novices and advanced learners [+W]
  • Focal node analysis showed advanced learners' central concepts converged with experts' after the game, while novices' remained divergent [+W]
  • Structural assessment measures domain understanding differently from verbal assessment [+W]
  • Verbal assessment showed novices improved on procedural items and advanced learners improved on declarative items [+W]
