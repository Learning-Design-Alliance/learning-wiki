Education journal editors adopt staged TOP-aligned transparency policies, enforced with the TOP Statement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that editors, publishers, and editorial committees at educational psychology journals adopt transparency policies in stages, emulating higher-rated journals in other branches of psychology. Editors can "filter policies by the topic that is easiest to engage with", modeling Level 2 mandates for materials and code sharing while adopting Level 1 disclosure for data transparency. Use of the TOP Statement, a standardized disclosure questionnaire, can simplify enforcement and complies with funder expectations such as IES's SEER standards.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to the TOP Factor database of journal policies (www.topfactor.org) and the TOP Statement questionnaire as templates for policy language and enforcement

Constraints¶

  • The article notes materials and code sharing has fewer complications than data sharing, so data policies may need to start at Level 1 disclosure rather than mandated transparency

Target Learners¶

  • journal editors and editorial committees in educational psychology
  • publishers and academic societies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing journal transparency and reproducibility standards
  • earning recognition through TOP Factor ratings

Affordances¶

  • Five Stage Culture Change Roadmap Open Science

Claims¶

  • Offering Open Science Badges is associated with increased uptake of data and materials sharing and preregistration [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Editors and reviewers should reward transparency about limitations so researchers are not incentivized to overstate implications
  • Publish an alternative note explaining why data or materials cannot be shared when confidentiality prevents it
  • Adopt open science practices—data and code sharing, pre-registration, and Registered Reports—to improve reproducibility and replicability
