Strategies

During a study, researchers should visit regularly, give back to the school community, minimize classroom disruption, and share initial data for feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

This strategy keeps researchers present and useful throughout data collection: visit regularly not only to collect data but to continue conversations, get to know the school environment, and give back; be unintrusive with a light footprint; and share initial data in staff meetings, inviting feedback and questions for later phases. Uncapher's team made an estimated 20-30 site visits per school, presented Brain Day events, and shared learning-sciences findings applicable in classrooms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Well-trained research staff who know the intention to have as light a footprint as possible
  • Advance communication of logistical details such as who would be in the school, when, and for how long

Constraints¶

  • Having outsiders in the classroom can be distracting and time-consuming, so intrusion must be minimized

Target Learners¶

  • elementary and middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • metacognitive awareness and application of neuroscience findings to learning

Related Strategies¶

  • After a study, present final data in school-useful forms, remain available for ongoing conversations, and plan carefully to avoid data overload
  • Before a study, researchers should present the proposal to district administration, hold conversations with administrators and teachers, and host parent events to build buy-in
  • Multi-stakeholder analytics partnership using improvement science with strict data governance
  • Pair movement-based analysis with temporal analysis and discuss camera intrusiveness when collecting detailed data
