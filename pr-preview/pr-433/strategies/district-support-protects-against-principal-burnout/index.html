District and collegial social support may protect principals against burnout in high-demand contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review identifies social support from colleagues (including other principals in networks) and supervisors as correlated with lower burnout and as buffering the negative association between burnout and workload. Participating in district-sponsored professional learning networks was associated with lower burnout. Conversely, lack of central office support was associated with burnout. This suggests district-level support structures and principal networks may serve a protective role.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Support must come from colleagues, principal networks, or central office; community-wide support alone may not be protective

Constraints¶

  • One study found support from the broader community was related to higher prevalence of burnout, hypothesized to reflect principals shouldering community stress

Target Learners¶

  • school principals
  • district administrators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • preventing or reducing principal burnout
  • sustaining principal wellbeing and retention

Claims¶

  • Teachers' effectiveness increases at a greater rate in supportive, collegial working environments and when they accumulate experience in the same grade level, subject, or district [+M]
  • Cohort-based principal preparation is associated with program completion, perceived preparedness, and supportive collegial environments [+M]
  • Cognitive reappraisal protects teachers from emotional exhaustion and burnout, while expressive suppression and surface acting are negatively linked to teaching effectiveness [+M]
