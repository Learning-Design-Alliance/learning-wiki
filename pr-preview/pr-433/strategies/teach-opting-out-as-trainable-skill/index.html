Teach opting out as a trainable self-regulation skill within curricula¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review urges educators to treat opting out as a trainable skill with pedagogical benefits rather than a sign of low motivation. It recommends "a staged" approach to integrating the opting-out skill into curricula, aligned with learners' developing capabilities for strategic choice, to enhance "students' strategic effort regulation". Educators are also called to foster classroom environments that support opting out as a proactive, positive strategy rather than a weakness.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Integration demands "a deliberate and thoughtful approach, recognizing it as a trainable skill with pedagogical benefits, yet considering potential pitfalls"

Constraints¶

  • Teaching must align "with the developing capabilities of learners to make strategic choices"; the review flags potential pitfalls including excessive opting out and harm to long-term learning

Target Learners¶

  • students in learning and assessment contexts, from primary school to higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • strategic effort regulation, effective use of opt-out options, and preparation for real-life decisions

Affordances¶

  • Region Of Proximal Learning Opting Out

Claims¶

  • Opting out of tasks can indicate effective effort regulation, not merely low motivation (Sidi & Ackerman, 2024 review) [+W]
  • Attention and self-regulation skills are trainable in children, with reported behavioral and neural changes across curricula and intervention programs [+W]
  • Allowing opt-out options improves output-bound accuracy in adults, primary-school children, and preschoolers [+W]
  • Opting out can improve thinking efficiency by cutting wasted effort on doomed attempts [+W]
  • People underuse opt-out options, and explicit presentation substantially increases their use [+W]
