Teach even-odd digit-pair-to-image encoding as a memory aid in educational and training programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends its findings for programs using digit-image mnemonics as a memory aid: trainees learn a conversion table of 100 unique images for 2-digit combinations (00–99) based on phonological or semantic similarity, then practice converting digit pairs into vivid images. The authors state the results "suggest that the encoding of even-odd digit pairs is effective in creating vivid and distinctive mental images, which can enhance memory performance".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A conversion table associating 2-digit combinations with images (e.g., via phonological similarity in Chinese), and extended practice (the study used 22 days with a minimum of 6 h per day)

Constraints¶

  • Benefits were largely specific to trained tasks with limited generalization; transfer effects to domains such as problem-solving, decision-making and creativity remain untested

Target Learners¶

  • children in primary or middle school; memory-training program participants

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved short-term memory for digit sequences (and number-noun paired associates)

Affordances¶

  • Encoding Pattern Vs Processing Efficiency Models

Claims¶

  • 22-day digit-image mnemonic training improves children's short-term digit memory relative to a no-contact control group [+W]
  • Mnemonic training yields limited far-transfer: only the number-noun pairs measure improved, persisting at 4-month follow-up [~W]
  • Validation analyses find no generally reduced neural activity after mnemonic training, with increased alpha power uncorrelated with memory gains [~W]
  • The training-induced odd-even P200 and theta effects predict the magnitude of children's memory improvement [+W]
  • After mnemonic training, children show enhanced P200 amplitude for even- versus odd-position digits during encoding [+W]
  • After mnemonic training, children show increased left anterior frontal theta power for even- versus odd-position digits during encoding [+W]
