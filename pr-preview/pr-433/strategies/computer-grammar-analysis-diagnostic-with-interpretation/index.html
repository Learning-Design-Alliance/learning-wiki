Use computer grammar analysis as a diagnostic tool for teachers, paired with interpretive information and holistic teacher assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that computer analysis of vocabulary use and syntax serve as a diagnostic tool for teachers who lack the time or linguistic knowledge to analyse syntactical patterns themselves. Because raw statistics alone are not actionable, information stating whether features such as average sentence length or connective percentages are desirable would need to be supplied alongside the numbers. Teachers must still assess genre details qualitatively so that assessment includes all elements contributing to effective writing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Statistical output must be accompanied by information stating whether features are desirable or not
  • Assessment must consider writing holistically as well as grammatically

Constraints¶

  • The computer cannot ascribe national curriculum levels from grammatical maturity alone; without holistic assessment it makes superficial judgements based on surface features

Target Learners¶

  • primary teachers assessing Key Stage 2 pupils' writing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • formative assessment and feedback on children's written grammar

Claims¶

  • Counting the connective 'and' separately from other basic connectives highlights immature writing and identifies a writer's stage [+W]
  • Mean sentence length indicates both insecure and secure full-stop use, with roughly 12 to 16 words per sentence the aim for mature narrative writing [+W]
  • The ratio of mean sentence length to commas was the most useful computer test for discriminating maturity level in children's writing [+W]
  • Story length correlates with National Curriculum attainment level in children's Key Stage 2 narrative writing, with roughly 300 words needed for Level 4 and 400 or more for Level 5 [+W]
  • Harpin's type-token ratio of the first 100 words indicates vocabulary diversity, but the rate of TTR drop after the first 100 words does not differentiate attainment levels [+W]
