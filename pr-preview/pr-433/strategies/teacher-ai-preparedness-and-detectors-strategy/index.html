Prepare teachers to use AI expeditiously and provide AI-text detectors for evaluation, alongside updated academic-integrity rules¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's forward-looking recommendations for institutions adopting AI tools in courses. The authors state that "teachers should be prepared to use AI expeditiously and that detectors for text generated by AI should be available for evaluation on using this powerful tool." They also report that a third of surveyed students did not know about modifications to the academic-integrity regulations, supporting their call for "urgent modifications to the code of academic integrity and the application of new ethics for the use of AI." The strategy frames institutional readiness, detection tooling, and student awareness of integrity rules as prerequisites for responsible AI integration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional modification of academic-integrity codes to cover AI use
  • Availability of AI-text detectors for evaluation purposes

Constraints¶

  • The article reports students' awareness of regulation changes was low: a third of the surveyed population did not know about the modifications

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students and their instructors in AI-integrated courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible and academically honest use of generative AI in coursework

Related Strategies¶

  • Design task-sensitive institutional AI policies rather than blanket permitted/prohibited distinctions
  • Institutions should adopt a balanced AI-integration approach including AI literacy courses and instructional guidelines
  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
  • Institutional integrity programs combining ethical AI-use guidelines, clear definitions of dishonesty, and risk-awareness education
  • Position AI detection tools as one component within a broader institutional decision-making process, with regularly updated benchmarks
  • Institutions should adopt clear, prominently displayed AI policies and cultivate AI literacy for ethical, employability-oriented use
