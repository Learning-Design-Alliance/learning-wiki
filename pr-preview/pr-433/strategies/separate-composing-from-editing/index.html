For children, keep editing separate from composing: revise during writing, edit grammar after content is complete¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends sequencing writing support in distinct stages for young writers: "for children, editing is best left until after the text content and organisation has been completed, as preoccupation with achieving correct grammar (including spelling) can interrupt and stifle children's flow of ideas (Smith, 1982)." HARRY implements this by separating the writing process into a composing/revising stage, a grammar-editing stage, and a spelling/technical stage in MS Word. Teachers and tool designers should likewise structure revision before editing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Writing support must be sequenced so grammar and spelling correction follows completion of content and organisation

Constraints¶

  • The article notes word processors alone do not increase children's willingness or ability to revise and edit; they need supplementary strategies

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school children composing narratives

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Maintaining flow of ideas during composition
  • Improving grammar, punctuation and spelling through staged editing

Claims¶

  • Reflecting on vocabulary and sentence revision during the composing stage temporarily degraded children's use of full stops and basic connectives [+W]
  • HARRY's second-stage editing prompts improved James' sentence constructions, reducing 'and' overuse and improving vocabulary variety [+W]
  • HARRY-assisted stories received more 'good' CHECK TEXT grammar and style reports than control stories in the case studies [+W]
  • Editing suggestions restored but did not fully correct Sophie's punctuation: her final draft showed no improvement in full-stop use over control [+W]
