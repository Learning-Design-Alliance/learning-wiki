Apply Borup et al.'s video-versus-text feedback research when designing feedback in colleges shifting to digital¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article states that it "describes the value of their research, how it can be applied, some limitations, and future areas of research in a time where colleges are shifting to digital." The strategy is for institutions and instructors moving instruction online to draw on the Borup, West, and Thomas (2015) findings when choosing between text and video feedback formats, treating that research as an applicable evidence base rather than a one-off study.

Claims¶

  • In Borup et al.'s (2015) blended-course study, students and instructors perceived text and video feedback as equally valuable on quality and delivery, but valued text's efficiency over video's affective benefits [~M]
  • Research has shown that video feedback can help increase conversational and affective communication [+W]
  • Borup et al. (2015) found video feedback more convenient for student support and text-based feedback more convenient for exploratory feedback [~W]
  • Despite video feedback's benefits, instructors and students might prefer text-based feedback [~W]
  • Providing video feedback is easier than ever, making its cost-benefit question timely [+W]
