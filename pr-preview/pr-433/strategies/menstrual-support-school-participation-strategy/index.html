Address menstrual-related barriers to participation in school: toilet access, product provision, stigma, and focus difficulties¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes developing school-, family-, and community-based interventions to mitigate the educational impact of menstrual symptoms, informed by mechanisms identified through further research. A patient and public involvement group of adolescent girls currently in secondary school reported concrete barriers: "difﬁculties focusing during menstruation, restricted toilet access due to school behavioural policies, embarrassment and stigma, and insufﬁcient period product provision". Schools designing support should therefore consider practical provision (toilets, products) alongside informational and stigma-related support.

Constraints¶

  • The barriers listed come from a patient and public involvement group of adolescent girls, not from a tested intervention

Target Learners¶

  • adolescent girls and people who menstruate in secondary school (age 15/16 in this cohort)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • school attendance and engagement
  • educational attainment at examinations

Claims¶

  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding is associated with lower GCSE scores and lower odds of achieving five standard passes [+W]
  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding in adolescence is associated with more school absence, including persistent absence of 10% or more [+W]
  • Menstrual pain shows a weaker and less consistent association with educational attainment than heavy or prolonged bleeding [+W]
  • Severe menstrual pain in adolescence is associated with more school absence, including persistent absence [+W]
