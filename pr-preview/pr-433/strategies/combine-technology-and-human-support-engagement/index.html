Combine technological and human-level factors to promote engagement with mobile interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article argues engagement is critical because "no mobile intervention can be effective if students do not engage with it." It recommends combining technological and human-level factors: participatory design with learners, fostering children's enjoyment of the app, and integrating support from teachers, parents, or peers — for instance by "integrating reflections about the mobile intervention intolessons" and helping with technical difficulties. Research in mHealth shows interventions are more effective with human support.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Attention to perceived usefulness and ease of use (Technology Acceptance Model), plus enjoyment for younger students

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions "against a technocratic approach that seeks to solve the challenge of engagement through technological solutions alone"

Target Learners¶

  • school students, including children as the target group

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained engagement with mobile learning interventions
  • self-regulated learning

Claims¶

  • Mobile reminder prompts causally increased children's vocabulary-app study on prompted days (PROMPT micro-randomized trial) [+W]
  • Breathing exercise showed no main effect on children's negative affect or relaxation but increased relaxation on higher-worry days (UPWIND trial) [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Consider students' phase of interest when implementing UVIs, and consider combining them with interventions fostering beliefs that interest can develop
  • Prompt self-generated planning rather than simple reminders to protect student agency and avoid overreliance
