Provide AI-free proctored spaces so some take-home work is verifiably unassisted¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article proposes institutional interventions such as AI-free study halls: lightly proctored workspaces where students check in and out to complete portions of take-home assignments using nothing beyond simple word processors. This is offered because faculty designing AI-proof assignments alone cannot bear the large-scale loss of the ability to assess homework, so at least some take-home assignments need a setting where unassisted completion is verifiable. The proposal is framed as possibly necessary rather than as a tested solution.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional provision of lightly proctored workspaces with check-in and check-out procedures.

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as a speculative institutional intervention that might be necessary, not as an approach evaluated in the study.

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students completing take-home writing assignments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • verifiable unassisted academic writing practice

Claims¶

  • Educators run more proctored AI-free assessments conditionally on compromised outcomes, and reject AI detectors based on classroom experience of false positives [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Restrict: preserve AI-free cognitive sanctuaries where unassisted capability is practiced and assessed
  • Pair AI-permitted take-home assignments with supervised modifications, oral defense, prediction, and transfer tasks to verify understanding
  • Redesign assessment toward supervised, oral, process-based and authentic formats to verify students' own reasoning under GenAI
  • Replace detector-centred assessment with a four-stage process-oriented model: declared starting point, versioned drafting, process artefacts, and targeted oral verification
