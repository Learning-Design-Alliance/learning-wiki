Promote social practices supporting mutual insight and address reflexivity dissonance through targeted interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that facilitators address potential dissonance between a student's reflexive profile and the demands of an online learning environment through targeted interventions. Specifically, "the social practices that were seen in this study to support the development of mutual insight within asynchronous online discussions could be speciﬁcally promoted to students", with facilitators supporting students in developing and making use of these practices and drawing attention to the reflexivity that underpins them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Facilitators must be available to support students in developing and using the practices

Constraints¶

  • The study concerned learners who had already adjusted to the reflexive demands of many aspects of their learning environments

Target Learners¶

  • postgraduate students in fully online Masters degrees

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained student engagement and mutual insight in online learning

Claims¶

  • Collective reflexivity centred on shared goals underpinned mutual insight in online discussion boards [+W]
  • Fractured and restricted forms of collective reflexivity were linked to student disengagement on joint tasks [+W]
  • Online postgraduate students exercised several modes of reflexivity concurrently rather than displaying one dominant mode [+W]
  • Uncertainty in challenging tasks was linked to meta-reflexive deliberation about improving one's own learning [+W]
  • Archer's model of agency helps explain how interviewed online students engaged in their learning [+W]
