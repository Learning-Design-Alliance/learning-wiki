Explore the 5R affordances combined with evidence-based teaching practices to develop OER-enabled pedagogies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that faculty, students, and instructional designers go beyond adopting cheaper textbooks: they should use OER together with evidence-based teaching and learning practices and explore "the implications of the 5R affordances in order to find novel, OER-enabled pedagogies" if they aim to radically improve student outcomes. The 5R activities are retain, reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute — the permissions open licensing grants on teaching, learning, and research materials.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • OER whose open licensing grants the 5R permissions
  • combination with evidence-based teaching and learning practices

Constraints¶

  • The article states cost savings and gap-closing benefits of OER adoption will likely decrease over time as price differences shrink, so affordability alone is not a long-term strategy

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students, including 2-year degree students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • dramatically improving student outcomes such as completion and success for all students

Claims¶

  • OER efficacy studies are frequently confounded by simultaneous changes in pedagogy or delivery method. [~W]
  • Synthesized studies of OER in college courses found academic outcomes comparable to traditional textbooks and positive perceptions among students and instructors [+M]
  • Across 2002-2018, OER efficacy research consistently shows that OER does not harm student learning, with a small positive impact. [+M]
