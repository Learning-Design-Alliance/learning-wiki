Train instructional designers in prompt engineering, articulating prompt objectives, context, and example outputs to improve GenAI-assisted design¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests that biases in ChatGPT-generated content can be mitigated by training instructional designers in effective prompt engineering. It cites the recommendation that "good engineering prompts clearly articulate the objective of the prompt, provide context, and suggest examples of desired outputs." Prompt engineering can also extend to supporting students' help-seeking, remediating tasks, and practicing new skills.

Claims¶

  • Instructional designers' main challenges with ChatGPT are output quality (53.8%), tool limitations (42.3%), and faculty unpreparedness (3.8%) [+W]
  • Pessimistic Evaluators' foremost concern is misleading, inaccurate, or biased ChatGPT-generated information [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Introduce prompt-engineering training before students use Gen AI for structured self-study techniques
  • Train teachers in prompt engineering, evaluating AI-generated responses, ethical and privacy considerations, and integrating AI outputs into pedagogical decision-making
  • Teach genAI operations, prompt engineering, failure modes, and supervised overreliance before graded genAI-assisted writing
  • Provide teacher guidance on GenAI use, prompt engineering, ethics, and targeted classroom activities for translation
