Shared book reading as a potential intervention for increasing lexical diversity in young children's learning environments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 5 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 3 of 5 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends caregiver shared book reading as a practical route to enriching the lexical diversity of young children's input. It states that parent speech generated in the context of shared book reading "may be a key factor of observed individual differences in language ability among young children and also a potential intervention for increasing lexical diversity in the learning environments of children." Because reading frequency varies systematically with socio-economic status, the strategy targets caregivers who read less often.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Caregivers must actually read picture books to children; the article notes book reading is common but by no means universal and varies with socio-economic status.

Constraints¶

  • The article estimates text contributes only about 3% of a typical child's yearly input at one book a day and about 6% at two books a day, though a larger share of child-directed speech for low-SES children.

Target Learners¶

  • infants and young children read to by caregivers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary growth through increased lexical diversity of input

Affordances¶

  • Lexical Diversity Input Drives Learnability

Claims¶

  • At matched token sizes, samples of picture-book text almost never contain fewer unique word types than samples of child-directed speech [+W]
  • The greater diversity of book input arises mainly because different books sample the language more broadly than different conversations do [+W]
  • Individual picture books typically contain more unique words at a given sample size than individual conversations [+W]
  • The text of children's picture books contains more unique word types than a length-matched sample of child-directed speech [+W]
  • Dialogic Reading Improves Language Outcomes [+M]
  • Joint Book Reading Predicts Literacy Success [+M]
