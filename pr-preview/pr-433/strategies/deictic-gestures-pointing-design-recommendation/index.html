Use deictic pointing gestures by the lecturer to integrate the lecturer with relevant illustrative material in educational videos¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy implements the article's core design recommendation: lecturers in educational videos should point at the relevant parts of accompanying pictorial material, because "Social cues should be implemented in a way to create illustrative associations within the learning material." Deictic gestures guided attention to learning-relevant visuals, raised social presence and attention, lowered mental effort, and improved retention and (in Experiment 2) transfer. It applies to video-based instruction with a visible human lecturer plus additional illustrations, such as the MOOC-style videos the authors describe.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The video must contain additional pictorial material whose relevant parts the lecturer can point at, and pointing should occur at the moment the information is verbally presented

Constraints¶

  • When the lecturer is not the only focus of the video, social cues not interacting with the material may not foster learning because learners split attention between lecturer and illustrations; the article warns social cues should be implemented carefully in such cases

Target Learners¶

  • university students in multimedia learning settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retention and transfer of factual lecture content presented in educational videos

Claims¶

  • Lecturers performing deictic gestures are perceived as more learning-facilitating, credible, and engaging (agent-persona perception, Experiment 2) [+W]
  • Deictic gestures of a human lecturer in an educational video enhance retention performance compared with no gestures and with beat gestures [+W]
  • Deictic gestures enhance social presence and attention toward the lecturer in both experiments [+W]
  • In Experiment 2, deictic gestures also improved parasocial interaction facets beyond attention, including accuracy of persona-relevant information and descriptively higher evaluation [+W]
  • Meaningful pointing gestures produced higher retention scores than beat gestures (review attributes this to Beege et al., 2020) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future studies should integrate the temporary instructor more strongly with relevant learning materials and balance instructor-present and instructor-absent slides
