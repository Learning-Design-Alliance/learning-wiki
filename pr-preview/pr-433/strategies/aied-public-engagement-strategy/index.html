Engage the AI in Education community actively with the public and key stakeholders to mitigate the in-credible AI effect¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that the "AI in Education community should attempt to be more actively engaged with the public to help mitigate such an effect", including providing training opportunities on AI basics and creating shared understanding among stakeholders such as educators, the public, and developers. They argue academics should be given time and encouragement to provide accessible research evidence and guidance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Accessible research evidence and guidance for public, practitioners, and developers

  • A detailed discussion of mitigation is stated to be outside the paper's scope

Target Learners¶

  • general public
  • educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • public understanding of AI in Education
  • accurate evaluation of educational research evidence

Claims¶

  • Composite credibility scores differ significantly between the AI frame and both the neuroscience and educational psychology frames [+W]
  • Regression models identify attitudes toward educational psychology's scientific adherence as the main predictor of credibility judgments [+W]
  • Familiarity with the article topic correlates positively with bias against credibility of framed evidence [+W]
  • Framing educational research evidence within AI reduces its perceived credibility compared with neuroscience and educational psychology frames (the in-credible AI effect) [+W]
  • The public perceives AI as less helpful for understanding how children learn, less adherent to scientific methods, and less prestigious than neuroscience and educational psychology [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Cross-stakeholder policy, research, and practice actions for collaborative AI initiatives in education
  • Foster interdisciplinary collaboration and actively include student perspectives in institutional GenAI responses.
  • Involve domain experts throughout the AI safety evaluation pipeline, especially for defining unsafe content
  • Involve key stakeholders directly in defining privacy and designing LA systems
