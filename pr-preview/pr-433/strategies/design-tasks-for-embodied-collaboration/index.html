Design learning tasks for educational technologies with possibilities for embodied collaboration in mind¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that learning goals should influence the kinds of embodied and collaborative moves that unfold, and that "designing learning tasks for educational technologies with possibilities for embodied collaboration in mind may best leverage these technologies." They also advise researchers to consider which portions of their data may be richest and best suited for MAET, and to match multimodal data streams to their system's affordances.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning goals that can be served by embodied and collaborative moves
  • Technologies supporting shared embodied interaction

Constraints¶

  • Different learning technologies have different affordances for capturing multimodal data (e.g., body tracking, first-person recording)

Target Learners¶

  • Learners using collaborative immersive educational technologies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Collaborative mathematical reasoning through embodied interaction

Affordances¶

  • Maet Multimodal Analysis Embodied Technologies

Claims¶

  • Collaborative gestures are associated with higher performance than individual gestures, per studies the review cites [+W]
  • In shared holographic AR, scale, positioning of the representation, and body positioning emerged as important design considerations [+W]
  • In the motion capture game The Hidden Village, pose creation spurred gesturally embodied argumentation expressed more in gesture than in precise mathematical speech [+W]
  • In shared VR, teacher-learners showed a recurring multimodal sequence of discussion before embodied action on virtual objects [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design CSCL tasks aiming for cognitive achievements beyond the reach of individual team members without computer supports
  • Set up closed, goal-oriented tasks with a limited number of possible outcomes to encourage negotiation of meaning in CMC
