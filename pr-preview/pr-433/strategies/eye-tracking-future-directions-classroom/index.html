Future eye tracking research in education should examine interacting pairs and groups, emotions, and combinations with other process measures¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The author identifies three future trends: widening focus from individual eye movement behavior to interacting pairs and groups, since teacher-student interaction and peer influence are elementary aspects of classroom teaching; advancing understanding of the role of emotions in learning and teaching, where very little is known about the relationship between emotions and eye movements in learning contexts; and combining eye movement recordings with head, body, effector, psychophysiological, and brain activity measures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Analyzing simultaneous eye movement behavior of pairs or groups poses novel data analysis challenges that must be solved; multichannel data collection, analysis, and interpretation carry challenges.

Constraints¶

  • Combinations of different measures potentially provide more information than eye movement data alone, but there are challenges in collecting, analyzing, and interpreting multichannel data.

Target Learners¶

  • students and teachers in classroom contexts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding social interaction, emotional and cognitive processes underpinning learning

Claims¶

  • Combining eye tracking with other process measures such as machine vision features provides useful information about student engagement [+W]
  • Analyzing fluctuation of engagement over time rather than averaging on-task and off-task instances across a lecture provides novel insights into student behavior [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Complement outcome measures with continuous process measures to assess social effects and enable adaptive pedagogical agents
  • Collect qualitative data on learners' experiment-design decisions and actions to understand the processes underlying scaffold effects
