Sequence collaboration and individual work on the same ITS problems, then compare error and hint process data across conditions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' methodological strategy is to analyze "the hints and errors of students over time from process data" to understand why a combined collaborative-individual condition performs differently, looking at error rates "both when working collaboratively and individually" and hint requests relative to the other conditions. This complements pre/post learning-gain comparisons with within-activity behavioral traces rather than relying on outcome scores alone.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Process logging of hint requests and errors must be available from the learning system.

Constraints¶

  • The authors present these analyses as an initial exploration of a possibly valuable combination, not a settled account of when combining is beneficial.

Target Learners¶

  • elementary students in ITS-supported mathematics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding how collaboration and individual practice interact during problem-solving

Claims¶

  • Students in the combined condition tended to make fewer errors and request fewer hints over time than students in individual-only or collaborative-only conditions [+W]
  • The combined condition had higher learning gains than individual-only and collaborative-only conditions, but only among 4th grade students [+W]
  • Error rates per completed problem-solving step did not significantly differ between conditions [-W]
