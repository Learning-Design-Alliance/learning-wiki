Strategies

Partner with and train in-service local teachers to co-instruct AI workshops and become ambassadors for AI education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Rather than having researchers teach students directly, the authors recruited 11 in-service middle school teachers from around the United States and trained them to co-teach the workshops. Teachers served simultaneously as recruiters, professional-development trainees, instructors, and teaching assistants. Daily end-of-day sessions let teachers reflect and shape upcoming lessons, and teacher feedback from the first workshop round was implemented in the second, with the expectation that teachers would carry AI education into their own classrooms afterwards.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Compensation and structured professional development before and during the workshops
  • Mechanisms (daily reflection sessions) for teachers to modify lessons and introduce new ideas between workshop iterations

Constraints¶

  • Recruited from a roster of 59 teachers who expressed interest via the Amazon Future Engineer mailing list, so participating teachers were self-selected

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students recruited by their own teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI literacy delivered by classroom teachers rather than external researchers

Related Strategies¶

  • Program-leader recommendations for strengthening teacher apprenticeship experiences
  • Recruit residency candidates locally from the communities they will teach in, with LEA partners participating in admissions
  • Develop high-retention pathways — Grow Your Own programs and teacher residencies — to build and diversify local teacher supply
  • Design future AI curricula to fit the school day, centering student and teacher needs for K-12 in-person and virtual learning
