Provide explicit prompting instruction and expert scaffolding before or during novice LLM activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Based on the observed limits of trial-and-error prompting, the authors recommend that educators guide trial-and-error efforts through explicit instruction: encouraging more effective prompts using expert-informed techniques, deliberate control over LLM outputs, metacognitive planning of which phases suit LLM support, and solid subject-specific knowledge for evaluating outputs. They state directly that "Future studies should replicate this intervention, first providing students with basic information about prompting and its principles, in order to examine whether explicit guidance enhances their ability to engage effectively with LLMs." The recommendation responds to students' limited use of advanced strategies and their overgeneralized conclusions about model incapability.
