Use GenAI as a reflective prompt in learning design, not as a shortcut for efficiency¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

A specific implementable practice the article recommends: educators can strengthen learning design by prompting GenAI in ways that provoke their own reflection rather than outsourcing design decisions. The implications for practice state that "Educators can strengthen learning design by using GenAI as a reflective prompt, not as a shortcut for efficiency". This positions GenAI as a partner in the educator's thinking rather than an automated substitute.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators retain ownership of design decisions and treat GenAI output as input to their own reflection

Constraints¶

  • The article does not report empirical testing of this strategy; it is offered as a practice implication of the discourse analysis

Target Learners¶

  • higher education educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • robust constructive alignment of outcomes, activities and assessment

Related Strategies¶

  • Reposition educational developers as active pedagogical collaborators in constructive alignment rather than technology implementers
  • Scaffolded AI integration with teacher training and reflective tasks to reduce over-reliance
  • Train teachers in prompt engineering, evaluating AI-generated responses, ethical and privacy considerations, and integrating AI outputs into pedagogical decision-making
  • Embed metacognitive scaffolds in GenAI search systems — prompting sub-question formulation, guiding output comparison, and encouraging reflection on AI-user knowledge discrepancies
