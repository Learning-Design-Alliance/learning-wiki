Teach prompting and AI literacy as epistemic practice with structured guidance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The review treats prompting in chemistry as an epistemic act, not merely a technical skill: a useful prompt specifies chemical constraints, relevant representations, assumptions, and adequacy criteria. It reports that "Preservice chemistry teachers initially relied heavily on copy-and-paste prompting and benefited from structured guidance", and that semester-long evidence suggests prompts become more purposeful with repeated instruction. AI literacy extends beyond tool operation to knowing when not to use GenAI, recognizing uncertainty, checking provenance, and understanding model- and version-dependent behavior.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Structured guidance and repeated instruction across a course
  • Ethical discussion tied to actual chemical tasks rather than generic policy topics

Constraints¶

  • Teacher preparation is especially important because teachers must make these verification decisions for their own work and model them for students

Target Learners¶

  • Preservice chemistry teachers
  • University chemistry students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Purposeful prompting with chemical constraints
  • AI literacy including provenance checking and uncertainty recognition

Affordances¶

  • Verification Centered Johnstone 3P Genai Architecture

Claims¶

  • Teacher-AI design collaboration faces recurring challenges including prompt literacy, curriculum misalignment, epistemic uncertainty, and explainability barriers [+W]
