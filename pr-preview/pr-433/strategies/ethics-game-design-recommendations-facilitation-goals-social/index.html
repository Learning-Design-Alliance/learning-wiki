Design and implement ethics games with attention to facilitation, clarity of instructions, alignment of goals, and social dynamics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Building on the identified dilemmas, the authors propose recommendations for designing and implementing games in ethics education, "addressing aspects such as facilitation, clarity of instructions, alignment of goals, and social dynamics". The recommendations aim to balance engaging gameplay with meaningful integration of ethics-related objectives and to support educators and designers in creating effective, ethically informed game-based learning experiences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implementing game-based learning in ethics teaching is resource-intensive and requires careful integration with broader instructional designs and complementary pedagogical methods

Constraints¶

  • The article states the effectiveness of explicit goal setting varies across stages of knowledge acquisition within moral expertise development, limiting a one-size-fits-all approach

Target Learners¶

  • educators and designers of game-based ethics education for adult learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • meta-ethical competencies and meaningful, ethically informed learning experiences

Claims¶

  • Six pedagogical dilemmas emerge when integrating game-based learning into ethics education: rules, social modes, identity framing, systems nudging, personalized design, and ethics positioning [+W]
  • When ethics games lack explicit cues distinguishing ethical from unethical choices, some players fail to recognize the intended dilemma and the games risk encouraging unethical behavior [+W]
  • Adult learners report both benefits and challenges when playing ethics board games, and these cluster into 21 benefit and 21 challenge themes across the forethought, performance, and reflection phases of self-regulated learning [+W]
