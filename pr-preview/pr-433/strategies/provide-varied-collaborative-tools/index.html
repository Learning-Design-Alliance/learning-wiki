Strategies

Provide and structure online collaborative tools that support both task coordination and socio-emotional exchange.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Provide students with a varied set of synchronous and asynchronous tools (e.g., discussion forums, chat, virtual campus tools) that sustain communication, collaboration and coordination, since the article found these tools positively influence both student interaction in work groups and intra-group emotional support. Tools should integrate social resources and support problem and project solving, considering the cognitive and social aspects of positive interaction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must have access to effective tools for collaboration, synchronous or asynchronous, that allow for fluent interaction and peer support.

Constraints¶

  • The article found a medium-low effect size for the influence of tools on interaction and emotional support, so tools support but do not substitute for interaction and emotional bonding.

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education students in online collaborative courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Collaborative learning
  • Group communication and coordination

Claims¶

  • Students communicate more frequently but with lower-quality content in the synchronous chat room than in the asynchronous discussion forum [~W]
  • Forum discussion contains more social and regulative comments and longer messages; anchored discussion is briefer with more messages [~W]
  • Synchronous private messages are shorter, easier to read, and use fewer academic words than asynchronous notes in dual-mode online courses [~W]
