Replicating Allen et al.'s pretest-controlled design in different contexts is the most viable approach to increasing the base of significant OER efficacy studies.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The author states that "replicating Allen et al. ( 2015) in different contexts is the most viable approach to increasing the base of signiﬁcant efﬁcacy studies on OER", using a pretest at the beginning of a course to account for pre-existing student differences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administer a pretest at the beginning of a course to account for any pre-existing student differences.

Constraints¶

  • Randomly assigning students to open and traditional textbooks would be difficult to pursue.

Target Learners¶

  • college students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring OER efficacy for learning outcomes

Claims¶

  • OER efficacy studies are frequently confounded by simultaneous changes in pedagogy or delivery method. [+W]
  • Substituting OER for traditional textbooks does not appear to decrease student learning outcomes in higher education. [+W]
  • Across 2002-2018, OER efficacy research consistently shows that OER does not harm student learning, with a small positive impact. [+W]
