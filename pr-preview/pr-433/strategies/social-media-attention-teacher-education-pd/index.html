Attend to social media collaboration in teacher education and professional development, recognizing differential activities and support outcomes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implications section argues that because different collaboration activities relate differently to digital social support, teacher education and professional development should give social media use more attention. The authors state these findings "point to the potential of social media use contributing to teacher professional development and indicate that it deserves more attention in both teacher education and research." They also recommend future research explore what prevents teachers from collaborating on such platforms and how collaboration changes over time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recognition that teachers' social media activities are not monolithic and that different uses have different predictors and outcomes.

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and self-reported, so causal relationships are not established.

Target Learners¶

  • in-service teachers

  • teacher professional development

Claims¶

  • Different forms of Instagram collaboration are differentially associated with emotional, informational, and instrumental digital social support [+W]
  • Teachers on Instagram predominantly engage in low-cost collaboration (information seeking) and rarely in high-cost collaboration (co-creating) [+W]
  • Teachers with greater enthusiasm for teaching are more likely to use Instagram to seek information [+W]
  • Seeking information on Instagram is negatively related to perceived informational support but positively related to perceived instrumental support [+W]
  • Teachers with higher self-efficacy are more likely to share information on Instagram, while enthusiasm for teaching is unrelated to sharing [+W]
  • A newly developed survey instrument measures three distinct, reliable forms of teacher collaboration on Instagram: information seeking, information sharing, and co-creating [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design educators should recognize and study learning in student-generated social media spaces rather than only the formal curriculum
