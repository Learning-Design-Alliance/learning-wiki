Develop meta-motivational strategies that increase learners' insight into the motivational aspects of their own learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose that future systems develop modelling of meta-motivational issues: "Meta-motivational strategies are aimed to increase the learner 's insight into the motivational aspects of their own learning", including tactics learners can apply to motivate themselves and understanding of their own reactions to success, failure, praise and criticism. On Maehr's view, the long-term aim for motivationally intelligent systems should be to assist learners to develop their learning skills per se and their insight into them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prior educational experiences focused on encouraging a continuing personal investment in learning, so motivation can be deployed in new contexts

Constraints¶

  • The authors frame this as a line of development for future systems, not an implemented capability of MORE

Target Learners¶

  • learners across contexts who must sustain motivation in new learning situations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning how to learn, including direction, persistence and continuing motivation

Claims¶

  • A motivational strategy is unlikely to succeed unless learners personally value the gains of their effort and fear the losses of not exerting it [+W]
  • A tutoring system can adjust its behaviour in response to a dynamically modelled motivational state, though some motivational rules are not appreciated by learners [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Immediate possibilities for tutoring systems to engage learner values: help teachers frame the system, provoke discussion of purpose, and hold a conversation about purpose and outcomes
