Structure GenAI integration in higher education around feedback-literacy curricula and reflective course design¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article draws a practical implication for universities: because the association between GenAI use and self-reported critical analytical skills runs mainly through feedback literacy and is strongest among reflective learners, institutions should pair GenAI integration with feedback-literacy curricula and reflective course design. The abstract states: "Implications for AI-pedagogy integration, feedback-literacy curricula and reflective course design in higher education are discussed." The introduction adds that results "inform how universities can structure GenAI integration so that increased AI use is more likely to support, rather than undermine, students' analytical capacity."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional capacity to embed feedback-literacy instruction and reflective-thinking activities alongside GenAI use in coursework

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and self-reported, so the design recommendation rests on associations rather than demonstrated causal mechanisms

Target Learners¶

  • university undergraduates

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking
  • feedback literacy
  • reflective thinking

Affordances¶

  • Genai Dual Route Pattern Reflectivity

Related Strategies¶

  • Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Scaffolded AI integration with teacher training and reflective tasks to reduce over-reliance
  • Design GenAI governance and cross-role contact around integrity disputes, the principal site of sustained faculty-student contact
