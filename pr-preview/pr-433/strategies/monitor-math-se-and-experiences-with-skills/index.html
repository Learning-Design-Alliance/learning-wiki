Strategies

Monitor children's math learning experiences and self-efficacy beliefs alongside math skill development from the early school years¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that when supporting math skill development, teachers should also monitor learners' self-efficacy and math learning experiences, since children whose self-efficacy and positive experiences declined had math skills that remained at an average level. It suggests support focusing both on changes in the environment, such as adaptive feedback and models, and on tailoring math learning tasks and goals to learners' needs to ensure mastery experiences, while helping children reframe negative emotions and interpret failures as integral to learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sensitivity to the interpretations and meanings children give to math learning situations
  • Questionnaire assessment of math-related beliefs and experiences, which the article showed is feasible at an early age

Constraints¶

  • The article states it cannot recommend best practices to promote math SE or math skills solely based on this study's findings; experimental research is needed

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school children, especially girls and children with consistently low or declining math self-efficacy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Math self-efficacy, positive math learning experiences, and math skill development

Claims¶

  • Declining math self-efficacy is characterized by decreasing mastery experiences and increasing negative emotional and physiological arousal [+W]
  • Primary school children follow four distinct trajectories of change in math self-efficacy over a school year: high stable, low stable, increasing, and decreasing [+W]
  • Girls are overrepresented in the low-and-stable math self-efficacy trajectory [+W]
  • Increasing math self-efficacy co-occurs with increasing verbal persuasion, vicarious experiences, and mastery experiences and decreasing negative arousal [+W]
  • Math self-efficacy trajectories differ in initial math skills and rate of math skill development [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide early intensified support for struggling arithmetic learners and design mathematics environments that promote positive emotions
  • Monitor children's math anxiety from the earliest stages of schooling to support proficient math performance
  • Promote motivation alongside executive functioning and early numeracy development from the earliest stages of mathematics education
