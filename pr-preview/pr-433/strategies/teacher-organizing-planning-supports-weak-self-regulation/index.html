Train teachers to organize, plan, and manage the classroom to support self-regulation in students with weak initial self-regulation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers deliberately plan instruction and organize the classroom — explaining the purpose of activities, describing upcoming activities, using routines, and providing scaffolded opportunities for students to rehearse task-related behaviors — because structured settings reduce the attentional and cognitive burden on students who enter first grade with weaker self-regulation. The ISI intervention's professional development focused on organizing, minimizing non-instructional time, and planning, and the study suggests this training "can shrink this variability in teacher practice" and support growth in students with weaker self-regulation skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Professional development that explicitly trains organizing, planning, and classroom management strategies, including use of small groups and centers with meaningful independent seat work

Constraints¶

  • The self-regulation benefit was evident only for students scoring in the lowest quartile of initial self-regulation; students with strong fall self-regulation made gains regardless of classroom condition

Target Learners¶

  • First-grade students, especially those with weak initial self-regulation

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-regulation growth (attention, working memory, inhibitory control)
  • Early literacy skills

Affordances¶

  • Self Regulation Teacher Practices Model Isi

Claims¶

  • For first graders with weak initial self-regulation, ISI is associated with greater self-regulation gains, increasing with teachers' A2i use [~W]
  • The ISI intervention shows no main effect on first graders' self-regulation gains from fall to spring [-W]
  • ISI teachers demonstrate stronger classroom management than control teachers, with more instructional time and fewer disruptions [+W]
