Embed adaptive SRL scaffolding and short learning-skills training into online courses, with targeted social-engagement support for students with limited socialization skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the five identified SRL profiles, the article recommends varying the kinds of SRL scaffolding designed into online courses: instructors should provide SRL interventions such as short learning-skills training for students in moderate or lower profiles, promote peer interaction so competent regulators help others co-regulate, and support students with limited socialization skills through discussion prompts and timely feedback. The article states that "students' SRL skills are trainable, so this adaptive scaffolding can help students enhance their SRL skills."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors able to identify or infer students' SRL profiles to tailor scaffolding
  • Online course environments that allow embedding SRL training and discussion facilitation

Constraints¶

  • The article notes effectiveness evidence for the cited training intervention comes from prior work (Bernacki et al., 2021), not from this study; this study did not measure learning outcomes such as GPA

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in online STEM courses, especially those in lower SRL profiles

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning strategy use
  • social engagement and social presence in online learning

Affordances¶

  • Coi Extended Srl Learning Presence

Claims¶

  • Latent profile analysis of 750 online undergraduates identifies five distinct SRL profiles, including a qualitatively distinct low-regulators-with-limited-social-skills profile [+W]
  • Gender predicted SRL profile membership, with male students more likely in lower SRL profiles; major, prior experience, and time commitment did not predict membership [+W]
  • Students in higher SRL profiles perceive significantly higher teaching, social, and cognitive presence than students in lower SRL profiles [+W]
  • The low regulators with limited social skills profile showed significantly lower social presence than the low regulators profile, with no significant difference in teaching or cognitive presence [+W]
  • SRL sub-constructs correlate substantially with all three CoI presences, and a seven-factor OSLQ model fits better than the original six-factor model [+W]
