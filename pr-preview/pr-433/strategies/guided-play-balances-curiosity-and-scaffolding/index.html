Design guided play environments that balance child-initiated curiosity with social scaffolding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes guided play as a play-based educational setting that dynamically incorporates both individual and social learning: 'learning in guided play scenarios balances child-initiated curiosity and social scaffolding 2 often in enriched learning environments'. The teacher carefully designs environments with tools children are likely to explore and learn from, and applies scaffolding where appropriate. This implements the Janus-faced balance between exploitation of known knowledge and exploration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A teacher who designs enriched environments with tools children are likely to explore and applies scaffolding in the learning process where appropriate

Constraints¶

  • The article presents guided play as an illustrative implementation of the framework rather than as a tested design evaluated here

Target Learners¶

  • young children in preschool and early childhood settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fostering exploration, curiosity-driven learning, and construction of understanding alongside acquisition of cultural knowledge

Affordances¶

  • Janus Faced Learning Framework

Claims¶

  • Guided play outperforms direct instruction or free play on a few early-childhood outcomes, with no reliable difference on most others, in one meta-analysis [+W]
  • Children choose exploratory approaches when less is known and social learning when reasonable solutions are at hand [~W]
  • Environments with limited ready-made information spur children's exploration [+W]
  • Learning from others acquires usable information efficiently but limits the potential for innovation [~W]
  • Educational factors supportive of adolescent creativity include balancing freedom with guidance, openness to student ideas, trust, and varied learning resources [+W]
