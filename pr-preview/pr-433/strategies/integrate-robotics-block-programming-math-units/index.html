Integrate robotics with visual block programming into mathematics units on coordinates and integers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that "educational authorities include robotics and programming in the educational context of sixth-grade students in the area of mathematics", especially units on coordinates and integers, where students receive immediate feedback when inserting coordinates or numbers into the robot. They advise starting from basic concepts such as sequences and loops and progressing to intuitive robots from fifth grade, using visual block programs such as Scratch, Blockly or mBlock.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Visual block programming environments so students at this level can work and program intuitively
  • Active methodological approach with problem-based and collaborative activities

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation grounded in math-area results; the science application showed no significant improvement

Target Learners¶

  • sixth-grade primary students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding integers and coordinates
  • acquiring computational concepts

Claims¶

  • Computational concepts (sequences, loops, conditionals, parallel execution, events, robot programming) improved significantly versus control [+W]
  • After a lesson series, two sixth graders could switch flexibly between perspectives when comparing negative integers; one distinguished number-line order from context-based absolute-value reasoning about debts [+W]
  • Students' initial correct answers can mask reasoning based on a subtraction strategy not previously reported for integer order comparisons [+W]
