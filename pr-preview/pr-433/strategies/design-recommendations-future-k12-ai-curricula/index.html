Design future AI curricula to fit the school day, centering student and teacher needs for K-12 in-person and virtual learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors conclude with design recommendations for future AI curricula, especially for K-12 in-person and virtual learning. They argue schools should make time to teach AI in classrooms given its relevance to children's lives, and that curricula become practical for classrooms by centering student and teacher needs in design — the approach they demonstrate through active learning, embedded ethics, and low barriers to access across their three curricula.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Designing curricula that fit into the school day
  • Preparing in-service teachers with the knowledge and resources to support their students, and making tools suitable for classrooms with different technological needs

Constraints¶

  • The authors frame these as steps toward classroom-practical curricula drawn from their own summer deployment, not as validated prescriptions from comparative trials

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in in-person and virtual settings, especially middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI literacy including creating and critiquing AI artifacts

Claims¶

  • After the Creative AI workshop, more middle school students correctly answered how a GAN discriminator's feedback drives the generator (70.6% post vs 41.2% pre) [+W]
  • Middle school students enter AI workshops already aware of and familiar with AI, but misattribute AI status to everyday technologies and view their relationship with AI as symbiotic [+W]
  • In the Exploring GANs activity, 65% of student responses correctly identified what the generator was trying to generate and 61% correctly identified the discriminator's dataset [+W]
  • Students identified more benefits than harms of GAN tools overall, with benefits concentrated in purely artistic tools and harms in tools generating human faces or videos [+W]
  • Students' deepfake-detection quiz scores did not significantly improve after instruction in deepfake-spotting strategies [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Frame genAI virtual patient experiences: prompt experimentation with AI capabilities and orient students to its limits
  • Fit VR learning applications to a specific target audience and support learners with little prior knowledge
  • Partner with and train in-service local teachers to co-instruct AI workshops and become ambassadors for AI education
