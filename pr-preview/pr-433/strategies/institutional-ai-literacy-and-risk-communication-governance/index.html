Build institutional AI literacy and transparent risk-communication governance for agentic AI browsers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that institutions act now to reduce harm and increase adversarial awareness rather than waiting for regulation, since current policies focus almost exclusively on student output while AI browsers introduce data security and privacy risks current frameworks were not designed to mitigate. Leadership, faculty, and staff must understand how AI browsers function, including that these tools can access all content visible within an active browser session and that hidden instructions in web content can redirect their actions. Policies should prioritize transparent risk communication over unenforceable prohibitions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit, operationally grounded guidance that defines AI browsers as distinct entities and articulates the risks they pose to the institutional digital perimeter
  • Massive-scale faculty development so instructors are not fighting a high-tech war with low-tech tools

Constraints¶

  • Institutions cannot fully eliminate the vulnerabilities introduced by agentic AI browsers and cannot resolve the challenge in isolation; liability under GDPR, KVKK, and FERPA remains unclear in the Bring Your Own Agent reality

Target Learners¶

  • higher education faculty and staff
  • institutional leaders
  • K-12 educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • institutional AI literacy
  • data protection awareness
  • adversarial awareness of agentic tools
