Select TTS dialogue format by learner characteristics, with LMS pre-assessment routing and role disclosure supports¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that, rather than delivering a single dialogue format uniformly, designers select or recommend formats based on learners' experiential learning style. As a concrete practice, "prioritizing the TT format for learners high in the CE factor is expected to enhance motivation, while anchoring instruction in the TS or SS format for learners high in the RCE factor is likely to yield more stable motivation and learning outcomes". Supporting measures include an LMS routing function with a brief pre-assessment, letting learners choose formats themselves, disclosing speaker roles before viewing, and pairing the TT format with an observation frame and post-viewing reflection.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A brief pre-assessment of experiential learning style (e.g., implemented as an LMS routing function) to recommend or deliver a format

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the TT format's lower overall evaluation and that findings are preliminary given small-to-medium interaction effect sizes

Target Learners¶

  • high school students in LLM+TTS dialogue-based lessons

Target Learning Goals¶

  • motivation and comprehension in AI-generated dialogue lessons

Affordances¶

  • Ati Framework Format Personalization

Claims¶

  • Interactions between learner characteristics and dialogue format for learning outcomes showed only trends toward significance, not significant effects [-W]
  • Student–student and teacher–teacher dialogue formats elicited significantly higher ARCS motivation than the teacher–student format [+W]
  • In TTS dialogue-based lessons, the teacher–teacher format significantly moderates the motivation effects of both the CE factor (positive) and the RCE factor (negative) [+W]
