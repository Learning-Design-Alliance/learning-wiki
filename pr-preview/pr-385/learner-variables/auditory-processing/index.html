Auditory Processing¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 38 claims (20 show a difference, 13 mixed, 5 no difference) · 21 studies (7 causal, 7 associational, 3 review, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 theoretical), q1–q3 · 6 of 21 report an effect size · 36 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Auditory processing is what the brain does with sound once it has been heard: locating sounds, telling them apart and interpreting them as meaningful, so that a listener can attend to, understand and remember speech and control their own speaking. It underpins spoken language and listening comprehension and lays the foundation for reading and writing, including phonological awareness, at every age the LVN covers from pre-kindergarten to adulthood. It is still developing through adolescence, when academic demands on listening rise. Difficulties show up most where speech must be followed against background noise, as in a busy classroom, and learners with dyslexia may process speech sounds differently. It is measured with standardised batteries of auditory attention, auditory memory and phonological skills, and with tests of auditory processing speed. It is distinct from hearing itself, which access covers, though hearing loss affects it; in older adults, age-related decline in hearing speech in noise is part of the picture.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Classroom noise makes listening harder; quieter rooms, seating near the speaker and written or visual back-up help.
  • Instruction delivered mainly by speech puts learners with auditory processing difficulties at a disadvantage.
  • Learners of a new language may struggle to hear sound contrasts that their first language lacks.

Target Learners¶

  • Young children building speech, phonological awareness and early reading.
  • Learners with dyslexia, hearing loss or auditory processing difficulties; older adults.
  • Multilingual learners.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Listening comprehension and spoken communication.
  • Phonological awareness, word recognition and reading comprehension.

Measures¶

  • Test of Auditory Processing Skills (Martin, Brownell & Hamaguchi, 2018): auditory attention, auditory memory and phonological skills for ages 4 to 18.
  • Auditory Processing Speed Test (APS-T) (Cameron et al., 2014): speed of processing spoken language for ages 6 to 12.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Access: hearing loss diminishes auditory processing, while congenital blindness can sharpen it.
  • Attention: attending to speech in noise depends on auditory processing.
  • English-Learner Status: non-native sound contrasts are hard to hear, though bilingualism can help with filtering noise.
  • Sensory Integration: auditory processing difficulties may contribute to sensory integration difficulties.
  • Communication Skills: auditory processing difficulties go with communication and listening difficulties.
  • Working Memory: holding and manipulating heard information depends on auditory processing.
  • Academic and Critical Literacy: learners with auditory processing difficulties may need added support with reading and language.
  • Executive Function: managing several languages may shape how inhibition operates on what is heard.
  • Prior Knowledge: heard information has to be processed before it can become background knowledge.
  • Adverse Experiences: maltreatment is associated with more auditory processing difficulties.
  • Phonological Awareness: speech perception underlies phonological awareness and processing.
  • Decoding: auditory processing difficulties often come with weaker word recognition.
