Coaching as a partnership among principals, coaches, and teachers¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report adopts a partnership framing of instructional coaching, attributed to Knight (2007), in which principals, coaches, and teachers all "define, understand, and engage in coaching as a partnership." Under this account, engaged principals are key to a successful coaching program, administrators chiefly support coach-teacher collaboration, and administrators can also collaborate closely with their coach. The report organizes its five recommended administrator actions under two partnership roles: supporting coach-teacher collaboration and collaborating closely with the coach.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Principals, coaches, and teachers share a common understanding of coaching as a partnership rather than an evaluation or a solo-driven program.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • School administrators, instructional coaches, and K-12 classroom teachers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Effective instructional coaching leading to improved teaching practices

Examples¶

  • Meet consistently with the coach throughout the school year in dedicated one-on-one time
  • Trust coaches to make decisions around coaching, granting autonomy while staying involved
  • Hold a kick-off event aligning coaching with school and district goals, and leverage early volunteers' testimonials to build teacher buy-in
  • Protect coaches' time so the majority is spent directly with teachers
  • Instructional Coaching
  • Clearly define the role of the coach and support principals in working with math coaches
