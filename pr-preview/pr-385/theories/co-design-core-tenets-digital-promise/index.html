Co-design framework with core tenets of power sharing and inclusion, prioritizing relationships, and capability building¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 5 studies (4 qualitative, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report defines co-design as "a process in which different stakeholders come together to find a solution to a shared problem through inclusive research and development," bringing together school communities, edtech companies, and researchers so the people who will use the solution are involved in its design. Participants leverage three core tenets: ensure power sharing and inclusion (sharing power throughout research, ideation, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation); prioritize relationships (co-designers must share a meaningful relationship based on trust and respect); and focus on capability building (everyone teaches and learns, increasing collaboration and fostering better stakeholder relationships).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires trusting, respectful relationships among co-designers and structures that ensure all stakeholders' ideas and perspectives are considered across the full process.
  • Relationship-building between districts, providers, and partners is foundational, not optional; rushed timelines undermine authentic collaboration

Constraints¶

  • The report cautions that teacher voice is necessary but should not exclude other expertise; teachers may be less likely to envision fundamentally different or research-aligned approaches, so subject matter experts and researchers should be active participants, not just consulted afterward.
  • Co-design does not progress in a straight line; deliverables may look incomplete at various stages, which the article calls normal and healthy

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 educators, students, district leaders, edtech providers, and researchers participating in co-design
  • District leaders
  • Edtech solution providers
  • Funders of co-design initiatives

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Developing student success solutions informed by direct educator and student input
  • Developing responsive, human-centered student success solutions through multi-stakeholder collaboration
