Use multi-method, methodologically rigorous measurement when studying achievement goals and academic dishonesty¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that future research on achievement goals and academic dishonesty improve measurement rigor and combine methods. They state: "we call for more methodological rigor in the measurement of goals and dishonesty as well as multi-methods approaches when investigating their interplay." This follows from their finding that measure type moderated performance-goal associations and that most studies relied on self-reports. The strategy applies to researchers designing studies of cheating, not to classroom instruction directly.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Studies should measure goals and dishonesty with validated, reliability-checked instruments and combine self-report, behavioral, and intention measures

Constraints¶

  • Behavioral measures were rare in the existing literature (seven effect sizes), so multi-method designs require new data collection rather than reuse of typical survey data

Target Learners¶

  • students in secondary and tertiary education
  • educators in academic institutions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding and preventing academic dishonesty
  • motivational regulation of achievement behavior

Claims¶

  • Most research on achievement goals and academic dishonesty relies on correlational designs, self-report dishonesty measures, and personal goal measures [+W]
  • Academic dishonesty measure type moderates performance goal associations: behavioral and intention measures reveal positive links that self-reports mask [+W]
  • Educational level moderates the mastery-approach–dishonesty association, with no correlation in elementary samples [+W]
  • Mastery-avoidance goals show no significant association with academic dishonesty [+W]
  • Mastery goals are negatively associated with academic dishonesty across studies [+W]
  • No evidence of publication bias was found in the achievement goal–dishonesty literature [+W]
