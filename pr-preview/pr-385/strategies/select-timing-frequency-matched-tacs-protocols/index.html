Use timing- and frequency-matched tACS protocols (online theta or gamma, offline theta) targeted to PFC or PPC to improve cognitive performance in healthy young adults¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The meta-analysis supports selecting tACS protocols by timing and frequency band: online-tACS with theta or gamma bands and offline-tACS with theta band showed significant cognitive-performance benefits, especially when targeted at prefrontal or posterior parietal cortex and aimed at executive function. The authors conclude that "tACS protocols with specific timing and frequency band may effectively improve cognitive performances" and recommend future protocols minimize side effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Match stimulation frequency band to the desired effect (theta 4–7 Hz or gamma 31–139 Hz for online; theta for offline)
  • Target PFC or PPC cortical regions

Constraints¶

  • Effects are small (SMD range 0.175–0.247) and possibly limited by a ceiling effect in healthy young adults
  • Phosphenes may occur near occipital cortex or above 1.5 mA intensity

Target Learners¶

  • healthy young adults

Target Learning Goals¶

  • executive function and complex attention
  • perceptual-motor function

Affordances¶

  • Tacs Synchronization Ltp Mechanism Account

Claims¶

  • Offline-tACS with theta frequency band significantly improves cognitive performance, but offline alpha, beta and gamma bands do not [+W]
  • Online-tACS with theta and gamma frequency bands significantly improves cognitive performance, while delta, alpha and beta bands do not [+W]
  • tACS cognitive benefits concentrate in executive function for theta protocols and in executive and perceptual-motor function for online gamma protocols [+W]
  • tACS effects on cognitive performance depend on targeted brain region: theta on PFC and PPC and online gamma on PPC are effective [+W]
  • tACS protocols produce a small significant overall improvement in cognitive performance in healthy young adults [+W]
  • Side effects of tACS are mild and common: about 46.2% of participants in studies reporting them may experience effects such as itching, tingling and phosphenes [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future research should test theta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling co-stimulation protocols and extend tACS benefits to cognitively impaired populations
