Use knowledge of the specific points and lengths of exposure where children are susceptible to contextual effects to structure interventions aimed at reducing learning inequities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review argues that identifying when and for how long children are susceptible to contextual effects is a prerequisite for effective intervention against learning disparities. It states that "a fundamental first step in structuring effective responses to promote learning equity is a comprehensive knowledge base detailing the specific points and lengths of exposure where children are susceptible to contextual effects." Interventions should target modifiable familial and neighborhood factors while attending to structural causes of inequity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal evidence identifying developmental timing and duration of exposure to modifiable factors

Constraints¶

  • Attention must remain on systemic and structural issues as root sources of inequitable circumstances, not individuals, families, or subgroups

Target Learners¶

  • children in inequitable social and economic circumstances

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reducing learning disparities and supporting children to reach their learning potential

Affordances¶

  • Temporality Duration Timing Mobility Framework

Claims¶

  • Each additional period of exposure to social and economic adversity accumulates to produce greater effects on educational and cognitive outcomes, though effects may plateau at a threshold [+W]
  • Chronic (persistent) exposure to adverse social and economic circumstances is associated with greater effects on children's educational and cognitive outcomes than intermittent or no exposure [+W]
  • Specific developmental stages of exposure show distinct effects: antenatal maternal mental health was significant in all pregnancy-stage studies, and early-childhood economic resourcing showed significant associations with outcomes [+W]
  • Movement in and out of social and economic exposure (mobility) across childhood has implications for educational and cognitive outcomes, with upward and downward changes differing in benefit and risk [+W]
  • A minority of longitudinal studies (12%) found no evidence that temporality of childhood circumstances relates to educational and cognitive outcomes [-W]
  • Most longitudinal studies of childhood social and economic circumstances find evidence that temporality (duration, timing, or mobility of exposure) relates to educational and cognitive outcomes [+W]
