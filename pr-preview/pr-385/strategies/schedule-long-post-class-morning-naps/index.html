Schedule relatively long (30-60 min) post-class morning naps to enhance school learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools use post-class morning naps of at least 30 minutes after lessons whose retention matters. Naps are "a scalable, low-cost" boost: students sleep up to 60 min in a quiet dark room immediately after a morning class, a duration the authors note can fit most school programs. Benefits appeared only for naps longer than 30 minutes, so nap length is the actionable parameter.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Naps must be relatively long (>30 min); short naps showed no benefit
  • A quiet dark room with mats and eye masks available right after the class period

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one 5th-grade classroom in a low SES Brazilian community with morning start at 07:15 and drowsy, sleep-debt-carrying students; sleep was self-report verified, not device-monitored

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students attending morning classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • declarative memory retention of curricular content

Claims¶

  • Long post-class naps (>30 min) improve retention versus waking with specific interference (another lecture in the same discipline) [+W]
  • Long post-class naps (>30 min) improve retention of curricular content versus waking with non-specific interference (a break) [+W]
  • Short post-class naps (<30 min) show no significant retention benefit [+W]
  • Retention did not differ between contents tested 3 versus 4 days after learning [~W]
