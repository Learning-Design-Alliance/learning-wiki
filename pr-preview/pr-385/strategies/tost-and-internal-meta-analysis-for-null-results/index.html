Use equivalence testing (TOST) and internal mini meta-analysis to report null intervention effects transparently¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends and demonstrates a cumulative-evidence workflow for intervention research: pre-register hypotheses, set a smallest effect size of interest (d = 0.40), apply the "Two One-Sided Test" equivalence procedure to "confirm or reject the presence of such effects", and combine one's own experiments in an internal mini meta-analysis. The authors argue this "switches the focus from the statistical significance of the results and their corresponding p-values, to the sizes of the effects" and reduces the file-drawer effect by reporting all studies, including null ones.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A pre-specified smallest effect size of interest used in the a priori power analysis
  • All experiments conducted on the research question reported, not only significant ones

Constraints¶

  • The authors state their internal meta-analysis was not meant "to establish generalities about jigsaw teaching", only to synthesize their own five experiments

Target Learners¶

  • education researchers and methodologists designing intervention studies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • credible, replicable evaluation of teaching-method effectiveness

Claims¶

  • An internal meta-analysis across the five experiments estimated the jigsaw effect on learning at ES = 0.00, 95% CI (-0.10, 0.09) [+M]
  • In a two-hour randomized experiment, sixth graders in a jigsaw condition did not outperform those in an individualistic condition on a mathematics learning test [+W]
  • With scrambled randomization and novel material, jigsaw produced no difference versus individualistic learning on a life-sciences test [+W]
  • In a semester-long crossover experiment with a real teacher, learning scores under jigsaw were not higher than under teaching-as-usual in physics and chemistry [+W]
  • Equivalence testing (TOST) indicated the observed jigsaw effect sizes were statistically equivalent to zero in the single-session experiments [+W]
