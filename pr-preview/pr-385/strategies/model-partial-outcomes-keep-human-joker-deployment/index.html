Model partial and uncertain defending outcomes and keep the facilitator human when deploying drama-based robot interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's deployment recommendations are twofold. First, the narrative should model realistic outcome uncertainty: the story shows that "confronting the bully can unfold in more than one way", so children learn defending can matter even when bullying is not fully stopped. Second, for real-world adoption the authors argue "the role of the joker should ideally not be automated", with a drama educator taking that role, while the Wizard's narrative-control role should be fully automated, potentially via simplified teacher-facing interfaces and LLMs.
