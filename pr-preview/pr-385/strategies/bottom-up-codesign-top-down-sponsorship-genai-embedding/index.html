Combine bottom-up co-design with top-down executive sponsorship, cross-unit collaboration and dedicated R&D capacity to embed institutional GenAI¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's lessons-learned set for embedding GenAI at institutional scale: bring together diverse experts and facilitate their collaboration; secure senior institutional oversight so innovation aligns with priorities and gains executive awareness; ensure broad institutional buy-in and sharing of lessons; and maintain dedicated R&D units with capacity to explore new pedagogies and technologies outside mainstream operations. The authors report that "Key enabling factors included senior executive sponsorship, cross-unit collaboration between the two R&D units of KMi and IET, Digital Services, and Faculties, and the iterative DBR cycles to refine the functionality of AIDA in response to user feedback."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Dedicated R&D units with staff time for research and development
  • Senior executive sponsorship (e.g. steering boards chaired at Pro-Vice-Chancellor level)
  • Champions within the organisation and broad staff engagement mechanisms such as communities of practice

Constraints¶

  • Diverse expert collaboration is crucial but "not sufficient to embed AI into business as usual"; at the time of writing AIDA's student-facing development had stalled despite these conditions

Target Learners¶

  • distance learning students in large higher education institutions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible, at-scale institutional adoption of GenAI learning tools

Affordances¶

  • Sharples Embedded System Approach Genai

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed anticipatory governance in institutional strategy via Chief AI Officers, AI task forces, faculty AI-literacy development, and student co-design mechanisms
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Co-create contextualized AI literacy policies via multi-stakeholder task forces with phased roadmaps
  • Staged institutional implementation: audit interactions, map to teaming levels, build AI literacy, co-design, and evaluate iteratively
  • Combine top-down theoretical and bottom-up empirical approaches when developing domain-specific heuristics
  • Concentrate AI-edtech adoption priorities through collaborative networks that share tools, policies, and evaluation practices
  • Use the EPIS model's four stages (Exploration, Preparation, Implementation, Sustainment) to bring an educational technology intervention to scale in elementary schools
