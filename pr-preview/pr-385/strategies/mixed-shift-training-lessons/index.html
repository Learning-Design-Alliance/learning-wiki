Devise supplementary training lessons that require shifting between processes and sets within one lesson¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Where drill materials isolate one process or one set at a time, the article recommends supplementing them with lessons that combine contrasting demands so pupils practise changing set. In arithmetic, 'A lesson was framed to give training in changing from one process to the other', mixing examples from earlier lessons with time allowances proportionate to their sources; in reading, material was devised so the need of change was made inherent, with pupils underlining parts previously unnoticed and later completing a timed four-part test requiring change of set.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Exaggerate and play up the shift factor; include examples drawn from the lessons already practised with proportionate time allowances
  • Comprehension should be checked so that faster performance reflects real reading

Constraints¶

  • In the arithmetic illustration pupils initially took longer to work the mixed lesson or did not finish in the prescribed time; the article poses but does not answer how much such training must be special versus transferring

Target Learners¶

  • elementary grade pupils in arithmetic and reading lessons

Target Learning Goals¶

  • rapid adjustment between processes and mental sets
  • accuracy and speed on mixed-process tests
  • flexible reading rate suited to material

Affordances¶

  • Mental Set Rapid Adjustment Framework

Claims¶

  • In arithmetic practice tests, pupils carry the previous operation's mental set into the next examples, and a supplementary lesson in shifting processes improves both accuracy and speed [+W]
  • Mental set acquired for one task persists and impairs performance in spelling recall and in geography problem-solving [+W]
  • Merely making pupils conscious of the need to change mental set improved silent reading rate while comprehension was checked [+W]
  • More effective readers are those who vary their reading rate to suit the type of material [+W]
