Evidence¶

Bo Yang 2024¶

Bo Yang. (2024). Combining Danmaku and Discussion Boards: Toward A Scalable and Sociable Environment for Mass Collaboration in MOOCs. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09426-3

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Discussion-level analysis mapped observed behaviors onto the A3C framework (Jeong et al., 2017): lurkers engaged in attendance, active posters in coordination, distributed co-answering in cooperation, and knowledge co-construction in collaboration, but "collaboration... was observed only in a small portion of the knowledge-related discussions."

"However, collaboration, necessitating extensive joint interaction in various forms to achieve shared understanding and collective responsibility, was observed only in a small portion of the knowledge-related discussions."

Related Claims¶

  • All four A3C interaction types can contribute individually to learning and may exist in parallel during mass collaboration — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Mass participation formats in online communities depend on the tools used (Danmaku versus discussion board) — a broader claim this one bears on

  • Danmaku learners engage in parasocial greetings and compliments to the instructor and seek co-learning presence, phenomena absent on the discussion board — related
  • Danmaku interaction is predominantly socio-emotional while discussion-board interaction tilts cognitive, and the tools share only four content categories — related
  • Danmaku primarily carries tacit knowledge sharing via personal experience while the discussion board carries explicit mathematical knowledge — related
