Evidence¶

Dianne A. Vella-Brodrick · Krystyna Gilowska 2022¶

Dianne A. Vella-Brodrick · Krystyna Gilowska. (2022). Effects of Nature (Greenspace) on Cognitive Functioning in School Children and Adolescents: a Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:1217–1254. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09658-5

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports two quasi-experiments: Johnson et al. found "a small positive effect" for children aged 8–15 after a forested-trail walk, and Stevenson et al. found positive effects for speed and stability of responses in children aged 10–14, with "no improvement in executive attention".

"Two studies (Johnson et al., 2019 and Stevenson et al., 2019) using similar computer attention tests based on the fish flanker task, reported a significant positive effect on selective attention performance after a 30-min walk in a natural environment with trees."

Related Claims¶

  • Nature interventions improve working memory in school children relative to control conditions, for both immersive and passive exposures — related
  • In university students, walks or sitting in natural settings after cognitive fatigue improve some attention and working memory measures, with null results for others — related
  • In secondary school students, breaks with green views or walks in larger green spaces improve attention performance — related
  • School-garden playtime showed no significant effect on impulse control in primary school children — related
  • In elementary school students, short-term green breaks restore attention and working memory, with effects on inhibition mixed — related
  • Effects on sustained/selective attention are mixed across studies, with benefits in some green settings but no effect in others regardless of exposure duration — related
