Qian 2026¶

Qian, Y. (2026). Governing generative AI in higher education: emerging policy approaches and support ecosystems at innovative U.S. Universities. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00233-x

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative document analysis with inductive thematic analysis of official institutional websites of the 50 U.S. universities ranked most innovative, accessed September 30, 2025. The findings section reports that "33 of 50 universities provided syllabus, course-policy, or instructor-facing guidance" making faculty the primary translators of AI expectations into course rules.

  • Institutional GenAI guidance is more pro-use than computing course syllabus policies, where 92% of syllabi set explicit permissions and half prohibit use outright — related
  • Universities pair academic-integrity guidance with AI literacy and student readiness supports, treating literacy as strategic rather than punitive — related
  • Universities converge on disclosure and attribution expectations as the condition distinguishing legitimate AI assistance from misrepresentation — related
  • A scaffolded participatory exercise in which students wrote rules governing the instructor's own AI use produced an accountability framework the instructor found more usable than institutional guidance — related
  • Nearly all top-50 universities provide faculty GenAI guidelines, with 94% emphasizing establishing and communicating course-specific GenAI policies — related
  • Course syllabi name specific GenAI tools — ChatGPT in 90 of 98 — while institutional guidelines contain no code for specific tools — related
  • Students co-designing a GenAI course policy prioritized training for students and instructors, standardized disclosure procedures, and stronger institutional support — related
  • Institutions diverge in default GenAI permission stances, ranging from unauthorized-unless-authorized to program-level carve-outs where instructors may not ban AI use — related
  • Both institutional and course-level guidance emphasize transparency and acknowledging or citing GenAI use, with 83% of syllabi providing explicit citation guidance — related
  • Comprehensive institutional GenAI guidelines translate to course-level guidelines at higher rates (48.3–71.4%) than moderately comprehensive ones (30.8–31.2%) — related
