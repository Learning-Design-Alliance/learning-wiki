Evidence¶

Burton 2023¶

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Experiment 1 solving-phase repeated-measures ANOVA on proportion solved by JOS type was significant, F(2, 150) = 27.41, p < .001, η²p = .27, but the S versus NS pairwise comparison was not (p = .67). "solving rates were not significantly higher for anagrams that had received S JOSs versus NS JOSs".

"In sharp contrast, solving rates were not significantly higher for anagrams that had received S JOSs versus NS JOSs (p = .67), t hus S JOSs were discriminating during the JOS phase but were not predictive of later solving."

  • In a self-regulated solving phase, S JOSs predicted solving success and AS and NS JOSs predicted effort regulation — reports the opposite
  • Solvable judgments of solvability discriminate solvable from unsolvable anagrams even after excluding already-solved judgments — related

  • JOS discrimination decreased across blocks as anagram presentation duration was reduced, driven by different mechanisms for AS versus S JOSs — related
