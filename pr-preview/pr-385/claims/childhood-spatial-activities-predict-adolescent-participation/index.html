Evidence¶

Emily Grossnickle Peterson 2020¶

Emily Grossnickle Peterson, Adam B. Weinberger, David H. Uttal, Bob Kolvoord and Adam E. Green. (2020). Spatial activity participation in childhood and adolescence: consistency and relations to spatial thinking in adolescence. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-020-00239-0

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

Linear regression predicting adolescent spatial activities (SAQ) from childhood activities (CAQ) with covariates in a sample of 346 high school students; the model was significant (F5,302 = 17.06, adjusted R2 = .21) and "childhood spatial activ- ities were predictive of later adolescent activities" with β = .47.

"Results revealed that childhood spatial activ- ities were predictive of later adolescent activities ( b = .60, β = .47, SE = .07, p < .001)."

Emily Grossnickle Peterson 2020 (2)¶

Emily Grossnickle Peterson, Adam B. Weinberger, David H. Uttal, Bob Kolvoord and Adam E. Green. (2020). Spatial activity participation in childhood and adolescence: consistency and relations to spatial thinking in adolescence. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-020-00239-0

q2 · i3 · associational · r2

A second linear regression added a gender × CAQ interaction to the same model predicting SAQ. The interaction was significant, "indicating that the positive association between childhood and adolescent spatial activities was greater for female participants" (β = −.55, p = .04).

"This yielded a significant interaction term ( b = −.29, β = −.55, SE = .14, p = .04), indicating that the positive association between childhood and adolescent spatial activities was greater for female participants"
