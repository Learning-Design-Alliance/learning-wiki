Evidence¶

Jansen 2017¶

Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.10.001

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review of Barrett et al. (2014), in which participants watched a 15-minute lecture on APA style and answered 20 free response questions by computer or handwriting. The review reports a significant interaction without an effect size.

"Results showed a significant interaction effect. Those for which the note-taking and testing procedures were congruent (i.e., both writing or both typing) scored better on the free response test than participants in the incongruent conditions."

Jansen 2017 (2)¶

Jansen, R. S., Lakens, D., & IJsselsteijn, W. (2017). An integrative review of the cognitive costs and benefits of note-taking. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.10.001

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review of Bui et al. (2013, study 2, n = 76), a 2x2 design with organised versus transcribed notes and immediate versus 24-hour delayed free recall tests. The review reports the crossover without an effect size.

"When administering the test immediately after the 11 minute lecture, performance was best with transcribed notes compared to the organised notes. When the test was delayed 24 hours after the end of the lecture, performance was better for people who had taken organised notes."
