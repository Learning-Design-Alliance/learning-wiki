Evidence¶

Dumontheil Iroise 2016¶

Dumontheil Iroise, Wolf Laura K., Blakemore Sarah-Jayne. (2016). Audience effects on the neural correlates of relational reasoning in adolescence. Neuropsychologia. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2016.05.001

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Behavioural analysis of the fMRI study's 2×2×2 mixed-design ANOVA on accuracy (19 adolescents, 14 adults) found a Task × Audience interaction, "driven by a significant decrease in accuracy in the Peer" condition for Relational trials; the Control condition showed no significant Peer–Alone difference (p>0.19).

"which was driven by a significant decrease in accuracy in the Peer (88.9%±1.6) relative to the Alone condition for the Relational condition (91.8%±1.3; F(1,31)=6.46; p=0.016; ηp2=.172)"

  • Individual differences in audience effects are not correlated with self-reported resistance to peer influence — related
  • Across all age groups, relational reasoning accuracy is lower in the friend-present condition than in the experimenter-present condition overall — related
  • The study found no evidence of age differences in relational reasoning performance, possibly due to design choices maximising sensitivity to audience effects — related
