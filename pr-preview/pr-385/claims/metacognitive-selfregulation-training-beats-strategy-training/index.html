Evidence¶

Hertzog C 2011¶

Hertzog C, Dunlosky J. (2011). Metacognition in Later Adulthood: Spared Monitoring Can Benefit Older Adults' Self-regulation. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3903298/

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports its first intervention study (Dunlosky, Kubat-Silman, & Hertzog, 2003), in which older adults received self-testing plus selective-restudy training; "This training produced greater performance improvements than more traditional encoding strategy training" under self-paced study, shown in Figure 3.

"This training produced greater performance improvements than more traditional encoding strategy training (see Figure 3) when individuals were allowed to self-pace their study, which affords differential allocation of time and effort to unlearned items."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Younger and older adults select study items in an easy-to-difficult order, consistent with the region of proximal learning model — related
  • Older adults restudy easy and medium items more than younger adults, and lower memory self-efficacy correlates with avoiding difficult items — related
  • Aging does not materially reduce the relative accuracy (resolution) of judgments of learning during encoding, with cross-sectional evidence of no age declines and even age-related increases in aggregate JOL resolution across ages 18 to 81 — related
  • Learners misjudge which learning strategies are effective — related
  • Older adults allocate proportionally more study time to easy and medium items while younger adults allocate more to difficult items, yet older adults recall less despite studying more — related
  • Self Regulated Learning Predicts Achievement — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Regulating GA proceeds via metacognitive monitoring of adequacy and accuracy plus control through outward- and inward-oriented strategies — related
  • Age did not interact with associative relatedness or effective encoding-strategy use in predicting JOLs, indicating no age differences in the use of these two sources of information when making judgments of learning — related
