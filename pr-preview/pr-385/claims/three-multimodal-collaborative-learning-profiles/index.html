Evidence¶

Jauwairia Nasir 2021¶

Jauwairia Nasir, Aditi Kothiyal, Barbara Bruno, Pierre Dillenbourg. (2021). Many are the ways to learn identifying multi-modal behavioral profiles of collaborative learning in constructivist activities. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn (2021) 16:485–523. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09358-2

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Cluster analysis of 48 teams reduced to 32 (children aged 9 to 12) in the JUSThink robot-mediated activity; Table 3 reports mean Absolute_LG of 0.678* (Expressive Explorers) and 0.616 (Calm Tinkerers) versus 0.383* (Silent Wanderers) with near-identical Last_Error (0.461/0.393/0.393), showing "the performance (Last_Error) in the task is very similar for each group".

"Approach B gives 3 behavioral clusters with the first two exhibiting high learning and the third lower learning; hence, with respect to learning, the groups can be named as type 1 gainers, type 2 gainers, and non-gainers, respectively. Note that the performance (Last_Error) in the task is very similar for each group."

Related Claims¶

  • Three learning profiles emerge among university students: organized-deep, unorganized-deep, and dissonant — related
  • Calm Tinkerers display significantly lower negative valence and arousal than Silent Wanderers, while Expressive Explorers show high arousal and negative valence similar to Silent Wanderers — related
  • Multimodal behavioral profiles of dyads' collaborative learning are identifiable by integrating video, audio, and log data, with emergent processes more explanatory than individual behaviors — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Speech activity and speech overlap distinguish gainer dyads from non-gainer Silent Wanderers across both gainer profiles — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The two gainer profiles differ in problem-solving strategy: Expressive Explorers enact global exploration with reflection on past solutions, Calm Tinkerers enact local exploration with in-the-moment correction — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In open-ended constructivist activities, task success or failure is not a reliable indicator of learning, so learner models based only on performance risk misjudging learners — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
