Evidence¶

Frenzel 2024¶

Frenzel, A. C., Dindar, M., Pekrun, R., Reck, C., & Marx, A. K. G. (2024). Joy is reciprocally transmitted between teachers and students: Evidence on facial mimicry in the classroom. Learning and Instruction, 91, 101896. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101896

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Analysis of the distribution of the dyadic mimicry index across 69 instructor–student dyads nested within 13 instructors. The article reports the ICC as 0.164 and describes the index as "predominantly idiosyncratic and dyad-specific".

"The resulting ICC was 0.164, indicating that while some variance (16.4%) was attributable to the instructors, the larger proportion of the variance (83.6%) was within instructors, implying that the cross-recurrence of joy between instructors and their students was a predominantly idiosyncratic and dyad-specific index."

Related Claims¶

  • Instructor-led facial joy mimicry predominated: 58% of dyads were instructor-led and the critical lag window's midpoint was around +500 msec after the instructor's expression — related
  • Facially expressed joy during class correlated positively with post-session self-reported session joy for both students and instructors, though the instructor correlation did not reach significance
  • Classroom membership accounts for 11% of variance in students' perceived competence, justifying multilevel analysis — related
