Evidence¶

Palacios 2022¶

Palacios, M., Kibler, A., Farren Burho, J., Garrett Sandstead, M., Wiger, S., & Woodruff, K. (2022). Who is in charge of English Learner services? Lessons learned from a national co-teaching and collaboration study. WestEd: National Research & Development Center to Improve Education for Secondary English Learners. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED625797

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Website contact search for secondary-level EL leaders in the national district sample. The review found incomplete information: "name, title, and phone number, but not their email address," and out-of-date organizational charts. The article also reports fewer than half of web-form emails were answered.

"In many instances, we were able to locate the individual's name, title, and phone number, but not their email address. On other sites, we found organizational charts with names and positions only, but no contact information."

Palacios 2022 (2)¶

Palacios, M., Kibler, A., Farren Burho, J., Garrett Sandstead, M., Wiger, S., & Woodruff, K. (2022). Who is in charge of English Learner services? Lessons learned from a national co-teaching and collaboration study. WestEd: National Research & Development Center to Improve Education for Secondary English Learners. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED625797

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Phone-calling phase of the contact protocol using a script at main district numbers. The review found that after navigating phone trees, messages left on voicemail were "only rarely received return calls," and few superintendents responded to emails.

"If we reached voicemail after navigating the phone tree, we left messages, but only rarely received return calls. In such cases, we settled on sending our email survey invitation to the superintendent."
