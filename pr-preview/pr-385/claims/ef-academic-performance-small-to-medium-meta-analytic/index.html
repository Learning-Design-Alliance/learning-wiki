Theobald 2026¶

Theobald, M., Kerner auch Koerner, J., Breitwieser, J., Buhr, L., Karbach, J., Könönen, T., Nobbe, L., Reinelt, T., Schaaf, M., Schmiedek, F., Wieland, L. M., & Blume, F. (2026). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10157-0

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports meta-analytic findings (citing Allan 2014; Jacob and Parkinson 2015; Titz 2014) that EF–academic performance relations are "generally small to medium in magnitude". No effect size values are printed in the quoted text.

"Meta-analytic findings indicate that relations between EF and academic performance are generally small to medium in magnitude, with inhibition showing small effects and broader EF factors showing medium-sized effects on reading and mathematics achievement"

Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10157-0

q3 · i? · review · r?

The review reports, citing Jacob and Parkinson 2015, a ranked pattern across EF subcomponents: "cognitive flexibility (attention shifting) tends to show the strongest medium-sized effects", then WM, then response inhibition. No numeric effect sizes are printed.

"Within this work, cognitive flexibility (attention shifting) tends to show the strongest medium-sized effects, followed by slightly smaller medium effects for WM and small effects for response inhibition (Jacob and Parkinson 2015)."

Related Claims¶

  • Inhibition/interference, working memory and processing speed correlate with adolescent academic achievement, while cognitive flexibility and social skills do not — reports the opposite
  • Children's executive function at the beginning of pre-kindergarten predicts growth in mathematics achievement across the pre-kindergarten year — related
  • Cool EF (Stroop inhibition and CNT cognitive flexibility) predicts academic performance, while no hot EF variable does — related
  • Executive functions and working memory correlate moderately to strongly with mathematics achievement (r = .28 to .40), from preschool through elementary school — related
  • Response inhibition shows a small negative association with reading fluency (r = −.15) in a single study — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Gender differences appear in academic achievement and several cognitive parameters but not in inhibition/interference or social skills — related
  • Executive functions show significant associations with reading outcomes in elementary school children (meta-analysis of 60 studies, 275 effect sizes) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Executive functions, especially working memory, inhibition, and cognitive flexibility, are consistently associated with core SRL activities — related
  • EF explains substantial achievement variance (about 20–60% in school children) while SRL shows positive but low-effect-size relations to achievement — reports the opposite
  • Executive functions show consistent positive meta-analytic associations with academic outcomes — related
  • Working memory span, updating, and cognitive flexibility positively predict English writing, response inhibition positively predicts reading, and working memory span negatively predicts listening — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
