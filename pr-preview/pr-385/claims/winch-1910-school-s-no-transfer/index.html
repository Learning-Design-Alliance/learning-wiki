Evidence¶

W. H. Winch 1910¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Controlled group experiment in a municipal girls' school (Standards VI-VII, average age 13 years 2 months); two equal groups matched on preliminary reasoning marks, one practised rule sums while the other did other school work. The author concludes there "appears to be no transfer of improvement in accuracy"; both groups had averaged 20.6 on the preliminary tests.

"It is difficult to resist the conclusion that the group prac- iised in accuracy of computation shows no improvement over <he group not thus practised, i. e., there appears to be no transfer of improvement in accuracy from arithmetical com- putation to arithmetical reasoning."

Winch 1910¶

Winch, W. H. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy "transfer"? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0072557

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Matched-groups classroom experiment in School S (spring 1909): a whole class was split into two equal groups on preliminary reasoning marks; the practised group worked rule sums while the other group did history or drawing, and both took a final problem test. The author reports "no transfer of improvement in accuracy from arithmetical com- putation to arithmetical reasoning".

"i. e., there appears to be no transfer of improvement in accuracy from arithmetical com- putation to arithmetical reasoning."

Related Claims¶

  • Accuracy of arithmetical reasoning and accuracy of numerical computation correlate highly positively across the four schools (r = +.69 to +.85) — related

  • Practice in rule sums produced considerable improvement in numerical accuracy within the practice medium itself in Schools I and O.K. — related
  • In School B, the practised group outscored the non-practised group in final arithmetical reasoning, but the author judged transfer unsafe to conclude — related
  • In School I, the practised group showed an advantage in three of four sections of arithmetical reasoning, but its highest section was worse than the non-practised one — related
  • In School O.R. the practised group scored higher overall in final reasoning tests, but the section-by-section excess improvement was uncertain — related
  • In School O.K., the practised group obtained higher final reasoning marks on the whole, but the section-by-section excess improvement over the non-practised group was uncertain — related
