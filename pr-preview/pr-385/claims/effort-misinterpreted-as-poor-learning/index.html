Evidence¶

Onan 2022¶

Onan, E., Wiradhany, W., Biwer, F., Janssen, E. M., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2022). Growing Out of the Experience: How Subjective Experiences of Effort and Learning Influence the Use of Interleaved Practice. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09692-3

q2 · i? · causal · r3

Template analysis of open-ended responses about strategy choices and VFP prompt questions, coded iteratively by three authors, with a blind third coder applying the template to the first two datasets (50% of responses); a separate scoring of how well VFP answers matched participants' actual ratings was independently re-scored on 30% of the data (intraclass correlation = 0.89). Participants "were vulnerable to misinterpretation of effort", which led them to choose blocked practice.

"In monit oring effort, we observed that participants were vulnerable to misinterpretation of effort: High effort, associated with interleaved practice, indicated poor learning; whereas, low effort, associated with blocked prac- tice, indicated high learning."

Onan 2022 (2)¶

Onan, E., Wiradhany, W., Biwer, F., Janssen, E. M., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2022). Growing Out of the Experience: How Subjective Experiences of Effort and Learning Influence the Use of Interleaved Practice. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09692-3

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The article's summary of its qualitative findings: students' initial beliefs about strategy efficacy were inaccurate, and "on-task experiences overrode the influence of prior beliefs" in strategy decisions.

"Qualitative results revealed that students initially had inaccurate beliefs about the efficacy of learning strategies, but on-task experiences overrode the influence of prior beliefs in learning strategy decisions."
