q3 i1 In an online interaction with a fictitious disagreeing partner, performance-approach goals predicted competitive regulation only when the partner was presented as more competent (β = .49, p < .001, partial η² = .09), not when equally competent; the interaction effect was small (partial η² = .03). → Sommet 2015

q3 i3 With a more-competent partner, higher performance-approach goals predicted higher competitive regulation; with an equally competent partner the relationship was not observed. → Sommet 2015

Evidence¶

Sommet 2015¶

Sommet, N., Darnon, C., & Butera, F. (2015). To Confirm or to Conform? Performance Goals as a Regulator of Conflict With More-Competent Others. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(2), 580–598. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037240

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Internet study with 139 university students who believed they interacted with a disagreeing partner presented as a bachelor (same-competence) or PhD (superior-competence) student. Regression showed the predicted interaction, β = .19, F(1, 129) = 4.38, p < .04, partial η² = .03 (small effect), with the simple slope in the superior-competence condition at partial η² = .09.
