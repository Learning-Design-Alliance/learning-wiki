Evidence¶

Charlotte Dignath & Marcel V. J. Veenman 2021¶

Charlotte Dignath & Marcel V. J. Veenman. (2021). The Role of Direct Strategy Instruction and Indirect Activation of Self-Regulated Learning—Evidence from Classroom Observation Studies. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09534-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

Observation study using the ATES instrument to code teachers' direct and indirect promotion of SRL and its relation to the development of students' performance. The review reports that "explicit strategy instruction was associated with a gain in performance", while a great amount of strategy teaching took place in an implicit way and supportive learning environments were rare.

"In contrast to implicit strategy instruction, explicit strategy instruction was associated with a gain in performance."

Related Claims¶

  • Teachers' direct instructional support is associated with students' use of self-regulation strategies, while autonomy as indirect activation was negatively associated with reading performance — related
  • Explicit L2 grammar instruction leads to larger learning effects than implicit instruction, as reported in the reviewed synthesis literature — related
  • Explicit grammar teaching outperforms implicit teaching; one review found the advantage only for complex rules, but a meta-analysis found it for simple rules too — related
  • Explicit teaching of the control-of-variables strategy shows no advantage over not explicitly teaching it, per the Schwichow et al. meta-analysis cited in the review — reports the opposite
  • Direct SRL promotion in observed classrooms was dominated by implicit strategy instruction, metacognitive support, and metacognitive reflection, with explicit strategy instruction rare — related
