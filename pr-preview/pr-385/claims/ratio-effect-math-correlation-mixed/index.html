Evidence¶

Gilmore Camilla K. 2010¶

Gilmore Camilla K., McCarthy Shannon E., Spelke Elizabeth S. (2010). Non-symbolic arithmetic abilities and achievement in the first year of formal schooling in mathematics. Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.02.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Experiment 2's analysis of ratio effect scores used easier comparison ratios (5:7, 5:8, 5:9) that all should elicit above-chance performance, unlike Experiment 1 where children performed at chance on the most difficult ratio. The authors conclude students across the achievement spectrum focused on number.

"In contrast to Experiment 1, there was no relationship between children’s ratio effect scores on the non-symbolic addition task and their achievement in mathematics (r = .007, n.s.)."
