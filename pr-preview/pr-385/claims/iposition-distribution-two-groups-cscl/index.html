Evidence¶

Aaron J. Peltoniemi 2025¶

Aaron J. Peltoniemi, Joni Lämsä, Sami Lehesvuori, Raija Hämäläinen. (2025). Understanding the role of I-positions facilitating knowledge construction in a computer-supported collaborative learning environment. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-025-09447-6

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Utterance-level coding of audio recordings for both groups, with I-positions grounded at the utterance level and reviewed through intersubjectivity among the four authors. The printed utterance counts per position for group A and group B are reported as "I–it (16 utterances)" and "I–me (26 utterances)" respectively.

"the two most common I-positions for Group A were I–it (16 utterances) and I–me (16 utterances), whereas the two most common I-positions for group B were I–me (26 utterances) and I–we (24 utterances)."

Related Claims¶

  • Group A, who failed the learning task, devoted most dialogues to orientation and conceptualization and asked yes-no questions at the personal level, while group B, who succeeded, distributed dialogue across investigation and discussion
  • In group B, usage of I–we gradually increased and peaked in the final stage during critical evaluation, suggesting intrapersonal collective voice drives critical evaluation of evidence — related
  • Group B showed a shift toward intrapersonal voices (I–you/I–we) during its later empirical evaluation, while group A remained interpersonal (I–it/I–me) even when investigating, with un-negotiated inputs — related
