Evidence¶

Goetz 2006¶

Goetz, T., Frenzel, A. C., Pekrun, R., Hall, N. C., & Lüdtke, O. (2006). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Latent correlations among five emotion factors per domain from SEM in both grade samples. "Conspicuously strong correlations were found between boredom and anger" (.81–.90) and between enjoyment and boredom (−.76 to −.91); the authors note these are unreliability-corrected upper-bound coefficients.

"Conspicuously strong correlations were found between boredom and anger (range /H11005.81 to .90, across domains and grade levels) and between enjoyment and boredom (range /H11005/H11002.76 to /H11002.91, across domains and grade levels)."

Goetz 2006 (2)¶

Goetz, T., Frenzel, A. C., Pekrun, R., Hall, N. C., & Lüdtke, O. (2006). Between- and Within-Domain Relations of Students' Academic Emotions. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Within-domain SEM correlations across four domains and two grade levels. The article reports "anger seemed to be more strongly related to boredom than to anxiety," contradicting the expected clustering of the two negative-activating emotions.

"However, in the present results, anger seemed to be more strongly related to boredom than to anxiety."

Related Claims¶

  • Academic emotions show weak between-domain relations: the same emotion in different school subjects is only weakly correlated, even for similar domains — related
  • Positive academic emotions correlate positively with interest, effort, flexible learning strategies, and perceived self-regulation, while boredom and hopelessness correlate negatively with all of these motivational variables — related
  • Aggregated state emotions converge substantially but imperfectly with self-reported trait emotions, with averaged correlations ranging from r = 0.391 (shame) to r = 0.608 (boredom) — related
