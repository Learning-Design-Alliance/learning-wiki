Evidence¶

Pozuelos 2019¶

Pozuelos, J.P., Cómbita, L.M., Abundis-Gutiérrez, A., Paz-Alonso, P.M., Conejero A., Guerra, S., & Rueda, M.R. (2019). Metacognitive scaffolding boosts cognitive and neural benefits following executive attention training in children. Developmental Science, 22: e12756. https://doi.org/10.1111/desc.12756

q3 · i? · causal · r?

The abstract reports the study's between-group comparison of intelligence gains among typically developing preschoolers across three conditions: metacognitive scaffolding training, regular training, and untrained active control. The metacognitive group showed "larger gains on intelligence"; no effect size is printed.

"Compared to children in the regular training protocol and an untrained active control group, children in the metacognitive group showed larger gains on intelligence"
