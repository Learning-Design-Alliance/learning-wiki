Catherine P. Bradshaw 2012¶

Catherine P. Bradshaw, Tracy E. Waasdorp, Philip J. Leaf. (2012). Effects of School-Wide Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports on Child Behavior Problems. Pediatrics. https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2012-0243

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Multilevel logistic regression in the 4-year group RCT on aggregated dichotomous teacher-reported ODR indicators; children in SWPBIS schools were "33% less likely to receive an ODR" (AOR = 0.67, 95% CI 0.57–0.79). No standardized effect size is printed for this finding.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • SWPBIS effects on discipline referrals and school services tended to be greatest among children rated at-risk or high-risk at baseline — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • SWPBIS does not significantly reduce out-of-school suspensions in this trial — related
  • At-risk children in SWPBIS schools are slightly less likely to receive an office discipline referral than at-risk peers in comparison schools (41.3% vs 46.5%, d = -0.13) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • SWPBIS reduced receipt of counseling for inappropriate behavior among at-risk and socially-emotionally skilled children — related
  • Schools implementing all three PBIS tiers with fidelity had significantly fewer students with OSS, fewer days missed from OSS, and fewer OSS incidents than matched non-PBIS schools during 2020–21 — related
  • School suspension rates declined significantly within SWPBIS schools but not within comparison schools, though the two conditions were not tested against each other — related
  • In SWPBIS-trained schools the share of students with an office discipline referral fell slightly but significantly (18.8% to 18.1%), with no comparison-school data — related
  • Out-of-school suspensions decreased 41% at a middle school implementing PBIS under the TEAM Student framework — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Students in MTSS-coached schools were significantly less likely to have at least one behavior incident — related
  • A home visit before the school year reduced the likelihood of a disciplinary incident (effect size = +0.10) — related
  • Implementing PBIS with at least Tier 1 fidelity showed no statistically significant effect on school disciplinary exclusions during the 2020–21 pandemic year — related
