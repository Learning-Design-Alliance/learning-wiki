Related Claims¶

  • Mathematics teachers appreciate ChatGPT-generated generative learning activities as hands-on, collaborative, engaging, open-ended, differentiated, and using multiple representations — reports the opposite
  • Teachers report ChatGPT-generated math questions misalign with grade level and topic, are unreliable or unanswerable, and cost time — related
  • Teachers raise big-picture concerns about GenAI including surveillance, over-reliance, reduced teacher collaboration, job replacement, and training needs — related
  • AI pilots found models not yet capable of high-quality math output generation, including multiple choice problems, coherent word problems, and math visuals — related
  • Educators use AI tools mainly for personalized learning and language support and frequently use generative AI for lesson planning — related
  • Generic AI tools can undermine curriculum coherence and pedagogical goals, producing low-quality artifacts that do not match district beliefs — a broader claim this one bears on
  • ChatGPT shows high success on open-ended general chemistry exam questions but lower accuracy at the university level, and unverified copying of its answers is a reported risk — related
  • 90% of open-ended survey responses about the ChatGPT activity were neutral in sentiment, with 2% positive and 8% negative — related
  • Teachers designed tasks with dilemmas, unfamiliar scenarios, and differentiation intended to support transfer, without assessing capability — related
  • Lesson designers report LLM-generated lessons save substantial time drafting scenarios and multiple-choice questions — related
  • Teachers evaluated the LLM-robot system positively, citing ease of use for differentiated, multilingual lessons and no extra preparation time — reports the opposite
  • Teachers viewed ChatGPT as a source of inspiration rather than a generator of genuinely innovative lesson content — related
  • Early adopters report time savings from ChatGPT automation, including a Wharton professor's exam-writing productivity increase of 100% — related
  • Most teacher-created lessons did not rely substantially on existing OER, with teachers preferring Google search over OER repositories — related
  • Students frequently reported that personalized problem topics did not match their interests — related
  • Teachers actively use AI tools but face challenges with content accuracy and usage guidelines — related
  • For exam-oriented learners, trust in AI tutors hinges on curriculum fit: students abandon mathematically correct solutions that use out-of-syllabus methods — related
