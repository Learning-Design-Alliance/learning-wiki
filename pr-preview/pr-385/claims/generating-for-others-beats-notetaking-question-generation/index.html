Evidence¶

Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026¶

Sarah Shi Hui Wong, Stephen Lim. (2026). The Powerful Teacher: A Power Hypothesis for the Benefits of Learning-by-Teaching. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10097-1

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Randomized between-subjects experiment (N = 242; notetaking n = 81, explaining n = 80, teaching n = 81); 3 (learning method) × 2 (study topic) ANOVA on create question generation was significant, F(2, 236) = 18.23, p<.001, with "d = 0.57 and 0.91" for explaining and teaching versus notetaking.

"the explaining (M = 1.54, SD = 1.67) and teaching (M = 2.16, SD = 1.93) groups outperformed the notetaking (M = 0.75, SD = 1.06) group, p=.001 and p<.001, d = 0.57 and 0.91, respectively"

Sarah Shi Hui Wong 2026 (2)¶

Sarah Shi Hui Wong, Stephen Lim. (2026). The Powerful Teacher: A Power Hypothesis for the Benefits of Learning-by-Teaching. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10097-1

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same experiment's topic main effect on create question generation: more questions for "vanishing songbirds" than "origins of the moon". The learning method × study topic interaction was non-significant, "indicating that the advantages of explaining and teaching generalized across both topics."

"participants generated more create questions for the “vanishing songbirds” (M = 2.06, SD = 1.90) than “origins of the moon” (M = 0.92, SD = 1.22) topic, F(1, 236) = 35.35, p<.001, ŋp2 = 0.13"

Related Claims¶

  • Explaining and teaching both improve basic comprehension relative to notetaking, but do not differ from each other — related
  • Teaching (writing a verbatim lecture script) produces better create question generation than explaining (writing a textbook explanation) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Prompting learners to self-explain, one generative strategy, improves learning by a moderate average amount; other generative activities are not tested by the evidence recorded here — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Laptop note-takers transcribed more verbatim and did worse on conceptual questions than longhand note-takers in one set of experiments, but a direct replication found no consistent difference in test performance — related
  • Learning conditions did not differ in total questions attempted, ruling out differential effort quantity as the source of create-question differences — related
