Ying Xu 2023¶

Ying Xu, Kunlei He, Julian Levine, Daniel Ritchie, Zexuan Pan, Andres Bustamante, and Mark Warschauer. (2023). Artificial Intelligence Enhances Children's Science Learning from Television Shows. https://osf.io/j7upd

Same RCT regression analysis (four outliers excluded). Printed standardized effect size: 0.16 standard deviations — "0.16 standard deviations higher than those in the pseudo-interactive condition (p = .04)", with interactive vs non-interactive at p < .001 in the same model (Table 3, Figure 2B).

"children in the interactive condition scored 0.32 standard deviations higher than those in the non-interactive condition (p < .001) and 0.16 standard deviations higher than those in the pseudo-interactive condition (p = .04)"

Related Claims¶

  • Children who watched AI-powered interactive videos with contingent feedback scored 0.32 standard deviations higher on a science post-test than children who watched the non-interactive broadcast version — related
  • Pseudo-interactive videos also outperformed non-interactive videos by 0.16 standard deviations on science learning — related
  • Contingent AI interaction increased children's likelihood of responding over time (OR = 1.026 per question), while pseudo-interactive interaction decreased it (OR = 0.96) — related
  • Pseudo-interactive responses showed significantly more repetition: a lower type-token ratio than interactive responses (t = 3.371, p = .001) — related
  • Verbal engagement correlated more strongly with science learning in the interactive condition (response rate r = .485) than in the pseudo-interactive condition (r = .193) — related
  • Children in the interactive condition responded to 77.94% of Elinor's questions versus 68.13% in the pseudo-interactive condition — related
