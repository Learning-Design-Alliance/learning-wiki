Evidence¶

Kim Young-Suk Grace 2017¶

Kim Young-Suk Grace, Schatschneider Christopher. (2017). Expanding the developmental models of writing: A direct and indirect effects model of developmental writing (DIEW). Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000129

q2 · i? · associational · r2

SEM model (Figure 2) fitted to data from 193 Grade 1 children with excellent fit (CFI = .99, RMSEA = .06) tested the relation of higher-order cognitive skills to writing quality. The authors suggest children's inferencing ability appears important to writing quality for establishing global coherence.

"inference (β = .27, p = .003) was independently related to writing, whereas theory of mind (β = .08, p = .36) was not, after accounting for spelling, handwriting fluency, and working memory. A total of 59% of variance in writing was explained."

Related Claims¶

  • Discourse-level oral language, spelling, and handwriting fluency completely mediate the relations of language and cognitive skills to writing quality in Grade 1 — related
  • Higher order cognitive skills (inference, perspective taking, monitoring) are differentially related to writing quality, writing productivity, and correctness in writing in Grade 2 — related
  • Total effects on writing quality are largest for discourse-level oral language (.46), working memory (.43), and spelling (.37) — related
  • Discourse-level oral language is directly predicted by theory of mind, vocabulary, and working memory, but not inference or grammatical knowledge, in Grade 1 — related
  • Transcription, planning, revision, and self-efficacy explain 76% of writing-quality variance in Grades 4-6 and 82% in Grades 7-9 — related
