Evidence¶

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026¶

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

q2 · i? · review · r?

The review reports, citing studies using the optimal offloading paradigm grounded in rational choice theory, that individuals fail to reach optimal offloading levels and persistently overestimate tool effectiveness; biases persist even when incentives are introduced.

"individuals often fail to reach the optimal of ﬂoading level, typically using more reminders than necessary compared to the optimal level (Gilbert et al., 2020)."

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye 2026 (2)¶

Yufei Guo & Qun Ye. (2026). Meta-cognitive insights into cognitive offloading: mechanisms, interventions, and educational implications. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-06621-5

q2 · i? · review · r?

The review reports correlational evidence across multiple studies (Gilbert et al., 2020; Gilbert, 2024; Sun et al., 2025) linking reminder bias — deviation from optimal offloading strategy — to subjective confidence and reality-deviating metacognitive biases.

"Researchers have found strong positive correlations between reminder bias and both individual subjective con ﬁdence and metacognitive biases that deviate from reality across multiple studies"
