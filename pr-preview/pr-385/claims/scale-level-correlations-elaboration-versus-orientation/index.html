Evidence¶

Gonny Schellings 2011¶

Gonny Schellings. (2011). Applying learning strategy questionnaires: problems and possibilities. Metacognition and Learning, 6, 91–109. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9069-5

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Scale-level correlational analysis in the comparative study (16 ninth-graders), restricted to the two scales with moderate-to-high reliability; Elaboration & evaluation correlated r=.60, Orientation & planning r=.24.

"It appeared that the Elaboration & evaluation scale of the questionnaire reached a significant correlation ( r =.60) with the same scale in the think-aloud method, whereas the Orientation & planning scale did not ( r=.24)."

Related Claims¶

  • Low questionnaire–think-aloud correlations may have two alternative explanations besides poor self-report ability: the methods may measure different strategies or be aimed at different tasks — a broader claim this one bears on
  • A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Schellings' questionnaire-thinking-aloud convergent correlation of .51 rests on a small sample and is questionable against other studies — reports the opposite
  • Most subscale reliabilities of the task-specific questionnaire were moderate to low (three of four subscales with 16 students, all four with 190), despite acceptable full-instrument reliability — related
  • Self-report factors correlate weakly with metacognitive behavior while within-factor subcomponent correlations are larger, per studies reviewed for the meta-analysis — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In the case study, students answering the questionnaire while thinking aloud showed misunderstanding of items, general rather than task-specific reference perspectives, middle-category 'neutral' answering, and probable social desirability — related
  • Think-aloud reports are incomplete and sometimes nonveridical, particularly for metacognitive processes — related
