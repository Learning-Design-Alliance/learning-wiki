Evidence¶

Roland G. Fryer 2010¶

Roland G. Fryer, Jr. (2010). Financial Incentives and Student Achievement: Evidence from Randomized Trials. NBER Working Paper No. 15898. http://www.nber.org/papers/w15898

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The article compares its experimental effect sizes to those of well-known interventions, noting the Dallas reading comprehension effect (.180–.249 SD) is similar in magnitude to lowering class size from 24 to 16 students (.22 SD).

"relative to achievement-increasing education reform in the past few decades – Head Start, lowering class size, bonuses for eﬀective teachers to teach in high need schools – student incentives for certain inputs provide similar results at lower cost."

Related Claims¶

  • Financial incentives for reading books produce negative achievement effects for Spanish-speaking students in Dallas, consistent with a crowd-out hypothesis — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • DC math impacts of about one-third of a standard deviation are similar in magnitude to New Orleans reforms and larger than some well-known interventions — related
  • Instructional expenditures show the largest consistent association with achievement, with roughly 84% of variation in spending effectiveness driven by class size reductions, teacher salary increases, and reduced teacher turnover — related
  • Paying middle-school students for attendance, behavior, uniforms, and homework yields marginally significant reading gains and non-significant math gains in Washington, DC — related
  • Paying second-grade students to read books increases reading comprehension by roughly 0.18–0.25 standard deviations in Dallas — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Paying students for performance on standardized tests or for course grades does not increase achievement test scores in urban public schools — related
  • After three years of Teacher Incentive Fund implementation, pay-for-performance bonuses had small positive impacts on student math and reading achievement in ten evaluation districts — related
  • Offering pay-for-performance bonuses in 10 TIF districts increased student reading achievement by 1 percentile point after two years, with a similar-magnitude but statistically non-significant math effect — related
