Related Claims¶

  • Black students are suspended at the highest rate of any racial group, at 12% in 2017–18, with persistent disparities across all years analyzed — related
  • Black students and students with disabilities in Maryland were suspended and expelled at more than twice the rates of other students — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Intersecting identities compound suspension risk: 0.1% of Asian girls without special education services in elementary schools were suspended versus 27% of Black boys with disabilities in secondary schools — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Youth with an IEP experience bullying and suspension at higher rates and are less engaged in school and social activities than peers, though most youth with and without an IEP feel positive about school — related
  • For the same types of infractions, Black students and students with disabilities were significantly more likely to receive out-of-school suspensions than other subgroups — related
  • Students with low prior test scores and students with disabilities were suspended at substantially higher rates than their peers in CPS — related
  • Students in alternative schools experience extraordinarily high lost-instruction rates, with Black boys losing 235 days per 100 enrolled — related
  • Racial differences in suspension rates reflect policies and adult biases, not differences in behavior — related
  • Students experiencing homelessness change schools at triple the rate of other students and are twice as likely to receive an out-of-school suspension — related
  • Students with disabilities are overrepresented in school discipline: 16 percent of enrollment but 25–28 percent of suspensions and expulsions — related
