Evidence¶

Stevens 2012¶

Stevens, Courtney; Bavelier, Daphné. (2012). The role of selective attention on academic foundations: A cognitive neuroscience perspective. Developmental cognitive neuroscience, vol. 2S, p. S30–S48. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2011.11.001

q2 · i? · review · r?

The review's second section, on the developmental time course of selective attention, argues this two-part account: developmental differences reflect deployment and response-conflict systems, whereas "once effectively deployed, selective attention acts through very similar neural mechanisms across ages." No effect sizes are printed in the available text.

"It is argued that developmental differences in selective attention are related to the neural systems important for deploying selective attention and managing response conflict. In contrast, once effectively deployed, selective attention acts through very similar neural mechanisms across ages."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Selective attention processes are hypothesized to relate to language, literacy, and mathematics, three domains important to early academic skills
  • Attention and self-regulation skills are trainable in children, with reported behavioral and neural changes across curricula and intervention programs — related
  • Children of higher maternal education showed a significantly larger attention effect on neural responses (mean attended–unattended difference 1.7 vs. 0.6 μV) — related
  • The effect of selective auditory attention on early neural processing differs between children of higher and lower maternal education (significant group × attention interaction within 100–200 msec) — related
  • The group difference in neural attentional modulation is tied to reduced distractor suppression in the unattended channel, not to differences in response to attended stimuli — related
