Evidence¶

Lonigan 2016¶

Lonigan, C. J., & Phillips, B. M. (2016). Response to Instruction in Preschool: Results of Two Randomized Studies with Children At Significant Risk of Reading Difficulties. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000054

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Study 1 moderation analysis of language outcomes by qualification status. Dual-qualified intervention children outscored controls on CELF Expressive Language, t(44.17) = 2.50, p < .02 (ES = .42), whereas language-only children did not differ from controls (p = .78).

"Children who qualified for both the language and code-related instruction (Adj. M = 20.44, SD = 6.67) scored significantly higher than did children in the control group (Adj. M = 17.66, SD = 6.64) at posttest on the Expressive Language subscale of the CELF, t(44.17) = 2.50, p < .02 (ES = .42)"

Lonigan 2016 (2)¶

Lonigan, C. J., & Phillips, B. M. (2016). Response to Instruction in Preschool: Results of Two Randomized Studies with Children At Significant Risk of Reading Difficulties. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000054

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Study 1 moderation analysis on the Definitional Vocabulary subtest of the Pre-CTOPPP. Language-only qualified intervention children scored significantly below controls, t(53.02) = 2.79, p < .01 (ES = -.87); dual-qualified children did not differ from controls (p = .93).

"Children who qualified for just the language instruction (Adj. M = 34.41, SD = 9.60) scored significantly lower than did children in the control group (Adj. M = 45.59, SD = 13.49) at posttest on this outcome, t(53.02) = 2.79, p < .01 (ES = -.87)"
