Evidence¶

Ehrlich 2016¶

Ehrlich, S.B., Pacchiano, D.M., Stein, A.G., & Luppescu, S. (2016). Essential organizational supports for early education: The development of a new survey tool to measure organizational conditions. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research and the Ounce of Prevention Fund. http://consortium.uchicago.edu/surveys

q2 · i? · design · r2

The brief reports, citing Bryk et al. (2010), that responses to the K-12 5Essentials surveys "strongly predicted which schools were most and least likely to improve over time." This is prior research reported as background for the early education adaptation, not a new analysis in this brief.

"elementary schools strong in three or more of these supports were 10 times more likely than schools weak in most supports to substantially improve student math and reading test scores over the next few years"

Sebring P. 2014¶

Sebring P., & Montgomery, N. (2014). The Five Essential Supports for School Improvement: Mobilizing the Findings. Pensamiento Educativo. Revista de Investigación Educacional Latinoamericana, 51(1), 63-85. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/five-essential-supports-school-improvement-mobilizing-findings doi:10.7764/pel.51.1.2014.7

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The UChicago Consortium's 2010 book-length research on public elementary schools compared schools strong on clusters of the five essential supports with schools weak in these supports, reporting a ten-fold difference in likelihood of improving reading and mathematics learning. The overview reports this as the study's headline finding without printing an effect size.

"Schools strong on clusters of these supports were 10 times more likely to improve learning of reading and mathematics, compared to schools weak in these supports."

Sebring 2006¶

Sebring, P. B., Allensworth, E., Bryk, A. S., Easton, J. Q., & Luppescu, S. (2006). The Essential Supports for School Improvement. Research Report, Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/essential-supports-school-improvement

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of Chicago public elementary schools, 1990-1996, using ITBS-based academic productivity measures and 1994 CCSR teacher and student surveys to measure the essential supports. The report found schools strong in most supports "at least ten times more likely" to show substantial gains, and very unlikely to stagnate.

"We found that schools strong in most of the essential supports were at least ten times more likely than schools weak in most of the supports to show substantial gains in both reading and mathematics."

de la Torre 2013¶

de la Torre, M., Allensworth, E., Jagesic, S., Sebastian, J., & Salmonowicz, M.; with Meyers, C., & Gerdeman, R. D. (2013). Turning Around Low-Performing Schools in Chicago. University of Chicago Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/turning-around-low-performing-schools-chicago-full-report

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The report attributes this finding to prior CCSR research examining 100 elementary schools that made significant progress over seven years and 100 that did not. It is reported second-hand as supporting context; the underlying study's design is not described in this report.

"schools strong on at least three of five essential elements—effective leaders, collaborative teachers, strong family and community ties, ambitious instruction, and safe and orderly learning climate—were 10 times more likely to improve and 30 times less likely to stagnate than those that were strong on just one or two."
