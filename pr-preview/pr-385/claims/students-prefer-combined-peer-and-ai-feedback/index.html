Evidence¶

Sperber 2025¶

Sperber, Lisa; MacArthur, Marit; Minnillo, Sophia; Stillman, Nicholas; Whithaus, Carl. (2025). Peer and AI Review + Reflection (PAIRR): A human-centered approach to formative assessment. Computers and Composition 76. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compcom.2025.102921

q2 · i? · design · r2

Post-intervention survey across all courses in both terms; the article reports the majority of students preferred combined feedback, with a significant portion still preferring peer feedback alone (36%). Only 6% preferred ChatGPT feedback alone, per the same results section.

"the majority of all students preferred to receive feedback from both ChatGPT and peers (58%), though a significant portion still preferred peer feedback alone (36%)"

Sperber 2025 (2)¶

Sperber, Lisa; MacArthur, Marit; Minnillo, Sophia; Stillman, Nicholas; Whithaus, Carl. (2025). Peer and AI Review + Reflection (PAIRR): A human-centered approach to formative assessment. Computers and Composition 76. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compcom.2025.102921

q2 · i? · design · r2

Post-intervention survey item on usefulness (Fig. 3) shows near-identical agreement counts for peer and ChatGPT feedback, though peer feedback generated a higher variation of opinion; AI utility was experienced in the context of human feedback.

"312 students agreeing or strongly agreeing that peer feedback is useful and 308 students agreeing or strongly agreeing that ChatGPT feedback is useful"
