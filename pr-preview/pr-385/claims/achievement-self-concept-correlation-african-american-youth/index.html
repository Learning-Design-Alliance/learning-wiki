Okeke 2009¶

Okeke, N. A., Howard, L. C., Kurtz-Costes, B., & Rowley, S. J. (2009). Academic race stereotypes, academic self-concept, and racial centrality in African American youth. Journal of Black Psychology, 35(3), 366–387. https://doi.org/10.1177/0095798409333615

Correlational analysis of Study 1 descriptive data (N = 237) before main analyses. The article prints the correlation r = .40 (p < .01) between EOG math and reading percentile scores and academic self-concept.

Related Claims¶

  • Among African American eighth graders high in racial centrality, stronger endorsement of the traditional academic race stereotype is associated with lower academic self-concept, controlling achievement and demographics (Study 1) — related
  • African American middle school students on average endorse the traditional academic race stereotype while reporting high academic self-concept — related
  • In an independent seventh-grade sample, stereotype endorsement predicted lower academic self-concept at average and high racial centrality but not at low centrality (Study 2 replication) — related
  • The attitude-achievement paradox: disadvantaged minority youth may show low achievement despite high motivation to achieve upward mobility through schooling — related
