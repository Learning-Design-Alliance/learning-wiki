q2 · i? · review · r1

The primer reports, citing Firetto et al. (2019) and Noroozi et al. (2013), that scaffolding prompts yield "significantly more domain-specific and domain-general knowledge". The primer prints no effect sizes or study details.

"Prompts and scaffolds that help students paraphrase, criticize, ask questions, and synthesize arguments facilitate knowledge construction, resulting in students gaining significantly more domain-specific and domain-general knowledge (Firetto et al., 2019; Noroozi et al., 2013)."
