Evidence¶

Hughes 2018¶

Hughes, J. N., West, S. G., Kim, H., & Bauer, S. S. (2018). Effect of Early Grade Retention on School Completion: A Prospective Study. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7), 974-991. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000243

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Moderator analysis of the graduation versus dropout contrast using propensity-score-weighted multinomial logistic regression. The article reports the omnibus three-way interaction as "significant (χ2(2) = 6.94, p = .031)" with the printed follow-up contrasts; no standardised effect size is printed.

"The overall retention x ethnicity x gender interaction was significant (χ2(2) = 6.94, p = .031) with a trend for Hispanics (z = −1.82, p = .07, odds ratio = 0.04) and a significant effect for Blacks (z = −2.88, p = .004, odds ratio = 0.003) relative to Whites."

Hughes 2018 (2)¶

Hughes, J. N., West, S. G., Kim, H., & Bauer, S. S. (2018). Effect of Early Grade Retention on School Completion: A Prospective Study. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7), 974-991. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000243

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Descriptive pattern of the significant three-way interaction reported in the Results moderator section: Hispanic males were less negatively affected than Black and White males, while "Black females were the most negatively affected among females"; descriptive, no test statistic printed for this sentence.

"In contrast, White females were less negatively affected by early grade retention than Hispanic females. Black females were the most negatively affected among females from the three ethnic groups."
