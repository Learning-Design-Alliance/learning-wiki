Evidence¶

Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente 2010¶

Nicole C. Krämer & Gary Bente. (2010). Personalizing e-Learning. The Social Effects of Pedagogical Agents. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9123-x

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review summarizes HCI evaluation studies (e.g., Reeves and Nass; Sproull et al.) showing that "even minimal cues and similarity with humans suffice to lead users to show behavior that would be expected in human –human interaction."

Related Claims¶

  • The review reports no evidence that the mere visual presence of a human-like body produces distinct effects, though likeable appearance improves motivation and transfer — reports the opposite
  • Manipulating anthropomorphism shows no clear causal effect on trust in controlled post-generative-AI studies, though long-term use and cue type condition its effects — related
  • A 2024 vendor white paper cites research that avatars provide social presence and that realistic avatars in intelligent tutoring systems can increase student-tutor engagement — related
  • Participants remain socially sensitive to conversational stance even while categorical identity inference fails — related
  • Human embodiment in video: perceived social presence benefits learning ratings, but instructor-face inclusion shows no significant learning-performance difference, and learners prefer human over robot presenters with mixed recall — related
  • Users alter expectations and behavior based on perceived AI capabilities even when actual AI performance is unchanged (the placebo effect of AI) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • CASTLE's activation hypothesis states that social cues activate social schemata and trigger social processes — related
  • Social presence from social cues can improve task performance (social facilitation) but may impair performance on complex tasks (social inhibition) — related
