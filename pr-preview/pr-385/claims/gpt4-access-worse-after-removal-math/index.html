Related Claims¶

  • How genAI is used, not who uses it, predicts whether independent performance declines or improves — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Heavy reliance on generative AI can undermine learning: students with GPT-4 access performed better on practice problems but worse on exams once the tool was removed — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • AI-induced gains are not purely delegation: treated participants do not perform worse once AI is removed, and lower-education participants retain part of their gain, though a 0.200 SD gap re-emerges — reports the opposite
  • Performance improvements from generative AI assistance diminished once the assistance was removed — related
  • Students given AI assistance in peer feedback tended to rely on the AI-generated feedback and struggled to perform the task when the assistance was removed — related
  • Actors internalized the AI's questioning style, self-generating similar questions after the tool was removed — related
  • Access to a standard GPT-4 chat tutor during practice substantially improves practice performance in high school mathematics — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In a randomized experiment on fraction problems, participants who used an AI assistant solved fewer unassisted test problems and skipped more than control participants — related
  • Students do not perceive the learning harm from copying GPT solutions and overestimate their exam performance — related
