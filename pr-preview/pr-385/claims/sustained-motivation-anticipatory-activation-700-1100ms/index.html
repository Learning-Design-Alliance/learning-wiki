Evidence¶

Banerjee Snigdha 2015¶

Banerjee Snigdha, Frey Hans-Peter, Molholm Sophie, Foxe John J. (2015). Interests shape how adolescents pay attention: The interaction of motivation and top-down attentional processes in biasing sensory activations to anticipated events. The European Journal of Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.12810

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Exploratory statistical cluster-plot analysis (point-wise paired t-tests, alpha criterion 0.05 sustained ≥20 consecutive points) followed by repeated-measures ANOVAs over right and left parieto-occipital ROIs in 24 adolescents.

"The high motivation condition was of greater amplitude for all 4 time-periods (700-800 ms, F(1,23) = 9.54, p = .005; 800-900 ms, F(1,23) = 9.48, p = .005; 900-1000 ms, F(1,23) = 8.67, p = .007; 1000-1100 ms, F(1,23) = 9.28, p = .006)."

Related Claims¶

  • Motivation modulates anticipatory attentional processing: high-interest stimuli produced larger LDAP-period amplitude over right parieto-occipital cortex — related
  • Sensory evoked responses to the cue stimuli did not differ between high- and low-interest blocks, arguing against block-level arousal differences — related
  • Adolescent anticipatory alpha-band activity lacks the adult-like lateralized suppression pattern, instead showing higher right-parieto-occipital alpha and stronger modulation for cue-left than cue-right — related
  • Adolescents show adult-like anticipatory broadband ERP attention components (ADAN, LDAP, BRN) contralateral to the attended location — related
  • Adolescents show higher target-detection sensitivity (d-prime) for high-interest than low-interest stimuli, with better performance in the right hemifield — related
  • Motivation did not interact with visuospatial attention effects on post-stimulus visual evoked potentials to imperative stimuli — related
