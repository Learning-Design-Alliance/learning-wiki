Evidence¶

Roderick 2000¶

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Cohort tracking of students retained in 1997 and 1998 through their retained year, including a new January retest chance in 1998-1999 that about one-quarter used. Performance was "only slightly better than in 1997."

"After two years in the same grade and two summers in Summer Bridge, 53 percent of third graders re- tained in 1998 were able to meet the test score cutoffs, compared with 43 percent of third grad- ers retained in 1997."

Roderick 2000 (2)¶

Roderick, M., Nagaoka, J., Bacon, J., & Easton, J. Q. (2000). Update: Ending Social Promotion: Passing, Retention, and Achievement Trends Among Promoted and Retained Students 1995-1999. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://www.consortium-chicago.org

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Cohort tracking of retained and Transition Center eighth graders. The brief attributes the dismal progress partly to dropout: the one-year dropout rate for these students was roughly comparable in 1998 and 1999.

"Only about 38 percent of eighth grad- ers who were retained or sent to T ransition Cen- ters in 1998 were able to raise their scores to the test cutoff by August."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Early-grade retention rose after the policy, and roughly 20 percent of first and second graders who were retained experienced a second retention in third grade — related
  • Students who failed both reading and math made up the majority of retained students, and fewer than 20% of them passed both subjects in Summer Bridge — related
  • Second-year outcomes were worst for retained eighth graders and Transition Center students: only 27% met the criteria by May 1998 and about 16% of officially retained eighth graders dropped out — related
  • Only 43% of retained third graders and 47% of retained sixth graders met the test criteria by summer 1998 despite four chances, yet over two-thirds were promoted the next year largely due to waivers — related
  • Retained students showed smaller three-year achievement gains than previously socially promoted students, though Rasch adjustment erased the gap for once-retained third graders — related
  • Fewer than 60 percent of retained third and sixth graders in 1998 and 1999 raised their test scores to the promotional cutoff even with an extra January chance to pass — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Eighth-grade achievement rose substantially in postpolicy cohorts, with the 1998 cohort's included students more than half a standard deviation above prepolicy levels
  • In 1997, 31% of third graders and about 20% of sixth and eighth graders were excluded from Chicago's promotional test-score policy, with bilingual education the main exclusion reason in third grade and special education in sixth and eighth — related
