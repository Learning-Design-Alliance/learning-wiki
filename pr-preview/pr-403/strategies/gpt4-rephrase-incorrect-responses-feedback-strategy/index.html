Use GPT-4 few-shot rephrasing of incorrect trainee responses to deliver corrective explanatory feedback at scale¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 9 claims (7 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (4 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend integrating GPT-4 classification and rephrasing into template-based feedback systems so novice tutors receive real-time explanatory feedback showing the desired response form. Because "including the correct answer within the feedback substantially enhances its efficacy", rephrasing the trainee's own incorrect response into a correct form personalizes corrective feedback while mitigating the time and effort demands of human graders.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Lesson-specific response principles and scenario context in the prompt
  • A small set of paired correct/incorrect example rephrasings for few-shot prompting
  • Expert-annotated examples to establish classification reliability

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness on tutor skill acquisition, retention, and real-world application not yet evaluated; the authors plan longitudinal controlled studies
  • Results demonstrated on three lessons only

Target Learners¶

  • novice tutors and trainees in online scenario-based tutoring training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • giving effective praise
  • reacting to errors
  • determining what students know

Affordances¶

  • Framework Quality Rephrased Responses

Claims¶

  • A GPT-4 few-shot binary classifier effectively identifies correct/incorrect trainee responses across three tutor-training lessons, with average F1 of 0.84 and AUC of 0.85 [+M]
  • Few-shot learning with increasing shots generally improves GPT-4 classification performance, with the 5-shot variant strongest for Reacting to Errors (F1 0.867, AUC 0.866, TNR 0.83) [+M]
  • Classification was weakest for the Determining What Students Know lesson, where zero-shot learning scored F1 0.66 and AUC 0.668 [~M]
  • For the Giving Effective Praise lesson, GPT-4-rephrased responses were rated significantly more accurate than human-expert rephrasings (p < 0.001), with no significant difference in responsiveness [+M]
  • For Determining What Students Know, no significant difference in accuracy or responsiveness was found between GPT-4 and human rephrasings [+M]
  • For Reacting to Errors, GPT-4 rephrasings were rated more accurate than human rephrasings (few-shot p < 0.01, zero-shot p < 0.05) with no significant responsiveness difference [+M]
  • GPT-4o's final-turn correctness labeling on CoMTA is only slightly less accurate than expert human annotators, which the authors read as close to human-level performance on a challenging task. [+M]
  • In zero-shot evaluation, GPT-4's multi-label classification results were similar to what might be expected of fine-tuned classifiers, with SetFit and GPT-3.5 showing contrasting precision/recall patterns [+W]
  • AI scoring shows a quality-conditioned pattern: strong agreement on fully correct and fully incorrect responses but substantial degradation on mid-range responses [~M]
