Use generative AI as an educator content-creation tool for student critique, supported by explicit guidelines and training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that generative AI might serve educators more effectively as a content-creation tool rather than a student resource: AI generates essays, articles, case studies or data sets, which students then critically evaluate. The authors state this approach "aligns with the observed strengths in students' critical evaluation skills" and could offer educators time savings, provided the AI-generated content meets academic standards set through appropriate parameters.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The quality of the AI-generated content must meet certain academic standards, which educators control by setting appropriate parameters

Constraints¶

  • The article states ChatGPT is not a substitute for human-led academic enquiry

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students, including STEM and healthcare programmes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation of AI-generated content
  • active rather than passive engagement with material
  • analytical skills and reasoned judgment

Claims¶

  • Marks for ChatGPT-generated essays show no significant difference from participants' overall course mark averages [+W]
  • ChatGPT-generated essays showed high written English and PEEL-framework structure across native and non-native speakers [+W]
  • The main concern in all critical evaluations was the veracity of ChatGPT-provided references, which often do not exist [+W]
  • Critical evaluations of ChatGPT-generated essays scored significantly higher than participants' overall course averages [+W]
  • Focus-group participants preferred creating their own essays while viewing ChatGPT as an initial resource for fundamentals and idea generation [~W]
