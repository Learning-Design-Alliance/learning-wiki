Teachers should design interaction processes carefully to foster synchronization in learning behaviors and neural activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article closes with a concrete recommendation for future teaching activities: teachers should plan their interaction processes deliberately and work to engage students' attention. It states that "teachers should design their interaction processes with students carefully and scientiﬁcally and strive to catch students' attention and engage them, thereby fostering synchronization in learning behaviors and neural activities between teachers and students." This translates the study's IBS findings into practice guidance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must plan the interaction schedule in advance and deliver it during the teacher-student interaction phase following a pre-class lecture video

Constraints¶

  • Derived from a preliminary, one-on-one laboratory study; the article calls for larger samples and real-world classroom tests before broader application

Target Learners¶

  • university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • declarative conceptual knowledge of taught content

Affordances¶

  • Ibs Neural Foundation Structured Interaction

Claims¶

  • Flipped classroom teaching alone shows enhanced class-related interbrain synchrony in left DLPFC, STG, and S1 [+W]
  • Perceived quantity of teacher-student interaction does not predict learning improvements [+W]
  • Flipped classroom teaching with structured interaction improves learning outcomes more than lecture-based teaching and a video-lecture control [+W]
  • Structured teacher-student interaction yields higher interbrain synchrony in left DLPFC than spontaneous interaction [+W]
