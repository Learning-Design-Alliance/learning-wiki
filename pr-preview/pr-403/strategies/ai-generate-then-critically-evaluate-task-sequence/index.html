AI-generated-essay-then-critical-evaluation task sequence for teaching critical evaluation skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy embeds ChatGPT into pedagogy as a catalyst for critical thinking rather than a task-completion shortcut. Learners first receive an orientation on ChatGPT's capabilities and limitations, then generate an essay with the tool and critically evaluate that output, anchoring their critique in peer-reviewed literature. The article frames this as "critically evaluating the output using peer-reviewed sources", making each AI statement something that "warrants verification" and driving deeper engagement with the literature.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners are first introduced to ChatGPT's capabilities and limitations, including prompt formulation and editorial adjustment, in an orientation session

Constraints¶

  • The article states AI should be used as a supplement, not a dependency, and that over-reliance risks curtailing learners' capacity for original thought

Target Learners¶

  • postgraduate master's students in biomedical science programmes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation of scientific content
  • reading and assessing academic literature
  • research and analytical skills

Claims¶

  • Marks for ChatGPT-generated essays show no significant difference from participants' overall course mark averages [~W]
  • ChatGPT-generated essays showed high written English and PEEL-framework structure across native and non-native speakers [~W]
  • The main concern in all critical evaluations was the veracity of ChatGPT-provided references, which often do not exist [+W]
  • Critical evaluations of ChatGPT-generated essays scored significantly higher than participants' overall course averages [+W]
  • Focus-group participants preferred creating their own essays while viewing ChatGPT as an initial resource for fundamentals and idea generation [~W]
  • Against similar course assessment types, critical evaluation marks improved and essay marks decreased, but neither difference was significant [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed AI as a learning partner and redesign assessments for AI-rich contexts
  • Redesign assessments to require critical thinking, originality, interactive activities, and real-time or proctored formats
  • Design AI-integration activities that set explicit expectations for AI-assisted work, integrate reflective assessments of AI outputs, and build tasks strengthening critical thinking and academic integrity
