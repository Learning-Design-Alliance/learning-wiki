Use PLC WhatsApp group logs as a noninvasive, continuous data source for monitoring community processes and professional development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that researchers and community leaders use WhatsApp group logs as "a source of data for a noninvasive continuous diagnosis of group behavior, relationships, and online dynamics". Because the channel was spontaneous, unguided, and minimally supervised, it reflected participants' needs, behaviors, and professional development, enabling supervisors to track changes in key PLC characteristics and adjust meeting programs responsively. The approach integrates SNA structure analysis with content coding for macro, meso, and micro insights.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A WhatsApp group used by the community with minimal supervisor intervention, plus background questionnaires for participant attributes; analysis combines SNA metrics with content annotation.

Constraints¶

  • The study was carried out within an established community with previous acquaintance and common professional knowledge; communities newly established during the pandemic may yield different results.
  • Setting the unit of analysis as a single WhatsApp message may bias results because individuals use multiple messages to talk around the same point.

Target Learners¶

  • STEM teacher leaders and PLC supervisors
  • researchers of teacher professional development

Target Learning Goals¶

  • monitoring professional development and community dynamics of teacher learning communities

Affordances¶

  • Plc Whatsapp Discourse Coding Scheme

Claims¶

  • COVID-19 accelerated professional knowledge exchange (CK and PCK) in the L-PLC WhatsApp discourse, with tags significantly higher in pandemic cohorts [+W]
  • Requests for and sharing of technological knowledge rose sharply during COVID-19 (TK from 9 to 206 tags; ASK-TK from 6 to 82 tags between cohorts I and III) [+W]
  • Positive Feedback tags peaked significantly in the first pandemic cohort, interpreted as socioemotional support during a time of crisis [+W]
  • Sharing of teaching materials became a norm in the L-PLC during COVID-19, rising significantly in pandemic cohorts and remaining steady thereafter [+W]
