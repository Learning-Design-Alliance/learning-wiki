Design school and college interventions that enhance cognitive reappraisal to improve student wellbeing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools and colleges develop interventions targeting cognitive reappraisal, on the grounds that reappraisal is positively linked to school wellbeing in both between- and within-person longitudinal models and is "a strategy that is modifiable by intervention". Because wellbeing also predicted later reappraisal, the authors argue that enhancing students' sense of wellbeing is beneficial for promoting the development of cognitive reappraisal, so interventions may act on either side of the reciprocal loop.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should target reappraisal skills, such as reframing negative appraisals of academic situations

Constraints¶

  • The study is longitudinal and correlational; it establishes reciprocal predictive relations, not tested intervention effects

Target Learners¶

  • secondary and upper secondary students aged 11-19 in schools and colleges

Target Learning Goals¶

  • school-related wellbeing
  • emotion regulation

Affordances¶

  • Eras Emotion Regulation Achievement Situations

Claims¶

  • Cognitive reappraisal positively predicts subsequent school-related wellbeing in both between-person (CLPM) and within-person (RI-CLPM) longitudinal models [+W]
  • Positive feedback loops: wellbeing mediates reappraisal stability and reappraisal mediates wellbeing stability over 12 months [+W]
  • School-related wellbeing positively predicts subsequent cognitive reappraisal, making the relation reciprocal [+W]
  • Reciprocal relations models fit better than unidirectional or baseline alternatives for both CLPM and RI-CLPM [+W]
  • Expressive suppression is not significantly related to school-related wellbeing over time in either model [~W]
  • Reappraisal negatively predicts subsequent suppression, but suppression does not predict subsequent reappraisal [~W]
