Develop intelligent techniques for collaboration at scale in massive online learning settings¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 mixed, 4 against) · 5 studies (2 theoretical, 1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper argues that current MOOCs use simple technology—video, quizzes, forums—with little AIED involvement, and that intelligent support for learning together at distance and scale is an opportunity. It states "There are opportunities for intelligent techniques that make learning together possible at distance and at scale, with personalized guidance on content and on the collaboration process – even intertwined with each other." Foundations exist in AIED, but demonstrated results are not yet mature enough.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • AIED's existing group-modeling foundations, plus solutions for dynamically joining and leaving participants from different cultures

Constraints¶

  • The paper states demonstrated results are not mature enough today to fully exploit the potential to support instructors and participants of large-scale online classes

Target Learners¶

  • participants in massive open online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective collaborative learning supported by personalized guidance

Claims¶

  • MOOCs failed to deliver on their proponents' promises of transforming higher education [-W]
  • MOOC video engagement declines with video length, with median engagement under half the duration for videos longer than nine minutes [-W]
  • A review of music education asserts that MOOC completion is typically under 10% and that embodied musical learning resists short video lectures and self-graded quizzes [-W]
  • A University of Pennsylvania study found only about half of MOOC registrants viewed a lecture and only about four percent finished a course entirely [-W]
  • The UDMOOC reaches a learner demographic unlike typical MOOC cohorts, with a 42% completion rate [~W]
