Use educative and restorative behavior supports rather than punitive and exclusionary discipline¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Gateway's approach to behavior is restorative rather than punitive. The report states that "Gateway's behavior supports and practices are educative and restorative rather than punitive and exclusionary" and that "Through restorative practices, students receive guidance around effective communication, conflict resolution, and problem-solving." The restorative process focuses on repairing relationships, restoring community, and building skills for maintaining positive relationships with peers, faculty, and staff.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Initial groundwork established by a school culture of strong relationships and inclusivity, which the report says makes the restorative practices effective

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness is attributed to the pre-existing relational culture, so the practice depends on that foundation

Target Learners¶

  • middle and high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective communication and conflict resolution
  • relationship repair and community membership

Claims¶

  • SEL programs cannot produce meaningful long-term growth in authoritarian, punitive, or exclusionary environments; restorative practices are needed [+W]
  • Restorative practices reduce exclusionary discipline and misbehavior and improve school climate, safety, and achievement, though implementation requires sustained investment [+W]
  • Shifting to restorative practices reduced exclusionary discipline and its racial disparities while improving climate and achievement [+W]
