Instructors foster metacognition through reflection on process, direct immediate feedback, and taught study habits¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Grounded in the finding that better-calibrated students deployed more expert-like lateral prefrontal activity, the authors recommend classroom practices that nurture metacognition and self-awareness. Instructors "might provide opportunities for students to reﬂect on their coursework by focusing on the learning process rather than on the content itself", provide "direct and i m m e d i a t ef e e d b a c k", and teach effective study habits so students can act on their metacognitive judgments. These strategies aim to move STEM students toward more expert model evaluation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction must include opportunities for self-reflection on the learning process, immediate feedback following errors, and explicit teaching of study habits students may lack

Constraints¶

  • The neural findings are correlational; the authors state that whether instructional manipulations such as immediate feedback impact students' brain activity is a question for future research

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate STEM students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning
  • error detection and learning from errors
  • expert-like scientific model evaluation

Affordances¶

  • Metacognitive Calibration Conceptual Change Account

Claims¶

  • Undergraduate life sciences students with higher metacognitive calibration show greater right dorsolateral prefrontal activity when evaluating error-containing biology models, controlling for accuracy [+M]
  • Metacognitive measures show minimal correlations with course grades except meta-d', while task accuracy correlates with grades [~W]
  • Self-regulated learning improves achievement [+M]
