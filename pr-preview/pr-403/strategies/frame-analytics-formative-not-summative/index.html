Frame automated writing analytics as formative guidance rather than summative grading¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

AWA deliberately positions its feedback not as a summative grade but as formative guidance for improving draft writing, presented as a critical prototype students are told to challenge if they disagree with its output. The design emphasizes making the tool's mode of action transparent — what the authors term algorithmic accountability — including bolding the linguistic expressions that triggered each highlight to help users understand why sentences were flagged.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students receive a face-to-face briefing introducing the tool and its prototype status
  • Discipline-specific sample texts and examples of each rhetorical move in the user guide

Constraints¶

  • The parser does not yet provide feedback above sentence level, leaving whole-text positioning to student reflection

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students drafting analytical essays

Target Learning Goals¶

  • formative self-assessment of writing
  • metacognitive reflection on writing quality

Claims¶

  • Students reported AWA highlighting provoked reflection on their writing despite questioning its accuracy [+W]
  • Students identified lack of sensitivity to linguistic nuance as a limitation of AWA feedback [+W]
  • Students showed clear appetite for automated writing support and comfort with machine-generated feedback [+W]
