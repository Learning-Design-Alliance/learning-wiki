Evidence¶

Wei 2026¶

Wei, Y., and Perkins, M. (2026). Generative AI and student collaboration: a scoping review of group work processes, outcomes, and risks. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00217-x

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Baskara, Patole and He et al., perceived cognitive offloading risks; the review notes only two empirical investigations (He et al. 2024; Scala et al. 2025) support critical-thinking concerns.

"Overreliance on GenAI might demotivate learners from thinking critically (Baskara 2024; Patole 2024), and participants expressed concern that "I'm not critically thinking" (He et al. 2024) when delegating problem-solving tasks to AI, highlighting perceived risks of cognitive offloading."

Wei 2026 (2)¶

Wei, Y., and Perkins, M. (2026). Generative AI and student collaboration: a scoping review of group work processes, outcomes, and risks. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00217-x

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Scala et al.'s (2025) randomised experiment comparing human-only ideation with AI-supported teams, in which the AI collaborator "produced more innovative suggestions" without significantly enhancing participants' overall innovativeness; equivalence was not tested.

"a randomised experiment comparing human-only ideation with AI-supported teams revealed that while the AI collaborator produced more innovative suggestions, it did not significantly enhance participants' overall innovativeness."
