q3 · i? · causal · r2

Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc robot comparisons in the N = 81 repeated-measures experiment; estimated marginal means were R1: M = 3.17, R2: M = 3.17, R3: M = 3.10. The article concludes "the type of robot did not meaningfully influence participants' trust". No effect size is printed for these comparisons.

"Pairwise comparisons between robots revealed no statistically significant differences in trust. Differences between R1–R2 (p<.05), R1–R3 (p = .069), and R2–R3 (p = .062) did not reach significance after Bonferroni adjustment."

Related Claims¶

  • The interaction between robot appearance and task on trust was only marginal, suggesting appearance may slightly modulate expectations without determining trust — related
  • Task type strongly determines trust in educational robots, with highest trust for procedural instruction, moderate trust for teaching, and lowest trust when robots request personal information — related
  • Trust evaluations were strongly and positively correlated across all robot–task combinations, suggesting trust in social robots may be a stable, generalized attitude — related
  • Instructional approaches do not differ significantly in monitoring or reflecting, and omnibus differences in orienting and planning show no significant pairwise differences — related
  • Hypotheses that specific robot appearances would be trusted more in matching tasks (humanoids for teaching, task-oriented robots for instructions, soft robots for personal disclosure) were not confirmed — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
