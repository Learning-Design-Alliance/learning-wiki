Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In an initial eye-tracking study, time on action predicted self-explanation (16-second threshold) and gaze shifts were most reliable at detecting its absence
  • Time and gaze-shift evidence are complementary predictors of self-explanation: time alone yields higher sensitivity, gaze shifts alone higher specificity, and the combination the best accuracy
  • Adding eye-tracking evidence to ACE's student model significantly improves assessment of implicit self-explanation over a time-only model — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Customizing knowledge-node priors from pretest scores improves model accuracy, but tailoring SE-tendency priors yields no significant improvement — related
  • Leave-one-out cross-validation over 36 students confirms significant accuracy and stability gains from eye-tracking in both self-explanation and exploration assessment — related
