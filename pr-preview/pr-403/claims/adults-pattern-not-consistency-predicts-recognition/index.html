Evidence¶

Hsiao 2022¶

Hsiao, J. H., An, J., Hui, V. K. S., Zheng, Y. & Chan, A. B. (2022). Understanding the role of eye movement consistency in face recognition and autism through integrating deep neural networks and hidden Markov models. npj Science of Learning 7:28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00139-6

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Multiple regression reanalysis of data from Chuk et al. (adults): "recognition performance was best predicted by eye movement pattern" (R2 = 21.8%); entropy added no significant variance (ΔR2 = 0.002). Using Chan et al. data, pattern correlated with performance (r = −0.25) but consistency did not.

"recognition performance was best predicted by eye movement pattern, R2 = 21.8%, p = 0.001, and adding consistency did not significantly account for additional variance, ΔR2 = 0.002, p = 0.726"

Related Claims¶

  • A less eyes-focused pattern and lower eye movement consistency jointly predict poorer recognition beyond age and cognitive abilities — reports the opposite
  • Older age is associated with lower eye movement consistency in face recognition, reflected in higher overall entropy and C3 entropy — related
  • Lower eye movement consistency (higher C3 entropy) is associated with poorer selective attention and inhibitory control — related
  • A more eyes-focused scanning pattern and higher eye movement consistency are each correlated with better face recognition performance — related
  • In typically developing children aged 6–11, face recognition performance is predicted by eye movement consistency but not by eye movement pattern — related
  • Children's eye movement consistency is associated with selective attention and executive function, whereas eye movement pattern relates to working memory — related
  • In partially trained DNN+HMM models simulating children, eye movement consistency (lower entropy) predicts recognition accuracy but eye movement pattern does not — related
  • In well-trained DNN+HMM face-recognition models, an eyes-focused eye movement pattern yields higher recognition accuracy than a nose-focused pattern — related
