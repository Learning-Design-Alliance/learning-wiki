Evidence¶

Sommet 2015¶

Sommet, N., Darnon, C., & Butera, F. (2015). To Confirm or to Conform? Performance Goals as a Regulator of Conflict With More-Competent Others. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(2), 580–598. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037240

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Paper-and-pencil experiment with 73 French vocational school students (3 outliers dropped) randomly given performance-approach or performance-avoidance instructions. The regression interaction on the model-preference score showed "β =.28, F(1, 66) =5.60, p <.03, ηp2 =.08".

"As expected, the predicted inter- action between goals and partner’s competence was significant, /H9252/H11005.28, F(1, 66) /H110055.60, p /H11021.03, /H9257p2 /H11005.08."

Sommet 2015 (2)¶

Sommet, N., Darnon, C., & Butera, F. (2015). To Confirm or to Conform? Performance Goals as a Regulator of Conflict With More-Competent Others. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(2), 580–598. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037240

q3 · i3 · causal · r?

Simple-slope analyses of the Study 2 regression. The higher-competence slope was "β =.39, F(1, 66) =4.90, p <.03, ηp2 =.07"; the equal-competence slope was not significant (p =.28).

"In comparison to performance-avoidance goals, performance-approach goals pre- dicted more preference for the “confirmation model” over the “compliance model” (i.e., a more positive difference score) when the partner’s competence was higher, /H9252/H11005.39, F(1, 66) /H110054.90, p /H11021.03, /H9257p2 /H11005.07, than when it was equal"
