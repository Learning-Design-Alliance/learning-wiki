Evidence¶

Fiveash 2023¶

Fiveash, A., Ladányi, E., Camici, J., Chidiac, K., Bush, C. T., Canette, L.-H., Bedoin, N., Gordon, R. L. & Tillmann, B. (2023). Regular rhythmic primes improve sentence repetition in children with developmental language disorder. npj Science of Learning 8:23. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00170-1

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Within-subjects experiment: 15 children with DLD and 18 TD children heard 32-s regular or irregular rhythmic primes before repeating sentences with relative clauses; cumulative link mixed models showed "higher performance after regular" primes, estimate = 0.46, d = 0.44, 95% CI [0.10, 0.79].

"revealed higher performance after regular, emmean = 2.67, SE = 0.45, 95% CI [1.78, 3.55] than irregular, emmean = 2.20, SE = 0.45, 95% CI [1.33, 3.08] primes, estimate = 0.46, SE = 0.18, z-ratio = 2.551, p = 0.01, d = 0.44"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Sentence repetition performance increases with chronological age (r = 0.74), but the rhythmic priming effect itself was not moderated by age — related
  • Digit span and beat-based rhythm perception scores did not significantly influence sentence repetition performance or the priming effect — related
  • The rhythmic priming benefit on sentence repetition did not differ significantly between children with DLD and TD (no prime × group interaction) — related
  • Higher reading age is associated with a larger rhythmic priming effect in children with TD, but not in children with DLD — related
  • Children with TD outperform children with DLD on sentence repetition after rhythmic primes, with a very large effect — related
  • Regular rhythmic primes do not improve performance on a non-grammatical visual cancellation control task — related
