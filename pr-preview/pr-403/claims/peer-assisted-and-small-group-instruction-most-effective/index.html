Related Claims¶

  • Small group instruction is associated with significantly larger effect sizes than CAI and incentive components in targeted secondary interventions — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Targeted school-based interventions have positive short-term effects on standardised reading and mathematics tests for K-6 students with or at risk of academic difficulties — a broader claim this one bears on
  • K-PALS shows statistically significant positive effects on Rapid Letter Sounds for kindergarten students in all three training-support conditions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • PALS shows strong evidence of positive effects on first-grade literacy, with an average effect size of +0.23 across three studies — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • PALS has potentially positive effects on reading achievement for English language learners, with a combined effect size of 0.31 but no statistically significant effects on individual measures — related
  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed a substantively important negative (but statistically non-significant) effect on reading comprehension versus adult tutoring — related
  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed no statistically significant effects on seven alphabetics outcomes compared with adult tutoring — related
  • Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies showed a substantively important positive (but statistically non-significant) effect on WRMT-R Passage Comprehension for first graders — related
  • PALS showed no statistically significant effects on near-transfer and far-transfer reading fluency for first graders — related
  • In adjusted meta-regressions, peer-assisted and small-group instruction outperform computer-assisted instruction, coaching, incentives, and progress monitoring — related
  • Teachers and students report generally favorable impressions of PALS, with least favorability toward team-based structure components — related
