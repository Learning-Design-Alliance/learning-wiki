Evidence¶

Manu Kapur 2012¶

Manu Kapur, Nikol Rummel. (2012). Productive failure in learning from generation and invention activities. Instr Sci 40:645-650. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9235-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

Laboratory-based study with undergraduates in a first-year physics lab course inventing methods for uncertainty in the slope of best-fitting lines; guided invention (GI) added question, self-explanation, and peer-interaction prompts versus unguided invention (UI). No effect size is printed.

"GI students invented methods that included more conceptual features and ranked the given datasets more accurately than those in the UI condition, although the quality of their mathematical expressions was not improved."

Manu Kapur 2012 (2)¶

Manu Kapur, Nikol Rummel. (2012). Productive failure in learning from generation and invention activities. Instr Sci 40:645-650. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9235-4

q2 · i? · review · r2

Learning-strategy level results from the same laboratory study: guided-invention students showed more unprompted self-explanations and more frequent method revision, including on unprompted components.

"GI students showed more and better instances of unprompted self-explanations and they revised their methods more frequently—even on components of the task that were not supported by the metacognitive prompts—than the UI students."
