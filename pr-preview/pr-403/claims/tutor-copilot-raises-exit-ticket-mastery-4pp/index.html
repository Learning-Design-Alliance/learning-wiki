Evidence¶

Rose E. Wang 2025¶

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017

q4 · i? · causal · r2

Preregistered intent-to-treat regression from the randomized controlled trial (900 tutors initially assigned; 4,136 sessions), with student covariates and school-by-grade fixed effects. The article reports a significant positive treatment effect: "4 percentage points (p.p.) more likely to pass their exit tickets (p< 0.01". No standardized effect size is printed.

"Students working with treatment tutors were 4 percentage points (p.p.) more likely to pass their exit tickets (p< 0.01, 62%→ 66% student passing rate from control to treatment)."

Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)¶

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Same ITT analysis, secondary session-level measures from Table 2. Attempting the exit ticket was marginal (p<0.1); conditional passing was significant (p<0.01). Both effects are positive (2 and 3 percentage points), but the article prints no standardized effect size for either, so the magnitude is coded i?.

"We also found that students were 2 p.p. more likely to attempt the exit ticket (p< 0.1), and conditional on their attempt, they were 3 p.p. more likely to pass the exit ticket (p< 0.01)."

Rose E. Wang 2025 (3)¶

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Two-stage least squares analysis disentangling actual use from mere access, reported in a Results-section footnote. Students of tutors who used the tool were "14 p.p. more likely to pass their exit tickets (p< 0.01". No standardized effect size is printed.

"Appendix H reports results on the tutors using Tutor CoPilot, rather than just having access to it, where students were 14 p.p. more likely to pass their exit tickets (p< 0.01)."

Kirk Vanacore 2026¶

Kirk Vanacore, Ryan S. Baker, Avery H. Closser, and Jeremy Roschelle. (2026). The Path to Conversational AI Tutors: Integrating Tutoring Best Practices and Targeted Technologies to Produce Scalable AI Agents. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19303 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2602.19303

q3 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this large-scale randomized controlled trial by Wang et al., a human-in-the-loop design where humans review and edit AI-generated messages. The printed gain is a positive "4 percentage points on average"; no standardized effect size is printed, so the magnitude is coded i?. The review does not name Tutor CoPilot, the population or the exit-ticket measure; it describes the outcome as mastering knowledge components, and those details come from the trial itself (Wang et al. entries above).

"In a large-scale randomized controlled trial, Wang et al. [78] found that providing human tutors with real-time, LLM-generated pedagogical suggestions increased the probability of students mastering knowledge components by 4 percentage points on average."

Mutlu Cukurova 2026¶

Mutlu Cukurova. (2026). What do you mean by 'human–AI collaboration'? Prerequisite functions and the affordances needed to achieve it. Submitted to the Handbook of AI and the Future of Education. https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5843-4854

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The chapter reports, citing Wang et al., a randomised controlled trial of a human–AI system in live tutoring in which "situational-level real-time guidance for tutors raised students' topic mastery by four percentage points overall", and by nine points for students of lower-rated tutors.

"in a randomised controlled trial of a human–AI system in live tutoring, situational-level real-time guidance for tutors raised students' topic mastery by four percentage points overall, and by nine for the students of lower-rated tutors"
