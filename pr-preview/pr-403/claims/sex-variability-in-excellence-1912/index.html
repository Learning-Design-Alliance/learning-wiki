Evidence¶

L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane 1912¶

L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane. (1912). Correlation of Excellence in Different School Subjects Based on a Study of School Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Analysis of the sex of pupils ranked first, second and third, and last three, in each of the six subjects. Weighting ranks (first worth five points, second three, third one), boys scored 32 points positive and 33 negative versus girls' 22 and 21, which the authors say bears out the theory of greater male variability.

"as a result of Problem IV it seems clear that (1) boys are more likely to rank at the top of the grade scale, and (2) that they are also more likely to rank at the bottom of the grade scale. In short the generally accepted theory that the chances for either exceptional ability or exceptional dullness are greater among boys than among girls is borne out by our group."
