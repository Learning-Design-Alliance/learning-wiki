Evidence¶

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008¶

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck. (2008). Effects of problem size, operation, and working-memory span on simple-arithmetic strategies: differences between children and adults? Psychological Research (2008) 72:331–346. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-007-0112-8

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Regression analyses per grade on trial-level data showed span predicted retrieval use only in second grade (β = .218, R² = .073) and retrieval latency in second and fourth grade (β = −.153 and −.218), with no span effect in sixth grade.

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck 2008 (2)¶

Ineke Imbo · André Vandierendonck. (2008). Effects of problem size, operation, and working-memory span on simple-arithmetic strategies: differences between children and adults? Psychological Research (2008) 72:331–346. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-007-0112-8

q3 · i? · causal · r2

In the per-grade regressions of procedural latencies, span was predictive in all grades but reversed direction in sixth grade; the article explains this via strategy selection, since high-span sixth graders used procedural strategies almost exclusively on large problems (procedural RTs 3,707 ms vs. 3,089 ms on large problems).

"high-span sixth graders were less eYcient procedural strategy users than were low-span sixth graders."
