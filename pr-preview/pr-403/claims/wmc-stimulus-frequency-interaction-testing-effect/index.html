Evidence¶

Zheng 2024¶

Zheng, Y., Shi, A. & Liu, X. L. (2024). A working memory dependent dual process model of the testing effect. npj Science of Learning 9:56. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00268-0

q3 · i? · theoretical · r3

Behavioral evidence from Zheng et al. examining the interplay between WMC and WM demands in the testing effect, using image-word pairs without feedback. The article reports that "low-frequency stimuli actually yielded a negative testing effect" for low-WMC individuals.

"individuals with high WMC exhibited the testing effect irrespective of sti- mulus frequency. Conversely, for individuals with low WMC, testing enhanced retention only for high-frequency stimuli; low-frequency stimuli actually yielded a negative testing effect, where restudy proved more effec- tive than testing."

Related Claims¶

  • Feedback moderates the role of WMC in the testing effect, reducing WM load for low-WMC learners — related
  • In high-frequency (low WM demand) associations, the testing effect occurred regardless of working memory capacity, with no Condition by WM interaction — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In low-frequency (high WM demand) associations, the testing effect depended on WM capacity: higher-capacity participants showed it, lower-capacity participants showed a non-significant trend toward a negative effect — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The testing effect is moderated by a three-way interaction between retrieval condition, stimulus frequency, and working memory capacity — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • A modified SAC simulation with a dual-process account replicated the critical three-way interaction, including the absence of a testing effect for low-WM participants on LF associations — related
  • Retrieval practice is costly and benefits learning only when working memory capacity is abundant, as stated in the corrected article's title — a broader claim this one bears on
