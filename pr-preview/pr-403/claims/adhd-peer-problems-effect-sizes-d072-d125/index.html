Evidence¶

Mikami 2015¶

Mikami, A. Y., & Normand, S. (2015). The Importance of Social Contextual Factors in Peer Relationships of Children with ADHD. Curr Dev Disord Rep. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40474-014-0036-0

q3 · i2 · review · r2

The review cites a meta-analysis comparing peer problems of children with ADHD to typically developing youth, reporting "d=.72 for children with ADHD and no comorbidities to d=1.25 for children with ADHD and comorbid conduct problems", described as medium to large effects.

"One meta-analysis reported the effect size of peer problems relative to typically developing youth as d=.72 for children with ADHD and no comorbidities to d=1.25 for children with ADHD and comorbid conduct problems"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Existing child-focused treatments for ADHD, including the MTA's intensive behavioral management and medication, and clinic-based social skills training, have not improved peers' liking of children with ADHD
  • In a small randomized pilot study, children with ADHD showed better sociometric peer functioning when the MOSAIC classroom inclusion components were added to behavioral management and social skills training
  • Peer rejection incrementally predicts subsequent behavioral, emotional, and academic maladjustment in children with ADHD beyond ADHD status itself
  • Peers hold the most negative automatic judgments about children displaying hyperactive/impulsive behaviors, and the ADHD label itself evokes social devaluation
  • Children with ADHD show marked peer rejection and friendship impairment: approximately four times more likely to be rejected, 52–82% scoring over 1 SD above the classroom mean in rejection, and nearly twice as likely to have no classroom friends — related
  • Findings are mixed on whether friendship buffers the negative effects of peer rejection for children with ADHD — related
