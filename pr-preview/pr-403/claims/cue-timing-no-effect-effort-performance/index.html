Evidence¶

Waldeyer 2024¶

Waldeyer, J., Endres, T., Roelle, J., Baars, M., & Renkl, A. (2024). How to Optimize Self-Assessment Accuracy in Cognitive Skill Acquisition When Learning from Worked Examples. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 103. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09944-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 ANOVA on subjective mental effort (9-point scale, ω = 0.95) with N = 129. "the ANOVA did not reveal a statistically significant main effect of timing of cue prompt" on mental effort; prompted cue type and the interaction were also non-significant.

"In terms of mental effort, the ANOVA did not reveal a statistically significant main effect of timing of cue prompt, F(1, 125) = 0.96, p = 0.330, ηp2 < 0.01."

Waldeyer 2024 (2)¶

Waldeyer, J., Endres, T., Roelle, J., Baars, M., & Renkl, A. (2024). How to Optimize Self-Assessment Accuracy in Cognitive Skill Acquisition When Learning from Worked Examples. Educational Psychology Review, 36, 103. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09944-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 ANOVA on problem-solving performance (rated solutions to four problems) with N = 129: "There was no main effect of timing of cue prompt" and no effect of prompted cue type or interaction, so up-front cue information did not compromise performance.

"There was no main effect of timing of cue prompt, F(1, 125) = 1.03, p = 0.311, ηp2 < 0.01, and also no main effect of prompted cue type and no interaction effect"
