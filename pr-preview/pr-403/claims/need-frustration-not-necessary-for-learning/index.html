Evidence¶

Axel Grund 2024¶

Axel Grund, Stefan Fries, Matthias Nückles, Alexander Renkl, Julian Roelle. (2024). When is Learning “Effortful”? Scrutinizing the Concept of Mental Effort in Cognitively Oriented Research from a Motivational Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09852-7

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Narrative review argument synthesizing research on autonomous motivation, flow, interest, and need for cognition: these approaches characterize effective learning as connected to positive affect despite high object-level demands, so "experienced effort-by-need frustration, or “pain,” nei- ther can be a necessary requirement of high-quality learning." Theoretical argument, no effect size.

"From these perspectives, experienced effort-by-need frustration, or “pain,” nei- ther can be a necessary requirement of high-quality learning nor should it be toler - ated as a typical concomitant of it."

Related Claims¶

  • Students' self-reports of task effortfulness do not validly indicate a task's object-level cognitive requirements — related
  • Well-designed games can promote persistence in practice and high-quality engagement, and when basic needs of autonomy, competence, and relatedness are met, learning becomes its own reward, reducing the temptation to outsource cognitive effort to LLMs. — related
  • Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not — related
  • Self-determination theory holds that autonomy support raises intrinsic motivation, and a meta-analysis finds expected tangible rewards undermine it — related
  • Learners invest more effort in tasks they perceive as interesting, personally important, or useful to their future goals. — related
  • No significant impact of frustration or boredom (or anxiousness, discouragement, distractedness) on practice performance was found — related
