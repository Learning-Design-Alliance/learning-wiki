Evidence¶

Wallin 1921¶

Wallin, J. E. W. (1921). Intelligence Irregularity as Measured by Scattering in the Binet Scale. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journal/edu

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Cross-scale comparison of average scattering across the same clinic populations, with the author's stated caveats about unequal numbers of tests, base-determination methods, and extent of testing across scales. The author reports being "struck by the unusually low bases which subjects frequently obtain in this scale."

"we find that the scattering is considerably greater in the Stanford scale, the average excess amounting to 0.24 year as compared with the 1911 scale, and 0.61 year as compared with the 1908."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Scattering on the Binet scales does not support the claim that unequal development is the specific characteristic of the feeble-minded; normal pupils scatter most and imbeciles least — related
  • Sex differences in scattering reverse across scales: girls scatter more on the 1908 scale, boys on the 1911 and Stanford scales — related
  • Scattering varies with intelligence-age level: smallest at the lowest mental ages and possibly largest in the middle range, with age-level differences exceeding diagnostic-category differences — related
  • In an aphasic patient, vocabulary-test failures scatter evenly across difficulty levels rather than heaping at the hard end
  • The year scale and the point scale yield disparate results and differ in diagnostic accuracy for delinquent populations, per Haines's studies — related
