Evidence¶

Glawe 2025¶

Glawe, F., Schmeckel, T., Brauner, P., & Ziefle, M. (2025). Human Autonomy and Sense of Agency in Human-Robot Interaction: A Systematic Literature Review. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22271 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2509.22271

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review's discussion of four anthropomorphism studies reports no clear overall effect, while individual findings diverge: a higher sense of agency with human-like robots versus a servomotor, a moderate correlation of perceived human-likeness with autonomy, and qualitative reports that humanoid appearance could raise or lower autonomy depending on perceived paternalism.

"In this review, no clear effect emerged for either sense of agency or human autonomy, yet several mechanisms are plausible."

  • A conceptual framework paper reports, citing a meta-analysis (Roesler et al., 2021), that anthropomorphism benefits human-robot interaction with effects varying across task contexts and design implementations — related
  • Manipulating anthropomorphism shows no clear causal effect on trust in controlled post-generative-AI studies, though long-term use and cue type condition its effects — related
  • Robot adaptiveness effects on human autonomy are mixed: autonomy-supportive behaviours and as-needed assistance help, but mere choice or high interactivity may not — related
  • Robot communication cues affect sense of agency: predicted-path display and averted gaze reduce it, direct gaze raises it, and facial expression shows no effect — related
