Evidence¶

Bruno Sauce 2021¶

Bruno Sauce, John Wiedenhoeft, Nicholas Judd and Torkel Klingberg. (2021). Change by challenge: A common genetic basis behind childhood cognitive development and cognitive training. npj Science of Learning 6:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00096-6

q3 · i? · associational · r2

The WM change model on 344 participants included an age × sample term. The article reports a significant interaction (p = 0.04) with opposite age patterns in the two samples; no effect size for the interaction is printed.

"We also found that there was no main effect of age on the change in WM ( p = 0.16), but an interaction of age with the type of sample (p = 0.04). For the developmental sample, younger participants changed more than older participants. This pattern was inversed in the training sample, with older participants improving more from the training."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The polygenic score for cognitive performance significantly explains interindividual differences in WM change in both a developmental and a training sample (β = 0.15, p = 0.04; 2.2% of variance). — related
  • Both samples showed significant WM gains: a 14% mean increase after 2 years of development (β = 0.60, p < 0.001) and a 34% mean increase after 25 days of training (β = 2.10, p < 0.001). — related
  • The effect of cogPGS on WM change does not depend on whether gains came from typical development or from training (no cogPGS × sample interaction, p = 0.46). — related
  • Sensitivity reanalyses (outlier removal and sample-standardized age) confirmed that PGS is significantly associated with WM change (all p < 0.01) with no PGS × group interaction. — related
