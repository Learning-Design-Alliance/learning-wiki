Evidence¶

Kidd 2015¶

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Schulz and Bonawitz's (2007) experiment in which children played with boxes and levers under confounded or unconfounded demonstrations, then played alone for 60 seconds. Children in the confounded condition preferentially re-explored the demonstrated red box, consistent with play structured to reduce uncertainty.

"During this period, children in the confounded condition preferentially explored the demonstrated red box over the novel yellow one."

Kidd 2015 (2)¶

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review reports Cook, Goodman, and Schulz's (2011) toy-box study where a connected bead pair made the causal source of music ambiguous. When given ambiguous evidence, children tested individual beads and, when beads were permanently connected, held them so only one side touched the box to isolate each bead's effect.

"Children were effective at both selecting and designing informative interventions to figure out the underlying causal structure when it was unclear from the demonstration."
