Evidence¶

Tarja Pietarinen · Tuire Palonen · Marja Vauras 2021¶

Tarja Pietarinen · Tuire Palonen · Marja Vauras. (2021). Guidance in computer-supported collaborative inquiry learning: Capturing aspects of affect and teacher support in science classrooms. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn, 16, 261–287. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09347-5

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Video-analysis of four teachers' real-time guidance across 913 coded activity sequences; descriptive frequencies shown in Fig. 1. The article reports "the teachers observed the groups rather intensively (40%)" and socio-emotional support at 4%. Descriptive, no effect size reported.

"almost half of the working period (48%) was spent providing support in task management (20%), content knowledge (13%), and technical guidance (15%). However, the teachers observed the groups rather intensively (40%)"

Related Claims¶

  • Teacher guidance distribution tracked perceived willingness to collaborate, not group outcome level or prior science competence
  • Highest-outcome groups did not receive more guidance than other groups
  • Teachers' self-perceived guidance and affect varied across the three inquiry phases and between teachers
  • Teacher self-reported affect was compounded by students' self-reported affect across working phases
  • A regulation macro script structured collaboration to support socio-cognitive and socio-emotional monitoring among teacher-education students
  • Making rounds minimizes off-task behaviour because pupils monitor for the teacher's approach and adjust their behaviour — related
  • Teachers use extracted analytics more for supervision than for guidance, with monitoring the dominant observed action — related
  • Teachers' guidance and support in collaborative science inquiry differed by teacher perceptions and group outcomes, and teacher affect was compounded by student affect, per the editorial's report of Pietarinen et al. — related
