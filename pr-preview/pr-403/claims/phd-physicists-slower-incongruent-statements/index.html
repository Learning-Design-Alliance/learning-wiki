Evidence¶

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021¶

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Response times in the same fMRI statement-verification experiment with 25 Ph.D. physicists. Physics means were "x̄congruent 3542 ± 754 ms; x̄incongruent = 4181 ± 811 ms"; in biology the congruency effect on response time was also significant (d = 0.9).

"Verifying the scienti ﬁc value of the congruent statements relative to incongruent ones was also faster in both areas of expertise (Fig. 1c). (Physics: t(24) = 10.988, p < 0.001, d = 2.1, CI 95% [ −759.1, −519.0]; x̄congruent 3542 ± 754 ms; x̄incongruent = 4181 ± 811 ms."

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette 2021 (2)¶

Geneviève Allaire-Duquette, Lorie-Marlène Brault Foisy, Patrice Potvin, Martin Riopel, Marilyne Larose, Steve Masson. (2021). An fMRI study of scientists with a Ph.D. in physics confronted with naive ideas in science. npj Science of Learning 6:11. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00091-x

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive comparison of congruency-driven response-time increases across the two expertise domains in the same 25-participant study, as printed in the behavioral results; no inferential test of the domain difference is reported.

"In physics, response time increased twice as much as in biology between congruent and incongruent statements ( Δphysics =+ 639 ms or 18%; Δbiology = +273 ms or 9%)."
