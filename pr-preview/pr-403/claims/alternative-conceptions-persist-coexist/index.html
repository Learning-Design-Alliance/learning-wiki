Evidence¶

Alexandra Renouard and Yves Mazabraud 2018¶

Alexandra Renouard and Yves Mazabraud. (2018). Context-based learning for Inhibition of alternative conceptions: the next step forward in science education. npj Science of Learning 3:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0026-9

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Theoretical argument in the article, attributed to prior behavioral and brain-imaging literature (e.g., work cited as showing scientific knowledge suppresses but does not supplant earlier intuitions). The article offers this synthesis: the system is "a conﬂictual, but dynamic and evolving system". Not a new empirical result.

"the individual conceptual system is a conﬂictual, but dynamic and evolving system, marked by the coexistence of scientiﬁc conceptions and persisting alternative conceptions, more or less inhibited according to the individual ’s expertize."

Related Claims¶

  • Naive science ideas are extremely persistent and probably never disappear even after extensive training such as a Ph.D. in physics, so conceptual change as replacement may never truly be achieved — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Scientists with a Ph.D. in physics are less accurate when judging the scientific value of incongruent statements that trigger naive ideas than matched congruent statements, in both physics and biology — related
  • Instruction that confronts science misconceptions (conceptual-conflict interventions, refutation text) produces conceptual change more often than standard exposition — related
  • When physicists with a Ph.D. confront naive ideas in their domain of expertise, MFG and IFG associated with inhibitory control are significantly more activated by incongruent than congruent statements — related
  • Intermediate mechanics students start the semester more familiar with the integration constants (+C) method than with the limits method, and the +C method remains preferred by at least one student even after instruction modeling the limits method — related
  • Metaconceptual teaching activities shifted all four 7th-grade participants from alternative conceptions to scientifically accurate understanding of the republic — reports the opposite
