King and Kitchener's Reflective Judgment Model: a seven-stage developmental framework of epistemic assumptions and justification¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The Reflective Judgment Model (RJM) is a theoretical framework organizing how people make and justify judgments about ill-defined problems, which "cannot be defined with a high degree of completeness and... cannot be solved with a high degree of certainty." It posits seven stages (Table 1) spanning absolute, authority-based knowing (Stage 1) to knowledge "constructed through a process of inquiry" justified "probabilistically" (Stage 7). King and Kitchener's longitudinal and cross-sectional work found development occurs slowly, is facilitated by educational settings, and follows stage-related patterns without lock-step progression. This study used the model's stage definitions as a coding rubric for teacher educators' interview narratives.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Structured interview protocol with scripted questions and standard ill-defined problems, administered per King and Kitchener's original procedures
  • Trained raters using the seven-stage definitional matrix as a rubric

Constraints¶

  • Development has stage-like properties but does not evolve in a lock-step, one-stage-at-a-time fashion
  • The specific components of an educational environment that facilitate development could not be determined

Target Learners¶

  • late adolescents and adults
  • teacher educators and college faculty

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • reflective judgment about ill-defined problems
  • epistemic assumptions and justification of beliefs

Examples¶

  • Use structured, formal learning beyond the profession (e.g., classical liberal arts content) to grow mid-career faculty reflective capacity
  • Teacher educators should recognize preservice teachers' reflective judgment levels and provide guided reflection tailored to their epistemological assumptions
