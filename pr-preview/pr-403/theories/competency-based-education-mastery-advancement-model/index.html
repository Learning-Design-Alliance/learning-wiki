Competency-based education: students advance through material independently upon demonstrating content mastery¶

Theory · All theories
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article defines competency-based education as a model in which "students move through material independently, advancing when they demonstrate content mastery." Proponents view competency-based approaches as a potential solution to student demand for flexible, career-relevant programs and to employer demand for skilled workers. The evaluated instantiation was a set of online competency-based information technology programs offered by a consortium of three community colleges under a federal TAACCCT grant.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs must allow students to advance when they demonstrate content mastery rather than on seat time.

Constraints¶

  • In the evaluated community college context, the model appeared best suited for mature, academically well-prepared students.

Target Learners¶

  • community college students
  • adult workers seeking re-employment and training

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • demonstration of content mastery
  • completion of career-relevant credentials

Claims¶

  • A three-college consortium successfully implemented online competency-based programs as planned, meeting most implementation milestones and outcome targets [+W]
  • Gatekeeper course completion rates were slightly lower for CBE participants than for comparison students, and credential completion differences varied by college [~W]
  • Consortium-wide, 35 percent of participants completed a grant-funded program of study, with high employment rates and wage growth above the national average [+W]
