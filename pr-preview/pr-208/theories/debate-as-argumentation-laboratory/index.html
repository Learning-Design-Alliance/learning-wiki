Educational debate as an argumentation laboratory requiring basic theories of argumentation and reasoning¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper's organizing framework is that any debate format is legitimate as education only if it applies argumentation theory and gives students a laboratory for testing advocacy. The author states that "forany debate to be a legitimate educational venture--regardless of format--itmust apply basic theories of argumentation and reasoning," and that debate is "a process wherein advocacy is tested." He cites Gass's view that debate is heuristic, a forum for students to theorize about argument, and Cirlin's claim that theory should benefit the performer. The framework underpins all eight of his reform proposals.
