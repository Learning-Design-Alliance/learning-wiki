Three-circle framework for computational thinking integration: skills, practices, and inclusive pedagogies¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The guide recommends a framework for integrating computational thinking into K-12 teaching built as three concentric circles. Computational thinking skills are the cognitive processes for engaging computational tools; practices combine multiple skills to solve applied problems; and inclusive pedagogies are strategies for engaging all learners. The guide states that "In order to integrate computational thinking into K-12 teaching and learning, educators must define what students need to know and be able to do to be successful computational thinkers. Our recommended framework has three concentric circles."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators must first define what students need to know and be able to do to be successful computational thinkers

Constraints¶

  • The framework is recommended for K-12 integration contexts as described by the guide

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students, especially those experiencing marginalization in computing

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Computational thinking skills and practices across disciplines

Claims¶

  • Computing education has historically excluded marginalized student populations, and large inequities in access persist [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Design early learning (K-3) CT opportunities both plugged and unplugged, with devices leveraged where appropriate
  • Integrate computational thinking into existing disciplinary teaching across grade bands rather than adding it on
  • Build computational thinking skills in the younger grades through plugged and unplugged activities
