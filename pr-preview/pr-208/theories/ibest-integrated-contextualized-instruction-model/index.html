Contextualized integrated basic skills and occupational training model¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 6 studies (5 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), q3–q4 · 3 of 6 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

I-BEST rests on an instructional model that integrates adult basic education with occupational skills training instead of the traditional sequence in which basic skills courses must be completed before college-level occupational training. The report states I-BEST "uses a contextualized instruction approach, where students learn basic skills in the context of their course of study" — for example, emphasizing medical terms in a nursing program. This model "aims to improve the motivation and achievement of students by providing them experiences where they can see the usefulness of basic skills instruction in their chosen field," offering an alternative to a traditional track that generally results in low rates of advancement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Integration of adult basic education and occupational skills training so students can pursue credit-bearing, short-term certificate programs as well as college degrees

Constraints¶

  • The traditional track of providing adult basic education prior to occupational training generally results in low rates of advancement between basic skills and occupational training

Target Learners¶

  • Adults referred to basic skills training, most of whom never enroll in college-level courses under the traditional sequence

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Literacy, math, work, and college-readiness skills learned in the context of an occupational field

Claims¶

  • Ibest Positive Effects Credential Completion [+M]
  • I-BEST has potentially positive effects on short-term earnings, with mixed results across states (average improvement index 0) [+M]
  • I-BEST has no discernible effects on credit accumulation [-W]
  • I-BEST has potentially positive effects on short-term employment (improvement index +10) [+W]

Examples¶

  • Wraparound student supports: navigator, financial supports, and job placement assistance
  • Use accelerated, contextualized basic-skills instruction with cohort support to re-engage adults with low skill levels
