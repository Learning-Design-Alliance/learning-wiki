Framework of 35 metacognitive behaviors for coding reading-strategy use¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The researchers created a coding framework of 35 metacognitive behaviors, skills, and strategies drawn from a literature review, used to judge whether student interview responses demonstrated metacognition. Behaviors range from setting the purpose for reading and understanding the task to predicting, using fix-up strategies, summarizing, testing, revising, evaluating, checking, and rereading. A response matching a listed behavior was counted as metacognitive; otherwise it was considered not metacognitive and ignored.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student responses must be transcribed verbatim and coded against the behavior list; a response matching a listed behavior counts as metacognitive

Constraints¶

  • The framework was derived from a literature review by the researchers; responses not matching a listed behavior were ignored as not metacognitive

Target Learners¶

  • students in grades 2 through 12 (no grade 4)

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • reading comprehension
  • metacognitive strategy use in reading

Claims¶

  • Students Use 19 Of 35 Metacognitive Behaviors [+M]
  • Predicting Contextual Analysis Rereading Most Frequent [+M]
  • Percentage of metacognitive interview responses varied by grade band: 66% (grades 2-3), 97% (grade 5), 82% (grade 6), and 41% (grades 7-12) [+M]
  • Upper-level students most often reported contextual analysis, setting the purpose for reading, monitoring comprehension, and rereading [+W]
  • Students showed deficiencies in maintaining and monitoring their reading plan within the three-element view of metacognition [+W]
