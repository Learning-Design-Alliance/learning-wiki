Construct evaluation framework: operational definition, empirical grounding, and conceptual integrity¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

A framework for evaluating the characteristics of psychological constructs prior to automated coding, grounded in educational psychology measurement practices and validity theories (Cronbach & Meehl, 1955; Kane, 2013; Messick, 1995). It comprises three dimensions — "Operational Definition," "Empirical Grounding," and "Conceptual Integrity" — each with sub-dimensions such as clarity, specificity, granularity, concreteness, objectivity, measurability, theoretical coherence, context sensitivity, and sensitivity to individual sources of variability. The authors used it to rate persistence, self-efficacy, interest, perceived challenge, and prior KSAs, finding persistence among the less well-defined constructs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Constructs must be clearly defined with information about what constitutes evidence for them based on existing theoretical and empirical research on assessment

Constraints¶

  • The authors state the construct analysis may reflect a degree of arbitrariness or confirmation bias and should be viewed as a descriptive foundation for future refinement; reliability of construct ratings was not calculated

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students classified as English learners

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • measuring persistence, self-efficacy, interest, perceived challenge, and prior KSAs from conversational assessment data
