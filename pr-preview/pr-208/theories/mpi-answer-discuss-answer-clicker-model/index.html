Modified peer instruction answer-discuss-answer model for clickers in mathematics content courses¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes an answer-discuss-answer implementation "similar in nature to the modified peer instruction model (MPI) described by Milner-Bolotin et al. (2010)", aligned with think-pair-share. Students answer individually, a bar graph of responses is displayed, students discuss with groupmates arguing approaches and rationale, the class answers again with the correct answer shown, and a full-class discussion follows. The model was used in six lessons with seven to nine multiple-choice questions each.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Effective in-class participation, which the article notes was a norm promoted through frequent group activities using elementary-level manipulatives

Constraints¶

  • In courses with less frequent use of hands-on or collaborative activities, specific attention may need to focus on encouraging appropriate and sufficient discussion

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service elementary teachers in college mathematics content courses

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • communication about and with mathematics
  • deeper and personally meaningful understandings of mathematics content

Claims¶

  • Learning Attributed To Process Not Technology [+M]
  • Clicker Mpi Liked By Preservice Teachers [+M]
  • Students perceived clickers' greatest course impact as lesson variation and fun, with learning influence a distant second [+W]
  • Most pre-service elementary teachers believed clicker use assisted their learning and understanding of course material [+W]
  • No instructor bias was evident in students' liking of the clicker implementation or perceived learning benefit [+W]
  • Peer Discussion Improves Conceptual Understanding [+M]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Implement clickers grade-free, with a classroom set and limited frequency, to support positive student perceptions
  • Pair clicker questions with at-home follow-up reflections asking students to explain each statement
