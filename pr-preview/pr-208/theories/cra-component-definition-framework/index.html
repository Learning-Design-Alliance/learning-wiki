Culturally Responsive Assessment framework built from component definitions of culture, responsiveness, and assessment¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The guide defines CRA through a component-based approach: instructors and students first build working definitions of the individual terms culture, responsiveness, and assessment before assembling a definition of CRA. The collaborative defines culture as "a social concept that describes complex traditions, customs, and practices of a society or social group", encompassing implicit components like social identity and shared experiences, and shaped by power dynamics. Responsiveness is framed as active, empathetic engagement that may extend toward sustaining cultural richness, and assessment is expanded beyond individual tests to community and systemic factors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Users engage with definition resource banks and commentary to develop their own working definitions rather than adopting a single authoritative definition

Constraints¶

  • The guide states the field is not settled and it does not endorse any single emerging definition as authoritative, since definitions stem from specific isolated contexts

Target Learners¶

  • graduate students in educational measurement and assessment programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • develop a working definition of culturally responsive assessment
  • understand culture, responsiveness, and assessment as components of CRA

Claims¶

  • Rapid proliferation of culturally focused assessment terms after 2020 risks the jingle-jangle fallacy [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Model CRA by co-designing course assessment with students through an in-class assessment conversation
