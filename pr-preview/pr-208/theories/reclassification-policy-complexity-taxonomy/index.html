Taxonomy of EL reclassification policy complexity based on the number and type of test-based criteria¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The report organizes state reclassification policies along a complexity axis defined by the type and number of test-based benchmarks students must meet. States are ranked "from least to most complex based on the type and number of benchmarks EL students must meet to become eligible for reclassification": single ELP composite requirement (States A, B), then multiple criteria including ELP composite, domain-specific scores, and state assessments (States C, D). This taxonomy, introduced in the series' first report, structures all three research questions. The report explicitly does not account for rigor in the form of minimum scores.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Test-based reclassification criteria for each state must be identifiable from state policy documents to classify complexity.

Constraints¶

  • The analysis focuses exclusively on the type and number of criteria and does not account for rigor in the form of minimum scores.

Target Learners¶

  • English Learner (EL) students in K-12 schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Timing and likelihood of exiting EL status and transitioning to mainstream English-only classes
