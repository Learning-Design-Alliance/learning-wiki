Ten developmental experiences framework: five action and five reflection experiences underpin social-emotional development¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Drawing on the UChicago Consortium's Foundations for Young Adult Success framework, the report organizes arts learning opportunities around ten developmental experiences. As printed: "These 10 developmental experiences include five action experiences (encountering, tinkering, choosing, practicing, and contributing) and five reflection experiences (describing, evaluating, connecting, envisioning, and integrating)." Arts education is argued to provide particularly rich opportunities for all ten, which the report uses to analyze how arts activities build social-emotional competencies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students need richer, more frequent, and more varied opportunities for action and reflection for development to occur

Constraints¶

  • The framework was drawn from neurobiology/epigenetics and learning-sciences literatures as reported by Nagaoka et al. (2015), not developed from arts-specific experimental evidence

Target Learners¶

  • Children and adolescents in K–12 arts and general education settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Social-emotional competencies developed through action and reflection experiences

Claims¶

Related Theories¶

  • Theory of action: art practices carry social-emotional components that, cultivated over time, build social-emotional competencies
  • Developmental relationships: experiences shape development most within strong, supportive, sustained relationships with adults and peers

Examples¶

  • Educators in non-arts subjects can apply the mechanisms by which arts learning supports social-emotional development
