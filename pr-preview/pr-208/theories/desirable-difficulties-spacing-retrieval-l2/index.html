Desirable difficulties: spacing and retrieval practice for L2 vocabulary¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes its rationale around desirable difficulties, defined as "activities or situations which increase the difficulty of a task initially," which slow learning and can lower short-term performance while improving long-term recall and transfer. Spaced repetition with pre-set intervals and retrieval practice after forgetting are the two difficulties built into the GoldList Method, drawing on the performance-versus-learning distinction of Soderstrom and Bjork as the article presents it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Difficulty must remain "desirable" — not so challenging that learners cannot process the material or lose motivation

Constraints¶

  • Short-term performance may be negatively affected by spaced studying, per the literature the article reviews

Target Learners¶

  • University L2 learners of English

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Long-term retention and transfer of L2 vocabulary

Claims¶

  • Standard Two Week Spacing Significant Over Baseline [+M]
  • Faster rate of learning may be negatively related to long-term retention (efficiency-effectiveness trade-off) [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Three-phase model of learning in the brain: encoding, consolidation, retrieval

Examples¶

  • GoldList Notebook Method (headlist plus three distillations)
  • Classroom implementation practices for the GoldList Notebook Method
  • Sequence simpler desirable difficulties (retrieval, spacing, interleaving) during initial L2 vocabulary encoding, reserving generation for post-encoding consolidation
