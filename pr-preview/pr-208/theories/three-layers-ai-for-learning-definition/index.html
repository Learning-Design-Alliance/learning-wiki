Three-layer definition of AI for learning: computational intelligence, specific capabilities, and a futures toolkit¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report frames AI for educators through three layers: AI as computational intelligence brought to bear on educational challenges as an additional resource; AI as a set of specific rapidly advancing capabilities (perception, representation and reasoning, learning, natural interaction, societal impact) engineered into solutions; and AI as a toolkit for imagining futures. The report states: "we conceptualize three aspects of AI—as an ambitious leading edge of computing, as a set of specific capabilities that are rapidly advancing, and as a toolkit for synthesizing and exploring possible futures for learning and teaching." This framing underpins the panel's scenarios and design concepts.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators and researchers adopt a shared multi-layer vocabulary rather than a single accepted definition of AI

Constraints¶

  • AI does not have a single accepted definition; the layers are the report's own conceptualization

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 and higher education learners supported by AI-enabled learning technologies

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Understanding what AI is and can contribute to learning among educational leaders and researchers

Claims¶

  • Experts identified pervasive limitations of today's AI for learning, including biased data, inequity, non-graceful failure, and weak context grasp [~W]
  • Weak grasp of context can make AI misdiagnoses systematically harmful when a flawed decision pattern is replicated across many students [~W]
