Workload dynamics framework: temporal metrics of workload variation, intensity, and distribution over a term¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes analyzing workload beyond static or average time-on-task by quantifying its temporal patterns. "By workload dynamics, we refer to the temporal patterns and fluctuations in workload over a specific period of time, such as the academic term." The framework comprises dispersion measures (range, SD, CV), time-on-task slopes between consecutive weeks, and a peak analysis classifying high-intensity weeks as Type 1 (25% above course average), Type 2 (50%), and Type 3 (100%). These metrics are computed from weekly timesheet survey and LMS trace data and correlated with perceived difficulty and learning attainment to inform course design.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Weekly self-reported time-on-task data from a timesheet survey configured to the course's activity design, and/or LMS click-stream trace data with session delineation and de-duplication
  • Course-level aggregation with sufficient weekly response rates to compare workload patterns across courses

Constraints¶

  • Both self-reported and LMS measures primarily reflect elapsed time rather than actual engaged learning time, as both may include periods of inactivity and distractions
  • Perceived learning attainment was obtained at course-level aggregation, so student-level analysis was not possible
  • A student-level comparison of WTS and LMS data was not possible, as it would have required a 100% response rate

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate engineering students in higher education

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Understanding and managing student workload across an academic term to support course design and student well-being
