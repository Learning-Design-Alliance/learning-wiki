Five-goal framework for K-12 social sciences education directed toward civic competence¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The California framework organizes all K-12 social sciences programs around five goals: disciplinary understanding, skills, diversity, values, and social participation. It states that programs 'will be directed toward the achievement of civic competence' and that the central purpose is to 'develop responsible citizens who will preserve and continue to advance progress toward a just society.' Each goal is elaborated in a dedicated section with discussion and illustrative objectives.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs must be planned to address all five goals through selected studies of human experience in American and world societies

Constraints¶

  • Implementation materials and courses of study are to be developed at the school district level; the framework itself is explicitly not a course of study

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten through grade twelve students in California public schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Civic competence
  • Interdisciplinary conceptual understanding
  • Social sciences skills
  • Valuing and social participation

Claims¶

  • The framework directs social sciences programs toward five stated student outcomes including understandings, skills, respect for diversity, values clarification, and participation [+W]
  • The framework's first Goal I program goal is helping students understand interdisciplinary concepts through studies of human experience, with the remaining goals implicit in the illustrative objectives [+W]
  • Social sciences instruction is drawn from seven disciplines: anthropology, economics, geography, history, political science, psychology, and sociology [+W]

Related Theories¶

  • Taxonomy of interdisciplinary concepts for organizing K-12 social sciences curricula

Examples¶

  • Grade-span structure of program goals with illustrative, non-sequential level objectives
  • Adopted instructional materials should reflect learning principles, pupil maturity, major cultures, varied teaching methods, and individual differences
  • Use studies of human experience across world societies with an interdisciplinary focus through grade eight
