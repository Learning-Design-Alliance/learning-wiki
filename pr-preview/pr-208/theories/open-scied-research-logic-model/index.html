OpenSciEd research logic model with five components¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 9 claims (4 for, 5 mixed) · 7 studies (5 review, 2 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The white paper organizes OpenSciEd research using a logic model with a five-part structure. As the report states, "This logic model has five main components: design (distinctive principles and affordances), classroom (enactment), teacher supports, system-level (implementation), and outcomes." The authors use this structure to map the 24 reviewed papers by primary focus area and to frame gaps, arguing that district-level implementation and student outcomes remain under-studied relative to design and enactment.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research papers must be classifiable by their primary area of focus within the five components

Constraints¶

  • The report notes most high school-focused papers are design-focused and preliminary because the units were released more recently than middle school units

Target Learners¶

  • middle school science students
  • high school science students

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • three-dimensional NGSS-based science proficiency

Claims¶

  • OpenSciEd classrooms showed greater science achievement growth than non-OpenSciEd classrooms in one district comparison [+W]
  • No significant differences in science learning or attitudes between OpenSciEd and mySci eighth-grade classes in one small comparison [~W]
  • OpenSciEd enactment faces key challenges: project fatigue, unit length and pacing, pseudoagency, and gaps in student sensemaking practices [~W]
  • Formative assessment practices coherent with OpenSciEd are constrained by grading expectations misaligned with formative goals and by the time cost of evaluating and giving feedback on three-dimensional tasks. [~W]
  • Teachers are able to support rich and participatory science discussions during OpenSciEd enactment, per detailed case studies of experienced teachers [+W]
  • Students' sense of belonging predicts their contributions to classroom knowledge building at both student and teacher levels [+W]
  • Collective enterprise and care in equitable classroom cultures are positively associated with students' science interest [+W]
  • OpenSciEd curriculum design goals, especially coherence from the student perspective, come into tension during unit design [~W]
  • OpenSciEd grade 7 units align with some data-practice frameworks but are less aligned with digital tools, large data sets, and full data cycles [~W]
