Three-dimension framework distinguishing self-initiated learning, self-direction as personal attribute, and self-direction as educational goal¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Following formulations by Caffarella and O'Donnell, the authors propose that debate about self-directed learning is "crucially including three dimensions": self-initiated learning occurring within or outside formal contexts; self-directed learning as a personal attribute of the learner; and self-direction in learning as a goal of education. The framework tidies a debate in which, as Caffarella and O'Donnell warn, over-defining can make the concept vacuous by applying to nothing or to everything. The review's own focus is self-initiated learning outside formal contexts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Distinguishing the three dimensions is needed to avoid the definitional confusion the review documents, including Oddi's identification of ten different uses of the terms.

Constraints¶

  • The publication is explicitly not about andragogy or self-direction in formal learning situations, but about learning occurring mostly outside the influence of continuing education professionals.

Target Learners¶

  • adults learning outside formal institutions

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • clarifying research and practice definitions of self-directed adult learning

Claims¶

  • Research on adults' self-directed learning has been extensive in North America but almost absent in Britain over the past twenty-five years [+W]
  • Penland's 1979 national survey found about 80% of US adults considered themselves continuing learners and over three quarters had planned their own learning projects in the prior year [+W]
  • Tough's 1967 study found all forty sampled graduates engaged in self-teaching, assisted on average by 10.6 helpers each [+W]
  • Tough's 1971 study found nearly all adults had undertaken a learning project in the past year, 68% self-planned and under 1% for credit, with women's categories logging fewer hours [+W]
