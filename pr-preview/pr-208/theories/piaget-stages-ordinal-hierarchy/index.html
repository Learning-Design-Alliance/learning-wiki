Piaget's stages as an ordinal hierarchy of schemes for coping with the environment¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Humm's overview paper presents Piaget's framework for physical educators: two functional invariants, adaptation and structure, operate from birth, with adaptation accomplished by assimilation and accommodation. Development proceeds through the sensori-motor, preoperational, concrete operational, and formal operational periods. The paper stresses that 'Piaget's stages are ordinal, being a description of a progressive hierarchy of schemes for coping with the environment,' so skills built on prerequisites must appear in order.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction must be matched to where the child is in development of the particular skill in question, since age or intelligence cannot be equated with physical skill

Constraints¶

  • The overview is explicitly a condensation and extrapolation of Piaget's work, and the author warns against oversimplification of the stages

Target Learners¶

  • young children in physical education and special physical education programs

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • motor skill development grounded in cognitive-developmental level

Claims¶

  • Both Baldwin and Piaget explained development through the complementary processes of assimilation and accommodation, with accommodation arising from conflict with habit [+W]
  • Baldwin, not Piaget, first proposed progressive stage-by-stage development and a genetic epistemology founded on assimilation and accommodation [~W]
  • Scholarly views differ on whether Piaget appropriated Baldwin's ideas or extended them with empirical support [~W]
  • Piaget denied Baldwin's influence, calling the theoretical similarities simple convergences, yet his mentor Janet had been strongly influenced by Baldwin [~W]
