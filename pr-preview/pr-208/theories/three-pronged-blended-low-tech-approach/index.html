Three-pronged approach to blended learning in low-tech settings: pedagogy training, mother-tongue content, and infrastructure¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The pilot's organizing framework is a "three-pronged approach" combining (1) training Haitian educators in tech-informed pedagogy, (2) increasing availability of culturally relevant, mother-tongue digital content, and (3) improving schools' access to solar power and satellite connectivity. It treats blended learning adoption in low-tech settings as requiring simultaneous investment in human capacity, content relevance and language, and physical infrastructure.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simultaneous attention to educator training, locally relevant mother-tongue digital content, and power/connectivity infrastructure

Constraints¶

  • Designed for low-tech settings where most learners lack devices and internet access

Target Learners¶

  • Rural primary school students in Haiti

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Science learning through blended instruction

Claims¶

  • Haitian teachers reported that pilot trainings improved their teaching, classroom management, and use of technology [+W]
  • Mead argued that treating the mother tongue as inferior impedes movement between home and school language structures [+W]
  • Preservation of the mother tongue predicts second-language achievement among migrant children [+W]
  • Students in the Haiti blended learning pilot showed statistically significant learning gains, with the lowest-baseline students gaining the most [+W]
  • In 75% of observed lesson segments in the Haiti pilot, all pupils were engaged [+W]
