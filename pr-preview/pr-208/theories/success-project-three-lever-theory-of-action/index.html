Success Project theory of action: three levers of social and cultural capital, grades-and-attention focus, and high school application support¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report articulates the program's theory of action, which held that "The Success Project intended to build three lev ers in schools for improving student outcomes": improving students' social and cultural capital via 6to16, increasing school-wide attention to grades and attendance, and getting students to apply to more and better-matched high schools. The levers were to be worked by full-time Success coordinators through three tasks: teaching the curriculum, monitoring grades and attendance, and facilitating applications. The model assumed the combination of levers would raise high school and college graduation chances.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A full-time dedicated Success coordinator in each school to carry out the three core tasks
  • Administrator and teacher buy-in and access to student-level data

Constraints¶

  • The early outcome analyses are formative and explicitly non-causal
  • The program was expected to need three to five years before measurable effects on student outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • Middle grades students (grades 6-8) in low-income urban neighborhood schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • College and high school readiness knowledge and beliefs
  • Study habits, grades, and attendance
  • Social and cultural capital
  • High school selection and application skills

Claims¶

  • Success Project Attendance Ontrack Improved [+W]
  • Success Project Gpas Little Improvement [~M]
  • After two years, fewer graduates attended career academies and neighborhood high schools and more attended charter schools; these shifts were no different from comparison schools [~W]
  • Students reported improved school-work priorities, peer academic behavior, teacher connection and support, and school connectedness after two years [+W]

Examples¶

  • Implement or expand IB Diploma Programs in urban neighborhood high schools to promote college readiness
