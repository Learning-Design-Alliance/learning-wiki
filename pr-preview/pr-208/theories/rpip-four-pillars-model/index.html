Research-Practice-Industry Partnership (RPIP) model with four pillars for involving educators in edtech development¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The RPIP model extends the Research Practice Partnership framework by bringing in industry voices, offering researchers and educators an opportunity to apply their experiences to a real world product. "An RPIP consists of four distinct pillars: 1) professional learning for educators...; 2) collaborative research design; 3) feedback loops between educators, researchers, and developers; and 4) the resulting improved product and practice improvements." A neutral third party facilitates the relationship and maintains a balanced power dynamic between educators and developers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Professional learning sessions that connect educators' practice and pedagogy to the tool
  • A neutral third-party facilitator to keep power dynamics balanced between educators and developers

Constraints¶

  • Edtech development has traditionally occurred independently from classroom practice, with educator feedback limited to user surveys and stand-alone focus groups

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 mathematics students, reached indirectly through their teachers' participation

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improved edtech products that support student algebra learning
  • Educator capacity to engage in design and research processes

Claims¶

  • Educators perceived FH2T as strongly connected to Common Core Standards for Mathematical Content and uniquely integrating the Standards for Mathematical Practice [+W]
  • Educators who implemented FH2T through partner or group work perceived it promoted peer collaboration and mathematical discussion [+W]
  • Educators perceived FH2T positioned students as doers of mathematics with agency and mathematical decision making in a safe practice environment [+W]
  • Educators used FH2T's teacher console insights to modify instruction, and recommended the console share students' mathematical capabilities and content-area difficulties [+W]
