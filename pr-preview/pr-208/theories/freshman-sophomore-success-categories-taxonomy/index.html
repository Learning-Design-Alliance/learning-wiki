Freshman and Sophomore Success Categories: a four-level indicator taxonomy for monitoring students¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report proposes parallel four-category systems for incoming sophomores (Freshman Success Categories) and for monitoring during sophomore year (Sophomore Success Categories). Each system layers GPA, course failures, and attendance onto on-track status: "This system contains four Freshman Success Categories: 1) Off-Track, 2) On-Track Warning, 3) On-Track Below 3.0, and 4) On-Track Above 3.0." The categories identify distinct groups needing different supports, from credit recovery for off-track students to grade maintenance for students above 3.0 seeking selective post-secondary access.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires schools to have access to timely data on students' on-track status, course failures, attendance, and GPA

Constraints¶

  • Outcomes analyses used the 2013-14 freshman cohort to include college enrollment; charter school students were excluded from the analyses

Target Learners¶

  • High school sophomores in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • High school graduation
  • College enrollment and access to selective post-secondary opportunities

Claims¶

  • Freshman Warning Indicators Identify 70 Percent Nongraduates [+M]
  • Sophomore Warning Indicators Flag 84 Percent Nongraduates [+M]
  • Freshman OnTrack remains a strong predictor of high school graduation even as on-track rates have risen [+M]
  • Student performance declines from freshman to sophomore year, with course failures rising in every subject and attendance declining sharply [+M]
  • Gains in sophomore performance were unequal: about one in five Latino and Black young men were off-track at the end of sophomore year [+M]
  • Sophomore OnTrack rose from 61 to 85 percent and predicts graduation, but 30 percent of non-graduates were on-track as sophomores [~M]
