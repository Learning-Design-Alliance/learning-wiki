SEL trajectory framework distinguishing status from growth as predictors of 9th-grade off-track outcomes¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes its inquiry around a framework contrasting two ways of summarizing a student's social-emotional learning trajectory: initial level versus change over time. It states the study examines "whether a student’s mean SEL score in 6th grade (status) or growth between 6th and 8th grade is more predictive of being off track academically in 9th grade." Growth models are fit for three SEL constructs shown to relate to successful secondary-school transition. The framework treats SEL development as a longitudinal quantity whose status and growth components carry different predictive information for early high school outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal SEL data across multiple timepoints (four years in this study) to fit growth models
  • SEL constructs shown to be related to successfully transitioning to secondary school

Constraints¶

  • Applied to students in a large urban district with about 3,000 students per timepoint
  • Growth window examined is between 6th and 8th grade; outcome is 9th-grade off-track status

Target Learners¶

  • Students in intermediate/middle school grades 6-8
  • 9th-grade students in a large urban district

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Identifying students off track academically in 9th grade
  • Understanding social-emotional learning development over time

  • Sel Status More Frequently Significant Predictor Off Track [+M]
