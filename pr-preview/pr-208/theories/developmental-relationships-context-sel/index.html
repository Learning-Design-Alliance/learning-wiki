Developmental relationships: experiences shape development most within strong, supportive, sustained relationships with adults and peers¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

A second organizing concept holds that experiences influence development most when embedded in strong relationships. The report states that "experiences are most influential in shaping the course of development when they take place within the context of strong, supportive, and sustained developmental relationships with important adults and peers." How students perceive a learning environment shapes their engagement and effort, which in turn shapes the developmental benefits they reap. Competencies are treated as potentialities brought forth by environments rather than fixed traits.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators must create environments of trust so students feel comfortable taking productive risks and being challenged

Constraints¶

  • The report notes teachers cannot control all elements of a student's environment, since arts education contexts include much beyond a teacher's control

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students learning within adult- and peer-supported settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Engagement, effort, and social-emotional competency development
