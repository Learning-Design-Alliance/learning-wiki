I3P stage-based model: open innovation as a strategy to foster Professional Learning Communities in higher education¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The I3P (Pedagogical Innovation Initiatives Incubator) is a stage-based program model proposed by UNAL's National Directorate of Pedagogical Innovation. As the article states, "a stage-based model was proposed (see Figure 1), based on Open Innovation (OI) as a strategy to foster the formation of Professional Learning Communities (PLC) among members of different campuses and faculties at UNAL". It operationalizes open innovation—the free flow of inbound and outbound knowledge—by letting community members (faculty, students, alumni, administrative staff) propose and run collaborative pedagogical innovation projects across calls with distinct foci, with team monitoring and differentiated support provided by the I3P leadership team.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Common interests that guide teamwork, enhanced group communication systems, and strategies enabling faculty to learn from and share practices with peers

Constraints¶

  • Cross-campus connections were less extensive than intended: 60.0% of teams were formed by individuals from the same campus and only one team (6.7%) included members from four campuses

Target Learners¶

  • University faculty, students, alumni, and administrative staff of the National University of Colombia

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Pedagogical innovation in experimental science teaching and professional development of the academic community

Claims¶

  • Plc Consolidation Independent Of Prior Connections [+M]
  • I3P Science Projects Benefited 1800 Students [+M]
  • Teams from all three I3P calls functioned as PLCs at the implementation stage, with no statistically significant differences between calls across the five PLC dimensions [+W]
  • Content analysis of project reports identified five factors contributing to PLC formation (common interests, communication systems, mutual learning, collaborative work, UNAL values) and three hindering factors (administrative management, project scheduling, personal non-compliance) [+W]
  • PLC development by promoting role: administrative-staff-led PLCs rated shared leadership lower but shared values higher than teacher- and student-led PLCs, with no statistically significant differences [+W]

Related Theories¶

Examples¶

  • Transfer strategy for building PLCs through innovation projects: common interests, communication systems, and peer learning of practices
