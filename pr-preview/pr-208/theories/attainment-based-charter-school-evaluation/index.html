Attainment-based evaluation of charter schools as a complement to achievement-test-based studies¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report frames its contribution as moving beyond the standard outcome measure in charter school research. It states that "past charter school studies focused on student achievement," whereas this study "analyzed the relationship between attendance and educational attainment in charter high schools." The approach treats high school completion and college attendance as the outcomes by which charter school impact is judged, complementing test-score-based evaluations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires longitudinal data linking school attendance records to later diploma receipt and college enrollment.

Constraints¶

  • Findings are specific to charter high schools in Chicago and Florida and to students who attended charter middle schools.

Target Learners¶

  • charter high school students in Chicago and Florida

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • high school completion
  • college attendance

Claims¶

  • Charter Schools Positive Attainment Effects [+M]
  • Charter middle school students who attend a charter high school are 7 to 15 percentage points more likely to earn a standard diploma than those who transition to a traditional public high school [+M]
  • The study examines charter high school effects on educational attainment in two settings, Florida and Chicago [+M]
  • Charter middle school students who attend a charter high school are 8 to 10 percentage points more likely to attend college than those who transition to a traditional public high school [+M]

Related Theories¶

  • Charter school effects as a multi-dimensional policy question spanning achievement, attainment, integration, and competition

Examples¶

  • Evaluate charter schools against longer-term outcomes such as college enrollment and completion, not only short-term achievement
