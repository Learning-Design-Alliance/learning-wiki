MAP Reading Fluency theory of action: hypothesized mechanisms of change and intermediate goals toward fluent reading with comprehension¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The MAP Reading Fluency theory of action is a causal model, in white-paper form, that shows the hypothesized mechanisms of change and intermediate goals leading to the overarching goal of helping all students read fluently with comprehension. As the abstract states, it shows the hypothesized mechanisms of change and intermediate goals that culminate in the goal of helping all students read fluently with comprehension. It frames the assessment within an equity and measurement agenda.

Claims¶

  • NWEA claims MAP Reading Fluency's mechanisms of change are hypothesized to lead to all students reading fluently with comprehension [+W]
  • Sentence Reading Fluency performance routes students between oral reading and foundational skills tracks [+M]
  • Four oral reading scores together best predict passage comprehension and enable individualized instruction [+W]
  • Adaptive routing and comprehension-based passage leveling structure the MAP Reading Fluency assessment [+M]
  • MAP Reading Fluency administered at scale, with 407,964 students receiving Foundational Skills scores and 277,920 receiving Oral Reading Fluency scores in 2020–2021 [+M]
