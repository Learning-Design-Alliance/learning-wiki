Making Self-Advocacy and Self-Determination Critical Priorities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 11 studies (6 review, 3 theoretical, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q1–q4 · 1 of 11 report an effect size

Description¶

This strategy treats self-advocacy (speaking up for one's needs, rights, and preferences) and self-determination (goal setting, choice-making, problem solving, and self-management) as explicit instructional priorities rather than incidental byproducts of schooling. It is carried out by embedding these skills into teacher preparation, IEP goals, assessment systems, and school accountability measures, and by giving students frequent, authentic opportunities to practice them — leading IEP meetings, making curricular choices, and negotiating accommodations.

Design Implications¶

Self-determination instruction is grounded in Self-Determination Theory: supporting students' autonomy, competence, and relatedness builds intrinsic motivation and engagement Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [+S]. Interventions that explicitly teach self-determination skills (goal setting, self-monitoring, decision making) produce measurable gains in those skills and in access to general education Test et al. review of self-determination interventions. [+M]. The strategy works only when students have genuine decision-making authority; simulated or token choices do not build the skill.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit instruction in component skills: goal setting, self-awareness, problem solving, decision making, and knowledge of one's own disability and accommodations
  • Authentic practice opportunities with real stakes — student-led IEP meetings, choice of learning strategies, self-graded work
  • Teacher preparation that includes self-determination pedagogy, and family education so adults do not inadvertently suppress student voice
  • Assessment and accountability structures that value student agency, not only content mastery

Constraints¶

  • Requires sustained investment of time, resources, training, and a mindset shift that all students can make active choices about their learning and lives
  • Fails when adults retain all decision-making authority and students are given only performative roles in meetings [-M]
  • Students with significant support needs are often excluded from self-determination instruction on the assumption they cannot benefit — an assumption contradicted by adapted curricula such as Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction implementations [+M]
  • Accountability systems that measure only attendance and test scores give schools no incentive to prioritize these skills [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Student-led IEPs — students prepare and present portions of their own IEP meeting, the most widely studied single practice
  • Embedded curricula — programs like the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction (SDLMI) integrate goal setting into academic instruction
  • School-wide — self-advocacy expectations built into advisory programs, transition planning, and family conferences
  • Policy level — including self-determination measures in school performance frameworks and teacher certification requirements

Target Learners¶

  • Students with disabilities, especially adolescents in transition planning, who show stronger post-school employment and independent-living outcomes when self-determination is taught [+M]
  • Students with significant cognitive disabilities, using adapted supports and augmentative communication for choice-making
  • Students with high self-doubt benefit because successful self-advocacy experiences build confidence Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence. [+M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-regulation: goal setting, self-monitoring, and self-evaluation of progress Self-monitoring improves self-regulation. [+M]
  • Motivational outcomes: task engagement and value Task value increases motivation and engagement. [+M]
  • Transition and life skills: independent living, employment self-advocacy, and postsecondary navigation

Instructions¶

  1. Build self-knowledge first — students identify their strengths, needs, and required accommodations through structured Research into their own disability and rights.
  2. Teach goal setting explicitly — students set personally meaningful goals using process-oriented framing rather than outcome-only targets Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices. [+M].
  3. Provide guided practice — use Coaching and role-play so students rehearse requesting accommodations and explaining their needs before high-stakes settings.
  4. Transfer authority — students lead their IEP or parent conference, presenting goals and progress with Whole-Class Sharing or Individual Response formats adapted to the meeting context.
  5. Embed across the school day — teachers offer structured choice in Collaboration tasks and learning strategies, so self-determination is practiced daily, not only at annual meetings.

Related Strategies¶

  • Self-Determination Theory — the motivational foundation; autonomy support is the teacher-side behavior this strategy operationalizes
  • Self-Regulated Learning — the cognitive skill set (goal setting, monitoring, strategy adjustment) that self-determination instruction develops

Examples¶

  • Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction (SDLMI) — University of Kansas Beach Center curriculum in which students cycle through goal setting, action planning, and self-evaluation with teacher coaching; validated across disability categories.
  • I'm Determined — Virginia Department of Education initiative providing student-led IEP templates, self-determination lessons, and youth leadership events.
  • Student-led IEP meetings — students prepare slides or scripts, present their strengths and needs, and chair portions of the meeting; widely implemented in transition programs.
