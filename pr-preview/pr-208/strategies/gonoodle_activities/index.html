GoNoodle Activities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review), q3 · 2 of 4 report an effect size

Description¶

GoNoodle (gonoodle.com) is a free digital platform of short interactive videos — dance breaks, mindfulness and breathing exercises, yoga, and movement-based academic content — designed for classroom use with children. Teachers deploy 2–10 minute activities as energizers, transitions, or calming routines, embedding physical activity and self-regulation practice into the school day rather than relegating it to recess.

Design Implications¶

Brief bouts of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity acutely improve attention and executive function in children Classroom physical activity improves attention. [+M], and single exercise sessions can transiently enhance memory and cognitive performance Acute exercise timing affects memory. [~M]. GoNoodle operationalizes this by making movement breaks fast, low-preparation, and repeatable — the design goal is not fitness but a cognitive reset that restores attention for subsequent instruction.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A screen or projector and internet access; a cleared space for movement
  • Predictable routines: breaks scheduled at known transition points (after seatwork, before testing) rather than at random
  • Clear start/stop signals so the activity does not dissolve into unmanaged play

Constraints¶

  • Benefits are acute and short-lived; a single break does not build fitness or durable self-regulation skill Acute exercise timing affects memory. [~M] — effects depend on regular, scheduled use
  • Poorly timed breaks (mid-task, or immediately before activities requiring calm focus without a settling routine) can disrupt rather than restore attention [-M]
  • High-stimulation videos used indiscriminately can add arousal without purpose; energizing content and calming content serve different moments and should be matched to the goal
  • Screen-based movement is a supplement to, not a substitute for, free play and physical education [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Energizers (e.g., "Pop 'N' Bop," "Release Your Warrior") before or during long seated blocks to raise arousal
  • Settlers (e.g., "Breathing Exercises For Focusing And Settling," "Nimble Nostrils") to down-regulate after recess or before assessments
  • Academic integration: videos that embed counting, spelling, or content review into movement, pairing Act It Out with recall
  • Product design: the same pattern — short, guided, movement-based interludes — can be built into learning apps as attention resets between tasks

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary-age children (K–5), who show the largest acute attention benefits from classroom activity breaks Classroom physical activity improves attention. [+M]
  • Learners with ADHD or high activity needs, for whom structured movement outlets support on-task behavior [+M]
  • Whole classes during transitions, where a shared routine also supports group cohesion and emotional regulation

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Attention restoration and readiness to learn (a precondition, not a content objective)
  • Emotional regulation and stress reduction through guided breathing and mindfulness [+W]
  • Incidental academic content (counting, vocabulary) via movement-embedded videos [~W]

Instructions¶

  1. Identify the transition or low-attention point in the lesson where a break will do the most good.
  2. Select an energizing or calming video matched to the goal — raise arousal before work, lower it after.
  3. Establish the routine: signal, participate, and end with a clear return-to-seat cue (Gain Attention in reverse).
  4. Follow the break immediately with focused instruction while the attention benefit is active Acute exercise timing affects memory. [~M].
  5. Use calming activities as a recurring Check-In routine to build self-regulation habits over the year.

Related Strategies¶

  • Active Recess — unstructured physical activity serving the same attention-regulation function without screens
  • Brain Breaks — the generic pattern GoNoodle productizes
  • Mindfulness Activities — the calming subset of GoNoodle's library

Examples¶

  • GoNoodle "Brez" and flow channels — guided breathing videos used before tests to settle a class (gonoodle.com)
  • "Pop 'N' Bop" / "Release Your Warrior" — high-energy dance and movement videos used as mid-morning energizers
  • Physical Activity Across the Curriculum (PAAC) — a research program embedding academic content in movement breaks, the evidence base this product category draws on
