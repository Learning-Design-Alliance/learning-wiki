Reciprocal Teaching: Peer Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS)¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 13 studies (6 quant-synthesis, 4 causal, 2 review, 1 qualitative), q2–q4 · 5 of 13 report an effect size

Description¶

PALS is a classwide peer-tutoring routine in which students work in pairs, alternating higher- and lower-performing roles ("Coach" and "Player"), and move through scripted activities — partner reading with retells, paragraph shrinking, and prediction relay — that operationalize the Reciprocal Teaching strategies of summarizing, questioning, clarifying, and predicting. The teacher trains the routines, monitors pairs, and awards points for accurate coaching, but the bulk of instructional time is spent in structured peer interaction rather than teacher-led instruction.

Design Implications¶

PALS combines the comprehension-strategy modeling of reciprocal teaching with the structural features of cooperative learning: role reciprocity, individual accountability, and group reward contingencies Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves outcomes. [+S]. The Coach role is itself a learning opportunity — explaining and correcting a peer produces gains for the tutor, not only the tutee Learning by teaching improves the tutor's own learning. [+S]. The scripts and point system function as Scaffolding that is gradually faded as pairs internalize the strategies Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Careful pairing: a stronger reader with a weaker reader, with the stronger reader reading first as the initial model
  • Explicit training phase in which the teacher models each coaching move and students rehearse the scripts (Coaching and Think-Aloud by the teacher)
  • Short, timed activity segments with clear role-switch cues to keep both students engaged
  • A point system that rewards correct strategy use and specific, positive corrections, not speed or competition between pairs

Constraints¶

  • Gains shrink when pairs are poorly matched or when the stronger reader dominates without executing genuine coaching moves [~M]
  • The scripted routines constrain strategy depth; strong comprehenders may plateau because the prompts cap the sophistication of their questioning and summarizing [~M]
  • Requires sustained implementation — effects emerge over weeks of regular sessions, not single lessons [-W]
  • Without teacher monitoring, pairs drift off-script and the point system degenerates into off-task point-keeping [~M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • PALS Reading (grades 2–6) vs. PALS for high school — same role structure with grade-appropriate texts and strategy demands
  • Kindergarten PALS (K-PALS) — phonemic awareness and letter-sound activities replacing comprehension strategies
  • PALS Math — same coaching structure applied to computation and problem-solving scripts
  • Sessions typically run 3–4 times per week for 25–35 minutes over 12–16 weeks

Target Learners¶

  • Struggling readers and students with learning disabilities in inclusive classrooms, who gain from the high rate of supervised practice and immediate feedback Feedback is most effective at the task and process levels. [+S]
  • English learners, who benefit from the oral rehearsal and structured turn-taking
  • Average and high performers also gain, primarily through the Coach role Learning by teaching improves the tutor's own learning. [+S]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading fluency through repeated oral reading with a responsive partner
  • Comprehension strategy use: summarizing, question generation, clarification, prediction
  • Self-regulation of reading: monitoring understanding and repairing breakdowns Contingent scaffolding improves learning. [+M]

Instructions¶

  1. Pair and assign roles. Rank students by reading performance, split the list, and pair across it; designate the stronger reader as the first Coach.
  2. Train the routines. Model each activity — partner reading, paragraph shrinking (summarize to the main idea in ten words or fewer), prediction relay — with the whole class rehearsing before pairs begin.
  3. Partner reading with retell. The Player reads aloud; the Coach follows the text, corrects errors, and awards points, then asks for a retell (Practice with immediate correction).
  4. Paragraph shrinking. Players read and summarize paragraph by paragraph; Coaches prompt with scripted questions and award points for accurate, brief summaries (Summarization).
  5. Prediction relay. Partners alternate predicting what comes next, reading to check, and generating new predictions — the reciprocal-teaching core (Questioning, Prediction).
  6. Monitor and fade. Circulate, reinforce accurate coaching, and progressively reduce script reliance as pairs internalize the strategies (Fading).

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — the teacher-led dialogic routine whose strategies PALS redistributes to peer pairs
  • Peer Tutoring — the broader family of same-age and cross-age tutoring arrangements PALS systematizes
  • Jigsaw — another cooperative structure with interdependent roles and individual accountability
  • Train students in tutor and group roles before routine use of peer tutoring or response groups
  • Train teachers to implement peer-tutoring programs through an all-day modeling and role-playing workshop plus a program manual
  • Arrange PALS tutor training through AIR by specifying training type, grade level, group size, and location

Examples¶

  • Vanderbilt Kennedy Center PALS (https://kc.vanderbilt.edu/pals) — the official PALS Reading, Math, and K-PALS programs with teacher manuals and training materials, developed by Doug and Lynn Fuchs.
  • Grade 3 inclusive classroom — 30 minutes of PALS Reading three times weekly for 14 weeks; the lowest third of readers showed fluency and comprehension gains on curriculum-based measures relative to control classrooms (Fuchs et al., 1997).
