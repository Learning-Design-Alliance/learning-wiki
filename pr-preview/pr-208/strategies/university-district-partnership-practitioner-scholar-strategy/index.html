Use university–district research partnerships to build district evaluation capacity and train practitioner-scholars¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's strategy is a university–district research partnership in which the university provides district-driven research support and the partnership's projects serve as authentic training for practitioner-scholars. Schools of education play a role in "preparing our K-12 leaders for future challenges by adding to our research-based knowledge," and the Multnomah County example shows a collaboration that simultaneously met district evaluation needs and developed scholars' rigorous evaluation skills through a real program evaluation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • District willingness to partner and identify evaluation questions
  • University capacity to support rigorous evaluation work

Constraints¶

  • Many partnership models struggle to develop research projects collaboratively, per the article's own framing

Target Learners¶

  • practitioner-scholars in university schools of education
  • K-12 district leaders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • rigorous program evaluation practice
  • collaborative research design

Related Strategies¶

  • Build agency analytic capacity by hiring technically skilled staff, partnering with vendors, and providing data-use training
