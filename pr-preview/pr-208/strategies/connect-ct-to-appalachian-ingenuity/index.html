Connect computer science learning to Appalachian ingenuity and community identity to move it from novelty to change¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The presentation's current guiding question is connecting computational thinking with what district leader Neil Arnett calls Appalachian ingenuity: asking "Who are the community makers? Who are the creators?" so that computer science education captures and perpetuates local creative traditions. Arnett reflects that this framing "takes [computer science] from novelty to actual change, and it ties it directly to the people on the ground."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Curriculum must be framed around local creators, makers, and historians rather than generic computing content

Constraints¶

  • Developed specifically for Appalachian districts where regional identity is a strong motivational asset

Target Learners¶

  • K-8 students in Eastern Kentucky

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Computational thinking
  • Identity as community makers and creators

Related Strategies¶

  • Connect computational thinking to local maker and problem-solver heritage to teach it responsively to local culture
  • Use named community strengths — kinship, hopes and dreams for the future, grit and resistance to injustice — as scaffolds in K-8 CS/CT instruction
  • Panel protocol: open with community cultural resources, then moderated discussion connecting community assets to computing education
