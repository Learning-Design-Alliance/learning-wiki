Use art-based sharing, short reviews, interviews, and Book Wheels when students each read different books¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

When each student reads something different, the article recommends activities that close the information gap: art projects (posters, bookmarks, comics) shared via Round Robin; Roving Reporter, where a numbered student visits other groups to collect ideas and book suggestions; short book reviews (star systems, one-paragraph critiques) shared via Round Robin or Three-Step Interview; and Book Wheels, a cardboard wheel with thinking questions spun to generate follow-up questions for each Reporter. Each is PIES-analyzed; e.g., in Three-Step Interview "Two students per group (50% of the class) are speaking simultaneously."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Groups of about four; rotating roles; for Book Wheels, teacher and/or students construct wheels with 8-12 quadrants each holding a thinking question.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes L2 students may lack the proficiency to explain unfamiliar books, which is why structured formats are needed; activities are suggestions, not tested interventions.

Target Learners¶

  • L2 students choosing their own extensive reading materials

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sharing about individually read books, recommending materials, and practicing L2 speaking

Affordances¶

  • Kagan Pies Cooperative Learning Principles

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement extensive reading via stand-alone courses, curricular components, or reading clubs, with self-report accountability and no comprehension testing
  • Sharing and Discussion of Independent Reading
  • Use structured CL techniques such as Showdown and Trade-A-Problem when the whole class reads the same book
