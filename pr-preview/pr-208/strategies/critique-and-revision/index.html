Critique And Revision¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 3 report an effect size

Description¶

Critique and revision asks learners to evaluate a piece of work against explicit criteria, identify specific weaknesses, and then produce a revised version that addresses them. The critique may target peers' work, exemplars, or the learner's own draft; the revision closes the loop by requiring the critique to be acted on rather than merely stated.

Design Implications¶

Critique and revision operationalizes Assessment for Learning: feedback only improves achievement when learners actually use it to close the gap between current and target performance Assessment for learning improves achievement. [+S]. Critiquing others' work also builds evaluative judgment — the ability to judge quality — which transfers to self-assessment of one's own work [~M]. Revision is where the learning happens; critique without an opportunity to revise produces little durable gain.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit criteria or rubrics so critique is anchored to standards, not taste
  • Work at an appropriate distance from learners' own level — neither flawless nor hopeless — so flaws are detectable
  • Protected time and expectation for a second iteration (Practice structured as draft → critique → revise)
  • Instructor modeling of high-quality critique (Think-Aloud or annotated exemplars)

Constraints¶

  • Feedback without revision opportunity has small and often negligible effects on achievement [-S] — the gap-closing action, not the feedback delivery, drives learning
  • Vague criteria ("make it better") produce vague critique and cosmetic revision [~M]
  • Peer critique of work far above or below learners' competence yields unreliable judgments and wasted effort [~M]
  • Social risk: unstructured peer critique can damage Belonging and willingness to share drafts; norms and protocols are required [-W]
  • Over-revision cycles can degrade Cognitive Load Management if every draft is fully reworked rather than targeted [~W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Gallery critique (Ron Berger / EL Education protocols): whole-class critique of one anonymous work, then individual revision
  • Two stars and a wish: structured peer feedback with fixed praise/suggestion slots
  • Exemplar comparison: learners rank and critique anonymized samples before touching their own drafts
  • Automated revision: systems like Revision Assistant (Turnitin) deliver criterion-linked feedback on argumentative writing for iterative revision

Target Learners¶

  • Intermediate learners who have enough domain knowledge to detect flaws but not yet to avoid them [~M]
  • Writing and design students, where iterative drafting is the authentic professional practice [+M]
  • Novices benefit more from critiquing annotated expert/non-expert exemplars than from unguided peer critique [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Evaluative judgment: internalizing quality criteria Assessment for learning improves achievement. [+S]
  • Deep revision of argument and structure rather than surface editing
  • Self-regulated learning: monitoring one's own work against standards

Instructions¶

  1. Establish criteria with learners using Non-Examples and annotated exemplars so quality is visible before any critique.
  2. Have learners critique a sample or peer draft against the criteria, naming specific locations and reasons — modeled first via Think-Aloud.
  3. Require a revision that responds point-by-point to the critique, not a rewrite from scratch.
  4. Repeat with Fading: shift from instructor-led critique to structured peer critique to independent self-critique as evaluative skill grows.

Related Strategies¶

  • Peer Review — the reciprocal form; critique and revision is the single-author version of the same cycle
  • Drafting And Feedback Cycles — the broader workflow this strategy instantiates

Examples¶

  • EL Education / Expeditionary Learning (https://eleducation.org) — "kind, specific, helpful" critique protocols and multiple-draft "beautiful work" projects across its network schools.
  • Calibrated Peer Review (https://calibratedpeerreview.org) — students first critique calibration samples with known scores, then review peers, then revise their own work.
  • Studio-based art and architecture instruction — the traditional "crit," where public critique of work-in-progress directly precedes revision.
