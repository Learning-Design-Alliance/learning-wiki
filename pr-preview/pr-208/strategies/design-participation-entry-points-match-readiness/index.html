Design participation deliberately: align entry points with partners' actual readiness rather than assuming a uniform baseline¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's lessons for the field hold that "Participation has to be designed for, not assumed." Evaluators working in formal evaluation contexts should make deliberate choices about when and how to create entry points for partner voice, and those entry points should align with partners' actual readiness rather than assuming a uniform baseline across sites. The report also recommends investing in relational groundwork as seriously as technical rigor, and sharing data through accessible formats such as site visit memos and shared interpretation sessions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires evaluators to invest in relationships, trust, and repeated communication with funders and program teams over time.

Constraints¶

  • When participatory practices are not built into the original scope, the depth of participation will depend on surrounding conditions including time, trust, funder expectations, and whether data are shared in usable ways.

Target Learners¶

  • teachers
  • coaches
  • mentors
  • program staff
  • students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • meaningful partner participation in evaluation design, interpretation, and use of findings
