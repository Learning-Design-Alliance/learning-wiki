  • Visual Cues - Pairing spoken or written information with graphics, diagrams, or gestures to support comprehension, attention, and retention.
  • Visual Mnemonics - Visual mnemonics involve creating visual images to cue a strategy or represent a concept.
  • Visual Organizers - Providing or prompting learners to create spatial-graphic representations (maps, matrices, diagrams) that make the structure of content visible.
  • Visual Prompts for Essay Writing - Using artwork paired with life-lesson statements as timed writing prompts to help adolescents generate and elaborate essay ideas.
  • Visual Reading Aids - Physical or digital tools — reading guides, colored overlays, tracking rulers, and finger/pointer tracking — that reduce visual stress and support place-keeping and line tracking during reading.
  • Visual Representations in Mathematics - Using schematic diagrams, number lines, strip diagrams, and other visuals to depict mathematical quantities and relationships so learners can reason about abstract concepts and solve problems.
  • Visual Schedules - A visual schedule presents the sequence of activities, steps, or transitions in an image- or symbol-based format so learners can anticipate, navigate, and self-manage their time.
  • Visual Supports - Visual supports use images, diagrams, graphic organizers, and other visual representations to complement or structure verbal information and reduce cognitive load.
  • Visual Timers - Visual timers make elapsed and remaining time visible, supporting time concepts, transitions, and on-task behavior for learners who struggle with abstract time.
  • Visualizations and Mind Maps - Use visuals such as infographics, diagrams, and mind maps to transform abstract concepts into clear, structured formats that show how elements connect.
  • Visualizing - Visualizing involves creating a mental image or external representation — a picture, diagram, table, or sketch — of a problem or text to aid understanding and problem solving.
  • Vocabulary In Context - Learners acquire word meanings by inferring them from meaningful reading or listening contexts, supported by multiple varied encounters rather than isolated definition study.
  • Vocabulary Instruction - Explicitly teaching target words and their meanings — with definitions, context, and repeated use — so students can understand and deploy new vocabulary in reading, writing, and discussion.
  • Vocabulary Instruction Small Groups - Small-group instruction that teaches high-value vocabulary through explicit explanation, multiple encounters, and collaborative word use.
  • Vocabulary Journals - Learners maintain a personal, ongoing record of target words with definitions, contexts, and self-generated elaborations, revisited over time.
  • Vocabulary Meaning Through Context - Instructors present vocabulary embedded in meaningful context so learners infer meaning themselves, aiding understanding and retention.
  • Vocabulary Pre Teaching - Teaching key word meanings before learners encounter them in a text or task, so that unfamiliar vocabulary does not block comprehension.
