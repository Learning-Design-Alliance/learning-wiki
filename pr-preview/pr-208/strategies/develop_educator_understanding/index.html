Develop Educator Understanding¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Educators develop their own understanding of self-awareness — their own and their students' — and how it affects learning by studying research, examining learner variability data, and reflecting on their practice. The strategy treats the educator as a learner first: before designing supports for students' self-awareness, teachers build accurate mental models of emotional regulation, metacognition, and the ways their own interpretations of student behavior can be biased or incomplete.

Design Implications¶

Professional learning that changes practice is sustained, active, collaborative, and connected to teachers' own classrooms — one-off workshops on "self-awareness" rarely alter instruction Teacher professional development changes practice when it is sustained, active, and job-embedded. [+M]. A critical starting point is recognizing that self-assessment of one's own competence is systematically unreliable, which applies to educators judging their own understanding of students as much as to students judging their learning Flawed self-assessment: people overestimate their competence without feedback or external standards. [+S]. Tools such as Digital Promise's Learner Variability Navigator make the research base navigable by mapping factors (e.g., self-regulation, emotion identification, growth mindset) that interact within whole learners rather than as isolated traits.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to research summaries, journals, and curated tools (e.g., the Learner Variability Navigator), plus protected time for reading and reflection
  • Structured opportunities to connect new knowledge to the educator's own students and lessons — reading alone does not transfer to practice Professional development changes teaching only when it includes active learning connected to teachers' contexts. [+M]
  • A feedback loop: observation, coaching, or student data that lets educators check whether their new understanding matches what actually happens in class

Constraints¶

  • One-shot workshops and passive reading produce little durable change in practice Professional development changes teaching only when it includes active learning connected to teachers' contexts. [-M]
  • Educators' confidence in their own self-awareness is a poor guide to its accuracy; without external feedback, misconceptions about students persist Flawed self-assessment: people overestimate their competence without feedback or external standards. [-S]
  • Research summaries can be misapplied as labels ("this student lacks self-regulation") rather than as descriptions of malleable, contextual factors

Implementation Variability¶

  • Individual study (curated readings, tool exploration) vs. collaborative structures (Professional Learning Communities, book studies, lesson study)
  • Depth varies: awareness-level exposure to the research vs. deep engagement through Case Studies and Coaching
  • Focus varies by need: self-awareness specifically, or broader learner variability (working memory, background knowledge, motivation)

Target Learners¶

  • Classroom teachers seeking to support students' self-awareness and emotional regulation
  • New educators building foundational models of learner variability before habits solidify
  • Experienced educators, who benefit most when materials challenge existing assumptions rather than confirm them Flawed self-assessment: people overestimate their competence without feedback or external standards. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Conceptual understanding: how self-awareness, emotion regulation, and metacognition develop and interact with academic learning
  • Applied understanding: selecting and adapting evidence-based supports for specific students and contexts
  • Reflective practice: recognizing how one's own interpretations and biases shape responses to student behavior

Instructions¶

  1. Identify a focus — e.g., why a particular student struggles to persist after setbacks — and gather research using the Learner Variability Navigator or curated Assigned Readings.
  2. Study the factors involved, noting which are malleable and contextual rather than fixed traits.
  3. Connect to practice through Case Studies of learners similar to your own students, or by analyzing anonymized work from your classroom.
  4. Reflect and articulate: write or discuss what you now believe about the student's behavior and what you would do differently (Class Discussion in a PLC or Coaching conversation).
  5. Test the new understanding in instruction, then use student response and Check-In data to revise — treating your own understanding as provisional.

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop Understanding — the student-facing pattern this strategy mirrors for educators

Examples¶

  • Digital Promise's Learner Variability Navigator — educators explore research-backed factors (self-regulation, emotion identification, belonging) across whole-learner models and pull matched strategies for their students.
  • Lesson study (jugyō kenkyū) — Japanese-origin professional learning in which teachers jointly study research, plan a lesson, observe it, and revise; the observation cycle supplies the external feedback that self-study alone lacks.
  • CAST professional learning on Universal Design for Learning — educators study the UDL Guidelines and learner variability research, then redesign a lesson and compare outcomes.
