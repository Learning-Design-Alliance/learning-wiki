Develop ICAP-based activity prompts, revise templates, and provide professional development for online course designers¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends a set of implementable steps for raising active learning in asynchronous online courses: develop prompts using the ICAP Framework, signal lower-level activities differently in templates, keep a record of every activity as part of the design process, and provide professional development for academic staff and learning designers. The authors state: "An improvement would be to develop prompts using the ICAP Framework," citing Hefter et al. on carefully constructed prompts increasing active learning such as self-explaining.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Time and workload scheduling for professional development, which the authors note is a challenge for academic staff given time constraints

Constraints¶

  • The authors note the template's shorthand icons may make it harder to develop active tasks outside the template, and lower-level activities are easier and quicker to design

Target Learners¶

  • academic staff and learning designers of online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • designing active learning activities
  • course-level learning design

Affordances¶

  • Revised Icap Feedback Asynchronous Online

Related Strategies¶

  • Support e-teachers' transition with professional development focused on reflecting on online-versus-F2F differences and the potential of the tools
