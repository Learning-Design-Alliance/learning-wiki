Three performance task platforms: technology-enhanced, technology-enabled, and paper-and-pencil¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study manipulated delivery of a mathematics performance task across three platforms: "technology-enhanced (technology-based including animation and interactivity)," "technology-enabled (computer-based without including animation and interactivity)," and "paper-and-pencil." This three-level modality contrast was evaluated with a counterbalanced quasi-experimental design on student self-reported cognitive engagement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Counterbalanced assignment of students across the three platform conditions to evaluate modality effects

Constraints¶

  • The technology-enabled platform was computer-based but excluded animation and interactivity, distinguishing it from the technology-enhanced platform

Target Learners¶

  • students in grades 6-8 in Oregon, Washington, and North Carolina

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive engagement in mathematics performance tasks

Claims¶

  • Performance task platform modality (technology-enhanced, technology-enabled, or paper-and-pencil) showed no relationship with grade 6-8 students' self-reported cognitive engagement in mathematics [~W]
  • Students appreciated specific features of paper-and-pencil tasks, namely the ability to navigate between items and to take notes [+W]
  • Students preferred taking tests with technology and showed favorability toward the technology-enhanced performance task, citing interactivity and animations that help visualize and work through problems [+W]
