Longitudinal SEL dataset from a large urban district with about 3,000 students per timepoint¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A four-year longitudinal dataset of social-emotional learning scores used to fit growth models for three constructs related to transitioning to secondary school. The article describes it as "four years of longitudinal SEL data from students in a large urban district with a sample size of about 3,000 students per timepoint." The dataset supports comparing a student's mean 6th-grade SEL score against growth between 6th and 8th grade as predictors of being off track academically in 9th grade.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal SEL measures collected across four years
  • Coverage of students in a large urban district across intermediate and early secondary grades

Constraints¶

  • Sample size of about 3,000 students per timepoint as reported
  • Drawn from a single large urban district, limiting generalizability beyond that setting

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 6-9 in a large urban district

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Modeling SEL growth trajectories
  • Predicting 9th-grade academic off-track status

Affordances¶

  • Sel Trajectory Status Growth Framework

Claims¶

  • In a large urban district, 6th-grade SEL status is more frequently a significant predictor of being off track academically in 9th grade than SEL growth between 6th and 8th grade [+M]
  • Understanding how a student develops social-emotionally over intermediate school can improve identification of students not on track to succeed in high school [+M]
  • Growth in self-management between 6th and 8th grade predicts being off track academically in 9th grade above and beyond 6th-grade self-management status [+M]

Related Elements¶

  • Four years of longitudinal social-emotional survey scores analyzed under competing IRT scoring models
