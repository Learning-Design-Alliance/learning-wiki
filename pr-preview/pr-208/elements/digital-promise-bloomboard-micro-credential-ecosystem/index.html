Digital Promise micro-credential ecosystem on the BloomBoard platform¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Digital Promise micro-credentialing ecosystem is "currently housed on a platform powered by the ed tech startup BloomBoard." Micro-credentials are described as mini-certifications in which K-12 educators "present evidence of their ability to demonstrate competency in teaching or leading school improvement and receive quick feedback from trained reviewers." As of June 2017, DP and its 40 content partners had developed over 300 micro-credentials, and the platform hosted more than 5,000 submissions from educators in approximately 550 districts. The report also describes platform functions: navigation from selection to submission, assignment of submissions, reviewer notes, data gathering, and paywall transactions.

Claims¶

  • As of mid-2017 the Digital Promise micro-credential ecosystem had drawn over 5,000 submissions from educators in approximately 550 districts, with about 55 percent of portfolio submitters awarded a micro-credential [+W]
  • In Kettle Moraine School District, about 80 percent of teachers earned at least one pre-approved micro-credential over three years, raising base salaries by $100 to $600 [+W]
  • Nine states offered CEUs for Digital Promise micro-credentials by 2017, but no coherent framework existed for valuing micro-credentials [+W]
  • Districts and universities increasingly attach concrete incentives to earning Digital Promise micro-credentials [+W]
  • About 31 percent of surveyed teachers report being extremely or very likely to try micro-credentials once introduced to the idea, with another 34 percent somewhat interested [+W]
  • At least ten state education agencies have launched official micro-credential pilots, with Tennessee's pilot reaching almost 800 educators [+W]
  • State certification directors are cautious yet hopeful about micro-credentials, citing quality control and compensation-system fit as concerns [+W]
