Anecdote task (my treasured possession): mingling retelling of a personal story across partner changes¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A low-preparation speaking task in which learners tell the story of a treasured possession to a classmate they normally do not work with, each speaking for 2 minutes, then change pairs and retell. Students get 1 minute of planning time beforehand, since "planning for challenging tasks is an important step that improves students' performance" (Ellis 2005, as quoted); notes may be taken but not used while speaking.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Guiding questions prepared to scaffold learners with more difficulty; prior practice with the needed vocabulary and past forms; cycles repeated only while the class benefits so the task does not become automated and too repetitive

Constraints¶

  • Planning notes cannot be used during the speaking task, otherwise it might turn into a reading activity

Target Learners¶

  • additional language learners practising past forms and personal-experience vocabulary

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fluency in extended personal storytelling; adjectives and past forms

Affordances¶

  • Task Repetition Slightly Altered Tasks

Claims¶

  • Across repetition cycles, attention can shift from message meaning to linguistic form, enabling recycled language and self-correction [+W]
  • Lynch and Maclean's carousel task showed immediate repetition benefits in pronunciation, vocabulary and grammar [+W]
  • Immediate task repetition benefits oral performance, including fluency, self-correction and reduced anxiety [+W]
  • Treating students' personal histories as sources of knowledge makes them part of the classroom curriculum [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Decision-making restaurant-choice task: pairs merge into larger groups across cycles while re-choosing a restaurant
  • Travel fair carousel task: pairs present a destination to rotating visitors across immediate repetition cycles

Examples¶

  • Convert ordinary textbook or internet speaking tasks into immediate repetition tasks by adding interlocutor change or task alteration
