Write to Succeed professional learning program for grade 4-8 teachers¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Write to Succeed is a school-year professional learning program in which teachers "engage in collaborative learning on evidence-based literacy practices" that develop students' language skills within content instruction and scaffold writing, especially for English learner students. It comprises coach professional learning (a two-day institute each semester plus three hours of ongoing support, about 19 hours per semester), six hours of teacher group professional learning with coach modeling, three hours of collaborative planning, and two to three hours of individual coaching with lesson observation and debrief per semester, plus a teacher binder and curriculum-integration guide. Practices are intended for use in all content areas and any curriculum, organized around a teacher-selected focal unit with a writing goal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A coach to lead group professional learning, model practices, and observe and debrief lessons; the report notes schools without a local coach had poor implementation

Constraints¶

  • Pilot participation was difficult to complete in full, and teachers and coaches recommended starting implementation earlier and aligning activities with curriculum timing

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 4-8 students, including English learner students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Writing and literacy within content instruction
  • Language development for English learner students

Claims¶

  • Teachers reported increased use of culturally and linguistically relevant instruction over the year, while teacher collaboration remained unchanged [~W]
  • Teachers with a local coach completed far more of the intended Write to Succeed professional learning activities than teachers without one [+W]
  • Most pilot teachers found the Write to Succeed materials helpful, and teachers and coaches benefited from seeing practices modeled [+W]
  • Most pilot teachers used the Write to Succeed practices and found them easy to use and helpful for students, with ease of use increasing across semesters [+W]
  • Teacher confidence in teaching literacy and English learner students increased over the pilot year, especially with a local coach [+W]
