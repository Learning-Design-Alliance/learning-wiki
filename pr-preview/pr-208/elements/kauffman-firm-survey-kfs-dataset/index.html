Kauffman Firm Survey (KFS) panel dataset of U.S. businesses founded in 2004¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The Kauffman Firm Survey (KFS) is a panel dataset on new U.S. businesses, sponsored by the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation and conducted by Mathematica Policy Research. The report states it is "a panel study of new businesses founded in 2004 and tracked over their early years of operation," and that it "collects data about the nature of new business formation activity; characteristics of the strategy, offerings, and employment patterns; the nature of the financial and organizational arrangements of these businesses; and the characteristics of their founders." It was created because only limited data existed on firms in their first years.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sponsorship and contractor infrastructure: the Foundation contracted with Mathematica Policy Research to conduct the KFS
  • A longitudinal panel design tracking the 2004 founding cohort over multiple early years of operation

Constraints¶

  • Limited to businesses founded in 2004 in the United States
  • Covers only the early years of operation, with a stated time frame of 2004-2013

Target Learners¶

  • Researchers and analysts studying new business formation and entrepreneurship

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding the nature of new business formation activity
  • Understanding firms' strategy, offerings, employment patterns, financial and organizational arrangements, and founder characteristics

Claims¶

  • Only a small amount of data is available about U.S. businesses in their first years of operation [-W]
