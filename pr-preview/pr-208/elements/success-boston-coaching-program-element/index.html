Success Boston Coaching: one-on-one transition coaching with five key components for underrepresented college students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Success Boston Coaching is "a coaching intervention for students who are traditionally underrepresented in college to help them transition from high school to college and progress in college". Students are paired with a dedicated coach starting as early as the spring of senior year of high school through their first two years of college, delivered via nonprofit coaching partners. The article states the program "is comprised of the following key components: one-on-one transition coaching, financial aid information and nudges, other direct services, financial support, and professional development for coaches". Coaching interactions range from three to 15 times per year, lasting 25 to 40 minutes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires nonprofit coaching partners staffed with coaches, college liaisons, and coordination with partner colleges; coach caseloads range from 12 to 65 students depending on the partner.

Constraints¶

  • The intervention was studied in the Greater Boston area with students traditionally underrepresented in college; effects are not known for outcomes such as college enrollment, degree attainment, employment, or earnings.

Target Learners¶

  • High school seniors transitioning to two- and four-year colleges who are traditionally underrepresented in college, including low-income, first-generation students, and students of color

Target Learning Goals¶

  • College persistence to the second year
  • Cumulative college grade point average
  • Life, study, help-seeking, and academic skills

Claims¶

  • In the Linkow et al. (2017) QED, coaching produced a statistically significant positive effect on cumulative college GPA [+W]
  • In the Linkow et al. (2017) QED, coaching produced a statistically significant positive effect on second-year college enrollment [+W]
  • In the Linkow et al. (2019) scale-up QED, coaching showed no statistically significant effect on cumulative college GPA (effect size 0.01, p = 0.82) [-W]
  • In the Linkow et al. (2019) scale-up QED, coaching produced a statistically significant positive effect on second-year college enrollment [+W]
  • Success Boston Coaching has potentially positive effects on academic achievement, with a significant effect in one study and an indeterminate effect in the other [+W]
  • Success Boston Coaching has potentially positive effects on progressing in college, based on two quasi-experimental studies meeting WWC standards with reservations [+W]
