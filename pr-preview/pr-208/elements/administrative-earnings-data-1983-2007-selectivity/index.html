Administrative earnings data spanning 1983 through 2007 used to estimate career-long returns to college selectivity¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's evidence base is administrative earnings data used to estimate the effect of attending a highly selective college on future earnings. The page states the paper "uses administrative earnings data" and draws on "earnings data from a longer time horizon (from 1983 through 2007)" plus a 1989 entering cohort, allowing returns to be estimated over a longer career span than the prior study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires administrative earnings records linked to college attendance for the studied cohorts

Constraints¶

  • Evidence described on the page covers earnings data from 1983 through 2007 and cohorts entering college in 1976 and 1989

Target Learners¶

  • College-bound students and college entrants

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding the career earnings payoff of attending a more selective college

Claims¶

  • Estimates of the return to college selectivity fall substantially, often to near zero, when earnings are tracked from 1983 through 2007 and for a 1989 entering cohort [+W]
  • The return to college selectivity remains large for black, Hispanic, and students from less-educated families [+W]
  • The study extends a previous paper that examined the 1995 earnings of a cohort of students who entered college in 1976 [+W]
