Framework for Leadership (FFL): Pennsylvania's tool for evaluating school leaders' effectiveness¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Framework for Leadership (FFL) is described as "Pennsylvania's tool for evaluating school leaders' effectiveness." It was piloted statewide over two school years, 2012/13 and 2013/14, and its measurement properties were examined across score variation, internal consistency, score stability, and concurrent validity. The element is the evaluation instrument itself, studied during pilot implementation rather than at scale.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Used within Pennsylvania's system for evaluating school leaders' effectiveness, as piloted in 2012/13 and 2013/14

Constraints¶

  • Evidence about the tool comes from its pilot implementation period, not full-scale use

Target Learners¶

  • School leaders (principals and other administrators) being evaluated

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate measurement of school leadership effectiveness

Claims¶

  • A multiyear pilot study analyzed the score variation, internal consistency, score stability, and concurrent validity of Pennsylvania's Framework for Leadership [~W]
  • The full FFL showed good internal consistency for both principals and assistant principals in the 2012/13 pilot [+W]
  • School leaders who scored higher in one category of FFL leadership practices tended to score higher in the other categories [+W]
  • Most FFL scores for specific leadership practices fell in the top two of four performance levels, indicating limited score variation [-W]
  • FFL scores were not associated with school leaders' estimated contributions to student achievement growth [-W]
  • Most school leaders received scores in the top two performance categories on FFL-measured practices [-W]
