Ewing Marion Kauffman Charter School Evaluation (Mathematica, 2011–2024)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An associated Mathematica impact evaluation project with the stated time frame 2011–2024, prepared for the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation. The Year 8 report "evaluates the impact of the Kauffman School on student college enrollment, high school graduation, achievement, attendance, and suspensions during its first eight years of operation," spanning the domains of education, college and career readiness, and school choice and charters.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Prepared for the Ewing Marion Kauffman Foundation as the associated project's funder

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 5 through 12 in Kansas City, Missouri

Target Learning Goals¶

  • College enrollment
  • High school graduation
  • Achievement, attendance, and suspension outcomes

Claims¶

  • Kauffman School college enrollment and achievement impacts are comparable to average effects for other high-performing charter schools [+W]
  • The Kauffman School has a positive and statistically significant impact on four-year college enrollment [+W]
  • The Kauffman School produces sustained positive achievement growth impacts in mathematics, English language arts, and science [+W]
  • Kauffman students had higher attendance rates and were less likely to be suspended than similar Kansas City students [+W]
  • The Kauffman School produced positive, statistically significant, and educationally meaningful impacts on student achievement growth in mathematics, English language arts, and science in each of its first five years, beyond the growth of students in other Kansas City public schools [+W]
  • The Kauffman School has higher ACT scores than the Kansas City high school average, but its impact on ACT scores could not be calculated [+W]
  • During its first seven years, the Kauffman School had substantial positive impacts on student achievement growth in mathematics, English language arts, and science beyond growth in other Kansas City public schools [+W]
