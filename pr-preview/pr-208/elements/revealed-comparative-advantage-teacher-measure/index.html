Revealed comparative advantage: a teacher-quality measure estimating teachers' impact on racial achievement differences¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article introduces "a novel measure of teacher quality—called revealed comparative advantage—that estimates teachers' impact on racial achievement differences." A teacher's comparative advantage for Black students is their differential impact on Black students relative to non-Black students; a positive value means the teacher closed existing racial achievement differences, and the measure "can be interpreted as the teacher's contribution to equity." It is constructed from the same race-specific value-added calculations used in the study and can be incorporated into policy decisions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires the same information used to compute value-added measures of teacher quality often used in teacher evaluations, at no additional cost.

Constraints¶

  • The brief cautions that the same criticisms raised for value-added measures may apply, and small sample problems are compounded because student-type-specific teacher effects are estimated with small sets of students.

Target Learners¶

  • Black and non-Black K-12 students in elementary and middle grades

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reducing racial achievement gaps in math and English test scores

Claims¶

  • Teacher effectiveness for Black students is only imperfectly correlated with effectiveness for non-Black students (correlations between 0.28 and 0.67 depending on subject and grade level) [+M]
  • Teacher impacts on Black students' test scores vary widely, from 0.2 standard deviations in middle school English to 0.5 standard deviations in elementary school math between high- and low-effectiveness teachers [+M]
  • Race-specific value-added scores predict teachers' subsequent effects: Black students taught by a teacher with high prior effectiveness for Black students show gains of about one standard deviation when the teacher moves schools or grades [+M]
  • Having a teacher highly effective for Black students shows no detrimental effect on non-Black students' test scores [+M]
  • Experienced teachers and teachers with a master's degree are highly effective overall but much less effective for Black than non-Black students [+M]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Incorporate teachers' variation in effectiveness by student race into district and school policy decisions, including matching teachers to students and targeting professional development
