Reading Apprenticeship® Academic Literacy curriculum for 9th-grade students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A year-long curriculum for 9th-grade students designed to support reading achievement, engagement, and fluency. The course is organized around three thematic units: (1) Reading Self and Society, (2) Reading History, and (3) Reading Science. It focuses students' attention on how they read (a metacognitive process) to help them better understand what they read; students read high-interest, challenging texts, analyze the way words and sentences are constructed, and use writing as a tool for learning. In the evaluated study it replaced a 9th-grade elective class, with students attending about 11 hours per month on average.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers receive professional development including a 5-day summer training institute, booster sessions, and coaching visits

Constraints¶

  • The curriculum is guided by the concept of flexible fidelity, meaning teachers have flexibility in how they implement it in day-to-day activities

Target Learners¶

  • 9th-grade students reading at least two years below grade level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • motivation, strategic and critical reading skills, and ability to construct meaning from academic texts

Affordances¶

  • Reading Apprenticeship Four Dimensions Framework

Claims¶

  • Reading Apprenticeship® showed a statistically significant positive effect on the GRADE reading comprehension subtest for Cohort 2 ninth-grade students [+W]
  • Reading Apprenticeship® showed no statistically significant effects on most GRADE outcomes: Cohort 1 comprehension and vocabulary, and Cohort 2 vocabulary [-W]
  • The WWC rates Reading Apprenticeship® as having potentially positive effects on comprehension for adolescent learners, based on one study [+W]
  • Reading Apprenticeship® has potentially positive effects on grade point average in core subjects, based on one qualifying study [+W]
