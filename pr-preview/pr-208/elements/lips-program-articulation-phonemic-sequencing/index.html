Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing (LiPS) program: articulation-based phonemic sequencing instruction for beginning readers¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

LiPS (formerly Auditory Discrimination in Depth) is a published program designed to improve reading and spelling by teaching students to decode and encode words and identify individual sounds and blends. "Initial activities engage students in discovering the lip, tongue, and mouth actions needed to produce specific sounds," after which students identify and order sounds within words during sequencing, reading, and spelling activities. It includes direct instruction in letter patterns, sight words, and context clues, is individualized, and is designed for emergent readers in grades K–3 and students of all ages with learning disabilities or reading difficulties.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers can learn to administer the program from the LiPS Teacher's Manual or via Lindamood Bell workshops; a classroom kit of materials costs $444.95 and a 3-day professional development workshop costs $879.

Constraints¶

  • The developer recommends instruction last 4–6 months for 1 hour a day, or 4–6 weeks for 4 hours a day.

Target Learners¶

  • Emerent readers in grades K–3
  • Students of all ages with learning disabilities or reading difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Decoding and encoding words
  • Identifying individual sounds and blends in words
  • Reading comprehension and spelling

Claims¶

  • In Gunn (1996), LiPS effects on word accuracy/fluency and phonemic decoding were negative and substantively important but not statistically significant [-W]
  • LiPS has mixed effects on alphabetics for beginning readers: positive and statistically significant in one trial, negative and substantively important (but not significant) in another [~W]
  • LiPS has potentially positive effects on comprehension for beginning readers, based on one randomized trial showing a statistically significant positive effect on passage comprehension [+W]
  • One-year follow-up and LiPS-vs-RWT supplemental comparisons showed mostly non-significant differences, with some follow-up alphabetics effects remaining significant [~W]
  • In Torgesen et al. (2010), LiPS produced statistically significant positive effects on word accuracy/fluency, phonemic decoding, phonological awareness, and spelling versus a basal reading program [+M]
  • Read, Write & Type!™ shows no statistically significant alphabetics advantages over the Auditory Discrimination in Depth® comparison [~W]
