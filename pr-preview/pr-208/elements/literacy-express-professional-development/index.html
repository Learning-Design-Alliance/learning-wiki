Literacy Express professional development model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Literacy Express implementation model pairs the curriculum with structured teacher professional development. In the standard package, "Teachers participate in a two-day professional development workshop at the beginning of the school year and in either two additional half-day workshops or one additional full-day workshop." In the Lonigan et al. (2005) trial, one intervention arm added regular classroom visits by trained project teacher-mentors on top of the workshops, and workshops included both teacher-directed and hands-on components. Professional development fees vary by the size of the group and the number of trainers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers and aides attend curriculum-specific workshops with teacher-directed and hands-on components at the start of and during the school year.

Constraints¶

  • The WWC report does not disentangle the effects of the workshop-only and workshop-plus-mentoring variants; both intervention groups were combined in the effectiveness rating.

Target Learners¶

  • preschool teachers and aides implementing Literacy Express

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fidelity of implementation of the Literacy Express curriculum
  • delivery of dialogic reading, phonological awareness, and print knowledge activities

Claims¶

  • Literacy Express shows no discernible effects on cognition and math for preschool children [-W]
  • Literacy Express has positive effects on oral language for preschool children, with significant effects in two of three studies [+W]
  • Literacy Express has positive effects on phonological processing for preschool children, with significant effects in two of three studies [+W]
  • Literacy Express has positive effects on print knowledge for preschool children, with significant effects in two of three studies [+W]
