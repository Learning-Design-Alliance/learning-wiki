Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching as a classroom-observation evaluation tool¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Charlotte Danielson Framework for Teaching divides teaching into four domains—Planning and Preparation, Classroom Environment, Instruction, and Professional Responsibilities—with principals rating observable components in the Classroom Environment and Instruction domains at four performance levels: unsatisfactory, basic, proficient, and distinguished. Chicago adopted it to replace a 30-year-old checklist, aiming to establish "a common definition of good teaching along a developmental continuum" and structure principal-teacher conversations about instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Trained raters; the study focused on the two observable domains, Classroom Environment and Instruction

Constraints¶

  • Year-one findings concern reliability only; validity—whether the Framework measures what leads to student learning—was deferred to the year-two study

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers in evaluation pilot schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • defining and measuring effective instructional practice

Claims¶

  • Principals rated some instruction components inconsistently, rating communicating with students, using assessment in instruction, and organizing physical space lower than observers, and student engagement higher [~W]
  • Principals and trained external observers gave aggregate-consistent Danielson Framework ratings in matched observations [+W]
  • The Danielson Framework identified far more teachers as low-performing than the prior CPS checklist (8 percent vs 0.3 percent) [+W]
  • Principals inflated ratings at the high end of the scale, more often identifying instruction as distinguished than observers [-W]
  • Fifty-seven percent of principals were highly enthusiastic about the Framework-based evaluation process while 43 percent held mixed to negative attitudes [+W]
  • Danielson Framework observation ratings are valid measures of teaching practice, with higher ratings corresponding to higher student test-score growth in reading and math [+W]
  • Differences between principal and observer ratings at the high end of the scale were explained largely by teachers' previous evaluation ratings [~W]
