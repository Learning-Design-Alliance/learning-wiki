Chicago's double-dose algebra policy (two periods of algebra with teacher supports)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (3 causal), q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An instructional support strategy in which "First-time ninth-grade students who tested below the national median on the eighth-grade math test enrolled in two periods of algebra coursework—a regular algebra class and an additional algebra support class, for a full academic year." The district provided professional development workshops three times a year and new curricular materials, plus three implementation guidelines: the same teacher for both courses, sequential scheduling, and the same classmates in both periods.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Same teacher in algebra and algebra support; courses offered sequentially; support class taken with the same students as the regular algebra class; teacher professional development and new curricular materials

Constraints¶

  • Did not specifically address accommodations for students with learning disabilities

Target Learners¶

  • Ninth-graders scoring below the national median on the eighth-grade math test

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Algebra skills, course passing, and preparation for advanced mathematics

Claims¶

  • Chicago's double-dose algebra policy raised algebra test scores for both low-skill and high-skill students [+M]
  • Double-dose algebra improved test scores but did not reduce algebra failure rates [+M]
  • Double-dose algebra was least effective for students with the weakest incoming skills, most of whom received special education services [~M]
  • Failure rates increased and grades declined for higher-skill students in single-period algebra after the policy [-M]
  • Evidence on the policy's effects on 10th-grade geometry outcomes was inconsistent across groups and years [~M]
  • Double-dose algebra improved algebra test scores for targeted students just below the cutoff, with a larger post-policy gain than for students just above the cutoff [+W]
  • The double-dose policy led schools to track algebra classes by entering math skills, making classrooms more homogeneous in ability [~M]
  • Double-dose algebra policy increased academic demand for both high- and low-skill students, with below-norm students' gains not attributable to peer ability changes [+M]
