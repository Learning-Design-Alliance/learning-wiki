National Network of Partnership Schools (NNPS) program model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

NNPS, established at Johns Hopkins University in 1996 by Dr. Joyce Epstein, guides schools, districts, and states to implement research-based partnership programs. Members "receive coaching, training, and planning materials to help them implement the program," and since its founding NNPS has partnered with over 5,000 schools and districts to improve family engagement. The model combines a framework of six types of involvement with an action team approach, and helps each team assess the quality and progress of its partnership work at the end of each school year.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Each participating school forms an Action Team for Partnerships (ATP), a standing committee or work group, composed of the principal, parents, teachers, others such as counselors, community members and alumni, and students in high schools.

Constraints¶

  • Evidence rating is Promising, based on one study of elementary schools; no technology is required (Technology: None).

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary school students and their families (study population); high school students where ATPs include students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved student attendance through structured family and community engagement
  • A welcoming school climate and student success goals tied to the School Improvement Plan

Claims¶

  • NNPS implementation increases elementary school average daily attendance more than matched comparison schools (effect size +0.07) [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • NNPS professional development: institutes, workshops, and quarterly series

Examples¶

  • Monthly Action Team meetings with shared leadership and activity-quality evaluation
