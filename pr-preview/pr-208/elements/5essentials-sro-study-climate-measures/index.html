5Essentials-based school climate measures (Student Physical Safety, Teacher Physical Safety, Student-Teacher Trust)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

This study used three annual survey measures as school climate outcomes. The Student Physical Safety measure is the School Safety measure from the 5Essentials Survey, containing five items with the stem "How safe do you feel…?" rated from Not safe to Very safe. The Teacher Physical Safety measure is a supplementary CPS annual survey measure with seven items on problems such as physical conflicts and disorder, reverse coded for interpretability. The Student-Teacher Trust measure contains five agreement items about feeling safe and comfortable with teachers. Scores were estimated with Rasch family models on the logit scale, and different measure scales are not comparable.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must have both a Student Physical Safety and Student-Teacher Trust score to be included in the survey outcome sample; scores require enough valid item responses to be estimable.

Constraints¶

  • The Teacher Physical Safety measure is not directly aligned to the 5Essentials framework, and the measures capture only portions of the WSS Framework pillars.

Target Learners¶

  • CPS high school students in grades 9-12
  • CPS high school teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring perceived physical safety and student-teacher relational trust as school climate outcomes

Affordances¶

  • Whole School Safety Framework Cps

Claims¶

  • SRO removal was not significantly related to changes in student or teacher perceptions of physical safety or in Student-Teacher Trust [~W]

Related Elements¶

  • 5Essentials Survey measures of school climate and organization
  • 5Essentials Survey Supportive Environment measures
  • 5Essentials Survey system measuring school organizational conditions
  • 5Essentials school survey as a measure of school relational trust and climate
