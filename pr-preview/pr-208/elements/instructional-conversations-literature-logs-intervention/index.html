Instructional Conversations and Literature Logs intervention for English language learners¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An intervention developed by William Saunders and Claude Goldenberg combining two components for English language learners. Instructional Conversations are "small-group discussions" in which teachers, "acting as facilitators", engage learners in discussion of stories, key concepts, and personal experiences. Literature Logs require learners "to write in a log in response to writing prompts or questions related to sections of stories", with responses shared in small groups or with a partner. The goal is reading comprehension ability along with English language proficiency.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher facilitation of small-group discussion
  • Writing prompts tied to story segments and sharing of log responses

Constraints¶

  • No information is available on cost or on the number or demographics of students, schools, or districts using the intervention

Target Learners¶

  • English language learners in grades 2–5

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension
  • English language proficiency

Claims¶

  • The intervention showed substantively important but WWC-unconfirmed effects on English language development, with non-significant CTBS Language and Writing outcomes [+W]
  • In a five-year quasi-experimental study, the intervention program showed a statistically significant positive effect on fifth-grade reading performance assessment but not on the CTBS Reading subtest [+W]
  • The WWC rates Instructional Conversations and Literature Logs as having potentially positive effects on reading achievement and English language development [+W]
  • Instructional Conversations and Literature Logs produce statistically significant positive effects on factual and interpretive story comprehension in a short-term randomized trial with English language learners [+W]
  • When implemented alone, neither Literature Logs nor Instructional Conversations produced statistically significant comprehension effects, in contrast to the combined intervention [+W]
  • The WWC improvement index is +31 percentile points for English language development and +3 percentile points for reading achievement [+W]
