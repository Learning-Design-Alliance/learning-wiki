EL Education's Teacher Potential Project (TPP): standards-aligned ELA curriculum with embedded professional development¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Teacher Potential Project is a program of EL Education that combines an English language arts standards-aligned curriculum with embedded professional development for teachers. The evaluation describes it as "a program which includes an English language arts standards-aligned curriculum and embedded professional development for teachers". Mathematica evaluated its impacts on teacher instructional practices and student ELA achievement from 2013 to 2019.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers participate in the program's embedded professional development alongside implementation of the standards-aligned ELA curriculum

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in English language arts classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English language arts achievement aligned to the Common Core State Standards

Claims¶

  • One year of Teacher Potential Project participation improved teachers' overall English language arts instructional practices relative to district curriculum and professional development [+M]
  • The Teacher Potential Project produced positive impacts on specific Common Core-aligned instructional practices, including engaging students with texts, supporting text evidence use, and supporting higher order thinking [+M]
  • Two years of teacher participation in the Teacher Potential Project improved students' English language arts achievement by roughly 1.4 months of typical improvement [+M]
  • A significantly greater proportion of TPP teachers demonstrated and sustained CCSS-aligned instructional practices compared with district-supported teachers [+M]
  • Significantly more TPP teachers encouraged students' higher-order thinking skills than district-supported comparison teachers [+M]
  • Significantly more TPP teachers asked students to cite evidence from texts in discussions and writing [+M]
  • Students taught by TPP teachers engaged significantly more often in reading, writing, and speaking about texts in the second year of TPP [+M]
