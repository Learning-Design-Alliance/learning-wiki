GoCPS centralized online high school application platform¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 10 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 3 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

Description¶

GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online platform that moved all high school program applications into a single system with one deadline and a single best offer per applicant. Students use it to "research school options, complete applications, learn about their application and offer status, and ultimately accept or decline admissions offers." It hosted nearly 290 programs including 11 SEHS programs, allowed ranking up to 20 choice and 6 SEHS programs, and centrally managed waitlists — an innovation because most other large urban districts did not include charter schools in their universal enrollment systems.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Participation of all charter and district-run high schools and programs (special education cluster programs and alternative schools were assigned outside the system)

Constraints¶

  • Students can rank at most 20 choice programs and 6 SEHS programs, which the authors note could matter for SEHS applicants since about 15 percent listed the maximum six

Target Learners¶

  • incoming ninth-grade students in Chicago Public Schools
  • families applying to high school programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • access to preferred high school programs
  • equitable navigation of the high school application process

Affordances¶

  • Deferred Acceptance Single Best Offer Mechanism

Claims¶

  • Ninth-grade enrollment overall and by school type and accountability rating stayed relatively stable after GoCPS implementation [+W]
  • Almost all CPS eighth-graders (92 percent) used the GoCPS platform to apply to high school in its first year [+W]
  • Most GoCPS applicants received an offer from a preferred program: 51 percent from their top-ranked and 81 percent from a top-three ranked choice program [+W]
  • Many applicants failed to complete post-application admission requirements, with Black and Latino students least likely to complete them [-W]
  • Program demand varied widely: 21 percent of programs had more than ten times as many applications as seats, led by arts, CTE, and SEHS programs [+W]
  • Black students and students from the lowest-SES neighborhoods ranked more programs but were less likely to rank a high-accountability school first [~W]
  • Students found the GoCPS application platform logistically easy to use, but applications to schools with additional requirements caused difficulties [~W]
  • Students expected acceptance or rejection notifications from all schools they applied to, and the single-best-offer notification design produced confusion and frustration [-W]
  • Confusion about the GoCPS true-preference ranking rationale was widespread among students, parents, and staff, and misinformation led some students into second-round applications [-W]
  • Students overwhelmingly relied on GoCPS, CPS, and school websites to learn about potential high schools, but found websites out of date and lacking student-voice information [~W]
