Urban school district value-added specification comparison (Working Paper 15)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A working paper analyzing urban school district data to investigate whether value-added model specifications—track indicators or classroom-characteristic controls—eliminate potential bias from student sorting into academic tracks. It was prepared for District of Columbia Public Schools as part of a "Value-Added Assessment System for DC Schools and Teachers" project (2009-2015). The paper compares specifications by subject and grade level and examines effects on estimate precision and distribution tails.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires urban school district data linking students to teachers, tracks, and classrooms

Constraints¶

  • Findings are specific to the studied urban district context; the two bias-reducing specifications cannot be combined without reducing precision

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in tracked middle and high school classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate estimation of teacher effectiveness in math and reading

Claims¶

  • Accounting for mean and standard deviation of classroom achievement may reduce bias for middle school math teachers [+W]
  • Including track indicators and classroom achievement controls simultaneously reduces precision of value-added estimates [+W]
  • Alternative value-added specifications produce substantially different teacher estimates but small changes in distribution tails [+W]
  • Including track indicators in value-added models may reduce tracking-related bias for high school reading teachers [+W]
