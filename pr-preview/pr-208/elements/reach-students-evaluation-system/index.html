REACH Students teacher evaluation system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 11 claims (6 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

REACH (Recognizing Educators Advancing Chicago's Students) is CPS's teacher evaluation system launched in 2012-13, replacing a 1970s-era checklist. It combines "multiple classroom observations" using a modified Danielson Framework rubric with student growth measures from Performance Tasks and value-added or expected gains scores. All administrators must be certified as evaluators through training modules and assessments. In its first year it applied with stakes to non-tenured teachers, generating over 36,000 observations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Certified administrator evaluators trained via modules and two assessments
  • Pre- and post-observation conferences for each formal observation
  • District data systems linking students and teachers for growth calculations

Constraints¶

  • In 2012-13 only non-tenured teachers received a summative evaluation score
  • Student growth capped at 25 percent of the evaluation score in the initial year

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Teacher instructional improvement
  • Differentiation of strong and weak teacher practice for personnel decisions

Claims¶

  • Administrators' dual role as evaluator and coach can undermine the observation process's professional learning benefits [~W]
  • REACH observations consumed substantial administrator time—about six hours per observation cycle and roughly two to three full weeks per year [~W]
  • Most teachers and administrators report that the REACH observation process supports teacher professional growth and improved instructional conversations [+W]
  • A majority of teachers believed REACH relied too heavily on standardized tests, with special education teachers especially critical [-W]
  • Most teachers judged their REACH evaluators fair and able to assess instruction accurately [+W]
  • Under REACH fewer teachers received ratings in the top two categories than under the prior checklist system [+W]
  • Missed observations left 24 percent of non-tenured teachers without a REACH rating, concentrated in a smaller number of schools undergoing organizational change [~W]
  • The Danielson Framework identified far more teachers as low-performing than the prior CPS checklist (8 percent vs 0.3 percent) [+W]
  • REACH evaluation ratings rose over time, with nearly 9 in 10 teachers receiving one of the top two ratings by 2016-17 [+W]
  • Fewer non-tenured teachers were rated in the top two categories under REACH (58%) than under the previous checklist system (87%) [+W]
  • Only about 25 percent of teachers received an individual value-added score, with most teachers instead receiving a school-wide literacy value-added score [~W]
