Teach For America (TFA) as a teacher-supply program for hard-to-staff subjects in high-poverty schools¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Teach For America is a program that supplies teachers for hard-to-staff subjects in high-poverty U.S. schools. The article describes it as "an important but controversial source of teachers for hard-to-staff subjects in high-poverty U.S. schools" and evaluates its secondary math teachers experimentally. The program requires only a two-year commitment from its teachers, a feature the authors explicitly address in their analysis.

Claims¶

  • This is the first large-scale experimental study of secondary math teachers from Teach For America [+W]
  • TFA teachers in their first two years of teaching are more effective than more experienced non-TFA teachers in the same schools [+W]
  • TFA secondary math teachers increase student math achievement by 0.07 standard deviations over one school year relative to other math teachers in the same schools [+W]
  • Secondary math teachers from Teach For America and the Teaching Fellows programs are as effective as teachers receiving traditional certification [+W]
  • In some cases, Teach For America and Teaching Fellows secondary math teachers are more effective than traditionally certified math teachers [+W]
  • TFA teachers show potentially positive effects on science achievement, based on one study [+W]
  • An elementary-school RCT found no statistically significant difference between TFA and non-TFA teachers in mathematics or reading achievement [~W]
  • A randomized controlled trial found TFA secondary math teachers outperformed non-TFA comparison teachers in mathematics achievement [+W]
