Two gameful assessment prototypes evaluated with Grades 3–7 students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article evaluates two “gameful assessment” prototypes — assessments incorporating features of games — with 391 Grade 3–7 students and 14 teachers in one Midwestern and one Northwestern school. The study used mixed methods to examine "student engagement and teacher perceptions". The prototypes are the instructional/assessment components whose engagement effects the study measures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assessments must incorporate features of games as designed in the two prototypes

Target Learning Goals¶

Claims¶

  • Students taking gameful assessments report higher satisfaction than students taking traditional assessments in a Grades 3–7 mixed-methods study [+W]
  • Teachers hold conflicting attitudes about whether gameful assessments support students’ intrinsic motivation and desire to learn [+W]
  • Traditional assessments are commonly perceived to suffer from student motivation deficits that gameful assessments propose to address [+W]
