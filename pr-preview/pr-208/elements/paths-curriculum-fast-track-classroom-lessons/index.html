PATHS curriculum: classroom-based social-emotional lessons delivered two to three times weekly within Fast Track¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q4 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Promoting Alternative THinking Strategies (PATHS) curriculum is the classroom-based component of Fast Track, delivered by teachers. The report states it "is intended to develop emotional communication, social understanding, self-control, and problem solving" and that "Lessons are delivered, on average, two to three times a week." An adapted version covered four skill domains: emotional understanding and communication, friendship building, self-control, and social problem solving. It was not delivered in the Durham, North Carolina schools because administrators would not allow it.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers deliver the lessons; teachers at intervention schools attended a 2.5-day training workshop and received weekly in-class support and coaching from Fast Track staff

Constraints¶

  • The classroom curricula were not delivered in the Durham, North Carolina schools because administrators would not allow it

Target Learners¶

  • First- through fifth-grade students identified as at high risk for long-term antisocial behavior

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Emotional communication
  • social understanding
  • self-control
  • social problem solving

Claims¶

  • Fast Track showed positive, statistically significant effects on some external behavior measures after one year, with no significant differences on eight other measures [+W]
  • Fast Track produced a positive, statistically significant effect on emotional/internal behavior for children at risk of emotional disturbance after one year [+W]
  • Fast Track produced a positive, statistically significant effect on reading achievement/literacy after one year, significant on the Spache DRS but not on the Woodcock-Johnson Letter-Word Identification subtest [+W]
  • Fast Track showed positive, statistically significant effects on three social outcome measures after one year, with no significant differences on three others [+W]
