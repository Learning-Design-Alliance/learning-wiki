5Essentials Survey supplemental measures of racial equity climate and school civic engagement¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study measured two school-level constructs using supplemental measures on the 5Essentials Survey. Racial equity climate measured "the extent to which each student perceived their school as fair and equitable" with items such as "race is a factor in decisions about discipline at this school" (1-4 scale, lower scores more positive). Civic engagement measured students' experiences of civic learning in courses, including current-events discussion and community action projects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student survey responses on supplemental 5Essentials items; school-level aggregation

Constraints¶

  • Racial equity climate scoring is reverse-coded: lower scores indicate more positive climate

Target Learners¶

  • High school students in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring school racial equity climate and student civic engagement as school-context predictors of graduation

Claims¶

  • High school civic engagement is associated with higher graduation rates for all students regardless of age at entry, race, or gender [+W]
  • Overage students at high schools with great racial equity climate are twice as likely to graduate in four years as those at schools with poor racial equity climate [+W]
