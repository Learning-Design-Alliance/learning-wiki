REACH (Recognizing Educators Advancing Chicago's Students) teacher evaluation system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 10 claims (2 for, 4 mixed, 4 against) · 4 studies (4 associational), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

Description¶

REACH is Chicago Public Schools' teacher evaluation system, which "replaced the previous checklist system, which rated nearly all teachers as excellent or superior and failed to provide much useful feedback for improving teacher practice." It combines classroom observation with student growth measures, with growth accounting for up to 25 percent of a teacher's rating in 2012-13 and 2013-14. The brief measures teacher and administrator perceptions of the system across its first two years.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires classroom observation processes and assessments used to measure student growth as components of teacher ratings

Claims¶

  • Beginning teachers are more positive about REACH than teachers with more years of experience [+W]
  • Teachers and principals report REACH is changing teaching practice and improving communication and collaboration [+W]
  • Majorities of teachers and evaluators question the effort required by REACH, saying it takes more effort than the results are worth [-W]
  • Teachers and principals report increased teacher stress and anxiety under REACH [-W]
  • Most teachers believe their REACH evaluation relies too heavily on student growth and question the fairness of the growth assessments [-W]
  • Teachers and principals remain optimistic about REACH in year two, but teacher satisfaction with the evaluation process declined from year one [~W]
  • Under REACH fewer teachers received ratings in the top two categories than under the prior checklist system [~W]
  • Fewer non-tenured teachers were rated in the top two categories under REACH (58%) than under the previous checklist system (87%) [~W]
  • REACH evaluation ratings rose over time, with nearly 9 in 10 teachers receiving one of the top two ratings by 2016-17 [~W]
  • Missed observations left 24 percent of non-tenured teachers without a REACH rating, concentrated in a smaller number of schools undergoing organizational change [-W]
