Safe Passage program expansion with adult monitors along routes to designated welcoming schools¶

Element · All elements Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The Safe Passage program, already active in 35 high schools and four elementary school communities, was expanded to include new routes near designated welcoming schools after the 2013 closings. CPS spent an additional $7.7 million to hire 600 more Safe Passage workers to "stand along and patrol predetermined routes in areas where schools were closed," with the goals of reducing students' likelihood of becoming victims of violent incidents and creating a safe, supportive school environment. CPS mailed Safe Passage maps to every displaced student and posted routes online.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Community-based organizations and vendors were hired to oversee the program and staff the routes

Constraints¶

  • Transportation assistance was offered only to current displaced students through elementary graduation, not to kindergartners or newly moved students

Target Learners¶

  • Displaced elementary school students from the 47 closed Chicago schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Safe travel to and from school so students can attend and engage in their new welcoming schools
