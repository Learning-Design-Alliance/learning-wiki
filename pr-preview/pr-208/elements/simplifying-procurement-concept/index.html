Simplifying Procurement concept: user-centered tools and case studies for navigating district purchasing¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

One of five concepts developed at the workshop, Simplifying Procurement is a set of user-centered tools meant to demystify district procurement. It proposes "district-specific maps of the procurement process and a collection of case studies and stories that describe how a product or service successfully entered the school district," spurring dialogue among vendors, administrators, and teachers. Its inspiration is customer-service experiences at companies like Nordstrom and Southwest, imagining the procurement office as a support service for teachers and vendors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • District-specific process maps and collected case studies must be created for each district

Constraints¶

  • Proposed as a concept with a possible next step, not yet tested in districts

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 district procurement staff, administrators, and teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Helping stakeholders navigate and understand the ed-tech procurement process

Affordances¶

  • Six Barriers Edtech Procurement Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Cohort purchasing through consolidated edtech procurement produced savings of nearly $200,000 and roughly 50 percent per-license discounts in Denver Public Schools [+M]
  • Students, teachers, and principals are perceived to have limited involvement in ed-tech procurement decisions, and providers are far less satisfied with end-user involvement than district stakeholders [-W]
  • Technology directors are the least likely to trust evidence from providers, with only 29 percent satisfied with the credibility of provider evidence [~W]
  • Ed-tech providers are overwhelmingly dissatisfied with procurement: only 4 percent say today's processes meet contemporary needs, and nearly two-thirds report the processes directly influence their product development [-W]
