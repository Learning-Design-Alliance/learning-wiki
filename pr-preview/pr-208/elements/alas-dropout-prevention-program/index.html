ALAS dropout prevention program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

ALAS (Spanish for "wings") is an intervention for middle and high school students addressing student, school, family, and community factors that affect dropping out. Each student is assigned a counselor/mentor who monitors attendance, behavior, and academic achievement, provides feedback, and coordinates interventions. It comprises six related strategies, including period-by-period attendance monitoring, a 10-week problem-solving and self-control curriculum (the ALAS Resilience Builder ©), parent training, recognition and bonding activities, and connection to community services.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A counselor/mentor team (supervisors, counselors, volunteers, clerical staff) housed full-time on the school campus
  • Staff and teachers trained to deliver the social problem-solving skills curriculum

Constraints¶

  • Originally designed for high-risk students identified by low academic performance and behavior problems; students who spoke no English were excluded because the intervention was not designed to accommodate them

Target Learners¶

  • high-risk middle and high school students at risk of dropping out

Target Learning Goals¶

  • problem-solving, self-control, and assertiveness skills
  • staying in school and progressing toward on-time graduation

Claims¶

  • ALAS effects on progressing in school and completing school fade or are non-significant after the intervention [-W]
  • ALAS increases the likelihood that high-risk students stay on track to graduate on time at the end of the intervention [+W]
  • ALAS effects on staying in school are not statistically significant two years after the intervention ends [-W]
  • ALAS increases the likelihood that high-risk students remain enrolled in school at the end of the intervention [+W]
  • The WWC rates ALAS as having potentially positive effects on staying in school and progressing in school [+W]
