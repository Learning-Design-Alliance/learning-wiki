Evidence¶

Seeskin 2018¶

Seeskin, A., Nagaoka, J., & Mahaffie, S. (2018). Hidden risk: Changes in GPA across the transition to high school. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/hidden-risk-changes-gpa-across-transition-high-school

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of the 2016-17 CPS freshman cohort across subject areas (Figure 8). The report states "students saw their grade decline by 0.81 points in PE/health between the eighth and ninth grades, and by 0.61 points in arts," attributing the gap partly to slower growth in non-core grades since 2013 (arts +0.08, PE/health +0.18 vs core +0.31).

"students saw their grade decline by 0.81 points in PE/health between the eighth and ninth grades, and by 0.61 points in arts—declines much larger than the average core GPA decline of 0.33 points"

Seeskin 2018 (2)¶

Seeskin, A., Nagaoka, J., & Mahaffie, S. (2018). Hidden risk: Changes in GPA across the transition to high school. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/hidden-risk-changes-gpa-across-transition-high-school

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive subgroup analyses (Figures 11 and 12). "Ninth-graders who received a core GPA lower than 2.0 in the eighth grade saw their average PE/health grade decline by 1.39 points"; the arts grades of Black young men fell by more than three-quarters of a point and their PE/health grades fell a full point.

"Ninth-graders who received a core GPA lower than 2.0 in the eighth grade saw their average PE/health grade decline by 1.39 points, from a B in eighth grade to a C- in ninth grade"
