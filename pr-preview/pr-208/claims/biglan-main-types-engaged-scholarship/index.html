Evidence¶

Doberneck 2017¶

Doberneck, D. M., & Schweitzer, J. H. (2017). Disciplinary Variations in Publicly Engaged Scholarship: An Analysis Using the Biglan Classification of Academic Disciplines. Journal of Higher Education Outreach and Engagement, 21(1). https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/1534-6104

q2 · i1 · associational · r1

Chi-square analysis of 171 RPT forms (Results section). Table 3: engaged research 62.8% pure vs 82.4% applied (chi-square 8.208, p=.003); teaching 82.6% vs 95.3% (chi-square 7.021, p=.007); service 60.5% vs 83.5% (chi-square 11.263, p=.001).

"Faculty members in applied and life disciplines were more likely than their pure and nonlife colleagues to report publicly engaged research and creative activities. Faculty members in applied disciplines were more likely than their pure colleagues to report publicly engaged teaching and learning."

Doberneck 2017 (2)¶

Doberneck, D. M., & Schweitzer, J. H. (2017). Disciplinary Variations in Publicly Engaged Scholarship: An Analysis Using the Biglan Classification of Academic Disciplines. Journal of Higher Education Outreach and Engagement, 21(1). https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/1534-6104

q2 · i2 · associational · r1

Chi-square analysis in Results; Table 3 shows commercialized activities at 22.1% hard vs 3.9% soft, chi-square 11.538, significance .000. The discussion warns RPT policies that do not encourage reporting commercialized activities may disadvantage hard-discipline faculty.

"Finally, faculty members in hard disciplines were more likely than their soft-discipline colleagues to report publicly engaged commercialized activities."

Doberneck 2017 (3)¶

Doberneck, D. M., & Schweitzer, J. H. (2017). Disciplinary Variations in Publicly Engaged Scholarship: An Analysis Using the Biglan Classification of Academic Disciplines. Journal of Higher Education Outreach and Engagement, 21(1). https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/1534-6104

q2 · i1 · associational · r1

Chi-square analysis in Results; Table 3 shows engaged service and practice at 81.6% life vs 57.4% nonlife (chi-square 11.881, p=.001) and engaged research at 81.6% vs 58.8% (chi-square 10.617, p=.001).

"Faculty members in applied and life disciplines were more likely than their pure and nonlife colleagues to report publicly engaged service and practice."

  • Faculty in applied, hard, and life disciplines are more likely than colleagues in pure, soft, and nonlife disciplines to report publicly engaged scholarship overall — related
  • Intensity of publicly engaged activity is higher in applied (2.29 vs 1.76) and life (2.28 vs 1.63) disciplines, with no significant hard/soft difference — related
  • Subtypes of publicly engaged scholarship vary by Biglan dimension, with applied faculty reporting five subtypes more often and soft faculty reporting two — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • No instances of service-learning were reported on the RPT forms, an unexpected null the authors attribute partly to the form's design — related
