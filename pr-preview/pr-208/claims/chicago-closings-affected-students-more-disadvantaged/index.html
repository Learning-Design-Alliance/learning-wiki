Descriptive comparison in Table 1 of 10,708 students in closed elementary schools against 33,564 students in the 129 schools initially considered for closure and 235,067 K-7 CPS students. Eighty-eight percent of affected students were African American, and neighborhood crime rates were nearly double the CPS average.

"Compared to the average CPS K-7 student, students affected by school closures were more likely to receive free or reduced-price lunch, to receive special education services, and to be old for their grade; they were less likely to have met the Illinois state standards on the ISAT math test."
