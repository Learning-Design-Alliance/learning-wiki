Teaching Games for Understanding improves tactical decision-making more than traditional physical education instruction.¶

Claim · All claims Evidence · 1 study · q3 network meta-analysis of quasi-experiments · i3 large · n=65 studies (8,027 participants)

Teaching Games for Understanding (TGfU) sets students a tactical problem in a modified game before any technical skill work, where traditional physical education drills the technique first and plays the game afterwards. The claim is about the comparison with that traditional sequence, in school physical education, on decision-making in games.

Evidence¶

Guo et al. 2026¶

Guo, J., Yu, Q., & Malik, Z. bin A. (2026). A network meta-analysis of pedagogical models in physical education: Evaluating multidimensional learning outcomes and instructional duration effects. Frontiers in Psychology, 17, 1766890. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1766890

q3 · network meta-analysis of quasi-experimental studies · i3 · SMD 0.83 on decision-making · n=65 studies (8,027 participants) · quant-synthesis · r3

A Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis of 65 pre/post studies from 16 countries, K-12 through higher education, with middle-school students the largest group. It compared five pedagogical models (Sport Education, TGfU, Cooperative Learning, Teaching Personal and Social Responsibility, and hybrids of these) with traditional direct instruction of physical education. None of the included studies was randomised, and study quality was rated with MINORS: 35 high, 26 medium, 4 low. The structured record of the study is observations/guo-2026.yaml.

Discussion¶

Why TGfU and not the model that ranked highest. Hybrid models ranked first for tactical decision-making in the network's probability ordering (SUCRA 80.1%), and TGfU ranked first for student motivation (SUCRA 82.1%). A SUCRA is a rank, not a size: it says where a model sits and nothing about how much better it is. On decision-making the hybrid estimate against traditional instruction was larger (SMD 1.41) but its credible interval ran from −0.07 to 2.92, and Sport Education, Cooperative Learning and TPSR all had intervals spanning zero. TGfU's is the one decision-making estimate the evidence actually separates from no effect, and it rests on direct comparisons rather than on inference through the network.

Strength is limited by the primary studies. Every included study is quasi-experimental, so the pooled estimate inherits whatever selection and teacher effects those designs carry. The publication bias found for skill execution is a warning that the same may hold for other outcomes, where it was not detected. The claim is rated moderate for that reason rather than on the size of the effect.

Duration matters for design. The 720-minute figure is a moderator across all models, not a TGfU result. For a designer it argues for committing a whole unit to a game-centred model rather than inserting one or two lessons into a technique-led programme.

Scope. School and university physical education, in person. Nothing here tests transfer of tactical understanding to other domains.
