Evidence¶

Kakimoto Akihiro 2026¶

Kakimoto Akihiro, Fujise Daisuke, Watanabe Shota, Otaka Yutaka, Minemoto Sakura, Yasumatsu Ayaka, Hasegawa Shin. (2026). Development and Educational Evaluation of a Radiation Visualization System in a Virtual Reality Training Module for Radiopharmaceutical Administration: Mixed Methods Study of Students’ Learning Experiences. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/104397

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Within-participants quasi-experimental comparison of 20 radiological technology students completing both conventional cold-run and VR-based training; Wilcoxon signed-rank test, effect size r=0.878, P<.001. The authors note this "should therefore be interpreted as confirmation of the intended system functionality rather than independent evidence of educational superiority."

"For the (A) radiation understanding and visualization domain, the mean score for conventional training was 1.85 (SD 0.45), whereas VR-based training yielded a significantly higher mean score of 4.70 (SD 0.25)."
