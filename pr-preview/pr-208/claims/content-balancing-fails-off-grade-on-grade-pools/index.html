Evidence¶

Li 2019¶

Li, S., & Meyer, J. P. (2019). Simulation study for evaluating MAP® Growth™ grade-level item pools. Portland, OR: NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/simulation-study-for-evaluating-map-growth-item-pools-with-grade-level-constraints/

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Content balancing analysis (Appendix E) against flag criteria for balanced, slightly unbalanced, and extremely unbalanced instructional-area item counts. Unbalanced reading conditions included the Grade 2 pool for lower Grade 1 or higher Grade 3 ability and the Grade 8 pool for lower Grade 7 ability.

"For reading, on average (assuming students with off-grade ability take the grade-level reading tests), using the on-grade item pool caused content balancing to drop from 99.8% to 82.3%."

Li 2019 (2)¶

Li, S., & Meyer, J. P. (2019). Simulation study for evaluating MAP® Growth™ grade-level item pools. Portland, OR: NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/simulation-study-for-evaluating-map-growth-item-pools-with-grade-level-constraints/

q2 · i? · design · r2

Language usage content balancing results across all simulation conditions. By contrast, mathematics showed unbalanced results for Grades 5, 6, and 8 (e.g., the Grade 5 pool shallow in Operations and Algebraic Thinking and Geometry), and reading Grades 6–8 were weak in the RIT range 180–200 except for Vocabulary.

"Language usage tests achieved the perfect results for simulations with both on- and off-grade simulees, and all three instructional areas were balanced in all test events."
