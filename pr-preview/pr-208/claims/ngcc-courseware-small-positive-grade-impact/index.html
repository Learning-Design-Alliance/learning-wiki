Barbara Means and Julie Neisler 2004¶

Barbara Means and Julie Neisler. (2004). Continuous Improvement for Student Success. Digital Promise Global. https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2004/12/06/the-bell-curve

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Takeaways from the NGCC impact analyses of quasi-experimental comparisons across higher education implementations, reporting that impacts were "small positive (but statistically significant)" and that top-quartile implementations reached "12 percentile points." No effect size is printed.

"On average, NGCC courseware had a small positive (but statistically significant) impact on course grades. The top 25% of implementations had estimated impacts equivalent to 12 percentile points."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Courseware impact varied by context: positive on average in 4-year institutions but insignificant in 2-year colleges, more positive in biology, psychology, and math/statistics, and positive when the comparison course was face-to-face rather than the same modality. — related
  • Of 77 impact data sets submitted by NGCC projects, only 28 had adequate sample sizes and reasonably comparable conditions; the rest were eliminated for small samples, confounds, or baseline inequivalence. — related
  • Courseware use did not disadvantage any student group and appeared especially promising for under-represented minority students, with grades independent of Pell, enrollment, age, gender, and first-gen status in 75% or more of analyses. — related
  • Prior GPA was the best predictor of students' course grade, whether or not students used courseware. — related
  • Adaptive courseware in a corequisite statistics course was associated with improved course outcomes, particularly for Pell-eligible and corequisite students, but effects are confounded with instructor practices — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
