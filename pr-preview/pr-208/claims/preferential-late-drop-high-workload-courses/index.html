Evidence¶

Borchers 2025¶

Borchers, C., Xu, Y., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). Workload Overload? Late Enrollment Leads to Course Dropout. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 1. https://github.com/CAHLR/enrollment-procrastination-edm

q2 · i0 · associational · r2

Two-factorial ANOVA with enrollment delay and late dropping (retained vs. dropped) as factors and average course workload as dependent variable, using the article's course load analytics predictions. The course-type main effect was significant (F(1) = 52.44, p < .001); the interaction was not (F(1) = 0.05, p = .306). Printed effect sizes are d = 0.17 and d = 0.18, both below d = 0.2.

"students who dropped courses late dropped courses with a higher predicted load than those courses that were retained. Averages and effect sizes ranged from M = 2.71 compared to M = 2.67, d = 0.17 for low-enrollment delay students to M = 2.72 compared to M = 2.68, d = 0.18 for high-enrollment delay students. Based on an ANOV A, there was no interaction effect between course type (late dropped vs. retained) and enrollment delay group (F (1) = 0.05,p =.306). Rather, both groups generally late-dropped comparatively high workload courses (F (1) = 52 .44,p < .001)."
