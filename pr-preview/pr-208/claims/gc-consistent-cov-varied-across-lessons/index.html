Evidence¶

McElhaney 2025¶

McElhaney, K. W., Hodkowski, N. M., McMahon, L. H., Jacobs, J., Penuel, W. R., Anton, G., Henson, K., Urban, R., & Parker, D. A. (2025). Promoting Mathematical Reasoning in OpenSciEd Middle School Curriculum Units. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/4ea4b3c5-c663-4484-8727-258bff346d2d

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Across 43 classroom observations of four focal lessons in the GC/CoV-rich units, field notes showed GC was addressed in "91% of the observed lessons, with 64% of lessons engaging students at the highest level," and did not vary substantially by teacher or lesson.

"teachers addressed GC in 91% of the observed lessons, with 64% of lessons engaging students at the highest level."

McElhaney 2025 (2)¶

McElhaney, K. W., Hodkowski, N. M., McMahon, L. H., Jacobs, J., Penuel, W. R., Anton, G., Henson, K., Urban, R., & Parker, D. A. (2025). Promoting Mathematical Reasoning in OpenSciEd Middle School Curriculum Units. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/4ea4b3c5-c663-4484-8727-258bff346d2d

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Observational analysis of the same 43 lesson enactments found CoV was addressed in all observed lessons but at differing trajectory levels — "12% of lessons engaging students at the Existence level, 55% at the Direction level, and 33% at the Amount/Rate level" — varying widely by lesson.

"Teachers addressed CoV in 100% of the observed lessons, with 12% of lessons engaging students at the Existence level, 55% at the Direction level, and 33% at the Amount/Rate level."
