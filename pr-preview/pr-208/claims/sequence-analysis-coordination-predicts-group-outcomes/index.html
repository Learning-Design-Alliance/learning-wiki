The review attributes this to Hoppe et al. (2021), who applied sequence alignment techniques from bioinformatics to compare full action sequences of group members in asynchronous online course interactions. No effect size or sample statistic is printed in the review.

"Their findings revealed that effective coordination—even in the presence of inactivity—was a stronger predictor of successful group outcomes than raw activity levels."
