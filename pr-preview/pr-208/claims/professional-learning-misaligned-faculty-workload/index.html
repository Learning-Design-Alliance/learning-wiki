Evidence¶

Jacob 2026¶

Jacob, S., Miceli, H., Schneider, H., & Peters Hinton, V. (2026). Institutional conditions for integrating emerging technologies in higher education. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/294

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Survey finding: professional development exists at virtually every institution studied, but 60% of faculty reported no compensated time for integrating new technologies; workshops rarely count toward requirements or carry recognition.

"These opportunities frequently conflict with faculty schedules, go uncompensated, and are often designed in ways that don’t reflect how faculty actually learn. For example, 60% of faculty reported that their institution does not provide compensated time to integrate new technologies."

Jacob 2026 (2)¶

Jacob, S., Miceli, H., Schneider, H., & Peters Hinton, V. (2026). Institutional conditions for integrating emerging technologies in higher education. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/294

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Focus group evidence: a biweekly 20-minute single-tool session was reported to yield stronger engagement than long sessions; an administrator noted faculty experienced cognitive overload when too much was covered in a single session.

"One training designer described a recurring session on technical innovation: “Every two weeks we have a 20-minute lesson on one individual tool.” That structure—regular, contained, and directly applicable—yielded stronger engagement than longer, infrequent sessions."
