    Elements¶

    Instructional building blocks — the components you compose into patterns.

    240 entries · 0 stable · 239 in review · 1 drafts

    In Review¶

    Jump to: A · C · D · E · F · G · H · I · J · K · L · M · N · O · P · Q · R · S · T · V · W

    A¶

    • Act - is the execution phase of a challenge-based or inquiry cycle, where learners move from investigation and planning into producing, testing, sharing, or implementing a response in the real world.
    • Activation - Learners are encouraged to recall and activate prior knowledge to prepare for new learning.
    • Adaptive Difficulty - Adaptive difficulty adjusts task challenge in response to learner performance so work remains demanding enough to promote growth without becoming discouraging or trivial.
    • Adaptive Learning - Adaptive learning tailors content, pacing, support, or task sequence in response to evidence about a learner's current performance, needs, or prior knowledge.
    • Adaptive Mastery Learning - Learners progress through levels of difficulty with personalized challenges and just-in-time feedback.
    • Advance Organizers - Concept maps, outlines, or visual guides that help learners structure new information before instruction.
    • Analogies - Analogies connect a new idea to a more familiar situation, structure, or experience so learners can reason about the unfamiliar in terms they already understand.
    • Analogies and prior knowledge activation - Uses comparisons to familiar concepts to support understanding.
    • Anchored Instruction - Anchored instruction situates learning around a shared, meaningful scenario or \"anchor\" that gives learners a concrete context for inquiry, analysis, and problem solving.
    • Application - Learners actively apply knowledge in meaningful tasks.
    • Application of Knowledge - Learners apply concepts to real-world problems or case studies.
    • Argument Construction - Learners develop structured arguments with supporting evidence.
    • Argumentation - Learners construct logical, evidence-based arguments to support their claims.
    • Articulation - Learners verbalize their thought processes and reasoning.
    • Assess performance - Measures learner progress and determines mastery of content.
    • Assessment - Assessment is the structured collection of evidence about learner understanding, performance, or progress for the purposes of feedback, decision making, grading, or revision.
    • Assigned Positions - Learners are assigned a specific stance on an issue and must argue from that perspective.
    • Assigned Readings - Learners engage with pre-selected texts, articles, or book chapters to build foundational knowledge.

    C¶

    • Case Studies - Case studies are the element in which learners analyze a concrete scenario, incident, or example in order to reason about concepts, decisions, or consequences.
    • Case Study - Learners analyze a real or hypothetical scenario to apply concepts.
    • Case-Based Learning - Learners analyze real-world scenarios to apply theoretical concepts.
    • Challenge Identification - Challenge identification is the element in which learners or instructors surface the central obstacle, tension, or problem to be addressed.
    • Class Discussion - Structured conversations where learners analyze and reflect on content.
    • Coaching - Instructors provide individualized support and guidance.
    • Cognitive Apprenticeship - Cognitive apprenticeship is the element in which learners observe expert thinking, practice with coaching, and gradually assume more responsibility.
    • Cognitive Conflict - Deliberately introduces contradictions or challenges to a learner’s existing knowledge to provoke deeper reasoning.
    • Cognitive Load Management - Cognitive load management is the element in which instruction is designed to reduce unnecessary processing and preserve effort for learning-relevant work.
    • Collaboration - Collaboration is the short-form canonical target for learners working together toward a shared product, understanding, or decision.
    • Collaborative Decision-Making - Collaborative decision-making is the element in which learners evaluate options together and negotiate a shared choice or recommendation.
    • Collaborative Discussion - Collaborative discussion is the element in which learners build understanding together through shared talk, comparison of ideas, and negotiated meaning.
    • Collaborative Inquiry - Collaborative inquiry is the element in which learners investigate a question together through shared sensemaking and evidence use.
    • Collaborative Learning - Learners work in structured teams to solve problems and complete tasks.
    • Collaborative Problem-Solving - Learners work together to analyze and solve problems.
    • Communication Channels - Communication channels are the element in which learners use designated tools or spaces to coordinate, discuss, ask questions, and share updates during a learning activity.
    • Comparing Cases - Comparing cases is the element in which learners inspect two or more examples, solutions, texts, or scenarios side by side in order to notice meaningful similarities, differences, and governing principles.
    • Competency-Based Learning - Competency-based learning is the element in which progression is organized around demonstrated competence rather than seat time.
    • Concept Mapping - Concept mapping is the element in which learners represent concepts and their relationships visually, typically as nodes and labeled links.
    • Conceptual Overviews - Conceptual overviews are elements that present the broad structure, central idea, or organizing logic of a topic before moving into details.
    • Conceptual Questioning - Learners answer thought-provoking questions designed to challenge assumptions and deepen understanding.
    • Conceptual Scaffolding - Conceptual scaffolding is the element in which learners are given temporary structures, prompts, or representations that help them understand the underlying ideas of a domain before handling them independently.
    • Concrete Modeling - Concrete modeling is the element in which physical or tangible representations are used to make abstract relationships visible.
    • Consensus Building - Learners work collaboratively to synthesize differing viewpoints into a shared understanding.
    • Consensus Discussion - Learners work collaboratively to agree on a shared perspective or decision.
    • Contextualization - Contextualization is the element in which new ideas, texts, or tasks are framed within a meaningful situation, purpose, domain, or prior experience.
    • Continuous Review - Learning materials and concepts are revisited systematically to reinforce retention and prevent forgetting.
    • Create a Low-Stress Environment - Create a low-stress environment is the element of designing routines, norms, and spaces that reduce unnecessary anxiety during learning.
    • Create a Relaxed Learning Environment - Create a relaxed learning environment is the element of arranging social and physical conditions that support safety, calm, and participation.
    • Creating Visual Representations - Creating visual representations is the element in which learners externalize understanding through diagrams, sketches, models, or other visuals.
    • Criteria Development - Learners create rubrics or evaluation frameworks for assessment.

    D¶

    • Data Analysis - Data analysis is the element in which learners inspect, interpret, organize, or transform data in order to answer questions, identify patterns, or justify conclusions.
    • Debate - Debate is the element in which learners take positions, justify them with evidence, and respond to alternatives in a structured exchange.
    • Debriefing - Debriefing is the element in which learners reflect on an activity, simulation, or performance to consolidate what happened and why.
    • Decision-Making - Learners evaluate options and make informed choices within a structured learning activity.
    • Demonstration - A demonstration presents a complete or partial model of a skill, process, or solution so learners can observe expert performance before attempting it themselves.
    • Differentiated Team Roles - Learners take on distinct roles in collaborative learning to enhance accountability and engagement.
    • Digital Learning - Digital learning is the element in which instruction, resources, or interaction are mediated through digital tools, platforms, or networks.
    • Digital Tools - Digital tools are the element in which learners use software, platforms, or digital applications to create, collaborate, practice, or receive feedback.
    • Direct instruction - Presents new content through explicit explanations, lectures, or demonstrations.
    • Discrepant Events - Discrepant events are demonstrations, observations, or scenarios that violate learner expectations in a noticeable way.
    • Discussion Prompt - A structured question or statement initiates meaningful conversation.
    • Discussion Sections - Smaller, instructor- or TA-led sessions where learners analyze and discuss course material in depth.
    • Discussion-Based Learning - Discussion-based learning is the element in which talk, interpretation, and response are central vehicles for learning.
    • Drill & Practice - Drill and practice is the element in which learners repeatedly perform a focused skill or response pattern until it becomes more fluent or automatic.
    • Dual Coding - Dual coding is the element in which verbal and visual representations are used together to support understanding and memory.

    E¶

    • Eliciting Student Thinking - Encourages learners to express their ideas, reasoning, and problem-solving approaches verbally.
    • Engage - Engage is the element in which instruction opens with an activity or prompt that draws attention and establishes interest.
    • Enhance retention and transfer - Helps learners apply knowledge to new contexts through reflection and extension activities.
    • Epitome (Elaboration Theory) - A simplified version of the full concept is introduced first before elaborating on details.
    • Erroneous Examples - Erroneous examples are worked examples that intentionally contain a mistake for learners to identify, explain, and correct.
    • Evidence-Based Reasoning - Learners evaluate competing models based on real-world evidence and refine their understanding.
    • Expert Groups - Learners become subject-matter experts on a topic and teach their peers.
    • Expert Modeling - Expert modeling is the element in which a skilled performer demonstrates not only what to do, but how an expert notices cues, makes decisions, and checks quality during performance.
    • Explicit Teaching - Explicit teaching is the element in which instructors directly explain, model, and sequence key knowledge or procedures rather than leaving them to be inferred.

    F¶

    • Fading - Fading is the element in which instructional support is deliberately reduced as learner competence grows.
    • Fading Scaffolding - Fading scaffolding is the element in which supports such as hints, prompts, or models are progressively reduced so that learners take on more of the task independently.
    • Feedback - Learners receive input from instructors or peers to refine their work.
    • Final Exam - A cumulative assessment evaluating learners' mastery of the entire course material.
    • Flipped Classroom - Flipped classroom is the element in which initial content exposure happens before class so in-class time can focus on application and feedback.
    • Formative Assessment - Ongoing, low-stakes assessment to provide feedback and adjust instruction.

    G¶

    • Gain attention - Engages learners with an unexpected event, question, or real-world scenario to capture focus.
    • Gamification - Gamification is the element in which points, progress indicators, challenges, or other game-like structures are used to shape participation and persistence.
    • Goal Setting - Goal setting is the element in which learners or instructors establish clear targets for performance, progress, or improvement.
    • Gradual Release - Gradual release is the element in which responsibility for performance shifts from teacher or system to learner across stages of modeling, guided practice, and independent work.
    • Graphic Organizers - Graphic organizers are visual structures that help learners sort, compare, and connect ideas.
    • Group Work - Group work is the element in which learners work together on a shared task, product, or problem.
    • Guided Discovery - Guided discovery is the element in which learners investigate, infer, or notice patterns for themselves, but with prompts, constraints, examples, or feedback that keep the exploration productive.
    • Guided Inquiry - Guided inquiry is the element in which learners investigate questions or problems with structured prompts, facilitator support, or staged resources that keep the inquiry productive.
    • Guided Practice - Guided practice is the element in which learners attempt a task with active support before moving to independent work.

    H¶

    • Hands-on Learning - Hands-on learning is the element in which learners manipulate materials, tools, or environments directly while applying ideas in practice.
    • Hints - Hints are partial prompts or cues that help learners continue a task without fully giving away the answer.
    • Hypertext Navigation - Learners explore interconnected digital content at their own pace.

    I¶

    • Immediate Feedback - Immediate feedback is the element in which response information is delivered close to the learner's action so it can still shape the next step.
    • In-Class Activities - Learners engage in active, structured tasks during class to apply knowledge.
    • Individual Reflection - Learners independently analyze their experiences, thoughts, or learning progress.
    • Individual Response - Learners answer a question or solve a problem independently before group discussion.
    • Information Literacy - Information literacy is the element in which learners identify information needs, locate sources, evaluate credibility, and use information responsibly.
    • Inquiry and Research - Learners investigate a topic through questioning and data gathering.
    • Inquiry-Based Exploration - Learners investigate open-ended problems and generate solutions.
    • Inquiry-Based Learning - Inquiry-based learning is the element in which learners investigate questions, evidence, or problems in order to build understanding through guided exploration.
    • Integration - Learners reflect on and connect learning to their own experiences and future applications.
    • Investigate - Investigate is the element in which learners collect information, examine evidence, or test ideas to answer a question.
    • Iterative Learning - Iterative learning is the element in which learners improve through repeated cycles of attempt, feedback, revision, and retry.

    J¶

    • Jigsaw - Jigsaw is the element in which parts of a topic are distributed across learners who then teach one another to assemble the whole.
    • Jigsaw Learning - Jigsaw learning is the short-form canonical target for the jigsaw-style distribution of expertise and peer teaching.
    • Journaling - Journaling is the element in which learners record reflections, observations, questions, or progress over time in written form.
    • Just-in-Time Information - Instruction is provided at the point of need rather than upfront.
    • Just-in-Time Learning - Just-in-time learning is the element in which information, guidance, or resources are provided at the moment they are needed for the task at hand rather than front-loaded in advance.
    • Justification - Justification is the element in which learners explain why a claim, choice, or solution is warranted using reasons, criteria, or evidence.
    • Justification & Argumentation - Learners defend their conclusions with evidence, fostering critical reasoning.

    K¶

    • Knowledge Synthesis - Learners integrate multiple perspectives and sources to develop a cohesive understanding.
    • Knowledge-Building - Knowledge-building is the element in which learners collectively construct, refine, and extend ideas rather than only consume or repeat them.
    • Knowledge-Building Discourse - Learners engage in structured discussions to deepen understanding.

    L¶

    • Learner Choice - Learner choice is the element in which learners are given meaningful options about task, process, product, or pathway.
    • Learning Management Systems - Learning management systems are the element in which course organization, materials, communication, and tracking are coordinated through an LMS.
    • Learning Outcomes - Learning outcomes are explicit statements of what learners should know, understand, or be able to do by the end of an instructional experience.
    • Lectures - Instructor-led presentations that deliver structured content to learners.
    • Literature Review - Literature review is the element in which learners gather, evaluate, and synthesize existing sources on a topic in order to understand the current state of knowledge or argument.

    M¶

    • Mastery Learning - Mastery learning is the element in which progression depends on demonstrated understanding rather than time alone.
    • Mastery Progression - Learners cannot advance until they demonstrate mastery of foundational content.
    • Metacognition - Metacognition is the element in which learners monitor, explain, and regulate their own thinking.
    • Metacognitive Reflection - Metacognitive reflection is the element in which learners examine how they thought, what strategies they used, where they were confused, and what they should do next.
    • Metacognitive Strategies - Metacognitive strategies are deliberate learner moves for planning, monitoring, checking, and revising thinking during learning.
    • Metaphors - Metaphors are explanatory comparisons that map a less familiar concept onto a more familiar image or experience.
    • Midterm Exam - A structured, formal assessment given at the midpoint of a course.
    • Mission-Based Learning - Learners engage in a structured, goal-oriented challenge.
    • Model Revision - Learners refine their conceptual models based on evidence and feedback.
    • Modeling - Instructors or advanced learners demonstrate expert thinking and behavior.
    • Moderation - An instructor or facilitator guides discussions to ensure productive engagement.
    • Multimedia Instruction - Multimedia instruction is the element in which teaching combines spoken or written language with visuals, diagrams, animation, or other media in a coordinated instructional sequence.
    • Multimedia Learning - Multimedia learning is the element in which learners engage with coordinated combinations of words, visuals, audio, animation, or interactive media.
    • Multimedia Projects - Multimedia projects are the element in which learners create artifacts that combine text, visuals, audio, video, or interaction.

    N¶

    • Negotiation - Negotiation is the element in which learners work through competing interests, priorities, or interpretations in order to reach a decision, compromise, or shared position.
    • Non-Examples - Non-examples are intentionally chosen instances that do not meet the target concept, criterion, or quality standard.
    • Note-Taking - Note-taking is the element in which learners record, organize, and condense information in ways that support later understanding and retrieval.

    O¶

    • Observation - Observation is the short-form canonical target for instructional activities in which learners watch people, processes, or contexts carefully in order to notice patterns, behaviors, or constraints.
    • Observation/Shadowing - Observation/shadowing is the element in which learners watch practice in context in order to notice decisions, routines, and tacit moves before attempting similar work themselves.
    • Open-Ended Discussion - Facilitated dialogue where learners explore topics without predetermined answers.
    • Optional Quizzes or Discussion Sections - Supplementary activities designed to reinforce learning and provide formative feedback.

    P¶

    • Part-task practice - Isolated, repeated practice of elements that require automation for efficient task performance.
    • Peer Assessment - Peer assessment is the element in which learners evaluate the work or reasoning of peers against shared criteria.
    • Peer Collaboration - Learners work together to complete tasks or solve problems.
    • Peer Discussion - Learners engage in structured conversations with classmates to explore and refine ideas.
    • Peer Explanation - Learners verbalize their thinking to peers, strengthening their own understanding.
    • Peer Feedback - Peer feedback is the element in which learners respond to one another's work with comments intended to improve revision or performance.
    • Peer Feedback/Peer Review - Peer feedback/peer review is the element in which learners evaluate one another's work against criteria and provide revision-oriented feedback.
    • Peer Instruction - Peer instruction is the element in which learners explain, compare, and revise answers with one another around conceptual questions.
    • Peer Interaction - Learners engage with one another through discussions, debates, or collaborative tasks.
    • Peer Review - Peer review is the element in which learners examine and respond to one another's work using shared criteria, prompts, or examples.
    • Peer Teaching - Learners explain concepts to peers, reinforcing their own understanding.
    • Performance-Based Assessment - Performance-based assessment is the element in which learners demonstrate knowledge or skill by performing, producing, or solving something observable rather than only selecting answers.
    • Personalized Pacing - Personalized pacing is the element in which learners move through content or practice at different rates based on need and progress.
    • Perspective-Taking - Learners consider multiple viewpoints to develop empathy and deeper understanding.
    • Persuasion - Learners develop techniques to convince others using logic and evidence.
    • Position Development - Learners construct and refine their stance on an issue through evidence-based reasoning.
    • Practice - Encourages learners to apply new knowledge or skills through structured activities.
    • Pre-Class Preparation - Pre-class preparation is the element in which learners engage with readings, videos, prompts, or other materials before a live class session so that class time can focus on discussion, application, or problem solving.
    • Pre-Class Video/Lecture - Instructional content is delivered via video or recorded lecture before in-class activities.
    • Pre-Reading Activities - Pre-reading activities are tasks completed before reading in order to activate prior knowledge, establish purpose, surface key vocabulary, or prepare attention for important ideas.
    • Pre-Reading Questioning - Pre-reading questioning is the element in which learners preview a text through guiding questions before or during initial reading.
    • Pre-Seminar Preparation - Learners review content before engaging in discussion-based activities.
    • Prior Knowledge Activation - Prior knowledge activation is the element in which learners recall, surface, or organize what they already know before engaging with new material.
    • Problem Presentation - Learners are introduced to a real-world problem to analyze and solve.
    • Problem Scenario - Learners engage with a structured real-world scenario that requires problem-solving.
    • Problem Scenarios - Problem scenarios are concrete situations presented to learners as the context for inquiry, analysis, or solution development.
    • Problem Solving - Problem solving is the element in which learners identify, analyze, and respond to a challenge by generating and evaluating possible solutions.
    • Problem-Based Learning - Problem-based learning, as an element, refers to the use of an authentic or ill-structured problem as the immediate driver of inquiry, information seeking, and solution development inside a larger design.
    • Problem-Solving Tasks - Learners complete structured activities that require critical thinking and application.
    • Procedural information - Step-by-step instructions and just-in-time guidance to assist learners in acquiring procedural fluency.
    • Procedural Learning - Procedural learning is the element in which learners acquire stepwise routines, methods, or operations through guided performance.
    • Progress Tracking - Progress tracking is the element in which learners or instructors monitor advancement through goals, tasks, competencies, or milestones over time.
    • Promote a Sense of Home - Promote a sense of home is the element of creating belonging, familiarity, and social warmth within a learning environment.
    • Provide feedback - Gives learners corrective or reinforcing feedback to improve performance.
    • Provide guidance - Offers scaffolding, modeling, or examples to support learning.
    • Public Display - Public display is the element in which learner work is made visible to a wider audience in the classroom or beyond.
    • Public Product - Public product is the element in which learners create work intended for real audiences beyond the teacher alone.
    • Public Speaking - Public speaking is the element in which learners present ideas aloud to an audience with attention to clarity, organization, evidence, and delivery.

    Q¶

    • Question Formulation - Learners generate their own questions to guide inquiry.
    • Quizzes - Short, low-stakes assessments that reinforce learning and provide feedback.

    R¶

    • Real-World Math - Real-world math is the element in which mathematical reasoning is embedded in practical or authentic contexts.
    • Real-World Problems - Real-world problems are tasks framed around authentic issues, needs, or dilemmas outside purely academic abstraction.
    • Reassessment - Learners revisit and revise their responses after discussion or additional learning.
    • Rebuttals - Learners counter opposing viewpoints with evidence-based arguments.
    • Recall prior knowledge - Activates learners' existing mental models to connect new information.
    • Reciprocal Teaching - Learners take turns guiding discussion and questioning content.
    • Reflection - Learners analyze their experiences, actions, or decisions to deepen learning.
    • Reflection Activities - Reflection activities are structured tasks that ask learners to examine what they did, understood, or would change next.
    • Representational Drawing - Representational drawing is the element in which learners sketch, diagram, or draw structures and relationships to support understanding.
    • Research - Learners conduct investigations to explore topics, gather evidence, and develop conclusions.
    • Resource Evaluation - Learners assess the credibility and relevance of sources.
    • Resource Hubs - Resource hubs are centralized collections of materials, links, tools, or references that learners can return to during a course or project.
    • Retrieval Practice - Retrieval practice is the element in which learners recall information from memory rather than only re-exposing themselves to it.
    • Review - Learners reflect on their own or peers’ work before finalizing.
    • Revision - Learners improve their work based on feedback and self-reflection.
    • Rhetorical Strategies - Rhetorical strategies are deliberate moves learners use to persuade, frame, emphasize, appeal to an audience, or strengthen the force of an argument.
    • Role Reversal - Learners switch sides in a debate or discussion to argue from the opposite perspective.
    • Role-Playing - Role-playing is the element in which learners adopt roles and act through a scenario, interaction, or decision process from that perspective.
    • Rubric Design - Rubric design is the element in which criteria and performance levels are made explicit through a structured evaluative framework.
    • Rubrics - Rubrics are the element in which criteria and performance levels are made explicit to guide quality work and evaluation.

    S¶

    • Scaffolded Inquiry - Gradually shifts learners from guided exploration to independent inquiry.
    • Scaffolding - Scaffolding is the element in which support is added temporarily so learners can perform tasks they could not yet complete independently.
    • Scenario-Based Learning - Scenario-based learning is the element in which instruction is organized around a realistic situation, case, or mission that requires learners to interpret information and make decisions in context.
    • Scientific Reasoning - Scientific reasoning is the element in which learners generate explanations, evaluate evidence, test claims, and revise models using disciplinary norms of inquiry.
    • Self-Assessment - Self-assessment is the element in which learners judge their own work, understanding, or progress against explicit criteria.
    • Self-Explanation - Self-explanation is the element in which learners explain to themselves why something is true, why a step works, or how a solution fits the problem.
    • Self-Monitoring - Self-monitoring is the element in which learners track their own attention, behavior, progress, or accuracy during learning.
    • Self-Paced Learning - Self-paced learning is the element in which learners control the rate or sequence of progress through material within a designed structure.
    • Self-Testing - Self-testing is the element in which learners quiz themselves or otherwise attempt to retrieve knowledge without immediate external prompting.
    • Seminar Format - Seminar format is the element in which learning is organized around sustained discussion of a shared text, question, or issue with facilitator support but strong learner participation.
    • Shared Documents - Shared documents are the element in which multiple learners contribute to a common editable artifact.
    • Simple-to-complex sequencing - Learning progresses from basic concepts to more detailed, complex ideas.
    • Simulations - Simulations are the element in which learners engage with realistic, model-based scenarios that approximate authentic systems or environments.
    • Small-Group Instruction - Small-group instruction is the element in which learners work with an instructor or facilitator in a reduced-size group for targeted teaching, discussion, or support.
    • SMARTER Goals - SMARTER goals are a structured goal-setting element that asks learners to define goals that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound, evaluated, and revised or rewarded, depending on the local formulation.
    • Social Learning - Social learning is the element in which interaction with peers, models, or communities is treated as a direct source of learning.
    • Socratic Questioning - Socratic questioning is the element in which prompts are used to probe assumptions, clarify reasoning, test evidence, and extend implications.
    • Socratic Seminar - Socratic seminar is the element in which learners build understanding through text-centered questioning and discussion.
    • Solution Development - Learners synthesize information to propose and test solutions.
    • Spaced Learning - Spaced learning is the element in which practice or review is distributed over time rather than massed together.
    • Spaced Repetition - Spaced repetition is the element in which key material is revisited at strategically increasing intervals rather than massed into a single session.
    • Spiral Curriculum - Spiral curriculum is the element in which important ideas are revisited repeatedly over time, with each return expanding complexity or depth.
    • State objectives - Clearly communicates the learning goals to set expectations and focus attention.
    • Structured Academic Controversy - Structured academic controversy is the element in which learners argue multiple sides of an issue before synthesizing a shared conclusion.
    • Structured Debate - Structured debate is the element in which learners examine opposing claims using explicit roles, turn structures, evidence requirements, and response rules.
    • Structured Discussion - Structured discussion is the element in which talk is guided by prompts, roles, turns, protocols, or evidence requirements rather than left entirely open.
    • Structured Questioning - Instructors guide learners with sequenced, purposeful questions to scaffold deeper inquiry.
    • Submission - Learners submit work for assessment, feedback, or peer review.
    • Summarization and Synthesis - Learners distill key concepts from a discussion, reading, or experience.
    • Summative Assessment - Summative assessment is the element in which a learner's performance is judged at the end of a unit, course, or other instructional sequence.
    • Supportive information - Supportive information provides the cognitive strategies, mental models, and conceptual frameworks necessary for tackling complex tasks.

    T¶

    • Task Management - Task management is the element in which learners organize, track, assign, or sequence work across a project or collaborative activity.
    • Team-Based Inquiry - Students work in structured groups to analyze problems, construct solutions, and reflect together.
    • Technology Integration - Technology integration is the element in which digital tools are used in direct service of learning goals, feedback, access, or collaboration.
    • Think-Aloud - Think-aloud is the element in which an instructor, peer, or learner verbalizes reasoning while performing a task.

    V¶

    • Video Prompts - Video prompts are short video clips used to launch attention, curiosity, discussion, or analysis.
    • Video-Based Reflection - Video-based reflection is the element in which learners review recorded performance to analyze choices, patterns, and next steps.
    • Virtual Whiteboards - Virtual whiteboards are the element in which learners sketch, annotate, and collaborate in a shared digital visual workspace.

    W¶

    • Whole-Class Sharing - The entire class participates in discussion or presentation of ideas, often following small-group activities.
    • Whole-task performance - Whole-task performance engages learners in authentic, complex tasks that integrate multiple skills and knowledge areas simultaneously, mirroring the conditions of real-world practice.
    • Worked Examples - Worked examples are the element in which learners study complete or partial solutions before attempting similar problems independently.

    Draft¶

    • Text underlining and annotating - A study aid involving marking text to indicate importance.
