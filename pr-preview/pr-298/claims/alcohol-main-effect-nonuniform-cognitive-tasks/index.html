Evidence¶

Dry MJ 2012¶

Dry MJ, Burns NR, Nettelbeck T, Farquharson AL, White JM (2012) Dose-Related Effects of Alcohol on Cognitive Functioning. PLoS ONE 7(11): e50977. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0050977

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Mixed-design experiment with 56 adults randomized to alcohol or control, tested at four BAC time-points on six psychometric tests. The ANOVA found a "strong and significant main effect of alcohol" with printed effect sizes partial η² = 0.07 to 0.37 for all tasks except TSP and SART-OM.

"Repeated measures analysis of variance indicated that for each of the tasks excepting TSP and the SART-OM there was a strong and significant main effect of alcohol, with effect sizes (partial g2) ranging from 0.07 to 0.37 (Table 2)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • TSP (complex spatial problem solving) and SART-OM (vigilance for frequent events) showed no significant alcohol effects, an outcome the authors report as unexpected — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Only SOPT, IT and SART-CO showed significant impairment at the lowest blood alcohol concentration of 0.048%, with large effect sizes (d = 0.76, 0.65 and 1.06) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The authors conclude Inspection Time has strong potential as a single marker test sensitive to the time course of alcohol intoxication — related
  • SART-CO errors rose sharply at BAC 0.048% but changed little thereafter across the 0.048% to 0.10% range, with a suspected ceiling effect — related
