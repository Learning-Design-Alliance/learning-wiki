Evidence¶

Stahl Aimee E. 2019¶

Stahl Aimee E., Feigenson Lisa. (2019). Violations of Core Knowledge Shape Early Learning. Topics in Cognitive Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12389

q1 · i? · review · r2

Gumball machine experiment (Stahl & Feigenson, in preparation b) with 2- and 3-year-olds: a pink toy emerged from a machine containing 10 purple and 10 pink toys (probable) or 20 purple toys (impossible), followed by novel word teaching and a referent-selection test.

"children who saw the probable outcome (50% likelihood) failed to learn the novel word, whereas children who saw the impossible outcome (0% likelihood) succeeded"

Stahl Aimee E. 2019 (2)¶

Stahl Aimee E., Feigenson Lisa. (2019). Violations of Core Knowledge Shape Early Learning. Topics in Cognitive Science. https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12389

q1 · i? · review · r2

Three additional groups saw pink toys emerge at 10%, 5%, or 2.5% likelihood; all failed to learn, showing step-like rather than continuous performance, contrasting with prediction-error accounts where learning scales with statistical expectancy.

"In all of these cases, children failed to learn the novel word used to label the pink toy. Interestingly, there was no evidence of graded performance; learning did not improve as the event became more surprising"
