Computational experiments solved the formulation with Gurobi as an exact classical benchmark and via D-Wave's hybrid CQM solver, with each instance solved five times to assess stochastic variability. The article reports a proof-of-concept assessment, not a quantum advantage.

"The computational experiments provide a proof-of-concept assessment of the applicability of hybrid quantum optimization to the considered school aggregation problem, while also highlighting the current computational advantage of classical optimization and the limitations of the tested benchmark instances."
