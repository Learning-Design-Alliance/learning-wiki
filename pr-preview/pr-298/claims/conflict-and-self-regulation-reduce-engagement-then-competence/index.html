Evidence¶

Portilla Ximena A. 2014¶

Portilla Ximena A., Ballard Parissa J., Adler Nancy E., Boyce W. Thomas, Obradović Jelena. (2014). An Integrative View of School Functioning: Transactions between Self-Regulation, School Engagement, and Teacher-Child Relationship Quality. Child development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12259

q3 · i? · associational · r2

In the nested path models, fall teacher-child conflict predicted a reduction in school engagement by kindergarten spring, "associated with a similar reduction in school engagement by spring (B = −.08, β = −.14, p < .05)", over and above concurrent covariation.

"Further, greater conflict between teacher and child in the fall was associated with a similar reduction in school engagement by spring (B = −.08, β = −.14, p < .05)."

Portilla Ximena A. 2014 (2)¶

Portilla Ximena A., Ballard Parissa J., Adler Nancy E., Boyce W. Thomas, Obradović Jelena. (2014). An Integrative View of School Functioning: Transactions between Self-Regulation, School Engagement, and Teacher-Child Relationship Quality. Child development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12259

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Post-hoc MODEL INDIRECT analyses in Mplus tested mediation: a "significant indirect effect (B = −.13, β = −.06, p < .01)" from inattention/impulsivity through school engagement to first-grade academic competence; the conflict pathway was only marginally significant.

"These supplemental analyses found a significant indirect effect (B = −.13, β = −.06, p < .01) from inattention/impulsivity to academic competence through school engagement. The indirect effect from conflict to academic competence was only marginally significant (B = −.04, β = −.04, p < .10)."
