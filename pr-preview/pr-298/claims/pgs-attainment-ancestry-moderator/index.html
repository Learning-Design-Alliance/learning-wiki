Evidence¶

Wilding 2024¶

Wilding, K., Wright, M., & von Stumm, S. (2024). Using DNA to Predict Education: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09928-4

q4 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-regression of the attainment estimates with sample ancestry (binary European versus non-European) as moderator; ancestry was the only individually significant moderator, "with European ancestry samples showing stronger polygenic score predictions compared to African American samples".

"Only sample ancestry emerged as significant moderator of polygenic score predictions of educational attainment (β = − 0.209, p < 0.001, 95% CI from − 0.29 to − 0.13), with European ancestry samples showing stronger polygenic score predictions compared to African American samples."

Wilding 2024 (2)¶

Wilding, K., Wright, M., & von Stumm, S. (2024). Using DNA to Predict Education: a Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09928-4

q4 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Adjusted pooled estimate after modeling sample ancestry in the attainment meta-regression; the association rose to ρ = 0.287, p < 0.001. Ancestry accounted for only 3.34% of total heterogeneity, which remained significant (Q = 851.832, p < 0.001).

"Accounting for sample ancestry increased the association between polygenic score predictions and educational attainment to ρ = 0.287, p < 0.001 (95% CI from 0.242 to 0.332)"
