Daches Cohen L and Rubinsten O 2017¶

Daches Cohen L and Rubinsten O (2017) Mothers, Intrinsic Math Motivation, Arithmetic Skills, and Math Anxiety in Elementary School. Front. Psychol. 8:1939. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01939

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Pearson correlation between mothers' and children's variables (Table 3) in the study of 27 mother-child pairs. The article reports "Lower scores in the math ﬂuency subtest were associated with higher levels of maternal help" and the other printed correlations; calculation was also negatively correlated with help (r= −0.42) and monitoring (r= −0.36).

"Lower scores in the math ﬂuency subtest were associated with higher levels of maternal help ( r=− 0.51, p < 0.01), press for intellectual development ( r = −0.39, p < 0.025), and monitoring ( r = −0.53, p < 0.01)."

Related Claims¶

  • Preliminary evidence of intergenerational transmission of math anxiety: mothers' math anxiety relates to children's lower intrinsic math motivation and stronger attentional bias toward math stimuli — related
  • Implicitly measured math anxiety (attentional bias) in children is predicted by environmental factors, specifically mothers' numerical task anxiety and maternal pressure — related
  • Children's arithmetic skills are the strongest predictor of their explicitly measured math anxiety, with higher skills associated with lower anxiety — related
  • Maternal education has the largest peak association with cognitive development: a high-school-to-college increase predicts about 0.4 SD higher reading and math scores at the peak. — related
  • Maternal cognitive support was only modestly associated with emotional support and largely independent of intrusiveness and negativity — related
