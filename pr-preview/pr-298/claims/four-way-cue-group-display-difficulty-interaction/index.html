Evidence¶

Moores 2011¶

Moores, E., Cassim, R. and Talcott, J.B. (2011). Adults with dyslexia exhibit large effects of crowding, increased dependence on cues, and detrimental effects of distractors in visual search tasks. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2011.10.005

q2 · i3 · causal · r2

Four-factor ANOVA on crowding-analysis accuracy (set size eight spread vs crowded, uncued vs pre-cued, easy vs difficult) for 15 AwD and 16 controls. Significant cue-group, cue-display and cue-display-group interactions (η2p =.23, .26, .15) indicated group differences were largest for uncued crowded displays.

"There were also significant interactions of cue and group (F1, 29=8.85, p<.01, η2p =.23); cue and display type (F1, 29=10.36, p<.01, η2p =.26); and cue, display type and group (F1, 29=5.03, p<.05, η2p =.15). These findings suggested that performance of AwD and controls differed more when stimuli were uncued and crowded."
