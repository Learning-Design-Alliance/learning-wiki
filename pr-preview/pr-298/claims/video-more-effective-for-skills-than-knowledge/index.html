Evidence¶

Noetel 2021¶

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2021). Video Improves Learning in Higher Education: A Systematic Review. Review of Educational Research, 91(2), 204–236. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654321990713

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r3

Moderation analysis by outcome assessment type within the replacement meta-analysis. The review reports video "was more effective when students were assessed on skill acquisition (g = 0.44, 95% CI [0.24, 0.64])" than knowledge (g = 0.18).

"The type of outcome measured was a significant moderator (R22 = 0.15, R32 = 0.00, p = 0.02). Video was more effective when students were assessed on skill acquisition (g = 0.44, 95% CI [0.24, 0.64], n = 67, k = 38) compared with assessments of their knowledge (g = 0.18, 95% CI [0.01, 0.35], n = 99, k = 60)."
