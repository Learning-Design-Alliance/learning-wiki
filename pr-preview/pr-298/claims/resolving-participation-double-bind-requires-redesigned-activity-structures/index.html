In one participatory budgeting case, Latino immigrant participation lapsed under outreach with interpretation and was sustained only after the activity structure was redesigned¶

Claim · All claims Evidence · 1 study · qualitative r2 · q2 · n=7 Latino community volunteers (Iteration 1), Chicago's 49th Ward (21% Spanish-speaking at home, 30% foreign-born), fiscal years 2012-2014

An embedded case study of a Chicago participatory budgeting process found that outreach plus sequential interpretation failed to sustain Latino immigrant participation (attendance dropped from 7 to 0 within two meetings), while a subsequent formative intervention that redesigned the activity's tools and roles — a dedicated Spanish-Language Committee with native-language deliberation — sustained participation through a full cycle and was later adopted by other wards.

Meléndez conducted an embedded, interventionist ethnography of Chicago's Ward 49 participatory budgeting process (PB49) across two iterations, applying Cultural-Historical Activity Theory's expansive-learning framework and coding meeting transcripts for stabilizing/destabilizing discourse and claims-making language. The first iteration's naive-inclusion design (outreach + sequential interpretation into an English-run committee) failed to sustain Latino participation; a subsequent formative intervention — a redesign of the activity's structures (see Shared power and co-creation of educational systems), instantiated as a Spanish-Language Committee — sustained participation and produced observable collective-level (claims-making) and system-level (adoption elsewhere) learning.

Discussion¶

As a single embedded case with the researcher acting as co-designer of the intervention, positionality is a real limitation the author himself acknowledges; there is no comparison ward or control condition, so alternative explanations (e.g., general momentum from continued outreach, unrelated changes in ward leadership) cannot be fully ruled out. The dramatic before/after attendance shift (7→0→sustained) and the qualitative claims-making evidence are nonetheless a clear, well-documented pattern, and the system-level adoption by other wards — while not systematically measured — is a meaningful signal that the redesign was not merely locally effective. This is the empirical basis for Cultural-Historical Activity Theory's collective-vs-system-level distinction in expansive learning.
