Evidence¶

Palladino 2025¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

A multilevel model for repeated measures on 305 children tested twice, nested in three schools, predicting English vocabulary from metacognition and emotions. The final model explained "73.5% of the total variance (R²c)". Metacognitive control had the larger estimate (2.01) versus confidence judgments (0.38).

"Both confidence judgments ( t(415) = 2.60, p =.010) and metacognitive control ( t(415) = 3.75, p = < 0.001) were found to predict English vocabulary performance from T0 to T1."

Palladino 2025 (2)¶

Palladino, P., Trotta, E., Bonvino, A., Carlucci, L., & Cottini, M. (2025). How do you feel during English class? Emotions and metacognition in primary school children learning English as a second language. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09414-4

q3 · i? · associational · r2

An additional hierarchical regression checked whether metacognitive measures predicted T1 vocabulary over and above T0 vocabulary, because confidence judgments and control "have often been shown to be confounded with performance". Both remained significant predictors.

"Hierarchical regression analysis showed that both confidence judgments and metacognitive control predicted English vocabulary at T1, despite performance on the English vocabulary test at T0 being partialed out"
