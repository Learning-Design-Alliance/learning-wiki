Evidence¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Conditions for Effective Learning Without Upfront Instruction: How Practice with Feedback Supports Memory, Generalization, Motivation, and Metacognition. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10103-6

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Study 1 measured instructional time directly: the explanatory feedback condition averaged "7.5 minutes, compared to 9.9 minutes in the lecture condition, t(291) = 4.00, p <.001—a time savings of 2.4 minutes, or approximately 24%"; correct-response feedback was 2.6 minutes faster (p <.001). Combined with comparable or better performance, the authors conclude practice with elaborated feedback was more efficient.

"Participants in the explanatory feedback condition completed the learning ses- sion in an average of 7.5 minutes, compared to 9.9 minutes in the lecture condi- tion, t(291) = 4.00, p <.001—a time savings of 2.4 minutes, or approximately 24%."

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho 2026 (2)¶

Michael W. Asher & Paulo F. Carvalho. (2026). Conditions for Effective Learning Without Upfront Instruction: How Practice with Feedback Supports Memory, Generalization, Motivation, and Metacognition. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10103-6

q3 · i? · causal · r3

Study 2 estimated efficiency via an imputation procedure (1000 resampled datasets for lecture baseline knowledge). Elaborated feedback reached "6.93 points per minute, 3.4 times as efficiently as those in the lecture condition (2.05 points per minute)"; correct-response feedback was 2.6 times as efficient (p =.006), and elaborated feedback was 2.1 times as efficient as lecture for generalization (p =.041).

"For memory scores, participants in the prac- tice with elaborated feedback condition learned at an estimated rate of 6.93 points per minute, 3.4 times as efficiently as those in the lecture condition (2.05 points per minute), t(265.8) = −4.22, p <.001."
