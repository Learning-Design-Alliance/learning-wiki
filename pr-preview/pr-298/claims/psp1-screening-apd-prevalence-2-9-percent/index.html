Evidence¶

Iva I. Hrastinski 2023¶

Iva I. Hrastinski, Ana M. Bonetti, Mladen A. Hedjever. (2023). Relationship between auditory processing skills and academic achievement of elementary school children. Specijalna edukacija i rehabilitacija, 22(2), 95-116. https://doi.org/10.5937/specedreh22-37415

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Screening study of 412 mainstream-school children in grades 1–4 in Croatia administered the PSP-1 battery individually. The authors report that "12 (2.9%) participants exhibited difficulties that can be categorized as auditory processing disorder" under the stated cutoff criteria.

"However, after applying diagnostic criteria for APD (results ≤ 2 standard deviations on at least two PSP-1 subtests), 12 (2.9%) participants exhibited difficulties that can be categorized as auditory processing disorder."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students with APD were rated below average by teachers far more often than non-APD peers, especially in reading and language skills
  • Dichotic listening subtests were the most challenging PSP-1 tasks, especially for children with auditory processing difficulties
  • PSP-1 performance increases with age across grades 1–4, with significant differences between adjacent age groups on total score
  • A right-ear advantage appears in both dichotic listening tasks and decreases with age on the Dichotic Sentences subtest
  • Teacher ratings of language and reading skills correlate significantly with PSP-1 total scores, whereas grade point average does not
