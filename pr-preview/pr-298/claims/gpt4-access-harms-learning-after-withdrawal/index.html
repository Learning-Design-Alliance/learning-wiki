Evidence¶

Mutlu Cukurova 2026¶

Mutlu Cukurova. (2026). What do you mean by 'human–AI collaboration'? Prerequisite functions and the affordances needed to achieve it. Submitted to the Handbook of AI and the Future of Education. https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5843-4854

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The chapter reports, citing Bastani et al., a randomised controlled trial with nearly a thousand high-school mathematics students, where "unrestricted access to a GPT-4 interface raised assisted practice performance by 48 per cent" while withdrawal left students 17 per cent worse off than never-users.

"unrestricted access to a GPT-4 interface raised assisted practice performance by 48 per cent; yet when access was subsequently withdrawn, the same students performed 17 per cent worse than controls who had never used the tool"

Reshaping Undergraduate Computer Science Education in the Generative AI Era 2026¶

Reshaping Undergraduate Computer Science Education in the Generative AI Era. (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07545

q2 · i? · qualitative · r1

The whitepaper reports this second-hand, citing Bastani et al.: a randomized controlled trial with nearly 1,000 students in which "unrestricted GPT -4 access" raised practice performance 48% but exam scores fell 17% once AI was removed. The whitepaper does not name the students' level or subject in this passage; its reference list gives the trial's title as "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics". The 48% and 17% are percentage differences, not a d-family effect size, so impact is coded i? (not reported), which is not a null effect.

"Bastani et al. [3] conducted a randomized controlled trial with nearly 1,000 students and found that unrestricted GPT -4 access, while improving practice performance by 48%, led to a 17% decline in exam scores when AI was subsequently removed."

Zemin Guo 2026¶

Zemin Guo, Wenhui Li, Laure Lu Chen, Qianru Liang, & Yuxuan Wu. (2026). Reconceptualizing digital literacy in the AI era: A systematic review of validated frameworks from an ecological perspective. Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 11, 100679. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100679

q1 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes this finding to Bastani et al. (2025) in its micro-level ecology section, using it to illustrate the performance–mastery distinction: AI may inflate observable performance "without necessarily producing durable acquisition." In its text the review names neither the model (GPT-4) nor an unrestricted-access condition, and does not describe the trial's design; it says only "generative AI". The subject and level appear only in its reference list, which titles the study Bastani et al. (2025), "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics". The underlying study was not read for this extraction, so GPT-4, unrestricted access and the randomised design are not established by this entry.

"Bastani et al. (2025) found that, relative to traditional instruction, generative AI could raise students' immediate performance but impair delayed exam scores when AI access is removed."

Khosravi 2026¶

Khosravi, H., Gašević, D., Sadiq, S., Yan, L., Lodge, J. M., Tangen, J. M., Denny, P., DiCerbo, K., Buckingham Shum, S., & Baker, R. S. (2026). Building AI Companions that Prioritise Learning over Performance. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04816. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.04816

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The paper reports, citing a large randomised experiment in high school mathematics (its reference [10], Bastani et al., 2025), that AI access improved in-task problem solving but harmed durable independent learning after removal. It calls the tool only "an AI assistant": it does not name GPT-4 or say access was unrestricted. No effect size is printed in the supplied text.

"students given access to an AI assistant showed improved problem-solving during learning but suffered significant harm to their durable, independent learning once the AI was removed"
