Biology instructors should be open to implementing PS-I approaches in introductory courses¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on Experiment 1, the article recommends that instructors of introductory biology consider problem-solving-first sequencing. The authors state that "biology instructors should be open to implementing PS-I approaches in introductory courses", since low prior-knowledge students benefited on near-transfer problems even though they performed poorly during the initial problem-solving phase. The recommendation is scoped to introductory contexts and near-transfer outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • PS-I implementation should follow productive failure guidelines, including prompts to generate possible solutions and compare sub-problems, followed by explicit instruction

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on near-transfer results only; far-transfer effects were not significant, and Experiment 2 suggests PS-I may hinder learning for students with higher prior knowledge

Target Learners¶

  • introductory undergraduate biology students with low topic-specific prior knowledge

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applying causal mechanistic knowledge of noncovalent interactions to similar problems

Affordances¶

Claims¶

  • For introductory biology students with low topic-specific prior knowledge, problem-solving-first (PS-I) outperformed instruction-first (I-PS) on a near-transfer test [+W]
  • In Experiment 1, I-PS students solved the initial problem better but PS-I students learned more from the subsequent instructional video [+W]
  • In Experiment 2, I-PS students outperformed PS-I students on problem-solving, idea generation, and embedded video quizzes [-W]
  • For biochemistry students with relatively higher topic-specific prior knowledge, instruction-first (I-PS) outperformed problem-solving-first (PS-I) on a near-transfer test [-W]
  • Neither instructional sequence produced significant far-transfer effects in either experiment [~W]
  • Prior knowledge score did not significantly moderate the sequence effect within either experiment, though each experiment covered a restricted prior-knowledge range [~W]
  • The relationship between learners' prior knowledge and the optimal instructional sequence may not be straightforward [~W]
