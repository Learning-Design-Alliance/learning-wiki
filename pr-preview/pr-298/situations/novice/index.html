Designing for novice¶

Expertise · little or no prior knowledge of the target · All situations

141 rows from 131 pages say what to change when the brief's expertise is novice; 123 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Sequencing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are absolute novices and the task's parts interact heavily (several decisions at once) (from the wording) Practise the two or three hardest parts in isolation first, then join them in a whole task within the same week Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition. [~M] (one experiment; the joining step is untested)
Goal is a procedure, novice learners, an immediate test Show a worked example before the first problem, then alternate example and problem Example–problem sequences reduce cognitive load and improve learning outcomes [+M] (secondary-school novices, circuit troubleshooting, immediate test); the advantage was not detected at the final test with identical pairs (identical pairs [~M])
Beginners in a foreign or second language (from the wording) Start with listening comprehension and relax demands for speaking at first; bring reading and writing in after learners understand the spoken forms they will read listening first [+W] (a review reporting Postovsky's experiments second-hand); reading cannot exceed listening [+W] and oral language predicts reading [+W] (both reported second-hand from first-language work, no data printed)

Anchored Instruction¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices on the concept the anchor is meant to carry (from the wording) Make the concept available explicitly (a short explanation or modelled step) before or alongside the work in the anchor Minimal guidance is less effective for novices [-M]
The anchor's problem needs several new interacting ideas at once and learners are complete beginners (from the wording) Isolate one component first, then return to the whole anchor Part-task practice reduces load for absolute novices [~W]

Clear Structure¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice and can build or study a concept map Have them construct a node-link map of the material, or study a prepared one; constructing gave the larger effect Graphic organizers support novice comprehension [+M]
Novices meeting new technical vocabulary in a fast-moving tutorial (from the wording) Add brief definitions, staged progression and checkpoints that test understanding before moving on Definitions, checkpoints and staged pacing improved clarity for novices [+W]

Cognitive Load Management¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
A complex whole task that novices cannot yet coordinate (from the wording) Teach the interacting parts in isolation first, then combine them in the whole task Isolated parts first helped novices on complex tasks [+M]
Novices learning a structured problem type (mathematics, science procedures) (from the wording) Begin with worked examples before unaided problems Worked examples improve mathematics performance [+M]

Cognitive Load Theory¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices on a multi-step procedure (from the wording) Open with a full worked example, not a problem: examples only or example–problem pairs, then problems; use full worked examples rather than completion problems at first Example-first sequences beat problem-first ones for novices on an immediate test, with lower rated effort [+M]; Lower-prior-knowledge learners did better with full than completion examples [+M]
A complex whole task that novices cannot yet coordinate (from the wording) Teach the interacting parts separately first, then the whole task Isolated parts first helped novices on complex tasks [+M]

Problem-Based Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices on the content the problem needs (from the wording) At step 2, teach the content first or alongside (short explanation, worked example, a short text to read before the group discusses the problem) and use the problem to apply it Minimal guidance is less effective for novices [-M]; Elaborating without relevant prior knowledge lowered recall [-M] (one experiment, university students)
Learners are absolute novices and the problem has many interacting parts (from the wording) Before the whole problem, give short practice on its key parts in isolation, then pose the integrated problem Part-task practice helped absolute novices on high-interactivity material [~M] (one experiment, not in PBL)

Simulations & Immersive Virtual Environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task or system Add a 5–10 minute primer on parts and terms; start with a simplified scenario framed in concrete, familiar terms; give a goal and prompts during every run; never begin with free exploration Pretraining learners on a system's key parts and terms before the main lesson improved knowledge and real-world transfer in one VR experiment [+M]; Assimilator learners gained significantly more in the VR (guided exploration) mode [+M] (one experiment; the learners' ages are not stated on the claim page); concrete framing from Concrete-task participants achieved higher performance success than intermediate-task participants in simulation-based inquiry [~W] (first-year students, one experiment)
Learners' prior knowledge of the domain differs (novice vs advanced) Give novices worked examples of an experiment or case before they run; give advanced learners reflection phases after the run; do not rely on prompts alone Prior knowledge moderates scaffolding effectiveness [~M]; Presence versus absence of individual scaffolding types did not support the added-value hypothesis, but scaffolding combinations partly did [~M] (higher education)

Universal Design For Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this content Curate options to two or three; integrate representations rather than offering many in parallel; model each choice before asking for it Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] and Multiple representations improve learning [~M] (benefit depends on skill at connecting representations); bounding the number of options is untested
Learners are adult or professionals, including older beginners or learners with disabilities (from the wording) Ask learners which barriers they meet; keep individual accommodations; offer formats drawn from their own work modifications may be necessary for older beginners and learners with disabilities [~W] (one practitioner's judgment, adult ESL)

4C/ID (Four-Component Instructional Design)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are complete novices and the task has many interacting elements (from the wording) Start with brief practice on isolated elements, then move to the whole task Part-task practice reduces load for novices [+M]

Active Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are meeting the topic for the first time (novice) Give a worked example or short explanation before the task, and scaffolds and feedback during it; do not leave learners to discover the key content unaided Minimal guidance is less effective for novices [~M]

AI Tutoring¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices in the domain (from the wording) Step-based tutoring with contingent hints and worked steps available; a coach or tutor agent rather than open chat Agents as coaches and tutors are most useful with novices [~M]; Guidance that helps novices becomes redundant with expertise [~S]

Always report and interpret effect sizes alongside p values for primary outcomes¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The reader is a teacher, leader or purchaser, novice at statistics, or has low confidence with numbers Lead with one sentence in percentile points or raw-score difference on a test they know, then the d and its interval; keep the p value in a note The guide treats an effect size of +0.25 or higher as substantively important, equivalent to raising performance at least 10 percentile points [+W] (a reporting convention, not tested with readers)

Analogical Reasoning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices in the domain of the cases (from the wording) Use comparison; the experiments recorded here used novice undergraduates (negotiation) and seventh graders (algebra). Supply both cases rather than relying on a source they are assumed to know Analogical reasoning improves transfer [+M]; Comparing cases side by side improves learning and transfer [+M]

Annotating¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the topic and have no criteria for what matters Give the reading purpose and three to five margin codes (?, claim, evidence), mark the first paragraph together, and check that the marks fall on the main claims before readers go on alone Test performance follows whatever is underlined, relevant or not [~M] (67 college freshmen, immediate test); the codes and the check are untested proposals

Assessment for Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices, or the tasks are unfamiliar (from the wording) Train self-assessment with modelling examples before relying on it, and check self-ratings against scored work Self-assessment accuracy is low without training [~M]; Prior knowledge needed for accurate self-assessment [~M]

Authentic Assessment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the domain (first course, first placement) Spend the first half of the unit on component practice with worked examples; use a simpler, well-structured version of the task for the first draft; plan two revision cycles; give feedback on each component before the whole task. Guided rather than unassisted discovery [~S]; its studies did not use authentic assessment tasks, and the meta-analysis does not break results down by prior knowledge

Authentic Audiences & Purposes¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice writers who write as if for the teacher Add an audience-analysis step before drafting: who will read it, what they already know, and therefore what background must be given Audience prior knowledge determines information density [+W] (an argument); Skilled EFL writers keep readers in mind [+W] (nine skilled writers, no comparison)

Autonomy¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are new to the task (novice) and the brief asks for wide choice Offer two or three options that target the same objective, model how to choose, and widen the options as unsupported performance improves; state process goals (the steps to attend to) early, outcome goals once the skill holds bounded choice is an untested proposal, bounded by connectivist courses [~M]; Process goals beat outcome goals for novices [~M] (assigned goals, carried to choice untested)

Balanced pronunciation curriculum covering suprasegmentals, segmentals, and fluency, with a short list of central features selected by diagnosis¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are beginners (novice) with limited vocabulary Start with segmentals and word stress on words they already know; use ASR read-aloud for segmentals; keep communicative tasks short and scripted at first. Fluspeak [~W] (good results for beginners on phonemes; one program, reported second-hand); CAPT [~S]

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice for this relation Open with one worked example or short explanation; then a single, narrow "why" prompt per step; give a model answer after each attempt; no open discovery task first. Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction [~S]

Cognitive Disequilibrium¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Novices meet the problem before any instruction, with no support (from the wording) Add scaffolding to the attempt so that confusion and surprise lead somewhere; unscaffolded attempts produced more confusion, and emotions related to transfer differently by condition Emotion dynamics predict learning in a way that depends on scaffolding [~M]

Cognitive Flexibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Expertise novice in the domain, high element interactivity Start with one case and part tasks; add the second and third case only after the simple version is completed unaided; prompt the named dimension of comparison. Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition. [~M]; population not reported on the claim page

Cognitive Styles¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task, or advanced Novice: give the structure or worked model to all, regardless of style. Advanced: drop the frame and check the approach on one unaided task; prior knowledge, not style, sets how much guidance to give Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~S] (cognitive-load studies; does not address styles)

Coherence Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the content, and the explanation has many interacting parts Apply the audit at full strength, and expect it to matter most here Design principle effects moderated by pacing and complexity [~M] (g = 0.70 complex, 0.20 simple, across principles); Multimedia principles benefit novices [~M]

Collaborative Evaluation¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the domain (cannot yet tell strong from weak work unaided) Use anchor exemplars with known quality, three or four criteria with described levels, teacher modelling each session, and the reference comparison after every round; keep criteria to what learners can see in the object. Delay judging classmates' work until alone judgments of anchors are within a level. Prior knowledge and self-assessment [~M] and self-assessment accuracy [~M], both about judging one's own performance, carried to judging others'

Combine intentional and incidental vocabulary learning rather than relying on incidental learning alone¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the language (beginners, below about the first thousand words) Lead with deliberate study (about two thirds of vocabulary time); use graded or simplified texts with most unknown words glossed; check coverage on a 100-word sample before any text Correct word inference in reading requires knowing roughly 95-98% of surrounding words… [+M] (second-hand, one review); ratio untested

Competency-Based Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices on the task being judged (from the wording) Frame early attempts and feedback around the process criteria (the steps and strategy), and judge against the outcome threshold once the process is in place; withdraw prompts across attempts before the judgement that counts Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices [~M] (high-school writers learning one revision skill; a goal-setting finding carried to assessment criteria); Fading support transfers responsibility [~M]

Concept Attainment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the domain (cannot yet name the parts of an instance) Teach what the instances are first; keep two or three candidate attributes and give the checklist; use only clear exemplars until the rule is stable; give feedback on each classification and state the rule soon after the induction rather than waiting for the class to find it. Guided against unguided discovery [~S]; the meta-analysis does not break results down by prior knowledge

Concreteness Fading¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices who cannot yet say what the symbols mean (from the wording) Use all three stages, with the links in steps 3–4 Fading condition outperformed generic and concrete conditions [+W]; Manipulatives require connection to concept [+M]

Considering The Opposite¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the domain, or their alternatives are implausible Supply the alternative (a rival explanation, a second case, the common misconception) and ask learners to make its best case, side by side with their own; drop to self-generated alternatives only when they meet the threshold in the arc above Comparing cases side by side improves learning [+M] (carried from case comparison; not a test of this move); expertise reversal [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; the reverse case)

Constructive Alignment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the assessed tasks Add a worked or modelled example and at least one practised attempt with feedback per outcome before the graded task; show an exemplar against the criteria. Practice presence [+M] for including practice (undergraduates, one computer-based lesson); the per-outcome dose and exemplar are untested proposals

Contiguity Principle (Spatial and Temporal)¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Novice learners; a diagram with a separate legend, or text on another page or screen (from the wording) Put labels on the diagram parts and the text beside the region it explains, all on one page or screen Split attention degrades learning [+M] (g = 0.63, 58 comparisons)

Criterion- and Norm-Referenced Testing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The decision is what to teach next for novice learners early in a domain Use short criterion-referenced checks on the first skills in the teaching order, three to five items per skill; report mastered / not yet per skill; no percentiles Items should represent essential skills in a teaching hierarchy [+W] (an argument about reading tests, carried to other domains)

Cultural & Life Experiences Connections¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the content and have little experience related to it Teach the concept explicitly first, with one familiar context as the example; ask for learners' own contexts only after they can explain the concept once Advance organizers aided learning in a 1980 meta-analysis, but activating prior topic knowledge did not improve primary pupils' text comprehension in one experiment [~M] (carried from topic-knowledge activation in reading to cultural connection)

Debriefing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task (first simulations, first placements) Facilitator-led throughout; give the criterion or a model performance before analysis; ask about cues and steps, not outcomes; name what the expert would have noticed; one change each, stated as an action Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals. [+M] (high-school writing revision, carried to debrief focus); Self Assessment Accuracy Is Low Without Training [~M]

Deliberate Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this task, at first exposure Do not isolate components yet: model the whole task, use guided practice with support, and set process goals; start targeted refinement only once the learner can perform unaided Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies, carried to skill refinement); process goals for novices [~M]

Develop Understanding¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this idea Keep steps 2–3 in full: explain first, two or three worked cases with explanation prompts and sentence starters before any case learners work alone; give hints in response to their explanations. Explicit instruction against unassisted discovery [+S]; the meta-analysis does not break results down by prior knowledge

Digital Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task Use items close to worked examples, the full cue–hint–worked-step ladder, an explanation prompt every three to five items, and feedback on every item Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M] (one-to-one tutoring, carried to software)

Discussing Race¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the topic, or describe their subject as neutral and see race as irrelevant to it Start from a shared data set or case in the subject itself where outcomes differ by race, and ask what explains the difference; do not open with identity or experience. Name race plainly when the data show it, even if learners call it culture or background; some learners will judge the connection inappropriate, so record that as an observation and keep naming it in the analysis BIPOC students perceived statistics as a neutral subject and did not discern explicit racial or cultural inclusion in the courseware, generally viewing that neutrality as appropriate [~W] (undergraduate statistics interviews; a limit on, not a test of, the move)

Dual Coding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this content Supply a complete, labelled visual first, with linking prompts; then ask for a drawing of named parts and relations, keeping a partly completed diagram for a learner who cannot start Graphic organizers for novices [~M] (lower-ability learners benefited from maps, higher-ability did not, in small, low-powered subsets)

Educators should act as mediators and facilitators of learning rather than content disseminators¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the target and cannot begin a representative task Keep a short explanation and one worked example before the task; give prompts and feedback during it; do not leave the key idea to be found Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction [~S] (its table counts 13 distinct studies)

Elaboration Theory¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice and the content is high in element interactivity Keep the epitome very short (two or three ideas); pre-teach the one or two elements it depends on in isolation, then present the epitome; make each level small and end it with a learner-built synthesis. Isolated parts first, for novices [~M] (one experiment; sample and horizon not reported on the claim page)

Elaborative Reasoning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on a calculation, rule or procedure Explain it or show a worked example first; put prompts on the steps of the example; do not replace a second reading with why-prompts on a lesson met cold Elaborative interrogation produced lower posttest accuracy than reading the lesson twice in a written lesson on posterior probability [-M] (undergraduates, one written lesson)

Emergent Curriculum¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the content the thread leads into Keep the topic theirs but teach the content inside it explicitly: short demonstrations, worked examples or a visitor's explanation at the point learners need them, with feedback on what they make. Unassisted discovery [~S]: 5 distinct studies in its table; carried from discovery and inquiry tasks to an interest-led thread

Employ flexible error treatment strategies matched to objectives, competence, affect, and correction effectiveness¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice with the form, or low proficiency Give the correct form plus a one-line rule; avoid bare codes, underlines or rising-intonation prompts until the form has been taught Direct and metalinguistic feedback with explanations beat control at the immediate posttest; code-only did not [+M] (low-intermediate Korean EFL learners, written feedback, four sessions); code-only caught up two weeks later [~M], so the gap is in the short term

Engagement¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task and withdraw after early failure Shorten steps, give a worked example first, set process goals; do not add hooks or rewards Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals. [~M] (high-school writing revision); the step-size move is untested

Epistemic Games¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice to the practice Open each session with a mentor modelling one decision aloud; one decision type per early session; feedback with the reason after every decision; fade prompts only when a learner justifies unprompted. Guided against unguided discovery [~S], carried from classroom and laboratory studies to games

Experience with Languaging Activities (ELAs) guided by a sequence of experience-based questions¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the content, or the text is hard for them Give the meaning of key terms and the gist first; make step 2 a checked account (compare with the text) rather than free recall; give a stem for every step for longer (sessions 1–5); correct each "learned" answer against the named idea. Activation [~S] and activation instructions [~M]: topic activation alone did not help (primary pupils; one other experiment), carried to this routine

Experiential Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novices on the target task Teach or demonstrate the key idea first, with a worked example; narrow the experience to one or two variables; supply the reflection prompts and the vocabulary for naming the concept; check an unaided step before removing support Unassisted discovery produces less learning than explicit instruction, while discovery enhanced with guidance outperforms other instruction [~S]; tested on discovery tasks, school and mixed domains, not on placements

Experiential Learning Cycle¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the target task Demonstrate the first loop's task with a worked example before the experience; let one or two variables vary; supply the debrief prompts and the vocabulary for stating the principle; check each learner's rule before the retry; keep this support for at least the first two loops. Guided against unassisted discovery [~S]; discovery tasks with mostly school learners and mixed domains, not placements or simulations; the meta-analysis does not break results down by prior knowledge

Explicit Instruction: Vocabulary¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are second-language learners, novice in the language Gloss unfamiliar words in the text (first-language or picture glosses), teach the most frequent words first, and do not rely on inference from context until most surrounding words are known Glossing yields medium-to-large positive effects on L2 vocabulary learning… [+W] (second-hand report of meta-analyses); Correct word inference in reading requires knowing roughly 95–98% of surrounding words… [~W] (second-hand); Proficiency moderates gloss effects… [~M] (one EFL study: benefits at middle proficiency levels only); first-language glosses: All three L1 gloss conditions significantly improve immediate vocabulary recall… [+M] and, from the same study of 135 Taiwanese undergraduates, only the gloss-with-L2-example and in-text gloss forms held on all three recall tests (Only L1-gloss-L2-ex and L1-in-text-gloss conditions… [~M]), so prefer those to marginal glosses where retention matters

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task Run the loop for them: task-level information on every attempt, a matched retry at once, the step re-modelled after two failed cycles Contingent scaffolding [+M] (children, one-to-one, long division)

Fostering Communities of Learning (FCL)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the domain Teach the core ideas directly before research; give each research group a short, pre-selected source pack and a note frame; check each group's understanding before jigsaw. Guided against unguided discovery [~S]: explicit instruction beat unassisted discovery, guided discovery beat other instruction; the meta-analysis does not break results down by prior knowledge, and is not of this pattern

Free-Choice Learning Environment Design¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice in the topic, or have low confidence or low literacy Keep the first action wordless; narrow the choice to two or three challenges at first; offer a worked starter project to copy and change; let a facilitator or peer start alongside Moderate agency in game-based learning [~W] (second-hand report of one game study: moderate agency beat none and high); connectivist courses [~W] (open online courses, second-hand: not all could direct themselves). Both carried to this setting

Game-based Learning¶
