Working Memory¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 68 claims (43 show a difference, 15 mixed, 8 no difference, 2 unmarked) · 59 studies (17 causal, 16 associational, 14 review, 7 quant-synthesis, 3 qualitative, 2 theoretical), q1–q4 · 10 of 59 report an effect size · 63 claims rest on one study

Description¶

How much a learner can hold and manipulate at once. It is the best-evidenced dimension here and the most directly actionable: it converts into element counts per screen, whether two sources must be read together, and whether a problem is given whole or as a worked example. Capacity is near-fixed, but effective capacity is not — prior knowledge supplies the schemas that let several items be held as one, which is why the same screen overloads a novice and not an expert [~S].

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Composite working memory r = .38, working memory .35 (k = 829); numerical .34, visual-spatial .31, verbal .30. Claim

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Sets how many elements a screen may hold at once, and settles split-attention and worked-example decisions.
  • Load is a property of the material and the learner together, never of the material alone — which is why a load rating belongs to a placement rather than to a component.
  • The remedies reverse with expertise: support that helps a novice becomes redundant and then harmful [~S].

Target Learners¶

  • Novices in the domain, who lack the schemas to chunk and are most exposed to overload.
  • Learners with attention or language demands already consuming the same resource.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Affects acquisition of anything with interacting parts — procedures, multi-step problems, integrated diagrams.
  • Barely affects tasks whose elements can be learned one at a time, which is why load-reduction is not a universal good.

The evidence by question¶

  • Do learners with lower working memory capacity gain more, or less, from retrieval practice, interleaving and other desirable difficulties? Studies disagree on whether low or high working memory learners benefit more from retrieval practice, and several find no relationship [~M]
  • Can training or practice raise working memory itself, and by how much? CCT yields small significant gains in nonverbal memory, working memory, and visuospatial skills, but only weak evidence for verbal memory [+M]

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Prior knowledge — supplies the schemas that raise effective capacity; the two are inseparable in practice.
  • Attention — a different bottleneck often mistaken for this one: attention is what reaches the store, capacity is what it holds.
  • Reading and language — decoding that is not automatic spends this budget before comprehension starts.

Examples¶

  • Cognitive Load Management — the element that names the design response
  • Worked Examples — substitutes studying a solution for searching for one
  • Just-in-Time Information — defers detail until the moment it is needed rather than front-loading it
  • Chunking to Reduce Working Memory Load — the direct application
  • Segmenting — breaks a continuous presentation into learner-paced parts
  • Faded Worked Examples — withdraws the support as expertise makes it redundant
  • 4C/ID (Four-Component Instructional Design) — sequences whole tasks simple-to-complex against entry capacity
  • Cognitive Load Theory — the explanatory frame for all of the above
  • Expertise Reversal Effect — why every remedy here has an expiry date
