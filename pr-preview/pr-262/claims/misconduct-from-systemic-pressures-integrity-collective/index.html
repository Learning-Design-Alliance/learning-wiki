Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix 2026¶

Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix. (2026). Purpose before policy: academic integrity, generative AI, and rhetorical stance. Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-026-01706-1

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Conceptual argument with historical illustration: the authors cite late Ming dynasty exam bribery despite capital punishment to show misconduct has persisted under extreme penalties. They draw on ICAI's six core principles of integrity, including honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility, and courage.

"What persists is not inherent dishonesty but environments that reward performance over learning. Audit cultures, competitive rankings, high-stakes assessments, financial precarity, and massified classrooms intersect with internal pressures"

Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix 2026 (2)¶

Tristan B. Taylor & Travis LaCroix. (2026). Purpose before policy: academic integrity, generative AI, and rhetorical stance. Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-026-01706-1

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Definitional/conceptual claim in the integrity section, citing Balalle & Pannilage (2025), that integrity concerns positive epistemic commitments such as sincerity, diligence, accuracy, and justification, and the collective conditions enabling reliable knowledge production. Theoretical, with no empirical test in this article.

"Integrity cannot be achieved individually; it is a collective endeavour embedded within institutional and social systems (Balalle & Pannilage, 2025)."
