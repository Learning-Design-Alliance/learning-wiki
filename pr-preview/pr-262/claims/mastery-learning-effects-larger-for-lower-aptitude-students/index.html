Evidence¶

Kulik et al. 1990¶

Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 60(2), 265–299. doi:10.3102/00346543060002265

q4 · meta-analysis (13 studies reporting effects by aptitude) · i2 · medium effect, ES = 0.61 SD low-aptitude vs 0.40 SD high-aptitude · n=13 studies · quant-synthesis · r?

Thirteen evaluations reported examination effects separately for students of different aptitude. In 9 of them the effect was stronger for less able students; on average low-aptitude students gained 0.61 SD and high-aptitude students 0.40 SD, a difference the authors report as not significant. Separately, in 37 of the 52 studies reporting score spread, final examination scores varied less in mastery classes (on average 77% of the control group's variation), and the correlation between aptitude and achievement was lower.

"The average effect size in all 13 studies was also higher for the less able students (M = 0.61) than for the more able ones (M = 0.40). The difference, however, is not significant (t = 1.23, p > .10)."

Discussion¶

Both groups gain, so mastery gating does not trade the stronger students' learning for the weaker students'. The aptitude difference itself is not significant in 13 studies, so the narrowing of score spread is the firmer of the two results.
