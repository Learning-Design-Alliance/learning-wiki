OLI cost structure: low or no cost courses with per-student maintenance fees for credit delivery¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 9 studies (7 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 3 of 9 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report details OLI's cost components for implementers. OLI "does not incur any personnel costs beyond those normally associated with teaching a course"; most courses are free to independent learners, though a few charge a $10 maintenance fee. For credit-bearing delivery, "Most OLI courses delivered for credit will charge students a maintenance fee of $25, with fees ranging from $10 to $80 per student." Development was funded by the Hewlett, Gates, Kresge, Lumina, NSF, Spencer, and Walter S. Johnson foundations, with ongoing support from Carnegie Mellon University.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • All OLI courses require internet access and an up-to-date web browser.

Constraints¶

  • Hybrid-format delivery requires physical space to accommodate periodic face-to-face instruction; students remain subject to their college's tuition fees.

Target Learners¶

  • postsecondary students
  • independent learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • access to low- or no-cost online college coursework

Claims¶

  • OLI has mixed effects on academic achievement among postsecondary students [~M]
  • OLI has potentially positive effects on credit accumulation and persistence among postsecondary students [+M]
  • Only three of twelve identified OLI studies met WWC group design standards [~W]
  • OLI statistics students completed the semester's material in approximately half the regularly scheduled time [+W]
  • OLI evidence base covers community college and postsecondary students across diverse racial and ethnic groups [+W]
  • Suh et al. (2019) found a positive but non-significant effect on course passing rate (effect size 0.80) and a negative non-significant effect on final exam score [~W]
