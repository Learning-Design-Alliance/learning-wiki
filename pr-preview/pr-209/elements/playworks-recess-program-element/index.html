Playworks school-based recess program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Playworks is a school-based program that guides students in organized activities during recess and makes improvements to the recess play yard. In the evaluation described here, it was delivered for one school year in schools randomly assigned to receive it, and its effects on boys' and girls' physical activity were measured with accelerometers and recess observations. The article describes it as one of the "School-based programs, such as Playworks" that might increase physical activity, especially for girls.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implementation during recess over a school year, with organized activities and play-yard improvements

Constraints¶

  • Effects on accelerometer-measured activity were significant for girls but not for boys in the trial

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • physical activity during recess

Claims¶

  • Playworks showed significant impact on some measures of girls' physical activity but no significant impact on measures of boys' physical activity [+M]
  • Girls in Playworks schools had significantly higher accelerometer intensity counts and spent more time in vigorous physical activity than girls in control schools [+M]
  • Girls in Playworks schools were less likely to be sedentary and more likely to engage in jumping, tag, and playground games during recess [+M]
  • No significant accelerometer-based differences in physical activity were found for boys in Playworks schools compared with control schools [~M]
  • In a randomized controlled trial, children in Playworks schools spent more time in vigorous physical activity at recess than students at non-Playworks schools, and more teachers in Playworks schools reported students engaged in intense recess activity [+M]
