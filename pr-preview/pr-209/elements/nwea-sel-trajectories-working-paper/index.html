NWEA working paper on SEL trajectories and 9th-grade off-track identification¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A NWEA working paper and companion research brief by James Soland and Megan Kuhfeld examining whether middle school SEL skills predict being off-track to graduate high school. The page lists it under topics including “Social-emotional learning” and “High school,” and summarizes that the study examined SEL stability and the relation of 6th-grade SEL level and growth to successful transition to secondary school.

Claims¶

  • Middle school social-emotional learning skills predict being off-track to graduate high school [+W]
  • Students' 6th-grade SEL level and SEL growth from 6th to 8th grade relate to successful transition to secondary school [+W]
  • Growth in self-management between 6th and 8th grade predicts being off track academically in 9th grade above and beyond 6th-grade self-management status [+M]
  • Understanding how a student develops social-emotionally over intermediate school can improve identification of students not on track to succeed in high school [+M]
  • In a large urban district, 6th-grade SEL status is more frequently a significant predictor of being off track academically in 9th grade than SEL growth between 6th and 8th grade [+M]
  • Whether students' SEL skills are stable over time, and whether initial level or change better flags later academic risk, was largely unknown before this study [+W]
  • Successful transition from intermediate to secondary school depends on both academic preparation and social-emotional learning skills suited to a more independent environment [+W]
  • Successfully transitioning from intermediate school to secondary school is pivotal for students to remain on track to graduate [+W]
