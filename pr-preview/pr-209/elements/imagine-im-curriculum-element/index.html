Imagine IM: an Illustrative Mathematics-certified print and digital problem-based K-12 core math curriculum¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Imagine IM is "an Illustrative Mathematics–certified print and digital math program delivering the core K–12 curriculum, enhanced with additional features." It is a problem-based core curriculum designed for face-to-face, student-led, whole group instruction, aiming to provide conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, strategic competence, and adaptive reasoning. Students learn by doing, working through problems in mathematical and real-world contexts and constructing arguments using precise language.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Each implementing classroom needs one instructional teacher; no additional staffing is required.

Constraints¶

  • Requires networked computers with mice or tablets and access to the digital platform, though print materials can be purchased by the district instead.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in elementary grades K-5 in the evaluated studies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematics achievement, conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, strategic competence, adaptive reasoning

Claims¶

  • Imagine IM shows consistent positive impacts on student math achievement across three prospective quasi-experimental studies, with an average effect size of +0.11 across four studies [+W]
  • In a small urban district in Iowa with over 4,400 students in grades K-5, Imagine IM showed directionally positive impacts on FastBridge earlyMath (+0.10) and aMath (+0.04), with significant subgroup effects and no meaningful differences on the state math assessment [+W]
  • In a Maryland rural fringe district study of over 700 students in grades K-5, Imagine IM showed significant impacts on Acadience Math (+0.26) and Grade 2 NWEA MAP Growth Math (+0.39), with positive but non-significant effects on the state MCAP assessment [+W]
  • A small retrospective matched study of 502 students in grades K-4 found positive impacts on Acadience (+0.13) and Grade 2 MAP Growth (+0.42), with no differences for 4th grade students on a state math assessment [+W]
  • In a matched suburban-district study, Imagine IM showed no differences on GCAS for grades 3-5 but significant positive impacts on the MAP (effect size = +0.38), strongest for students with disabilities and economically disadvantaged students [+W]
