NLTS 2012 dataset and design documentation¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The National Longitudinal Transition Study (NLTS) 2012 is a national dataset on youth with disabilities receiving services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, sponsored by the U.S. Department of Education. The accompanying design documentation "describes how the NLTS 2012 data were constructed and prepared for analyses," covering "the sample design, data collection instruments and procedures, data preparation, weights, and nonresponse bias analyses." It supports secondary analysis of the experiences and outcomes of students with disabilities in transition to adulthood.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Users should consult the design documentation on sample design, weights, and nonresponse bias analyses before analyzing the data

Constraints¶

  • The dataset covers youth with disabilities receiving services under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act, as defined by the study's sample design

Target Learners¶

  • youth with disabilities receiving services under IDEA

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding the experiences, services, and transition outcomes of students with disabilities

Claims¶

  • Youth with an IEP are more likely than their peers to be socioeconomically disadvantaged and to face problems with health, communication, and completing typical tasks independently [+W]
  • Youth with an IEP experience bullying and suspension at higher rates and are less engaged in school and social activities than peers, though most youth with and without an IEP feel positive about school [+W]
  • Youth with an IEP are more likely than youth without an IEP to struggle academically, yet less likely to receive some forms of school-based support [+W]
  • English learner students with disabilities are less likely than other students with disabilities to receive extra time on schoolwork but attend transition-planning meetings at a comparable rate, are suspended less, and have parents with higher expectations [+W]
  • Youth with an IEP lag their peers in planning and taking steps to obtain postsecondary education and jobs [+W]
  • English learner students with disabilities are more likely to struggle academically and less likely to participate in activities than both other students with disabilities and other English learners [+W]
  • Participation in key transition activities, including discussing transition plans with school staff and student employment during high school, declined over the decade [+W]
