Dynamic Learning Project (DLP) coaching model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The DLP is a school-based instructional technology coaching program that placed one site-based, full-time coach in each of 50 underserved schools for one year. Coaches ran "four eight-week coaching cycles" in which teachers selected, tackled, and reflected on classroom challenges, supported by classroom modeling, co-teaching, and observations. Coaches and principals received mentoring, a Summer and Winter Institute, and membership in a Professional Learning Network. In the pilot year more than 1,110 of 2,250 teachers participated.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • District and school buy-in: principals must support program fidelity, protect coach time, and ensure the coach does not play an evaluative role; districts fund and sustain the coach position.

Constraints¶

  • The report notes that measuring improvement in student achievement and attributing it to a coaching intervention is complicated from a research perspective, and funding for coach positions is a sustainability challenge in resource-strapped districts.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers in underserved, low-income schools
  • instructional technology coaches
  • school principals

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Powerful use of technology for student collaboration, creativity, communication, critical thinking, and agency
  • teacher integration of technology into content and pedagogy

Claims¶

  • Coaches reported significantly greater confidence in their coaching skills by year end, rising from 62 to 89 percent rating themselves high or extremely high [+W]
  • DLP teachers reported significant increases in using technology for both content and pedagogy, but no significant difference from non-DLP teachers in basic technology skills [+W]
  • Teachers coached for more than one cycle reported more powerful technology use and greater confidence, though a breadth-versus-depth tradeoff remains open [+W]
  • Principals' confidence in leadership skills related to instructional coaching rose from 60 to 95 percent over the pilot year [+W]
  • Teachers reported increasing encouragement of risk-taking while coaches were less confident teachers actually took risks [+W]
  • After one year of DLP coaching, coached teachers reported more frequent and more powerful technology use than non-participating colleagues [+W]
  • DLP participants reported more frequent meaningful coaching activities than other coaching programs, but fewer opportunities to observe other teachers [+W]
  • Dynamic Learning Project research found teachers who worked with edtech coaches used technology more frequently and felt more confident than peers who did not [+W]
  • DLP-coached teachers reported greater increases in frequency of technology use than non-DLP teachers [+W]
