MATHia intelligent tutoring system with adaptive mastery-based instruction¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

MATHia is Carnegie Learning's intelligent tutoring system for mathematics, developed over 25 years ago at Carnegie Mellon University. It employs machine learning algorithms to analyze students' problem-solving step by step, identifying misconceptions and gaps and responding with feedback, adapting instruction in real time based on "knowledge components" or granular representations of learning goals. Students work through topics at their own pace to achieve mastery. It is used by over 700,000 students in over 2000 schools in the United States.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Well-instrumented data logging so student transactions can be collected, stored, and exported for analysis.

Constraints¶

  • Adaptive pacing means students reach target content at different times, which traditional experiment designs handle poorly; UpGrade enrollment-on-encounter addresses this.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 mathematics students in the United States

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mathematics skill mastery and personalized problem-solving support

Claims¶

  • Localizing word-problem character names improved MATHia performance only for specific subgroup-majority schools, with no improvement in sense of belonging overall [+W]
  • Rewriting MATHia word problems for struggling readers, by human experts or LLMs, sped completion by 30% and improved mastery rate [+W]
