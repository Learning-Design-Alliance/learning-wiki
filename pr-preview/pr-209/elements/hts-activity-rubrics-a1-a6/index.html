Historical Thinking Skills Activity Rubrics (A1-A6) for assessing learning opportunities in world history assignments¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A set of six rubric dimensions (A1-A6) meant to study the extent to which high school world history classroom activities provide opportunities for students to engage in historical thinking practices. Per the article, "EacK GLPHQVLrq KaV IRXU OHYHOV (0-3)", where 0 generally indicates absence of a historical thinking practice in the activity, 1 emergence, 2 partial presence, and 3 solid presence. The dimensions cover historical argumentation claims and evidence, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing. The rubric was piloted with three expert world history teachers scoring 19 assignments.

Target Learners¶

  • High school world history students in on-level or pre-AP 9th/10th grade classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Historical thinking skills including argumentation, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing

Affordances¶

  • Historical Thinking Skills Six Dimension Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Trained scorers applied the historical thinking skills rubrics with good overall inter-rater consistency [+W]
  • Two rubric dimensions (claims and evidence, comparison) showed low inter-scorer consistency in the pilot [-W]
  • Scorers found all rubric dimensions important, with feedback bearing mainly on the scoring and training process rather than rubric design [+W]
  • Scorers had difficulty distinguishing rubric scoring from everyday grading and AP scoring, and training took longer than expected [~W]
  • World History Project assignments scored higher on most learning opportunity rubric dimensions than other assignments [+W]
