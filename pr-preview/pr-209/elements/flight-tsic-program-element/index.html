FLIGHT program (Take Stock in Children®)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

FLIGHT is a Take Stock in Children® program providing "mentoring, scholarships, and additional support services" to help disadvantaged middle and high school students stay on track for graduation, apply to college, and enroll in college. Students receive individualized assistance and group workshops during the school day from volunteer mentors, student advocates, and, in one study site, College Enrollment and Retention Specialists. Eligible students can participate for up to eight years, from grade 6 through the first three semesters of college, and must meet criteria including free or reduced-price lunch eligibility, a minimum 2.0 GPA, grade-level performance in reading and math, and limited suspensions and absences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must meet eligibility criteria including free or reduced-price lunch eligibility, minimum 2.0 GPA with no Ds or Fs the prior year, grade-level reading and math performance, limited suspensions and no more than 10 unexcused absences, and public school attendance; parents must agree to adhere to TSIC's® policies.

Constraints¶

  • The effects of FLIGHT are not known for middle school outcomes, college readiness, staying in or completing high school, college outcomes, degree attainment, or labor market success.

Target Learners¶

  • low-income middle and high school students in public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • staying on track for high school graduation
  • applying to college
  • enrolling and succeeding in college

Claims¶

  • The supporting evidence for FLIGHT comes from a single randomized controlled trial with compromised random assignment [~W]
  • FLIGHT shows no discernible effects on high school attendance [-W]
  • FLIGHT shows no discernible effects on general high school academic achievement (GPA) [-W]
  • FLIGHT may increase college enrollment among disadvantaged students, with 97% of the FLIGHT group versus 83% of the comparison group enrolling in college [+W]
