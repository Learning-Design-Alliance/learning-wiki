Eight research-recommended online instructional practices measured in the Suddenly Online survey¶

Element · All elements Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The survey measured whether each student's post-COVID course used "eight instructional practices identified through past research as contributing to more effective online teaching and learning": reflective assignments about what was learned and still needed, breaking activities into shorter pieces, frequent quizzes or assessments, live question-and-discussion sessions, breakout groups, personal messages to individual students, real-world examples, and group project work outside meetings. The count of practices present in a course predicted satisfaction, and the report calls the practices students identified "a good starting point" for future instructional guidance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Practices must be incorporated into the online course design; the survey tabulated which of the eight each course included

Constraints¶

  • Measured in courses moved online with little preparation in spring 2020; the association with satisfaction is observational

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate students in courses moved fully online during COVID-19

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Maintaining course satisfaction and motivation in online instruction
