Montevidisco conversational simulator¶

Element · All elements Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Montevidisco, conceptualized by Edward Schneider and Junius Bennion, is an interactive video program simulating a visit to a Mexican village in which the student is the main protagonist, encountering tour guides, hotel clerks, and market salespeople and responding to questions and situations in context. The student selects from on-screen options and speaks the response, which is recorded for teacher or proctor evaluation; the tour guide then has one of four possible answers directing the student to different branches, each with further branches. The adventure game element is intended to motivate students to explore the town and be exposed to a great deal of native Spanish.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The student must respond aloud in context; responses are recorded for a teacher or proctor to evaluate.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the student could spend many hours exploring Montevidisco and never see all of the branches.

Target Learners¶

  • second language learners of Spanish

Target Learning Goals¶

  • conversational language use in simulated social situations
  • exploring language and culture in context
