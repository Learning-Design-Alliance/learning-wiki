Mathematical Imagery Trainer for Proportion (MIT-P): an embodied-interaction environment where the screen turns green only when cursor heights relate by a target ratio¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The MIT-P is a design solution to students' enduring challenges with proportional relations: a student sits before a large red screen and is asked to 'make the screen green', which occurs only when the cursors' heights relate by the correct ratio (e.g., 1:2). The activity advances through stages, each launched when the instructor introduces a new display overlay — cursors, a grid, numerals along the y-axis — after the student satisfies a protocol criterion, with a full design including a ratio table for indirect control. An iPad version was released at the printed URL.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A facilitator who incrementally overlays display elements after protocol criteria are met, and tasks sequenced from making green to maintaining green while moving

Constraints¶

  • Analyses reported in the article treat only the 1:2 ratio item with Grade 4–6 participants

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 4–6 mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • proportional equivalence

Affordances¶

  • Ecological Dynamics Mathematics Learning Theory

Claims¶

  • Eye-tracking results confirm that just before students articulate a new manipulation strategy, their visual attention shifts from figural screen elements to a newly invented attentional-anchor location [+W]
  • Overlaying a grid onto the MIT-P display reifies the attentional anchor and abruptly shifts students into a new unit-based interaction routine [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Tutor–student co-enactment: distribute or co-operate the control devices, negotiate leadership silently, and progressively hand over agency until the student solo-enacts
