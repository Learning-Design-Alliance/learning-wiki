Compact Writing Assignments: student-created diverse writing formats for social studies¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Compact Writing Assignments (CWAs) are student-created writing activities piloted in Sunnyside classrooms that "introduc[e] diverse writing formats including script writing, text exchange, comic book, and response to student-driven questions." They were designed to build writing confidence, engage students in peer-to-peer feedback, and provide broader opportunities for written expression. A teacher noted that knowing the CWAs were created by students "created more buy-in, because they knew a student who knows their community was involved."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Writing topics and formats must be relevant and interesting to students
  • The student authorship of the assignments must be made visible to participating students

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • disengaged secondary students, particularly ninth-grade social studies students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing confidence
  • written expression
  • peer-to-peer feedback
  • engagement in social studies research and writing

Claims¶

  • Sunnyside students participating in the Compact Writing Assignments pilot completed quarterly research papers at a rate more than 15 percent higher than non-participants [+W]
  • In Pilot District 3, majorities of students agreed that Compact Writing Assignments gave them topic choice and that autonomy and varied expression formats improved engagement and writing quality [+W]
