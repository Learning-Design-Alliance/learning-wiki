MAP Growth Goal Explorer: a tool for setting fall-to-spring growth goals against academic benchmarks¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The MAP Growth Goal Explorer is a tool "designed to support and simplify the goal-setting process by showing a range of possible fall-to-spring growth goals against the backdrop of important academic benchmarks." Users enter grade, state, fall RIT score, and subject, and the tool displays Average Growth, Aspirational Growth, and Custom Growth options, with projected spring scores compared against the national achievement average and state proficiency cut scores. It is intended for teachers, families, and students working together.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires the student's grade, state, fall RIT score, and test subject (MAP Growth K–12 math or reading) as inputs
  • State proficiency benchmarks require a published linking study between MAP Growth and the state summative assessment

Constraints¶

  • Should only be used with RIT scores from MAP Growth K–12 reading and mathematics assessments, not course-specific math scores
  • College-readiness benchmarking is not yet included; a future update awaits an updated MAP Growth/ACT linking study
  • Uses a subset of full growth norms with instructional weeks fixed at default values, so projections may differ slightly from other MAP reports

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students in MAP Growth math and reading

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Setting realistic and meaningful fall-to-spring academic growth goals

Claims¶

  • Aspirational Growth defaults to 60th-percentile growth (met by about 40% of similar students) and is selectable from the 55th to 95th percentile in 5-percentile increments [+W]
  • Average growth maintains rather than improves a student's achievement percentile; growth must exceed average for a student to gain ground [+W]
  • Fall-to-spring growth rates in high school grades, particularly in reading, are often close to zero and can be negative [~W]
  • Pursuing unrealistic goals can lead to discouragement and frustration, so goals should balance rigor with realism [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • MAP Growth Goal Explorer: data visualization supporting fall-to-spring growth goal setting
  • NWEA College Explorer tool linking MAP Growth scores to higher-education benchmarks

Examples¶

  • Set growth goals collaboratively so they balance meaningfulness and realism
  • A three-part goal-setting roadmap: collaborate on goals, establish autonomous activities, and check in regularly
  • Use score-linked college exploration alongside goal setting and parent conferences to build post-secondary plans
