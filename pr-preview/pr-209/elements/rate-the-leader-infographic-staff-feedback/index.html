Rate the Leader infographic on staff feedback in principal evaluation¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A published infographic from the Regional Educational Laboratory Mid-Atlantic, released by the Institute of Education Sciences, that "discusses the use of staff feedback in evaluating school leaders' performance based on evidence from teacher surveys." It is a communication artifact summarizing evidence for practitioners considering staff surveys in leader evaluation, and it links to a companion full report, Exploring the Potential Role of Staff Surveys in School Leader Evaluation, by the same project team.

Context¶

  • Readers seeking the underlying evidence must consult the companion full report the infographic points to

  • The supplied page prints no data, methods, or findings of its own

Target Learners¶

  • school leaders (principals) as the evaluated group
  • district and school administrators conducting leader evaluation

Target Learning Goals¶

  • using staff survey evidence to inform evaluation of school leadership performance

Claims¶

  • Staff feedback from teacher surveys can serve as evidence in evaluating school leaders' performance [+W]
  • When a school gets a new principal, Insight survey domain scores change more than student proficiency rates [+W]
  • Insight survey domain averages are more stable across years when a school keeps the same leader, suggesting principals affect these measures [+W]
  • Teacher survey measures correlate most with the Instruction, Talent, and School Culture domains of DCPS principal supervisor assessments [+W]
  • Most teacher survey domains and scales used by DCPS meaningfully differentiate among schools [+W]
  • Nearly all teacher survey domains and scales correlate small-to-moderately (0.26 to 0.46) with supervisors' ratings of principals' leadership [+W]
