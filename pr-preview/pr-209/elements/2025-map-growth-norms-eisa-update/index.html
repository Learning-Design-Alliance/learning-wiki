2025 MAP Growth norms update with Enhanced Item Selection Algorithm (EISA) alignment¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The 2025 MAP Growth norms update recalibrates the national reference group for RIT scores, achievement percentiles, conditional growth percentiles, and projected proficiency. It was prompted by post-COVID achievement and growth patterns falling "substantially below prepandemic trends" and incorporates technical enhancements, most notably that "the update aligns norms to the EISA, which strengthens alignment of MAP Growth to grade level content." NWEA updates its norms every three to five years; supporting documentation includes the 2025 Norms Technical Manual and Norms Toolkit.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Users interpreting norm-referenced metrics must account for the recalibrated national reference group when comparing results across norm sets or applying fixed thresholds.

Constraints¶

  • Projected proficiency in math is affected in mixed directions: higher growth expectations can raise projections while EISA-related higher proficiency thresholds can make proficiency harder to reach.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students assessed with MAP Growth

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Norm-referenced measurement of achievement and growth for accountability, screening, and evaluation

Claims¶

  • EISA-based MAP Growth testing produced slightly lower fall Mathematics scores but higher winter and spring Mathematics scores than traditional MAP Growth, and equipercentile linking was used to align the two versions for norming [+W]
  • Under the 2025 norms, higher math growth expectations lower conditional growth percentiles for higher-growth students [+W]
  • Under the 2025 MAP Growth norms, the same reading RIT score can rank higher in achievement percentile due to national declines in achievement [+W]
  • Norm shifts can change teacher evaluation CGP outcomes independently of instructional quality [+W]
  • Norm shifts can raise achievement percentiles for schools serving struggling students even when RIT gains and projected proficiency do not improve [+W]
  • Static percentile screening cut points carried across norm sets can under-identify students who remain below grade level [+W]
  • The 2025 MAP Growth norms were derived from one of the largest norming samples ever created, spanning 116,225,831 test events from 13,804,182 students in 29,720 schools across 7,000 districts over six testing terms [+W]
