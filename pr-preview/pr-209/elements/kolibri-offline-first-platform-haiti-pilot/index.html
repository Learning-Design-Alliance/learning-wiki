Kolibri: Learning Equality's open-source, offline-first platform used to facilitate blended learning¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Kolibri is Learning Equality's "open-source, offline-first" platform, which the Haiti pilot leverages "to facilitate blended learning approaches" in schools lacking reliable internet. Its offline-first design suits the low-connectivity rural settings described, where most Haitians lack devices and internet access. It is paired in the pilot with low-cost satellite internet and solar power.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools need access to the platform plus some connectivity or power solution; the pilot pairs it with low-cost satellite internet and solar power

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Rural primary school students in Haiti

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Science learning through blended instruction

Claims¶

  • In 75% of observed lesson segments in the Haiti pilot, all pupils were engaged [+W]
  • Students in the Haiti blended learning pilot showed statistically significant learning gains, with the lowest-baseline students gaining the most [+W]
  • Most students felt the piloted math tools let them work at their own pace, and observation data suggested enrichment-seeking students were more engaged than struggling learners [+W]
  • Math confidence increased slightly among students completing both baseline and end-of-year surveys, but the difference was not statistically significant [~W]
