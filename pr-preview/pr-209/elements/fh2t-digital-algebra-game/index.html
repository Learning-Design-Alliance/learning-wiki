From Here to There (FH2T) digital game for algebraic expressions and equation solving¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

FH2T is a digital game "designed to strengthen students' understanding of algebraic expressions and equation solving." Students "interact directly with algebraic notation by dragging and transforming expressions using mathematically valid operations." The game presents a starting expression and a mathematically equivalent target expression, and students manipulate the original to match the target, practicing distribution, factoring, inverse operations, and order of operations. It is intended for middle and high school students in introductory prealgebra or algebra.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student device with a modern web browser, e.g. Chrome on iPad or Chromebook
  • Can be used independently by students; ideally the related GM tools are integrated into teacher instruction and classroom activities

Constraints¶

  • Positioned as a supplemental instructional tool to practice foundational math skills, not a core curriculum

Target Learners¶

  • Middle and high school students learning introductory prealgebra or algebra

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding of algebraic expressions and equation solving
  • Foundational math skills such as distribution, factoring, inverse operations, and order of operations

Claims¶

  • FH2T was evaluated with nine 30-minute sessions spread across one academic year as a supplemental program [+W]
  • FH2T use raised Grade 7 students' algebraic understanding significantly more than an active comparison condition (g = 0.14) in an RCT [+W]
  • Algebra ability level differences appear on both criteria with no treatment-by-ability interaction [~W]

Examples¶

  • Deploy FH2T as supplemental practice with GM tools integrated into teacher instruction
  • Add a gestures-teaching world to enable differentiated starting points, and expand content beyond linear equations
