Years-as-EL student-level dataset from a California district (N = 41,343)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study analyzes student-level data from a single school district in California chosen for its large populations of Chinese- and Spanish-speaking English learners. The abstract reports the sample size as "N = 41,343" and describes the district as "a unique district in California with a large number of Chinese and Spanish ELs." The dataset supports by-subgroup and by-subject course-taking analyses comparing ELs by years spent classified as EL.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires student-level records linking EL classification duration to high school course-taking and eighth-grade test scores

Constraints¶

  • Drawn from one district in California, so findings are scoped to that district's context

Target Learners¶

  • High school students classified as English learners (Chinese- and Spanish-speaking subgroups)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Access to general and advanced academic coursework across subject areas

Claims¶

  • High school course-taking differs substantially among English learners depending on years spent as an EL [+W]
  • Years-as-EL course-taking differences disappear once eighth-grade test scores are considered [+W]
  • The program's impact was estimated with a difference-in-differences-in-differences approach on student-level data from a large urban California district [+W]
  • Summer credit recovery shows suggestive evidence of positive effects on English proficiency for newcomer ELs [+W]
  • Summer credit recovery increased the number of math, English Language Arts, science, and social science classes taken by newcomer EL students [+W]
  • Effects of summer credit recovery on 4- and 5-year graduation rates for newcomer ELs were imprecisely estimated [+W]
