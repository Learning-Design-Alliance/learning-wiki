Chicago reform evidence base: 22-school longitudinal case studies, 269-school principal and teacher surveys, and system-wide administrative data¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The book's evidence base combines three data sources: longitudinal case study data on 22 schools, survey responses from principals and teachers in 269 schools, and supplementary system-wide administrative data. The overview notes that "Few studies intertwine so completely extensive narratives and rigorous quantitative analyses," positioning the design as a strength. These sources jointly support the politics taxonomy, the change-effort classification, and the one-third-of-schools finding.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to school-level survey and administrative records plus sustained case study fieldwork

Constraints¶

  • Covers Chicago elementary schools during the first four years of the 1989 decentralization

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary school staff and school community leaders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding how governance reform relates to organizational and instructional change

Claims¶

  • In about a third of Chicago elementary schools, expanded local democratic participation served as a strong lever for systemic change focused on improved instruction [+W]
  • Case studies of six actively restructuring schools show the principal's role recast, growing social support for change, and external ideas and information brought to bear [+W]
  • School change efforts under the reform are classifiable as either systemic or unfocused [+W]
