Promotion power: a school-effectiveness measure separating school contributions from student background¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Promotion power is a measure of school effectiveness that the study defines as distinguishing "a school's contributions to student outcomes from the contributions of the background characteristics of the students it serves." It accounts for prior achievement and other background characteristics, and complements value-added measures by applying similar methods to longer-term outcomes such as graduation and college enrollment.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student outcome data including prior achievement and background characteristics, plus longer-term outcomes such as high school graduation and college enrollment

Constraints¶

  • Scores are estimates of schools' contributions rather than direct observations of school practice; the study covers DC public high schools

Target Learners¶

  • Public high school students in the District of Columbia

Target Learning Goals¶

  • High school graduation
  • College readiness
  • College enrollment

Claims¶

  • Student background characteristics are less strongly related to promotion power scores than to status measures [+W]
  • Schools with high promotion power for high school graduation tended also to have high promotion power for college enrollment [+W]
  • DC public high schools show wide variation in promotion power for college-ready SAT scores, graduation, and college enrollment [+W]
