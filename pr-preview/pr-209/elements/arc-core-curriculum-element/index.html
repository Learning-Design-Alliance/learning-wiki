ARC Core K-12 literacy curriculum with knowledge-building units¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

ARC Core is a K-12 English and Spanish literacy curriculum in which students develop agency and expertise "through extensive reading, writing, research, and analysis". It is organized as knowledge-building units integrating science, social studies, and literature content while developing literacy skills and vocabulary, with "four 9-week units per grade". Teachers receive new topically aligned materials each unit while instructional structures stay consistent across the year.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implemented by classroom teachers during an integrated literacy block; the evaluated kindergarten implementation replaced regular literacy instruction for 120 minutes per day for the full school year

Constraints¶

  • Teachers reported that full implementation, including student learning centers and individual meetings, required additional classroom support

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten students (evaluated); K-12 students as designed scope

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Literacy skills and vocabulary development
  • Knowledge building in science, social studies, and literature content

Claims¶

  • The evidence base for ARC Core's kindergarten efficacy is one randomized controlled trial covering 792 students, with an average effect size of +0.14 and a strong evidence rating. [+M]
  • In a multi-site randomized controlled trial, kindergarten students in ARC Core classrooms scored significantly higher on AIMSWeb Letter Naming Fluency than control students (ES = +0.28). [+M]
  • In the same randomized controlled trial, kindergarten students in ARC Core classrooms scored significantly higher on WMRT Passage Comprehension than control students (ES = +0.16). [+M]
  • Teachers implementing ARC Core in the Philadelphia study reported positive effects on student engagement, learning, and behavior, but noted full implementation required extra classroom support and occasional supplementing of materials. [+W]
  • Meta-analyses consistently validate extensive reading benefits across languages and populations, with program length as a moderator [+M]

Related Elements¶
