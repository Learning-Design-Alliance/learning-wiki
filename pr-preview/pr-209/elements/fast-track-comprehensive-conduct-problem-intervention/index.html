Fast Track: a comprehensive, multi-component intervention program to reduce conduct problems and promote academic, behavioral, and social improvement¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q4 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Fast Track is a comprehensive intervention program designed to reduce conduct problems and promote academic, behavioral, and social improvement. Students are identified as at risk for long-term antisocial behavior prior to first grade through teacher and parent reports of conduct problems, and delivery begins in first grade and continues through tenth grade. The report states that "Fast Track consists of seven integrated intervention components: the Promoting Alternative THinking Strategies (PATHS) curriculum, parent groups, parent–child sharing time, child social skills training groups, home visiting, child peer-pairing, and academic tutoring." After the first year, "the frequency of the supports is reduced based on the assessed functioning of the students and their families." The report estimates the cost at $58,283 per student over a 10-year period in 2004 US dollars.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Identification of at-risk students prior to first grade via teacher and parent reports of conduct problems
  • Delivery across school day, 2-hour extracurricular enrichment programs involving parents and children, and the home

Constraints¶

  • After the first year, the frequency of supports is reduced based on the assessed functioning of the students and their families

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten and elementary students identified as behaviorally disruptive and at high risk for long-term antisocial behavior

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reduce conduct problems
  • academic improvement
  • behavioral improvement
  • social improvement

Claims¶

  • Fast Track showed positive, statistically significant effects on some external behavior measures after one year, with no significant differences on eight other measures [+M]
  • Fast Track produced a positive, statistically significant effect on emotional/internal behavior for children at risk of emotional disturbance after one year [+M]
  • Fast Track produced a positive, statistically significant effect on reading achievement/literacy after one year, significant on the Spache DRS but not on the Woodcock-Johnson Letter-Word Identification subtest [+M]
  • Fast Track showed positive, statistically significant effects on three social outcome measures after one year, with no significant differences on three others [+M]

Related Elements¶

  • PATHS curriculum: classroom-based social-emotional lessons delivered two to three times weekly within Fast Track
