Four-stage dropout timing definitions for cross-institutional persistence comparison¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article collapses Simpson's nine potential dropout stages into four timing-based definitions of dropout (Table 2), distinguishing drops before course start, during the initial or Orientation week, and during instruction, and counting only "active drops" while excluding dormant (MIA) students. The author offers these "definitions of the time a student withdraws" to other researchers hoping to enable cross-institutional aggregation, since inconsistent attrition definitions make onground–online comparisons like comparing "apples and oranges."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Withdrawal requests must be recorded with a time of withdrawal relative to course start and instruction

Constraints¶

  • The study includes only active drops; dormant students and near-end failures are excluded
  • Reason-giving on withdrawal requests is voluntary and coded via textual analysis

Target Learners¶

  • adult continuing professional education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding and comparing persistence and attrition across course modalities

Claims¶

  • After instruction has begun, drop rates are essentially the same in online and onground continuing education classes [+W]
  • Online attrition during Orientation Week is twice that of onground classes' first week [+W]
  • Online continuing education courses show lower persistence than comparable onground courses (79% vs 84%) over eight quarters [+W]
  • Self-reported withdrawal reasons are largely similar online and onground, led by no/other reason, transfer, and schedule conflict [+W]
