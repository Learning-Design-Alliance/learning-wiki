Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 1 of 3 report an effect size

Description¶

The Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction (SDLMI) is a teaching model in which the student, not the teacher, sets the learning goal. The teacher acts as coach, guiding the student through three phases — set a goal, take action, adjust goal or plan — each containing a student question, teacher supports, and an educational action. The model operationalizes Self-Determination Theory and self-regulated learning research into a repeatable instructional cycle Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [+S].

Design Implications¶

SDLMI converts goal-setting, self-monitoring, and self-evaluation from teacher functions into student functions, building the causal agency that predicts post-school employment and independence outcomes for students with disabilities Self-regulated learning [+M]. The teacher's role shifts from directing to coaching: prompting the student questions, scaffolding action planning, and feeding back on progress rather than supplying answers.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A teacher trained in the model's phases and coaching moves
  • Time for students to work through all three phases — typically across multiple sessions, not a single lesson
  • Authentic student choice of goal; a teacher-imposed goal defeats the model's purpose Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [-S]
  • A means of tracking progress toward the self-set goal (Assessment data the student can read and interpret)

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness drops when teachers complete the phases for students or steer goal selection toward their own targets Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [-M]
  • Requires sustained implementation; single-session use produces little measurable gain in self-determination
  • Students with severe cognitive or communication support needs need adapted materials and communication supports, or the student questions become inaccessible
  • Whole-class implementation can drift into generic goal-setting worksheets if the coaching dialogue is dropped

Implementation Variability¶

  • Individual coaching — the original format: teacher and student work one-to-one through the phases
  • Whole-class embedding — SDLMI questions integrated into existing lessons so all students self-direct a learning goal (Raley et al., 2018)
  • Adapted formats — picture-based or communication-device-supported versions for students with significant support needs
  • Content-embedded — applied within a subject (e.g., a student sets a self-directed goal in algebra or in employment-preparation instruction)

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents with intellectual and developmental disabilities — the population for which the model was developed and has the strongest evidence [+M]
  • Students transitioning to adulthood who need goal-directed behavior for employment and independent living [+M]
  • General education students benefit from the same self-regulation mechanics, though effects are less studied Self-regulated learning [~M]
  • Students with learned helplessness or low academic self-efficacy, for whom experiencing goal attainment is itself the intervention

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-determination: autonomy, self-initiation, and causal agency Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [+S]
  • Self-regulated learning: goal setting, self-monitoring, strategy adjustment Self-regulated learning [+M]
  • Academic goal attainment in specific content areas (the goal is student-chosen, so content varies)
  • Transition skills: employment, postsecondary, and independent-living goal pursuit

Instructions¶

  1. Phase 1 — Set a goal. Ask the student "What do I want to learn?" Support the student to identify a discrepancy between current and desired performance, state the goal, and commit to it (Challenge Identification).
  2. Phase 2 — Take action. Ask "What is my plan?" Support the student to choose a strategy, design an action plan, and self-monitor progress (Application, Check-In).
  3. Phase 3 — Adjust goal or plan. Ask "What have I learned?" Support the student to compare outcomes with the goal, self-evaluate, and either revise the plan or set a new goal (Assessment, Articulation).
  4. Throughout, coach rather than direct: prompt the student questions, model strategy options, and fade support as the student manages the cycle independently.

Related Strategies¶

  • Goal-Setting and Self-Monitoring — the component self-regulation techniques SDLMI packages into a full instructional model
  • Student-Led IEPs — a transition practice that operationalizes the same causal-agency goal
  • Choice Boards — a lighter-weight autonomy structure; SDLMI extends choice from task selection to goal selection

Examples¶

  • Transition education in secondary special education — a special educator coaches a student with an intellectual disability through setting an employment goal (e.g., "I want to learn to complete a job application"), planning practice steps, and evaluating progress weekly across a semester.
  • Whole-class algebra implementation — a high school math teacher embeds the three SDLMI questions into a unit so every student sets a self-directed mastery goal for the unit, tracks quiz data, and adjusts study strategies (Raley et al., 2018).
  • Kansas SDLMI research program — the model was developed and validated through federally funded research at the University of Kansas (https://sdlmi.ku.edu).
