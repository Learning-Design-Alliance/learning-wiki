Put students in charge of soliciting grammatical feedback through pre-taught correction questions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

To give corrective feedback without hindering communication, the teacher shifts the onus to students: in the journal space reserved for teacher comments, students ask specific questions about the grammatical correctness of their entries. The teacher pre-teaches a storehouse of such questions, for example "Did I spell _____ correctly?", "Is this sentence correct?", and "Is there a better way?". In this way correction is not always something done to the student; it is initiated and controlled by the student.

Target Learners¶

  • adult ESL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-monitoring grammatical accuracy in personal writing

Related Strategies¶

  • Design class-wide grammar lessons from common journal errors, and let students catalogue their own errors
  • Provide incidental corrective feedback through clarification requests, recasts, and a P.S.
  • Pre-teach target language forms deductively before journal entries incorporate them
  • Use sample journal entries to inductively highlight target language elements
