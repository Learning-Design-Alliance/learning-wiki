Set up closed, goal-oriented tasks with a limited number of possible outcomes to encourage negotiation of meaning in CMC¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Following Pica, Kanagy and Falodun (1993), the chapter endorses Pellettieri's (2000) recommendation to "set up goal-oriented tasks with a limited number of possible outcomes to encourage negotiation of meaning". In the study this was implemented as problem-solving and decision-making collaborative tasks embedded in five subject-specific ESP scenarios, each producing a written language product. The study's own results support the recommendation: closed tasks generated 50% more negotiation routine phases than open tasks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tasks must be meaning-focused and collaborative, with a limited number of possible outcomes

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is grounded in task-type comparisons within this ESP setting with French Master's biology students; the authors caution that results should not be generalised to other settings without great caution

Target Learners¶

  • non-native speaker university students of English for specific purposes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing negotiation of meaning and corrective feedback during task-based interaction
