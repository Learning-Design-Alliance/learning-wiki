All About Me Posters¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size

Description¶

Learners create a visual poster representing their identity, interests, family, and experiences, then present it to peers. The strategy serves two functions simultaneously: it surfaces each learner's prior knowledge and interests for the instructor to draw on later, and it builds interpersonal knowledge that underpins classroom community and a sense of belonging.

Design Implications¶

The strategy works primarily through Activation — prompting learners to retrieve and externalize self-relevant knowledge — and through social belonging, which predicts persistence and engagement in school settings A brief belonging intervention improves academic outcomes for marginalized students. [+M]. Because the content is self-referential, retrieval is easy and low-risk, making the activity an accessible early-semester entry point before more demanding academic tasks.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A structured template or prompt list (name, interests, a memory, a goal) so posters are comparable and shareable
  • A sharing format — gallery walk, small groups, or whole-class Class Discussion — since unshared posters build nothing social
  • Instructor follow-through: recording learner interests and referencing them in later lessons, or the activation value is lost

Constraints¶

  • Learners with stigmatized identities, unstable home situations, or privacy concerns may find self-disclosure stressful rather than community-building; forced sharing of personal content can reduce belonging for exactly the students it targets [~M]
  • As a one-off icebreaker, effects on community fade quickly; benefits depend on the instructor integrating poster content into subsequent instruction
  • Little direct evidence that the poster format itself improves academic outcomes — the supporting evidence concerns belonging and activation, not the artifact [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Digital versions (slide decks, Padlet boards) for online or low-art-confidence contexts
  • "All About Us" group posters shift the task from self-disclosure to collaborative identity-building
  • Subject-specific variants ("Math About Me," "Scientist Bio") connect personal content to disciplinary concepts

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary and middle-school learners, for whom self-referential tasks are developmentally engaging
  • New cohorts forming a community at the start of a course or year
  • Multilingual learners, for whom visual representation lowers the language barrier of self-presentation — though instructors should allow drawing and home-language content rather than demanding English-only text

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Affective and community goals: belonging, trust, peer familiarity
  • Metacognitive goals: articulating one's interests, strengths, and goals
  • Activation: surfacing prior knowledge and experiences the instructor can later connect to content

Instructions¶

  1. Model the task with the instructor's own completed poster, making the expected depth and honesty visible
  2. Provide a template or Choice Boards so learners control which facets of identity to share
  3. Give work time with the instructor circulating and asking questions (Coaching)
  4. Share through a gallery walk or small groups rather than mandatory whole-class presentation, reducing exposure pressure
  5. Record interests and reference them in later lessons to complete the Activation loop

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — the poster is a self-focused variant; both externalize what learners bring
  • Check-Ins — lower-stakes, recurring versions of the same social-bonding function

Examples¶

  • A 4th-grade teacher opens the year with poster creation, then photographs each poster and builds a "class directory" referenced during reading-conference conversations all year
  • Responsive Classroom's first-week practices use similar identity-sharing activities to establish community norms before academic work begins (https://www.responsivereading.com)
