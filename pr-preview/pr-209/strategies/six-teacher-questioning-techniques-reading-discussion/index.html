Apply six questioning techniques in whole-class discussion: simplify, moderate, provoke thought, challenge, follow up, and relate to students¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

After group discussion or role-play, whole-class discussion follows, and the article details six techniques for teacher questions: simplifying language, moderating difficulty to students' knowledge and thinking capability, asking thought-provoking questions, asking challenging questions, asking follow-up questions, and asking questions relevant to students' experience. It states that "Follow-up questions can avoid the one-time interactive phenomenon, increasing students' participation", and that questions related to students' life enhance the sense of participation and interest in reading more.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Question language simple, clear and within students' familiar vocabulary
  • Questions matched to students' level of knowledge and thinking capability
  • Follow-up questions with hints when answers are inadequate

Constraints¶

  • Too many information questions should be avoided in favour of questions that inspire thinking; overly easy questions are described as a waste of time

Target Learners¶

  • English majors in English Reading classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Cognitive skills, thinking and problem-solving abilities
  • Expression and exchange of opinions
  • Sustained participation in discussion

Related Strategies¶

  • Evaluate student contributions through praising, encouraging, quoting students' words, and gestures
  • Use role-play and drama techniques after reading to deepen understanding through lifelike language use

  • Probing Questions
