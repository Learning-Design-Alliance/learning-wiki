Establish Guidelines for Discussions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 1 of 5 report an effect size

Description¶

Establishing guidelines for discussions means co-creating or explicitly introducing behavioral norms before dialogue begins, so participants know how to engage when the conversation becomes difficult. Well-known formulations include Singleton's four agreements for Courageous Conversations About Race — stay engaged, experience discomfort, speak your truth, accept non-closure — and the "pause of respect" after a difficult disclosure. The guidelines function as a shared contract that lowers the social risk of honest participation.

Design Implications¶

Discussion quality depends less on the topic than on whether participants believe candor is safe and productive. Explicit norms create psychological safety — a shared belief that interpersonal risk-taking will not be punished — which predicts team and classroom learning behavior Edmondson's psychological safety research. [+S]. Norms also reduce the facilitation burden: participants self-correct against a shared standard rather than waiting for instructor intervention.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A facilitator who introduces the guidelines, models them, and reinforces them consistently when violated
  • Genuine participant buy-in — ideally through co-creation, which increases ownership of the norms
  • A physical or social environment where dissent will not carry relational penalties
  • A mechanism for revisiting and revising norms as the group's needs emerge

Constraints¶

  • Guidelines fail when participants engage in bad faith or use "speaking your truth" to shield harmful claims from scrutiny [-M] — norms must be paired with Argument Construction norms that distinguish opinion from evidence
  • One-time norm-setting decays; without ongoing reinforcement, groups revert to dominant-voice patterns [-M]
  • Overly rigid guidelines can suppress legitimate disagreement and produce false consensus [-W]
  • Norms cannot compensate for a facilitator who permits status or identity-based silencing [-S]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Instructor-imposed vs. co-created norms: co-creation takes more time but increases adherence
  • Written charters posted visibly vs. verbal agreements revisited at each session
  • Opening rituals such as a Check-In to surface moods and concerns before content discussion
  • Scaled versions: brief "discussion agreements" for a single seminar vs. sustained community agreements for a semester-long course or Community of Inquiry

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescent through adult learners discussing sensitive, identity-laden, or controversial topics
  • Diverse groups where status differences or prior intergroup tension would otherwise suppress participation Belonging interventions improve outcomes for at-risk students. [+M]
  • Less critical for low-stakes, factual discussions where social risk is minimal [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Productive participation in Class Discussion and deliberation on contested issues
  • Perspective-taking and Building Empathy across difference
  • Active listening and honest expression of reasoning and feeling
  • Tolerance for ambiguity — accepting that understanding is continuous, not immediately resolved

Instructions¶

  1. Introduce or co-draft the guidelines before the first substantive discussion; explain the rationale for each norm rather than presenting them as rules.
  2. Open each session with a brief Check-In to surface participants' current state and readiness.
  3. Frame the discussion task with Argument Construction expectations — claims supported by reasons, not just testimony.
  4. During dialogue, apply the "pause of respect" after difficult disclosures, and redirect violations by naming the specific norm, not the person.
  5. Close with reflection on how well the guidelines served the group, and revise as needed — supporting autonomy and ownership Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [+S].

Related Strategies¶

  • Active Listening — a skill the guidelines operationalize; norms make listening an observable, enforceable behavior
  • Establishing Classroom Norms — the broader practice of which discussion guidelines are a specific instance
  • Create a safe space in arts instruction so students can take productive risks and grow emotionally

Examples¶

  • Courageous Conversations About Race (Singleton, 2015) — the four agreements (stay engaged, experience discomfort, speak your truth, accept non-closure) used in equity-focused professional development and classrooms.
  • Harvard Business School case discussions — instructors establish cold-calling and build-on-comments norms in the first session, enabling the case-based learning method to function at scale.
  • Socratic seminar protocols (e.g., from Expeditionary Learning / EL Education) — printed discussion agreements covering evidence use, airtime equity, and response to disagreement.
