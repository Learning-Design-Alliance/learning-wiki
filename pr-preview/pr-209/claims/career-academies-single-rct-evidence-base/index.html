Evidence¶

design-1¶

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2015, September). Dropout Prevention intervention report: Career Academies. Retrieved from https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/70

q3 · i? · causal · r2

The WWC research summary describes the design of the one study meeting standards without reservations: a randomized controlled trial across nine sites in six states, with participants entering ninth or tenth grade, many from low-income or single-parent households. The final analytic samples ranged from 1,379 to 1,454 students depending on outcome, and no significant baseline differences were found between intervention and comparison students.

"Students were randomly assigned to one of three conditions: (1) enrollment in Career Academies, (2) waitlist for Career Academies enrollment, and (3) a comparison group. This resulted in a randomized sample of 1,064 intervention and 889 comparison students."

Related Claims¶

  • Career Academies show no discernible effects on staying in school: about 10% of academy students dropped out before the end of grade 12 versus about 12% of comparison students (effect size 0.14, not statistically significant or substantively important). — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Career Academies show no discernible effects on progressing in school: academy students accrued no more course credits than comparison students by the end of twelfth grade (effect size 0.09, not statistically significant or substantively important). — related
  • Career Academies have potentially positive effects on completing school: about 96% of academy students earned a diploma or GED within 8 years of expected graduation versus about 94% of comparison students (effect size 0.27, not statistically significant but substantively important). — related
  • The evidence base for the program is a single RCT of 3,074 randomly assigned youth, with 1,196 in the follow-up analysis sample, and the WWC rates the extent of evidence as small — related
