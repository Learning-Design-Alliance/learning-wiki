Evidence¶

Woloshyn 1992¶

Woloshyn, Vera E.; Paivio, Allan; Pressley, Mike. (1992). Using Elaborative Interrogation To Help Students Overcome Their Inaccurate Science Beliefs. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED356949

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Experiment 1: 60 grade 6 and 7 students, randomly assigned to elaborative interrogation or reading control, studied 32 science facts in three text formats. A 2x2x3 ANOVA showed significant strategy main effects, with "significant main effects for strategy" and g > 3.38 favoring elaborative interrogation.

"Across all the recall and recognition measures, there were significant main effects for strategy [F(1,58) > 14.02, 2,(.001]. Instructions to use elaborative interrogation produced better learning performances than did instructions to read for understanding (g > 3.38, p<.01)."

Woloshyn 1992 (2)¶

Woloshyn, Vera E.; Paivio, Allan; Pressley, Mike. (1992). Using Elaborative Interrogation To Help Students Overcome Their Inaccurate Science Beliefs. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED356949

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Experiment 2: 80 grade 6 and 7 students in four conditions (strategy x activation) studied 32 traditional-format science facts. ANOVA showed "a significant main effect for strategy" with F(1,76) > 50.31, p<.001, favoring elaborative interrogation.

"For both recall and recognition scores, there was a significant main effect for strategy [F(1,76) > 50.31, 2<.001]. Elaborative-interrogation subjects retained more study facts than did reading-controls (smallera= 7.82)."
