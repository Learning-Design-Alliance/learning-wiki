Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain 2015¶

Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Rethinking Universal Suspension for Severe Student Behavior. Stanford University and University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · causal · r2

The study looked separately at outcomes of students with low and high risk of experiencing suspension to address concerns that misbehavior disrupts other students' learning. The article reports high-risk students "felt safer"; no effect size is printed.

"The students at the highest estimated risk of suspension felt safer in their schools when suspensions for severe infractions were reduced."

Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain 2015 (2)¶

Rebecca Hinze-Pifer and Lauren Sartain. (2015). Rethinking Universal Suspension for Severe Student Behavior. Stanford University and University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Risk-stratified analysis of test score outcomes for low-suspension-risk students in the same CPS student-fixed-effect framework. The article reports the largest gains among low-risk students and no evidence of overall academic harm; no effect size is printed.

"Students at the lowest estimated risk of suspension, meanwhile, saw the largest test score gains. We do not find evidence that, overall, students’ academic achievement was harmed by the policy changes."
