Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/20

q2 · i? · review · r3

The guide's synthesis of four studies meeting WWC group design standards without reservations on solved problems in algebra instruction. Three showed positive effects on conceptual knowledge; the summary states "studying solved problems can improve achievement" relative to practice problems alone. No effect sizes are printed.

"This body of evidence suggests that, com- pared to asking students to solve practice problems alone, studying solved problems can improve achievement."

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Within the same four-study body of evidence for Recommendation 1, one study compared students who studied solved problems to students who used reflective questioning, and "solved problems had negative effects" on conceptual and procedural knowledge in that contrast.

"The remaining study found that solved problems had negative effects on conceptual and pro- cedural knowledge."
