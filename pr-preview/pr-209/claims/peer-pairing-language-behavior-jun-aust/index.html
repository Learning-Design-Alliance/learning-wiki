Evidence¶

What Works Clearinghouse 2007¶

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: Peer Tutoring and Response Groups. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Randomized controlled trial of 30 Korean English language learners in grades 1–6 in Tacoma, Washington, assigned by class to peer-pairing or non-peer-pairing conditions for 4.5 months. Appendix A3 reports effect sizes of 1.34 (talking to peer) and 2.16 (addressed by peer), both statistically significant, with improvement indices of +41 and +48.

"the analysis found peer pairing to have a statistically significant effect on student language behavior; there was no statistically significant effect when talking to the teacher and when being addressed by the teacher."

What Works Clearinghouse 2007 (2)¶

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: Peer Tutoring and Response Groups. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

q3 · i? · causal · r?

In the same RCT, the WWC pooled high and low integrative motivation subgroups within each condition. Listening comprehension showed an effect size of 0.51 (not significant, improvement index +19); teacher-directed language behaviors showed effect sizes of 0.09 and 0.04, both not significant. Equivalence was not tested.

"WWC combined subpopulation data to examine the overall effects of peer pairing compared with non-peer pairing and found no statistically significant effect on listening comprehension."
