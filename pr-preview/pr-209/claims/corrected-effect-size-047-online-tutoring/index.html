Evidence¶

Roschelle 2020¶

Roschelle, J., Cheng, B. H., Hodkowski, N., Neisler, J. & Haldar, L. (2020). Evaluation of an online tutoring program in elementary mathematics [Project Report]. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12265/94

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Meta-analysis of the five sites (each treated as one study) using the dmetar package in R on gain scores; the correction addressed a software limitation using pooled variance of gain scores. The corrected effect was "+.47 with a 95% confidence interval of [+.23, +.70]".

"we also ran a meta-analytic model that corrected for the potential overestimate of effect size and found a corrected effect size of +.47 with a 95% confidence interval of [+.23, +.70]."

Roschelle 2020 (2)¶

Roschelle, J., Cheng, B. H., Hodkowski, N., Neisler, J. & Haldar, L. (2020). Evaluation of an online tutoring program in elementary mathematics [Project Report]. San Mateo, CA: Digital Promise. http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12265/94

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation comparing their effect size with the prior best-evidence synthesis, which reported a weighted mean of g = .26 for one-to-one face-to-face tutoring; this is a stated comparison, not a new statistical test.

"the Pelligrini et al. (2018) meta-analysis of elementary math tutoring programs found g = .26 and was based largely on face-to-face tutoring studies. Thus, this study compares favorably to prior research on face-to-face mathematics tutoring with elementary students."
