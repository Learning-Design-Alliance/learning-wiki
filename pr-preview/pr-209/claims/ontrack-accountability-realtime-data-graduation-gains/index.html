Evidence¶

Allensworth 2018¶

Allensworth, E.M., Nagaoka, J., & Johnson, D.W. (2018). High school graduation and college readiness indicator systems: What we know, what we need to know. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://ncs.uchicago.edu/freshman-on-track-toolkit

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports that after Chicago integrated on-track rates into its high school accountability system, signaling that schools should pay more attention to ninth-grade performance, schools developed ninth-grade-failure-prevention practices that "have been credited with dramatic improvements in graduation rates in the district." This is the review's attribution, citing Roderick et al. (2014); no effect size is printed.

"Eventually, individual schools developed very different practices around dropout prevention than in the past, interventions that focused on preventing failures in the ninth grade. These changes have been credited with dramatic improvements in graduation rates in the district."

Allensworth 2018 (2)¶

Allensworth, E.M., Nagaoka, J., & Johnson, D.W. (2018). High school graduation and college readiness indicator systems: What we know, what we need to know. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://ncs.uchicago.edu/freshman-on-track-toolkit

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review states that "on-track rates only improved after the district developed real-time data reports" usable for early intervention and school improvement, and that accountability by itself was not sufficient. This is the review's interpretation of the Chicago chronology; no effect size is printed.

"In Chicago, on-track rates only improved after the district developed real-time data reports that schools could use for early intervention and guiding school improvement."
