Evidence¶

Montgomery 2010¶

Montgomery, N., & Allensworth, E. M., with Correa, M. (2010). Passing Through Science: The Effects of Raising Graduation Requirements in Science on Course-Taking and Academic Achievement in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/passing-through-science-effects-raising-graduation-requirements-science-course-taking

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Cohort comparison using grades as a proxy for learning, validated against test scores; the report found grade-test links where "Standardized science test score gains were minimal among students who earned Cs." No effect size is printed for this finding.

"Five out of six CPS students averaged a C or lower in science after the policy change. Standardized science test score gains were minimal among students who earned Cs in their science courses and absent among those who received Ds."

Montgomery 2010 (2)¶

Montgomery, N., & Allensworth, E. M., with Correa, M. (2010). Passing Through Science: The Effects of Raising Graduation Requirements in Science on Course-Taking and Academic Achievement in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/passing-through-science-effects-raising-graduation-requirements-science-course-taking

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Cohort comparison of graduate transcripts controlling for incoming characteristics; B-or-better completers of three years rose from about 19 percent to 30 percent, "an increase of 11 percentage points." No standardized effect size is printed.

"Prior to the policy, about 19 percent of graduates completed at least three years of science with a B average or higher in those courses (Figure 10). After the policy, the figure jumped to 30 percent, an increase of 11 percentage points."
