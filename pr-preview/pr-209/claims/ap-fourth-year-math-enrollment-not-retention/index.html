Evidence¶

Melissa Roderick 2013¶

Melissa Roderick, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller, and Thomas Kelley-Kemple. (2013). From High School to the Future: The Challenge of Senior Year in Chicago Public Schools. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-challenge-senior-year-chicago-public-schools

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Quasi-experimental matching of similar students across CPS schools (linked to National Student Clearinghouse data) estimated effects of senior coursetaking on four-year college enrollment and selective-college match; without selection controls the effects would be overestimated.

"After addressing this problem of selection, senior year coursetaking, particularly taking AP courses, is associated with an increase in the likelihood that students will attend colleges that they are qualified to attend."

Melissa Roderick 2013 (2)¶

Melissa Roderick, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller, and Thomas Kelley-Kemple. (2013). From High School to the Future: The Challenge of Senior Year in Chicago Public Schools. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-challenge-senior-year-chicago-public-schools

q2 · i0 · associational · r2

The same matched analysis examined two-year continuous enrollment in a four-year college and found no coursetaking effect on retention; the report notes it cannot examine grades or remediation without college transcript data, so equivalence was not tested on those outcomes.

"However, while coursetaking is strongly associated with college enrollment, there is no association between taking AP courses, fourth-year mathematics, or four core courses and retention in college."
