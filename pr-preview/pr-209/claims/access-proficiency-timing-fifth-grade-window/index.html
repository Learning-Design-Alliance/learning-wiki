Evidence¶

de la Torre 2019¶

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Descriptive analysis of time-to-proficiency on the ACCESS for ELLs exam among the three continuously-enrolled CPS cohorts. The report identifies the end of fifth grade as the point after which passing became unlikely before high school. No effect size is printed.

"More than one-half of students who began as ELs passed the ACCESS proficiency exam and became former ELs by third grade, and three-fourths passed the exam by the end of fifth grade. If students did not pass the exam by the end of fifth grade, they were unlikely to pass it in the remaining years before high school."

de la Torre 2019 (2)¶

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive comparison of the roughly one-fifth of began-as-EL students still classified as ELs at high school entry versus those who reached proficiency earlier. The report notes they did not differ in types of schools enrolled in. No effect size is printed.

"But they were more likely to be male and were much more likely to be identified as needing special education services than students who reached proficiency on the ACCESS exam before high school."
