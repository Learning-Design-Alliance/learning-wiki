Research-based design: edtech products should derive their purpose, framework, and design decisions from learning sciences research¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 review, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Research-based design is the organizing account of this report: edtech quality is judged by whether a product articulates a learning purpose and explicitly consults learning sciences research to drive design decisions, from a theoretical framework down to specific features. The report argues this is a more timely and generalizable quality metric than efficacy studies, which take years and generalize poorly. It claims that products using this approach "more clearly name the expected impact on learning" and can track progress toward a research-based goal.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A specific articulated learning purpose
  • An explicit, documented connection between learning sciences research and design decisions

Constraints¶

  • Efficacy studies remain the field's dominant quality metric; research-based design is proposed as a complementary leading indicator, not a replacement

Target Learners¶

  • learners using K-12 and adult edtech products

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • any learning goal the product articulates, from literacy to mathematics to language acquisition
