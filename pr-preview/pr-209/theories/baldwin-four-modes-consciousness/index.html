Baldwin's four successive modes of consciousness embedded in the growth of logical thought¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Baldwin proposed four "modes of consciousness" embedded in the growth of logical thought: prelogical, quasi-logical, logical, and hyperlogical. A mode is defined as "the most basic level of making sense of the world in a given period of development." Logical validities of one mode do not hold in the next but are succeeded through successive reorganization of function, producing what Baldwin called a comparative logic of successive reinterpretations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Modes are understood as developmental periods, each reorganizing the logical concepts of the previous mode

Constraints¶

  • The paper presents the modes through Baldwin's own writings and Cahan's (1984) summary; it reports no empirical validation of the mode sequence

Target Learners¶

  • developmental psychology scholars
  • historians of psychology

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding stage-like reorganization of logical thought in development

Claims¶

  • Both Baldwin and Piaget explained development through the complementary processes of assimilation and accommodation, with accommodation arising from conflict with habit [+W]
  • Baldwin, not Piaget, first proposed progressive stage-by-stage development and a genetic epistemology founded on assimilation and accommodation [+W]
  • Scholarly views differ on whether Piaget appropriated Baldwin's ideas or extended them with empirical support [~W]
  • Piaget denied Baldwin's influence, calling the theoretical similarities simple convergences, yet his mentor Janet had been strongly influenced by Baldwin [~W]
