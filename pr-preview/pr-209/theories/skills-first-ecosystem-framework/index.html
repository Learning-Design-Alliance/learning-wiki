The skills-first ecosystem framework¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The report defines the skills-first ecosystem as "all of the systems, technologies, policies, organizations (e.g., employers, educational and research institutions, training providers, community based organizations), and that are interconnected in ways that collectively support the development, implementation, and scaling of skills-first practices." It maps nine key contributor categories with example roles, from community-based organizations providing trauma-informed supports to technology developers building skills identification and job-matching tools, with workers and jobseekers as a critical driving component. The framework organizes the report's challenge categories and recommended solutions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interconnection among employers, educational and research institutions, training providers, and community-based organizations to collectively support development, implementation, and scaling of skills-first practices

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • frontline workers and jobseekers, including opportunity youth and veterans

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • skill identification, articulation, and demonstration; career and economic mobility; navigation of learning and career pathways

Claims¶

  • Skills First Challenges Across Learn And Work Ecosystem [+M]
  • Worker and jobseeker input on skills-first pathway design and implementation is far too often limited or missing [+W]
