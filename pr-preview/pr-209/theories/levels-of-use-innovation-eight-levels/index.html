Levels of Use of the Innovation: eight developmental levels with decision points¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Levels of Use (LoU) is a CBAM diagnostic dimension that "focuses on describing, in operational terms, the behaviors that innovation nonusers and users demonstrate relative to the innovation." Eight levels are operationally defined (0 Nonuse, I Orientation, II Preparation, III Mechanical Use, IVA Routine, IVB Refinement, V Integration, VI Renewal), each separated by a Decision Point, with seven behavior categories (Knowledge, Acquiring Information, Sharing, Assessing, Planning, Status Reporting, Performing) described at each level.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Measuring an individual's Level of Use requires collecting data on the Decision Points, which "make a fairly clear demarcation between each level"

Constraints¶

  • Movement above LoU IVA routine use "is not so clearly understood at this time (Hall, 1978)"

Target Learners¶

  • Teachers and professors as individual innovation users

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Diagnosing an individual's developmental status in using an educational innovation

Examples¶

  • The LoU Chart: operational definitions of the eight levels across seven categories of user knowledge and activity
  • The Levels of Use Interview: a 20-minute focused branching interview assigning a LoU level to any individual
  • Use LoU data to monitor implementation, plan staff development, select research samples, and evaluate programs
  • Change facilitators should work in a diagnostic/prescriptive mode, matching interventions to the user's level
  • For refinement-level use, shift the unit of intervention from the individual to the school building
