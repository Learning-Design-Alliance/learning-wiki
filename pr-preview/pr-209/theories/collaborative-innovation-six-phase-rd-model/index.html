Collaborative Innovation: a co-leadership, co-research, and co-design R&D model unfolding through six phases¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Collaborative Innovation is Digital Promise's structured approach to research and development in which districts, schools, communities, and program partners "co-research educational challenges and co-design solutions" for students furthest from opportunity. The model unfolds through six phases (Readiness; Connect and Commit; Inquire and Investigate; Design and Develop; Implement and Iterate; Sustain and Scale) and is anchored in six core tenets split into Practice Tenets and Impact Tenets, including collective ownership, student voice and leadership, context expertise and proximity, continuity of commitment, reimagining progress, and building capacity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The model requires positioning those closest to the challenge as co-investigators and co-designers rather than having solutions designed for them by outside experts.

Constraints¶

  • The report notes the model is not simply a set of steps but a way of working that centers equity, lived experience, and community expertise at every stage.

  • district and school leaders

  • families

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • community capacity to identify and solve equity-related educational challenges
  • sustaining equity-centered research and design

Claims¶

  • Time constraints, limited capacity, funder expectations, and communication gaps were the most consistent barriers to participation, offset by recurring structures, relational trust, actionable data, and networked channels [+W]
  • Partners in participatory evaluation demonstrated capacities in data engagement, contextual and pedagogical knowledge, and openness and improvement-oriented dispositions [+W]
  • The study's findings derive from structured reflections of 10 researchers and 11 program designers, not direct partner reports [+W]
  • Relational trust built through prior rigorous evaluation was a precondition for introducing participatory approaches [+W]
  • Evaluator staff capacities centered on creating conditions for participation rather than technical control [+W]
  • Collaborative Innovation tenets varied in visibility: collective ownership and context expertise were most evidenced, reimagining progress least [+W]
