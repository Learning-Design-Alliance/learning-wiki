Three-category state PreK4 investment classification (High, Growing, Low)¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report develops a classification scheme that sorts states into three PreK4 investment categories using NIEER Yearbook participation rates for four-year-olds at two time points (AY2011-12 and AY2022-23). High states had "relatively stable, high PreK4 participation rates over time" (at least 28% in both years, increase under 12 points); Growing states increased participation by at least 12 percentage points; Low states had rates of 22% or less in both years with increases under 12 points. The report calls this "a novel strategy for classifying states based on their PreK4 investment."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State PreK4 participation data at two time points (NIEER Yearbook) to assign categories

Constraints¶

  • Categories reflect state-funded prekindergarten for four-year-olds only; district-level NCES rates may include non-state-funded programs

Target Learners¶

  • four-year-old children in state-funded prekindergarten

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • early literacy and numeracy readiness for kindergarten

Claims¶

  • High Prek4 States Greater Kindergarten Math Growth [+M]
  • Districts in the sample increased PreK4 participation across all three state categories, with substantial within-category variation in enrollment trends [+W]
  • Low and Growing states have nearly identical average math scale scores, but students in Low states are more likely to improve or maintain grade level (64% vs. 60%) [+W]
  • Two additional state characteristics are marginally associated with kindergarten math improvement: proportion of districts offering state-funded PreK and meeting all ten quality benchmarks [+W]
