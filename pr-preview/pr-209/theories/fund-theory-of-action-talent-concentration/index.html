The Fund's theory of action: concentrating talented teachers and leaders in a school moves it toward improvement¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 9 claims (6 for, 3 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report evaluates The Chicago Public Education Fund's theory of action, which holds that a concentration of talented teachers and leadership in a school will move the school toward improvement. The most positive findings, particularly the LAUNCH/NBCT cluster results, 'begin to suggest support for The Fund's theory of action, that a concentration of talented teachers and leaders in a school will move the school toward improvement.' The theory was tested by comparing schools combining trained principals with NBCT clusters against schools with similarly experienced principals.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires concentrations of program-trained principals and national-board-certified teachers within the same school to be observable in sufficient numbers

Constraints¶

  • Causation cannot be established with the available cross-sectional data; cluster analyses rested on only two schools

Target Learners¶

  • Chicago Public Schools students in elementary and high schools

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • School improvement in organizational climate, professional capacity, and student learning gains

Claims¶

  • Launch Nbct Cluster Schools Dramatic Effects [+W]
  • Elementary LAUNCH principals report significantly more data-driven decision making than comparable or veteran principals [+W]
  • Elementary schools led by LAUNCH principals show higher teacher-parent interaction and more reformed literacy practices [+W]
  • High schools with clusters of at least three NBCTs score higher on professional capacity measures [+W]
  • Elementary schools with NLNS principals show greater innovation and reflective dialogue and less traditional literacy practice [+W]
  • No differences in student learning gains found between LAUNCH or NLNS principals and comparable or veteran principals [-W]
  • Schools with teams of Fund-supported leaders and NBCT clusters show few differences from other schools [-W]
  • One-year learning gains similar for schools with leadership teams and schools without Fund leaders or NBCTs [-W]
  • NBCTs report holding school leadership positions at higher rates than other teachers (50% vs 32%) [+W]
