Four regulatory tracks framework for edtech efficacy studies¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 5 studies (3 review, 2 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The guide organizes edtech study planning around four broad regulatory pathways determined chiefly by whether the research team can ascertain student identity. As a practical planning framework, most edtech studies fall into internal product development (no IRB), Not Human Subjects Research, Exempt Research, or Expedited/Full Board Review. The pivotal planning question is whether the team can readily ascertain individual student identities, because identity access drives both review tier and parental-permission burden.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teams must determine whether direct and indirect identifiers are permanently removed, suppressed, or firewalled before reaching the research team

Constraints¶

  • Individual IRBs retain discretion, and two IRBs reviewing the same protocol may legitimately reach different conclusions

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students participating in edtech efficacy studies

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Generating external evidence of edtech efficacy for district leaders, grantors, and buyers

Claims¶

  • Researcher access to identifiable student information, not research intent alone, determines NHSR status [+W]
  • School districts act as independent gatekeepers whose requirements can exceed IRB determinations [+W]
  • Maintaining NHSR status requires no researcher access to identifiable records, no added procedures, and no non-standard prompts [+W]
  • Small additions of research-specific procedures can shift a study out of exempt review [+W]
  • Survey item sensitivity determines the parental permission pathway, from full waiver to active opt-in [+W]

Examples¶
