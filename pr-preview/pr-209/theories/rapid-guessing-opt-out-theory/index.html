Rapid guessing as an opt-out decision within the measurement process¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This account explains rapid guessing not as a knowledge deficit or random noise but as a deliberate behavioral decision: the test taker chooses to stop engaging with the measurement process on an item. The authors state their assertion is "based on belief that rapid guessing on an item represents a test taker's choice to 'opt out' of the measurement process." Under this view, an opted-out response carries no measurement information, which motivates excluding it from scoring.
