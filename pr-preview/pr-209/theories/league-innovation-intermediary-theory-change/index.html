The League of Innovative Schools as an innovation intermediary and community of practice¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report frames the League as an innovation intermediary, defined as "organizations or groups within organizations that work to enable innovation", which initiates, facilitates, and supports cross-sector collaborations to promote innovative practices in K-12 public schools. A logic model (Appendix A) lays out a theory of change in which convenings, working groups, study tours, and communications support produce immediate impacts (professional growth, expanded networks) and intermediate impacts (innovative learning opportunities for students, empowered teachers). The network is nurtured through biannual fall and spring meetings and working groups on topics such as competency-based education, maker learning, personalized learning, professional development, and open education resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • District superintendents apply and are evaluated on vision for innovation, implementation record, leadership and teacher engagement, community engagement, and impact on underserved students

Constraints¶

  • The report notes it takes "time and sustained interaction" to build a community of practice

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 public school district leaders and superintendents

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Professional growth in innovation leadership
  • building regional and national professional networks
  • implementing innovative teaching practices
