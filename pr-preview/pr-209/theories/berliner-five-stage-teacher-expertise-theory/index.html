Five-stage developmental theory of pedagogical expertise: novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, expert¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article presents a stage theory of skill acquisition in teaching, following the Dreyfus brothers' general model. Novices learn context-free rules and are 'rational, relatively inflexible'; advanced beginners meld experience with verbal knowledge; competent performers make conscious choices and set priorities; proficient performers gain intuition and holistic similarity recognition; experts act in fluid, nonanalytic ways. Berliner offers it as tentative, with heuristic value for teacher education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Experience in real classrooms, since real-world experience appears far more important than verbal information in the early stages

Constraints¶

  • The article states its application to pedagogy is still unknown; duration in stages varies widely; a person may show characteristics of another stage in particular situations; expertise is highly contextualized and may not transfer well

Target Learners¶

  • preservice and beginning teachers
  • teacher educators

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • understanding developmental stages of teaching skill acquisition

Claims¶

  • Expert teachers interpret complex classroom phenomena more accurately and fluidly than novices and postulants [+W]
  • Expert teachers' well-practiced routines make their performance fluid, as seen in homework-review and lesson-teaching comparisons [+W]
  • Experts attend selectively to atypical events, merging typical student information into a group picture, while novices and postulants study all student details [+W]
  • Experts discern instructional significance in classroom scenes while novices and postulants treat all visual stimuli as equally important [+W]
  • Experts predict classroom phenomena and student errors far more than novices, who rarely anticipate misalgorithms [+W]
  • Expert teachers show intense emotionality and sense of responsibility for their performance, unlike affectless novices and postulants [+W]
  • Teacher expertise level shows no statistically significant difference on any of five selected indicators of effective teaching in elementary physical education [-W]

Related Theories¶

  • Berliner's stages of pedagogical expertise development as a grouping framework for teacher expertise research
  • The expert performance approach as a three-stage framework for studying expertise

Examples¶

  • Pursue competence, not expertise, as the realistic goal for the vast majority of teacher education graduates
  • Match teacher education, mentoring, and evaluation to developmental stage: routines and scripts for novices, analytical mentors from the competent and proficient, and stage-appropriate evaluation
