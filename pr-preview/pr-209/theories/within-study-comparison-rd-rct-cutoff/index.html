Within-study comparison of RD and RCT estimates at the shared cutoff as a test of RD validity¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's methodological framework is the within-study comparison: each of 15 studies assesses "the correspondence between causal estimates from an RD study and a randomized control trial (RCT) when the estimates are made at the same cutoff point where they should not differ asymptotically." Because statistical error, imperfect design implementation, and many analysis options could still produce differences, the framework tests whether they do, "assuming that the bias potential is greater with RDs than RCTs." It is used both to test internal validity and, by examining variation in bias across studies, external validity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires studies containing both an RD estimate and an RCT estimate made at the same cutoff score that determines treatment assignment.

Constraints¶

  • Estimates might nonetheless differ due to statistical error, imperfect design implementation, and a plethora of different possible analysis options.

Target Learners¶

  • Policy researchers and evaluators assessing causal inference methods
