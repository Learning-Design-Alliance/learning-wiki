Five-category taxonomy of noncognitive factors related to academic performance¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review defines noncognitive factors broadly, going "beyond a narrow reference to skills and include strategies, attitudes, and behaviors," and organizes the literature into "five general categories of noncognitive factors related to academic performance: 1) academic behaviors, 2) academic perseverance, 3) academic mindsets, 4) learning strategies, and 5) social skills." Each category is defined and its evidence reviewed in separate chapters, using students' course grades as the outcome of interest. The taxonomy reconciles loosely defined constructs across economics, psychology, and sociology.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires organizing loosely defined constructs (persistence, grit, self-efficacy, etc.) into categories of similar constructs to synthesize a fragmented literature

Constraints¶

  • The authors note studies often examine one factor in isolation, and the relative strengths of paths in the model remain largely untested

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents in middle grades, high school, and transition to college

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Academic performance as measured by course grades and GPA

Claims¶

  • Academic behaviors have the most immediate effect on course grades; directly targeting grit is not an evidenced lever [+W]
  • Course grades and GPA predict high school and college outcomes better than standardized test scores or coursework [+W]
  • Best leverage points for improving performance lie in teacher strategies for classroom context, mindsets, and learning strategies [+W]
  • Positive academic mindsets increase perseverance and academic behaviors, yielding higher grades; negative mindsets produce a self-defeating cycle [+W]

Examples¶
