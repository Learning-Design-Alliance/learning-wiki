Process-based theory of action for observation-driven teacher evaluation¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article articulates the mechanism by which observation-based evaluation is expected to improve achievement: iterative observation and conferencing change teacher practice in lesson planning, classroom environment, and instruction, which then improves student performance. As the article states, "changes to teacher practice through an iterative process of observation and conferencing—all focused on improving lesson planning and preparation, the classroom environment, and instruction—should lead to direct changes in student performance." This contrasts with output-based value-added metrics, which the authors argue do little to alter instructional practice. The theory motivates EITP's design around principal capacity, instructional feedback, and school learning climate.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires classroom observations coupled with principal–teacher conferences that provide structured feedback on instructional practice

Constraints¶

  • The authors note they are unaware of causal evidence linking test-score-based evaluation systems to achievement; the theory applies to process-oriented, observation-driven systems

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers evaluated through classroom observation

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improving the quality of classroom instruction and, ultimately, student achievement
