Response styles as scale-usage differences independent of true score¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Response styles are patterns in how survey respondents use Likert scale categories. The article defines them as occurring when "respondents use the categories of the Likert scale differently despite having the same true score on the construct of interest," giving extreme-category overuse as an example. The framework treats response styles as a construct-irrelevant source of bias in survey scores, which the study examines for its consequences for longitudinal growth estimation on social-emotional constructs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal Likert-scale survey data on a psychological construct
  • Scoring models that can either account for or ignore response styles

Constraints¶

  • Effects on growth estimates vary by psychological construct, response style, and model used, so the framework's consequences are not uniform across measures

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students completing social-emotional learning surveys

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Accurate measurement of social-emotional construct growth over time

Claims¶

  • Response Styles Affect Growth Estimates Sel [+M]
  • Differing response styles create a construct-irrelevant source of bias that distorts fundamental inferences made from survey data [+W]

Examples¶
