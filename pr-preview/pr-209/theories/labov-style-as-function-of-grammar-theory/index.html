Labov's five-segment theory of language makes style the function of grammar¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The note reports Labov's new, then-unwritten theory of language, in which "style the function of grammar" — grammar itself carries the stylistic work of an utterance. The theory is presented as a production model moving through five interrelated segments, from a semantic network of content words through orientation, lexicalization, grammaticalization, and phonologization. The note is a second-hand report of a conversation, not a published statement of the theory by Labov himself.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A theory of language must account for both the systematic and the random aspects of language, as Labov states

Constraints¶

  • Nothing of the theory was written down at the time; the account is based on a conversation during the first week of September 1970

Target Learners¶

  • linguists and language researchers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Labov theorizes that communication can occur by simply saying the content words, especially in multilingual situations [+W]
