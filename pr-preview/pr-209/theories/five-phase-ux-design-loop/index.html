The 5-Phase UX Design Loop: a five-step iterative process for user-informed product development¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The 5-Phase UX Design Loop is "a practical, iterative process for reducing uncertainty, learning from users, and quickly making informed product decisions." It comprises five phases: (1) Get Aligned, defining the problem and key product decisions with the team; (2) Design the Concept, starting from users' "jobs to be done"; (3) Translate to Testable, using AI tools to build a functional, testable app; (4) Test with Real People, testing "just enough" to make product decisions; and (5) Synthesize and Decide, then returning to Step 1. Education R&D teams can adopt it to develop products grounded in user needs and evidence.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teams align first on the problem, the key product decisions, and what they need to learn before designing concepts
  • Testing must build in enough interactivity that users can give meaningful feedback on a tangible prototype

Examples¶

  • AI prototyping and research toolset: V0, Cursor, Mobbin, Figma, and Great Question
  • Distinguish product users from purchasers and recruit representative users through school-like community settings
  • Use small-scale learning metrics during iteration and reserve proof metrics for later stages
  • Product Testing
  • Use iterative design with short feedback loops and multiple user-feedback methods during product development
