Four options for accounting for co-teaching in value-added models¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper organizes the policy problem of co-teaching in value-added models around four named methods: "the partial credit method, the teacher team method, the full roster method, and the full roster-plus method." Each option represents a different way of assigning credit for jointly taught students to teachers in the model. The taxonomy serves as the paper's organizing framework and the basis for its practical guidance to policymakers and model developers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A value-added model that must attribute achievement credit for co-taught classrooms to individual teachers

Constraints¶

  • Developed in the context of value-added assessment for District of Columbia Public Schools

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in co-taught classrooms

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Accurate teacher evaluation and accountability measurement in value-added systems

Claims¶

  • The full roster and full roster-plus methods are empirically similar [+W]
  • The partial credit and teacher team methods for co-teaching are impractical [+W]
  • The Teacher Team Method and Full Roster Method provide a more stable approach than the Partial Credit Method for estimating teacher effects when students have multiple teachers for the same subject. [+W]
  • The Full Roster Method offers the most promise for robust, practical implementation among the three co-teaching value-added methods considered. [+W]

Examples¶
