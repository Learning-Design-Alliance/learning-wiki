Value-added methods as the measure of teacher quality for studying mobility¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article measures teacher quality using value-added methods, defined in its framing as quality "measured using value-added methods," and focuses on "the variation in these effects across the effectiveness distribution." This makes the effectiveness distribution — the spread of teachers' value-added effects — the organizing axis for its mobility analysis, allowing comparison of mobility behavior for teachers at different points in the quality distribution.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher quality must be estimable as value-added effects for the teachers whose mobility is tracked

Constraints¶

  • Quality is measured by value-added estimates, so findings are conditional on that measurement approach

Target Learners¶

  • early-career public school teachers

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • teacher retention and mobility as it affects student achievement

Claims¶

  • Teacher mobility behavior is heterogeneous across the teacher performance distribution [+W]
  • On average, more effective early-career teachers remain in the teaching profession and stay in their initial schools [+W]
  • Student demographics and achievement levels affect early-career teacher mobility [+W]
