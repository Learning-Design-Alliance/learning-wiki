Working Students Success Network comprehensive integrated-services strategy¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Working Students Success Network (WSSN) is described as "an innovative, comprehensive strategy for supporting low-income students and students of color," implemented in nineteen community colleges as an expansion of the Working Families Success Network. It "brings together and integrates access to a full range of services and supports" spanning financial knowledge, budgeting, financial-product choice, and career planning, aiming to put students "on a path to securing marketable postsecondary credentials and achieving economic success." Mathematica and DVP-Praxis documented implementation and analyzed service receipt relative to academic outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Integration of a full range of services and supports across financial, employment, and education pillars within participating colleges

Constraints¶

  • Studied in the WSSN context of nineteen community colleges serving low-income students and students of color

Target Learners¶

  • low-income community college students
  • students of color

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • college completion
  • financial knowledge and budgeting skills
  • achievable career planning
  • securing marketable postsecondary credentials

Claims¶

  • Colleges may reach more students with less intensive group-delivered services, while one-on-one services can be customized to individual needs and goals [~W]
  • Receiving an intensive, one-on-one service may matter more than receiving services across multiple pillars [~W]
  • One-on-one service delivery may not be the only way to target services to individual student needs [+W]
