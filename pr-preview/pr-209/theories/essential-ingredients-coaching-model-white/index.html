White et al.'s coherent model of coaching's essential ingredients: coach factors, teacher factors, and coach-teacher interaction factors¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The paper presents White et al.'s (2015) organization of research findings on coaching's "essential ingredients" into a coherent model with three factor groups. Coach factors include content expertise, classroom experience, technology proficiency, and interpersonal skills, plus co-planning goals and video-reflection cycles. Teacher factors include willingness to collaborate and an asset lens believing students can succeed. Interaction factors include rapport, mutual respect, trust, and positive non-evaluative feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Effective coaching requires rapport, mutual respect, and trust between coach and teacher, and coaching conversations with positive feedback so the process does not feel evaluative

Constraints¶

  • The paper notes empirical research on the mechanisms that make instructional coaching more effective is still modest

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers and their coaches

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Building effective coaching relationships that improve teaching practice

Claims¶

  • Effective coaches require content expertise, classroom experience, technology proficiency, flexible scheduling, partnership orientation, and interpersonal skills [+W]
  • Framework identifies teacher prerequisites: basic content knowledge, willingness to engage, and genuine concern for improving student learning [+W]
  • Teacher buy-in and willingness to engage are viewed as strong mediators of coaching benefits [+W]
  • Four relational components—positive feedback, rapport, mutual respect, and reciprocal trust—form the relational framework of coaching [+W]
