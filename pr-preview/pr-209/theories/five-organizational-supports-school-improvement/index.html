Five essential organizational supports for improving student learning¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 4 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article reports a Chicago framework in which schools effective at improving student learning have strong organizational structures across five areas: "leadership, professional capacity, partnerships with parents and community, learning climate, and instruction." The framework treats the school as an organization whose supports, not just individual teacher skill, set the stage for instructional effectiveness and improvement in mathematics and reading.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Strong organizational structures across all five areas, tied together with strong instructional practices

Constraints¶

  • Developing a safe, orderly climate is more challenging when a school serves disadvantaged student populations

Target Learners¶

  • Students in urban public schools, including those in high-poverty communities

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Improvements in mathematics and reading achievement

Claims¶

  • Collaboration Beats Individual Teacher Quality [+M]
  • Teacher Quality Gains Depend On School Climate [+M]
  • Schools serving highly disadvantaged students with strong organizational supports improve learning and retain teachers as well as more advantaged schools [+W]
  • Teachers are more likely to stay in schools where parents support their work and where students feel safe and peers behave appropriately [+W]
  • Teachers are more likely to stay in schools where they perceive colleagues as collaborators, feel influence over their work environment, and trust their principal as an instructional leader [+W]
  • Chicago school organizational supports (leadership, professional capacity, parent involvement) improved across eras, but instructional quality did not, and student-reported teacher support declined after 2005 [+W]
  • The five essential supports matter in combination: strength on clusters of supports, not isolated domains, predicts improvement [+W]
  • Family voices contributed to differentiating strong and weak organizational conditions across all essentials, not only Involved Families [+W]
