School choice architecture as a behavioral design framework¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article organizes its inquiry around the idea that the architecture of school choice — the ways school information is presented to parents — systematically shapes parents' knowledge, attitudes, and decisions. It frames presentation design as a set of manipulable factors: "The study examined the effects of five design factors on knowledge about the schools, attitudes about using the online school chooser tool, and school choice behavior." This treats choice interfaces as nudges whose defaults and formats can be engineered to improve decisions.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Choice platforms whose information presentation can be systematically varied and evaluated

  • Developed and tested in the context of low-income parents using an online school chooser tool

Target Learners¶

  • Low-income parents choosing schools for their children

  • Informed school choice decisions

Claims¶

  • Five Design Factors Impact Parent School Choice Outcomes [+M]
  • Setting the default sort order to academic performance instead of distance from home induces parents to choose academically higher performing schools [+W]
  • Changing the default order in which schools are presented induces meaningful changes in the types of schools low-income parents choose [+W]
  • Displaying data with icons or concise summaries led parents to choose schools with higher academic performance than graphs, numbers, or detailed displays [+W]
  • Display design affected parents' understanding of school information and their self-reported satisfaction and ease of use [+W]
  • How school information is presented can affect how parents rank their school choices [+W]
