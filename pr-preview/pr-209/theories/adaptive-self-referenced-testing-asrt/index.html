Adaptive Self-Referenced Testing (ASRT): a framework for measuring individual achievement growth via computerized adaptive testing¶

Theory · All theories Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

ASRT is a proposed framework, arising from the project's finding that norm-referenced and criterion-referenced approaches lack a common structure, for measuring individual improvement in achievement during instruction. The report states "ASRT can incorporaw-,P intot tramework the notions.of inter-subtestbranching, intelr-time 4 ,f..thching, And mAstery testing." It measures growth on an individual basis, using the student's performance at an earlier point in time as the starting point for later measurement, and is designed to track an individual's growth in one achievement area as a function of time, identifying the degree and extent of learning as it occurs. It is described as designed uniquely for computer-assisted and computer-managed instruction, permitting continuous evaluation of student progress and a non-normative definition of when and how much learning has occurred while minimizing testing time.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires computerized adaptive testing combined with ICC theory, since the report states the framework is only possible by combining these

Constraints¶

  • The report notes the generalization of unidimensional self-referenced testing to the multidimensional case incorporates the inter-test branching problem and remains to be developed

Target Learners¶

  • students in computer-assisted or computer-managed instructional environments
  • trainees in military and postsecondary instructional settings

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • measuring individual achievement gains during and after instruction
  • mastery decisions
  • tracking learning as it occurs
