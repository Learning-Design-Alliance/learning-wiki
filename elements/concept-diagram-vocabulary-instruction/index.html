Concept diagram for vocabulary instruction¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A graphic organizer used as the study's independent variable. The concept model "utilized a diagram encompassing the vocabulary word, the definition and characteristics, and examples and non-examples." During intervention the teacher presented a blank diagram, wrote the word and its definition, then elicited and listed characteristics that were always, sometimes, and never present, followed by examples and non-examples linked to those characteristics. Paper copies and overhead transparencies of the diagrams were the core materials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher-led sessions with an overhead projector or paper copies of blank diagrams
  • A pre-written concept diagram for each target vocabulary word for experimenter use

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated in this study only with seventh-grade students with learning disabilities learning mathematical vocabulary in a resource language arts classroom

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students with learning disabilities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • content-area (mathematical) vocabulary knowledge

Claims¶

  • The concept model of vocabulary instruction produced larger gains in content-area vocabulary test performance than the definition/sentence-writing model for all six middle school students with learning disabilities [+W]
  • Students with learning disabilities reported high satisfaction with learning vocabulary through concept diagrams [+W]
  • After concept-model instruction, students' posttest vocabulary scores ranged from 57.78% to 82.22% correct, compared with pretest scores of 0% to 8.89% [+W]
