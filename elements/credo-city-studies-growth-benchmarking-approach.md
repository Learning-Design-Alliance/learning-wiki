# CREDO City Studies Project growth-benchmarking analysis approach

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The City Studies Project is a recurring reporting and analysis program that compares academic performance of student groups in selected U.S. cities to average statewide performance, "while accounting for diﬀerences in student demographic characteristics based on statistical analysis." It focuses on academic growth to isolate a school's impact on learning, compares charter, magnet, and innovation students to similar district students within each city, and translates effect sizes to days-of-learning using a benchmark (large >|0.2|, medium |0.05|-|0.2|, small <|0.05|) proposed by Kraft (2020). Cohort 1 covers 10 cities over the 2014-15 to 2018-19 growth periods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Statewide comparison data and student demographic characteristics used for statistical controls
- Test-taker data by city, sector, and subgroup (e.g., the 2018-19 snapshot in Table 1)
#### Constraints
- The report states objective interpretation and comparison of effect sizes across cities is difficult due to city- and state-specific macro- and micro-level factors
- Washington DC growth is benchmarked against the city average rather than the state average

### Target Learners
- K-12 public school students in the 10 studied cities

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance growth in reading and mathematics
