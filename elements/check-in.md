# Check In

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A check in is a short, deliberate touchpoint — a question, poll, one-minute paper, or brief conference — through which an instructor or system elicits learners' current state: their understanding, misconceptions, progress, or emotional readiness. It functions as a diagnostic and relational mechanism, feeding information forward so instruction can be adjusted rather than delivered blind.

## Design Implications

Check ins make learner status visible early enough to act on, converting assumptions about comprehension into evidence [Formative feedback improves learning when it is used to adjust instruction.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. They also serve a relational function: regular, low-stakes check ins signal that the instructor is attending to individuals, which supports belonging and motivation [Autonomy-supportive teaching contexts raise intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. The value of a check in depends almost entirely on what happens next — data gathered and ignored erodes trust and adds no learning benefit.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A specific, answerable prompt (understanding, progress, or affect) — not a generic "any questions?"
- A low-stakes channel that makes honest responses safe (anonymous polls, exit tickets, one-on-one formats)
- A visible response loop: the instructor adjusts, re-teaches, or acknowledges what was heard ([Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Check ins that collect data but produce no instructional change waste time and teach learners that responses don't matter [Formative assessment benefits depend on instructional adjustment, not data collection.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [-M]
- Public or graded check ins suppress honest reporting of confusion, especially for anxious or low-status learners
- Frequent interruption of task flow for check ins can fragment attention and add extraneous load [~W]
- In large asynchronous settings, check ins without rapid response loops decay into unread surveys

### Target Learners
- Novices, who often cannot judge their own comprehension accurately and benefit from external diagnosis [~M]
- Learners in online or large-enrollment settings where instructors otherwise have little visibility into individual progress
- Learners who benefit from autonomy support; check ins framed as "what do you need?" rather than "did you do the work?" support intrinsic motivation [Autonomy-supportive teaching contexts raise intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulated learning: prompting learners to monitor their own understanding and progress
- Misconception diagnosis: surfacing errors early enough for targeted re-teaching
- Affective readiness: establishing emotional state and belonging before demanding tasks

### Affordances
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — a check in is the lightest-weight formative assessment move: gather evidence, interpret it, adjust instruction within the same session
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — check ins provide the diagnostic information that makes scaffolding contingent rather than fixed [Contingent scaffolding responsive to learner state improves outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- [Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) — regular check ins sustain social and teaching presence, the relational substrate of online learning communities
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — well-designed check ins model the monitoring phase of self-regulation, prompting learners to ask of themselves what the instructor asks of them

## Related Elements
- [Assessment](assessment.md) — check ins are ungraded, low-stakes cousins of assessment; both gather evidence of learner state
- [Coaching](coaching.md) — one-on-one check ins are the entry point of a coaching cycle
- [Articulation](articulation.md) — asking learners to state their current understanding is a check in that doubles as an articulation exercise
- [Feedback](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — the instructional response to a check in is where its learning value is realized

## Examples

**[5-Minute Writing Conferences](../strategies/5-minute_writing_conferences.md)** — Brief one-on-one check ins during writing workshop in which the teacher asks about the draft's direction and offers one targeted suggestion.

**["What's My Emotion?" Game Check-In](../strategies/whats_my_emotion_game_check-in.md)** — An affective check in that has learners identify and name emotional states, building readiness and emotional vocabulary before instruction.

**[Muddiest Point](../strategies/active-listening.md)** — End-of-class prompt asking "What was most confusing today?"; responses drive the opening of the next session.

**[Pulse polls in Canvas/Moodle](https://www.instructure.com/canvas)** — One-question anonymous polls at the start of an online module, with results summarized and addressed in the next announcement or synchronous session.
