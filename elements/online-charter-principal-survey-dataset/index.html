National Study of Online Charter Schools principal survey¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A nationwide survey of online charter school principals, described as the data source for "the first nationwide data and analysis of the operations and instructional approaches of online charter schools." It was completed by 127 principals of online charter schools across the country and was collected as part of research funded by the Walton Family Foundation. The survey underpins the brief's highlights of operational and instructional practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Completion by principals of online charter schools, as the survey respondents are described as 127 principals of online charter schools across the country.

Constraints¶

  • The brief page reports only highlights; full survey findings reside in the full publication.

Target Learners¶

  • Students enrolled in online charter schools at elementary, middle, and high school grade levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding the operations and instructional approaches of online charter schools

Claims¶

  • Few studies of online instruction's operations and effects existed before the National Study of Online Charter Schools [+W]
  • The National Study of Online Charter Schools provides the first nationwide data and analysis of online charter schools' operations and instructional approaches, based on a survey of 127 principals [+W]
  • About 200 online charter schools operate in the United States, serving about 200,000 students across elementary, middle, and high school grades [+W]
  • Maintaining student engagement is the greatest challenge reported by online charter school principals, identified nearly three times as often as any other challenge [+W]
  • Student-driven, independent study is the dominant mode of learning in online charter schools, with 33 percent offering only self-paced instruction [+W]
  • Online charter schools place significant expectations on parents, with 43, 56, and 78 percent at high school, middle, and elementary levels expecting active parental participation in instruction [+W]
  • Online charter school students receive less live teacher contact time in a week than conventional-school students receive in a day [+W]
