# Inquiry

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Inquiry places learners in the role of investigator: they pose or pursue questions, collect and evaluate evidence, and construct explanations with instructor support. It ranges along a continuum from structured (instructor-provided question and procedure) to open (learner-generated questions and methods), with the instructor acting as [Coaching](coaching.md) and facilitator rather than primary explainer.

## Design Implications

Inquiry supports learning by engaging learners in knowledge construction, which can deepen conceptual understanding and retention compared with passive reception [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends heavily on structure: unguided or minimally guided inquiry tends to overload working memory for novices, while scaffolded inquiry — with questions, prompts, and worked models — produces strong outcomes [Unguided inquiry overloads novice working memory; scaffolding is required.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~S]. Cognitive conflict between predictions and evidence is a key mechanism, driving conceptual change when learners confront discrepant data [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-posed question or problem that is genuinely answerable through investigation
- Scaffolds: guiding questions, evidence organizers, or partially worked procedures that structure the investigation ([Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md), [Case Studies](case-studies.md))
- Instructor [Coaching](coaching.md) and feedback during the investigation, not only at the end [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- A synthesis phase where learners articulate and defend conclusions ([Articulation](articulation.md), [Argumentation](argumentation.md))

#### Constraints
- Minimally guided inquiry is ineffective for novices, who lack the schemas to direct their own search [Unguided inquiry overloads novice working memory; scaffolding is required.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]
- Inquiry is time-intensive; coverage-driven curricula often cannot afford the investigation cycle
- Learners can draw wrong conclusions from evidence without instructor checking, entrenching misconceptions [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [~M]
- Benefits diminish or reverse for advanced learners, who learn more from direct problem solving than scaffolded exploration [Unguided inquiry overloads novice working memory; scaffolding is required.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge, enough to interpret evidence but not yet to self-direct [Unguided inquiry overloads novice working memory; scaffolding is required.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Learners holding misconceptions that evidence-based conflict can surface and revise [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M]
- Novices require substantial structure; pure discovery is not appropriate for them [Unguided inquiry overloads novice working memory; scaffolding is required.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding and conceptual change, especially in science and social studies
- Epistemic practices: forming hypotheses, evaluating evidence, arguing from data ([Argumentation](argumentation.md))
- Transferable investigative and self-directed learning skills

### Affordances
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — inquiry is a canonical enactment: learners generate and manipulate ideas rather than receive them, producing the engagement and retrieval conditions active learning research identifies
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — structured inquiry manages load by constraining the question, providing procedures, and sequencing evidence so novices investigate without unguided search
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the inquiry cycle enacts the exploration and articulation phases, with learners practicing expert investigative methods under coaching
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — group investigation distributes evidence-gathering and forces learners to negotiate explanations, exposing reasoning to scrutiny

## Related Elements

- [Case Studies](case-studies.md) — a structured inquiry format using authentic cases as the object of investigation

- [Argumentation](argumentation.md) — the practice of defending conclusions from evidence, central to inquiry outcomes

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — inquiry into an authentic case with instructor facilitation
- [Collaborative Inquiry](../patterns/collaborative-inquiry.md) — groups jointly posing and investigating questions
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — exploration phase, where learners attempt investigations with coaching and fading support

## Examples

**[Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md)** — Learners analyze an authentic problem case, identify what they need to know, investigate, and defend a resolution; the case supplies structure while the investigation remains learner-driven.

**BSCS Science: An Inquiry Approach** — A published secondary science curriculum organized around structured inquiry sequences (engage, investigate, explain) with scaffolded evidence analysis. [https://bscs.org](https://bscs.org)

**[Class Discussion](class-discussion.md) with discrepant events** — Instructor presents a demonstration whose outcome contradicts learners' predictions, then facilitates discussion where learners reconcile evidence with prior beliefs.
