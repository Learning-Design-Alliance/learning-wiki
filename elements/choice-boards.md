# Choice Boards

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A choice board is a visual matrix of learning activities — often a 3×3 "tic-tac-toe" grid — from which learners select a required subset (e.g., any three in a row, one from each column). It converts a single assigned task into a bounded set of options, giving learners autonomy over *how* they engage with content or demonstrate learning while the instructor retains control over *what* is learned.

## Design Implications

Choice boards support learning primarily through motivation: perceived autonomy satisfies a basic psychological need and raises intrinsic motivation and engagement [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. The design problem is bounding the choice — options must be genuinely equivalent in rigor and aligned to the same learning goal, or learners will select on effort rather than fit. Choice works best when it is meaningful but limited; the board structure itself is a [chunking](../principles/chunking.md) device that keeps the decision manageable.

### Context
#### Requirements
- All options target the same learning goal or standard; the board differentiates *process or product*, not the objective
- Options of comparable rigor and time demand, so no path is a "shortcut"
- Clear completion rules (how many, which pattern, by when) so autonomy does not become ambiguity
- Success criteria or a rubric shared across all options, so assessment stays comparable ([Assessment](assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Too many options (or options that are hard to compare) increases decision time and can depress motivation rather than raise it [~M] — choice overload effects documented in consumer and classroom contexts
- Choice over *what to learn* shows weaker effects on performance than choice over *how to learn or respond* [~M]; the meta-analytic benefit of choice is strongest for motivation, smaller for achievement
- Novices often lack the knowledge to choose productively and may pick tasks misaligned with their actual needs [~M] — guidance becomes more valuable as expertise decreases [Learning support must adapt to learner expertise.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- If easier options are visibly "cheaper," learners gravitate toward minimal effort, undermining the board's rigor [-W]

### Target Learners
- Learners with developed self-regulation who can plan across multiple sessions [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Learners who benefit from [Accommodations](accommodations.md) — choice boards are a core Universal Design for Learning structure for offering multiple means of action and expression
- Less effective for complete novices, who need more instructor-structured pathways until they can evaluate options [Learning support must adapt to learner expertise.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Application and synthesis: showing understanding through varied products ([Application](application.md))
- Motivation and engagement goals: increasing ownership and time-on-task [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Self-regulation: planning, sequencing, and committing to choices across a unit

### Affordances
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — every board option is a task the learner *does* (create, argue, model, perform), so the board enacts active learning by construction rather than by assignment
- [Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md) — a well-designed board lets learners self-select entry points matching their readiness or interest, a low-tech analogue to adaptive sequencing; pairing with [Adaptive Difficulty](adaptive-difficulty.md) tightens the fit
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — the grid format presents a bounded, scannable option set, keeping the choice decision itself from consuming working memory
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — when all options map to shared criteria, the board lets learners choose their demonstration mode while keeping evidence of learning comparable

## Related Elements
- [Accommodations](accommodations.md) — choice boards operationalize "multiple means of action and expression" for learners with processing or expression differences
- [Adaptive Difficulty](adaptive-difficulty.md) — tiered boards (columns by complexity) let learners self-calibrate challenge
- [Application](application.md) — most board options are application tasks rather than recall tasks
- [Assessment](assessment.md) — shared rubrics keep varied products assessable against one standard
- [Check-In](check-in.md) — brief conferences verify that choices match readiness and keep plans on track

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md) — boards structure the in-class application time with learner-selected practice paths
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — multiple demonstration options let learners evidence the same competency in different ways
- [Blended Learning](../patterns/blended-learning.md) — digital boards (linked slides or docs) organize station and online options in one place
