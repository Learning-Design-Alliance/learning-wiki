# Concept-Based Language Instruction with Gal'perin's verbalization phases

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Concept-Based Language Instruction is a classroom approach SCT-L2 researchers have investigated for about 15 years, based on P. Gal'perin's educational model. It integrates two verbalization phases: one in which "learners explain to other individuals such as peers or even the teacher, their understanding of a particular language concept", and another in which students explain the concept to themselves. Lantolf frames the first as interpersonal and the second as intrapersonal communication/thinking; Merrill Swain labeled these communication-oriented phases as focused on internalizing conceptual knowledge rather than typical social interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presentation of a language concept in class followed by both interpersonal and intrapersonal verbalization phases
#### Constraints
- Research to date covers grammar, pragmatics, and some reading and writing, but Lantolf is unaware of C-BLI research on pronunciation or vocabulary

### Target Learners
- second language learners in ordinary L2 classroom settings

### Target Learning Goals
- internalization of conceptual knowledge of the target language, including grammar and pragmatics
