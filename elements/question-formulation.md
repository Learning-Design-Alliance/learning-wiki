# Question Formulation

## Description
Question Formulation asks learners to generate, refine, and prioritize their own questions about a topic before or during inquiry, rather than responding only to instructor-posed questions. The questions learners produce become the driving agenda for research, discussion, or investigation, positioning them as owners of the inquiry process.

## Design Implications

Generating questions forces learners to surface gaps in their own understanding, which supports monitoring of what they do and do not yet know [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. The act of formulating a question also requires learners to represent the problem space themselves, which deepens processing compared with answering pre-set questions [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. Quality depends on scaffolding: learners need criteria for what makes a question productive (open vs. closed, researchable vs. speculative) and a structured procedure for improving raw questions.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A topic or stimulus rich enough to admit multiple genuine questions
- Explicit criteria or a protocol for evaluating and refining questions (e.g., open vs. closed, prioritization)
- A downstream purpose — the questions must actually drive subsequent [Research](research.md), discussion, or investigation, or generation becomes a disconnected exercise
- Time and psychological safety for divergent generation before evaluation

#### Constraints
- Novices often generate surface-level or unanswerable questions without models of good questions or feedback on drafts [-M]
- Unstructured question generation can send learners into unproductive search, increasing extraneous load when prior knowledge is low [~M]
- If generated questions are never used, learners perceive the activity as busywork and engagement drops [-W]
- Less effective under high time pressure, which biases learners toward closed, low-risk questions [-W]

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and advanced learners in research-heavy disciplines and seminar settings, who have enough domain knowledge to ask meaningful questions
- Learners developing [Self-Regulated Learning](../principles/self-regulated-learning.md) capacity, since question generation is a core self-monitoring move [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Novices benefit only with heavy scaffolding, question stems, or worked models of good questions [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Curiosity and motivation: ownership of the inquiry agenda increases task value [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Metacognitive monitoring: identifying what is unknown and worth knowing
- Deep analysis: framing open, evaluative, and comparative questions rather than recall questions
- Research skill development: translating curiosity into researchable inquiries

### Affordances
- [Metacognition](../principles/metacognition.md) — generating questions makes learners' knowledge gaps explicit to themselves, enacting monitoring of comprehension
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../principles/self-regulated-learning.md) — learner-generated questions set the goals that drive subsequent planning, monitoring, and evaluation cycles
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — question formulation replaces passive reception with generative cognitive work
- [Cognitive Activation](../principles/cognitive-activation.md) — open, learner-owned questions provoke elaborated thinking rather than reproduction of taught content

## Examples

**[Right Question Institute — Question Formulation Technique](https://rightquestion.org/what-is-the-qft/)** — A published six-step protocol: produce questions in response to a prompt, improve them (open↔closed conversion), prioritize, and use them. Widely adopted in K-12 and higher education.

**[PBL learning issues (Maastricht University)](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/education/problem-based-learning)** — In the seven-step Maastricht PBL method, students generate "learning issues" — the questions they must answer before the next tutorial — which then structure their self-directed study.

**IB Extended Essay (International Baccalaureate)** — Students must formulate their own research question as the foundation of a 4,000-word independent research project; the quality of the question is an explicit assessment criterion.
