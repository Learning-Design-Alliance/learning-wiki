# Learning Objectives

## Description
Learning objectives are explicit statements of the knowledge, skills, or dispositions learners should acquire, typically phrased in observable terms ("analyze," "construct," "compare" rather than "understand" or "appreciate"). They function as the design contract of a learning experience: aligning content, activities, and assessment around a shared target.

## Design Implications

Well-specified objectives improve learning primarily by orienting learners' attention and enabling self-monitoring, and by forcing designers to align instruction and assessment [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Objectives stated in specific, observable terms support better assessment design than vague goals, because they make the evidence of success decidable [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Objectives should be visible to learners, referenced during instruction, and revisited at closure — an objective that exists only in the lesson plan does little work.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Observable, measurable verbs tied to the target performance (Bloom's taxonomy or similar)
- Alignment: every activity and assessment maps to at least one objective ([Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md))
- Learner-facing presentation at the start of instruction ([Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md))
- Criteria or rubrics that translate objectives into assessable evidence ([Assessment](assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Objectives limited to lower-order verbs can narrow instruction to recall and depress higher-order thinking [~M] — listing only "identify" and "define" objectives signals that analysis is not valued
- Overly granular objective lists fragment learning and add [cognitive load](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) without benefit [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Objectives fixed in advance fit poorly with emergent, inquiry-driven learning, where forcing pre-specified outcomes can undercut exploration [~W]
- Learners may treat objectives as a checklist to satisfy rather than a standard to internalize, encouraging surface compliance [-W]

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack the background knowledge to infer what matters and benefit from explicit signposting [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners developing self-regulation, who need targets against which to monitor their own progress [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Less necessary for advanced learners, who can set their own goals within a domain

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill acquisition: specifying the observable performance to be mastered
- Conceptual understanding: framing what learners should be able to explain, apply, or transfer
- Assessment and credentialing: defining competencies for [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md)

### Affordances
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md) — objectives enact this principle by giving learners an advance organizer for the lesson, reducing uncertainty about what to attend to
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — objectives provide the reference point against which feedback and self-assessment become meaningful; without a stated target, feedback is just commentary
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — a small number of well-chosen objectives tells learners what to prioritize, protecting working memory from irrelevant detail
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — objectives define the trajectory along which support is faded, making the endpoint explicit

## Examples

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Every exercise and unit is labeled with specific skill objectives ("Solve two-step linear equations"), which drive the mastery-tracking system and let learners see exactly what each activity targets.

**Understanding by Design (Wiggins & McTighe)** — A published curriculum design framework that begins with "desired results" (objectives) and works backward to evidence and learning plan; widely used in K–12 curriculum development.

**[Coursera](https://www.coursera.org)** — Course and module pages lead with "What you'll learn" objective lists, giving prospective and enrolled learners explicit targets before content begins.
