# SimCalc curricular activity system integrating dynamic representation software, workbooks, and teacher professional development

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SimCalc intervention is an integrated "curricular activity system" combining dynamic representation software, paper workbooks, and teacher professional development, used as roughly three-week replacement units on rate and proportionality (7th grade) and linear function (8th grade). Its hallmarks include anchoring mathematics in "familiar motions, which are portrayed as computer animations," having students build and edit graphs and algebraic functions that control animations, and a pedagogy cycle of predicting, comparing, and explaining. The software links position-time animations to graphical, algebraic, and tabular representations, and the article stresses the integration of components rather than the technology alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must not skip lessons, must allow students to use the technology, and must discuss the key mathematical ideas of the unit, per the case-study findings
#### Constraints
- The findings are specific to interventions using SimCalc-style dynamic representations integrated with workbooks and professional development; the authors expect technology alone or weakly aligned components would not produce the same effects

### Target Learners
- Middle school students in 7th and 8th grade mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of rate, proportionality, and linear function on the path to Algebra
