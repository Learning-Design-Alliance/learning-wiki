# Assigned Readings

## Description
Assigned readings place curated texts — textbook chapters, articles, cases, or primary sources — in learners' hands so they build conceptual foundations at their own pace. The element shifts first exposure to content outside of group time, freeing synchronous sessions for application, discussion, and clarification.

## Design Implications

Assigned readings support self-paced encoding and allow learners to revisit difficult passages, but their effectiveness depends on what learners *do* with the text: unstructured reading assignments are frequently skipped or skimmed [~M]. Prompting learners to explain material to themselves while reading substantially improves comprehension [Prompting learners to self-explain text improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S], and reading guides with embedded questions or annotations convert passive reading into generative processing. Readings should be chunked into manageable segments with clear purpose statements, since long undifferentiated assignments overwhelm working memory and motivation [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear purpose statement — why this text, and what learners should be able to do afterward
- Reading prompts, guiding questions, or annotation tasks that elicit generative processing ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), [Self-Explanation](self-explanation.md))
- Accountability structures: pre-class quizzes, discussion roles, or social annotation so reading is visibly consequential
- Text difficulty matched to learner reading ability, or scaffolds ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md), glossaries) where it is not

#### Constraints
- Rereading and passive exposure produce weak retention; readings without retrieval or application yield poor learning [Rereading is among the least effective study techniques relative to practice testing and elaborative strategies.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031308) [-S]
- Assignments without accountability are widely skipped; compliance rises only when reading is assessed or used in class [~M]
- Dense or discipline-unfamiliar texts can overload novices, producing surface skimming rather than comprehension
- Less effective for procedural or motor skills, where text is a poor substitute for demonstration and [Practice](practice.md)

### Target Learners
- University students and self-directed professionals with sufficient reading fluency and study strategies
- Learners with prior knowledge who can integrate new text with existing schemas; complete novices need more scaffolding or a [Demonstration](demonstration.md) first
- Less effective for young learners or those with limited academic literacy without substantial support

### Target Learning Goals
- Building background knowledge and conceptual foundations before application
- Preparing learners for analysis, discussion, and case work
- Developing disciplinary literacy — reading primary sources and research as practitioners do

### Affordances
- [Constructivism](../principles/constructivism.md) — readings let learners actively construct meaning from text at their own pace, connecting new information to prior knowledge rather than receiving a pre-digested account
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — self-paced text lets learners pause, reread, and segment material, controlling the pace of information intake in a way lectures cannot
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — paired with annotation, self-explanation prompts, or social annotation, reading becomes a generative activity rather than passive exposure
- [Activation](../principles/activation.md) — pre-reading questions and advance organizers activate relevant prior knowledge before learners encounter the text

## Examples

**[Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)** — Social annotation platform where learners annotate assigned readings collaboratively; the platform scores annotation quality, adding built-in accountability.

**[Hypothesis](https://web.hypothes.is)** — Open web annotation used in courses to make reading a visible, social activity; instructors seed annotations with guiding questions.

**Reading guides with embedded self-explanation prompts** — Instructors insert prompts ("Explain in your own words why this result holds") at intervals in the text, a technique grounded in self-explanation research [Prompting learners to self-explain text improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].
