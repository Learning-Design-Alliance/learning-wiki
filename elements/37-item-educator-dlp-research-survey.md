# 37-item educator survey on DLP research perceptions

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 9 studies (9 associational), `q2` · 0 of 9 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 37-item survey developed to identify perspectives of K-12 and higher education educators on research conducted in DLPs, adapted from a questionnaire previously administered at Rice University. It used branching logic to tailor questions by educational context, with 18 common items on research involvement, awareness, and ethics on a 1-to-5 agreement scale, plus K-12 and higher education context items. It was disseminated nationally from February 2025 to June 2025 and yielded 43 respondents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Branching logic to tailor questions based on the participant's primary educational context
#### Constraints
- Participants were not required to answer every item, producing minor discrepancies between total respondents and per-item responses

### Target Learners
- K-12 educators
- higher education educators

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding educator perceptions of DLP-based research benefits, challenges, and ethical considerations

## Claims

- [Both educator groups rank research to inform best practices in teaching and learning as the most important research goal](../claims/best-practices-research-top-goal-educators.md) [+W]
- [K-12 and higher education educators express generally positive feelings about research on digital learning platforms, with no negative respondents](../claims/educators-positive-feelings-dlp-research.md) [+W]
- [Access to evidence-based strategies and understanding how students learn are top motivators for both groups' interest in DLP research](../claims/evidence-based-strategies-top-motivator.md) [+W]
- [Higher education educators report greater awareness of research on digital learning platforms than K-12 educators](../claims/higher-ed-greater-dlp-research-awareness.md) [+W]
- [K-12 educators report little direct involvement in DLP research while higher education participants are evenly split, and report higher-oversight roles](../claims/k12-lower-dlp-research-participation.md) [+M]
- [K-12 educators value product-improvement research more, and individual-adaptation research less, than higher education educators](../claims/k12-product-functionality-goal-difference.md) [+W]
- [Educators rate student data privacy as very or extremely important, but K-12 educators largely remain unaware of the privacy measures DLP research takes](../claims/k12-unaware-dlp-privacy-measures.md) [+M]
