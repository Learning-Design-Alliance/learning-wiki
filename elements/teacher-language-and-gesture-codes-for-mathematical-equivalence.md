# Teacher Language and Gesture Coding Scheme for Mathematical Equivalence

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A coding scheme with four language codes (ISA, "is the same as"; DEF, definition; BAL, balance; FORM, equation form) and two gesture codes (GBAL, balance gesture; GSIDES, equation sides gesture) for a teacher's talk about equations. The authors developed it iteratively from classroom video, settling on a scheme "that focused on the teacher’s use of language around equations and the meaning of the equal sign during whole-group discussion."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Transcribed classroom video of the lessons that address mathematical equivalence
- Iterative, collaborative coding by several team members with group consensus, following Syed and Nelson's (2015) recommendations as the article describes
#### Constraints
- Applied to whole-group discussion only
- The analysis covered one of the two intervention classrooms and seven of its eighteen lessons

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten students in the Northeastern U.S. taking part in an early algebra intervention

### Target Learning Goals
- A relational understanding of the equal sign as indicating the equivalence of two amounts
- Acceptance of equations in forms other than a + b = c
