New Jersey's three high school equivalency exam options (GED, HiSET, TASC)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Since 2014 the New Jersey Department of Education has offered three high school equivalency exams for nongraduates seeking credentials: the GED, the High School Equivalency Test (HiSET), and the Test Assessing Secondary Completion (TASC). The study analyzed takers of these exams using records of students who were grade 8 public school students between 2008/09 and 2013/14 and attempted at least one exam between March 2014 and December 2018. The exams serve as alternative credential pathways, and the state sets and revises their passing thresholds.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Offered by the New Jersey Department of Education for nongraduates seeking high school equivalency credentials

Constraints¶

  • The study covers exam attempts in New Jersey between March 2014 and December 2018 by students who had been grade 8 public school students in the state between 2008/09 and 2013/14

Target Learners¶

  • Nongraduates seeking a high school equivalency credential in New Jersey

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Attainment of a high school equivalency credential

Claims¶

  • GED takers had higher grade 8 test scores and were more likely White and less likely lunch-eligible than HiSET and TASC takers [+W]
  • HSE exam takers completed fewer years of school and were more likely to be lunch-eligible and to identify as Black or Hispanic than non-exam takers [+W]
  • Under the new thresholds, two-thirds of GED takers and more than half of HiSET and TASC takers passed on the first attempt, and three-quarters of all takers ever passed each exam [+W]
  • Under New Jersey's original passing thresholds, first-attempt passing was more likely on the HiSET and TASC than on the GED after controlling for grade 8 test scores [+W]
  • After New Jersey reduced the passing thresholds for two exams, first-attempt passing rates were similar across the three HSE exams [+W]
