Title I Supplemental Educational Services (SES) tutoring program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

SES are "tutoring or other academic support services offered outside the regular school day, at no charge to students or their families, by public or private organizations that have been approved by the state as SES providers." Districts must offer SES to low-income students in Title I schools missing AYP for a third year. In the study, services averaged 21.2 hours per student (SD 8.8), with 36 percent of participants tutored in both reading and math, 55 percent in reading only, and 9 percent in math only.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State-approved providers; districts must reserve up to 20 percent of Title I, Part A funds for SES and school-choice transportation

Constraints¶

  • The study's estimates apply to students near the cutoff for an offer in oversubscribed districts, not to all eligible students

Target Learners¶

  • Low-income students in grades 3-8 in Title I schools that missed AYP for three consecutive years

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading achievement
  • Mathematics achievement

Claims¶

  • Offering SES to eligible applicants near the cutoff in oversubscribed districts has no statistically significant impact on reading or math achievement [-M]
  • Participating in SES (averaging about 21 hours) shows no statistically significant impact on reading or math achievement for students near the cutoff [-M]
  • Intensity of SES services and other provider characteristics are not significantly related to estimated impacts [-M]
  • Catapult Learning Wraparound SES significantly improves middle school math achievement (ES +0.21) [+W]
  • Wraparound SES math gains are notable for Hispanic, female, and Title I students [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • SES provider landscape and service delivery characteristics in oversubscribed districts
  • Wraparound support services: counseling and special education support alongside academic instruction
  • Regression discontinuity design exploiting oversubscription rationing of SEdS
  • Supplemental Educational Services (SEdS) under No Child Left Behind
