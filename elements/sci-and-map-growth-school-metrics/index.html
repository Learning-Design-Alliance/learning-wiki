School Challenge Index (SCI) and MAP Growth conditional growth metrics for school-level poverty and growth measurement¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The School Challenge Index (SCI) is an NWEA metric created during the 2011 norms development to consistently measure the level of challenge a school faces across the United States. The article explains that "The SCI is primarily based on the percentage of students eligible for FRL, but is moderated by other factors, such as location, school level, Title I eligibility, and school type." MAP Growth supplies the achievement and growth data, with conditional growth index (CGI) and conditional growth percentile (CGP) metrics designed to compare the relative growth of different students fairly. The study used median achievement and growth percentiles per school as its school-level measures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires precise measurement across the achievement distribution on an equal interval scale, which the article says adaptive tests such as MAP Growth provide

Constraints¶

  • FRL is a dichotomous measure that the article says does not provide as much information as a continuous poverty scale or multiple SES measures would

Target Learners¶

  • K-10 students in U.S. public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate measurement of student achievement and growth for school accountability and equity analysis

Claims¶

  • About 46% of the lowest 5% achieving schools show the same growth as the highest 5% achieving schools [+W]
  • 77% of the lowest 5% achieving schools (spring reading) grow their typical student better than the 30th percentile [+W]
  • Growth of schools selected by fall achievement differs by subject: bottom 5% math schools tended to grow worse, while reading showed similar average growth [+W]
  • School poverty (%FRL) accounts for about 50% of a school's median student achievement [+W]
  • School poverty is only weakly associated with median student growth in a school [+W]
  • Achievement of the lowest-growth schools varies dramatically, and fall achievement accounts for about 80% of spring achievement versus 14% for growth [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • MAP Growth computer-adaptive assessment and its national norms and dashboard
  • 2025 MAP Growth norms update with Enhanced Item Selection Algorithm (EISA) alignment

Examples¶

  • Use growth measures rather than achievement as the primary indicator of school effectiveness in ESSA accountability
