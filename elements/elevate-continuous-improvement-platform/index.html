Elevate continuous improvement platform¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Elevate is a software-enabled continuous improvement platform developed by PERTS and UCCSR. It “equips educators to use research-quality measures and analytics to assess, disaggregate, and track learning conditions over time,” letting educators test the impact of practices on conditions and work in teams to identify high-impact practices. Over the 2021–22 school year, more than 900 teachers at more than 150 schools used it with middle and high school students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators use the learning conditions measures repeatedly to make data-driven decisions about which practices to adopt, adapt, or abandon

Constraints¶

  • Observed benefits in the report's data required four or more planned improvement cycles; ad hoc implementations showed no improvement

Target Learners¶

  • middle and high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving classroom learning conditions such as teacher caring, meaningful work, and feedback for growth

Claims¶

  • Improvements in learning conditions between October and February predict higher likelihood of a B or better in math the following term [+W]
  • Schools running four or more planned continuous improvement cycles show marked improvements in learning conditions, while ad hoc or two-cycle implementations do not [+W]
  • Learning conditions predict math grades across racial and socioeconomic groups, with larger associations for FRPL-eligible and Black students [+W]
  • Learning conditions worsen slightly but significantly over a school year absent intentional efforts to improve them [+W]
  • Students rating learning conditions most positively are more than twice as likely to earn a B or better in math than students rating them most negatively [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Elevate survey for classroom-level student experience data

Examples¶

  • Investigate bright spots to identify and disseminate effective practices
  • Support deep CI implementation through capacity inventories, gradual-release coaching, structured collaboration opportunities, and student voice
