# IES study of 90 elementary schools comparing departmentalized and self-contained instruction in grades 4 and 5

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A study by Mathematica researchers for the Institute of Education Sciences that "examined the experiences of 90 schools that either voluntarily switched to departmentalized instruction or continued teaching all subjects to a single class in 4th and 5th grades for two years beginning in fall 2019." It compares departmentalizing—assigning upper elementary teachers to "teach their strongest subjects to multiple classes"—with self-contained instruction. The study focuses on implementation experiences rather than established outcome effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools in the comparison either voluntarily switched to departmentalized instruction or continued self-contained teaching in 4th and 5th grades
#### Constraints
- Schools voluntarily chose their instructional arrangement, so the comparison is not described as randomized

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary students in grades 4 and 5

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving student achievement and teacher retention through more efficient use of school resources
