# Project Topeka AES/AWE platform for middle school argumentative writing

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Project Topeka is an AES and AWE platform for 7th- and 8th-grade argumentative writing in which teachers access lesson plans, reading materials, and prompt questions, and students submit essay drafts. Students wrote and submitted draft essays through the platform, "receiving instant artificial intelligence (AI) generated scores on a 4-dimension rubric and dimension-level comments" and could revise and resubmit unlimited times until reaching mastery across Claim & Focus, Support & Evidence, Organization, and Language & Style. The report evaluates student score growth on the platform and compares its scores with teacher scoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers assign prompts and close assignments; intended use involves a baseline prompt followed by a revision prompt with iterative resubmission
#### Constraints
- AES feedback is given at the sentence level, which teachers worried students apply sentence by sentence rather than to the whole essay

### Target Learners
- 7th- and 8th-grade middle school students, recruited from schools with at least half free- or reduced-price lunch-eligible students of color

### Target Learning Goals
- Argumentative writing across four rubric dimensions: Claim & Focus, Support & Evidence, Organization, and Language & Style
