# Success for All comprehensive school reform program

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Success for All is "a comprehensive school reform program designed to ensure success in reading for children in high-poverty schools." It provides a K-5 reading curriculum covering phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary, beginning with phonetically-controlled mini-books in grades K-1. Cooperative learning is used extensively at all grade levels; struggling first graders receive one-to-one or small-group tutoring; children are frequently assessed on curriculum-based measures and regrouped across grade lines by current reading level; and a Solutions Team works with parents on attendance, behavior, and social-emotional support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation as a core part of the school program by all teachers and administrators
- A program facilitator, usually an existing staff member such as an assistant principal or Title I Coordinator
- Paraprofessionals allocated to serve as tutors
- About three days of workshop participation per teacher to begin implementation
#### Constraints
- No technology requirements except facilitator access to the internet-based data center; the computer-assisted tutoring option, if implemented, requires three laptops, desktops, or tablets

### Target Learners
- K-5 students in high-poverty elementary schools
- Struggling first-grade readers
- English language learners and special education students

### Target Learning Goals
- Phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary development in reading
