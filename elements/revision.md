# Revision

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Revision is the process by which learners rework a product — a text, design, solution, or argument — in response to [Feedback](feedback.md) and their own [Self-Assessment](self-assessment.md). It treats the first attempt as a draft rather than a final answer, making improvement itself the object of learning.

## Design Implications

Revision converts feedback into measurable learning gains only when learners actively diagnose gaps and decide what to change, not merely copy corrections [Feedback is most effective when learners act on it to close the gap between current and desired performance.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Structured revision cycles — draft, feedback, revise, resubmit — build self-regulatory skill because learners must monitor their own work against criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulated learning.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Requiring learners to explain why they made each change deepens conceptual understanding beyond surface editing [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear criteria or rubrics so learners know what "better" means
- Feedback that is specific, timely, and actionable — oriented to the task and process, not the person ([Feedback](feedback.md))
- Time and structural space in the schedule for a second attempt; revision cannot be an optional add-on
- Learner ownership of the changes — instructor-imposed edits without learner decision-making produce little transfer

#### Constraints
- Feedback alone does not improve work; if learners receive comments but are never required to act on them, performance gains are minimal [Feedback is most effective when learners act on it to close the gap between current and desired performance.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [-M]
- Overly detailed instructor feedback can shift responsibility away from the learner, undermining self-assessment skill development [~M]
- Revision of first drafts is less effective when learners lack the prior knowledge to interpret the feedback; novices may need [Fading](fading.md)-style support or [Coaching](coaching.md) to act on it
- Grade-first sequencing suppresses revision: when a grade accompanies feedback, learners ignore the comments [~S]

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners who have enough domain knowledge to interpret feedback but still make systematic errors
- Writing and research students, where iterative drafting is the discipline's native workflow
- Less effective for complete novices, who may not understand what the feedback refers to without additional modeling

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking: evaluating one's own work against criteria
- Quality refinement: improving accuracy, coherence, and craft of a product
- Self-regulation: developing monitoring and revision habits transferable to future work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulated learning.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — revision operationalizes the self-monitoring and strategy-adjustment phases of the SRL cycle; each revision pass is a full loop of evaluating performance against standards and adapting
- [Mastery Learning](../principles/mastery-learning.md) — revision enacts mastery learning by allowing resubmission until criteria are met, replacing one-shot assessment with iterative improvement toward a standard
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — feedback used for revision makes assessment formative rather than merely evaluative
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — instructor feedback functions as temporary external regulation that should fade as learners internalize criteria through [Self-Assessment](self-assessment.md)

## Related Elements
- [Feedback](feedback.md) — the input that drives revision; without it, revision is guesswork
- [Self-Assessment](self-assessment.md) — the internal complement; learners must eventually generate their own feedback
- [Iterative Learning](iterative-learning.md) — the broader cycle in which revision is one pass
- [Assessment](assessment.md) — rubrics and criteria give revision its target
- [Coaching](coaching.md) — supports learners who cannot yet act on feedback independently

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — revision is the learner action that closes the formative loop
- [Mastery Learning](../patterns/mastery-learning.md) — repeated revision until mastery criteria are met

## Examples

**[Calibrated Peer Review](https://calibratedpeerreview.org)** — Students review anonymized peer work against calibration assignments, then revise their own submissions using the review criteria they practiced.

**Portfolio-based writing programs** — e.g., [New Directions Writing Portfolio at Michigan State](https://wrac.msu.edu) — require multiple documented revision passes with reflective memos explaining what changed and why.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) mastery practice** — Learners retry exercises after hints and feedback until reaching mastery level, a low-stakes revision cycle for procedural skills.
