# Prototype practice framework for differentiated instruction in secondary mathematics

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a prototype practice framework developed within a broader study in regional Australia to assist secondary mathematics teachers in developing and implementing strategies for differentiated instruction. The article states it presents "a novel approach, built around a prototype practice framework, that should enable mathematics teachers, regardless of teaching experience, to progress on a continuum of practice, leading to differentiated instruction that is integral to their teaching." The framework is designed to explain the theoretical basis of differentiation relevant to classroom practice and to examine ways of implementing it, engaging boundary conditions from the REACH blueprint and generative dialogue guiding questions, with implementation via collaboration, co-creation and iterative DBIR processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers participate actively in research, design and implementation, trialling and evaluating practices in their own school context
- Collaboration and co-creation so teacher knowledge develops on an in-school basis
#### Constraints
- The framework is a prototype whose use would support teachers; it is presented as part of an ongoing project rather than a validated finished product

### Target Learners
- secondary mathematics teachers in mixed-ability classrooms in regional Australia

### Target Learning Goals
- bridging the theory-practice gap in differentiated instruction; improving teacher confidence, competence and efficacy in differentiating mathematics instruction
