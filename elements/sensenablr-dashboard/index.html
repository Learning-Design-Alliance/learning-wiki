SensEnablr: a student-facing learning analytics dashboard integrating descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analytics¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

SensEnablr is a student-facing LAD that integrates descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analytics in three columns, anchored in SCT, SRL, constructivism, and transformative learning. It displays engagement relative to class averages, predicted assessment and course outcomes, model interpretability via SHAP, rule-based explanations via Anchors, and counterfactual-based prescriptive feedback. The article states it "integrates a full spectrum of current analytics technologies for sense-making while anchoring them within theoretical educational frameworks."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access restricted to consenting, opted-in students; teachers and administrators were not granted access, per institutional ethics committee instructions

Constraints¶

  • Dashboard was made available four weeks into the semester and remained accessible until the end of the semester
  • Only participating students who provided informed consent could view the dashboard

Target Learners¶

  • tertiary (higher education) students in Business and Sciences courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning
  • student engagement with LMS resources
  • awareness of learning progress and risk status

Claims¶

  • CatBoost achieved the best classification performance among tested algorithms for predicting course completion risk (F-measure .77, accuracy 78%, AUC .87) [+W]
  • The proportion of participating students classified as high-risk fell from 59% in week 4 to 39% by week 6 of the semester [+W]
  • Students reported that the dashboard boosted learning motivation and that predictive and prescriptive insights were beneficial to their learning [+W]
  • Student engagement with LMS learning technologies and course resources increased significantly immediately following interactions with the SensEnablr dashboard [+W]
  • Students endorsed descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive data points driven by a desire to understand study progress and act on it [+W]
  • Comparative dashboard features found less support among online students than recommendations and contact features [~W]
