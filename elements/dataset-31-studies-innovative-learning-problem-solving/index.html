Coded dataset of 31 studies comparing innovative and conventional learning on junior high school mathematics problem-solving ability¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article assembles a coded dataset of 31 primary studies comparing an experimental group taught with an innovative model, strategy, method, or approach against a control group taught conventionally, with junior high school students' mathematical problem-solving ability as the dependent variable. The article states: "The research data of 31 research studies contain numerical information consisting of mean, standard deviation, and sample size for each experimental and control group." Coding recorded study year, sample size, means, and standard deviations for both groups, plus the independent variable (e.g., problem-based learning, SAVI, realistic mathematical approach). The dataset feeds the effect-size, heterogeneity, summary-effect, forest-plot, and trim-and-fill analyses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Numerical information (mean, standard deviation, sample size) for both experimental and control groups in each included study

Constraints¶

  • Data were drawn from studies published in journals, which the authors state as a limitation regarding attention to publication status

Target Learners¶

  • junior high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematical problem-solving ability

Claims¶

  • Individual study effect sizes span moderate to very high: eight studies medium, ten high, and thirteen very high by Cohen-criteria classification [+W]
  • Study effect sizes are heterogeneous (I² reported as 83.197%), so a random-effects model is used for the summary effect [+W]
  • Innovative learning is more effective than conventional learning for junior high school students' mathematical problem-solving ability (summary effect 0.95, 95% CI 0.71–1.19) [+W]
  • Trim-and-fill analysis shows no publication bias in the meta-analysis of innovative learning effects on mathematical problem-solving ability [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Trim-and-fill publication-bias analysis procedure applied to learning-effectiveness meta-analysis
