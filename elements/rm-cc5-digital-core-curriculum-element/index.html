Reasoning Mind Grade 5 Common Core Curriculum (RM-CC5)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

RM-CC5 is a comprehensive, adaptive, blended digital core curriculum for Grade 5 mathematics developed by the nonprofit Reasoning Mind, intended to replace textbook materials. Students progress through missions aligned to 49 grade-level learning objectives (plus below- and above-grade objectives), solving problems at three difficulty levels with immediate feedback, and complete 136 "Smarter Solving" test-preparation activities; game-like features such as narratives, characters, and points motivate students. Teachers receive real-time dashboards with performance data and instructional recommendations, and implementation coordinators coach against an implementation rubric.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sufficient school technology resources and daily use as the core mathematics resource, with approximately 60 hours of required teacher professional development over two years

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions its findings are "not generalizable to all digital offerings" and were obtained in predominantly White, largely rural West Virginia schools using the state WVGSA outcome

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 5 elementary mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mastery of Grade 5 Common Core mathematics learning objectives and preparation for end-of-year state assessment

Claims¶

  • The authors interpret the null result as partly reflecting a tension between adaptive mastery learning and curricular coverage: students who master early topics slowly may not reach later topics tested by the state assessment [~W]
  • Assigning schools to use RM-CC5 as their core Grade 5 mathematics curriculum for a full year did not significantly change state assessment achievement compared with business-as-usual schools [-M]
  • No significant differential treatment effects by prior (Grade 4) achievement level or gender; the Grade 4 Level 3 subgroup difference was marginal (p = .053) [~M]
  • Treatment students substantively engaged with RM-CC5 across the school year and all treatment schools met the program's implementation intensity benchmarks, though engagement varied widely between schools and students [+W]
  • RM-CC5 treatment teachers shifted toward one-on-one instruction with individual students, while control teachers led more whole-group and small-group instruction [+W]
  • Treatment classrooms used technology far more intensively than control classrooms, whose technology use remained supplemental [+W]
  • RM-CC5 usage metrics correlated positively with achievement, and together accounted for an additional 7.3% of variance in Grade 5 scores beyond prior achievement, though causality and relative contributions could not be inferred [+W]
