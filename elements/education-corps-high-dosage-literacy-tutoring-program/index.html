Education Corps in-person high-dosage literacy tutoring program model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Education Corps partners with school districts to deliver "in-person high-dosage literacy tutoring to low-performing K-5 students", designed primarily for K-3 students scoring below grade-level benchmarks. It recruits community tutors requiring only a high school diploma, trains them with a 16-hour asynchronous science-of-reading course plus a 2-hour synchronous capstone, and supports them with former-teacher Learning Coaches. Tutors serve small groups of up to 4 students at least 3 times per week in 30-minute sessions, with a minimum of 30 sessions per student per year, using mClass intervention curriculum or equivalent.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A Tutoring Lead in each school building; districts hire tutors
  • High-quality instructional materials; a progress monitoring system identifying beginning-, middle-, and end-of-year scores

Constraints¶

  • Designed primarily for students in grades K-3 who score below grade-level benchmarks on early literacy skills

Target Learners¶

  • K-3 students scoring below grade-level benchmarks in early literacy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Foundational literacy skills: phonemic awareness, phonics, oral reading fluency, high-frequency words, and word fluency

Claims¶

  • Education Corps tutoring effects are stronger for boys (+0.33) than for girls (near zero), attributed to girls benefiting more from Tier 1 instruction [~M]
  • Education Corps tutoring effects are stronger for students who were not chronically absent (+0.21) [~W]
  • Among third graders, Education Corps tutoring showed a positive but non-significant effect on the state End-of-Grade reading assessment (+0.13) [+W]
  • Education Corps tutoring gains appeared early, with a mid-year effect size of +0.17 [+W]
  • Education Corps high-dosage literacy tutoring raises end-of-year DIBELS Composite scores more than business-as-usual supports for below-benchmark K-3 students [+W]
