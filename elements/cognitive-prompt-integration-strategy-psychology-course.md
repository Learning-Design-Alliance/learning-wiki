# Ad hoc instructional strategy integrating deep- or shallow-processing cognitive prompts into a psychology coursebook chapter

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article designed an instructional strategy that embeds cognitive prompts directly into online textbook content. Two versions of a 'Memory' chapter were built: a deep version with prompts activating "elaboration, organisation" and a surface version with the same number of prompts activating rehearsal strategies. Development followed a revised Bloom's taxonomy to set learning outcomes, then prompt elaboration based on the model of Glogger et al. (2013). Students responded to prompts during a 3-week study task integrated into regular teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompts must be formulated in connection with agreed-upon learning outcomes aligned with the course
- Student responses must be rated for strategy quality to ensure only quality strategy use is analysed
#### Constraints
- Tested only in a compulsory psychology course on one chapter ('Memory') in a realistic setting where variables could not be rigorously controlled

### Target Learners
- psychology students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension of complex scientific course content
- activation of deep learning strategies (elaboration, organisation)
