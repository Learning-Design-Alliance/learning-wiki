# Review

## Description
Review places learners in the role of evaluator: they examine their own or peers' work against criteria before a final version is produced. The act of judging work — identifying strengths, diagnosing weaknesses, and articulating improvements — converts evaluation from an instructor-only function into a learning activity in its own right.

## Design Implications

Review supports learning because evaluating work requires comparing it against a mental model of quality, which surfaces gaps and deepens understanding [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. Reviewing peers' work is particularly effective when reviewers apply explicit criteria or rubrics rather than giving unstructured impressions, and when feedback is used for a subsequent revision rather than terminating the task. Review also builds self-regulatory habits: monitoring one's own work against standards is the core metacognitive skill [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear evaluation criteria or a rubric that makes quality visible and judgeable
- Work that is genuinely revisable — review must precede a final version, not follow it
- Training or calibration for reviewers (e.g., practicing on anonymized samples with instructor-scored benchmarks)
- Time and structure for the work to actually be revised in response to feedback

#### Constraints
- Untrained reviewers give vague or inaccurate feedback; without calibration, peer review can mislead [-M]
- Review of a one-off product with no revision opportunity yields little benefit — feedback without use does not improve performance [-S]
- Novices may lack the schema to recognize quality even when they see it, producing confident but wrong judgments [~M]
- Social dynamics (friendship bias, fear of criticism) can suppress honest peer feedback unless reviews are anonymized or lightly moderated [~M]

### Target Learners
- Students in writing-intensive courses, where iterative critique-and-revision is the disciplinary norm [+M]
- STEM and project-based learners evaluating designs, proofs, code, or lab work against criteria
- Developing self-regulated learners who need structured opportunities to judge work against standards [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Deepening conceptual understanding through evaluation and diagnosis [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- Internalizing quality criteria — learning what "good" looks like in a domain
- Metacognitive monitoring and self-regulation of one's own work

### Affordances
- [Metacognition](../principles/metacognition.md) — review operationalizes metacognition by requiring learners to evaluate work against explicit standards, then apply that evaluative lens to their own drafts
- [Formative Assessment](../principles/formative-assessment.md) — review is formative assessment enacted by learners: feedback arrives while revision is still possible, closing the loop between assessment and learning
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — learners internalize the criteria by which they will be judged, making assessment transparent rather than a black box
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — reviewing is an generative act; learners construct judgments rather than passively receiving them

## Related Elements
- Peer Review — the social form of review; peers exchange work and apply criteria to each other's drafts
- Self-Assessment — the internal form; learners judge their own work against the same criteria
- Revision — the essential follow-on; review without revision is critique without consequence
- [Assessment](assessment.md) — the instructor-led counterpart; review shifts some evaluative responsibility to learners
- [Reflection](reflection.md) — the broader metacognitive activity of which review is a task-specific instance

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — review supplies the learner-side feedback loop
- Self-Regulated Learning — review trains the monitoring and evaluating phases of the self-regulation cycle

## Examples

**[Calibrated Peer Review](https://cpr.molsci.ucla.edu)** — UCLA-developed system in which students first review calibration samples scored by the instructor, then review peers' writing; calibration ensures reviewers apply criteria accurately before their reviews count.

**[Peergrade / Kritik](https://www.kritik.io)** — Platforms that anonymize peer review, distribute submissions, and hold reviewers accountable by scoring feedback quality.

**Portfolio review with rubrics in writing programs** — Students self-assess a draft against a rubric, exchange reviews with a peer, and submit a revision with a cover memo explaining changes — making the review–revision cycle visible for grading.
