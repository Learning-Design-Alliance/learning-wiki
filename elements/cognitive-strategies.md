# Cognitive Strategies

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Cognitive strategies are deliberate mental procedures learners apply to process information: **rehearsal** (repeating or copying), **elaboration** (connecting new material to prior knowledge), **organization** (imposing structure such as outlines or hierarchies), and **comprehension monitoring** (checking understanding while working). As an instructional element, cognitive strategy instruction means explicitly teaching these procedures — naming them, modeling them, and prompting their use — rather than assuming learners will discover them independently.

## Design Implications

Strategy instruction improves learning most when strategies are taught explicitly and applied to authentic content, not practiced in isolation [Strategy instruction improves academic performance across domains.](../claims/strategy-instruction-improves-academic-performance.md) [+S]. Elaborative and organizational strategies consistently outperform rehearsal strategies for complex material, because they build connections rather than merely maintaining information in working memory [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. Learners need guided practice and feedback on *how* they are processing, not just whether their answers are correct [Feedback most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit modeling of the strategy in use ([Think-Aloud](think-aloud.md) or worked demonstrations), so learners see when and why to deploy it
- Content worth processing — strategies applied to trivial material produce trivial gains
- Multiple practice opportunities across varied content so the strategy becomes flexible and self-initiated
- Prompts or cues that fade as learners internalize the strategy [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Rehearsal strategies (rereading, verbatim repetition) are among the least effective techniques for durable learning and retention [-S] — they create fluency illusions without building connections
- Strategy instruction benefits diminish — and can reverse — for learners who already use effective strategies spontaneously [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Strategies taught as rote procedures without conditional knowledge (when and why to use them) rarely transfer to new tasks [-M]
- Strategies impose processing costs; for novices, an elaborate strategy can overload working memory and reduce performance on the primary task [Example-problem sequences reduce cognitive load.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices and struggling learners who lack spontaneous strategy use — the largest gains come from teaching procedures experts use automatically [+S]
- Middle-grade through adult learners for metacognitive strategies; younger learners need simpler, more scaffolded versions
- Less beneficial for advanced learners who already deploy effective strategies [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension of complex text and expository material
- Durable retention and transfer of learned content
- Self-regulated learning: building learners' capacity to monitor and control their own processing

### Affordances
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — an organizational strategy that enacts [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) by grouping information into meaningful units that fit working memory limits
- [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — a combined rehearsal–organization strategy; externalizing selections and notes supports later retrieval and review
- [Analogies](analogies.md) — an elaboration strategy that connects unfamiliar material to well-structured prior knowledge, producing deeper encoding than repetition
- [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md) — an organizational scaffold presented before instruction that gives learners a structure into which new material can be integrated
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — cognitive strategies supply the "what to do" component; monitoring strategies supply the "when and whether it's working" component

## Examples

**[Reciprocal Teaching](reciprocal-teaching.md)** — Palincsar and Brown's procedure teaching four comprehension strategies (predicting, questioning, clarifying, summarizing) through modeled, then student-led, group practice. One of the best-replicated strategy instruction programs.

**[Learning Scientists posters](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/6/23-1)** — Classroom materials teaching six evidence-based strategies (spaced practice, retrieval practice, elaboration, interleaving, concrete examples, dual coding), grounded in [Dunlosky et al.'s (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) review.

**[SQ3R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQ3R)** — Robinson's survey-question-read-recite-review routine for study reading; an organizational and rehearsal composite taught as an explicit procedure.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Hints and worked examples model solution strategies step by step before independent practice, embedding strategy modeling in the practice loop.
