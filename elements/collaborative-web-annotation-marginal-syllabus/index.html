Collaborative web annotation (Marginal Syllabus): low-bandwidth asynchronous discussion with social justice intent¶

Element · All elements Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Collaborative annotation with the open-source Hypothes.is tool engages learners in co-construction and critique of knowledge. The article notes "the technology is open-source, free and low-bandwidth, and Marginal Syllabus organizers ensure that all articles included are available open access". Marginal Syllabus explicitly centers social justice focused texts, giving the practice a cultural social justice purpose.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Articles included must be available open access; annotation sessions were extended from hour-long to several days to open participation across time zones

Constraints¶

  • Negative if annotating canonical texts by privileged authors, if it opens room for abuse or harassment of authors, or for students from historically marginalized populations

Target Learners¶

  • educators and learners participating in open academic conversations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • deep critical reading and co-construction of knowledge
