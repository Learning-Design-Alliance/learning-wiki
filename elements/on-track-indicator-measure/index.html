The on-track indicator as a first-year high school achievement measure¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The on-track indicator is a binary first-year achievement measure used by the Consortium on Chicago School Research to judge whether ninth-graders are progressing toward graduation. The report defines it operationally: "In order for a student to be 'on track' to graduate, he or she must have no more than one 'F' in a core academic course and have enough credits to be promoted to the next grade." It is used here to track Vivaldi graduates' first-year achievement across destination schools, cohorts, and gender, and the report calls it strongly related to students' likelihood of graduating.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Transcript data on core-course grades (English, math, social science, science) and credits must be available for the receiving high school; students with missing data are generally given the benefit of the doubt and considered on-track

Constraints¶

  • Students enrolled in an Academic Preparatory Center are considered off track
  • On-track rates cannot be calculated for charter schools because transcript data are unavailable

Target Learners¶

  • Chicago Public Schools students in their first year after eighth grade, including those entering high schools or Academic Preparatory Centers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Staying on track to graduate: passing core academic courses and earning enough credits for on-time grade promotion

Claims¶

  • Systemwide CPS first-year on-track percentages ranged from 44.8 to 50.8 across the 1997–2001 cohorts [+W]
  • The on-track indicator is strongly related to students' likelihood of graduating [+W]
  • Vivaldi students' first-year on-track rates differed across destination high schools for the 1997 cohort [+W]
  • Vivaldi students' first-year on-track rates differed across destination high schools for the 2001 cohort [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track status predicts graduation more strongly than prior achievement, with a 55 percentage point gap after controls [+W]
  • Ninth-graders who are on-track are 3.5 times more likely to graduate than those who are off-track [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track ELL students in Chicago were up to 3.5 times more likely to graduate than off-track students [+W]
