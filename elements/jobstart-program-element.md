# JOBSTART: four-component alternative education and training program for disadvantaged high school dropouts

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
JOBSTART was a nonresidential alternative to Job Corps designed to improve the economic prospects of young, disadvantaged high school dropouts by increasing educational attainment and occupational skills. It had "four main components": basic academic skills instruction with GED preparation (at least 200 self-paced, individualized hours), occupational skills training (at least 500 classroom-based hours), support services such as transportation and childcare assistance, and job placement assistance. It served youth aged 17 to 21 reading below an 8th-grade level who were economically disadvantaged.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sites were required to offer all four components, though implementation varied across sites
- Participation was voluntary and targeted youth 17 to 21 who had dropped out and were economically disadvantaged
#### Constraints
- JOBSTART is no longer an active program and has no current available developer or contact information
- Sites differed in implementation; some offered components in-house while others linked participants with outside agencies

### Target Learners
- Young disadvantaged high school dropouts aged 17 to 21 reading below an 8th-grade level

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic academic skills and GED attainment
- Occupational skills for specific high-demand occupations
- Job placement
