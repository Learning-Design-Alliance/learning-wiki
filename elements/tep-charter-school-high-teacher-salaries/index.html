The Equity Project (TEP) Charter School teacher-compensation model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Equity Project Charter School is a New York City charter school whose distinguishing feature, as named in the brief's title, is "High Salaries for Teachers". Mathematica evaluated its impacts on student achievement over a 2009-2014 time frame, prepared for the Gates Foundation, comparing TEP students with similar students in comparable New York City public schools. The brief reports positive achievement impacts across subjects and cohorts.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Operates as a New York City charter school serving students comparable to those in other city public schools, per the evaluation's comparison framing

  • The brief reports impacts through the 2012-2013 school year within a 2009-2014 evaluation; it does not print the salary mechanism details or statistical tests

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in New York City public charter schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student achievement in math, English language arts, and science

Claims¶

  • TEP's cumulative four-year effect equals approximately 78% of the Hispanic-white achievement gap in math, 17% in English language arts, and 25% in science [+M]
  • Four years of TEP attendance produced English language arts gains equal to an additional 0.4 years of school relative to comparable NYC public school students [+M]
  • Students who attended TEP for four years had math test score gains equal to an additional 1.6 years of school compared to similar students in comparable New York City public schools [+M]
  • Four years of TEP attendance produced science gains equal to an additional 0.6 years of school relative to comparable NYC public school students [+M]
  • TEP's achievement impacts were consistently positive across subjects and cohorts through 2012-2013, with especially large effects in math [+M]
  • Students who attended TEP for four years had math test score gains equal to an additional 1.6 years of school compared to similar students in comparable New York City public schools [+M]
  • TEP's student population was similar to other neighborhood schools: high poverty, mostly Hispanic, with similar rates of special education services [+M]
  • TEP's achievement impacts were consistently positive across subjects and cohorts, with especially large effects in math, by the end of the 2012-2013 school year [+M]
