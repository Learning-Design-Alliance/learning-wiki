# Good Behavior Game classroom management strategy

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Good Behavior Game is "a specific classroom management strategy that aims to improve social skills, minimize disruptive behaviors, and create a positive learning environment." Teachers place students into teams and reward them for following classroom rules. It can be implemented as the PAX Good Behavior Game, the AIR version, or a local adaptation, and is used from prekindergarten through grade 12, often in classrooms showing high levels of disruptive behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training from a developer or researcher before leading the game, with ongoing coaching support commonly provided
- A timer and a whiteboard or poster to record and display game rules and team scores, plus teacher-chosen rewards
#### Constraints
- PAX and AIR trainings require purchase of teacher training kits, ranging from $325 to $580 per teacher; kits do not include student rewards

### Target Learners
- Students in prekindergarten through grade 12, including classrooms demonstrating high levels of disruptive behaviors

### Target Learning Goals
- Decreasing disruptive behavior
- Improving academic engagement and prosocial behaviors
- Creating a positive learning environment
