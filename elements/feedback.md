# Feedback

## Description
Feedback is information provided to learners about their performance or understanding, intended to close the gap between current and desired performance. Effective feedback answers three questions: Where am I going? How am I doing? Where to next? (Hattie & Timperley, 2007). It functions as the corrective mechanism in any instructional cycle that includes [Practice](practice.md) or [Assessment](assessment.md).

## Design Implications

Feedback is among the most powerful influences on learning, but its effects are highly variable — it can improve or degrade performance depending on design [Feedback effects on performance are highly variable and can be negative.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.120.2.254) [~S]. Feedback should focus on the task and the process, not the person; praise directed at the self carries little informational value and can undermine motivation. Feedback is most effective when learners have an opportunity to act on it — feedback delivered after grading, or without a revision opportunity, is largely wasted [Formative feedback with opportunities to act improves achievement.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308317959) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear success criteria so learners can interpret the feedback against a target
- Timing that lets learners apply the information while the task is still active
- Specific, actionable comments tied to the task or strategy, not global judgments
- A revision or retry cycle — feedback without a subsequent action is inert ([Formative Assessment](formative-assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Feedback on performance level alone (grades, scores) without process information adds little and can reduce performance for struggling learners [Feedback effects on performance are highly variable and can be negative.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.120.2.254) [-S]
- Overly detailed feedback can overwhelm novices; too much guidance at once increases extraneous load [~M]
- Feedback that threatens the ego (public correction, comparative ranking) triggers disengagement rather than improvement [-M]
- Immediate feedback is not always superior — delayed feedback can benefit retention for some tasks [~W]

### Target Learners
- All learners, but the *kind* of feedback should shift with expertise: novices benefit from task-level corrective feedback, while advanced learners benefit more from process-level prompts that trigger [Self-Explanation](self-explanation.md) [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- Struggling learners need feedback framed around process goals rather than outcome goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Learners monitoring their own progress benefit when feedback builds self-assessment capacity [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge acquisition: correcting misconceptions and errors close in time to performance
- Skill refinement: iterative improvement of procedural and compositional work
- Self-regulation: developing learners' capacity to evaluate and adjust their own work

### Affordances
- [Feedback Loops](../principles/feedback-loops.md) — feedback is the mechanism that closes the loop between performance and adjustment; the design question is loop latency (how quickly learners learn whether their attempt worked)
- [Mastery Learning](../principles/mastery-learning.md) — mastery models are unworkable without feedback; learners must know what remains unmastered and receive targeted input before re-attempting
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — feedback converts assessment from measurement into instruction; the assessment itself becomes the feedback event
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — feedback is a responsive scaffold that fades as learners internalize evaluative criteria [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

## Examples

**[Action-Oriented Feedback](../strategies/action-oriented_feedback.md)** — Comments phrased as a next action ("add a counterexample here") rather than a judgment ("weak argument"), making the revision path explicit.

**[5-Minute Writing Conferences](../strategies/5-minute_writing_conferences.md)** — Brief one-on-one conferences delivering targeted feedback on a single writing focus, keeping the loop short and actionable.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Immediate item-level feedback on practice exercises with hints and linked explanations, allowing retry until mastery.

**[Draftback / Google Docs commenting](https://www.google.com/docs/about)** — Inline, asynchronous teacher comments on drafts before final submission, preserving the revision cycle.
