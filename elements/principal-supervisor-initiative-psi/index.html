Principal Supervisor Initiative (PSI)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Principal Supervisor Initiative is a district-level reform program launched by The Wallace Foundation in 2014. It is described as "a four-year, $24 million effort to redefine principal supervision in six urban school districts." The initiative aimed to shift the supervisor role from administrative compliance work toward developing principals as instructional leaders, and this report evaluates how the role differed in PSI districts compared with other large urban districts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • District participation in a multi-year initiative supported by The Wallace Foundation to redefine the principal supervisor role
  • District participation in redefining the principal supervisor role over the four-year initiative period

Constraints¶

  • Implemented in six urban school districts over four years
  • Funded as a four-year, $24 million effort by The Wallace Foundation

Target Learners¶

  • Principal supervisors in urban school districts
  • Principals in urban school districts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Developing and supporting principals to be effective instructional leaders
  • Developing principals' skills as instructional leaders who foster high quality instruction and learning

Claims¶

  • PSI districts were more likely than other urban districts to offer programs for new and aspiring supervisors [+W]
  • PSI district principal supervisors were more likely to receive role-specific training and rated it more highly than supervisors in other urban districts [+W]
  • Principal supervisors in PSI districts supervised fewer principals than those in other urban districts [+W]
  • Principal supervisors in PSI and other urban districts reported similar perceptions of central office support and structures [~W]
  • Districts and principal supervisors made substantial changes to principal supervision as a result of the PSI [+W]
  • Principals' perceptions of their work with their supervisor and of the supervisor's effectiveness improved under the PSI [+W]
  • The PSI did not improve teachers' perceptions of principals' performance [-W]
  • Principal supervisor roles in PSI districts differed from other urban districts in key ways while supervisors' work with principals showed important similarities [+W]
