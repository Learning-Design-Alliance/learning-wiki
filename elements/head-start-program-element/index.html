Head Start program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Head Start is a national, federally funded program providing services to promote school readiness for children from birth to age 5 from predominantly low-income families. Services cover education, health and nutrition, family engagement, and other social services, and are delivered through center-based or family child care, home visits, or combinations operating full or half days for 8-12 months per year. The report states the program "seeks to promote school readiness by bolstering a child’s development and learning," with services responsive to each child and family's ethnic, cultural, and linguistic heritage.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Local grantees must provide a 20% cash or in-kind match to federal funds, and no more than 15% of total program costs may be used for program administration.

Constraints¶

  • Program administrators have flexibility in service delivery, leading to considerable variability in the services offered across communities.

Target Learners¶

  • children from birth to age 5 from predominantly low-income families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • school readiness
  • language and literacy skills
  • cognition and general knowledge
  • physical development and health
  • social and emotional development
  • approaches to learning

Claims¶

  • The evidence base for Head Start's school-readiness effects is small: only one of 40 eligible studies met WWC group design standards [~W]
  • Substantial crossover occurred in the Head Start impact study comparison group, with 17.3% of the 3-year-old and 13.9% of the 4-year-old comparison groups enrolling in non-study Head Start programs [~M]
  • Head Start shows no discernible effects on mathematics achievement for 4-year-old children [-M]
  • Head Start shows no discernible effects on social-emotional development for 3- and 4-year-old children [-M]
  • Head Start has potentially positive effects on general reading achievement for 3- and 4-year-old children, based on parent-rated emergent literacy [+M]
  • Supplemental analyses show statistically significant positive Head Start effects on one social-emotional subscale and two mothers' race/ethnicity subgroup comparisons for the 3-year-old cohort [+M]
