# Attention

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Attention is the gateway to learning: information that is not attended to is not encoded, regardless of its quality or presentation. As an instructional element, "attention" refers to the deliberate design moves — signaling, salience, segmentation, and distraction management — that direct learners' limited attentional resources toward the content and interactions that matter.

## Design Implications

Because working memory is severely limited and attention is selective, instruction that fails to manage where learners look and think wastes capacity or actively harms learning [When instruction overloads working memory, learning degrades.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]. Effective design makes the critical features of content perceptually and semantically salient rather than hoping learners find them. Attention management is not a motivational nicety; it is a precondition for every downstream process — encoding, integration, retrieval practice.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear analysis of *what* learners must attend to (the critical features, steps, or relationships in the content)
- Signaling or cueing (highlighting, arrows, emphasis, vocal stress) that marks essential information [Signaling essential information improves learning outcomes.](../claims/signaling-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Segmentation of complex material into learner-paced units so attention can be refreshed between segments
- An environment with manageable distraction — seductive but irrelevant details compete for the same resources [Seductive details interfere with learning.](../claims/seductive-details-harm-learning.md) [-S]

#### Constraints
- Novelty and salience can backfire: emotionally interesting but irrelevant material draws attention away from the learning goal [Seductive details interfere with learning.](../claims/seductive-details-harm-learning.md) [-S]
- Sustained attention degrades over time; long unbroken expositions lose learners regardless of quality, so attention must be re-engaged periodically rather than assumed
- Learners cannot attend to two demanding streams simultaneously — redundant on-screen text read while listening to narration splits attention [Redundant on-screen text with narration harms learning.](../claims/redundancy-harms-learning.md) [-S]
- Attention captured by extrinsic rewards or gamified cues can displace attention from the content itself when the reward becomes the focus [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack the knowledge to distinguish essential from incidental features and therefore benefit most from explicit cueing [Signaling essential information improves learning outcomes.](../claims/signaling-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Young learners and learners with attention difficulties, who need environmental structure and shorter segments; physical activity breaks can improve subsequent attention in children [Classroom physical activity improves children's attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]
- Experts need less external cueing and may find heavy signaling redundant or intrusive [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Any goal requiring encoding of new information — attention is upstream of all of them
- Discrimination learning: directing attention to the features that distinguish cases
- Procedural learning: keeping attention on the current step rather than the whole overwhelming task

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — attention management is the attentional half of load management: signaling and segmentation free working-memory capacity for schema building rather than search
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — chunking works by giving attention a unit-sized target; each chunk is one thing to attend to at a time [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- [Clear Structure & Presentation](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md) — predictable structure tells learners where to allocate attention next, reducing effort spent on orientation
- [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md) — organizers pre-direct attention to the relationships the upcoming material will present [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]

- [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) — scripted, segmented presentation keeps attention on one clear point at a time
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — modeling makes expert attentional moves explicit so novices attend to the right features
- [Gagné's 9 Events](../patterns/gagnes-9-events-of-instruction.md) — "gain attention" is the first event, on the recognition that nothing downstream works without it

## Examples

**[Signaling and highlighting in multimedia lessons](../strategies/signaling.md)** — Arrows, color, and bolding that mark the relevant part of a diagram or equation as it is explained, rather than leaving learners to scan.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Short, segmented videos (typically under 10 minutes) with on-screen writing that appears in sync with narration, keeping visual attention aligned with the explanation.

**[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.org)** — Brief lessons with immediate visual and auditory feedback on the exact item attended to; session length is calibrated to sustain attention.

**[Text-marking and annotation routines](../strategies/annotation.md)** — Requiring learners to mark key claims and evidence converts passive reading into attention-directing activity; distinctive marking exploits salience effects (see the [Von Restorff effect in text marking](../theories/von-restorff-effect-text-marking.md)).
