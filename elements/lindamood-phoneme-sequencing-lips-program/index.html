Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing® (LiPS®) program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 12 claims (5 for, 4 mixed, 3 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

Description¶

LiPS (formerly Auditory Discrimination in Depth) is a program "designed to teach students the skills they need to decode words and to identify individual sounds and blends in words". Initial activities engage students in discovering the lip, tongue, and mouth actions needed to produce specific sounds; subsequent activities in sequencing, reading, and spelling use the oral aspects of sounds to identify and order them within words. The program also offers direct instruction in letter patterns, sight words, and context clues, and is designed for emergent readers in kindergarten through grade 3 or for struggling, dyslexic readers. The version tested involved computer-supported activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The developer recommends instruction last four to six months for one hour a day, or four to six weeks for four hours a day
  • Teachers can learn to administer the program from the Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing® Teacher's Manual or via LiPS® workshops

Constraints¶

  • The WWC considers the extent of evidence for LiPS® on students with learning disabilities to be small in all five domains examined

Target Learners¶

  • emergent readers in kindergarten through grade 3
  • struggling or dyslexic readers
  • students with learning disabilities or difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • decoding words
  • identifying individual sounds and blends in words
  • phonemic awareness and phonemic decoding
  • spelling and self-correction in reading and speech

Claims¶

  • The evidence base for LiPS with students with learning disabilities is small: one RCT met WWC standards out of 31 reviewed studies [~W]
  • LiPS has no discernible effects on reading comprehension for students with learning disabilities [-W]
  • LiPS has potentially negative effects on writing: comparison students significantly outperformed LiPS students on the KTEA Spelling subtest [-W]
  • LiPS has potentially positive effects on alphabetics for students with learning disabilities, with statistically significant advantages on two of eight measures [+W]
  • LiPS shows potentially positive effects on math: no statistically significant effect on the WJ–R Calculation subtest, but a positive effect size of 0.30 judged substantively important [+W]
  • LiPS shows potentially positive effects on reading fluency: authors found significant effects on GORT–III Reading Accuracy and Reading Rate, but WWC analyses did not confirm significance [+W]
  • One-year follow-up and LiPS-vs-RWT supplemental comparisons showed mostly non-significant differences, with some follow-up alphabetics effects remaining significant [~W]
  • In Torgesen et al. (2010), LiPS produced statistically significant positive effects on word accuracy/fluency, phonemic decoding, phonological awareness, and spelling versus a basal reading program [+W]
  • In Gunn (1996), LiPS effects on word accuracy/fluency and phonemic decoding were negative and substantively important but not statistically significant [-W]
  • No individual alphabetics outcome reached statistical significance in the WWC analysis [~W]
  • LiPS has mixed effects on alphabetics for beginning readers: positive and statistically significant in one trial, negative and substantively important (but not significant) in another [~W]
  • LiPS has potentially positive effects on comprehension for beginning readers, based on one randomized trial showing a statistically significant positive effect on passage comprehension [+W]
