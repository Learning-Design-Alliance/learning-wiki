# Submission

## Description
Submission is the act of learners delivering completed or in-progress work for evaluation, feedback, or peer review. It functions as a structured checkpoint that makes learning visible to instructors and to learners themselves, converting private effort into an assessable artifact.

## Design Implications

Submission creates the accountability structure that makes practice productive: work that must be completed and shown is worked on more deliberately than work that is optional [~M]. Its learning value depends less on the act of handing work in than on what follows — timely, specific feedback and opportunities to act on it. Frequent low-stakes submissions outperform infrequent high-stakes ones, because regular checkpoints support self-monitoring and error correction before misconceptions consolidate [Self-monitoring improves self-regulated learning outcomes.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear criteria or rubrics so learners know what "good" looks like before submitting ([Assessment](assessment.md) aligned to goals)
- A feedback loop: submission must be followed by [Feedback](feedback.md) the learner can act on, ideally with a revision opportunity
- Deadlines and cadence that match the pacing of the learning tasks — frequent enough to catch problems, spaced enough to allow substantive work
- Low stakes early, higher stakes later, so early submissions invite risk-taking rather than defensive minimalism

#### Constraints
- Submission without timely feedback becomes a bureaucratic ritual; work handed in and returned weeks later produces little learning [-M]
- High-stakes single submissions promote procrastination and surface compliance rather than mastery; learners optimize for the grade, not the learning [~M]
- Overly frequent submissions can crowd out time for deep work and increase anxiety, particularly for novice learners still building confidence [~W]
- In large-enrollment settings, feedback capacity often cannot keep pace with submission volume, degrading the entire cycle

### Target Learners
- Learners in formal education, project-based courses, and corporate training where artifacts of competence are required
- Learners building self-regulation, for whom external deadlines scaffold planning and monitoring [Self-monitoring improves self-regulated learning outcomes.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Learners whose motivation benefits from visible progress and recognition of their work [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Competency demonstration: producing evidence that a skill or standard has been met
- Self-regulation: deadline-driven planning, monitoring, and revision cycles
- Quality improvement: iterative refinement of work through feedback and resubmission

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — submission is the mechanism by which assessment becomes formative: the artifact gives both teacher and learner something concrete to evaluate against criteria, closing the gap between current and target performance
- [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) — submissions serve as the evidence base for competency decisions; learners submit when ready rather than on a fixed schedule, and resubmit until the standard is met
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — a submission requirement converts passive consumption into production; learners must generate an artifact, not just attend or read
- [Mastery Learning](../principles/mastery-learning.md) — paired with resubmission policies, submission enables the mastery cycle of attempt, feedback, correction, and re-attempt until proficiency is demonstrated

## Examples

**[GitHub](https://github.com)** — Pull requests are submissions in a professional context: work is submitted for review, receives line-level feedback, and is revised before merging. The model has been widely adopted in computing education.

**[Canvas / Moodle assignment workflows](https://www.instructure.com/canvas)** — LMS submission points with rubrics, due dates, and resubmission settings operationalize the submit–feedback–revise cycle at scale.

**[Writing process portfolios](https://www.nwp.org)** — National Writing Project approaches use staged submissions (draft, peer review, revision, final) so that submission is a checkpoint in a process rather than a terminal event.
