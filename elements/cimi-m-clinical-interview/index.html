Clinical Interview for Moral Injury—Military Version (CIMI-M)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The CIMI-M is a structured, descriptive, non-diagnostic clinical interview for mapping veterans' exposure to potentially morally injurious experiences and the impact of those experiences across six moral injury domains, supporting case conceptualization and treatment planning. "The final version used for the study included 26 questions and 36 potential follow-up questions, grouped under the abovementioned headings, as well as an introduction and instructions." Its final structure comprises an introductory clinician section, a text read aloud to the veteran, the interview itself organized into context, PMIE(s), domains of moral injury, and desired change/readiness for change, plus a case conceptualization template. It was designed to be readily trainable and to complement existing self-report scales.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clinicians receive the interview with its instructions manual and an individual briefing on administration, as done in the study
  • Approximately 90 minutes should be scheduled for administration

Constraints¶

  • It offers no cut-off point or severity score and cannot be used to diagnose moral injury or track change
  • Context-related questions about deployments and military service would require modification for civilians or other professions

Target Learners¶

  • military veterans in tertiary psychotrauma care
  • clinicians assessing moral injury

Target Learning Goals¶

  • descriptive assessment of PMIE exposure and moral injury domains
  • case conceptualization and treatment planning for moral injury

Affordances¶

  • Six Domains Moral Injury Framework

Claims¶

  • An exploratory configural caseness rule formalizes when a veteran warrants treatment for moral injury [+W]
  • Veterans and clinicians rate the CIMI-M as feasible, acceptable, and clinically useful in a preliminary evaluation [+W]
  • Qualitative analysis finds the CIMI-M helped veterans share experiences and helped clinicians build trust and plan treatment [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Provide brief interviewer training on the moral injury concept and interview structure
  • Screen with the MIOS before administering the CIMI-M and deploy it at intake or before trauma treatment
