Self Efficacy¶

Element · All elements
Evidence · 103 claims (59 for, 38 mixed, 6 against) · 89 studies (37 associational, 23 causal, 16 review, 8 quant-synthesis, 4 design, 1 qualitative), q1–q4 · 28 of 89 report an effect size · 98 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Self efficacy is a learner's belief in their capability to succeed at a specific task or domain, not a general sense of self-worth. It functions as a proximal driver of learning: learners with higher efficacy set more ambitious goals, persist longer through difficulty, and recover faster from failure [Bandura, 1997]. Because it is domain-specific and malleable, it is a design target rather than a fixed learner trait.

Design Implications¶

Instructional design shapes self efficacy primarily through the four sources Bandura identified: mastery experiences, vicarious experience, verbal persuasion, and physiological/affective states. Mastery experiences — genuine success on challenging tasks — are the strongest source [+S], so sequencing tasks so learners experience early, authentic success is the highest-leverage design move. Vicarious experience works through Demonstration by peer or near-peer models [+M]; seeing a similar other succeed raises efficacy more than watching an expert. Feedback design matters: feedback that attributes success to effort and strategy rather than fixed ability builds efficacy, while ability-focused praise or harsh evaluative feedback undermines it Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tasks calibrated slightly above current ability so success is achievable with effort (Scaffolding)
  • Visible progress indicators and mastery criteria so learners can attribute improvement to their own actions
  • Feedback that is specific, process-oriented, and delivered in time to be used Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S]
  • Models of similar others succeeding, not only expert performance (Demonstration)

Constraints¶

  • Efficacy built on easy success collapses at the first real difficulty; success must be earned on challenging tasks to transfer [-S]
  • High self efficacy without commensurate skill produces overconfidence, reduced monitoring, and premature disengagement from help [~M]
  • Unfavourable social comparison (public leaderboards, norm-referenced grading) lowers efficacy for struggling learners even when their absolute progress is good [-M]
  • Verbal encouragement alone ("you can do it!") without mastery evidence has weak, short-lived effects [+W]

Target Learners¶

  • Novices and low-achieving learners, who benefit most from early mastery experiences and near-peer models [+S]
  • Learners recovering from repeated failure, who need re-attribution of failure to strategy and effort rather than ability [+M]
  • Less critical for already-confident experts, for whom additional efficacy boosting adds little [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Persistence and engagement on challenging, multi-step tasks
  • Self-regulated learning: goal setting, monitoring, and strategic revision Self-regulated learning
  • Affective goals: reducing anxiety and avoidance in high-stakes domains (math, writing, public speaking)

Affordances¶

  • Scaffolding — graduated support guarantees early success experiences, the strongest source of efficacy; fading [+W] then lets learners attribute the success to themselves rather than the support
  • Assessment for Learning — low-stakes formative assessment with actionable feedback converts assessment from an efficacy threat into an efficacy builder
  • Self-Determination Theory — competence is one of three basic needs; efficacy-building task design simultaneously supports autonomy and relatedness
  • Social Learning Theory — vicarious experience through peer modeling is a core efficacy source and the theoretical bridge between observation and belief change

The evidence by question¶

  • Which instruction raises learners' self-efficacy: close goals, feedback on progress, guided practice? Proximal goals and ability attributional feedback for early learning raise self-efficacy more than distant goals and effort feedback, by a review's report of earlier studies [+M]
  • Does a gain in reported confidence mean a gain in skill? Self-efficacy judgments correspond less well to behavior in learning settings than in performance settings [~M]
  • Does a learner's belief that they can succeed at a task predict whether they start and persist, and which goals and feedback raise it? Learners' task-specific self-efficacy beliefs predict whether they initiate, persist through, and recover from difficulty on academic tasks. [+M]

Related Elements¶

  • Demonstration — provides vicarious mastery experiences; near-peer models are more efficacy-building than expert models
  • Coaching — delivers the process-level feedback and attributional reframing that sustain efficacy through difficulty
  • Check-In — surfaces affective states (anxiety, discouragement) that are themselves a source of efficacy information
  • Fading — timing of support removal determines whether learners credit themselves or the scaffold

Examples¶

  • Achievable Micro-Goals — breaking large tasks into short, winnable steps creates a rapid sequence of mastery experiences, the strongest efficacy source.
  • Mastery-based platforms (e.g., Khan Academy) — learners advance only after demonstrating proficiency, so visible progress and success rate are structurally protected; hints and worked examples keep early tasks winnable.
  • Writing conferences — brief one-to-one feedback naming a specific strategy the writer used successfully, building efficacy through verbal persuasion anchored in real evidence.
  • Reciprocal teaching — students take turns leading comprehension strategies, giving each learner a public mastery experience and modeling for peers.
  • Build relational school resources (teacher–student relationships, peer support, encouragement, extracurriculars) to scaffold self-regulation for low-efficacy marginalized students
  • Balance AI integration in SRL so technological advantages are harnessed without undermining student self-efficacy
