# Criteria Development

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Criteria development asks learners to construct the rubrics, checklists, or evaluation frameworks against which work will be judged, rather than receiving criteria pre-defined by the instructor. By articulating what quality looks like and why, learners internalize the standards of the discipline and can later apply them to monitor and improve their own work.

## Design Implications

Involving learners in defining quality criteria converts assessment from an external judgment into an analytical exercise, strengthening their understanding of both the criteria and the work being evaluated [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. The activity works best when learners draft criteria from authentic examples, negotiate and justify them, and then apply them to real work — including their own — so the framework is exercised, not just written [Rubrics and criteria support self-regulated learning when learners use them to judge work, not merely receive them.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to exemplars (strong and weak) from which criteria can be induced
- Instructor guidance on the domain's quality dimensions, so learner-generated criteria are not superficial
- Opportunities to apply the criteria to actual work ([Peer Assessment](peer-assessment.md), [Self-Assessment](self-assessment.md)) and revise them based on mismatches
- Time for discussion and negotiation of criteria, not one-off rubric writing

#### Constraints
- Novices often generate surface-level criteria (length, neatness, effort) rather than discipline-relevant ones without instructor scaffolding [-M]
- Co-constructed criteria lose value if the final assessment actually uses different, hidden criteria — learners perceive the exercise as inauthentic [-M]
- Time-intensive; low payoff when the task is one-shot and criteria will not be reused
- Can produce criteria so generic they transfer to nothing ("clear," "well-organized") unless anchored in contrasting examples

### Target Learners
- Learners in project-based or writing-intensive settings where work is revised over multiple drafts
- Students developing evaluative judgment, e.g., in assessment literacy or teacher-training programs
- Less suitable for complete novices, who lack the exemplar knowledge to induce meaningful criteria; they benefit more from [Rubric Design](rubric-design.md) done for them first

### Target Learning Goals
- Evaluative judgment: understanding what distinguishes strong from weak work
- Analytical reasoning: decomposing a complex product into assessable dimensions
- Self-regulation: internalizing standards for monitoring and revising one's own work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Metacognition](../principles/metacognition.md) — drafting criteria forces learners to make their implicit notions of quality explicit, then test and revise them against real work
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — owning the criteria gives learners the standards they need for self-monitoring and self-directed revision cycles
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — criteria development makes learners participants in the assessment process rather than its objects, aligning with formative use of evaluation
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — learners construct the evaluative framework rather than receiving it, producing deeper processing of quality dimensions

## Related Elements

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Inquiry-Based Learning](../patterns/inquiry-based-learning.md) — learners set the standards by which their investigations will be judged
- [Self-Directed Learning](../patterns/self-directed-learning.md) — criteria development supplies the evaluative framework self-directed learners need to monitor progress

## Examples

**[Writing rubric co-construction](../strategies/co-constructing_rubrics_with_students.md)** — Students analyze annotated sample essays, name the qualities that distinguish strong from weak writing, and draft rubric language the class refines before drafting begins.

**[Project-based learning contracts](../strategies/learning_contracts.md)** — Learners propose success criteria for their project deliverables, negotiate them with the instructor, and use them for checkpoint self-evaluation.

**[Examination board peer review in medical education](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3850873/)** — Trainees develop rating criteria for clinical presentations before rating peers, sharpening discrimination between performance levels.
