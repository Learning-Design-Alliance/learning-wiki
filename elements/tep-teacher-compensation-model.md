# The Equity Project (TEP) charter school teacher compensation and responsibility model

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
TEP is a New York City charter school with what the report calls "a unique approach to rewarding and developing high quality teachers—including salaries of $125,000 and substantial professional responsibility." The model operates "while receiving only the standard public funds available to any New York City charter school," making the teacher-pay strategy the school's distinctive design feature. The evaluation links this personnel model to the school's measured achievement impacts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Operates within the standard public funding available to any New York City charter school
- Operates as a New York City charter school serving students comparable to those in other city public schools, per the evaluation's comparison framing
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a single charter school serving a high-poverty, mostly Hispanic neighborhood population
- The brief reports impacts through the 2012-2013 school year within a 2009-2014 evaluation; it does not print the salary mechanism details or statistical tests

### Target Learners
- high-poverty, mostly Hispanic K-12 students in New York City charter schools
- K-12 students in New York City public charter schools

### Target Learning Goals
- improving student achievement in math, English language arts, and science through teacher quality and retention
- Student achievement in math, English language arts, and science

## Description
The Equity Project Charter School is a New York City charter school whose distinguishing feature, as named in the brief's title, is "High Salaries for Teachers". Mathematica evaluated its impacts on student achievement over a 2009-2014 time frame, prepared for the Gates Foundation, comparing TEP students with similar students in comparable New York City public schools. The brief reports positive achievement impacts across subjects and cohorts.
