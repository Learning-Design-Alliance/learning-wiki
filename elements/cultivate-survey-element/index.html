Cultivate Survey: anonymous twice-yearly student survey of classroom learning conditions in grades 5-12¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Cultivate Survey is a research-based student survey measuring nine classroom learning conditions through example items such as "This teacher gives us meaningful work to do, not busy work" and "In this class, my ideas are taken seriously." Students take it anonymously in grades 5-12, for about 15-20 minutes, twice per year (fall and spring), responding about one randomly assigned core class (math, social studies, English language arts, or science), the same class in fall and spring. It includes an impact question on whether students believe responses will be used to improve the school.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools receive reports if at least 50% of students complete the survey

Constraints¶

  • Data are school-specific and not intended for comparing scores across schools; results are not public and not used for evaluation or accountability

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 5-12 in core academic classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding and improving students' experiences of classroom instruction and learning environments

Affordances¶

  • Cultivate Framework Conditions Beliefs Wellbeing

Claims¶

  • The Cultivate Survey's impact question indicates whether students experience authentic voice and influence in school improvement [+W]
  • Schurz High School's Cultivate-driven feedback-for-growth improvement work coincided with improved student experiences and out-of-school suspensions dropping to one-half of the prior year's number [+W]
  • Students' survey reports of their school experiences predict later academic and well-being outcomes including attendance, grades, test scores, graduation, college enrollment, and well-being [+M]
  • All nine Cultivate learning condition measures show strong Rasch person reliability (0.82-0.87) in CPS administrations [+M]
  • Each individual learning condition predicts I am Valued gains, with Classroom Community the largest (15.0 middle grades, 16.9 high school percentile points) [+M]
