Five-year longitudinal cohort dataset of 4,228 students assessed up to three times per year comparing ever-SPED and never-SPED growth¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's evidence base is a longitudinal dataset following "one cohort of 4,228 students for five years" with assessments "up to three times per year," enabling separation of within-year (school-year) growth from across-year (summer) growth in math and reading from kindergarten to 4th grade. Students are classified as ever-SPED or never-SPED. The design supports seasonal decomposition of growth disparities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Repeated within-year and fall assessments to separate school-year growth from summer loss
  • SPED participation records to classify students as ever-SPED or never-SPED

Constraints¶

  • Findings describe one cohort followed from kindergarten to 4th grade

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten through 4th grade students, including students with disabilities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring math and reading growth across school years and summers

Claims¶

  • Ever-SPED students grew more than never-SPED students during the 1st and 2nd grade school years in math and the 1st, 3rd, and 4th grade school years in reading [+W]
  • Ever-SPED students lost more learning than never-SPED students during every summer from kindergarten through 4th grade [+W]
  • Ever-SPED students started kindergarten with lower math and reading scores and grew less than never-SPED students during the kindergarten school year [+W]
  • Test score disparities between ever-SPED and never-SPED students grew from under 0.4 standard deviations in kindergarten to 0.9 standard deviations in 4th grade [+W]
