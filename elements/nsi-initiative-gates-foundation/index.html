Networks for School Improvement (NSI) initiative¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 13 claims (7 for, 2 mixed, 4 against) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 13 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Networks for School Improvement (NSI) initiative is a Gates Foundation program that "support[s] networks of schools in using continuous improvement (CI) to improve outcomes for students who are Black, Latino, or experiencing poverty." The foundation sponsored an evaluation covering network formation, schools' use of CI, and impacts on student outcomes, comparing NSI schools to similar non-participating schools. This report covers impacts after schools' second year of participation, for 8th-grade on-track, 9th-grade on-track, and well-matched postsecondary enrollment NSI models.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools participate in networks that use continuous improvement (CI) to improve targeted student outcomes, sponsored by the foundation.
  • intermediary organizations to lead each network and coordinate school participation

Constraints¶

  • The foundation expected the NSI to have their full impact after three years; this report presents impacts after schools' second year, which was heavily affected by COVID.
  • impacts varied by network type, with college-ready on-track NSI showing no impact on any prioritized outcome

Target Learners¶

  • Students who are Black, Latino, or experiencing poverty in NSI secondary schools
  • middle school students
  • high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved student outcomes including GPA, core course pass rates, credit completion, FAFSA completion, and college enrollment
  • course pass rates
  • grade point average
  • math achievement
  • FAFSA completion
  • college enrollment

Claims¶

  • After schools' second year of participation, the 8th-grade on-track NSI did not impact the targeted student outcomes [-M]
  • The impact of the 9th-grade on-track NSI appeared to increase over time [+M]
  • After schools' second year, the 9th-grade on-track NSI positively impacted GPA, core course pass rate, and credit completion [+M]
  • After schools' second year, the well-matched postsecondary enrollment NSI positively impacted FAFSA completion but not college enrollment [+M]
  • The postsecondary enrollment NSI's first-year impact on college enrollment did not persist into schools' second year [-M]
  • Most schools engaged in inquiry cycles, but teams completed only about half of the cycles they initiated, averaging 3.2 cycles per year [~W]
  • Most schools laid CI groundwork (72 percent root cause analysis), but fewer than two-thirds tied change ideas to their theory of practice improvement, and equity attention centered on achievement rather than access, identity, or agency [~W]
  • 8th-grade on-track NSI positively impacted math test scores, moving students from the 37th to the 40th percentile [+W]
  • 8th-grade on-track NSI did not improve ELA test scores, attendance, or suspension rates [-W]
  • 9th-grade on-track NSI improved GPA by about 0.11 points on a 4-point scale [+W]
  • 9th-grade on-track NSI improved course pass rates by 3 to 4 percentage points but did not reduce suspension rates [+W]
  • College-ready on-track NSI did not impact GPA, advanced course taking, math and ELA test scores, graduation, or college enrollment [-W]
  • Well-matched postsecondary enrollment NSI increased FAFSA completion by 3 to 5 percentage points but did not increase college enrollment rates [+W]
