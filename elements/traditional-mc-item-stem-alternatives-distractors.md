# Traditional multiple-choice test item structure: stem, alternatives, and plausible distractors

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
The article defines the traditional multiple-choice test item as consisting 'of two basic parts: (1) a problem (stem ), and (2) a list of suggested solutions (alternatives/options )', with one correct or best alternative and a number of incorrect alternatives (distractors). Distractors should appear plausible to students who have not achieved the objective and implausible to those who have. The article cites the prescription that distractors are best constructed when comparable in length, complexity, and grammatical form to the answer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Distractors constructed to be plausible distractors for students who have not achieved the objective being measured
#### Constraints
- The article notes MC testing has increased difficulty in probing a student's ability to formulate opinions and organize critical arguments, skills better assessed by essays, portfolios, non-algorithmic problem tests and project performance

### Target Learners
- teachers constructing multiple-choice tests with little background knowledge of traditional procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing students' learning efficiently over large numbers of students and many disciplines
