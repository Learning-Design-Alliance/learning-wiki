Combined tSSNA and ONA method for analyzing collaborative knowledge creation processes¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This analytic method combines temporal socio-semantic network analysis (tSSNA) and ordered network analysis (ONA) to analyze collaborative knowledge creation. tSSNA tracks idea improvement via total degree centrality in moving windows over discourse, segmenting sessions into phases. ONA models ordered relationships between epistemic codes, capturing which actions respond to which. The article states this work "demonstrates the potential of combining tSSNA and ONA for analyzing collaborative KC processes."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Transcribed discourse segmented by turns of talk
  • A coding framework (here, shared epistemic agency with seven categories)
  • Bilingual analysts when data spans languages

Constraints¶

  • Applied to three teams in 30-minute sessions; tSSNA used moving window of four turns, network lifetime of two windows, 287 unique key terms

Target Learners¶

  • learning analytics researchers
  • CSCL researchers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • analyzing collaborative knowledge creation processes
  • understanding idea improvement in design teams

Claims¶

  • Engineering, product design, and service design teams differ significantly in shared epistemic agency during collaborative prototyping [+W]
  • Engineering team focused on generative collaborative actions, product design team on creating shared understanding, and service design team on alleviating lack of knowledge [+W]
  • All teams showed divergent-then-convergent idea improvement patterns across prototyping sessions [+W]
  • Engineering team converged earlier than other teams, concluding prototyping two minutes before the time limit [+W]
  • Differences in shared epistemic agency between design teams change across prototyping phases [+W]
  • Each team showed statistically significant changes in shared epistemic agency across prototyping phases [+W]
