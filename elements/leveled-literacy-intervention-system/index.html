Leveled Literacy Intervention (LLI)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

LLI is a short-term, supplementary, small-group literacy intervention designed to help struggling readers achieve grade-level competency, developed by Irene C. Fountas and Gay Su Pinnell in 2009. It delivers "explicit instruction in phonological awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, reading comprehension, oral language skills, and writing" through fast-paced daily lessons in which teachers alternate texts at students' independent and instructional levels. The Primary Systems (K–2) are intended to run 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week, for 12–18 weeks with groups of three students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Delivered as a supplement to regular classroom literacy instruction in small groups, with teachers using the Fountas & Pinnell Benchmark Assessment System to match students to texts of progressing difficulty

Constraints¶

  • The reviewed evidence covers only grades K–2; this report does not present any evidence of effectiveness for the LLI Intermediate Systems

Target Learners¶

  • Struggling readers in grades K–2 reading below grade level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • General reading achievement, reading fluency, alphabetics, phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, comprehension, and writing about reading

Claims¶

  • In the 2010 RCT, LLI produced a statistically significant positive effect on reading fluency for grades 1–2 (effect size 0.27, improvement index +11) [+M]
  • In a 2010 RCT in Georgia and New York, LLI produced a statistically significant positive effect on general reading achievement (effect size 0.35, improvement index +14) [+M]
  • In a 2013 Denver RCT, LLI showed a statistically significant positive effect on BAS reading achievement, a negative but non-significant difference on DRA2, and a positive but non-significant difference on STAR for female students in grades 1–2 [+M]
  • In the 2010 RCT, LLI showed no discernible effects on alphabetics (domain average effect size 0.13, not statistically significant) [~M]
  • Leveled Literacy Intervention has positive effects on general reading achievement, potentially positive effects on reading fluency, and no discernible effects on alphabetics for beginning readers in grades K–2 [+M]
  • In the 2013 RCT, subgroup findings for female students in grades 1–2 on the STAR Early Literacy Assessment were positive but not statistically significant, yet large enough to be considered substantively important [+M]
