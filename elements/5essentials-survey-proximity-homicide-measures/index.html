5Essentials Survey measures of school climate and organization¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The 5Essentials Survey is completed every spring by students in grades 4-12 and all teachers in CPS pre-K-12 classrooms, plus key staff, and is "designed to capture important elements of school climate and organization." For this study the team identified 14 student-survey and 11 teacher-survey measures that prior research suggests might be protective after adverse experiences, and used OLS regression to relate these measures to school-specific changes in student outcomes with proximity to homicide.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Completed annually by students in grades 4-12 and teachers across the school to yield school-level climate measures

Constraints¶

  • The study used it correlationally; the analysis cannot determine which measures were comparatively more important

Target Learners¶

  • CPS students in grades 4-12
  • K-12 teachers and school staff

Target Learning Goals¶

  • School climate and organization supporting academic and behavioral outcomes amid community violence

Claims¶

  • Schools that mitigated proximity-to-homicide effects had stronger, more positive school climates, including engaging instruction and trusting relationships [+W]
  • Some high schools mitigated the typical negative effects of proximity to homicide on student outcomes while most schools saw declines [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • 5Essentials-based school climate measures (Student Physical Safety, Teacher Physical Safety, Student-Teacher Trust)
