Technology Integration¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 24 claims (11 for, 11 mixed, 2 against) · 23 studies (8 associational, 4 review, 4 qualitative, 3 design, 2 theoretical, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 2 of 23 report an effect size · 24 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Technology integration is the element in which digital tools are used in direct service of learning goals, feedback, access, or collaboration.

Design Implications¶

Affordances¶

  • Digital Learning
  • Multimodal Instruction

Claims¶

  • Teachers receiving 11 or more hours of training on integrating digital content report roughly double the reliance on software and the Internet compared with untrained teachers [+W]
  • A one-to-one wireless laptop initiative in Hiawassee, Georgia was accompanied by improved test scores, a 24 percent rise in adult education enrollment and a 71 percent rise in G.E.D. completions [+W]
  • Integrated instructional hours correlate more positively with technology integration ability than stand-alone technology courses [+W]
  • Both case-study teachers increased web and technology use for lesson preparation, presentation, and classroom exploration over the project [+W]
  • PLI students digitally collaborated significantly more than non-PLI students with the same technology access, and digital collaboration had a small significant association with SBAC scores [+W]
  • Integrating AI in collaborative learning raises challenges including algorithmic bias, over-reliance on technology, and teacher training needs [~W]
  • Students' overall AI knowledge improved significantly after four integrated AI-biology lessons [+W]
  • Higher reliance on AI tools is correlated with lower critical-thinking performance, mediated by cognitive offloading [~W]
  • The most critical challenge in integrating AI into instructional design is pedagogical alignment (14 of 28 studies, 50.0%) [~W]
  • Coached teachers report significantly higher confidence in their ability to implement impactful technology use [+W]
  • A rural district that closed device and connectivity divides and trained teachers saw graduation rates rise from 67 percent to over 94 percent and college enrollment double [~W]
  • Access to a structured conversational AI tutor increased post-test content scores over a no-AI holdout with ambient consumer AI available [+M]
  • Teachers' practical use of AI in science and green energy learning significantly predicts their AI integration ability (β = 0.391, p < 0.01, f² = 0.180) [+W]
  • Teachers actively use AI tools but face challenges with content accuracy and usage guidelines [~W]
  • Learner-level challenges of ChatGPT in STEAM IBL include over-reliance, inaccurate or irrelevant feedback, conceptual confusion, and diminished creativity [-W]
  • Effective AI use increases AI over-reliance, which negatively affects sustainable learning performance [-W]
  • Staff highlight equity concerns about student access to and proficiency with GenAI tools and digital literacy, and are uncertain about staff responsibilities in training students. [~W]
  • AI integration in online learning raises interconnected challenges of academic integrity, equity and bias, ethics and privacy, and institutional policy gaps [~W]
  • Students rated ChatGPT highly for completing activities more quickly, among the highest-scoring survey items in both courses [+W]
  • Educators' use of AI in PBL is accompanied by persistent ethical concerns about data privacy, algorithmic bias, and educator autonomy [~W]
  • AI-based educational technology use is positively associated with critical thinking among undergraduate medical students [+W]
  • After workshops, students' active GenAI use concentrated in uncertain design phases and decreased or disappeared in more certain phases [~W]
  • Nursing academics fear GenAI overreliance undermines critical thinking, knowledge translation and graduates' readiness for safe clinical practice [~W]
  • Faculty with negative versus positive AI pedagogical orientation differ in how they interpret AI's role in thinking: cautious peripheral use versus integrated use with managed concerns [~W]
