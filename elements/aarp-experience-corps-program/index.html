AARP Foundation Experience Corps volunteer tutoring program for struggling K-3 readers¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Experience Corps engages "volunteer tutors ages 50 or older to work with struggling readers in grades K-3". Tutors work one-to-one or in small groups of up to four students at least twice weekly for an average of 30 minutes per session, committing up to 15 hours a week, with or without a stipend. Tutors receive extensive training (usually about 25 hours) and structured materials to support their tutoring.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Volunteers ages 50 or older committing up to 15 hours a week
  • Extensive training (usually about 25 hours) and structured materials

Constraints¶

  • Sessions average 30 minutes and occur at least twice weekly
  • Tutors may or may not receive a stipend

Target Learners¶

  • Struggling readers in grades K-3

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading literacy

Claims¶

  • Experience Corps tutoring improves reading outcomes for struggling K-3 readers, with an average effect size of +0.13 across two qualifying studies [+W]
  • Experience Corps showed significant differences on ECLAS in one study and on Woodcock Johnson Passage Comprehension in the other [+W]
  • Among third graders, Education Corps tutoring showed a positive but non-significant effect on the state End-of-Grade reading assessment (+0.13) [+W]
  • Education Corps high-dosage literacy tutoring raises end-of-year DIBELS Composite scores more than business-as-usual supports for below-benchmark K-3 students [+W]
