# First Step to Success early intervention program (screening, CLASS classroom intervention, HomeBase parent training)

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
First Step to Success is "an early intervention program designed to help children who are at risk for developing aggressive or antisocial behavioral patterns." A trained behavior coach works with the student, class peers, teacher, and parents for approximately 50 to 60 hours over three months. It comprises three interconnected modules — screening, classroom intervention (CLASS), and parent training (HomeBase) — in which students earn points toward behavioral goals, peers reinforce appropriate behavior, and parents are coached weekly in six competencies such as communication, cooperation, and limit setting. Implementation costs approximately $500 per student.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained behavior coach who works with the student, class peers, teacher, and parents; the CLASS module requires 30 program days across coach, teacher, and maintenance phases
#### Constraints
- Used with students in kindergarten through third grade at risk for antisocial behavior patterns; no information on the scope of use or demographics of program users is available

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten through third-grade children at risk for emotional disturbance or antisocial behavior

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing problem behavior, increasing adaptive prosocial behavior, and improving social and academic competence
