REACH practice framework for differentiated instruction (Rock et al., 2008)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

REACH is a practice framework for differentiated instruction reported in the article as produced by Rock et al. (2008), with five components the article enumerates: "1) Reflecting on will and skill; 2) Evaluating the curriculum; 3) Analysing the learners; 4) Crafting research-based lessons; and, 5) Honing in on the data." The article uses REACH as a successful blueprint whose boundary conditions inform its own prototype framework. Each component is described: reflection on readiness and misgivings, identifying critical content and big ideas, analysing student readiness and interests, crafting lessons with entry at performance level, and data-driven instructional decisions.

Context¶

  • Formative assessment data are available to evaluate curriculum and hone in on data

Target Learners¶

  • secondary mathematics students in mixed-ability classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • differentiated instruction that caters for student readiness, interests and needs in mathematics

Claims¶

  • Sporadic, disconnected professional learning does not change practice long term, whereas well-structured programs can build differentiation skills with positive student outcomes [+W]
  • Teacher efficacy underpins the ability to differentiate instruction effectively, with efficacy dependent on pedagogical content knowledge [+W]
