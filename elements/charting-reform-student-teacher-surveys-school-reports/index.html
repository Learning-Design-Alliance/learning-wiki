Charting Reform student and teacher surveys with individualized school reports¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An element consisting of two surveys, Charting Reform: The Students Speak and Charting Reform: The Teachers' Turn, administered in Spring 1994 to sixth, eighth and tenth grade students and to elementary and high school teachers. The Consortium produced individualized school reports with summary profile graphs comparing a school to all participating schools and to similar schools, plus detailed Part II scale distributions with top and bottom quartile benchmarks and interpretation tables for scale scores.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A minimum of 50 percent of teachers and/or students completing questionnaires for a school to receive a report, reduced to 42 percent for teachers due to inconsistencies in base numbers

Constraints¶

  • Confidentiality was promised to students and teachers, and reports remain the property of the school with the Consortium not distributing copies to anyone else

Target Learners¶

  • sixth, eighth and tenth grade students and their teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • assessing school strengths and weaknesses and the effectiveness of improvement efforts

Claims¶

  • The Charting Reform surveys reached 266 elementary schools and 40 high schools in Chicago in Spring 1994 [+W]
  • Teacher views of Local School Council contribution differ sharply between top and bottom quartile schools [+W]
  • Students' reports of parental support for learning are far higher in top quartile schools, where two-thirds report moderate or very supportive parents [+W]
  • Principal leadership regard is nearly unanimous in top quartile schools but low for about 40 percent of teachers in bottom quartile schools [+W]
  • Most students, even in bottom quartile schools, report feeling mostly safe at school [+W]
  • Teacher influence over school decisions is minimal or limited for most teachers in bottom quartile schools but extensive or moderate for most in top quartile schools [+W]
