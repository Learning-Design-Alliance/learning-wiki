# Erroneous Examples

## Description
Erroneous examples are worked examples that intentionally contain a mistake for learners to identify, explain, and correct. They are useful when the design goal is to make misconceptions visible and strengthen conceptual discrimination, not just demonstrate a correct procedure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A correct comparison standard**: Learners need criteria, prior knowledge, or a correct model to evaluate the error.
- **Explanation prompts**: The activity should ask what is wrong, why it is wrong, and how to repair it.
- **Instructional safety**: Learners need to treat errors as analyzable, not shameful.
#### Constraints
- **Absolute novices may be confused**: Without a baseline model, learners may encode the wrong procedure.
- **Trivial mistakes do little**: The error should illuminate a real misconception, not a typo.

### Target Learners
- Learners with enough prior knowledge to critique a partially wrong solution.

### Target Learning Goals
- Strengthen conceptual understanding, misconception detection, and self-monitoring.

## Examples
- Students inspect a flawed algebra solution and explain where the reasoning breaks.
- Learners review a paragraph with a weak claim-evidence link and revise it to meet the rubric.
