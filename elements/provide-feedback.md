# Provide Feedback

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Providing feedback gives learners information about the gap between their current and desired performance, so they can correct errors and refine understanding. Effective feedback answers three questions: Where am I going? How am I doing? Where to next? (Hattie & Timperley, 2007). It functions as the corrective loop that makes [Practice](practice.md) productive rather than merely repetitive.

## Design Implications

Feedback is among the most powerful influences on learning, but its effects are highly variable — it can improve or *degrade* performance depending on design [Feedback effects are highly variable and can be negative.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.120.2.254) [~S]. Feedback should be specific, task-focused, and actionable, directing attention to the work rather than the person. It works best when learners act on it — feedback followed by revision or retry produces far more learning than feedback that is merely received [Formative feedback loops improve achievement.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308317959) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear performance goal against which the work is evaluated
- Specific, task-level information about what was done well and what to change — not just a grade or "good job"
- Timing matched to the task: immediate for procedural skills, delayed for deeper conceptual work
- An opportunity for learners to act on the feedback (revise, retry, self-explain) [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Feedback that directs attention to the self (praise, grades, social comparison) often has null or negative effects on performance [Feedback effects are highly variable and can be negative.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.120.2.254) [~S]
- Overly detailed feedback can overwhelm novices; too much information increases extraneous load [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — limit to one or two actionable points per iteration
- Immediate feedback is not always superior: for complex or conceptual tasks, delayed feedback supports retention better [~M]
- Feedback without opportunity to apply it is largely wasted effort

### Target Learners
- All levels, but especially learners in formative stages who still have room to revise [Formative feedback loops improve achievement.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308317959) [+S]
- Low-achieving learners benefit most from cues and task-level guidance; high achievers may benefit more from delayed, less scaffolded feedback [~M]
- Learners with low self-efficacy need feedback framed around progress and strategy rather than ability [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Error correction: identifying and repairing misconceptions and procedural mistakes
- Knowledge refinement: moving from rough to polished performance through iterative revision
- Self-regulation: building learners' capacity to monitor and evaluate their own work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — feedback is the core mechanism by which assessment becomes formative rather than merely evaluative; the assessment itself is only useful if its results are translated into actionable feedback
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — well-targeted feedback reduces unproductive search by telling learners exactly where their model diverges from the target, rather than leaving them to diagnose errors unaided
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — feedback closes the loop on practice; it converts output into a new learning event by prompting revision, self-explanation, or retry

## Examples

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Immediate correctness feedback on practice exercises with hint ladders; learners can retry until mastery before advancing.

**[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — Item-level immediate feedback with spaced re-testing of missed items, enacting feedback plus [spaced repetition](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md).

**[Writing portfolios with draft-revision cycles](https://www.edutopia.org/article/using-portfolios-student-reflection)** — Teacher and peer feedback delivered on drafts, with mandatory revision, so feedback is always acted upon.

**Peer review protocols (e.g., [Calibrated Peer Review](https://calibratedpeerreview.org))** — Structured rubric-based peer feedback that also builds learners' evaluative judgment.
