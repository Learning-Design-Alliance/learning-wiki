Early Education Essentials surveys (now The Essential 0-5 Survey)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Early Education Essentials are surveys "created in partnership with Start Early" that "use teacher and parent perceptions to measure six organizational conditions of early childhood education programs." They extend and complement existing measures of ECE quality constructs and are intended to give leaders and practitioners actionable data about organizational supports. The instrument has since been renamed The Essential 0-5 Survey.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires teacher and parent survey responses from ECE sites to compute scale scores

Constraints¶

  • Measures organizational conditions rather than directly observing classroom practice

Target Learners¶

  • early childhood education practitioners and program leaders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring and improving organizational conditions that support effective work with children and families

Claims¶

  • The Early Education Essentials is reliable across multiple ECE settings [+W]
  • The Early Education Essentials is sensitive to site-level differences [+W]
  • Some, but not all, measured organizational conditions are associated in expected directions with site-level ECE quality metrics [+W]
  • Strongly organized ECE programs create contexts more supportive of teaching, learning, and family engagement than weakly organized programs [+W]
  • Parents offer a unique perspective best captured by a distinct sixth essential, Parent Voice [+W]
  • Family voices contributed to differentiating strong and weak organizational conditions across all essentials, not only Involved Families [+W]
  • Programs with strong essential supports are more likely to have higher teacher-child interactions and better child attendance than programs with very weak supports [+W]
