Project On-Track high-dosage small-group literacy tutoring program with Amplify Reading mCLASS intervention¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Project On-Track is a technology-supported, small-group tutoring intervention for grade 1–3 students in northeastern Tennessee, launched in 2021. All schools maintain 1:3 tutor–student ratios, 30-minute sessions at least twice weekly for at least 12 weeks a semester, and use the mCLASS intervention component of Amplify Reading, which "draws on data from regular progress monitoring to generate customized tutoring lessons that are highly structured and prescribed." mCLASS has three components: the DIBELS assessment, supports for tutor-delivered small-group instruction, and an online platform with reading games.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • All tutors, regardless of teaching experience, must participate in the same training on the Project On-Track model, including asynchronous modules, a debrief, and trauma-informed care training
  • Schools must ensure all students work a minimum of 30 minutes with a human tutor per session; platform time does not count

Constraints¶

  • Project On-Track did not collect reliable data about whether schools used the blended learning approach

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grade 1-3 identified as struggling readers or most at risk for reading difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Foundational literacy skills: phonological awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and comprehension aligned with the science of reading

Claims¶

  • Slightly fewer than half (42 percent) of students who started the year in the at risk DIBELS category moved to a lower risk category after a full year of Project On-Track tutoring [+W]
  • Among students who started the year most at risk for reading difficulties, none of the three implementation features (timing, frequency, tutor qualifications) was associated with end-of-year literacy progress [~W]
  • Rural schools were far more likely than nonrural schools to offer Project On-Track tutoring during school only [~W]
  • Timing of tutoring was significantly associated with tutor qualifications and with student grade level and baseline risk [~W]
  • Sites offering tutoring during school were much more likely than out-of-school-hours sites to offer tutoring more than twice a week [~W]
  • Schools using only teachers, administrators, or paraprofessionals as tutors were more likely to serve students with higher baseline DIBELS scores [~W]
