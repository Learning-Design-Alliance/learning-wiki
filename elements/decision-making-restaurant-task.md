# Decision-making restaurant-choice task: pairs merge into larger groups across cycles while re-choosing a restaurant

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Adapted from a textbook task, pairs compare three real English menus and must agree on one restaurant, then merged groups of four and eight re-run the choice with the previously chosen and rejected menus, creating immediate repetition cycles. The post-task elicits the linguistic and non-linguistic reasons for choices and polls the most influential speaker's convincing strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Printed menus in English gathered beforehand; the task works well for a group of 16 students or more; modelling of the task before performance
#### Constraints
- Some quiet learners tend not to participate in bigger groups, so the teacher should circulate and ask them for their opinions; adding a new menu increases cognitive load and requires adjustment time

### Target Learners
- additional language learners studying food vocabulary and suggestion forms, ages 12 to 55 in the author's practice

### Target Learning Goals
- making, responding to, agreeing and disagreeing with suggestions; justifying choices with arguments
