All Day Kindergarten (ADK) Program: extra half day of individualized language-based instruction for underachieving kindergartners¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The ADK Program, instituted in Columbus Public Schools in January 1972, provides underachieving kindergarten pupils "an extra half day of instruction in addition to the half day of instruction provided in the regular kindergarten classroom." In 1992-93 an equivalent of 14.5 program teachers served 18 high-priority schools, each teacher instructing two groups limited to 12 pupils, serving 405 pupils for an average of 10.8 hours of instruction per week. The program operates on the philosophy that additional help and attention will better prepare pupils for first grade.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Serves underachieving kindergarten pupils selected by rank-ordered scores on the Early Development Checklist and Letter Identification instruments

Constraints¶

  • Groups limited to 12 pupils each; pupils not immediately served were placed on a waiting list

Target Learners¶

  • Underachieving kindergarten pupils in high-priority elementary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Early concepts about print and language development to prepare for first grade

Claims¶

  • Process evaluation found no major recordkeeping or pupil-selection problems in ADK classrooms [+W]
  • 78.9% of ADK pupils attending at least 80% of sessions met the print-concepts objective of 12 or more of 17 items on the Balloons test [+M]
  • Balloons posttest scores in the ADK evaluation sample had a median of 15 items correct, ranging from 2 to 17 [+W]
  • ADK program teachers rated the four September 1992 inservice meetings very positively, averaging 4.7 on a 5-point scale [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Balloons: A Concepts About Print Assessment, a criterion-referenced 17-item measure from the Kindergarten Assessment Portfolio
