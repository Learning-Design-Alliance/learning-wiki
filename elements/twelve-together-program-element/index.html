Twelve Together peer support and mentoring program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Twelve Together is a one-year, voluntary peer support and mentoring program for middle and high school students offering "weekly after-school discussion groups" of about 12 students mixing high- and lower-academic-risk students, led by two trained volunteer adult facilitators. Discussions focus on personal, family, and social issues chosen by student interest. The program also provides homework assistance, a weekend camping outing to build group cohesion, college campus visits, and social events. Participants agree to study regularly, not skip classes, and work to improve their grades.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Trained volunteer adult facilitators (usually college students) to lead weekly discussion groups and provide homework assistance

Constraints¶

  • The program is no longer active and has no current developer or contact information

Target Learners¶

  • middle and early high school students at risk of academic failure

Target Learning Goals¶

  • staying in school
  • improving grades
  • regular study habits and class attendance

Claims¶

  • The WWC improvement index for Twelve Together is +13 percentile points for staying in school and –6 percentile points for progressing in school [~W]
  • Twelve Together shows no discernible effects on progressing in school as measured by highest grade completed [-W]
  • Twelve Together has potentially positive effects on staying in school, based on a lower (non-significant) dropout rate than control [+W]
  • The evidence base for Twelve Together is a single randomized controlled trial that met WWC standards only with reservations due to differential attrition [~W]
