# Self Efficacy

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Self efficacy is a learner's belief in their capability to succeed at a specific task or domain, not a general sense of self-worth. It functions as a proximal driver of learning: learners with higher efficacy set more ambitious goals, persist longer through difficulty, and recover faster from failure [Bandura, 1997]. Because it is domain-specific and malleable, it is a design target rather than a fixed learner trait.

## Design Implications

Instructional design shapes self efficacy primarily through the four sources Bandura identified: mastery experiences, vicarious experience, verbal persuasion, and physiological/affective states. Mastery experiences — genuine success on challenging tasks — are the strongest source [+S], so sequencing tasks so learners experience early, authentic success is the highest-leverage design move. Vicarious experience works through [Demonstration](demonstration.md) by peer or near-peer models [+M]; seeing a similar other succeed raises efficacy more than watching an expert. Feedback design matters: feedback that attributes success to effort and strategy rather than fixed ability builds efficacy, while ability-focused praise or harsh evaluative feedback undermines it [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks calibrated slightly above current ability so success is achievable with effort ([Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md))
- Visible progress indicators and mastery criteria so learners can attribute improvement to their own actions
- Feedback that is specific, process-oriented, and delivered in time to be used [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Models of similar others succeeding, not only expert performance ([Demonstration](demonstration.md))

#### Constraints
- Efficacy built on easy success collapses at the first real difficulty; success must be earned on challenging tasks to transfer [-S]
- High self efficacy without commensurate skill produces overconfidence, reduced monitoring, and premature disengagement from help [~M]
- Unfavourable social comparison (public leaderboards, norm-referenced grading) lowers efficacy for struggling learners even when their absolute progress is good [-M]
- Verbal encouragement alone ("you can do it!") without mastery evidence has weak, short-lived effects [+W]

### Target Learners
- Novices and low-achieving learners, who benefit most from early mastery experiences and near-peer models [+S]
- Learners recovering from repeated failure, who need re-attribution of failure to strategy and effort rather than ability [+M]
- Less critical for already-confident experts, for whom additional efficacy boosting adds little [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence and engagement on challenging, multi-step tasks
- Self-regulated learning: goal setting, monitoring, and strategic revision [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)
- Affective goals: reducing anxiety and avoidance in high-stakes domains (math, writing, public speaking)

### Affordances
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — graduated support guarantees early success experiences, the strongest source of efficacy; [fading](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) then lets learners attribute the success to themselves rather than the support
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — low-stakes formative assessment with actionable feedback converts assessment from an efficacy threat into an efficacy builder
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) — competence is one of three basic needs; efficacy-building task design simultaneously supports autonomy and relatedness
- [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) — vicarious experience through peer modeling is a core efficacy source and the theoretical bridge between observation and belief change

## Related Elements
- [Demonstration](demonstration.md) — provides vicarious mastery experiences; near-peer models are more efficacy-building than expert models
- [Coaching](coaching.md) — delivers the process-level feedback and attributional reframing that sustain efficacy through difficulty
- [Check-In](check-in.md) — surfaces affective states (anxiety, discouragement) that are themselves a source of efficacy information
- [Fading](fading.md) — timing of support removal determines whether learners credit themselves or the scaffold

## Examples
- **[Achievable Micro-Goals](../strategies/achievable_micro-goals.md)** — breaking large tasks into short, winnable steps creates a rapid sequence of mastery experiences, the strongest efficacy source.
- **Mastery-based platforms (e.g., [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org))** — learners advance only after demonstrating proficiency, so visible progress and success rate are structurally protected; hints and [worked examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) keep early tasks winnable.
- **Writing conferences** — brief one-to-one feedback naming a specific strategy the writer used successfully, building efficacy through verbal persuasion anchored in real evidence.
- **Reciprocal teaching** — students take turns leading comprehension strategies, giving each learner a public mastery experience and modeling for peers.
