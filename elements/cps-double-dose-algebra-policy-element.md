# Chicago Public Schools 2003 double-dose algebra policy with three structural guidelines for support courses

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (4 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), `q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
A district-wide policy requiring all first-time ninth-graders scoring below the national median on the eighth-grade math test to enroll in a regular algebra class plus an additional algebra support class for a full academic year. The district suggested schools program support courses so that "double-dose algebra students should have the same teacher in algebra and algebra support," courses be sequential, and support classmates be the same students; most schools followed these guidelines in 2003, with adherence declining after the same-teacher guideline was removed in 2004.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An eligibility cutoff (eighth-grade test scores below the national median)
- Scheduling capacity for a second algebra period; the district removed the same-teacher guideline in 2004 after scheduling complaints
#### Constraints
- The district did not mandate a particular curriculum, and the study does not address variation in instructional content across schools

### Target Learners
- Below-norm ninth-grade students in all CPS high schools, including 60 neighborhood, 11 magnet, and 6 vocational schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Ninth-grade algebra achievement and course passing
