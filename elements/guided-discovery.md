## Description
Guided discovery is the element in which learners investigate, infer, or notice patterns for themselves, but with prompts, constraints, examples, or feedback that keep the exploration productive. It is useful when the goal is active sensemaking without leaving learners entirely unguided.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A discoverable pattern, concept, or relationship**
- **Prompts or constraints that focus attention**
- **Feedback or debrief to consolidate what was discovered**
#### Constraints
- **Too little structure can overwhelm novices**
- **Too much structure collapses discovery into direct telling**

### Target Learners
- Learners who can benefit from active inference when support is available.

### Target Learning Goals
- Build conceptual understanding, noticing, and transfer through supported exploration.
