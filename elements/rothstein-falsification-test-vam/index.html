Rothstein falsification test for teacher value-added models¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 5 against) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A falsification test for teacher value-added models, introduced by Jesse Rothstein (2010) and re-assessed in this paper. It rests on the observation that standard VAMs "suggest implausible future teacher effects on past student achievement, a finding that obviously cannot be viewed as causal", which "appears to indicate bias in VAM estimates of current teacher contributions to student learning". The present paper evaluates whether a failed test truly demonstrates bias.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires VAM teacher effect estimates and the test's implausible future-teacher-effect diagnostic as described by Rothstein (2010)

Constraints¶

  • The paper shows the test can reject VAMs even when estimates are unbiased and students are randomly assigned conditional on model covariates, so a rejection is not definitive evidence of bias

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students whose teachers are evaluated with value-added models

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Fair and accurate estimation of teacher contributions to student achievement for personnel policy

Claims¶

  • Rothstein (2010) found that standard VAMs imply implausible future-teacher effects on past student achievement [+W]
  • The Rothstein falsification test is not definitive in showing bias in VAM teacher effect estimates [-W]
  • The Rothstein test can reject VAMs even when students are randomly assigned conditional on the covariates in the model [-W]
  • The Rothstein falsification test can reject value-added models even when estimated teacher effects are unbiased [-W]
  • The Rothstein falsification test generates large and implausible future teacher effects even when students are randomly assigned to classrooms [-W]
  • Theoretically and in simulations, the Rothstein test often falsifies unbiased VAMs and fails to falsify biased VAMs [-W]
  • Tracking based on lagged student achievement is usually accounted for in value-added models, which is why the falsification test can mislead [~W]
  • Detecting that students are tracked to teachers does not necessarily imply bias in teacher effectiveness estimates [~W]
