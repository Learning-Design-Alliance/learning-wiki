Chart No. 1: eight generic instructional formats mapped to generic learning outcomes from a mastery viewpoint¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Chart No. 1 presents the author's proposed analysis of the black box called the instruction/learning interface, taking a mastery viewpoint on eight generic learning outcomes. It lists coded generic instructional formats (A Lecture through H Programmed Instruction), each with a generic description of the instructional format, the related generic learning outcomes, and group size, drawing on taxonomies by Bloom, Krathwohl, Harrow, Simpson, Gagne, and others. For example, lecture is described as formal oral presentation of primarily one-way communication, linked to student acquisition of cognitive and affective types of knowledge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Each instructional format described in terms of the observable morphology of that format, with a generically described learning outcome

Constraints¶

  • The author presents it as one solution, a fairly limited follow-up to his 1972 tuition-pricing chart with generic but undefined instructional formats

Target Learners¶

  • post-secondary education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • linking instructional formats to cognitive, affective, and psychomotor learning outcomes

Affordances¶

  • Black Box Analysis Instruction Learning Interface

Claims¶

  • Data Element 1107 (Method of Instruction) shows three deficiencies: higher-education bias, non-descriptive morphology, and no stated relation between instructional methods and learning outcomes [+W]
  • In one synthesis of group-based mastery learning, five observational studies found more time-on-task in mastery classes and one community-college evaluation found lower attrition in seven of eight disciplines [+W]
