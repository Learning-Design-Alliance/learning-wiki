# Nine types of positive interdependence teachers can structure into cooperative activities

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Digest 4 examines nine types of positive interdependence and how teachers can incorporate them into cooperative learning activities: goal, resource, role, identity, task, reward, outside enemy, environmental, and fantasy interdependence. Goal interdependence requires a group goal all members must reach for the group to succeed; resource interdependence limits shared materials; role interdependence assigns specific roles; task interdependence sequences members' jobs like an assembly line; reward interdependence gives a group-level reward only if the whole group succeeds; and fantasy interdependence embeds the task in simulated life-or-death scenarios. The general recommendation is at least three different types per cooperative activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All properly constructed cooperative activities have goal interdependence
- The general recommendation is that there be at least 3 different types of positive interdependence in every cooperative activity
#### Constraints
- Identity interdependence isn't as strong as some of the other interdependences and shouldn't be relied on by itself
- Outside enemy interdependence can generate bad feelings between groups and spawn negative behaviors associated with competition
- Rewards should not be of any great value and should not be used when activities are already of high interest
- Not all cooperative activities require roles; contrived roles are likely to be trivial or ineffective

### Target Learners
- elementary and secondary students

### Target Goals
- sustained cooperation toward shared goals
- individual accountability within groups
