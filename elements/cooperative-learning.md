# Cooperative Learning

## Description
Cooperative learning organizes instruction so that small groups of learners work interdependently toward a shared goal, with each member responsible for both their own learning and their teammates' learning. It is distinguished from mere group work by deliberate structuring: positive interdependence, individual accountability, promotive interaction, social skills, and group processing (Johnson & Johnson's five elements).

## Design Implications

Cooperative learning reliably improves achievement when group goals and individual accountability are both present; removing either component sharply reduces or eliminates the effect [Cooperative learning improves achievement when group rewards are tied to individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. The task must genuinely require collaboration — if it can be completed faster alone, groups default to division of labor with minimal interaction. Roles, turn structures, and shared products should force learners to explain, elaborate, and question one another, since the learning benefit comes primarily from peer explanation and negotiation rather than proximity [Cooperative learning improves achievement when group rewards are tied to individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task with genuine positive interdependence — success requires every member's contribution
- Individual accountability: each learner's mastery is assessed separately (quizzes, random reporter selection, individual products)
- Explicit teaching of collaborative skills (turn-taking, disagreeing with ideas not people) rather than assuming them
- Time for group processing — brief reflection on what the team did well and what to change

#### Constraints
- Unstructured group work without individual accountability allows free-riding and produces little gain [Cooperative learning improves achievement when group rewards are tied to individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-S]
- Group competition *between* teams without interdependence *within* teams does not produce the effect [Cooperative learning improves achievement when group rewards are tied to individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [~M]
- Heterogeneous grouping matters: groups of similar-ability peers reduce the elaboration benefit of peer explanation [~M]
- Tasks requiring sustained individual concentration (e.g., extended writing) may be disrupted by group formats [~W]

### Target Learners
- Learners across achievement levels benefit, but low achievers gain most from peer explanation, while high achievers gain from the elaboration required to teach [Cooperative learning improves achievement when group rewards are tied to individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+M]
- Learners who struggle socially need explicit social-skills instruction and structured roles to participate productively
- Very young learners (below ~7) need simpler structures with shorter interaction cycles

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding through peer explanation and elaboration
- Problem solving with multiple approaches to compare and negotiate
- Social and communicative competencies as explicit outcomes
- Retention — explaining to peers is a form of [retrieval and elaboration](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) that strengthens memory

### Affordances
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — cooperative learning is the most rigorously structured enactment of this principle; the five elements specify *how* to make collaboration pedagogically effective rather than merely social
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — group problem-solving and peer explanation replace passive reception with generative processing
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — individual accountability checks make each learner's mastery visible to the group and teacher, feeding back into team support
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — distributing subtasks across members lets the group tackle problems no individual could hold in working memory alone, provided the task is genuinely divisible and interdependent

## Examples

**PALS (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies)** — Structured reciprocal peer tutoring in reading and math used widely in elementary schools; pairs alternate coach and player roles with scripted routines ([https://interventioncentral.org](https://interventioncentral.org)).

**STAD (Student Teams–Achievement Divisions)** — Slavin's structure: teams study together, then members take individual quizzes; team scores are based on individual improvement, embedding accountability in the reward system.

**Jigsaw (Aronson)** — Each member masters a unique segment, then teaches it to teammates; interdependence is structural because no one can complete the task without every segment. ([https://www.jigsaw.org](https://www.jigsaw.org))

**Kagan Structures** — A library of turn-taking formats (e.g., RoundRobin, Numbered Heads Together) that enforce equal participation and individual accountability in any subject.
