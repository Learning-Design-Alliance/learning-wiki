Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) whole-school intervention¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TAP is a whole-school intervention that aims to improve schools by raising teacher quality. It provides teachers with "opportunities for professional growth, promotion to school leadership roles without leaving the classroom, structured feedback, and performance-based compensation." Chicago Public Schools began implementing TAP in 2007, and this report evaluates its first-year impacts in the district's K-8 schools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Whole-school implementation across participating schools

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated only in Chicago Public Schools K-8 schools during the 2007 start-up year

Target Learners¶

  • K-8 teachers and students in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Raising teacher quality to improve student achievement

Claims¶

  • Teachers in TAP schools reported significantly more mentoring and support than peers in similar non-TAP schools [+W]
  • TAP produced no measurable impacts on student test scores through March of the start-up year [-W]
  • TAP teachers were five percentage points more likely to return to their schools than non-TAP teachers [+W]
  • Chicago TAP did not raise student math or reading scores overall during the four-year rollout in Chicago Public Schools [-M]
  • Teachers in Chicago TAP schools at the start of the program were about 20 percent more likely than teachers in comparison schools to remain in the same school three years later [+M]
  • Chicago TAP increased the amount of mentoring, promotion opportunities, and compensation in participating schools relative to non-TAP schools [+M]
  • Chicago TAP did not fully implement its performance-based pay or value-added components as intended [~M]
