Standards-based alternate portfolio assessment with multi-domain scoring rubric¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An alternate assessment in which student portfolios—a purposeful and systematic collection of student work evaluated against predetermined scoring criteria—document progress toward the same content standards as all students, using alternate learning expectations. Entries span audio and videotapes, photographs, checklists, interviews, surveys, rating scales, and existing records. A focused, holistic, domain scoring rubric scored each entry in performance, appropriateness, and level of assistance domains, with a settings domain scored once per subject area.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A pre-defined portfolio structure specifying the number and type of entries
  • A stakeholder-developed scoring rubric and trained, qualified readers

Constraints¶

  • Non-scoreable entries were common, especially at the lower end of the score range

Target Learners¶

  • Students with significant disabilities in grades 4, 6, 8, and 11

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Progress toward state content standards in literacy and mathematics

Affordances¶

  • Body Of Work Standard Setting Approach

Claims¶

  • Performance classifications varied considerably across grades in both literacy and mathematics for the alternate portfolio assessment [+W]
  • A body of work standard setting process produced classification agreement rates ranging from 71% to 97% for an alternate portfolio assessment [+W]
  • A mean-classification cut-score method outperformed a contrasting-groups-style mean method because it was more robust to outliers [+W]
