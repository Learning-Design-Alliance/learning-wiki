Teachers and Parents as Partners (TAPP / Conjoint Behavioral Consultation)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TAPP is a family-school partnership intervention developed at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln that addresses children's behavioral and academic challenges through an individualized approach. "Implementation of TAPP involves three meetings between an identified school specialist, teacher and parent with each meeting averaging 45-50 minutes," in which the specialist leads needs identification, goal setting, joint plan implementation, and progress monitoring. It has been extensively studied and implemented in rural communities where specialized support services are hard to access.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An identified school specialist (e.g., school counselor, school psychologist, instructional specialist, trauma specialist, administrator, or special education teacher) is needed to lead the TAPP process with teachers and parents.

Constraints¶

  • The time invested by school specialists over the six-week intervention is approximately four hours per child.

Target Learners¶

  • K–3 students with disruptive behavioral and academic challenges

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved student adaptive and classroom behavior
  • Stronger family-school partnerships

Claims¶

  • Direct classroom observations in the rural trial showed TAPP students exhibited more on-task and prosocial behavior and less off-task behavior [+W]
  • TAPP improves school-related behavioral problems per teacher ratings in the rural trial (effect size = +0.45) [+W]
  • TAPP strengthens teacher-reported family-school relationships in both trials, with parent-reported gains appearing in the rural trial [+W]
  • TAPP improves teacher-reported student adaptive behavior and social skills in K–3 students with disruptive behaviors (RCT, 21 elementary schools) [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Getting Ready family-partnership program for early childhood educators

Examples¶

  • Train school specialists through six online modules covering all stages of the TAPP process
