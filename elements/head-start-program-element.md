# Head Start program

## Description
Head Start is a national, federally funded program providing services to promote school readiness for children from birth to age 5 from predominantly low-income families. Services cover education, health and nutrition, family engagement, and other social services, and are delivered through center-based or family child care, home visits, or combinations operating full or half days for 8-12 months per year. The report states the program "seeks to promote school readiness by bolstering a child’s development and learning," with services responsive to each child and family's ethnic, cultural, and linguistic heritage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local grantees must provide a 20% cash or in-kind match to federal funds, and no more than 15% of total program costs may be used for program administration.
#### Constraints
- Program administrators have flexibility in service delivery, leading to considerable variability in the services offered across communities.

### Target Learners
- children from birth to age 5 from predominantly low-income families

### Target Learning Goals
- school readiness
- language and literacy skills
- cognition and general knowledge
- physical development and health
- social and emotional development
- approaches to learning
