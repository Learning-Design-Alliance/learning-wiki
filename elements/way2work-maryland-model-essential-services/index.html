Way2Work Maryland model of essential services for students with disabilities¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Way2Work Maryland is a demonstration model for high school students with disabilities that enhances and customizes education and employment services to improve college and career readiness. Its essential services included "an early referral to the vocational rehabilitation agency, a match to a community rehabilitation provider, opportunities for multiple work experiences, and numerous service collaboration activities." The model was funded by a U.S. Department of Education, Rehabilitation Services Administration grant to the Maryland Division of Rehabilitation Services, with the University of Maryland overseeing implementation and providing training and technical assistance.

Claims¶

  • The vocational rehabilitation agency and the University of Maryland implemented the Way2Work Maryland model as designed, and participants received the model services as intended [+W]
  • Most Way2Work Maryland participants (92 percent) had at least one work experience, 74 percent had at least two with one paid, and 44 percent had at least three with one paid [+W]
  • Participants reported satisfaction with selected Way2Work Maryland service components [+W]
  • Service use differed across local school systems in Way2Work Maryland [~W]
  • Way2Work Maryland did not affect employment or expectation outcomes as of 24 months after enrollment [-W]
  • Control group members had ready access to transition-related services, and Way2Work services may have substituted for existing services [~W]
  • Way2Work Maryland increased vocational rehabilitation agency engagement, with almost all treatment group members using agency-provided work-based learning experiences [+W]
  • Despite apparent strong implementation, Way2Work Maryland did not generate its intended effects; the report lists five possible reasons [-W]
  • Way2Work Maryland did not affect postsecondary education and training outcomes, but more treatment group members completed high school within two years of enrollment [~W]
