MAP Growth: computer-adaptive, vertically scaled interim assessment on the RIT scale¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

MAP Growth is a "vertically scaled interim assessment" measuring achievement and growth in grades K-12 reading and mathematics and grades 2-12 language usage and science. Scores are reported on the Rasch Unit (RIT) scale, "a vertical scale with equal-interval units" ranging from about 100 to 350, administered up to four times per year. Tests are grade banded, adaptive, and drawn from deep item pools with longitudinal exposure constraints; every test must pass a computer adaptive test simulation of eight consecutive administrations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Item pools deep enough to implement longitudinal exposure constraints so a student never sees the same item more than once during a long period
  • Trained test proctors available to assist students during in-school or remote administration

Constraints¶

  • It is "less useful for providing more frequent feedback or a daily tailoring of instruction to student needs"; it is not a summative or formative assessment

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in reading and mathematics
  • grades 2-12 students in language usage and science

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring student achievement and growth to inform instruction and program evaluation

Claims¶

  • Adaptive testing yields better measurement precision than a fixed-form test of the same length [+W]
