# ChatGPT as a lesson-adaptation and brainstorming tool for mathematics teachers' generative learning design

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The chapter examines ChatGPT as a generative AI tool teachers used in two ways: adapting an existing classroom task to incorporate active or generative learning, and serving as a brainstorming partner that posed mathematical questions from photos of teachers' communities. The authors conclude that "ChatGPT seemed to be almost, but not quite, useful as a lesson planning tool" — outputs were detailed and engaging on the surface, but did not reflect pedagogical understanding of grade-level standards, lacked easily generated student-facing materials, and raised reliability and hallucination concerns. Some issues may be ameliorated by prompting expertise; others may be endemic to the AI itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must review, correct, and adapt outputs, since mathematical flaws and grade-level misalignment require human justification
#### Constraints
- Lesson plans seemed too general or generic to be used in practice in an immediately actionable way

### Target Learners
- Mathematics teachers of grades 6 through pre-calculus and Algebra 1

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting teacher lesson planning for generative learning and mathematical question posing
