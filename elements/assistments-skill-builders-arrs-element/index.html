ASSISTments Skill Builders with the ARRS delayed reassessment system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

ASSISTments is a free online formative assessment and tutoring system for middle school mathematics, used by 650 teachers with over 5,000 student users a day. Within its Skill Builder problem sets, students master a skill by answering three questions correctly in a row, after which "a single -item test is administered after a pre -defined period of time, with a gradually increasing space in between reassessments" through the Automatic Reassessment and Relearning System (ARRS). This study used ARRS test outcomes to define productive persistence versus wheel-spinning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must attempt 10 or more problems in a problem set and complete an ARRS test for the productive/wheel-spinning classification to apply

Constraints¶

  • Success on the ARRS test can be noisy due to slips or guessing, and ARRS administration can be customized by teachers, producing missing data

Target Learners¶

  • middle school mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retention of mathematics skills mapped to U.S. Common Core standards

Claims¶

  • The relationship between bottom-out hint use and wheel-spinning is nuanced, depending on whether students avoid hints entirely [+W]
  • Not requesting any hints in at least one problem is related to greater wheel-spinning, consistent with help avoidance [+W]
  • A 15-feature J48 decision tree model distinguishes wheel-spinning from productive persistence in ASSISTments Skill Builders at AUC ROC 0.684 under student-skill-level cross-validation [+W]
  • A retention-based definition of wheel-spinning classifies a much lower proportion of students as wheel-spinning than Beck and Gong's opportunity-count definition [+W]
  • Consistently short delays between problems of the same skill are associated with greater wheel-spinning [+W]
  • Two distinct feature combinations identify two types of wheel-spinning students in ASSISTments Skill Builders [+W]
