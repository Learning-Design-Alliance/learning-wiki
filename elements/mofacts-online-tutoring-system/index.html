MoFaCTS online tutoring system used to deliver the retrieval practice experiment¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

MoFaCTS is the online drill-trial problem authoring and deployment platform through which the experiment was delivered. It presented the pretest, reading, retrieval practice, and posttest portions, scored all items immediately as 1 or 0, and scored short answers by "matching words in the participants’ responses to key terms necessary to answer the question correctly", using regular expressions to accept synonyms and handle ordering. It also administered corrective feedback and the 10-second review period after incorrect responses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Short answer scoring depends on authoring regular expressions that capture acceptable synonyms and word ordering for each item

Constraints¶

  • Scoring is keyword-matching based, so acceptable variation must be anticipated through pilot testing

Target Learners¶

  • adult online learners (MTurk workers) in the experiment

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retrieval practice of factual and applied concepts from instructional text

Claims¶

  • Higher self-reported confusion predicts a much larger negative effect on future performance when learners answer more questions incorrectly [~W]
  • No significant impact of frustration or boredom (or anxiousness, discouragement, distractedness) on practice performance was found [~W]
  • Multiple choice practice items produce significantly higher performance during retrieval practice than short answer items, with applied short answer the most difficult condition [+W]
  • Practice condition (format/depth) showed no significant differences on posttest performance for either practiced or unpracticed concepts [~W]
  • Successful retrieval practice of more difficult items (short answer, applied) yields greater future predicted success than successful retrieval of easier items (multiple choice, factual) [+W]
  • Unsuccessful retrieval attempts confer a smaller benefit to future performance than successful retrievals, whose advantage grows with item difficulty [+W]
