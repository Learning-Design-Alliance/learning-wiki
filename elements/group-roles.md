# Group Roles

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Group roles are assigned or negotiated responsibilities — such as facilitator, recorder, checker, or resource manager — that structure how members of a small group contribute to a shared task. Roles function as a form of procedural scaffolding: they specify who does what, reduce coordination overhead, and prevent the free-riding and status hierarchies that undermine collaborative work.

## Design Implications

Roles improve the quality of collaboration when they distribute *cognitive* work, not merely administrative chores; groups with substantive, interdependent roles outperform groups where roles are decorative [Cooperative learning works best when group goals are combined with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. Roles should rotate across tasks so every learner practices each function, and each role must carry individual accountability — a deliverable or contribution for which that member alone is responsible [Cooperative learning works best when group goals are combined with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task complex enough that multiple distinct contributions are genuinely needed
- Role descriptions that specify observable behaviors, not just titles ("asks each member to explain their reasoning" rather than "leader")
- Individual accountability mechanisms — each member's contribution is visible and assessable ([Assessment](assessment.md), [Check-Ins](check-in.md))
- Rotation of roles across sessions so no learner is locked into a low-cognitive-demand role

#### Constraints
- Roles assigned by ability or self-selection tend to reproduce status hierarchies; treating status as the problem to solve requires deliberately assigning competence to lower-status members [~M]
- Roles that partition a task into isolated pieces eliminate the interaction that produces collaborative learning gains — parallel work is not cooperation [~S]
- Overly scripted roles can suppress productive spontaneous interaction and add extraneous load for novices still learning the task itself [-W]
- With groups smaller than three or tasks shorter than ~10 minutes, role overhead exceeds its benefit

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack the collaboration skills to self-organize; roles substitute for missing procedural knowledge [+M]
- Mixed-status or heterogeneous groups, where roles equalize participation [~M]
- Less beneficial for mature self-regulating groups, who may find assigned roles constraining [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative and communication skills: turn-taking, facilitation, constructive challenge
- Individual accountability within interdependent work
- Metacognitive and self-regulation practice through rotating evaluative roles (checker, questioner)

### Affordances
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — roles operationalize the two conditions that make collaboration effective: positive interdependence and individual accountability
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — a role is temporary external structure for collaboration skills; rotation and eventual removal fade the support as groups internalize the behaviors
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — by pre-allocating coordination duties, roles free working memory for the task content rather than group logistics
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — role-based deliverables make each member's contribution observable, enabling formative feedback on both content and collaboration

## Examples

**[PIM (Positive Interdependence Model) roles](https://www.jigsaw.org)** — Jigsaw classrooms assign each member a distinct segment of content to master and teach, making the role itself the interdependence.

**Complex Instruction (Cohen & Lotan)** — Roles (facilitator, recorder, reporter, materials manager) paired with "assigning competence" to low-status students; used across Stanford's Developing Mathematical Ideas programs.

**[PBL Works (Buck Institute)](https://www.pblworks.org)** — Gold Standard PBL project templates include suggested team roles with rotation schedules for project-based learning.

**Lab group roles in OpenSciEd** — Middle-school science curricula assign investigator, speaker, and director roles that rotate each activity to distribute hands-on access.
