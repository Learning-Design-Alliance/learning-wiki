# Linked learning communities model for developmental postsecondary students

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Linked learning communities are postsecondary programs "defined by having social and curricular linkages" that give undergraduates intentional integration of the themes and concepts they are learning. In the specific linked model reviewed here, "cohorts of students enroll together in a foundational course (e.g., writing) and at least one additional content course (e.g., American history)", combining shared curricular content across courses with co-registration of the same peer cohort for social continuity. The model rests on the theory that active learning in a community-based setting improves academic outcomes by increasing social and academic integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Incorporates a shared intellectual theme with a linked or integrated curriculum
- Includes a community or common cohort of learners
#### Constraints
- All six reviewed implementations were single-semester linkages of one developmental course with one or two other courses, the most basic form of the model; the extent of curricular integration varied across sites and over time

### Target Learners
- Developmental education students at community colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement
- Degree attainment
- Postsecondary enrollment and credit accumulation
- Progress through developmental education
