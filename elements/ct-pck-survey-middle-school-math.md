# CT–PCK Survey: a 16-item vignette-based measure of middle school math teachers' computational thinking pedagogical content knowledge

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Computational Thinking–Pedagogical Content Knowledge (CT–PCK) Survey is a vignette-based instrument in which "each of the items in the initial 25-item CT–PCK Survey presents a single vignette depicting a math classroom scenario, along with four options representing different instructional responses to that scenario." One option integrates one of five core CT practices. It was developed with two large urban Midwest districts as part of the ENACT partnership and reduced to 16 items after psychometric analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administered to in-service grade 6–8 math teachers; items were built using Skilling and Stylianides' (2020) framework for designing vignette items in education research
#### Constraints
- The study states the survey "is not intended to be used to evaluate teachers' performance, their instructional quality, or their math content knowledge"

### Target Learners
- Middle school (grades 6–8) math teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Pedagogical content knowledge for integrating computational thinking practices (pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, debugging, algorithm development) into math instruction
