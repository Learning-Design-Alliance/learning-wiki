Multi-occasion K-8 math and reading achievement dataset (2016-17, over seven million students)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's evidence base is a dataset of "math and reading test scores for over seven million students in kindergarten through 8th grade" collected "across the fall, winter, and spring of the 2016-17 school year." Its three within-year test occasions are what permit testing whether growth trajectories are linear rather than assuming so. The article uses it to evaluate the linearity assumption underlying school-year policy analysis and summer-loss research.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Scores from the same students at fall, winter, and spring occasions within one school year, so within-year growth shape can be estimated

Constraints¶

  • Covers kindergarten through 8th grade in the 2016-17 school year only, as described in the available text

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten through 8th grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate estimation of within-year academic growth in math and reading

  • Seasonal growth patterns were examined with math and reading scores for over seven million K-8 students across three within-year test occasions [+M]
  • A seven-million-student K-8 dataset spanning fall, winter, and spring of 2016-17 was used to test within-year growth linearity [+M]
  • Assuming linear within-year achievement growth is often not justified, particularly in reading [+M]
  • Within-school-year achievement growth is often not linear, particularly in reading [+M]
