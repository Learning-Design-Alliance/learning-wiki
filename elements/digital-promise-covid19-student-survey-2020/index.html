Digital Promise COVID-19 Student Survey (May-June 2020 topline dataset)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A topline survey report of college students whose in-person courses moved completely online during the COVID-19 outbreak. It was "conducted May 13-June 1, 2020, among a random national sample of 1,008 full- or part-time students" including 620 STEM-course students, produced by Langer Research Associates with Ipsos Public Affairs via KnowledgePanel. The instrument covers satisfaction, course practices, challenges, devices and internet access, grading, and future enrollment intentions, with STEM/non-STEM comparisons.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administered to students enrolled in two- or four-year colleges whose in-person courses transitioned completely online

Constraints¶

  • Margin of sampling error of 3.6 points for the full sample, 4.6 for STEM and 5.8 for non-STEM students; error margins are larger for subgroups

Target Learners¶

  • full- and part-time two- and four-year college students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • student experience of emergency remote instruction during COVID-19 campus closures

Claims¶

  • Students attributed online-course problems more to the unplanned move online (45%) than to inherent limitations of online learning (37%) [+W]
  • Undergraduate course satisfaction dropped sharply after courses moved fully online during COVID-19, with very satisfied students falling from 51 percent to 19 percent [+W]
  • STEM students' course satisfaction dropped sharply after courses moved fully online in spring 2020 [+W]
  • Personal instructor messages and reflective assignments showed the largest individual associations with online course satisfaction and motivation [+W]
  • Four or more major challenges were more prevalent among women, Hispanic and minoritized students, and lower-income students [+W]
  • The number of challenges a student experienced was strongly associated with post-COVID course satisfaction: 81 percent satisfaction with no challenges versus 32 percent with four or more [+W]
  • The number of research-recommended online instructional practices used predicted student satisfaction, rising from 43 percent net satisfaction with 0–2 practices to 74 percent with 6–8 [+W]
  • Staying motivated was the most pervasive challenge of learning remotely, and students missed instructor feedback, peer collaboration, and hands-on experiences [+W]
  • Motivation, internet connectivity, and hardware/software problems were widespread challenges in remote STEM courses [+W]
