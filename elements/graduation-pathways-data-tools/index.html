CPS Graduation Pathways data tools: freshman watch list, monthly freshmen success reports, and credit recovery reports¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Office of Graduation Pathways, working with Consortium researchers, developed a set of real-time data tools for identifying and monitoring ninth-graders at risk of going off-track. These included "a “freshman watch list” that flagged incoming ninth-graders at risk of weak attendance and poor academic performance," monthly "freshmen success reports" identifying students needing intervention, and a "credit recovery" report flagging spring-semester students needing to make up core credits. The tools filled a gap, since principals previously could not link cumulative days absent with course outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires school access to real-time data on attendance, grades, and credits, which CPS high schools lacked before the initiative

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the tools alone did not drive improvement; the strength of the Freshman OnTrack indicator lies in the multi-faceted responses it elicits from school staff

Target Learners¶

  • First-time ninth-grade students in urban public high schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Course passing and credit accumulation in ninth grade toward on-time high school graduation

Claims¶

  • Ninth-graders who are on-track are 3.5 times more likely to graduate than those who are off-track [+W]
  • Ninth-grade on-track status predicts graduation more strongly than prior achievement, with a 55 percentage point gap after controls [+W]
  • Three freshman warning indicators—off-track status, any course failure, and attendance below 85 percent—identify 70 percent of eventual non-graduates [+W]
  • Freshman OnTrack remains a strong predictor of high school graduation even as on-track rates have risen [+W]
  • Freshman attendance below 85 percent predicts non-graduation even among students with similar grades [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Provide schools real-time early warning data reports and credit recovery reports to monitor student performance and support on-track progress
  • Monitor freshman-year grades and intervene early, treating D students as needing attention comparable to F students
  • Focus school improvement efforts on the ninth-grade transition using real-time data to monitor and support at-risk freshmen
  • Support freshman success for students with disabilities through attendance-boosting measures and study-habit support tailored to learning and mild cognitive disabilities
