Five Essentials—Early Education surveys: teacher and parent surveys measuring five organizational constructs in pre-k settings¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Five Essentials—Early Education is a single survey tool measuring the five organizational constructs (effective instructional leaders, collaborative teachers, involved families, supportive environments, ambitious instruction) through the perceptions of teachers and parents in publicly-funded programs serving three- and four-year-olds. The teacher survey draws heavily on the K-12 5Essentials teacher survey "in use for over 20 years," while the parent survey was created anew. Questions are combined into measures analyzed with the Rasch IRT model, modeled after the K-12 5Essentials approach.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Designed for publicly-funded center-based programs (school-based or community-based) serving three- and four-year-old children; measures are constructed from survey items analyzed via the Rasch model

Constraints¶

  • Intended for center-based services, not home-based services; the parent survey is intended for any adult caregiver, and the teacher survey is being tested with other pre-k staff

Target Learners¶

  • Three- and four-year-old children in publicly-funded pre-k programs (Head Start, state-, and locally-funded)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring organizational conditions that support instructional quality and kindergarten readiness

Affordances¶

  • Five Essential Supports Early Education Framework

Claims¶

  • Parent focus groups showed parents could not report on specific classroom instructional interactions, so ambitious instruction items were developed for the teacher survey instead, and a new Teacher Care and Responsiveness towards Parents measure was created [+W]
  • The final spring 2015 parent-survey pilot collected 253 parent responses across seven school-based and nine center-based Chicago preschool sites, in English and Spanish [+W]
  • In the spring 2015 pilot, 19 of 27 teacher-survey measures met the 0.80 reliability threshold and all but one met 0.70; the Early Childhood Discipline measure was removed [+W]
  • The Early Education Essentials is reliable across multiple ECE settings [+W]
  • Strongly organized ECE programs create contexts more supportive of teaching, learning, and family engagement than weakly organized programs [+W]
  • Parents offer a unique perspective best captured by a distinct sixth essential, Parent Voice [+W]
  • Elementary schools strong in three or more of the five essential supports were ten times more likely to substantially improve student math and reading scores (attributed to Bryk et al., 2010) [+W]
  • Fewer than 10 percent of elementary schools with a sustained weakness in one essential support showed improvements in student outcomes (attributed to Bryk et al., 2010) [+W]
