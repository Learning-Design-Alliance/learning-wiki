# Gain attention

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Gaining attention is the opening move of instruction: an unexpected event, provocative question, vivid scenario, or perceptual cue that orients learners toward the material before content delivery begins. It functions by activating curiosity and directing working-memory resources to the upcoming task, rather than by transmitting content itself.

## Design Implications

Attention-gaining events increase readiness to learn by creating an information gap or perceptual salience that learners are motivated to resolve [~M]. Their effect is strongest when the hook is *relevant* to the learning goal — salience that points away from the content (decorative animation, irrelevant anecdotes) splits attention and harms learning rather than helping it [Seductive details harm learning more than they help.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M]. Attention capture is a precondition, not a substitute, for [Activation](activation.md) of prior knowledge and sustained engagement with the task itself.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A hook that connects to the learning goal, not merely to novelty ([Real-World Problems](real-world-problems.md), a discrepant event, a question)
- Timing at the start of a lesson or topic segment, before explanation
- A quick bridge from the hook into the task so arousal converts to engagement
- Perceptual salience (contrast, movement, sound) used sparingly and pointed at the content

#### Constraints
- Irrelevant but interesting "seductive details" divert attention from the target content and reduce recall [Seductive details harm learning more than they help.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [-S]
- Novelty effects decay quickly; repeated use of the same hook type loses its effect within a few sessions [~M]
- High-arousal hooks can overload working memory for novices, leaving fewer resources for encoding [~M]
- In self-paced e-learning, attention gains from multimedia cues are modest and depend on the cue marking essential content [~M]

### Target Learners
- All learners benefit from clear orientation, but novices gain most because they cannot yet distinguish central from peripheral content [Seductive details harm learning more than they help.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M]
- Learners with low initial task value benefit when hooks establish relevance and purpose [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Less necessary for highly self-regulated learners pursuing self-set goals

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivation and readiness: increasing willingness to invest effort at the start of instruction
- Attention direction: orienting learners to the features that matter in subsequent content
- Curiosity-driven inquiry: setting up a question the lesson will resolve

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Activation](../principles/cognitive-activation.md) — a well-designed hook poses a question or discrepant event that provokes learners to think before instruction begins, enacting cognitive activation rather than mere arousal
- [Cognitive Disequilibrium](../principles/cognitive-disequilibrium.md) — hooks that present a contradiction or unexpected outcome create the disequilibrium that motivates resolution through learning
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — an attention event that previews the coming task doubles as an advance organizer, signaling what learners should attend to
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — question- and scenario-based hooks pull learners into responding from the first minutes rather than receiving passively

## Examples

**[Jasper Woodbury Problem Solving Series](https://peabody.vanderbilt.edu/research/iris)** — The Vanderbilt anchored-instruction videos open each adventure with a dramatic real-world situation (e.g., planning a rescue trip) that generates the questions students then solve.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Lesson videos frequently open with a concrete question or puzzle ("How much paint do we need?") before formal explanation, converting the topic into a problem to resolve.

**Mystery-driven science openers (e.g., [Ambitious Science Teaching](https://ambitiousscienceteaching.org))** — Units begin with a puzzling phenomenon or discrepant event that students cannot yet explain, establishing the question that drives the unit.

**[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — Uses streaks, characters, and immediate feedback as attention and engagement devices at session start, illustrating gamified attention capture.
