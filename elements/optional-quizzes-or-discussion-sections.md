# Optional Quizzes or Discussion Sections

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Optional quizzes and discussion sections are supplementary, typically ungraded or low-stakes activities that let learners rehearse and consolidate course content outside required assessments. Quizzes enact [retrieval practice](../principles/retrieval-practice.md); discussion sections add social elaboration and instructor feedback. Because they are optional, their effectiveness depends heavily on whether learners actually choose to use them.

## Design Implications

Low-stakes retrieval opportunities strengthen retention more than rereading or passive review, and the effect holds even when the activity is voluntary [Spaced retrieval improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Discussion sections add value when they require learners to articulate and justify ideas rather than merely attend; explaining content to peers produces conceptual gains beyond listening [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. Because participation is voluntary, design must address motivation: making the activities genuinely useful for self-assessment and connecting them to graded outcomes increases uptake [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions or prompts that target the same knowledge and skills as graded assessments, so practice transfers
- Immediate or rapid feedback on quiz responses ([Formative Assessment](formative-assessment.md)); feedback delayed until after a graded exam sharply reduces the formative value
- Discussion prompts that require a position, artifact, or explanation — not just attendance
- A visible link to learner goals: showing quiz performance as progress toward mastery increases voluntary use [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Optional activities suffer severe self-selection: the learners who most need the practice are least likely to attend, widening achievement gaps [-M]
- Optional quizzes taken only once, immediately before exams, lose most of the spacing benefit [Spaced retrieval improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [-S]
- Unstructured "discussion" sections without facilitation or required contributions frequently devolve into passive Q&A with minimal participation [-M]
- If optional activities are perceived as busywork or disconnected from assessment, uptake collapses regardless of quality [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [-M]

### Target Learners
- Self-regulated learners in college courses and professional development, who can budget time for ungraded activities [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Learners preparing for high-stakes assessments who need low-risk rehearsal opportunities
- Less reliably reached: struggling learners with weak self-regulation, who tend to skip optional work — consider small incentives or required low-stakes versions for this group

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention and consolidation of factual and conceptual knowledge [Spaced retrieval improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Self-assessment: calibrating confidence against actual performance
- Elaboration and articulation of understanding through peer explanation [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Retrieval Practice](../principles/retrieval-practice.md) — quizzes are the canonical enactment: each question is a retrieval attempt that strengthens memory more than restudying the same material
- [Formative Assessment](../principles/formative-assessment.md) — low-stakes format makes errors safe and informative, letting both learner and instructor adjust before graded assessment
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — discussion sections convert lecture content into generative activity (arguing, explaining, applying) rather than re-exposure
- [Spaced Practice](../principles/spaced-practice.md) — scheduling optional quizzes across the term, rather than clustering them before exams, converts them into spaced retrieval events

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md) — optional quizzes check pre-class preparation; discussion sections are where in-class application happens
- [Traditional Lecture-Based Instruction](../patterns/traditional-lecture-based-instruction.md) — these activities supplement lectures with the retrieval and interaction lectures lack
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — optional quizzes are a low-stakes instantiation of the formative cycle

## Examples

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Optional practice exercises with immediate feedback and mastery tracking; the progress dashboard makes self-assessment value visible, increasing voluntary use.

**[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — Optional spaced-repetition flashcards; entirely learner-driven, illustrating both the power and the self-selection risk of optional practice.

**[Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)** — Social annotation platform where optional discussion happens anchored to readings, structuring contributions so discussion is generative rather than free-form.

**[Piazza](https://piazza.com)** — Course Q&A forums used for optional discussion sections in large lectures; instructor endorsement of answers provides rapid formative feedback.
