# Phonological awareness training practices: rhyme detection, blending, and segmentation activities

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Phonological awareness training is any practice targeting young children's ability to detect or manipulate the sounds in words independent of meaning. Practices "tend to focus on teaching children to rhyme or to detect alliteration in language" and include rhyme detection training (e.g., identifying which word in a series of three does not sound like the others), blending training (combining three spoken sounds into a word), and segmentation training (separating a short word such as "cat" into its sounds) at the phoneme, syllable, or word level. Training can occur in regular and special education classrooms, individually, in pairs, or in small groups, as core curriculum or a supplement. No single developer exists; the reviewed interventions were developed by the study authors.

### Target Learners
- preschool children with disabilities or developmental delays, including those with developmental delays and speech/language or learning disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- phonological awareness: detecting, deleting, segmenting, and blending segments of spoken words; identifying or producing rhyme and alliteration
