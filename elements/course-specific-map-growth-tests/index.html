Course-specific MAP Growth tests (Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2, Integrated Math 1–3, Biology/Life Science)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 10 claims (10 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A series of course-specific MAP Growth Mathematics and Science subject tests released starting in August 2017 to replace the older NWEA End-of-Course tests. Unlike the prior EOC tests taken only at the end of a course, these tests "can be administered multiple times throughout the school year, typically in the fall, winter, and spring, allowing for student growth to be evaluated in a content area over the duration of a course." They are item-level variable-length computerized adaptive tests of 41 to 43 items sharing the same RIT scales as regular MAP Growth math and science tests, though scores should not be used interchangeably across domains.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administration at multiple terms within a school year to support growth evaluation

Constraints¶

  • A score on a course-specific mathematics test should not be used interchangeably with the same score on MAP Growth Mathematics because they test different subject domains

Target Learners¶

  • U.S. secondary students in Grades 9–11 in the traditional or integrated mathematics pathways, and Grade 9 biology students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring course-specific achievement and growth in secondary mathematics and biology content

Claims¶

  • Average course-specific test scores decrease as grade level increases, reflecting ability self-selection [+W]
  • Normality assumptions of the true score model appeared reasonable for the course-specific tests [+W]
  • Updated norms are heavily weighted toward 2021/22 pandemic-era performance, with lower norm-group performance than the prior norms [+W]
  • Course-specific test volumes dropped sharply in spring 2020 and hit record highs in 2021/22 [+W]
  • True-score-model correlations between term scores exceed 0.90 for almost all course-specific tests [+W]
  • The course-specific MAP Growth norms were built from 747,936 test events administered to 342,821 students in Grades 6–12 across 50 states between Fall 2017 and Spring 2019. [+W]
  • The published norms allow normative interpretation of both status and fall-to-spring growth, e.g., an Algebra 1 student at the 55th fall percentile has an expected gain of 8 RIT with growth SD of 9, and an 11-RIT gain places the student at the 62nd growth percentile. [+W]
  • True-score-model correlations among fall, winter, and spring scores exceed 0.90 for almost all course-specific tests, indicating strongly correlated administrations across terms. [+W]
  • Q-Q plots, CDF curves, and residual checks support the joint normality assumption underlying the multivariate true score model for the course-specific tests. [+W]
  • Cross-grade score patterns show self-selection: lower-grade students taking a course-specific test tend to outperform students at the grade the course targets, and upper-grade students tend to underperform them. [+W]
