# Continuous Review

## Description
Continuous review is the systematic revisiting of previously taught material at expanding intervals throughout a course, rather than treating each topic as "covered" once and moving on. It functions as a structural commitment: earlier content is deliberately woven into later tasks, assessments, and warm-ups so that retention is maintained by design rather than left to chance.

## Design Implications

Continuous review counters the steep forgetting curve by re-exposing learners to material just as it begins to decay, with each successive retrieval strengthening the memory trace [Spaced retrieval produces durable retention compared with massed study.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S]. Its power comes from combining spacing with retrieval: review sessions should require learners to *reconstruct* knowledge, not merely re-read it [Retrieval practice produces stronger long-term retention than restudying.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Review is most efficient when it is brief, distributed, and cumulative — interleaving old and new content rather than blocking it.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A cumulative curriculum map identifying which prior concepts each new topic depends on
- Low-stakes review mechanisms: warm-up questions, cumulative quizzes, interleaved problem sets ([Retrieval Practice](retrieval-practice.md))
- Scheduling that spaces reviews at increasing intervals ([Spaced Repetition](spaced-repetition.md))
- Feedback so that errors surfaced during review are corrected, not rehearsed

#### Constraints
- Reviewing material learners have just mastered wastes time and can inflate confidence without adding retention [~M] — spacing must be long enough to require effortful retrieval
- Re-exposure without retrieval (rereading notes, re-watching lectures) produces weak gains and illusions of knowing [Retrieval practice produces stronger long-term retention than restudying.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [-S]
- Poorly designed cumulative review can overload working memory when too many topics are interleaved at once [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [~M]
- In non-cumulative or project-based subjects, forced review of isolated facts adds little value

### Target Learners
- Learners in cumulative domains — mathematics, science, languages — where later content presupposes fluent recall of earlier content
- Novices, who underestimate forgetting and overestimate the durability of a single study episode [~M]
- Less critical for experts, whose well-consolidated schemas require far less scheduled review

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of foundational facts, procedures, and vocabulary
- Fluency and automaticity in prerequisite skills
- Transfer: cumulative review that mixes problem types supports discrimination between them [~S]

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — distributing review over time keeps each session's demand low; small, spaced retrievals avoid the overload of massed cramming
- Spaced Learning — continuous review is the operational mechanism of spacing: it converts the spacing effect from a research finding into a course-level schedule
- Memory Consolidation — each spaced retrieval reactivates and stabilizes memory traces during consolidation, which is why distributed review outperforms equivalent massed study time [+S]
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — effective review is retrieval, not re-exposure; it enacts active learning by making learners reconstruct knowledge from memory

## Patterns That Use This Element
- Mastery Learning — repeated, criterion-referenced revisiting until fluency is reached
- Competency-Based Learning — ongoing review ensures previously demonstrated competencies remain current
- Spaced Learning — continuous review is the element that implements the spacing schedule across sessions

## Examples

**[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — Spaced repetition software implementing expanding-interval review with retrieval-based flashcards; widely used in medical education for high-volume cumulative material.

**[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.org)** — Language learning app that interleaves review of previously learned vocabulary and grammar into new lessons using a spaced-repetition model.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Mastery-based math practice that resurfaces earlier skills in "mastery challenges," mixing review items with new content.

**Cumulative quizzing (Roediger & Karpicke's classroom studies)** — Low-stakes quizzes that include items from all prior units produce substantially better end-of-course retention than quizzes covering only recent material [+S].
