# Rubric

## Description
A rubric is an explicit scoring guide that breaks a complex task into criteria and describes what performance looks like at each quality level. It functions simultaneously as an assessment tool for evaluators and a transparency tool for learners, converting tacit quality standards into visible, actionable expectations.

## Design Implications

Rubrics improve learning primarily by making quality criteria available *before* the task, so learners can plan, monitor, and evaluate their own work rather than guessing at standards [Rubrics support learning when used for self-assessment and feedback, not just grading.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]. Analytic rubrics (separate criteria with level descriptors) outperform holistic ones for instructional purposes because they localize where work falls short. Rubrics shared in advance reduce evaluation anxiety and perceived arbitrariness of grading [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Criteria that map onto the actual dimensions of quality in the task, not surface features
- Descriptors that describe observable performance differences between levels, not just quantity ("three sources" vs. "relevant, well-integrated sources")
- Distribution before the task begins, plus an activity that requires learners to apply it ([self-assessment](assessment.md), peer review, or revision)
- Language at learners' reading level; jargon-laden criteria are functionally invisible to novices

#### Constraints
- Rubrics used only post-hoc for grading have little instructional effect; the benefit comes from use during production [Rubrics support learning when used for self-assessment and feedback, not just grading.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [~M]
- Simple checklists of surface criteria can substitute for genuine quality judgment; learners optimize for the checklist rather than the underlying quality [Checklist-style evaluation tools show weak effects in online contexts.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [-W]
- Overly detailed rubrics (10+ criteria) fragment attention and encourage point-chasing over holistic quality judgment [-M]
- Less useful for highly creative or emergent work where quality dimensions cannot be specified in advance [~W]

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack internalized standards for what "good" looks like in a domain [+M]
- Developing self-regulated learners; rubrics give them the evaluation criteria experts apply automatically [+M]
- Less necessary for experts, who already hold internal criteria and may find detailed rubrics constraining [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Complex performance tasks: writing, projects, presentations, portfolios
- Self-regulation: planning against criteria and self-evaluating drafts
- Calibration: aligning learners' judgments of their own work with expert judgment
