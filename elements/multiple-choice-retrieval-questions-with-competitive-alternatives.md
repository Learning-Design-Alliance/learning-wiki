# Multiple-choice retrieval practice questions with competitive alternatives

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (1 for, 4 mixed, 1 against, 1 unmarked) · 8 studies (3 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q4` · 4 of 8 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A recognition-format retrieval practice item: a multiple-choice question whose incorrect options are competitive alternatives, intended to make recognition practice demand retrieval comparable to short-answer production. The study built its MCQs this way, "by including competitive alternatives in the MCQs to ensure that the MCQs cannot be attributed to being relatively easier than short-answer questions", scoring one point per correct response.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Incorrect options are competitive alternatives rather than easily dismissed distractors, following the method the article adopts from Little et al. (2012).
- Items were validated by the course instructor to match the intended level of thinking (lower-order vs higher-order).
#### Constraints
- The authors' claim that the MCQs were almost as difficult as the production questions is offered as a plausible explanation for the null format difference, not as a measured result.
- Evaluated with a single 980-word passage on Problem Solving in one course.

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduates enrolled in a Cognitive Psychology course with MUET Band 3 or above English proficiency

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension of a studied course passage, at lower-order and higher-order levels of thinking
