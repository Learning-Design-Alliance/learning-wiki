# Five Essentials—Early Education surveys: teacher and parent surveys measuring five organizational constructs in pre-k settings

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Five Essentials—Early Education is a single survey tool measuring the five organizational constructs (effective instructional leaders, collaborative teachers, involved families, supportive environments, ambitious instruction) through the perceptions of teachers and parents in publicly-funded programs serving three- and four-year-olds. The teacher survey draws heavily on the K-12 5Essentials teacher survey "in use for over 20 years," while the parent survey was created anew. Questions are combined into measures analyzed with the Rasch IRT model, modeled after the K-12 5Essentials approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designed for publicly-funded center-based programs (school-based or community-based) serving three- and four-year-old children; measures are constructed from survey items analyzed via the Rasch model
#### Constraints
- Intended for center-based services, not home-based services; the parent survey is intended for any adult caregiver, and the teacher survey is being tested with other pre-k staff

### Target Learners
- Three- and four-year-old children in publicly-funded pre-k programs (Head Start, state-, and locally-funded)

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring organizational conditions that support instructional quality and kindergarten readiness
