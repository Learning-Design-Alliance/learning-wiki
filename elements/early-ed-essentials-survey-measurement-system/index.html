Early Ed Essentials teacher and parent survey measurement system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Early Education Essential Organizational Supports measurement system (Early Ed Essentials) is a set of teacher and parent surveys developed by the Ounce of Prevention Fund and the UChicago Consortium "to measure the organizational supports of school and center-based ECE settings." Its purpose is to give programs reliable, valid data to diagnose strengths and weaknesses in organizational conditions so staff can strengthen the conditions that enable effective teaching. The original teacher survey included 26 measures and 164 items; the original parent survey included 9 measures and 54 items.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs must have at least three pre-k classrooms and at least six teachers so site-level scores are precise and respondent anonymity is protected
  • A minimum of six educator or parent responses per site to protect anonymity and reduce imprecision of site-level measure scores

Constraints¶

  • Designed for part- or full-day, publicly funded early education programs serving preschool children; child care and preschool programs without public early education funding were excluded

Target Learners¶

  • teachers and teaching assistants in pre-k programs
  • parents of preschool-age children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • diagnosing organizational strengths and weaknesses to design improvement strategies that support effective teaching and children's learning

Affordances¶

  • Five Essentials Framework Early Education Adaptation

Claims¶

  • Rasch-based refinement reduced the Early Ed Essentials surveys to 24 teacher measures (122 items) and 9 parent measures (42 items) [+W]
  • Site-level essential scores were created by averaging standardized measure scores grouped into essentials, informed by exploratory factor analysis [+W]
  • The validation study sample comprised 81 Chicago ECE sites with high teacher and parent survey response rates [+W]
  • Preschool classrooms in the validation sample scored lowest on Instructional Support relative to Emotional Support and Classroom Organization [+W]
  • Site-level pre-k attendance in the validation sample averaged 85.2 percent and varied widely across sites [+W]
  • Parent focus groups showed parents could not report on specific classroom instructional interactions, so ambitious instruction items were developed for the teacher survey instead, and a new Teacher Care and Responsiveness towards Parents measure was created [+W]
  • In the spring 2015 pilot, 19 of 27 teacher-survey measures met the 0.80 reliability threshold and all but one met 0.70; the Early Childhood Discipline measure was removed [+W]
  • The final spring 2015 parent-survey pilot collected 253 parent responses across seven school-based and nine center-based Chicago preschool sites, in English and Spanish [+W]
  • Parents offer a unique perspective best captured by a distinct sixth essential, Parent Voice [+W]
