Inquiry-Based Learning¶

Element · All elements
Evidence · 20 claims (13 for, 4 mixed, 3 against) · 23 studies (8 causal, 7 review, 4 quant-synthesis, 2 design, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 5 of 23 report an effect size · 16 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Inquiry-based learning is the element in which learners investigate questions, evidence, or problems in order to build understanding through guided exploration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A meaningful question or problem
  • Guidance for investigation and evidence use
  • A well-defined question or problem that is genuinely investigable with available materials and time
  • Embedded scaffolds: hypothesis templates, data-organization tools, prompts that direct attention to critical features
  • Prior knowledge activation before exploration begins (Activation)
  • Structured opportunities to articulate and revise explanations (Reflection, Argumentation)
  • Instructor monitoring to catch and correct misconceptions early, before they consolidate

Constraints¶

  • Open inquiry can drift without structure
  • Unguided or minimally guided inquiry is less effective than explicit instruction for novices, who lack schemas to evaluate what they discover Minimally guided discovery fails for novice learners. [-S]
  • Learners can construct incorrect or incomplete explanations and fail to notice; without verification against expert content, misconceptions persist [-S]
  • Time-intensive; covers less content per hour than direct instruction, a real constraint in curriculum-dense settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Build investigation, explanation, and evidence-based reasoning.
  • Conceptual understanding and conceptual change, especially in science where learners confront their predictions against evidence
  • Scientific practices: hypothesis formation, data interpretation, evidence-based argumentation
  • Epistemic understanding: how knowledge in a discipline is generated and justified

Affordances¶

  • Inquiry-Based Learning
  • Active Learning
  • Scaffolding — guided inquiry is essentially scaffolded exploration; the design decision is which scaffolds to embed and when to fade them as learners take over the investigative process
  • Cognitive Load Management — structuring the question, constraining variables, and providing data tools reduces the search burden that makes unguided discovery fail
  • Cognitive Apprenticeship — the investigation-explanation cycle mirrors expert practice in the discipline, especially when paired with Coaching and Articulation

Claims¶

  • Inquiry Based Teaching Improves Science Achievement [+M]
  • Teacher Guided Inquiry Outperforms Student Led [+M]
  • Mentored inquiry with staged mechanistic sensemaking supports the transition from intuitive to formal equation reasoning [+W]
  • Unassisted discovery produces less learning than explicit instruction, while discovery enhanced with guidance outperforms other instruction [+M]
  • Scripted personally meaningful inquiry is associated with gains in inquiry-decision accuracy, with mixed evidence on attitude and behavior change [+W]
  • Hands-on, inquiry-based science and math instruction with higher-order thinking was associated with higher English reading achievement on the Stanford Achievement Test in the AMSTI evaluation [+W]
  • Unguided or minimally guided instructional approaches are popular and intuitively appealing but ignore human cognitive architecture and the empirical evidence [-W]
  • Female low achievers from the general track started with the lowest knowledge scores but achieved the highest learning gains in Web-based collaborative inquiry [+W]
  • The PISG and PSGI script sequences showed no statistically significant differences on high- and low-level inquiry processes, with small effect sizes despite descriptive trends favoring PISG on high-level processes [~W]
  • TAIA students perceived more teacher support for hypothesis generation and argumentation and enjoyed inquiry more; perceived experiment-related support did not differ [+W]
  • The self-explanation-based environment elicits the most (self-)explanations and the inquiry environment elicits high hypothesis generation [~W]
  • All student groups benefited equally in inquiry skills improvement after Web-based collaborative inquiry, with no significant effects of gender, achievement level, or academic track [+W]
  • Self-explanation-based and inquiry learning environments elicit significantly more total learning-process instances than hypermedia and observational learning environments [~W]
  • Students in computer-based inquiry environments still held misconceptions about force and energy after instruction [-W]
  • Web-based collaborative inquiry produces a small but significant improvement in students' interest in science [+W]
  • Guidance increases the effectiveness of inquiry-based learning, and all six guidance types were equally effective [+W]
  • Open inquiry enhances students' science literacy and related skills such as environmental literacy, argumentation, autonomy and reflection [+W]
  • Video modeling conditions show stronger co-occurrence of self-regulation with scientific reasoning processes than unguided inquiry (ENA) [~W]

The evidence by question¶

  • Does inquiry-based teaching raise science achievement over the usual instruction? Inquiry Based Teaching Improves Science Achievement [+S]
  • Does adding guidance to inquiry improve what learners learn, and does the kind of guidance matter? Guidance during inquiry learning improves post-inquiry learning outcomes (d = 0.50) [+M]
  • Should novices be left to discover the target content without support? Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction [+S]

Examples¶

  • Use AI-based analysis of student log files to scaffold inquiry competencies and guide teachers in real time

Related Elements¶

  • Guided Inquiry
  • Problem-Based Learning
  • Scaffolding — the guidance structure that separates effective guided inquiry from ineffective discovery
  • Coaching — instructor moves during investigation that redirect unproductive exploration
  • Case-Based Learning — inquiry anchored in a rich case rather than a laboratory task
  • Reflection — consolidates what the investigation established
  • Demonstration — a complementary element; modeling before inquiry reduces unproductive search
