# Four-level (0-3) Historical Thinking Skills rubrics for scoring teacher activities and student work

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Digital Promise developed two sets of rubrics: one to evaluate the potential of teacher-assigned activities to provide opportunities to learn historical thinking skills, and one to assess the extent to which students used those skills in the resulting work. Each rubric scores six dimensions on four levels: "0 indicated the absence of a historical thinkingpractice/skill in the activity/student work, 1 indicated emergence, 2 indicated presence, and 3indicated rigorous presence." The highest level was drafted from national frameworks' age-appropriate expectations for secondary students, and the lowest applies when a skill is not called for or not employed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scorer observation of a teacher activity or student work to make an inference that the assessment target was met
#### Constraints
- Reading and writing literacy skills are not included as distinct rubric categories; the interdependence of historical thinking concepts can make independent claims about individual skills difficult

### Target Learners
- U.S. high school world history students

### Target Learning Goals
- Historical argumentation, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing
