5Essentials Survey¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The 5Essentials Survey is a school climate survey completed by students and teachers about their school and classroom experiences, whose "scores offer a useful summary of students' experiences school-wide." The brief reports its scores and score growth predict student success measures including GPA, attendance, test scores, and college enrollment, and that only 50% of a school's score is explained by the prior year's score. It is positioned as a data source schools can use for continuous improvement, alongside Cultivate survey data on classroom experiences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administration to students and teachers so that scores and growth in measure scores can be computed and tracked year to year

Constraints¶

  • School-level averages may not represent all students; the brief notes individuals within student groups can have widely differing experiences

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students and their teachers in elementary and high schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Diagnosing and improving school culture and climate to support attendance, learning, and well-being

Claims¶

  • Only 50% of a school's 5Essentials score is explained by the previous year's score, indicating substantial year-to-year malleability of school climate [+M]
  • Strong scores and growth on the 5Essentials Survey predict student success measures including GPA, attendance, test scores, and college enrollment [+M]
  • When Supportive Environment improves, the other 5Essentials essential supports often improve [+W]

Examples¶

  • Dig into item-level survey responses: examine how students and teachers answer specific questions as a starting point for learning and responding
  • Select one essential support area to improve—chosen for community interest, need, or buy-in—and continue working on it, since improvement in any essential positively impacts student success
  • Use a targeted universalist approach to identify student groups with less positive survey reports and respond with empathy, curiosity, and support
  • Use 5Essentials survey data to identify where to improve students' school experiences and teachers' relationships with parents to support attendance
  • Use student and teacher voice as reliable, valid inputs in school improvement efforts
