# Hypothesis Testing

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Hypothesis testing asks learners to commit to an explicit, testable prediction before encountering evidence, then to evaluate that prediction against the outcome. The commitment step is critical: a stated hypothesis makes the learner's prior conception inspectable, so subsequent evidence is interpreted as confirmation or disconfirmation of something specific rather than absorbed passively.

## Design Implications

Committing to a prediction before instruction activates prior conceptions and creates the cognitive conflict that drives conceptual change when evidence contradicts the prediction [Predictions that expose misconceptions create cognitive disequilibrium that motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M]. Hypothesis testing also structures inquiry: it converts open exploration into a focused question, directs attention to the variables that matter, and gives learners a criterion for judging what counts as relevant evidence [Active learning approaches that require learners to generate and evaluate ideas outperform passive reception.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. The hypothesis should be recorded and revisited after the evidence phase — the learning payoff comes from the comparison, not the prediction alone.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task or phenomenon with a genuinely uncertain outcome — if the answer is obvious, the prediction is empty
- A mechanism for learners to commit publicly or in writing to a specific prediction ([Predictions](prediction.md), written conjectures, or clicker votes)
- Evidence that is timely, unambiguous, and directly relevant to the prediction ([Experimentation](experimentation.md), data collection, or a [Case Study](case-study.md))
- A structured comparison step in which learners reconcile their hypothesis with the outcome ([Feedback](feedback.md) on the reasoning, not just the answer)

#### Constraints
- Confirmation bias: learners seek and weight evidence that supports their hypothesis and explain away disconfirming evidence [~M] — tasks must be designed so disconfirming evidence is unavoidable, and learners should be prompted to consider alternative hypotheses
- Testing only one's own hypothesis without generating alternatives produces weak discrimination learning; comparing contrasting cases improves what learners extract from the evidence [Comparing and contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Unguided hypothesis generation with minimal domain knowledge can leave learners testing superficial or irrelevant variables [~M] — novices need constraints on the hypothesis space or a worked example of a good hypothesis first
- If evidence arrives long after the prediction, the comparison loses its impact; delay degrades the discrepancy signal

### Target Learners
- Learners holding common misconceptions that the evidence will directly contradict — the prediction makes the misconception explicit and vulnerable
- Intermediate learners with enough domain knowledge to generate a plausible hypothesis; complete novices may need scaffolded hypothesis choices rather than open generation
- Less effective for learners with strong expertise, for whom prediction adds little beyond routine activation

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual change: replacing intuitive but incorrect models with evidence-consistent ones
- Scientific reasoning: understanding what makes a hypothesis testable and what evidence would count for or against it
- Inquiry skills: designing comparisons and interpreting data relative to a claim

### Affordances
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — hypothesis testing enacts this principle by requiring a generative act (committing to a prediction) before any exposition, rather than reception of a conclusion
- [Cognitive Activation](../principles/cognitive-activation.md) — the prediction step forces learners to reason from their current model and articulate a mechanism, which is precisely the cognitive engagement this principle targets
- [Cognitive Disequilibrium](../principles/cognitive-disequilibrium.md) — disconfirmed hypotheses create a targeted knowledge gap that motivates resolution, making the subsequent explanation more deeply processed
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — hypothesis prompts can be faded from structured templates (predict, observe, explain) toward independent question generation as learners develop inquiry competence

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [5E Learning Cycle](../patterns/5e-learning-cycle.md) — the Engage and Explore phases center on prediction and evidence
- [Experiential Learning Cycle](../patterns/experiential-learning-cycle.md) — hypotheses formed before experience are tested during it and revised in reflection
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — learners state diagnostic hypotheses early and revise them as case details emerge

## Examples

**[Peer Instruction](https://www.peerinstruction.net)** (Crouch & Mazur, Harvard) — Students commit to an answer via clicker, discuss with a peer holding a different answer, then re-vote. The initial commitment functions as a hypothesis test against peer argument and instructor explanation.

**Predict-Observe-Explain (POE)** — A published science-probe format (White & Gunstone, 1992) in which learners predict an outcome of a demonstration, watch it, and explain any discrepancy between prediction and observation.

**[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** — Learners can state a prediction about circuit behavior or gas pressure, then manipulate the simulation to test it, with immediate, unambiguous evidence.

**Problem-Based Learning tutorials** — Students generate hypotheses about the underlying mechanism of a presenting problem before self-directed study, then reconvene to test those hypotheses against new information.
