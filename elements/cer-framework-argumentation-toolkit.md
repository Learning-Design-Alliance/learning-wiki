# Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) framework and The Argumentation Toolkit

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The CER framework (McNeill & Krajcik, 2011) has students "formulate a claim, provide evidence, and offer reasoning for how the evidence logically connects to their claim", as instantiated in The Argumentation Toolkit resource. The primer notes CER is one of the most common classroom argumentation frameworks but has been critiqued as too simplistic; researchers report reducing fuller Toulmin-style structures to these three essential elements is just as useful and more manageable for representing the core of an argument.

## Design Implications

#### Constraints

### Target Learners
- Grade 5-8 and middle school science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Constructing scientific arguments
- Connecting evidence logically to claims
