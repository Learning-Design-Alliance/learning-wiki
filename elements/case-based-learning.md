# Case-Based Learning

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Case-based learning places learners in the role of analyst or decision-maker confronting a realistic scenario — a patient presentation, a business dilemma, a legal dispute — and asks them to apply theoretical concepts to interpret it and choose a course of action. The case functions as an authentic problem context in which abstract principles must be retrieved, adapted, and defended rather than merely recalled.

## Design Implications

Case-based learning improves exam performance and supports transfer by forcing learners to apply concepts in context rather than rehearse them in the abstract [Case-based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Its effectiveness depends on the case being genuinely ill-structured enough to require reasoning, and on discussion or feedback that surfaces and challenges learners' analyses rather than confirming them. Well-designed cases create productive [Cognitive Disequilibrium](cognitive-conflict.md): learners encounter gaps between their intuitions and what the case demands, motivating conceptual change [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic, realistic cases with sufficient detail and ambiguity to require judgment, not just recall
- Clear alignment between the concepts to be learned and the features of the case
- Structured analysis prompts or facilitation ([Class Discussion](class-discussion.md), [Socratic questioning](socratic-questioning.md)) that pushes learners beyond surface features
- A debrief or expert comparison so learners can evaluate their reasoning against expert analysis

#### Constraints
- Cases with too little scaffolding overwhelm novices, who lack the schemas to distinguish relevant from irrelevant detail [Expertise reversal: guidance that helps novices hinders experts.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M] — novices need more structure, experts more open-ended cases
- Poorly matched cases (surface realism without conceptual relevance) lead learners to fixate on irrelevant details
- Time-intensive: deep analysis of few cases trades breadth of coverage for depth [~W]
- Group discussion without individual accountability can allow free-riding [Cooperative learning requires group rewards with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-M]

### Target Learners
- Advanced novices and intermediate learners who have foundational concepts and need to learn when and how to apply them [Case-based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Professional students in business, medicine, law, and social sciences, where judgment under uncertainty is the target competence
- Complete novices benefit less without prior instruction or heavy scaffolding [Expertise reversal: guidance that helps novices hinders experts.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Application: bridging theory and practice in meaningful ways
- Decision-making and diagnostic reasoning under uncertainty
- Transfer: recognizing when abstract principles apply across varied surface contexts [Cognitive flexibility theory supports learning from multiple cases.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+W]

### Affordances
- [Situated Learning](../principles/situated-learning.md) — cases embed knowledge in the authentic contexts and practices of a discipline, so learning is tied to the situations of use rather than to decontextualized abstractions
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — case analysis followed by expert debrief makes disciplinary thinking visible; learners practice articulation and reasoning that experts model during debrief
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — learners must generate analyses, decisions, and justifications rather than receive explanations, producing the retrieval and elaboration that drive retention
- [Cognitive Flexibility](../principles/cognitive-flexibility.md) — multiple cases illustrating the same concept from different angles prepare learners to apply it flexibly across varied situations

## Examples

**[Harvard Business School case method](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/the-case-method/Pages/default.aspx)** — Students prepare a real business dilemma, then defend positions in discussion-facilitated classes; the instructor withholds the "answer" to keep analysis central.

**[National Center for Case Study Teaching in Science](https://www.nsta.org/national-center-case-study-teaching-science)** — Published peer-reviewed science cases (e.g., on genetics, epidemiology) with teaching notes, used in undergraduate STEM courses.

**[Problem-based medical curricula](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/education/why-um/problem-based-learning)** (e.g., Maastricht University) — Patient cases drive small-group inquiry, integrating biomedical knowledge with clinical reasoning.
