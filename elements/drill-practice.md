# Drill & Practice

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drill and practice is the element in which learners repeatedly perform a focused skill or response pattern until it becomes more fluent or automatic. It is useful when subskills need rapid, accurate execution to support later complex performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A specific subskill that benefits from automation**
- **Many opportunities for practice with feedback**
- Activities beyond narrowly focused practice should be introduced as soon as possible for cognitive and affective reasons
#### Constraints
- **Drill alone does not develop flexible transfer or conceptual understanding**
- Content-based and task-based contexts are doubtful to create in early stages of foreign language learning when learners' linguistic tools are limited

### Target Learning Goals
- Improve fluency, accuracy, and automaticity on foundational subskills.

## Description
The article presents systematic practice as "a house with many rooms": a spectrum far beyond mechanical drills. It cites Paulston and Bruder's distinction between "mechanical, meaningful, and communicative drills", notes communicative drills do make learners link form with meaning, and places role plays and information-gap tasks that make target forms essential or highly likely in the middle, with content-based and task-based teaching at the far end. The conclusion names the range "from initial communicative drills to role-playing activities, task-based learning, and content-based teaching".
