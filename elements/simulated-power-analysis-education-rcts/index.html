Simulated power analysis of typical education RCTs using the developed power formulas¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article applies its developed statistical power formulas in a simulated power analysis of clustered school-based RCTs of education interventions. The simulation examines whether typical RCTs have sufficient statistical power for linking impacts on teacher practice and student achievement outcomes, using "ordinary least squares and instrumental variable estimators." The simulation is the empirical demonstration of the formulas' implications for study design.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Power formulas for clustered RCTs with OLS and IV estimators

Constraints¶

  • Results pertain to typical education RCTs as simulated, not to any specific completed trial

Target Learners¶

  • Education researchers planning RCTs of education interventions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Determining statistical power for mediator-outcome linkage analyses in RCTs

Affordances¶

  • Power Formulas Mediator Linking Clustered Rcts

Claims¶

  • In clustered school-based RCTs, OLS mediator analyses yield precise teacher practice-achievement estimates only with about 150 to 200 study schools [+W]
  • The IV approach to mediator analysis in clustered education RCTs has very little statistical power [+W]
  • Typical clustered RCTs with 40 to 60 schools are often insufficient for binary outcomes [+W]
  • Sample size formulas for clustered designs with school- or teacher-level random assignment are derived using generalized estimating equation methods [+W]
