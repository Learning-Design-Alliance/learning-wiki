Teacher Incentive Fund (TIF) educator evaluation and compensation strategies¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (3 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This element comprises the two educator-effectiveness strategies examined in the brief's underlying IES studies: performance feedback, in which "research-based performance measures" are used "to evaluate educators and provide them with feedback," and pay-for-performance, which offered "bonuses to educators based on their performance ratings." The brief evaluates each strategy's effects on student achievement and educator satisfaction over multi-year implementations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Performance feedback requires research-based performance measures for evaluating educators
  • Pay-for-performance requires performance ratings on which bonuses are based

Constraints¶

  • The brief notes no single large-scale study evaluated the effects of a full, comprehensive program like those supported by TIF and TSL

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students taught by educators participating in TIF-supported programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving student math and reading achievement through educator evaluation and incentive strategies

Claims¶

  • Educators were largely receptive to evaluation and compensation strategies, with 65 to 84 percent satisfied with feedback and pay-for-performance improving some job satisfaction [+W]
  • Implementation of the pay-for-performance policy differed from the envisioned approach, with about 40 percent of teachers unaware they were eligible for a performance bonus [~W]
  • Providing educators with performance feedback and offering pay-for-performance bonuses can improve student achievement, with positive cumulative impacts on math or reading in some years [+W]
  • Many teachers misunderstood whether they were eligible for performance bonuses or the amount they could earn [~W]
  • Districts implemented most components of the performance-based compensation system throughout all four years, but fewer than half planned to keep offering performance bonuses once the grant ended [~W]
  • Few districts structured pay-for-performance bonuses to align well with TIF grant guidance [-W]
  • The average TIF bonus was about 4% of average teacher salary, below the 5% recommended by TIF grant guidance for substantial bonuses [~W]
  • After three years of Teacher Incentive Fund implementation, pay-for-performance bonuses had small positive impacts on student math and reading achievement in ten evaluation districts [+W]
