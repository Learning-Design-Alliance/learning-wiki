Chicago principal merit award program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A merit award program in which employers (the district) provide awards to school principals to induce greater effort. The program used an award threshold, allowing regression discontinuity comparison of principals who just exceeded versus fell just short of it. The article reports that "the award program incentives increased achievement," while award winners exited their schools at elevated rates the following year.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An effectiveness threshold determining award receipt

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated in the Chicago principal context using regression discontinuity and difference-in-differences methods

Target Learners¶

  • school principals

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inducing greater effort and productivity among school leaders

Claims¶

  • The merit award program's incentives increased student achievement in Chicago schools [+W]
  • Principals who just exceeded a merit award threshold are over twice as likely to exit their school the next year than principals who fell just short [~W]
  • Merit award winners were nearly three times as likely to leave their school the following year (16.9% vs 5.9%), mostly leaving the district [-W]
  • The merit award program produced modest achievement improvements concentrated in schools close to the award threshold [+W]
