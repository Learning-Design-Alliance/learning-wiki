# Research

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Research as an instructional element places learners in the role of investigator: they pose or refine questions, gather and evaluate evidence, and construct defensible conclusions. It functions as an extended, learner-driven activity that develops both domain knowledge and the transferable practices of inquiry — searching, evaluating sources, analyzing data, and communicating findings.

## Design Implications

Research tasks build analytical and information literacy skills by requiring learners to locate, evaluate, and synthesize evidence rather than receive it [Inquiry-based approaches with scaffolding outperform pure discovery learning.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]. Effectiveness depends on structure: guided research with clear questions, checkpoints, and feedback reliably improves achievement, while minimally guided "pure discovery" does not [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than guided instruction.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [~S]. Requiring learners to explain and justify their findings deepens conceptual understanding beyond simply producing an answer [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-scoped question or problem that is genuinely open but tractable with available resources
- Scaffolds for the research process: source evaluation criteria, data collection templates, milestone checkpoints ([Scaffolding](scaffolding.md), [Coaching](coaching.md))
- Structured opportunities to articulate and defend conclusions ([Articulation](articulation.md), [Argumentation](argumentation.md))
- Feedback loops where instructors or peers critique evidence quality and reasoning

#### Constraints
- Unguided research tasks overload novices, who lack the schemas to evaluate sources or structure an investigation [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than guided instruction.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [-S] — Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark's critique applies directly to open-ended research assignments
- Weak prior knowledge of a domain makes it difficult to judge source credibility or recognize relevant evidence; research tasks work best after foundational instruction, not as a substitute for it
- Long-duration projects without checkpoints encourage procrastination and shallow, last-minute synthesis; interim deadlines and [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) are needed
- Assessment focused only on the final product invites plagiarism and surface compliance; process artifacts (search logs, annotated bibliographies, drafts) must be assessed

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and graduate students in STEM, humanities, and professional programs where evidence-based reasoning is a disciplinary norm
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge, who can leverage foundations to evaluate new evidence; complete novices need substantial guidance first [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than guided instruction.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [~M]
- Advanced learners preparing for authentic disciplinary practice, where research is the core activity

### Target Learning Goals
- Information literacy: locating, evaluating, and synthesizing sources
- Analytical reasoning: drawing warranted conclusions from evidence
- Argumentation: constructing and defending claims with evidence
- Epistemic practice: understanding how knowledge is produced within a discipline

### Affordances
- [Inquiry-Based Learning](../principles/inquiry-based-learning.md) — research is the fullest enactment of inquiry: learners generate questions, gather evidence, and form conclusions rather than following a scripted investigation
- [Constructivism](../principles/constructivism.md) — learners actively construct knowledge from evidence they have gathered themselves, producing more durable and better-organized schemas than transmission alone
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — research tasks position learners in authentic disciplinary practice, with instructor coaching and articulation mirroring the apprenticeship cycle
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — framing research for a real audience (a report, poster, or proposal) raises task value and quality of effort

## Examples

**[C.R.E.A.T.E. Method](https://www.create4stem.info)** — A published pedagogy (Hoskins et al.) in which learners analyze primary research articles module-by-module — modeling the authors' methods, redesigning figures, proposing next experiments — making the research process itself the curriculum.

**[CUREs (Course-Based Undergraduate Research Experiences)](https://curenet.org)** — National framework embedding genuine research projects (e.g., the HHMI SEA-Phages program) into undergraduate courses, with students collecting and analyzing novel data.

**[IB Extended Essay](https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/dp-core/extended-essay/)** — A 4,000-word independent research project with supervisor checkpoints, exemplifying milestone-scaffolded research for pre-university learners.
