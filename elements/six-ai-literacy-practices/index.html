Six AI Literacy Practices for understanding and evaluating AI¶

Element · All elements Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The framework enumerates six actionable practices: Algorithmic Thinking, Abstraction & Decomposition; Data Analysis & Inference; Data Privacy & Security; Digital Communication & Expression; Ethics & Impact; and Information & Mis/Disinformation. These are 'actionable skills that learners can demonstrate' and 'define how users can Understand and Evaluate AI-enabled tools and how educators can support AI literacy development.' Each practice comes with descriptions and student look-fors, such as identifying how personal information is collected or evaluating the credibility of AI outputs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Practices can be operationalized into integrated learning opportunities, walk-through documents for coaches, or formative assessments for teachers

Constraints¶

  • The report recommends learners engage in these practices without AI tools, particularly in the early grades

  • K–12 learners
  • educators

Examples¶

  • Integrate AI literacy across grades and subject-area learning rather than confining it to electives
  • Design AI literacy tools with transparency in development and establish district policies for vetting AI tools
  • District actions for sustained, equitable impact with powerful technology
  • Support learners to evaluate AI outputs by verifying sources, reporting errors, and overriding recommendations
