# Digital Promise's Computational Thinking micro-credentials recognize educators whose students apply CT practices

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Computational Thinking Micro-credentials are professional learning tools in which "A micro-credential is a digital certificate that verifies an individual's competence in a specific skill or set of skills." To earn one, teachers submit evidence of student work from classroom activities applying a CT practice, along with documentation of lesson planning and reflection. The report positions micro-credentials as useful for professional learning and credentialing pathways, illustrated by a Compton teacher earning the Analyzing and Communicating Data micro-credential after a pandemic-data unit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Submission of evidence of student work from classroom activities plus lesson planning and reflection documentation
#### Constraints
- Micro-credentials verify a specific skill or set of skills rather than broad teaching competence

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognizing educator competence in teaching CT practices such as analyzing and communicating data
