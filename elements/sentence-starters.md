# Sentence Starters

## Description
Sentence starters are partial openings — "The evidence suggests that…", "One difference between X and Y is…", "I disagree because…" — that learners complete when speaking or writing. They function as a linguistic scaffold: the rhetorical and syntactic structure is supplied, so working memory and attention can go to the ideas being expressed.

## Design Implications

Sentence starters lower the barrier to productive academic language, particularly for novices and multilingual learners, by making the expected discourse structure visible [Graphic organizers and similar external structures support novice comprehension.](../claims/graphic-organizers-support-novice-comprehension.md) [+M]. They are most effective when the frame encodes the *thinking move* required (compare, justify, hypothesize) rather than generic filler, and when they are faded as learners internalize the discourse patterns [Contingent, responsive scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Frames matched to the specific discourse function of the task (arguing, comparing, summarizing — not one generic list for everything)
- Academic vocabulary consistent with the discipline, so learners rehearse the language of the domain
- A plan for fading: shifting from full frames to word banks to independent production [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility to the learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
- Opportunities to use the frames in [discussion](class-discussion.md) and writing, not just see them displayed

#### Constraints
- Overused or never-faded frames produce formulaic, template-bound writing and can suppress authentic voice [-W]
- Frames that pre-specify conclusions ("The answer is ___ because the teacher said…") become compliance scripts rather than reasoning supports [~M]
- Less valuable for fluent academic writers, for whom frames add redundancy and constrain expression [~W]
- If the frame's syntax is more complex than the learner's current proficiency, it adds rather than reduces load [~M]

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners and students still acquiring academic English [+M]
- Novices who know content but lack the discourse conventions for expressing it
- Students with writing or language-processing difficulties, as part of [accommodations](accommodations.md)
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who should be weaned onto independent phrasing [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic language development: rehearsing discipline-specific syntax and vocabulary
- Argumentation quality: frames like "The evidence shows… therefore…" structure claims, evidence, and reasoning [Argumentation benefits from structured support for claim-evidence-reasoning moves.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [~W]
- Participation equity: lowering the production barrier so more learners contribute to discussion [+W]

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — by externalizing sentence structure, starters reduce the generation demands of composing, freeing working memory for content [Chunking and external structure reduce working-memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) — starters make the target register explicit and attainable rather than leaving learners to infer it from exposure alone
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — a sentence starter is a temporary structure that must be faded; the design decision is the fading schedule, not the presence of the frame

## Examples

**[The Writing Revolution](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org)** — The Hochman Method embeds sentence-level activities (because-but-so frames, sentence expansion) as the foundation of writing instruction across subjects.

**[WIDA Can-Do Descriptors](https://wida.wisc.edu)** — Frame banks calibrated to English learner proficiency levels, so starter complexity matches learner language development.

**Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) frames in science classrooms** — Widely used starters ("The data show that…", "This pattern occurred because…") that structure scientific explanation; see the [Krajcik CER framework materials](https://www.learnwithkrajcik.com).
