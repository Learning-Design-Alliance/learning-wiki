Family Partnership Agreement as the record of home visiting goal setting and progress¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Family Partnership Agreement is a form used in Head Start programs nationally that provides a format for the home visitor and caregiver to set adult or child oriented goals and record progress throughout home visiting. In this study it was the data source from which goal content, number of goals, and recorded accomplishment were extracted across a full year of home visiting. The authors caution that, absent an integrity assessment, it is not certain the form accurately indicated goal activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Used jointly by the home visitor and caregiver to set and record goals and progress

Constraints¶

  • Without an integrity assessment, the form may not accurately indicate goal setting and accomplishment, as the article states

Target Learners¶

  • Head Start families receiving home-based services

Target Learning Goals¶

  • collaborative identification and monitoring of child development and adult goals

Claims¶

  • Family demographics were largely unrelated to goal setting, with child gender the only associated variable [+W]
  • Families who documented goals had longer enrollment in Head Start than families without goals [+W]
  • Slightly more than half of Head Start home-based families documented goals on the Family Partnership Agreement [+W]
  • Goals set in Head Start home visiting were largely adult centered, with only a small portion addressing child development [+W]
  • Families who documented goals received more home visits than families who did not [+W]
  • Goal attainment was low, and child development goals were accomplished at a higher rate than adult centered goals [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Head Start program

Examples¶

  • Provide home visitors training and a structure for collaborative goal setting with attainable, child-focused goals
