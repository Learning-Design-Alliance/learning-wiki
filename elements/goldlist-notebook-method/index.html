GoldList Notebook Method (headlist plus three distillations)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A pen-and-paper L2 vocabulary review method using a standard A4 notebook in place of flashcards or apps. The learner writes a "headlist" of up to 20 target terms with definitions from lesson context, then performs three self-quizzing "distillations" spaced in time, marking unrecalled items and rewriting them in mixed order. The article states it "is not a lesson to introduce new vocabulary, but a tool for review and to strengthen recall," providing spacing and retrieval desirable difficulties plus tactile pen-to-paper interaction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Vocabulary introduced in context first through a lesson before the method's steps
  • Adherence to the spaced review sessions and intervals between distillations

Constraints¶

  • Limited in this study to the EFL university classroom in Japan; modifications for other settings stated as beyond the article's scope
  • May be inappropriate for very young ELLs or newcomer ELLs, per the article's conclusion

Target Learners¶

  • University EFL students in Japan (CEFR B1–B2)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term retention of L2 vocabulary (idioms)

Claims¶

  • The GoldList Notebook Method increases long-term retention of L2 idioms compared with a vocabulary lesson alone [+W]
  • Only the Standard two-week-interval treatment showed a statistically significant improvement over the Baseline treatment [+W]
  • Uniform two-week spacing between distillations yielded the greatest benefit among the tested schedules, ranking Standard, Mixed, Frontloaded, then Baseline [+W]
  • Spaced retrieval practice produces better final retention than massed retrieval even though spacing lowers initial retrieval success, and more absolute spacing enhances long-term retention [+W]
  • Classroom implementation of the GoldList Method encountered pitfalls: forgotten notebooks, omitted dates, and unclear initial steps [~W]
