Online Algebra I credit recovery course¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

An online course format for recovering Algebra I credit, evaluated in a randomized trial against face-to-face credit recovery with 1,224 ninth-graders in 17 Chicago public high schools. Students taking the online course "reported that the course was more difficult" than face-to-face students, "were less likely to recover credit and scored lower on an algebra post-test." The article discusses the benefits and challenges of online learning for credit recovery in light of these findings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Serves students who failed Algebra I and need to recover the credit to stay on track for on-time graduation

Constraints¶

  • In this trial the online format produced worse first-year outcomes than face-to-face credit recovery, with no second-year differences by condition

Target Learners¶

  • Ninth-graders who failed Algebra I in urban public high schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Recovering Algebra I credit
  • Algebra skills and knowledge

Claims¶

  • Online and face-to-face Algebra I credit recovery showed no statistically significant differences on any second-year outcomes [+W]
  • Online Algebra I credit recovery students were less likely to recover credit and scored lower on an algebra post-test than face-to-face students [-W]
  • Students in online Algebra I credit recovery reported that the course was more difficult than students in face-to-face credit recovery reported [-W]
  • Before this trial, online credit recovery was widely used despite no rigorous evidence on its relative efficacy versus face-to-face courses [+W]
