# Reciprocal Teaching

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Reciprocal Teaching is a structured small-group procedure in which learners take turns acting as the discussion leader, applying four comprehension strategies — predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing — to shared text or content. The teacher initially models the strategies, then gradually hands the "teacher" role to students, who guide the dialogue while peers respond and contribute.

## Design Implications

Reciprocal Teaching improves comprehension by making strategic reading processes explicit and socially practiced: students articulate questions and summaries rather than silently enacting them [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. Its signature mechanism is gradual transfer of responsibility — the instructor models, coaches, then fades support as students assume the leader role [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]. Effects are strongest when the four strategies are taught explicitly and the dialogue stays focused on meaning-making rather than turn-taking mechanics.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit initial modeling of the four strategies (predicting, questioning, clarifying, summarizing) before students lead
- Texts or content at an appropriate difficulty — challenging enough that clarifying and questioning are genuinely needed
- Small groups (typically 4–6) so every student can take the leader role within a session
- Instructor monitoring and coaching during early student-led sessions, with progressive fading

#### Constraints
- Without initial expert modeling, groups drift into unfocused discussion and the strategies are never enacted properly [-M]
- Less effective when texts are too easy — students generate trivial questions and summaries, and the dialogue collapses into recitation [~M]
- Requires sustained implementation over multiple sessions; single-session use produces weak effects [-M]
- Groups without norms for respectful challenge can let dominant students monopolize the leader role, reducing participation benefits for others [-W]

### Target Learners
- Struggling readers and students with weak comprehension strategies, who benefit most from explicit strategy modeling [+S]
- Adolescents and adults learning content-heavy material in reading comprehension, STEM, and professional education
- Less beneficial for already-skilled comprehenders, for whom the strategies may be redundant [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension: constructing meaning from expository and narrative text
- Strategy acquisition: internalizing monitoring and repair moves (clarify, summarize, predict)
- Critical thinking and discussion skills: formulating questions, evaluating understanding, and building on peers' contributions

### Affordances
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — the teacher-led-to-student-led sequence is a canonical fading design; support is transferred as competence grows rather than removed abruptly
- [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) — students observe peers modeling strategic dialogue before enacting it themselves, and the rotating leader role makes every student both observer and model
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — comprehension is negotiated through dialogue; peers' questions and clarifications surface misunderstandings an individual reader would leave hidden
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the instructor's modeling phase makes invisible comprehension processes overt, then coaches students as they attempt the role

## Examples

**[Palincsar & Brown's original intervention](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0202_1)** — Seventh-grade poor comprehenders improved from ~30% to 70–80% on comprehension assessments after 20 days of daily reciprocal teaching sessions; the benchmark design for all later implementations.

**[Reciprocal Teaching in Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/comprehension/articles/reciprocal-teaching-comprehension-strategy)** — Widely used practitioner guide with classroom routines, role cards, and fading schedules for elementary and middle school.

**[T4S (Talk-for-Science)]** — Adaptations of the four strategies to science texts, where "clarifying" targets technical vocabulary and "summarizing" targets causal explanations in STEM reading.
