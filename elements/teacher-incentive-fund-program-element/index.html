Teacher Incentive Fund (TIF) performance-based compensation program for teachers and principals in high-need schools¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Teacher Incentive Fund is a federal grant program that "provides grants to support performance-based compensation systems for teachers and principals in high-need schools." The 2010 grantee cohort was required to implement four program components for teachers, and the program uses performance measures and pay-for-performance bonuses whose communication to educators proved challenging in early implementation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts receiving TIF grant funds were required to implement four program components for teachers.

Constraints¶

  • Early implementation was challenging; fewer than half of 2010 grantee districts implemented all four required components in the first year.

Target Learners¶

  • Teachers and principals in high-need schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Performance-based compensation tied to educator effectiveness

Claims¶

  • After the first year of implementation, fewer than half of 2010 TIF districts implemented all four required program components for teachers, though a large majority implemented at least three [~W]
  • In the 10 evaluation districts, educators did not always understand the performance measures and pay-for-performance bonuses used for TIF [~W]
  • More than half of teachers in the evaluation districts did not know they were eligible for pay-for-performance bonuses [~W]
  • Teachers in the evaluation districts underestimated the size of pay-for-performance bonuses they could earn through TIF [~W]
  • Few districts structured pay-for-performance bonuses to align well with TIF grant guidance [~W]
  • The average TIF bonus was about 4% of average teacher salary, below the 5% recommended by TIF grant guidance for substantial bonuses [~W]
