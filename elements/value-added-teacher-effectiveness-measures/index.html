Value-added measures of teacher effectiveness¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Value-added measures quantify a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains. The brief defines the approach: "'Value added' is a teacher's contribution to students' learning gains." It reports that newly emerging research uses these measures to shed light on disadvantaged students' access to effective teaching, and that variation in how researchers present such evidence complicates practitioner use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires student learning-gain data to estimate each teacher's contribution

Constraints¶

  • Individual researchers have varied in their presentation of this evidence, making it challenging for practitioners to draw lessons from the data

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 4-8

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable access to effective teaching measured through learning gains

Claims¶

  • Disparities in access to effective teaching between disadvantaged and non-disadvantaged students varied widely across the 29 districts studied [+W]
  • Disadvantaged students in grades 4-8 receive less-effective teaching on average than other students in the same grades [+W]
  • The teaching-effectiveness disparity represents a small share of the overall achievement gap for disadvantaged students [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • Four principal performance measures based on student test scores

Examples¶

  • Summarize emerging value-added evidence in practitioner-accessible briefs
