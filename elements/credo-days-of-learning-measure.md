# Days of Learning: CREDO's translation of growth in standard deviation units into days of a 180-day school year

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Days of Learning is CREDO's translation device for making statistical growth results accessible to non-technical audiences. The report explains that CREDO "uses those annual measures of average learning to represent a typical year of learning, and equates that to a typical 180-day school year," so extra or missing progress is expressed as days added to or subtracted from a 180-day base. In this report, growth in standard deviation units is converted into days of learning so results "can be assessed for statistical differences" while remaining interpretable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Annual achievement test scores across consecutive grades from which average year-to-year learning can be computed
- State testing data across consecutive grades from which average year-to-year learning can be computed for each grade transition
#### Constraints
- The measure is a translation of statistical growth estimates, not a direct count of instructional days attended
- Growth is expressed relative to the state average year of learning on a typical 180-day school year, not as an absolute measure of achievement

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in tested grades

### Target Learning Goals
- Annual academic growth in reading and mathematics
