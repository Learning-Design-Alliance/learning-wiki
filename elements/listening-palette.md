# Listening Palette

## Description
Adapted from choreographer Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process, a listening palette is a low-stakes, multimodal reflection task: learners watch or listen to content, then individually record their noticings in whatever form feels natural — words, sketches, gesture, invented notation, movement. Small groups then share their individual reflections, build on one another's ideas, and create a collaborative representation before presenting to the whole class. The protocol treats multiple modes of expression as equally valid evidence of understanding, rather than privileging verbal/written response.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content worth reflecting on (e.g., a short video) and no single prescribed response format
- Group time to build a shared representation before whole-class sharing
#### Constraints
- Evidence for this technique comes from a single qualitative study in informal, arts-integrated community settings — [Multimodal, culturally grounded assessment supports engagement and agency](../claims/multimodal-culturally-grounded-assessment-supports-engagement-and-agency.md) [~W]

### Target Learners
- Learners for whom verbal/written response is not their most comfortable mode of expression, or who benefit from creative/embodied modes being treated as legitimate

### Target Learning Goals
- Early, informal articulation of developing understanding, without the pressure of a single "correct" response format

### Affordances
- [Cultural & Life Experiences Connections](../principles/cultural-life-experiences-connections.md) — valuing whatever mode of expression a learner brings treats their existing cultural and creative repertoire as a resource

## Related Elements

## Examples
- Youth in an Embodied Physics Learning Lab used listening palettes after watching short physics-related videos, recording noticings through sketches, movement, and informal notation before building a group representation.
