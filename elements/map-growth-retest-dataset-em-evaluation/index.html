MAP Growth retest dataset for evaluating E-M scoring accuracy¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A unique dataset built from archived RIT-score data on MAP Growth assessments in math and reading from students who had been retested within a short period of time. Across two testing terms, instances of students retested within one day were identified, yielding over 5,000 cases with disengagement (10%+ rapid guesses) on the first test and zero rapid guesses on the retest. This dataset permits evaluation of E-M score-adjustment accuracy, which is otherwise challenging.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must have been retested within a short period, with one subgroup retested within one day.

Constraints¶

  • Evaluation rests on retest cases where the retest showed zero rapid guesses, assuming the retest reflects fully engaged performance.

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students taking MAP Growth math and reading assessments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate achievement measurement under test-taking disengagement

Claims¶

  • E-M scoring partially accounts for observed RIT score differences between disengaged first tests and engaged retests [+W]
  • Students who rapid-guess less on a MAP Growth retest tend to see score gains, while those who rapid-guess more tend to see declines [+M]
  • Seriously disengaged initial testers who stopped rapid-guessing on retest showed large RIT gains, averaging about 6 points in Reading and 12 in Math [+M]
  • Rapid guesses on MAP Growth reflect a lack of engagement rather than a lack of knowledge [+M]
  • Test-taking disengagement frequently differs between a MAP Growth initial test and a same-day retest [+M]

Related Elements¶

  • MAP Growth rapid-guessing invalidation criterion and four-level disengagement classification
  • Same-day MAP Growth retest dataset spanning four test types and four testing terms
  • MAP Growth interim computer adaptive assessment system
  • NCME 2020 presentation on effort-moderated IRT parameter estimation accuracy

Examples¶

  • Use secondary time thresholds to purify E-M scoring
