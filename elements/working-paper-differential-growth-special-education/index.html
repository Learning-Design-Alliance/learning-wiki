Working paper and research brief: Understanding differential growth during school years and summers for students in special education¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The page lists two associated NWEA artifacts: a working paper and a research brief, both titled "Understanding differential growth during school years and summers for students in special education." They underpin the webinar's "new research on the academic growth trends of students with disabilities in special education during the school year as compared to the summer before the pandemic." A related journal article, Supporting students with disabilities throughout the year, and a policy brief on school-year learning gains and summer learning loss are also listed.

Claims¶

  • New research examined academic growth trends of students with disabilities in special education during the school year compared to the summer before the pandemic [+W]
  • Steeper summer learning losses for students with disabilities contribute to widening disparities with peers [+W]
  • Ever-SPED students started kindergarten with lower math and reading scores and grew less than never-SPED students during the kindergarten school year [+W]
  • Ever-SPED students lost more learning than never-SPED students during every summer from kindergarten through 4th grade [+W]
  • Students with disabilities in grades K-4 grow as much or more academically during some school years than peers without disabilities [+W]
  • Test score disparities between ever-SPED and never-SPED students grew from under 0.4 standard deviations in kindergarten to 0.9 standard deviations in 4th grade [+W]
