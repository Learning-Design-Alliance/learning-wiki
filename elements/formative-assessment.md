# Formative Assessment

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Formative assessment is the ongoing, low-stakes gathering of evidence about learner understanding, used to provide feedback to learners and to adjust instruction in real time. Its defining feature is not the instrument (quiz, discussion, draft) but the *use* of the information: assessment becomes formative only when it changes what happens next for the learner or the teacher.

## Design Implications

Formative assessment closes the gap between current and desired performance by making learning visible while it can still be corrected. Feedback is most effective when it answers three questions — Where am I going? How am I doing? Where to next? [The feedback model of Hattie & Timperley organizes feedback at task, process, and self-regulation levels.](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487) [+S]. Frequent low-stakes retrieval also strengthens retention directly, independent of feedback effects [Testing as retrieval practice improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear learning intentions and success criteria shared with learners — without them, feedback has no reference point [Formative assessment requires explicit criteria against which the gap between current and desired performance can be evaluated.](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00117714) [+S]
- Low-stakes framing so results inform rather than grade; high-stakes use suppresses honest error display
- A mechanism for acting on results: reteaching, [Fading](fading.md) of support, or adjusted task difficulty
- Opportunities for learners to interpret and act on feedback themselves [Feedback that prompts self-regulation (self-monitoring, self-correction) produces larger gains than feedback on the self or task alone.](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602930600679059) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Feedback that directs attention to the self ("good job," grades alone) can reduce performance rather than improve it [Feedback at the personal-praise level is least effective and can undermine performance.](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487) [~S]
- Implementing formative assessment at scale is demanding: teachers often collect evidence but lack time or structures to act on it, which strips the practice of its effect [Large gains depend on changed teaching decisions, not merely on collecting assessment data.](https://doi.org/10.1080/0969595980050102) [~M]
- Frequent testing without feedback yields little benefit; the retrieval effect alone does not substitute for corrective information [Testing as retrieval practice improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Learners at all levels, but especially those in competency-based programs where progression depends on demonstrated mastery rather than seat time
- Struggling learners, who benefit most from early detection of misconceptions before they compound
- Learners developing self-regulation, when assessment includes self- and peer-evaluation against criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: surfacing and correcting misconceptions mid-course [Erroneous examples and error-focused feedback build conceptual knowledge.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [+M]
- Skill mastery: informing the next cycle of [Practice](practice.md) and support
- Self-regulated learning: building learners' capacity to judge their own work against criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — formative assessment is the operational core of this principle: assessment data is used *by and with* learners, not merely collected about them
- [Mastery Learning](../principles/mastery-learning.md) — formative checks supply the decision point for the mastery cycle: assess, remediate, re-assess before advancing
- [Feedback Loops](../principles/feedback-loops.md) — each formative check is one iteration of a loop; shortening the delay between performance and feedback is the key design variable
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — frequent low-stakes checks let instructors calibrate task difficulty to current competence, avoiding overload from tasks pitched beyond readiness

## Related Elements

- [Peer Review](peer-review.md) — scales feedback while building learners' criteria-based judgment

## Examples

**[5-Minute Writing Conferences](../strategies/5-minute_writing_conferences.md)** — Brief individual conferences using the student's own draft as the formative evidence; feedback is immediate and actionable before the next revision.

**[Activities for Student Self-Reflection](../strategies/activities_for_student_self-reflection.md)** — Structured reflection prompts that turn assessment data into self-regulatory decisions.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Mastery-tracking exercise system: hints, immediate correctness feedback, and spaced re-testing determine when a skill is marked "mastered," enacting a formative loop at scale.

**[ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org)** — Free math platform providing teachers real-time reports on student errors during homework, enabling next-day instructional adjustment; field studies link its use to measurable learning gains.
