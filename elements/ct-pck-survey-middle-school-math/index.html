CT–PCK Survey: a 16-item vignette-based measure of middle school math teachers' computational thinking pedagogical content knowledge¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (4 for, 4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Computational Thinking–Pedagogical Content Knowledge (CT–PCK) Survey is a vignette-based instrument in which "each of the items in the initial 25-item CT–PCK Survey presents a single vignette depicting a math classroom scenario, along with four options representing different instructional responses to that scenario." One option integrates one of five core CT practices. It was developed with two large urban Midwest districts as part of the ENACT partnership and reduced to 16 items after psychometric analysis.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Administered to in-service grade 6–8 math teachers; items were built using Skilling and Stylianides' (2020) framework for designing vignette items in education research

Constraints¶

  • The study states the survey "is not intended to be used to evaluate teachers' performance, their instructional quality, or their math content knowledge"

Target Learners¶

  • Middle school (grades 6–8) math teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Pedagogical content knowledge for integrating computational thinking practices (pattern recognition, abstraction, decomposition, debugging, algorithm development) into math instruction

Claims¶

  • The 16-item CT–PCK Survey measures CT pedagogical content knowledge for math as a single overall construct [+W]
  • Content experts and practitioner reviews supported the content and face validity of the CT–PCK Survey [+W]
  • The overall CT–PCK Survey functioned similarly across teacher characteristic groups, though some items showed raw differential item functioning above 0.64 logits [~W]
  • Teachers who were White, female, or had five or more years of experience scored about 14 percentage points higher; emergency-certified teachers scored 14 points lower [~W]
  • The 16-item CT–PCK Survey shows internal consistency reliability around .71–.72 and person separation reliability of .69 [+W]
  • Teachers scored across a wide range on the CT–PCK Survey, averaging about 61 percent correct, with 92 percent scoring above chance [+W]
  • ICC method flags seven items with the largest DIF indices, two of which show slight nonuniform DIF that the unsigned-area measure understates [~W]
  • Seven items exhibit absolute gender DIF by the LDF method, flagged when the total-score line falls outside .95 confidence bands [~W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Use the CT–PCK Survey to identify professional learning needs and evaluate CT-integration professional development, not for teacher evaluation
