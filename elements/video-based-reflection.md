# Video-Based Reflection

## Description
Video-based reflection is the element in which learners or practitioners review a recording of their own past activity to analyze choices, patterns, and next steps. The reviewer, not only an outside observer, does the interpreting — this is what distinguishes it from an instructor simply watching and evaluating footage. It has been used at very different grain sizes: young children interpreting video of their own unscripted play, and teacher teams interpreting video of their own classroom teaching within an ongoing professional-development partnership.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A recording of the reviewer's own meaningful activity, not a generic or someone-else's example
- Enough open-endedness in the reflection prompt that the reviewer's own noticings — not just researcher-predetermined categories — can surface
#### Constraints
- What video-based reflection is most useful *for* may depend on where the reviewer is in their own change process: one study found it most salient for surfacing tensions (problematization) and consolidating new practice (conservation), while other kinds of support (external practices, frameworks) were more salient in between — [PD support type and salience depends on a teacher team's current phase in the adaptive learning cycle](../claims/pd-support-salience-depends-on-adaptive-cycle-phase.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Studied across a very wide range: young children (K-1) reflecting on their own play, and in-service teacher teams reflecting on their own classroom practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Surfacing the reviewer's own sensemaking about a past episode — for children, mathematical and social-affective meaning in play; for teachers, gaps and tensions in current practice that motivate a change in approach

## Examples
- Four kindergarten/first-grade children reviewed video of their own shape-magnet play and surfaced both mathematical reasoning and social/affective meaning in what they had done, without researcher-imposed categories.
- Teacher teams in a multi-year PD partnership reviewed clips of their own teaching, selected around a question the team itself posed, as the basis for collaborative sensemaking with a facilitator.
