Curiosity Corner early childhood curriculum¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 5 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Curiosity Corner is an early childhood curriculum developed and distributed by the Success for All Foundation, emphasizing children's language and literacy skills. It comprises "two sets of 38 weekly thematic units—one for three-year-olds and one for four-year-olds," with daily sequential activities including Greetings and Readings, Clues and Questions, Rhyme Time, Learning Labs, Story Tree, Outside/Gross Motor Play, Snack Time, and Question/Reflection. It is implemented in more than 300 sites in 29 states, typically in settings of about 15 children with a teacher and an assistant, mostly in high-poverty neighborhoods.

Requirements¶

  • Teaching materials cannot be purchased without participating in training and other professional development activities, including an initial two-day training session and ongoing implementation visits.

Constraints¶

  • The WWC found no discernible effects on oral language, print knowledge, phonological processing, cognition, or math based on the two studies meeting its evidence standards.

Claims¶

  • Chambers et al. report a statistically significant expressive language effect for 3-year-olds but not 4-year-olds, which WWC recalculation does not confirm [~W]
  • Kindergarten follow-up shows statistically significant positive effects of Curiosity Corner on two print knowledge measures, not included in the effectiveness rating [+W]
  • Curiosity Corner shows no discernible effects on cognition in the Chambers et al. quasi-experimental study [-W]
  • Curiosity Corner shows no discernible effects on preschool math outcomes in the PCER randomized trial [-W]
  • Curiosity Corner shows no discernible effects on preschool oral language outcomes across two studies meeting WWC standards [-W]
  • Curiosity Corner shows no discernible effects on phonological processing in the PCER randomized trial [-W]
  • Curiosity Corner shows no discernible effects on preschool print knowledge in the PCER randomized trial [-W]
