Facing History and Ourselves character education curriculum¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Facing History and Ourselves is a character education program aiming to promote core values and help middle and high school students develop moral reasoning skills through examining historical events, particularly those leading to World War II and the Holocaust. The report describes that the curriculum 'begins with self-reflection on questions about identity, group membership, and obligations to others.' Typical units run 4-8 weeks or a semester and include class discussions of readings from the Resource Book, films with study guides, guest speakers, literature, and journal writing, with teachers guiding in-depth examination of a case study of pre-war Germany and the Holocaust and reflections connecting that history to students' own lives.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers participate in professional development seminars and apply the content and approaches to their own teaching or school program

Constraints¶

  • The program implementation evaluated in the qualifying study did not include schoolwide components
  • The WWC notes the program may have changed since the study was conducted

Target Learners¶

  • middle and high school students
  • eighth-grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • character education values
  • moral reasoning skills
  • critical thinking and moral decision-making

Claims¶

  • Four of seven knowledge, attitudes, and values outcomes favor the comparison group, including ethnic identity (effect size -0.19) and both moral reasoning measures, none statistically significant [-W]
  • The evidence base for Facing History and Ourselves is limited: only one of eight reviewed studies met WWC evidence standards, and it used a quasi-experimental rather than randomized design [~W]
  • Facing History and Ourselves shows no discernible effects on student behavior, with self-reported fighting differences neither statistically significant nor substantively important [-W]
  • Three of seven knowledge, attitudes, and values outcomes favor Facing History and Ourselves, including relationship maturity (best response) with effect size 0.45, but none remain statistically significant after WWC corrections [+W]
