Four early elementary math curricula evaluated: Investigations, Math Expressions, Saxon Math, and Scott Foresman-Addison Wesley Mathematics¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 5 studies (4 causal, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Evaluation of Mathematics Curricula project (time frame 2005-2013) examined four early elementary math programs. As the page lists them: "(1) Investigations in Number, Data, and Space (Investigations), (2) Math Expressions, (3) Saxon Math (Saxon), and (4) Scott Foresman-Addison Wesley Mathematics (SFAW)." These four curricula are the treatments compared in the study's achievement findings for first and second graders.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Implemented in early elementary classrooms as the math curriculum used by study schools
  • Schools must be assignable to one of the four curricula conditions in an experimental design, as in the federal Evaluation of Mathematics Curricula study.

Constraints¶

  • Findings reported here apply to first graders in the first cohort of 39 schools during the 2006-2007 school year.

Target Learners¶

  • First and second grade elementary students
  • First-grade elementary school students

  • Early elementary mathematics achievement

Claims¶

  • First-grade math achievement is significantly higher under Math Expressions and Saxon Math than under Investigations in Number, Data, and Space and Scott Foresman-Addison Wesley Mathematics [+W]
  • Four elementary math curricula were evaluated using an experimental design [+W]
  • A large-scale, rigorous study compared four elementary math curricula's effects on achievement across two years, first through second grade [+W]
  • A large-scale experimental study compared the effects of the four mathematics curricula on student achievement [+W]
  • The curriculum evaluation was the second report from a multi-year (2005-2013) IES-funded study of mathematics curricula [+W]
  • The study examines whether some early elementary school math curricula are more effective than others at improving math achievement in schools serving a high percentage of disadvantaged students. [+W]
  • The study is the largest experimental-design study of multiple math curricula, covering 110 schools with this report based on the first cohort of 39 schools [+M]
  • The better-performing early math curricula raised achievement for several student subgroups, including students in low-scoring and high-poverty schools [+M]
