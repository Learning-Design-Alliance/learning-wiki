Little Books: reproducible beginning-reading books designed for interactive reading between parents and children or teachers and students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), q3–q4 · 4 of 5 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Little Books are sets of reproducible little books designed to be colored and read at home or at school, intended for interactive book reading between parents and children or teachers and students. The books "use thematic topics familiar to children", are "written with high-frequency words and use simple phrases and sentences", and have "strong links between illustrations and text". Sets of 20 to 26 reproducible books were available for $11.95, published by Good Year Books.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parents and teachers were trained to use the Little Books, with parents shown a video and teachers attending a workshop

Constraints¶

  • Information is not available on the number or demographics of students, schools, or districts using Little Books

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Beginning reading and general reading achievement

Claims¶

  • The evidence base for Little Books is small: only one study met WWC standards, addressing only general reading achievement, with no studies meeting standards for alphabetics, fluency, or comprehension [~W]
  • The improvement index for Little Books on general reading achievement is +12 percentile points for each delivery variation, averaging +12 percentile points across the study [+W]
  • Using Little Books in kindergarten produces potentially positive effects on general reading achievement, with all three delivery variations (home only, school only, and home and school) outperforming a comparison condition on the Metropolitan Readiness Test [+W]
  • Joint Book Reading Predicts Literacy Success [+S]
  • Parent Involvement Improves Early Reading [+M]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Train teachers in a structured daily Little Books routine of opening, modeling, tryouts, and closing, with whole-class introduction followed by small-group reading and individual reading
