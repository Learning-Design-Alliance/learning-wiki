Early Intervention in Reading® (EIR®) supplemental beginning-reading program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

EIR® is a supplemental program providing extra instruction to groups of students at risk of failing to learn to read. The report states "The program uses picture books to stress instruction in phonemic awareness, phonics, and contextual analysis, along with repeated reading and writing." In grades K–2 it is whole-class instruction with additional small-group work for struggling readers; in grades 3–4 it is small-group instruction 20 minutes, four days a week. Instruction involves 15–20 minutes of daily supplemental instruction to the whole class or groups of five to seven struggling readers, developed by Dr. Barbara Taylor and distributed by the EIR® program.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers are trained for nine months using workshops and an Internet-based professional development program

Constraints¶

  • The study reviewed by the WWC focused on first grade students only

Target Learners¶

  • first-grade students at risk of failing to learn to read
  • struggling readers in grades K–4

Target Learning Goals¶

  • phonemic awareness
  • phonics
  • contextual analysis
  • reading fluency
  • reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • EIR® showed large alphabetics effect sizes and improvement indices of +29 to +42 percentile points in the one qualifying study [+W]
  • EIR® had a positive, substantively important but statistically non-significant effect on first-grade reading comprehension [+W]
  • EIR® produced statistically significant positive effects on first-grade alphabetics outcomes (segmentation and blending, vowel sounds) [+W]
  • The evidence base for EIR® is small: only one of seven reviewed studies met WWC evidence standards [~W]
