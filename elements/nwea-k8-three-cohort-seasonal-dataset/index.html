NWEA test-score dataset of over 2.5 million kindergarten through eighth-grade students across three cohorts (2015-2018)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A large-scale longitudinal test-score dataset used to examine seasonal patterns of racial/ethnic achievement gaps. The article states it draws on "test scores from over 2.5 million kindergarten to eighth-grade students" and follows "three different cohorts of students from 2015 to 2018," enabling comparison of gap growth when school is in versus out of session across grades K-8.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires seasonal test-score data allowing within-student growth comparisons across school and summer periods

Constraints¶

  • Restricted to students in the NWEA assessment population; the abstract notes prior seasonal studies were restricted to early grades, which this dataset extends through eighth grade

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten through eighth-grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic skills in reading and mathematics as measured by standardized achievement tests

Claims¶

  • Asian students generally pull ahead of White students at a faster rate during summers than during school periods [+W]
  • Black-White achievement gaps widen during school periods and shrink during summers among kindergarten through eighth-grade students [+W]
  • Racial/ethnic disparities observed among older students are largely already in place among kindergartners [+W]
  • Schools play less of a role in widening racial/ethnic achievement gaps than children's prekindergarten environments [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment and longitudinal achievement database
  • Multi-season math and reading score dataset for over seven million K-8 students (2016-17)
  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment dataset (3.4 million students) as the evidence base for seasonal learning analysis
  • National K–8 fall and spring achievement dataset covering 840,000 students in 8,800 public schools
