# Student-Led IEPs

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A student-led IEP is an Individualized Education Program meeting in which the student — rather than the case manager or parents — leads the discussion: introducing attendees, explaining their disability and accommodations, presenting strengths and needs, reviewing progress on goals, and helping set new goals. It functions as both an instructional intervention (building self-determination skills) and an authentic assessment of self-advocacy. The student's role is scaffolded, not thrown in: preparation typically spans weeks and includes explicit instruction, rehearsal, and [Coaching](coaching.md).

## Design Implications

Student-led IEPs convert a compliance meeting into a learning event, giving students authentic practice in self-knowledge, goal-setting, and communication with adults [~M]. Evidence links student involvement in IEP meetings to gains in self-determination and goal knowledge, though most studies are quasi-experimental or single-subject rather than large-scale randomized trials [~M]. Autonomy over meaningful decisions about one's own learning supports intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S], and leading the meeting is a high-stakes, authentic instance of that autonomy. Preparation should follow a gradual release: teacher models each meeting segment, students rehearse with feedback, then [Fading](fading.md) of adult support as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, developmentally appropriate instruction on the IEP's purpose, the student's disability, and their rights and accommodations — students cannot lead what they do not understand
- Structured preparation time with rehearsal and feedback (scripts, slide decks, or presentation templates for younger students)
- Adult facilitation that genuinely transfers the floor to the student rather than performing token participation
- Family and team members briefed on their role: asking the student questions, not talking over them
- Alignment with the student's communication profile; for some students, leading means co-presenting with an AAC device or a support person, not solo presentation

#### Constraints
- Token participation — a student who reads a prepared statement without understanding it — produces no self-determination gains and can mask continued adult control [~M]
- Students with limited self-awareness of their disability need prerequisite instruction first; leading the meeting without it can be a negative experience that damages confidence [-W]
- Meeting logistics (timing, number of attendees, legal formalities) can crowd out the student's voice if the team does not deliberately protect it
- Benefits accrue over multiple years; a single led meeting is unlikely to shift long-term self-advocacy outcomes [-W]

### Target Learners
- Students with high-incidence disabilities (learning disabilities, ADHD, autism) in upper elementary through transition years — the bulk of the research base [~M]
- Students preparing for postsecondary transition, where self-advocacy and disclosure decisions become the student's own responsibility
- Younger students can participate in scaled-down form (sharing strengths and one goal) rather than full leadership

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-determination: choice-making, decision-making, goal-setting, and self-monitoring
- Self-advocacy: articulating one's disability, needs, and legally entitled [Accommodations](accommodations.md)
- Self-regulated learning: reviewing progress data on one's own goals enacts the monitoring cycle of [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)
- Communication and presentation skills in an authentic, high-stakes context

### Affordances
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) — leading the IEP satisfies all three basic needs: autonomy (real decisions), competence (visible mastery of a demanding task), and relatedness (collaborating with the adults in one's life)
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — the IEP meeting is a genuine audience with real consequences, not a simulated exercise
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — preparation scripts, rehearsals, and adult prompting are temporary supports to be faded across years as the student takes on more of the meeting
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — the meeting itself surfaces what the student does and does not understand about their own learning profile, informing next year's instruction

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Self-Determination Learning Model of Instruction (SDLMI)](../strategies/self-determined-learning-model-of-instruction.md) — teacher-planned self-determination instruction that pairs naturally with student-led meetings

## Examples

**[I'm Determined](https://www.imdetermined.org)** (Virginia Department of Education) — state initiative providing student-led IEP templates, "Good Day Plans," and youth examples across grade levels.

**[Zarrow Center for Learning Enrichment](https://www.ou.edu/zarrow)** (University of Oklahoma) — publishes the *Self-Directed IEP* curriculum (Martin et al.), a step-by-step instructional package for preparing students to lead their meetings.

**[SDLMI](https://www.kucdd.org)** — the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction, in which students set and work toward education goals with teacher facilitation, culminating naturally in student-led goal review.
