# Midterm Exam

## Description
A midterm exam is a formal, summative assessment administered at the midpoint of a course to measure student understanding and retention of material covered so far. Beyond certification of progress, it functions as a high-stakes retrieval event: preparing for and taking the exam forces learners to reconstruct and consolidate course content.

## Design Implications

Midterms leverage the testing effect — the act of retrieving information strengthens long-term retention more than restudying [Retrieval practice improves retention relative to restudying.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693) [+S]. Their diagnostic value depends on feedback: a midterm followed by timely, specific feedback functions as [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md), informing both instructor pacing and student study strategies [+M]. Without feedback, the exam degrades into pure ranking and loses most instructional value [-M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment between exam items and the course's stated learning objectives and instructional activities
- Clear expectations in advance — format, coverage, and [Rubrics](rubrics.md) or scoring criteria where open-ended items are used
- Timely feedback and a follow-up mechanism (review session, item analysis, revision opportunity) so results change subsequent instruction
- Cumulative design where feasible — including earlier material supports spaced retrieval rather than cramming [+M]

#### Constraints
- High-stakes single exams promote cramming and surface learning strategies; distributed lower-stakes testing produces better retention [-S]
- Anxiety under high-stakes conditions can depress performance for test-anxious students, making scores a noisy measure of competence [-M]
- Poorly aligned or trick-heavy items measure test-taking skill rather than understanding, undermining validity [-M]
- A midterm early in a course, before learners have formed coherent schemas, may discourage rather than diagnose [~W]

### Target Learners
- Learners in structured settings — higher education, certification programs — where periodic formal checkpoints are institutionally expected
- Students who benefit from external deadlines and accountability structures to initiate retrieval and review [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Less suitable for novices in high-anxiety populations without prior low-stakes practice testing; success on a midterm supports persistence, and failure early can erode confidence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Consolidation and retention of declarative and conceptual knowledge across the first half of a course
- Diagnosis of gaps to inform remediation before later, dependent material
- Certification of interim mastery in [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) sequences

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — a midterm is the canonical checkpoint where assessment data should feed forward into adjusted instruction and study behavior, not just a grade
- [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) — in mastery-oriented courses, the midterm serves as a formal demonstration of proficiency on the first block of competencies, with retakes or revision pathways
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — a predictable midterm schedule gives learners a milestone around which to organize review and self-regulation
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — exam preparation itself is massed retrieval practice; cumulative item design extends this benefit across the whole course

## Examples
- **AP and IB course examinations** — mid-course and mock exams mirror the final external assessment's format, using past papers as practice tests
- **Medical licensing curricula (e.g., USMLE Step 1 preparation)** — block exams at the end of each organ-system unit function as midterms, with item feedback driving spaced review schedules
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) unit tests** — a low-stakes digital analogue: unit-level mastery checks that gate progress, embodying the midterm's diagnostic function without the stakes
