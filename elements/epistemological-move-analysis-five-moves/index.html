Epistemological Move Analysis (EMA) with five epistemological moves for analyzing a teacher's role in children's learning¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Epistemological Move Analysis is an analytical approach that analyzes a teacher's role in students' learning process, used in this study to examine how the teacher directed children's learning. The article distinguishes five moves: "(a) instructional moves," which direct children how to act so they can see what is worth noticing; (b) confirming moves; (c) reconstructing moves, giving children opportunity to reflect on experiences; (d) reorienting moves, encouraging another way to deal with the task; and (e) generative moves, enabling children to generate understanding by reporting perceived knowledge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Analysis requires observational data on what the teacher says and how material is managed, and the move categories must be applied to classroom dialogue and activity.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes, citing Lidar et al., that using the right epistemological move is not enough; there must also be a change in students' practical epistemology.

Target Learners¶

  • Teachers and researchers analyzing teaching in Montessori elementary classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding and developing how teachers direct children's learning during presentations and activities

Claims¶

  • After formative intervention, the teacher's presentations shifted from instruction-only moves to including generative, confirming, reconstructing, and reorienting moves [+W]
  • Reducing the number of children in a presentation and letting them describe similarities and differences made children's knowledge visible to the teacher [+W]
