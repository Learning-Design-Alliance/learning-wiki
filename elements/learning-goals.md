# Learning Goals

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Learning goals are explicit statements of the intended outcomes of instruction — what learners should know, understand, or be able to do. They function as the anchor for [Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md): activities, materials, and assessments are all designed backward from them, and learners use them to monitor their own progress.

## Design Implications

Well-specified goals improve achievement by directing learner attention to what matters and enabling self-regulated monitoring [Assessment for learning improves achievement when learners know the target and criteria.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Goals should be specific and observable rather than broad ("analyze competing explanations for X" rather than "understand X"); vague goals cannot guide study behavior or assessment design. Sharing goals with learners — not just holding them internally — converts them into reference points for [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md), and pairing them with success criteria supports accurate self-assessment [Assessment for learning improves achievement when learners know the target and criteria.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Goals stated in observable, assessable terms (action verbs, defined performance)
- Alignment: every activity and assessment maps to a stated goal ([Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md))
- Goals visible to learners, ideally with criteria for what meeting the goal looks like
- A small number of goals per unit; too many dilute attention and instructional time

#### Constraints
- Goals set at inappropriate difficulty — too easy or far beyond reach — reduce motivation and effort [~M]; moderately challenging, attainable goals produce the strongest performance effects
- Performance-proximal goals ("score 90%") can undermine learning compared to mastery-oriented goals ("be able to explain X") [~M]
- Overly detailed goal lists can fragment learning into checklist behavior, discouraging integration and transfer
- Learners with little prior knowledge may not understand abstract goal statements without concrete examples of the target performance

### Target Learners
- All learners benefit from clear goals, but the effect is strongest for those who cannot otherwise infer what is important — novices lack the expertise to extract the structure of a domain on their own
- Learners developing self-regulation benefit most when goals are paired with opportunities to check progress against them [Assessment for learning improves achievement when learners know the target and criteria.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Advanced learners may need goals framed as open problems rather than specified outcomes to avoid constraining exploration

### Target Learning Goals
- All outcome types: declarative knowledge, procedural skill, conceptual understanding, and transfer — goals are outcome-neutral by design
- Especially critical for goals requiring integration over time, where learners must track progress across multiple sessions
- Metacognitive goals: learning to evaluate one's own work against criteria

### Affordances
- [Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md) — learning goals are the first component of alignment; without them, activities and assessments cannot be coordinated around a common target
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — goals supply the reference against which feedback and self-assessment become meaningful; feedback without a stated goal is noise
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — a goal statement functions as a minimalist organizer, giving learners a schema for incoming content [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- [Activation](../principles/activation.md) — presenting goals before instruction primes learners to notice goal-relevant information and connect it to prior knowledge [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]

## Patterns That Use This Element

## Examples
- **Understanding by Design (Wiggins & McTighe)** — a published curriculum design framework in which units are planned backward from "desired results" (goals) to evidence to learning plan. See [ASCD's UbD resources](https://www.ascd.org/books/understanding-by-design)
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — each lesson and exercise is tagged to explicit skill goals, and mastery dashboards show learners their progress against them
- **SMART goal framing in course syllabi** — stating goals that are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound so both instructor and learners can verify attainment
