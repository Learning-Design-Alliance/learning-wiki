# Summer bridge program (Challenge Program at Georgia Tech)

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Challenge Program is a 5-week summer bridge program at a selective technical university in the southeastern United States, originally offered in 1981 as a developmental education program for minority students and operating since the early 1990s as a general education program for all incoming students. "Students in the program participate in short, non-credit-bearing courses in calculus, chemistry, computer science, and English composition, which are designed to resemble college-level for-credit courses." Upperclass students serve as peer educators and coaches, participants pay a nominal fee refunded based on GPA, and family members are integrated through information sessions and an awards luncheon.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students pay a nominal fee, fully or partially refunded based on their GPAs during the summer bridge program coursework
#### Constraints
- Evaluated at a single selective technical university; the study reviewed covers implementation there between 1990 and 2000

### Target Learners
- Incoming first-time fall matriculants at a selective technical university, historically including ethnic/racial minority, low-income, and first-generation students

### Target Learning Goals
- College-level academic skills in calculus, chemistry, computer science, and English composition; adjustment to college culture
