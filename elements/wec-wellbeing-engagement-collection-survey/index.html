South Australian Well-being and Engagement Collection (WEC) survey and derived indexes¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The WEC is an annual system-wide survey administered by the South Australian Department for Education each March to all public schools, covering students in Years 4–12, with over 900,000 observations since 2016. "The WEC survey consists of four constructs: 1) emotional well-being, 2) engagement with school, 3) learning readiness, and 4) health and well -being", formed from approximately 230 individual questions. The study created four indexes from three constructs plus a combined index, linked to census data and NAPLAN/PAT outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires data linkage to census demographic information and standardized academic outcomes (NAPLAN, PAT-R, PAT-M) facilitated by the education department

Constraints¶

  • Due to data limitations, only 2016–2019 data could be successfully linked for analysis; some scales (e.g., Psychological Distress, academic self-efficacy) were asked only in senior years and excluded

Target Learners¶

  • Students in Years 4–12 in South Australia's public education system

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Monitoring student emotional well-being, school engagement, and learning readiness alongside academic achievement

Claims¶

  • Learning readiness is the WEC component most strongly associated with academic outcomes (0.1–0.15 SD) [+W]
  • The relationship between the WEC index and academic achievement is stable across student year levels and census years, with no meaningful interaction effects [+W]
  • WEC index effects on academic outcomes are more pronounced for male students, with the well-being effect on literacy absent for females [+W]
  • The overall WEC well-being index has a weak positive association with academic outcomes, with effect sizes no larger than 0.1 SD [+W]
  • The emotional well-being index has the least predictive power on academic outcomes, with effect sizes in the hundredth-of-a-SD range [+W]
