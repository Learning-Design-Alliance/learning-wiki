# Case Study

## Description
A case study presents learners with a rich, realistic scenario — a business decision, a patient presentation, a legal dispute — that they must analyze, discuss, and resolve using concepts from the domain. The case functions as an authentic problem context: learners bridge theory and practice by deciding what matters, what principles apply, and what to recommend, rather than receiving those judgments pre-packaged.

## Design Implications

Case studies situate abstract concepts in a concrete, messy context, which supports transfer by requiring learners to apply knowledge under realistic conditions rather than recall it in isolation [Whole-task practice improves transfer compared with part-task or decontextualized practice.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]. Their effectiveness depends on active, structured analysis — discussion, debate, and written recommendation — not passive reading; cases assigned without a facilitated analysis task produce little more engagement than a reading [Case-based learning in medical education improves exam performance and student satisfaction versus traditional lecture.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Prompting learners to explain *why* a principle applies to the case deepens conceptual understanding beyond surface pattern-matching [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-written case with authentic complexity: multiple stakeholders, incomplete information, and genuine trade-offs
- A structured analysis task — guiding questions, a decision to make, or a deliverable — so learners do something with the case
- Facilitation that presses for reasoning and evidence rather than "the right answer" ([Class Discussion](class-discussion.md), [Socratic questioning](socratic-questioning.md))
- Sufficient prior knowledge of the underlying concepts, or scaffolds that supply it on demand

#### Constraints
- Novices without relevant schemas can flounder in open-ended cases, wasting working memory on unguided search; providing worked or expert analyses alongside the case mitigates this [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [~M]
- Cases teach the *specifics* of one context; learners may fail to abstract the general principle unless multiple contrasting cases are compared [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction of underlying principles.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- Poorly facilitated discussion can entrench misconceptions or reward confident-but-wrong reasoning
- Time-intensive: cases consume substantial class and preparation time relative to the number of concepts covered

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and advanced learners in professional domains — business, law, medicine, applied sciences — who have foundational concepts to apply [Case-based learning in medical education improves exam performance and student satisfaction versus traditional lecture.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Learners preparing for ill-structured professional judgment, where no single correct answer exists
- Less suitable for complete novices, who lack the schemas needed to interpret the case; guidance or [worked examples](worked-examples.md) should come first [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Application: using concepts to diagnose, decide, or recommend in a realistic situation
- Transfer: recognizing when and where principles apply across varied contexts
- Professional judgment and argumentation: weighing trade-offs, justifying positions, responding to counterarguments
- Integration: connecting multiple concepts within one coherent scenario

### Affordances
- [Situated Learning](../principles/situated-learning.md) — a case embeds knowledge in the social and professional context of its use, so learners practice the discipline's actual work rather than abstracted exercises
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — analyzing a case exposes learners to expert-style reasoning about messy situations; pairing with expert commentary or [Think-Aloud](think-aloud.md) makes that reasoning visible
- [Constructivism](../principles/constructivism.md) — learners must construct their own interpretation and solution, integrating new concepts with prior experience rather than receiving conclusions
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — case analysis requires decision-making and justification, not reception
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — cases are naturally discussed in groups, where divergent readings of the same facts surface assumptions and sharpen argumentation

## Related Elements
- [Scenario-Based Learning](scenario-based-learning.md) — broader family of simulated contexts; a case study is a text- or narrative-based scenario with analysis as the task
- [Problem-Based Learning](problem-based-learning.md) — cases drive the entire curriculum and learners identify what they need to learn, rather than applying already-taught concepts
- [Anchored Instruction](anchored-instruction.md) — instruction anchored to a rich case-like narrative that generates sub-problems
- [Class Discussion](class-discussion.md) — the primary facilitation method for case analysis
- [Argumentation](argumentation.md) — case conclusions must be defended with evidence from the case facts

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — the case is the organizing unit of instruction
- [Case-Based Learning Harvard Method](../patterns/case-based-learning-harvard-method.md) — cold-calling discussion of business cases under facilitator pressure
- [Anchored Instruction](../patterns/anchored-instruction.md) — video-anchored cases (e.g., The Jasper Woodbury Project) as the anchor for problem sequences
- [Cognitive Flexibility Theory](../patterns/cognitive-flexibility-theory.md) — multiple cases revisited in different contexts to build flexible, criss-crossed knowledge

## Examples

**[Harvard Business School case method](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/Pages/the-hbs-case-method.aspx)** — Students receive a real business case before class and must arrive prepared to defend an analysis; class sessions are entirely case discussion.

**[Ivey Business School case teaching](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/case-method/)** — Short, decision-forcing cases used daily across the MBA program, with a large published case library.

**[National Center for Case Study Teaching in Science](https://sciencecases.lib.buffalo.edu/)** — Peer-reviewed science cases (e.g., on genetics, epidemiology) with teaching notes, used in undergraduate STEM courses.

**[Problem-based medical curricula](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC200047/)** — Patient cases (e.g., at McMaster and Maastricht) that drive small-group learning of biomedical science.
