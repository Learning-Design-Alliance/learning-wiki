Knowledge Is Power Program (KIPP): a national network of public charter schools with extended day/year and a Commitment to Excellence¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

KIPP is "a nonprofit network of more than 200 public charter schools" serving prekindergarten through high school students, launched in 1994 in Houston. Schools operate autonomously under charter authorizers, use admissions lotteries when oversubscribed, have "an extended school day and an extended school year compared with traditional public schools", and require students, parents, and teachers to sign the Commitment to Excellence pledge covering attendance, homework, behavior, parental support, and teacher preparation. Principals can hire and fire staff and allocate resources.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Approval to operate from a charter school authorizer, with accountability standards reviewed periodically (typically every 3 to 5 years)

  • Per-pupil expenditure appears comparable or slightly higher than local districts (e.g., $13,500 vs. $13,000 in one Massachusetts study)

Target Learners¶

  • Prekindergarten through high school students in publicly funded charter schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic achievement in mathematics, English language arts, science, and social studies

Claims¶

  • KIPP charter schools have potentially positive effects on social studies achievement, with mixed study findings [+W]
  • KIPP shows no discernible effects on high school student progression (graduation within 4 years) [~W]
  • KIPP charter schools have positive effects on English language arts achievement for middle and high school students [+W]
  • KIPP charter schools have positive effects on mathematics achievement for middle and high school students [+W]
  • KIPP charter schools have potentially positive effects on science achievement for middle and high school students [+W]
  • A lottery-based randomized controlled trial of KIPP middle schools found statistically significant positive effects on state math and reading tests [+W]
