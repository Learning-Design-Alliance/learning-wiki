Chicago Public Schools 2003 double-dose algebra policy with three structural guidelines for support courses¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (4 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A district-wide policy requiring all first-time ninth-graders scoring below the national median on the eighth-grade math test to enroll in a regular algebra class plus an additional algebra support class for a full academic year. The district suggested schools program support courses so that "double-dose algebra students should have the same teacher in algebra and algebra support," courses be sequential, and support classmates be the same students; most schools followed these guidelines in 2003, with adherence declining after the same-teacher guideline was removed in 2004.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An eligibility cutoff (eighth-grade test scores below the national median)
  • Scheduling capacity for a second algebra period; the district removed the same-teacher guideline in 2004 after scheduling complaints

Constraints¶

  • The district did not mandate a particular curriculum, and the study does not address variation in instructional content across schools

Target Learners¶

  • Below-norm ninth-grade students in all CPS high schools, including 60 neighborhood, 11 magnet, and 6 vocational schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Ninth-grade algebra achievement and course passing

Claims¶

  • Double-dose algebra improved algebra test scores for targeted students just below the cutoff, with a larger post-policy gain than for students just above the cutoff [+W]
  • Policy effects on grades and failure rates differed by incoming ability: most below-norm students' grades improved slightly, but the very lowest-ability students' algebra grades declined [~W]
  • Evidence on the policy's effects on 10th-grade geometry outcomes was inconsistent across groups and years [~W]
  • The double-dose policy led schools to track algebra classes by entering math skills, making classrooms more homogeneous in ability [~W]
  • For targeted students overall, the policy produced only negligible changes in algebra grades and failure rates despite test-score gains [~W]
  • The double-dose algebra policy adversely affected non-targeted above-norm students: their algebra grades declined and failure rates increased post-policy [-W]
  • Chicago's double-dose algebra policy raised algebra test scores for both low-skill and high-skill students [+W]
  • Double-dose algebra policy increased academic demand for both high- and low-skill students, with below-norm students' gains not attributable to peer ability changes [+W]
  • Classroom peer skill levels explain about 20% of post-policy test score improvements among above-norm algebra students in Chicago [+W]
