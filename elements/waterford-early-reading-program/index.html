Waterford Early Reading Program™: a software-based K–2 reading curriculum with three year-long levels and 15-minute individual computer sessions¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (2 for, 5 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Waterford Early Reading Program™ is "a software-based curriculum for students in Kindergarten through second grade" promoting reading, writing, and typing, with literacy skills including letter mastery, phonological awareness, spelling, and comprehension strategies. It is divided into three levels, each designed for individualized, year-long instruction, and "Each level contains hundreds of songs and game-like activities with color graphics, digitized voices, and animation." Students worked individually on the multimedia computer for 15 minutes each session, with a teacher management system tracking daily time use; materials cost approximately $300 per student.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires the Waterford software plus assessment materials, classroom lessons, homework materials, classroom posters, and take-home books, CDs, and handouts
  • On-site training or online webinars and a detailed teacher's guide support initial training

Constraints¶

  • Used as a supplement to the regular reading curriculum, not a replacement
  • Cost of training is not available

Target Learners¶

  • Kindergarten through second grade students

Claims¶

  • Alphabetics improvement-index findings for Waterford Early Reading Program™ were inconsistent across outcomes, ranging from –26 to +37 percentile points [~W]
  • The evidence base for Waterford Early Reading Program™ is thin: only one of 36 reviewed studies met WWC evidence standards, and extent of evidence was rated small for both domains [~W]
  • In the qualifying quasi-experimental study, at-risk Kindergarten students using Waterford Level One for 15-minute daily sessions were matched to comparison students and pretested in fall and posttested in spring [+W]
  • Waterford Early Reading Program™ showed no discernible effects on comprehension, with a small non-significant effect size on vocabulary [~W]
  • The WWC rated Waterford Early Reading Program™ as having potentially positive effects on alphabetics, based on a substantively important average effect size with no statistically significant individual outcomes [+W]
  • Baseline equivalence was established only for low-achieving students, so analyses for other grades and subpopulations do not meet WWC evidence standards [~W]
  • No individual alphabetics outcome reached statistical significance in the WWC analysis [~W]
