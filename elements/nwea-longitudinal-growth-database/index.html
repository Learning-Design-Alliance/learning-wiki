NWEA longitudinal growth database used to track top students' achievement trends¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's data source is NWEA's longitudinal growth database, which the description says was used to track achievement trends: "achievement trends from NWEA's longitudinal growth database were used to track students who scored at or above the 90th percentile on this assessment." It supports multi-year follow-up of individual students' percentile positions across the middle school and high school span.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires longitudinal student assessment records spanning multiple school years, as the study tracked students from first to last year

Constraints¶

  • Findings are tied to students scoring at or above the 90th percentile on the NWEA assessment

Target Learners¶

  • Middle school and high school students scoring at or above the 90th percentile

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Tracking maintenance of high academic achievement over time

Claims¶

  • High-achieving students who fell below the 90th percentile typically stayed at the 70th percentile or higher [+W]
  • The study operationalized high achievement as scoring at or above the 90th percentile on the NWEA assessment [+W]
  • The high-flyer cohort comprised 91,643 students from more than 800 schools in 28 states [+W]
  • About three in five students identified as high-achieving in the first year remained high-achieving in the last year of the study [+W]

Related Elements¶

  • NWEA MAP Growth assessment and longitudinal achievement database
  • MAP Growth assessment data as the evidence base for national pandemic-recovery monitoring
  • CCSR longitudinal data archive on Chicago Public Schools
  • NWEA MAP Growth longitudinal dataset for DHH students, grades 2–8, seven reading and mathematics domains over 5 years
  • MAP Growth National Dashboard
