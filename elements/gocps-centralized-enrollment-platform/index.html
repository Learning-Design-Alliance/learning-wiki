GoCPS centralized application and single-best-offer platform¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 13 claims (9 for, 4 against) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 13 claims rest on one study

Description¶

GoCPS is Chicago Public Schools' online universal enrollment platform, adopted in 2017 to replace school-by-school applications with "a common application across all high school choice programs for incoming ninth grade students with a single deadline and single best offer." Students rank up to 20 of 273 choice programs (and up to six of 11 SEHS options); a computerized deferred-acceptance process assigns each student to the highest-ranked eligible program with available seats, and unassigned students go on ordered waitlists. The paper describes the platform's two application rounds, eligibility requirements, priority groups, and its four possible responses per ranked program (Offered, Higher Rank Offered, Waitlist, Not Eligible).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must meet each program's eligibility requirements (e.g., NWEA MAP minimums, information sessions) to be considered for admission
  • Programs' seat counts and pre-designated priority groups drive offer generation

Constraints¶

  • The paper notes that despite streamlining, "the various program requirem ents, priorities, and selection rules are still complicated, which may be challenging for families, schools, and the public to understand clearly"
  • Students may rank at most 20 programs, though the authors note most rank fewer and do not think the constraint is problematic

Target Learners¶

  • Incoming ninth-grade students in Chicago Public Schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable access to high school choice programs
  • Simplified, transparent application and offer processes for students and families

Claims¶

  • Lottery-based selection under GoCPS worked as intended: lottery numbers and offers were unrelated to student characteristics and to how highly applicants ranked programs [+W]
  • Most GoCPS applicants received an offer from a program they ranked: 93 percent in round 1 (51 percent at their top choice) and 85 percent in round 2 (61 percent at their top choice) [+W]
  • In points-based GoCPS programs, students were admitted strictly in order of application score within priority groups [+W]
  • Top-ranked program choices differed by student background: low-performing students were nearly three times as likely as high-performing students to rank a charter program first, and IB programs were most popular among high-performing students [+W]
  • Choice program demand varied widely: 19 percent of programs received more than 10 applications per seat while 8 percent received fewer applications than seats, with demand higher for Arts, CTE, points-based, and highly rated programs [+W]
  • Students found the GoCPS application platform logistically easy to use, but applications to schools with additional requirements caused difficulties [+W]
  • Ninth-grade enrollment overall and by school type and accountability rating stayed relatively stable after GoCPS implementation [+W]
  • Almost all CPS eighth-graders (92 percent) used the GoCPS platform to apply to high school in its first year [+W]
  • Students expected acceptance or rejection notifications from all schools they applied to, and the single-best-offer notification design produced confusion and frustration [-W]
  • Students overwhelmingly relied on GoCPS, CPS, and school websites to learn about potential high schools, but found websites out of date and lacking student-voice information [-W]
  • Confusion about the GoCPS true-preference ranking rationale was widespread among students, parents, and staff, and misinformation led some students into second-round applications [-W]
  • Many applicants failed to complete post-application admission requirements, with Black and Latino students least likely to complete them [-W]
  • Program demand varied widely: 21 percent of programs had more than ten times as many applications as seats, led by arts, CTE, and SEHS programs [+W]
