# Assessment

## Description
Assessment is the structured collection of evidence about learner understanding, performance, or progress for the purposes of feedback, decision making, grading, or revision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear criteria for what is being assessed
- A task or prompt aligned to the intended learning goal
- A use for the evidence, such as feedback, adjustment, certification, or reflection

#### Constraints
- Misaligned assessment can distort instruction and reward the wrong thing
- High-stakes assessment can suppress risk taking and reduce willingness to reveal partial understanding
- Evidence is weaker when criteria are hidden or inconsistent

### Target Learners
- All learners, especially when expectations and uses are transparent
- Instructors or facilitators who need evidence to adapt instruction
- Programs that must document growth, mastery, or readiness

### Target Learning Goals
- Determining current understanding or performance
- Guiding feedback and instructional adjustment
- Supporting accountability, grading, or progression decisions

### Affordances
- Makes learning visible
- Supports both formative and summative decision making
- Creates opportunities for self-assessment, peer review, and revision
