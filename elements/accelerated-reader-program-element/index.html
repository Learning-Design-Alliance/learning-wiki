Accelerated Reader™ guided reading practice and quiz system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (2 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Accelerated Reader™ is "a guided reading intervention used to supplement regular reading instruction in K–12 classrooms." Students select and read a book, then take a computerized quiz on the book's content and vocabulary; the software reports quiz performance to teachers so they can monitor progress and identify students needing more reading assistance. Students earn points based on correct responses, book length, and readability level, which teachers use to set goals; point accumulation is intended to motivate learning, though the developer does not require extrinsic rewards.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A dedicated 30- to 60-minute reading practice block; an initial norm-referenced standardized reading measure to determine each student's independent reading level; access to the computerized quiz software and book quizzes

Constraints¶

  • The program is designed to supplement, not replace, regular reading instruction; Renaissance Learning does not recommend or require the use of extrinsic rewards

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students, including the grade 4–8 adolescent learners covered by this review

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading skills, reading comprehension, vocabulary development, and reading fluency through reading practice

Claims¶

  • Accelerated Reader has no discernible effects on reading fluency or comprehension for adolescent learners [-W]
  • The evidence base for Accelerated Reader with adolescent learners is thin: only 2 of 318 reviewed studies met WWC standards [~W]
  • Bullock (2005) RCT found no statistically significant Accelerated Reader effects on comprehension (STAR and 4J Vocabulary) [-W]
  • Bullock (2005) RCT found no statistically significant Accelerated Reader effects on reading fluency (DIBELS Oral Reading Fluency) [-W]
  • Nunnery and Ross (2007) reported significant grade 5 but not grade 8 TAAS effects, which were non-significant in WWC recalculation [~W]

Related Elements¶

  • Reading interventions for grades 4–9 as defined by the WWC practice guide

Examples¶

  • Implement Accelerated Reader as a daily reading practice block with level-matched book selection and quiz-based teacher monitoring
