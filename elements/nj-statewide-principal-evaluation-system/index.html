New Jersey statewide principal evaluation system (2013/14)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

New Jersey implemented a new principal evaluation system to improve principal effectiveness, beginning with a 2012/13 pilot in 14 school districts and statewide implementation in 2013/14. In 2013/14, "half of a principal’s overall rating was composed of two measures of practice"—a district-selected or developed principal practice instrument and a state-developed evaluation leadership instrument—"and half was composed of measures of student achievement." The report analyzes this system's statistical properties.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires a principal practice instrument selected or developed by each school district and an evaluation leadership instrument developed by the New Jersey Department of Education

Constraints¶

  • Analysis covers 2013/14, the first year of statewide implementation, only

Target Learners¶

  • school principals in New Jersey public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring and improving principal effectiveness

Claims¶

  • Component measure ratings had low to moderate positive correlations with each other, suggesting the components measure distinct dimensions of principal performance [+W]
  • Principal practice instrument ratings and school median student growth percentiles showed moderate to high year-to-year stability, but growth percentiles changed more across years in smaller schools [+W]
  • Nearly all New Jersey principals (99 percent) received effective or highly effective overall ratings in the first year of statewide evaluation, with limited variation [+W]
  • Several component measure ratings and the overall rating had low, negative correlations with student socioeconomic disadvantage, suggesting possible bias against principals of more disadvantaged schools [-W]
  • Principals evaluated on school median student growth percentiles were less likely to receive highly effective overall ratings than principals not evaluated on that measure [~W]
  • Principal practice ratings varied across the score range in New Jersey's pilot year, but most principals received ratings of effective or highly effective [+W]
