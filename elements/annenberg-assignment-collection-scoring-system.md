# Chicago Annenberg classroom assignment collection and scoring system

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Teachers in grades three, six, and eight submitted typical and challenging writing and mathematics assignments; teacher raters scored them against rubrics for construction of knowledge, written communication, and connection to students’ lives, with many-facet Rasch adjustment onto a ten-point scale. In 1997 the analysis examined "a total of 349 assignments from 74 teachers," rising to 953 assignments in 1998 and 715 in 1999.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers were asked to provide four typical assignments per year and two challenging assignments per year, scored by raters working one standard at a time with random double-scoring to control for rater bias
#### Constraints
- Because of changes in the sample of schools over time and the variable amounts of data provided by different teachers, a statistical model was needed to estimate time trends

### Target Learners
- elementary school students in grades three, six, and eight in Chicago public schools

### Target Learning Goals
- intellectual quality of writing and mathematics assignments
- authentic intellectual work in classroom tasks
