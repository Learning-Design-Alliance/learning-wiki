Digital Tools¶

Element · All elements
Evidence · 76 claims (38 for, 34 mixed, 4 against) · 70 studies (15 review, 14 causal, 12 associational, 11 design, 7 theoretical, 6 quant-synthesis, 5 qualitative), q1–q4 · 9 of 70 report an effect size · 75 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Digital tools are the element in which learners use software, platforms, or digital applications to create, collaborate, practice, or receive feedback. They are useful when a tool meaningfully extends what learners can do.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A clear instructional use for the tool
  • Learner access and usability

Constraints¶

  • Tool novelty alone does not improve learning

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Support efficiency, interactivity, collaboration, or new forms of representation.

Affordances¶

  • Digital Learning
  • Engagement

Claims¶

  • Teachers receiving 11 or more hours of training on integrating digital content report almost double the reliance on software and the Internet compared with untrained teachers [+W]
  • Self-reported average level of technology use rose across three administrations of the LoA survey, though not for every technology [+W]
  • Learners used products outside class despite infrastructure limits: 65% of surveyed students reported doing software work outside class while mobile access was largely unavailable [~W]
  • PLI students digitally collaborated significantly more than non-PLI students with the same technology access, and digital collaboration had a small significant association with SBAC scores [+W]
  • LearnMate2 improved learning outcomes and user experience compared to a baseline combining an online learning platform and an LLM in a user study (n=16) [+W]
  • AI integration is associated with improvements in teaching, learning, and administrative efficiency in higher education when supported by adequate infrastructure [~W]
  • Reported benefits of AI integration were efficiency, personalization, and engagement, while challenges were equity, ethics, and academic integrity [~W]
  • ChatGPT was the most widely adopted AI platform in undergraduate higher education studies, with applications clustering around assessment automation and personalized learning support [+W]
  • Technology effects on TEFL outcomes trend upward across educational levels (primary g = 0.29, secondary g = 0.35, tertiary g = 0.44), though between-level differences are not statistically significant [~W]
  • Using ChatGPT during technology learning produces significantly higher post-test learning outcomes than conventional learning in a quasi-experiment of two classes of 31 students [+W] — attached 2026-10-16 from Hakiki et al. (2023), which proposed "ChatGPT as a conversational AI communication tool integrated into technology learning".
  • The iPad helped a student with ADHD focus attention and become more metacognitive about his reading [+W] — attached 2026-10-16 from McClanahan et al. (2012), which proposed "iPad with reading apps and downloadable activities as the medium for individualized reading intervention".
  • A representational tool offers beneficial affordances that supplement face-to-face communication for second-language learning [+W] — attached 2026-10-18 from Wen et al. (2015), which proposed "Representational tool for collaborative L2 writing in a networked classroom".
  • Students re-purpose social networking tools like Facebook as course back-channels, and user characteristics correlate with reported propensity to collaborate [~W]
  • Synchronous communication is the most common collaboration support integrated in educational VRLs, and few labs offer none [~W]
  • AIDA-supported participants spent on average twice as long in the course platform as controls, but the difference in usage time was not statistically significant [~W]
  • AI technological support is positively associated with innovative competence at all quantiles, with stronger associations at medium-to-high competence levels [+W]
  • Academic use of ChatGPT correlates most strongly with its use for language-related tasks [+W]
  • ChatGPT use in STEAM IBL is reported to improve teaching efficiency, student engagement, and flexible instructional design [+W]
  • GAI-related and GAI-unrelated AI tools show no significant difference in their moderating effects on K-12 STEM learning outcomes [~W]
  • Emerging technologies show a small-to-moderate positive overall effect on TEFL outcomes (g = 0.38, corrected to 0.28 for publication bias) [+W]
  • GenAI tools are already widely used in the academic work of the surveyed undergraduates [+W]
  • Across three physics assessments, students who declared GenAI use scored slightly higher than non-users, but none of the differences were statistically significant [~W]
  • Students reported high satisfaction with GenAI in STEAM learning, following gradual-improvement and fluctuation patterns [~W]
  • A preliminary iPad case study confirmed the creative potential of the elderly and tablets' potential to overcome ICT adoption challenges [+W]
  • Students using LecturaAgents reported stronger perceived understanding, assessment readiness, and future learning support than comparison-tool users [+W]
  • Large language models can support learning and teaching across education levels, from reading and writing support for elementary students to professional training [+W]
  • A struggling fifth-grade reader with ADHD gained one full grade level in reading over six weeks of iPad-mediated tutoring [~W]
  • Non-users cite lack of training, distrust of responses, preference for teaching staff, academic-integrity fear and lack of interest as reasons for not using chatbots [~W]
  • Distributing hardware without capable software produced no measurable effect on mathematics or language scores in a large randomized evaluation, as the article reports citing Cristia et al. [~M]
  • In second-language teaching for pupils with special educational needs, Pepper was associated with higher participation than digital tablets [~W]
  • Integrating scientific visualization into secondary education faces three named problems: basic scientific practices, classroom-format incompatibility, and infrastructure needs [~W]
  • Switching to an updated synchronous platform catalysed the teacher's ongoing teaching innovation [~W]
  • The main student use of GAI tools consistently in view was supporting language-usage and text production [~W]
  • Technology-supported educational interventions are associated with higher metacognitive outcomes on average (g = 0.653), with substantial between-study heterogeneity [+W]
  • A 4-week social media training workshop improved inhibitory control within the intervention group of older adults, with a large effect over the full study period [~W]
  • Students perceive GenAI tools as beneficial for academic engagement, particularly for overcoming language barriers and accessing up-to-date resources [+W]
  • Teacher use of LA for DBDM makes DLS use more effective for learning, and teacher support increases student usage [+W]
  • Year of study is only weakly positively associated with reported AI usage frequency, indicating tool normalization across cohorts [~W]
  • Educational technology interventions show positive effects on reading comprehension in elementary school [+W]
  • Synchronous and asynchronous communication technologies are commonly employed to support communication between students, industry partners and teaching staff during WIL [+W]
  • Reading comprehension is slightly worse on handheld devices than in print in between-participants studies (g = -.113) [-W]
  • Classroom tablet use is negatively associated with reading comprehension achievement in PISA 2018 data [-W]
  • Most ITS-versus-teacher studies in K-12 report a positive effect of ITSs on student performance, with medium-to-large effect sizes [+W]
  • In a randomized experiment in China, EdTech and paper workbook exercises of equal time and content had the same effect on math test scores and grades [-W]
  • Skills gained from GenAI integration vary with teaching methodology, tool choice, and assessment methods, with student performance varying by engagement level [~W]
  • ChatGPT-based training produced significantly higher ADAS activation accuracy than conventional video-and-manual training for the LKA, CA and ACC functions [+M] — attached 2026-10-27 from Murtaza et al. (2024), which proposed "ChatGPT-based interactive user manual for training drivers on ADAS and AV functions".
  • GAI tools enriched student ideation, aesthetic development and confidence in architecture and design education, especially for lower-competency learners [+W]
  • AI tool usage patterns differed by academic level [~W]
  • Engagement does not mediate the grade difference between tool users and non-users [~W]
  • In a five-participant user study, instructors found the emoji heat map saved time and helped identify interesting and difficult reading sections [+W] — attached 2026-10-28 from Blobstein et al. (2025), which proposed "Emoji heat map in NotaBene".
  • Instructors found the heat map better aligned with sections they believed students found interesting (4.4) than difficult (3.6) [+W] — attached 2026-10-28 from Blobstein et al. (2025), which proposed "Emoji heat map in NotaBene".
  • Teachers transitioned from using AI for basic routine tasks to leveraging it as a tool for innovation [~W]
  • Students using the AVDS tool scored higher on an AVL-tree performance test than controls [+W]
  • Mobile devices served as the primary tool for learners and teachers during the emergent remote teaching process of the pandemic [+W]
  • iVR users showed better synchronization of angulation and translation movements in the mobilization technique [+W]
  • Undergraduate physiotherapy students who used iVR alongside traditional teaching scored higher on overall manual therapy performance one week later [+W]
  • Effects of technology-delivered literacy instruction did not significantly differ by outcome domain, though comprehension effects appeared particularly small (g = 0.12) [~W]
  • 'Innovative' schools used more different tools per learning arrangement than 'traditional' schools [+W]
  • Prior mathematical ability moderates tablet-DGBL gains, with mixed direction across studies [~W]
  • Optimistic Advocates view ChatGPT as a time-saving tool for storyboarding, content editing, and differentiating learning experiences [+W]
  • Students perceived AI as helping their text comprehension in Greek language class [+W]
  • The GenAI-integrated group outperformed controls across course assessment components, with Cohen's d ranging from 0.65 to 0.88, and a higher proportion earning B+ or higher [+W]
  • Students in treatment schools used the Core5 digital component for an average of 78.4 minutes per week, with usage varying by grade [~W]
  • Generous IT infrastructure in learning studios led to increased digital technology use requiring teacher coordination [~W]
  • Teacher-prescribed tools dominate reading and interacting, while personal tools dominate creative uses [+W]
  • Teachers' most frequently cited barrier to classroom technology use was lack of access to technology, on both pre- and post-surveys [~W]
  • SVA daily usage and recommender reliance negatively predict verbal ability only in the younger cohort [~W]
  • EVS use showed no significant difference in student grades on a second-year Engineering mathematics module across two cohorts [~W]
  • Trails were only partially successful technologically due to device and network problems [~W]
  • Familiarity with technology gained from prior face-to-face tablet use does not significantly contribute to learning activities, cognitive activation, or effort in digital distance teaching [~W]
  • Technology was rarely used for interactive learning activities during digital distance teaching [-W]
  • New technologies and tools are emerging that cater to a broader range of genres, pedagogical settings, and approaches as technology-enhanced learning becomes more ubiquitous [+W]
  • Tablet use is positively associated with student satisfaction and engagement [+W]
  • Studies report both positive and negative effects of classroom screen use: tablets and interactive whiteboards can support learning and academic performance, but teachers report difficulty supervising children's screen activity. [~W]
  • Sensitivity testing indicates the performance effects of tablet use are robust and not subject to moderation effects [~W]

The evidence by question¶

  • Does putting instruction on a digital medium change learning by itself, or only through the method, time and teaching it carries? Media comparison studies produce uninterpretable "no significant difference" findings. [+M]
