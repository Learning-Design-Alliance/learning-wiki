# Product

## Description
A product is the concrete output of learning — an essay, prototype, solution, presentation, or performance — that makes learner competence visible and assessable. It functions as both the goal that organizes instruction and the evidence from which learning is evaluated. In task-centered designs, the product is what distinguishes completing a learning task from merely engaging with content.

## Design Implications

Defining the product up front gives learners a clear performance goal and gives instructors an authentic basis for assessment and feedback [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Products should mirror the contexts in which the knowledge will ultimately be used, so that learning transfers beyond the classroom [Authentic tasks and audiences increase engagement and transfer.](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) [+M]. Because products make thinking inspectable, they enable feedback cycles, revision, and peer critique that content-only instruction cannot support.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear specification of what a quality product looks like (rubrics, exemplars, or [Case Studies](case-studies.md))
- Tasks complex enough that the product requires integration of knowledge and skill, not recall alone
- Opportunities for feedback and revision before the product is finalized [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Alignment between the product, the learning goals, and the assessment criteria ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))

#### Constraints
- Polished products can mask weak underlying understanding; a finished artifact is not itself evidence of transferable skill [-M]
- High-production-value products (videos, apps, posters) can shift effort toward craft and away from learning goals [-M]
- Product-only assessment disadvantages learners who understand content but struggle with the production medium; offer alternatives or [Accommodations](accommodations.md) [~M]
- Novelty of format can add extraneous cognitive load for novices [Managing cognitive load is critical for novice learners.](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Learners beyond the earliest acquisition stage, who have enough foundational knowledge to produce rather than only reproduce [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Learners motivated by visible accomplishment and real audiences [Autonomy and purpose support intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Less appropriate as a first exposure for novices, who need [Practice](practice.md) with support before producing independently

### Target Learning Goals
- Application and transfer: using knowledge to create something new in a realistic context
- Integration: combining concepts, procedures, and judgment into a coherent whole
- Performance competence: demonstrating skill at the level at which it will be used professionally or civically

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the product is the artifact through which expert modeling, coaching, and articulation become concrete; apprenticeship sequences culminate in independent production
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — a product gives formative assessment something substantive to act on; feedback on drafts improves the final product and the underlying learning
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — producing an artifact is among the strongest forms of generative engagement, requiring retrieval, decision-making, and synthesis rather than recognition
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — products designed for real audiences raise the stakes and the quality of learner effort

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [4C/ID Four-Component Instructional Design](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — learning tasks are organized around whole products learners progressively produce
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the progression from modeling to independent creation of authentic artifacts
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — products serve as the demonstration of competency required to advance

## Examples

**Project-based science curricula (e.g., [Project-Based Learning / WISE](https://wise.berkeley.edu))** — Learners produce scientific explanations, models, and design solutions as the culminating product of inquiry sequences.

**[Codecademy](https://www.codecademy.com) capstone projects** — After guided exercises, learners build a working application from a specification; the running program is the product that demonstrates integrated skill.

**Design studio critiques (architecture, engineering)** — Learners produce drawings or prototypes and defend them in critique; the product anchors iterative feedback from experts and peers.
