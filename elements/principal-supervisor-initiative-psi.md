# Principal Supervisor Initiative (PSI)

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Principal Supervisor Initiative is a district-level reform program launched by The Wallace Foundation in 2014. It is described as "a four-year, $24 million effort to redefine principal supervision in six urban school districts." The initiative aimed to shift the supervisor role from administrative compliance work toward developing principals as instructional leaders, and this report evaluates how the role differed in PSI districts compared with other large urban districts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- District participation in a multi-year initiative supported by The Wallace Foundation to redefine the principal supervisor role
- District participation in redefining the principal supervisor role over the four-year initiative period
#### Constraints
- Implemented in six urban school districts over four years
- Funded as a four-year, $24 million effort by The Wallace Foundation

### Target Learners
- Principal supervisors in urban school districts
- Principals in urban school districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Developing and supporting principals to be effective instructional leaders
- Developing principals' skills as instructional leaders who foster high quality instruction and learning

## Description
The Principal Supervisor Initiative was a four-year, $24 million effort launched by The Wallace Foundation in 2014 to redefine principal supervision in six urban school districts. It aimed to help districts overhaul a position traditionally focused on administration, operations, and compliance into one dedicated to developing and supporting principals as effective instructional leaders. The report evaluates the initiative's experiences and effects on districts, supervisors, principals, and teachers.
