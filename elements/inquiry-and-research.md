# Inquiry and Research

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Inquiry and research place learners in the role of investigator: they pose or refine questions, gather and evaluate evidence, and build explanations from what they find. The element shifts the locus of knowledge construction from instructor to learner, with the instructor designing the question space, providing tools and [Scaffolding](scaffolding.md), and [Coaching](coaching.md) during the investigation.

## Design Implications

Inquiry supports deep conceptual understanding and transfer when learners have enough structure to avoid unproductive search, but pure discovery without guidance is consistently less effective than guided inquiry [Minimal guidance during inquiry produces poor learning outcomes for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [-S]. Structured prompts — question frameworks, data-organization templates, [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md) — preserve the motivational and epistemic benefits of investigation while preventing cognitive overload [Guided inquiry outperforms unguided discovery.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]. Requiring learners to explain and justify their findings converts investigation into durable understanding [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A genuine question or problem that is open enough to investigate but bounded enough to be tractable
- Access to data sources, tools, or phenomena learners can actually gather evidence from
- Structured supports: question templates, research protocols, or graphic organizers that scaffold the investigation
- Opportunities to articulate, defend, and revise findings ([Argumentation](argumentation.md), [Class Discussion](class-discussion.md))

#### Constraints
- Unguided discovery with novices produces minimal learning and can entrench misconceptions [Minimal guidance during inquiry produces poor learning outcomes for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [-S]
- Effectiveness declines sharply when learners lack domain knowledge to recognize relevant evidence [Guidance becomes more necessary as learner expertise decreases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~S]
- Open-ended research tasks can overload working memory when learners must simultaneously manage the question, the search process, and the content [Guided inquiry outperforms unguided discovery.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [-M]
- Time-intensive; poorly suited when learning goals require broad content coverage in limited time

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge, who have enough schema to evaluate evidence but still benefit from structure [Guidance becomes more necessary as learner expertise decreases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Advanced students and research-oriented learners who can sustain extended investigation with light scaffolding
- Novices only with substantial guidance — question framing, curated sources, and worked models of the inquiry process

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: building explanations of how and why phenomena work
- Epistemic and research skills: formulating questions, evaluating sources, reasoning from evidence
- Critical thinking and independent learning: self-directed investigation and revision of ideas

### Affordances
- [Inquiry-Based Learning](../principles/inquiry-based-learning.md) — inquiry and research is the operational core of this principle: the question drives the learning rather than the transmission of content
- [Constructivism](../principles/constructivism.md) — learners actively construct explanations from evidence rather than receiving them, enacting the constructivist account of knowledge building
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — investigation requires learners to generate, evaluate, and revise ideas rather than passively receive them
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — research processes can be made visible through expert modeling of how investigators formulate and refine questions

## Examples

**[Problem-Based Learning](../patterns/problem-based-learning.md)** — Learners receive an authentic problem (e.g., a patient case in medical education) and must research to identify what they need to know; the research process is the curriculum.

**[Case-Based Learning: Harvard Method](../patterns/case-based-learning-harvard-method.md)** — Students investigate a written case, gathering and weighing evidence before defending an analysis in class discussion.

**[WebQuests](https://webquest.org)** — Structured online inquiry tasks that curate sources and provide role-based question templates, exemplifying guided inquiry for novices.

**[iGEM](https://igem.org)** — International Genetically Engineered Machine competition: student teams formulate research questions and conduct original laboratory investigations, an open-inquiry format for advanced learners.
