5Essentials survey-based diagnostic tools¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A set of survey-based diagnostic tools created by designers and researchers building on the original five essential supports research. The tools "allow educators to assess the strengths and weaknesses of their own school in the five essential supports," producing results that principals, teachers, and parents use to craft an evidence-based narrative and diagnosis of their school and to develop action plans. The paper details the underlying research behind the tools, their creation, and their use in supporting school improvement.

Claims¶

  • Schools strong on clusters of the five essential supports were 10 times more likely to improve learning of reading and mathematics [+W]
  • The five essential supports matter in combination: strength on clusters of supports, not isolated domains, predicts improvement [+W]
  • Elementary schools strong in three or more of the five essential supports were ten times more likely to substantially improve student math and reading scores (attributed to Bryk et al., 2010) [+W]
  • Chicago schools strong in most of the five essential supports were about 10 times more likely to show substantial gains in reading and mathematics [+W]
