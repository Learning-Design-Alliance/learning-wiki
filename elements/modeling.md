# Modeling

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Modeling is the instructional element in which an instructor, expert, or advanced peer demonstrates expert thinking and behavior — making both the actions and the reasoning behind them visible. It functions as the observational entry point to skill acquisition: learners study a competent performance, including its decision points and self-monitoring, before producing their own attempts.

## Design Implications

Modeling provides structured exposure to expert-level reasoning, reducing the unguided search novices would otherwise face when approaching a new task [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]. Its effectiveness depends on making cognition visible: models that verbalize decisions, monitoring, and revision ("I'm stuck here, so I'll try…") outperform silent demonstrations of correct output. Modeling must be paired with [Practice](practice.md) and [Coaching](coaching.md); observation alone produces familiarity without competence [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- An accurate model of expert performance, including realistic struggle and self-correction, not just polished output
- Narration or annotation that externalizes reasoning ([Think-Aloud](think-aloud.md)), converting tacit expertise into observable steps
- A follow-on activity in which learners apply what they observed ([Practice](practice.md), [Coaching](coaching.md))
- A model whose skill level is attainable; observing far-out-of-reach experts can depress rather than raise confidence

#### Constraints
- Observation without application creates illusions of competence — learners judge modeled performance easier to reproduce than it is [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]
- Flawless expert models can intimidate learners or seem unattainable; models that show coping and error-recovery better support self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [~M]
- A single model can anchor learners to one solution path; contrasting multiple models or including [Non-Examples](non-examples.md) mitigates this
- Less useful for learners with strong prior knowledge, for whom explicit modeling is redundant [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices in skill-based domains — STEM problem solving, writing, clinical and vocational procedures — who lack a reference model of expert performance [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners with low self-efficacy, for whom a coping model (one who struggles and succeeds) is more beneficial than a mastery model
- Less beneficial for experts, who experience modeling as redundant [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill acquisition: seeing the sequence and conditions of expert actions
- Metacognitive strategy learning: observing how experts monitor, evaluate, and revise their own thinking
- Schema formation: building a mental model of task structure before independent problem solving
- Attitudinal and behavioral outcomes: social learning of norms, professional conduct, and persistence

### Affordances
- [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) — modeling is the core mechanism of observational learning; attention, retention, reproduction, and motivation are all driven by observing a model
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — modeling is the first phase of the sequence (modeling → coaching → fading), making expert processes visible before learners take over
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — a worked model externalizes intermediate steps so learners attend to understanding structure rather than generating solutions from scratch
- [Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) — a worked example is modeling applied to problem solving: the expert solution, annotated with reasoning, studied before independent attempt

## Examples

**[Think-Aloud Modeling](../strategies/think-aloud-modeling.md)** — Instructor verbalizes reasoning while solving a problem or reading a text, making metacognitive moves (monitoring confusion, checking work, revising) visible to learners.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Narrated video demonstrations of step-by-step problem solving, followed by practice exercises with on-demand hints that deliver sub-models.

**[Codecademy](https://www.codecademy.com)** — Annotated code demonstrations shown inline before learners write their own version, pairing each model with immediate practice.

**[National Writing Project](https://www.nwp.org)** — Teachers write alongside students, modeling drafting and revision decisions in real time rather than only presenting finished texts.
