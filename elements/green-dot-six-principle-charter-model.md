# Green Dot Public Schools model: small community high schools built on six design principles

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Green Dot Public Schools is a nonprofit charter management organization operating more than 20 public charter middle and high schools in California, Tennessee, and Washington. The model "creates small community high schools and emphasizes a set of six principles": recruiting, training, and supporting teachers who can accelerate student learning and close achievement gaps; autonomous school leadership with personalized coaching; a college-going culture with a college preparatory curriculum; comprehensive supports including health, wellness, gang intervention, mentorship, and college and career services; parent engagement through adult education and self-advocacy training; and replicability in similar schools serving low-income students with unionized workforces.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Public funding covers day-to-day operations; turnaround transitions may require additional costs covered largely by private foundations
#### Constraints
- The model aims to be replicable in similar schools serving low-income students with unionized teacher workforces and similar funding sources

### Target Learners
- High school students in low-income urban communities
- Middle and high school charter school students

### Target Learning Goals
- College readiness and college preparatory achievement
- Closing the achievement gap
- Student progression to graduation
