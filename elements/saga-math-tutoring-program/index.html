Saga Math high-dosage tutoring program (traditional and technology-enhanced models)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 10 claims (7 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Saga Math is a supplemental in-school math tutoring program for 9th and 10th graders offered in a traditional model at 1:2 ("research-proven"), 1:3, or 1:4 tutor-to-student ratios, and a technology-enhanced model at up to 1:8 in which half the students work with the tutor while the other half use "a computer-assisted learning platform called ALEKS", switching every other session. Tutoring is embedded in the school schedule at least three times weekly for 90 minutes total, aligned to Common Core standards, with tutorial instruction informed by assessments, observations, and progress-monitoring data.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutoring must be embedded in students' regular school schedules and occur a minimum of three times per week for 90 minutes
  • An assigned staff member is required in the classroom to support students, supervise tutors, and act as teacher-of-record
  • Tutor supervisors typically meet with primary math teachers weekly to align lessons

Constraints¶

  • The program is supplemental to students' primary math class and must not take away from Tier 1 instruction, extracurricular activities, or other responsibilities

Target Learners¶

  • 9th and 10th grade students in economically disadvantaged high schools, largely Black and Latinx, including at-risk students and diverse learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Secondary mathematics achievement aligned with Common Core standards and state exams

Claims¶

  • The average effect size of Saga tutoring across three RCTs and both models is +0.14, qualifying it for a Strong evidence rating [+W]
  • The Saga blended technology-enhanced tutoring model significantly improves standardized math scores, math GPA, and reduces math course failures (+0.19) [+W]
  • Saga tutoring significantly raises standardized math achievement for at-risk 9th and 10th graders in the first Chicago RCT (ITT +0.09, TOT +0.18) [+W]
  • A second larger Chicago RCT finds significantly positive effects of Saga tutoring on end-of-year math achievement (ITT +0.14, TOT +0.40) [+W]
  • Saga tutoring produces significant improvements in math course grades and failures and persistent gains one to two years after tutoring [+W]
  • Saga blended tutoring improved student math grades across districts and tutoring models [+W]
  • Saga blended tutoring produced large positive impacts on algebra scores in one district and geometry scores in another, but no effects on two other standardized tests [~W]
  • Implementation analysis identified student engagement as a challenge for technology-driven tutoring programs [~W]
  • Saga tutoring impacts were larger in schools with fewer staffing challenges and in tutoring groups of two students or fewer [~W]
  • Saga tutoring impacts were larger for students with lower prior math achievement and for Black students [+W]
