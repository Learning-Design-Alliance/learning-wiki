# The on-track indicator: a quantitative ninth-grade measure of adequate progress toward graduation

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The on-track indicator, developed by UChicago CCSR in the late 1990s, is "a simple quantitative measure of whether ninth-graders are making adequate progress to graduation based on their credit completion and course failures." A student is on-track if she earns enough credits for tenth-grade promotion and no more than one semester F in a core course. The summary reports on-track students are almost four times more likely to graduate, and the measure was designed to be "easily calculated, monitored, and ultimately acted and improved upon."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires credit-completion and course-failure data for ninth graders that schools can monitor in real time
#### Constraints
- As printed, the definition applies to first-year high school students' credits and core-course failures (English, math, science, or social science)

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade high school students in urban districts

### Target Learning Goals
- High school graduation and course success in the ninth-grade transition
