# Transfer Tasks

## Description
A transfer task asks learners to apply what they have learned to a new problem, context, or situation that differs in surface features from the original instruction. It functions both as an assessment of whether learning is flexible rather than rote and as an instructional event that itself deepens abstraction of the underlying principle.

## Design Implications

Transfer tasks are the primary way to verify and build far transfer, since performance on near-identical problems overstates learning [Analogical reasoning supports transfer to novel contexts.](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [+M]. Effective transfer tasks vary surface features while preserving deep structure, forcing learners to abstract the principle rather than memorize the procedure. Comparing multiple cases that share structure but differ in context improves both learning and transfer [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+S], and encountering the same concept across varied contexts builds the flexible representations transfer requires [Cognitive flexibility theory prescribes multiple representations across cases.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A target principle or schema that is genuinely invariant across the training and transfer contexts
- Sufficient initial instruction or worked examples so learners have something to transfer ([Practice](practice.md), [Demonstration](demonstration.md))
- Deliberate variation in surface features between instruction and the transfer task
- Prompts that direct attention to structural similarity, not just a new cover story ([Analogies](analogies.md))

#### Constraints
- Transfer fails when learners encode knowledge tied to a single context; instruction in one exemplar format produces poor far transfer [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [-S]
- Unsupported transfer tasks overload novices, who lack the schemas to bridge the gap between instruction and the novel problem [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S] — transfer tasks for novices need scaffolds or faded support
- Learners frequently fail to spontaneously retrieve relevant prior knowledge; without an activation prompt or analogy cue, they treat the new problem as unrelated [Analogical reasoning supports transfer to novel contexts.](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [~M]
- Transfer tasks are poor initial instruction — they assess or consolidate, but cannot substitute for building the schema first

### Target Learners
- Learners who have already achieved basic competence and need to generalize beyond the taught examples
- Intermediate learners benefit most from comparing multiple cases before attempting transfer [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Novices need heavily scaffolded or near-transfer versions first; unsupported far-transfer tasks overwhelm them [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Far transfer: applying principles to structurally similar but superficially different problems
- Conceptual understanding: distinguishing deep structure from surface features
- Adaptive expertise: preparing learners for problems not covered in instruction

### Affordances
- [Analogical Reasoning](../principles/analogical-reasoning.md) — a transfer task enacts this principle by requiring learners to map a known solution structure onto a new situation; well-designed tasks make the mapping the object of learning rather than an incidental byproduct
- [Cognitive Flexibility](../principles/cognitive-flexibility.md) — sequencing multiple transfer tasks across varied contexts enacts this principle by preventing the formation of single-context knowledge representations
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — transfer tasks require generative application rather than recognition or recall, producing the retrieval and elaboration that strengthen schemas
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — transfer tasks set in realistic contexts give learners a purpose for applying knowledge beyond "the teacher asked"

## Related Elements
- [Practice](practice.md) — transfer tasks are the most demanding form of practice; they follow, not replace, skill-building practice
- [Case Studies](case-studies.md) — multiple cases with shared structure are the raw material for building transferable schemas
- [Analogies](analogies.md) — explicit analogy cues help learners retrieve relevant knowledge during transfer
- [Fading](fading.md) — support on transfer tasks should fade as learners gain competence
- [Application of Knowledge](application-of-knowledge.md) — the broader element family; transfer tasks are the variant emphasizing novelty of context

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Cognitive Flexibility Theory](../patterns/cognitive-flexibility-theory.md) — ill-structured domains are taught through criss-crossing cases and transfer demands
- [4C/ID](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — learning tasks sequenced from simple to complex with increasing context variation
- [Authentic Assessment](../patterns/authentic-assessment.md) — transfer tasks double as assessments of applied competence
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — successive cases function as near-transfer opportunities [Case-based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]

## Examples

**[Comparing Cases](../strategies/comparing_cases.md)** — Learners study two worked examples with identical structure but different surface features, then solve a third novel problem; the comparison prompts abstraction of the shared principle.

**[PBL (Problem-Based Learning)](https://www.maa.org/external_archive/joma/Volume7/pbl/index.html)** — Medical and engineering curricula present novel clinical or design problems that require applying previously learned concepts to unfamiliar presentations.

**[Bransford & Schwartz's "Preparation for Future Learning"](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.1.60)** — Contrasting-case activities that precede instruction measurably improve learners' ability to transfer to new problems.

**[Khan Academy Mastery System](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Spaced, varied problem sets that re-present a skill in new contexts after initial practice, functioning as near-transfer tasks.
