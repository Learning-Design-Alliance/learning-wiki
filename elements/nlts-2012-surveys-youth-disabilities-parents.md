# National Longitudinal Transition Study 2012 (NLTS 2012) surveys of secondary-school youth with disabilities and their parents

## Description
NLTS 2012 is a national study "conducted as part of an assessment of IDEA," whose surveys of secondary school youth with disabilities and their parents were collected in 2012 and compared with earlier waves back to 2003. The brief summarizes its findings on disadvantage, academic struggle, transition activities, and disability-group risk profiles. It ran under an associated project time frame of 2010-2018 for the U.S. Department of Education's Institute of Education Sciences.

## Claims

- [From 2003 to 2012, youth with disabilities showed greater school engagement and support use but became less likely to participate in some key transition activities](../claims/engagement-up-transition-activities-down-2003-2012.md) [+M]
- [Among 2012 disability groups, youth with intellectual disability, autism, deaf-blindness, multiple disabilities, and orthopedic impairments were most at-risk in preparation for life after high school](../claims/five-disability-groups-most-at-risk-transition-preparation.md) [+M]
- [In 2012, youth with disabilities were more likely than other students to struggle academically and less likely to take steps to obtain postsecondary education and jobs](../claims/youth-disabilities-academic-struggle-fewer-postsecondary-steps.md) [+M]
- [In 2012, youth with disabilities were more socioeconomically disadvantaged than their peers, more likely to live in low-income households and with parents receiving federal food benefits](../claims/youth-disabilities-more-socioeconomically-disadvantaged-2012.md) [+M]
- [NLTS 2012 is the third in a series of federally sponsored studies of youth with disabilities under IDEA](../claims/nlts-2012-third-in-series-idea-youth.md) [+W]
- [English learner students with disabilities are less likely than other students with disabilities to receive extra time on schoolwork but attend transition-planning meetings at a comparable rate, are suspended less, and have parents with higher expectations](../claims/elswd-accommodations-transition-planning-suspension-parental-expectations.md) [+W]
