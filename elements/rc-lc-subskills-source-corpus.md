# Cross-compared corpus of subskills from applied linguistics theory, nine international EFL/ESL proficiency tests, and five coursebook series

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study assembles its evidence base from three source types: subskills named in theoretical and empirical applied linguistics papers; the manuals of nine internationally popular EFL/ESL tests, including "Test of English as a Foreign Language – Internet-Based Test (TOEFL IBT)" and IELTS; and reading and listening tasks from five widely used coursebook series (New English File, Face2Face, New Inside Out, Outcomes, Language Leader). Only tasks under reading and listening sections were checked; pronunciation or grammar sections were excluded. Items were tallied, cross-checked, and repetitions eliminated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Only tasks under reading and listening sections of the coursebooks were included; tasks under other sections such as pronunciation or grammar were not part of the list.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the study's limitation that subskills in diagnostic tests could have been investigated as well.

### Target Learners
- EFL/ESL learners at universities using the five coursebook series

### Target Learning Goals
- Second-language reading and listening comprehension
