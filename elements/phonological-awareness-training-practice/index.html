Phonological Awareness Training practice (rhyme detection, blending, and segmentation activities for young children)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Phonological Awareness Training is a general practice aimed at enhancing young children's phonological awareness, defined as "the ability to detect or manipulate the sounds in words independent of meaning." It can involve activities teaching children to "identify, detect, delete, segment, or blend segments of spoken words" or to detect, identify, or produce rhyme or alliteration. Practices can be used by teachers with individual children, in pairs, or in small groups, generally as a supplement to the regular classroom curriculum. No single developer exists; the interventions in the reviewed studies were developed by the study authors.

Claims¶

  • Training effects were larger on outcome measures aligned with the trained skill (blending, segmenting) than on less aligned measures [+W]
  • Phonemic segmentation training favored over rhyme/alliteration training on print knowledge, phonological processing, and early reading/writing (Yeh 2003), with substantively important but non-significant differences [~W]
  • Phonological Awareness Training has positive effects on phonological processing in preschool children, averaging +27 percentile points [+W]
  • Four of six standards-meeting studies showed statistically significant positive effects and two showed substantively important positive effects on phonological processing [+W]
  • Sound box training favored over sound sort training on phonological processing (Maslanka & Joseph 2002), with substantively important but non-significant differences averaging +11 percentile points [+W]
