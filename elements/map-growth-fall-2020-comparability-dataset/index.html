Fall 2020 MAP Growth remote-versus-in-person comparability dataset (over 535,000 K-8 students in 147 districts)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The analysis draws on test scores from "over 535,000 K-8 students in 147 school districts", of which 92 operated fully remotely in fall 2020 and 55 offered in-person instruction to all students. This large observational dataset underpins the brief's reliability, engagement, and score-trend comparability findings for remotely and in-person administered MAP Growth assessments.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires fall 2020 MAP Growth test score records tagged by administration mode (remote or in-person) and by district instructional mode.

Constraints¶

  • Districts analyzed were those operating fully remotely or offering in-person instruction to all students; partially hybrid districts are not described in the abstract.

Target Learners¶

  • K-8 students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring achievement and growth in reading and mathematics via MAP Growth assessments

Claims¶

  • Fall 2020 MAP Growth tests showed high levels of marginal reliability across all grades in both remote and in-person administration [+M]
  • Test engagement was high across all grades on fall 2020 MAP Growth tests in both administration modes [+M]
  • Remote and in-person MAP Growth test scores showed consistent trends for students in grades 3-8 in fall 2020 [+M]
  • Remote and in-person fall 2020 MAP Growth tests show comparable psychometric characteristics and indicators of test quality [+M]
  • Findings of reliability, engagement, and consistent score trends increase confidence in the quality of remotely administered MAP Growth data in grades 3 and up [+M]
  • The dashboard primarily focuses on grades 3 through 8 because remote and in-person MAP Growth results are largely comparable for grades 3 and up [+M]
