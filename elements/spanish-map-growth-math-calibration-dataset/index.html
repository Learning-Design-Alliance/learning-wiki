Spanish MAP Growth Math calibration dataset of 3,908 items and over 147,000 monolingual and 33,000 bilingual students¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's empirical basis is item and person data pulled from the NWEA Growth Research Database (GRD) covering five years of Spanish math testing. The item side comprises "3,908 Spanish math items across different tests" in the selected sample (3,955 collected), spanning 24 Spanish math tests from K-2 through 6+. The person side comprises 147,094 collected monolingual students (147,044 selected) and 33,624 collected bilingual students (33,607 selected), about 75% Hispanic across grades. The dataset supports the fixed-person-score calibration designs and all item and person results in the report.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student Spanish math test responses from the NWEA Growth Research Database, with English scores available for the bilingual calibration design

Constraints¶

  • Item selection filtered to an absolute old-new RIT difference of 20 RITs or less; person selection filtered to an absolute old-new score difference of 5 RITs or less

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students taking Spanish MAP Growth Math tests

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mathematics achievement measurement in Spanish

Claims¶

  • Adaptive testing explains why about one third of calibrated Spanish math items carry calibration status 10 [+W]
  • Outlier rates in Spanish item calibration are extremely low, which the authors take as evidence of very high calibration quality [+W]
  • The Spanish math test population is predominantly Hispanic (about 78%), unlike the English math test population (about 15%), with implications for Spanish norms [+W]
  • Old (English-borrowed) and newly calibrated Spanish item RITs differ negligibly, with correlations of 0.98 and no statistically significant paired t-test differences [+W]
  • Including or excluding calibration status 10 items in scoring has a negligible impact on Spanish student RIT scores for monolingual and bilingual students [+W]
  • Rescoring with new item parameters changed average ability estimates negligibly, with new and original scores correlated above 0.99 [+W]
  • DIF study finds most items in category A, with C DIF rare (~1%) except for the Native English/Bilingual group (6.66%) [+W]
