# Accommodations

## Description
Accommodations are changes to *how* students access instruction and demonstrate learning — extended time, alternative formats, assistive technology, preferential seating, chunked assignments — that level the playing field without altering the construct being assessed or the learning goal. They are distinguished from *modifications*, which change what is taught or expected. Accommodations function as targeted barrier removal: the designer identifies where a fixed presentation or response format disadvantages a learner and provides an alternate route to the same outcome.

## Design Implications

Accommodations improve access and performance for learners whose needs conflict with a default format, most robustly when the accommodation matches a documented barrier rather than being applied generically [+M]. Because many accommodations (captioning, plain language, flexible formats) benefit far more learners than those formally designated, designing them in from the start — the logic of Universal Design for Learning — is more efficient than retrofitting [+M]. Accommodations should preserve the target construct: extended time on a fluency measure, for example, invalidates what that measure is intended to assess [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of the actual barrier (decoding, processing speed, working memory, sensory access, attention) so the accommodation targets it directly
- Alignment between the accommodation and the construct being taught or assessed — the barrier removed must not be the skill being measured
- Consistent implementation across settings; an accommodation used only in one class or one test has attenuated effects [~W]
- Learner training in using the accommodation; untrained or unused accommodations show little benefit [~M]

#### Constraints
- Poorly matched accommodations can reduce validity of assessment — e.g., read-aloud on a decoding assessment measures the accommodation, not the skill [-M]
- Over-accommodation can suppress development of the targeted skill; supports that remove productive struggle may undercut growth if never faded [~M] (see [Fading](fading.md) if available in your element set)
- Accommodations applied without matching the learner's specific barrier show weak or null effects [-W]
- Stigmatizing delivery (visibly separate arrangements) can reduce uptake and belonging [-M]

### Target Learners
- Students with documented disabilities under IEP/504 plans — extended time is the most common and shows moderate positive effects on achievement for these students [+M]
- English language learners, for whom linguistic accommodations (glossaries, plain syntax — see [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)) reduce language load without changing content demands [+M]
- Struggling readers and students with working-memory constraints, who benefit from chunking and format changes [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Not limited to diagnosed learners: captioning and structured notes benefit most of the class [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- All goals — accommodations are goal-neutral by definition; they change the route, not the destination
- Equitable demonstration of mastery on [Assessment](assessment.md) tasks
- Sustained engagement and persistence for learners otherwise excluded by format barriers

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — accommodations such as chunked text, reduced clutter, and audio support manage extraneous load for learners with processing constraints [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — explicit organization, advance cues, and predictable formats function as accommodations for learners with attention or executive-function needs
- [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) — plain-language rewrites of instructions are a linguistic accommodation that preserves content demands
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — accommodations keep formative assessment interpretable by ensuring performance reflects the target skill rather than the access barrier

## Examples
- **Extended time on assessments** — typically 1.5× time for students with documented processing-speed or attention disabilities; the most frequently implemented accommodation in K–12 and higher education
- **Speech-to-text and screen readers** (e.g., built-in tools in [Microsoft Immersive Reader](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/immersive-reader), [Kurzweil 3000](https://www.kurzweil3000.com)) — response- and presentation-format accommodations for writing and reading barriers
- **Captioned video** — required for accessibility, but research shows captions improve comprehension and recall for learners generally [+M]
- **Preferential seating and reduced-distraction testing environments** — environmental accommodations for attention-related barriers
