# MAP (Measures of Academic Progress) computerised adaptive test system

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
MAP is a computerised adaptive assessment system published by NWEA since 1976, covering reading, language usage, mathematics and science. "MAP items are calibrated with the Rasch model (Rasch, 1961), and all MAP tests are vertically scaled", and the article reports that "the marginal reliabilities of test scores are consistently in the low- to mid- 0.90s range across grades, tests and states". Tests use fixed-length CAT with state-customised item pools organised into content-standard goals, and goal scores serve as the observed indicators in this study's MLGM analyses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State-customised item pools aligned with each state's content standards, with test algorithms surveying the pool within each goal or strand level to assure content domain coverage
#### Constraints
- Off-grade items are used for on-grade assessment for growth measurement, unlike NCLB proficiency reporting
- The ratio of test length to item bank size is about 1:50, producing very sparse data with roughly 98% missingness at the item level

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in grades 3-10 across US states

### Target Learning Goals
- Formative and interim measurement of achievement status and individual student growth in reading and mathematics
