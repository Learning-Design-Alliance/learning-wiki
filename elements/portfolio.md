# Portfolio

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A portfolio is a purposeful collection of learner work — artifacts, drafts, and reflections — assembled over time to document progress, demonstrate competence, and support self-evaluation. Unlike point-in-time [assessment](assessment.md), it captures process as well as product, and the learner typically participates in selecting and annotating what is included.

## Design Implications

Portfolios support learning primarily by requiring learners to review, evaluate, and justify their own work, which promotes self-regulation and metacognitive monitoring [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]. Their assessment value depends on clear criteria: without explicit rubrics and defined purposes, portfolio evaluation becomes unreliable and the collection becomes an archive rather than an argument [Portfolio assessment shows weaker reliability than structured assessments when scoring criteria are vague.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [~M]. Reflection prompts attached to each artifact are what convert a folder of work into a learning event.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear purpose (learning, assessment, or showcase) stated up front, since purpose drives selection criteria
- Explicit evaluation rubrics shared with learners before collection begins
- Structured reflection prompts (e.g., "Why did you choose this piece? What would you revise?") attached to each artifact
- Regular intervals for curation — portfolios assembled only at the end of a course lose their formative function

#### Constraints
- Scoring reliability is poor without analytic rubrics and rater training; holistic portfolio grades show low inter-rater agreement [-M]
- Heavy curation and reflection workload can displace time from the domain learning itself, particularly for younger learners [-W]
- In high-stakes contexts, portfolios invite presentation effects — learners select for appearance rather than authentic evidence of growth [-M]
- Weak fit for learning goals that are narrowly procedural or knowledge-recall based, where direct measures are more efficient

### Target Learners
- Learners developing self-regulation, who benefit from structured opportunities to evaluate their own work against criteria [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]
- Advanced and graduate learners producing complex, multi-draft work (writing, design, teaching, clinical practice)
- Less suitable for novices who lack the criteria knowledge to curate meaningfully without heavy scaffolding [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-assessment and metacognitive judgment: comparing one's work against standards
- Process documentation: showing development, revision, and reasoning over time
- Complex, authentic competencies that cannot be captured by a single test (writing, design, professional practice)

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — portfolios enact this principle by making the learner an active participant in evaluation: selecting artifacts and writing reflections generates feedback information about one's own growth rather than merely receiving a grade
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — a portfolio is assembled *for someone* (a committee, employer, exhibition), which gives curation and polish an authentic rhetorical purpose beyond the grade
- [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) — portfolios allow demonstration of competence through multiple, varied artifacts over time rather than a single snapshot, accommodating different pathways to the same standard

## Related Elements
- [Assessment](assessment.md) — portfolios are one assessment format; they need rubrics and scoring procedures to function as evaluation
- [Coaching](coaching.md) — instructor or mentor feedback on portfolio drafts sustains the iterative improvement the format is designed to capture
- [Articulation](articulation.md) — the reflective annotations learners write are an articulation task, making tacit reasoning explicit

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — portfolios serve as evidence collections demonstrating mastery of each competency
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — portfolios support the articulation and reflection phases by documenting the learner's evolving practice

## Examples

**[Portfolio Assessment Strategy](../strategies/portfolio-assessment.md)** — Learners select work samples across a term, write reflective rationales for each, and present the collection at a final conference.

**[Pathbrite / Portfolium](https://www.portfolium.com)** — An e-portfolio platform used by universities for program-level assessment and career showcase, with rubric-linked artifact evaluation.

**[National Board Certification](https://www.nbpts.org)** — Professional teaching certification built substantially on portfolio evidence: videoed instruction, student work samples, and written reflective commentary scored against published standards.

**[Seesaw](https://web.seesaw.me)** — K–12 digital portfolio app where students upload work and record audio/video reflections; teachers and families comment, creating an ongoing feedback loop.
