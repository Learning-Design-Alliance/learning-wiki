# Embodied Choreographic Assessment

## Description
Learners work in small groups to create a dance or movement sequence that represents a concept they have been studying (e.g., resonance, energy transfer). The task unfolds in cycles: collaborative brainstorming and initial choreography, a first sharing with performer reflection on what the dance represents (and misrepresents or omits), structured peer feedback, revision, and a final sharing. The task treats movement as a legitimate epistemic tool: learners must translate an abstract concept into kinetic and narrative form, make representational choices, and defend those choices through explanation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A concept abstract or dynamic enough to be usefully represented through movement/metaphor (e.g., a process, a relationship, a force)
- Structured peer feedback focused on noticing what works, asking clarifying questions, and suggesting refinements — not just evaluation
- Multiple iteration cycles, not a single one-shot performance
#### Constraints
- Evidence for this technique comes from a single qualitative study in informal, arts-integrated community settings — [Multimodal, culturally grounded assessment supports engagement and agency](../claims/multimodal-culturally-grounded-assessment-supports-engagement-and-agency.md) [~W]

### Target Learners
- Learners in settings where movement, music, and creative expression are already valued cultural resources

### Target Learning Goals
- Demonstrating and refining conceptual understanding through representational, non-verbal means
- Practicing justification: explaining why a given choreographic choice represents the concept accurately
