# Text underlining and annotating

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A study aid involving marking text to indicate importance. Underlining (or highlighting) designates important information within the text; annotating adds marginal notes or written responses that go beyond selection to interpretation. The two differ in cognitive depth: underlining is a selection activity, while annotating is a generative one.

## Design Implications

Underlining alone is one of the weakest studied techniques for durable learning; Dunlosky et al. rate it low utility because it rarely improves performance relative to simply reading [Highlighting and underlining show minimal benefit over reading alone across most studies.](../claims/experimenter-underlining-effective-as-student-underlining.md) [~M]. Its value depends on *what* gets marked: learners who underline high-relevant content perform better than those who mark low-relevant content, and many students — especially novices — select poorly [Nist & Hogrebe, 1985](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED265520) [~M]. Annotating, which requires learners to paraphrase, question, or connect ideas, engages generative processing and produces stronger comprehension than passive marking [Chi & Wylie, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0033173) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Text of appropriate difficulty; learners must be able to judge relevance to mark selectively
- Training or modeling in what to mark (topic sentences, definitions, key claims) — untrained students tend to over-mark or mark isolated facts
- For annotating: prompts or sentence starters that push beyond copying ("Why does this matter?", "This connects to…")
- A follow-up use: markings only help if the learner later reviews or transforms them into summaries, outlines, or self-test questions

#### Constraints
- Underlining can create an illusion of fluency — marked text *feels* learned, reducing later study effort [Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) [-M]
- Experimenter-generated markings reduce the number of decisions a reader makes regarding importance, which can remove the very processing that makes marking useful [Experimenter-provided underlining is at least as effective as student-generated underlining, likely because students select poorly.](../claims/experimenter-underlining-effective-as-student-underlining.md) [~M]
- Over-marking (more than roughly 10–20% of text) eliminates the selection benefit entirely, since everything becomes "important"
- Underlining isolated facts encourages verbatim memory of fragments rather than understanding of structure
- Less effective for learners with weak prior knowledge, who cannot yet distinguish central from peripheral content

### Target Learners
- College students and advanced readers who have enough domain knowledge to judge relevance [Nist & Hogrebe, 1985](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED265520) [~M]
- Novices benefit more from *provided* markings or [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md) than from generating their own
- Students trained in selective marking strategies outperform untrained peers

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of key facts and definitions for later test performance
- Reading comprehension monitoring — annotations externalize a reader's evolving understanding
- Preparation for deeper study activities (summarizing, outlining, self-testing)

### Affordances
- [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — this element is the concrete enactment of the annotating principle; marginal notes convert reading from passive reception into generative processing
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — annotation is a low-cost active-reading move; per the ICAP framework, generative marking (constructive) outperforms passive reading [Chi & Wylie, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0033173)
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — marking boundaries between ideas segments text into reviewable units, reducing the load of re-reading long passages
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — consistent marking conventions (e.g., underline = claim, margin note = question) make a text's argumentative structure visible on re-reading

## Related Elements
- [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md) — provide the relevance framework novices need before marking selectively
- [Summarization](../strategies/summarization.md) — the natural next step; annotations become the raw material for summaries
- [Practice Testing](practice-testing.md) — a more effective use of the same review time; annotations can be converted into self-test questions
- [Graphic Organizers](graphic-organizers.md) — externalize text structure the way annotations externalize reader response

## Examples
- **[Headings and Highlight Strategy](../strategies/headings-and-highlight-strategy-variant.md)** — learners use headings to predict content, then highlight only text that answers their predictions, constraining over-marking
- **SQ3R (Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review)** — classic study system in which marking during reading feeds a recitation/review cycle rather than substituting for it
- **Perusall (https://www.perusall.com)** — social annotation platform where students comment on shared readings; converts private marking into visible, discussable generative processing
- **Hypothesis (https://web.hypothes.is)** — open web annotation tool used in college courses for collaborative marginal commentary
