# Chicago Public Schools Summer Bridge program

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (3 for, 5 mixed, 3 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
Summer Bridge is a large mandatory summer school program required for Chicago third, sixth, and eighth graders who fail ITBS promotional test-score cutoffs, with over 21,000 students required to attend each year from 1997 to 2000. Third and sixth graders attended "three hours per day for six weeks—a total of 90 hours of instruction," and eighth graders attended four hours daily for seven weeks (140 hours). It is distinguished by small classes of about 16 students, a prescribed centrally developed curriculum aligned to the ITBS with daily lesson plans and materials provided, and a mandatory high-stakes design with retesting at summer's end.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have failed to meet the promotional test-score cutoffs on the ITBS in reading or mathematics in spring
#### Constraints
- The policy excludes students in special education and students who have been in bilingual education four years or fewer

### Target Learners
- Low-achieving third, sixth, and eighth graders in the Chicago Public Schools who failed promotional test-score cutoffs

### Target Learning Goals
- Remediation of reading and mathematics skills measured by the Iowa Tests of Basic Skills and meeting promotion cutoffs
