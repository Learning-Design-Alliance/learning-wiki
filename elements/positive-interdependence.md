# Positive Interdependence

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Positive interdependence is the structural condition — deliberately designed into a task, reward, role, or resource arrangement — under which group members perceive that they cannot succeed unless every member succeeds. It is the defining feature distinguishing cooperative learning from mere group work: it converts a collection of individuals into a genuine team with a shared fate.

## Design Implications

Positive interdependence is the mechanism through which cooperative structures outperform both individualistic and competitive arrangements for achievement and peer relationships [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. It works by making helping, explaining, and monitoring mutually rational: when outcomes are linked, peer tutoring and elaborated discussion emerge without instructor prompting. Interdependence must be paired with individual accountability — each member's contribution must remain visible and assessable — or stronger students will carry the work and weaker students will free-ride [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared goal that requires multiple contributions (one product, one score, one performance)
- Complementary roles or divided resources so no member can complete the task alone ([Assigned Positions](assigned-positions.md))
- Individual accountability: individual quizzes, random member reporting, or signed responsibility agreements
- Group processing time — brief structured reflection on how well the team worked together

#### Constraints
- Without individual accountability, interdependence produces social loafing and free-riding, especially in larger groups [-S]
- Task-level interdependence alone (shared goal, no reward linkage) is weaker than combined goal and reward interdependence [~M]
- Low-cohesion or newly formed groups need explicit team-building before interdependence feels binding; imposed interdependence on hostile groups can increase conflict [-W]
- For simple, low-divisibility tasks, structuring interdependence adds coordination overhead with little learning payoff [-W]

### Target Learners
- Learners across ages, with the strongest documented effects in elementary through undergraduate settings [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- Lower-achieving students, who benefit from peer explanation; higher-achieving students also gain through the tutor benefit of explaining [Learning By Teaching Improves Tutor Learning](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-tutor-learning.md) [+M]
- Less suited to learners who need private processing time or who experience social anxiety in evaluative group settings [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding through elaborated peer explanation
- Collaborative and interpersonal skills as explicit outcomes
- Transferable discussion, negotiation, and shared-regulation skills

### Affordances
- [Cooperative Learning](../principles/cooperative-learning.md) — positive interdependence is the first and load-bearing principle of the Johnsons' five-element model; without it the remaining elements (accountability, interaction, skills, processing) have nothing to operate on
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — interdependence supplies the structural motivation that collaborative talk requires; unstructured collaboration relies on goodwill, interdependence relies on design
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — linked outcomes force every member into verbal, participatory engagement rather than passive presence in a group
- [Self-Determination Theory](../principles/self-determination-theory.md) — shared goals support the need for relatedness, and well-designed role structures preserve autonomy within the team

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) — the defining element of the Johnson & Johnson model
- [Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md) — supplies the outcome linkage that distinguishes collaboration from parallel individual work
- [Jigsaw](../patterns/jigsaw.md) — resource interdependence in its purest form: each member holds a unique piece

## Examples

**[Jigsaw Classroom](https://www.jigsaw.org)** — Aronson's original design: each student masters a unique segment and must teach it to teammates, making resources and outcomes inseparable.

**[PALS (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies)](https://intensiveintervention.org/intervention-instructional-content/pals-peer-assisted-learning-strategies)** — Structured reciprocal peer tutoring in reading and math with team scores that depend on both partners' improvement.

**Team-based learning (TBL)** — Michaelsen's Readiness Assurance process: teams submit a single answer sheet and receive immediate group feedback, with individual and team scores both counting.
