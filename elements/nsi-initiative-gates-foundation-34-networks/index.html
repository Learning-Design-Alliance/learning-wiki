Networks for School Improvement (NSI) initiative¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Networks for School Improvement (NSI) initiative was a Gates Foundation investment in network-based continuous improvement from 2018 to 2025. The foundation awarded "more than $300 million to intermediary organizations that led 34 Networks for School Improvement (NSI) across the country." Networks were organized around grade-level and college-readiness goals including 8th-grade on-track, 9th-grade on-track, college-ready on-track, and postsecondary enrollment. This report evaluates student outcomes through schools' fourth year of participation.

Claims¶

  • 8th-grade on-track NSI positively impacted math test scores, moving students from the 37th to the 40th percentile [+W]
  • 8th-grade on-track NSI did not improve ELA test scores, attendance, or suspension rates [-W]
  • 9th-grade on-track NSI improved GPA by about 0.11 points on a 4-point scale [+W]
  • 9th-grade on-track NSI improved course pass rates by 3 to 4 percentage points but did not reduce suspension rates [+W]
  • College-ready on-track NSI did not impact GPA, advanced course taking, math and ELA test scores, graduation, or college enrollment [-W]
  • Well-matched postsecondary enrollment NSI increased FAFSA completion by 3 to 5 percentage points but did not increase college enrollment rates [+W]
  • After schools' second year, the well-matched postsecondary enrollment NSI positively impacted FAFSA completion but not college enrollment [+W]
