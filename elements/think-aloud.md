# Think-Aloud

## Description
Think-aloud is the element in which an instructor, peer, or learner verbalizes reasoning while performing a task. It makes otherwise hidden judgment, strategy, and error-checking processes visible so learners can observe not just what to do, but how to decide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A meaningful task**: There needs to be a visible decision process worth narrating.
- **Reasoning visibility**: The speaker should explain choices, checks, and uncertainties, not just steps.
- **Connection to later learner use**: Learners should apply, imitate, or critique the reasoning afterward.
#### Constraints
- **Expert blind spots**: Skilled performers may omit tacit decisions unless prompted.
- **Cognitive overload**: Dense narration can overwhelm novices if too many ideas are packed together.

### Target Learners
- Learners who need help seeing how experts monitor, choose, and revise during performance.

### Target Learning Goals
- Surface strategies, criteria, and self-monitoring moves.

## Examples
- An instructor solves a math problem while narrating why each step is chosen.
- A reader pauses during annotation to explain how they infer meaning from a difficult sentence.
