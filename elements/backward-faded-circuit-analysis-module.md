# Backward-faded worked-example computer module for series and parallel circuit analysis

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 4 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
A Director MX multimedia module that taught resistance calculation for parallel and series circuits through eight problems (two sets of four), each with three labeled solution steps revealed one at a time. "The first problem in each of the sets of four problems was fully solved (worked), whereas in the subsequent problems the worked steps were backward faded", so learners anticipated the last step, then the last two, then all three. Incorrect anticipations triggered textual or pictorial prompts under self- or external regulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners work through the linear module in a computer lab, with two attempts per missing step and correct solutions displayed afterward
#### Constraints
- Navigation was linear; participants could not return to previous steps and problems once they finalized their answers

### Target Learners
- High school students in grades eight through twelve without prior circuit-analysis instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Calculating total resistance of series and parallel circuits from basic principles including Ohm's Law
