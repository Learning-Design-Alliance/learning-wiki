Teacher Advancement Program (TAP): a whole-school teacher evaluation, compensation, and professional development model¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Teacher Advancement Program (TAP) is described as "a whole-school approach to evaluating and compensating teachers and providing professional development opportunities". Its stated aims are twofold: "to both improve teaching and help schools attract and retain good teachers". The program operates at the whole-school level rather than targeting individual teachers in isolation.

Claims¶

  • Chicago TAP increased the amount of mentoring, promotion opportunities, and compensation in participating schools relative to non-TAP schools [+W]
  • Educators were largely receptive to evaluation and compensation strategies, with 65 to 84 percent satisfied with feedback and pay-for-performance improving some job satisfaction [+W]
  • Chicago TAP did not fully implement its performance-based pay or value-added components as intended [~W]
  • Chicago TAP did not raise student math or reading scores overall during the four-year rollout in Chicago Public Schools [-W]
