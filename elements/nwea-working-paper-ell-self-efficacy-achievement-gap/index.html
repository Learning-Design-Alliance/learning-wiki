Description¶

An associated NWEA working paper titled "English Language Learners, self-efficacy, and the achievement gap: understanding the relationship between academic and social-emotional growth". The webinar page lists it under "Associated Research" as the extended research product accompanying the presentation, framing its focus as the link between academic and social-emotional growth for ELLs.

Claims¶

  • Self-efficacy is related to the achievement gap for English Language Learners [+W]
  • A companion working paper examines the relationship between academic and social-emotional growth for English Language Learners [+W]
  • ELLs start middle school roughly .6 SD lower in math and .65 SD lower in reading, with faster reading growth (.13 SD) but no faster math or self-efficacy growth [+M]
  • Fifth-grade self-efficacy is associated with later math and reading growth (.15 and .19 standardized units per SD), mediating ELL status's indirect negative association with growth [+M]
  • Self-efficacy declines steadily for all students between 5th and 8th grade even while math and reading achievement grow [+M]
  • Non-ELLs outscore ELLs in math, reading, and self-efficacy at every timepoint, with a 2015 self-efficacy gap exceeding .35 standard deviations [+M]
  • Longitudinal partial strong measurement invariance of the four-item self-efficacy survey justifies fitting growth models to its scores [+M]
  • Self-efficacy is related to the persistence of achievement gaps for English Language Learners [+W]
