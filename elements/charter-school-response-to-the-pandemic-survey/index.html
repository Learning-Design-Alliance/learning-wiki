Charter School Response to the Pandemic Survey¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A two-wave survey instrument developed by CREDO to capture how charter school leaders responded to COVID-19 closures. It "covered two time periods: March–June 2020 and the 2020–21 school year," was distributed to all New York charter schools in summer 2020 and to California and Washington schools in spring 2021, and used fixed-response and Likert-scale items analyzed at macro and micro levels. Five hundred twenty-four surveys were completed, representing schools educating approximately 230,000 students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School leader respondents able to report on both the spring 2020 closure period and the 2020-21 school year

Constraints¶

  • Response rates ranged from 100 percent in Washington to 21 percent in California, so California and New York findings represent responding schools only and should be generalized with caution

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 charter school students in California, New York and Washington State

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Continuity of instruction and student learning during pandemic school closures

Claims¶

  • Nearly 80 percent of surveyed charter schools operated fully remotely in April 2020, falling to about half by the 2020-21 school year [+W]
  • Nearly all surveyed charter schools provided remote-learning professional development to teachers, compared with under half of district reopening plans [+W]
  • Surveyed charter schools transitioned to remote education delivery in less than a week after physical closure in March 2020, averaging 3.5 days [+W]
  • Schools reported that about 40 percent of their students experienced considerable or major academic losses since March 2020 [+W]
  • Schools reported covering 86 percent of the ELA and 85 percent of the math curriculum in 2019-20, with foreign language and fine arts least covered [+W]
  • Communication challenges were the most reported barrier to supporting English language learner students, cited by 63 percent of schools [+W]
  • Planning start time was unrelated to how quickly schools began remote instruction; schools averaged 3 school days between building closure and the start of instruction [+W]
  • During COVID building closures, New York charter schools ranked sustaining student learning and engagement as their most urgent priority, while student well-being factors ranked lowest despite serious concern [+W]
  • Most New York charter schools reported decreases in learning time across subjects during closure, with low prior reading proficiency associated with larger ELA/Reading time losses [+W]
