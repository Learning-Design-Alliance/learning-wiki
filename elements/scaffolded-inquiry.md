# Scaffolded Inquiry

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
Scaffolded inquiry sequences learning tasks so that learners begin with heavily structured investigation — embedded prompts, worked models, and instructor-framed questions — and progressively take over the questioning, reasoning, and design decisions themselves. The instructor's role shifts from directing the inquiry to coaching and eventually observing as learners independently formulate questions, gather evidence, and draw conclusions.

## Design Implications

Scaffolded inquiry resolves the central tension of inquiry learning: open exploration benefits motivation and transfer, but unguided discovery overloads novice working memory and often fails [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than guided instruction.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]. Embedding scaffolds — question templates, evidence-checklists, [Coaching](coaching.md) prompts — preserves the epistemic experience of inquiry while preventing unproductive search [Inquiry teaching with scaffolds outperforms both pure discovery and pure direct instruction.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. The defining design decision is the fading schedule: supports must be withdrawn as competence grows, or learners remain dependent on external structure [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task with genuine investigative space — a question learners can actually reason about, not a disguised recall exercise
- Initial scaffolds calibrated to prior knowledge: prompts, [Advance Organizers](advance-organizers.md), or partially worked investigations
- A mechanism for diagnosing when to fade support (instructor observation, embedded checks, [Assessment](assessment.md) for learning)
- Opportunities for learners to articulate and justify their reasoning ([Self-Explanation](self-explanation.md) prompts, group argumentation)

#### Constraints
- Unguided or minimally guided inquiry produces weaker outcomes for novices than scaffolded or explicit approaches [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than guided instruction.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S] — pure discovery wastes working memory on unproductive search
- Scaffolds that are never faded create learned dependence; learners fail to develop independent inquiry routines [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [-M]
- Fading too early — before learners have usable schemas — causes frustration and abandonment of inquiry strategies [Inquiry teaching with scaffolds outperforms both pure discovery and pure direct instruction.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [~M]
- Requires substantial instructor time for diagnosis and responsive coaching; poorly resourced implementations often default to either full direction or full abandonment

### Target Learners
- Middle school through higher education learners with enough prior knowledge to generate hypotheses but not enough to structure an investigation alone
- Novices benefit most from heavy initial scaffolding; as expertise grows, extensive guidance becomes redundant and can depress performance [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Learners with weak self-regulation need explicit inquiry routines (question → predict → test → revise) before independent work

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking: formulating questions, evaluating evidence, reasoning from data
- Independent research skills: planning and monitoring an investigation
- Conceptual understanding through [Self-Explanation](self-explanation.md) during exploration [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]

### Affordances
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — scaffolded inquiry is scaffolding applied to the inquiry process itself; the fading schedule is the core design variable
- [Constructivism](../principles/constructivism.md) — learners build understanding through active investigation rather than transmission, but within a structure that keeps construction productive
- [Inquiry-Based Learning](../principles/inquiry-based-learning.md) — scaffolded inquiry is the guided variant of this principle, addressing its documented failure modes for novices
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the progression from modeling through coaching to independent exploration mirrors the apprenticeship sequence
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — early scaffolds offload task-structuring demands so working memory is spent on the science or reasoning itself

## Examples

**[POGIL](https://pogil.org) (Process Oriented Guided Inquiry Learning)** — Teams work through carefully sequenced question sets on data or models, with assigned roles; the structure is progressively reduced as students develop independent analytical routines in chemistry and other disciplines.

**[Problem-Based Learning](../patterns/problem-based-learning.md) (e.g., [Maastricht University](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl))** — Groups investigate authentic problems with a tutor who coaches rather than lectures, with tutorial support fading across program years.

**BSCS Science: An Inquiry Approach** — Published high school curriculum that sequences investigations from teacher-structured labs toward student-designed experiments across a multi-year arc.
