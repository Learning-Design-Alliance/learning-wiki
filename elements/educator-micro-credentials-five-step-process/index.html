Educator micro-credentials: five-step competency-based professional learning credential¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Digital Promise defines educator micro-credentials as a professional learning credential in which educators select a skill, develop evidence, submit it, and are evaluated by experts. 'There are five steps to earning a micro-credential,' beginning when 'Educators select the micro-credential they want to earn based on their students' needs and their own interests.' If evidence demonstrates competence, the educator earns the credential and may share a digital badge.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators must develop required evidence, such as classroom videos or student work, that experts evaluate against an associated rubric

Constraints¶

  • Awards depend on evaluation against the associated rubric; the badge is shared at the educator's choice

Target Learners¶

  • classroom teachers, librarians, principals, and instructional coaches

Target Learning Goals¶

  • demonstrating competence in specific classroom practices

Claims¶

  • BCPS embedded micro-credentials into PLCs with S.T.A.T. teachers and awarded continuing professional development credit [+W]
  • The Friday Institute used micro-credential stacks to close the implementation gap left by MOOC-Ed content at scale [+W]
  • KMSD's micro-credential compensation model supported teacher collaboration, with groups of 5 to 60 teachers earning together [+W]
  • KMSD tied micro-credential attainment to permanent base-salary increases, and over 80% of its teachers earned micro-credentials [+W]
  • Micro-credential reflections push educators toward metacognition and self-evaluation [+W]
  • MTLC developed the 11-credential Global Graduates stack to support personalized learning in Houston ISD's one-to-one PowerUp environment [+W]
