# Socratic Questioning

## Description
Socratic questioning is the element in which prompts are used to probe assumptions, clarify reasoning, test evidence, and extend implications. It is useful when the goal is not simply to elicit an answer, but to strengthen the quality of explanation and judgment behind the answer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A discussable claim or interpretation**
- **Questions aimed at reasoning, evidence, and implication**
- **Time for learners to respond and reconsider**
#### Constraints
- **Interrogation feel**: Poorly paced questioning can feel punitive rather than exploratory.
- **Surface participation**: Rapid-fire questioning can privilege quick responders over thoughtful ones.

### Target Learners
- Learners developing explanation, argumentation, and conceptual precision.

### Target Learning Goals
- Clarify assumptions, test evidence, and deepen reasoning.

## Examples
- Asking learners what evidence supports a claim and what would count against it.
- Probing a proposed solution with follow-up questions about assumptions and consequences.
