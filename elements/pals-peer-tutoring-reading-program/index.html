PALS: structured peer-tutoring program supplementing the primary reading curriculum¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies and Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies are "peer-tutoring programs that supplement the primary reading curriculum" in which students work in pairs, alternately acting as tutor and tutee, on partner reading, paragraph "shrinking," and prediction relay activities intended to improve reading accuracy, fluency, and comprehension. Sessions last 35 minutes and are implemented three to four times per week, with separate versions for kindergarten, grade 1, and grades 2–6. Teachers receive an all-day training workshop with modeling and role-playing, and a team point system motivates accurate reading and paragraph summarizing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers are trained to use the learning strategies through an all-day workshop plus a program manual

Constraints¶

  • Student reading materials are not provided in the grades 2–6 version; teachers select appropriate reading materials

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten students
  • grade 1 students
  • students in grades 2–3 (as part of a grades 2–6 set)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading accuracy
  • reading fluency
  • reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed no statistically significant effects on seven alphabetics outcomes compared with adult tutoring [~W]
  • PALS showed no statistically significant effects on near-transfer and far-transfer reading fluency for first graders [~W]
  • K-PALS shows statistically significant positive effects on Rapid Letter Sounds for kindergarten students in all three training-support conditions [+W]
  • Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies showed a substantively important positive (but statistically non-significant) effect on WRMT-R Passage Comprehension for first graders [+W]
  • For first-grade nonresponders, PALS showed a substantively important negative (but statistically non-significant) effect on reading comprehension versus adult tutoring [-W]
  • The WWC rates PALS as having potentially positive effects on alphabetics, no discernible effects on fluency, and mixed effects on comprehension for beginning readers [~W]
