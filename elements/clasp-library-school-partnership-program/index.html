CLASP (Connecting Libraries and Schools Project)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

CLASP was a three-year, $3.6 million pilot program initiated in 1991 by the DeWitt Wallace-Reader's Digest Fund to promote collaborative programs among the New York Public Library, New York City public schools, and neighborhood organizations. A total of 107 schools from three NYC school districts and 23 branch libraries were involved. Typical activities included parent workshops, class visits, craft programs, and summer reading clubs. CLASP also purchased 60,928 items to supplement branch collections and issued mini-grants to teachers for special projects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Dedicated CLASP librarians working from district offices
  • Advisory committees comprising school and public librarians, teachers, and parents

Constraints¶

  • The evaluation was more heavily focused on CLASP activities during summer months and less on the full academic year

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school children
  • young children and parents
  • young teens
  • speakers of English as a second language
  • disadvantaged populations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • library use
  • reading motivation
  • family literacy
  • independent learning

Claims¶

  • Communication, cooperation, and coordination were the most critical factors in successful CLASP programming [+W]
  • CLASP succeeded in bringing ethnically diverse and underserved users into the library but programs often failed to create links to books, reading, or library services [+W]
  • CLASP made limited progress on cooperative activities and little progress on developing replicable collaboration models [+W]
  • CLASP successfully established library use among children, reaching thousands of new users who were highly satisfied with programs [+W]
  • CLASP's impact centered on the number of programs offered rather than program type, with few differences between CLASP and non-CLASP library programs [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • CLASP programming should always create links to books, reading, information use, and library services
