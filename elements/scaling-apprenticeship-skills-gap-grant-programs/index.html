Scaling Apprenticeship and Closing the Skills Gap grant programs¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Two federally funded grant programs administered by the U.S. Department of Labor that this evaluation studies. The report describes apprenticeship as "an 'earn and learn' employer-based training model" that combines "classroom-based instruction with on-the-job training (OJT) under a mentor at the employer's worksite," with apprentices employed during training and earning progressively higher wages. DOL awarded almost $284 million to 51 grantees between 2019 and 2020 under the two programs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs fund apprenticeship models combining classroom-based instruction with on-the-job training under a mentor at the employer's worksite

Constraints¶

  • Traditionally used for construction-related occupations; DOL aims to expand apprenticeship to nontraditional occupations

Target Learners¶

  • American workers and jobseekers participating in apprenticeship grants, including those in nontraditional occupations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Occupational skills through combined classroom instruction and on-the-job training leading to employment and higher earnings

Claims¶

  • Registered apprenticeship had positive, statistically significant impacts on ninth-quarter earnings, ranging from $3,230 to $4,693 depending on the comparison group [+W]
  • Registered apprenticeship programs had large, statistically significant positive impacts on employment in the ninth quarter following enrollment relative to both community college students and Wagner-Peyser participants [+W]
  • Registered apprenticeship had strong ninth-quarter employment and earnings impacts for IT, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing sectors [+W]
  • Unregistered apprenticeships had statistically significant ninth-quarter employment and earnings impacts specifically for advanced manufacturing [+W]
  • Unregistered apprenticeship programs had positive, statistically significant impacts on ninth-quarter earnings, ranging from $3,368 to $3,490 [+W]
  • Unregistered apprenticeship programs had positive, statistically significant impacts on ninth-quarter employment, ranging from 3.9 to 4.8 percentage points [+W]
