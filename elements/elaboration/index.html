Elaboration¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 25 claims (12 for, 8 mixed, 5 against) · 30 studies (18 causal, 6 quant-synthesis, 3 review, 3 associational), q2–q4 · 11 of 30 report an effect size · 19 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Elaboration is the process of expanding on new information by relating it to what one already knows — generating explanations, examples, analogies, or "why" and "how" questions. As an instructional element, it means designing prompts and activities that require learners to produce these connections rather than passively rereading material. Elaboration deepens encoding by integrating new content into existing knowledge structures, making it more retrievable and more transferable.

Design Implications¶

Elaboration improves retention and comprehension because deeper processing at encoding produces more durable, better-connected memory traces Elaborative interrogation and self-explanation are among the most effective learning techniques. [+S]. The connections must be generated by the learner — instructor-provided elaboration is weaker than prompted self-generation, though it can scaffold learners who cannot yet elaborate independently. Prompts should target meaningful relations ("Why does this make sense?", "How does this relate to X?") rather than restating surface features.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Relevant prior knowledge to connect to; activate it first (Activation, Activating Prior Knowledge)
  • Prompts that demand explanatory or relational responses, not verbatim recall
  • Feedback or verification so learners do not entrench inaccurate elaborations Self-explanation prompts improve learning when learners receive corrective feedback. [+M]
  • Sufficient time — elaboration is effortful and slower than rereading

Constraints¶

  • Elaborating on inaccurate prior knowledge can strengthen misconceptions; verify the connections learners make [-M]
  • Learners with minimal domain knowledge may generate irrelevant or superficial elaborations that add load without benefit Cognitive overload degrades learning when demands exceed working memory capacity. [-M]
  • Under time pressure or high extraneous load, learners default to shallow processing and skip the elaboration step [-M]
  • Less effective for arbitrary, meaningless content (e.g., paired associates with no semantic structure) where nothing meaningful can be connected [~M]

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with moderate prior knowledge, who have enough schema to generate meaningful connections Elaborative interrogation benefits learners with some existing knowledge most. [+M]
  • Older students and adults; younger children often need modeled elaboration before they can self-generate
  • Less immediately effective for complete novices, who benefit from instructor-provided elaborations first, then a fade to self-generation

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term retention of factual and conceptual knowledge
  • Conceptual understanding: explaining why procedures and principles work
  • Transfer: building multiple retrieval routes and cross-domain connections Analogical reasoning supports transfer to novel problems. [+M]

Affordances¶

  • Analogical Reasoning — generating an analogy is elaboration in its most structured form; the learner maps new material onto a familiar domain, which both deepens encoding and supports transfer
  • Chunking — elaboration creates meaningful groupings that expand effective chunk size, letting learners hold more material in working memory Chunking reduces working memory load. [+S]
  • Cognitive Load Management — by connecting new content to existing schemas, elaboration moves information from effortful working-memory processing into well-organized long-term memory structures
  • Activation — elaboration prompts double as activation tasks, surfacing prior knowledge at the moment it is needed for integration Activating prior knowledge improves learning. [+M]

Claims¶

  • Elaborative-interrogation learning gains are durable, with performance maintained up to six months after study [+W]
  • Elaborative interrogation produced lower posttest accuracy than reading the lesson twice in a written lesson on posterior probability [-W]
  • Elaborative interrogation increased perceived difficulty and time spent with the lesson [~W]
  • The quality of elaborative-interrogation answers had little effect on retention; even failed or inadequate why-answers facilitated learning relative to reading [~W]
  • Elaborative interrogation instructions produce better recall and recognition of science facts than reading-for-understanding instructions [+W]
  • Elaborative-interrogation students are less confident in their incorrect recognition selections than reading controls, who express great confidence in erroneous answers [+W]
  • Elaborating with relevant prior knowledge yielded significantly higher immediate recall than elaborating without relevant prior knowledge [+W]
  • Elaboration during the problem-based discussion had no significant main effect on immediate or delayed recall [-W]
  • Quality of self-generated examples correlated with knowledge transfer and perceived learning control but not with knowledge retention [~W]
  • Inserted questions elicit more correct and fewer incorrect elaborations than post-reading questions, with no condition differences in paraphrases [~W]
  • Reading provided examples is not more effective than generating examples for learning relational categories [+W]
  • Anticipatory self-explanation prompts benefited higher-prior-knowledge apprentices on diagnostic strategy description scores, whereas retrospective prompts benefited lower-prior-knowledge apprentices [~W]
  • Self-explanation quality correlated with prior knowledge and post-test knowledge but not with learning gains [~W]
  • Generating own examples after instruction produced better retention and transfer than studying provided examples [+W]
  • Accumulation and cross-text elaboration are uncorrelated and show opposite-sign correlations with intertextual comprehension [~W]
  • Prompted and control groups show no significant differences in separative elaborations within domains or in additions [-W]
  • Older adults produce fewer internal details than younger adults during elaboration across both experiments [~W]

The evidence by question¶

  • Does answering 'why is this true?' about each statement improve memory and understanding of it over reading it, and when does it not? Elaborative Interrogation Improves Learning [+S]
  • Does having learners generate their own elaborations improve learning, compared with no elaboration or with elaborations supplied to them? Prompting learners to self-explain, one generative strategy, improves learning by a moderate average amount; other generative activities are not tested by the evidence recorded here [+M]
  • Is time spent elaborating on material better spent than the same time retrieving it? Quizzing outperforms no/filler activity, fewer-question testing, restudying, and other elaborative strategies, with the advantage shrinking as the control condition's reexposure increases. [-M]

Related Elements¶

  • Analogies — a specific elaboration form connecting new content to familiar domains
  • Advance Organizers — provide the prior-knowledge scaffold that elaboration then builds on
  • Articulation — verbalizing understanding is a form of prompted elaboration
  • Annotating — written marginalia as an elaboration channel during reading
  • Application — applying knowledge forces elaborative connections to context

Patterns That Use This Element¶

  • Elaboration Theory — Reigeluth's model sequences instruction from simple to complex with elaborating layers of detail
  • 5E Learning Cycle — the "Explain" and "Elaborate" phases explicitly require learners to extend and connect concepts
  • Cognitive Apprenticeship — the articulation and reflection phases prompt learners to elaborate on their own reasoning

Examples¶

Elaborative Interrogation — Learners answer "Why is this true?" or "Why does this make sense?" for each fact studied; one of the highest-utility techniques in the Dunlosky review.

Self-Explanation — Learners explain each step of a worked solution or each line of a text in their own words, connecting steps to underlying principles.

Cornell Note-Taking — The cue-column/question format prompts students to generate questions and summaries that elaborate on lecture notes rather than transcribe them.

Anki — Spaced-repetition flashcards work best when users add personal examples and mnemonics to card fields, embedding elaboration into review.
