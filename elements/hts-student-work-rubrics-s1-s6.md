# Historical Thinking Skills Student Work Rubrics (S1-S6) for assessing historical thinking in student products

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A companion set of six rubric dimensions (S1-S6) meant to study the extent to which high school world history student work exhibits historical thinking skills. As with the activity rubrics, "EacK GLPHQVLrq KaV IRXU OHYHOV (0-3)", where 0 generally indicates absence of a historical thinking practice or skill, 1 emergence, 2 solid presence, and 3 rigorous presence. The dimensions mirror the activity rubrics: historical argumentation claims and evidence, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing. The rubric was piloted on 54 samples of student work associated with the scored assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The assignment collection process was tightened to include only assignments requiring individual students to express themselves through prose, written or oral; non-verbal maps, figures, artwork cannot be scored
#### Constraints
- Only one of the World History Project assignments had student work associated with it, limiting comparative analysis of student work rubric scores

### Target Learners
- High school world history students in on-level or pre-AP 9th/10th grade classes

### Target Learning Goals
- Historical thinking skills including argumentation, causation, comparison, contextualization, continuity and change over time, and sourcing
