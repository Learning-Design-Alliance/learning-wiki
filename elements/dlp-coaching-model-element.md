# Dynamic Learning Project (DLP) coaching model

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The DLP is a school-based instructional technology coaching program that placed one site-based, full-time coach in each of 50 underserved schools for one year. Coaches ran "four eight-week coaching cycles" in which teachers selected, tackled, and reflected on classroom challenges, supported by classroom modeling, co-teaching, and observations. Coaches and principals received mentoring, a Summer and Winter Institute, and membership in a Professional Learning Network. In the pilot year more than 1,110 of 2,250 teachers participated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- District and school buy-in: principals must support program fidelity, protect coach time, and ensure the coach does not play an evaluative role; districts fund and sustain the coach position.
#### Constraints
- The report notes that measuring improvement in student achievement and attributing it to a coaching intervention is complicated from a research perspective, and funding for coach positions is a sustainability challenge in resource-strapped districts.

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers in underserved, low-income schools
- instructional technology coaches
- school principals

### Target Learning Goals
- Powerful use of technology for student collaboration, creativity, communication, critical thinking, and agency
- teacher integration of technology into content and pedagogy
