# Learning management systems (LMSs) as infrastructure for competency-based, personalized learning

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
LMSs are software applications schools and districts use to automate learning digitally. They let teachers create learning modules for specific competencies, upload activities offering students options for learning content and demonstrating learning, and provide "instant access to student performance data and analysis, often at the individual item level", enabling real-time tailoring of content. The publication illustrates their use with Lindsay Unified's Empower, which animates a performance-based system with no grade levels, and Irving ISD's Canvas, used for more than 33,000 learners with adaptive, AI-driven quiz branching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interoperable platforms and educator capacity to identify individual learning needs and gaps
#### Constraints
- The publication notes edtech products referenced do not constitute a Digital Promise endorsement

### Target Learners
- K-12 learners, including rural Latino learners in Lindsay and largely Latino and Black learners in Irving ISD

### Target Learning Goals
- Demonstration of competencies at flexible but ambitious paces; differentiated instruction
