Keep-On-Keep-Up (KOKU) digital falls prevention programme¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

KOKU is an NHS-approved, tablet-delivered digital programme offering "a progressive strength and balance programme with animated characters (eCoaches) providing instructions, guidance, safety information, automated reminders and in-app progress tracking", alongside health literacy games on nutrition, hydration and falls awareness. It was co-developed with older adults and falls prevention experts using behaviour change techniques and MRC complex-intervention guidance. In the trial it was used 20–30 minutes, three times a week for 12 weeks at home.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A tablet device and initial training at baseline to familiarise participants with using the programme, with ad hoc technical support available

Constraints¶

  • Several participants faced technical glitches and confusion regarding the dose of each exercise; the programme was felt by some to offer insufficient challenge for higher-functioning users and to feel relatable only to White Caucasian older adults

Target Learners¶

  • community-dwelling older adults aged 60 years and over

Target Learning Goals¶

  • strength and balance exercise capability
  • health literacy around fall prevention, nutrition and hydration

Claims¶

  • The KOKU digital programme improves balance function at 12 weeks compared with standard care in community-dwelling older adults, with the effect increasing over time [+M]
  • The 12-week KOKU programme is dominant over usual care in cost-effectiveness, with a significant QALY gain and non-significant cost savings [+M]
  • KOKU improves lower-limb function, concerns about falling and health-related quality of life, but not mood, physical activity, fatigue or fall rate [+M]
  • Older adults rated the KOKU digital programme with excellent usability and high acceptability [+M]
  • Qualitative findings: ease of use supports engagement and confidence, but challenge must be tailored to ability and content must be culturally relatable [+M]
