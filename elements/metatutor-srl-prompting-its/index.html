MetaTutor: a hypermedia-based ITS with pedagogical agents scaffolding SRL about the circulatory system¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

MetaTutor is "a hypermedia -based ITS containing multiple pages of content about the circulatory system" with mechanisms to help students self-regulate their learning via multiple speaking pedagogical agents. Its interface includes a table of contents, content text, a diagram, current goals and subgoals, a timer, and an SRL palette whose buttons initiate SRL activities such as making a summary, taking a quiz, or setting subgoals. Three agents deliver prompts based on student behavior: Pam the Planner (planning), Mary the Monitor (metacognitive monitoring via quizzes), and Sam the Strategizer (summarizing, reviewing notes).

Claims¶

  • More fixations on the learning content after an add-subgoal prompt are associated with higher learning gains, suggesting complying with that prompt may not be effective [~W]
  • Compliance with MetaTutor's summarize, stay-on-subgoal, move-to-next-subgoal, and open-diagram prompts shows no significant effect on learning gains [-M]
  • No behavioral measure of compliance with MetaTutor's stay-on-page and move-to-next-page prompts relates to learning gains [-W]
  • Higher compliance with MetaTutor's review-notes prompt is associated with larger proportional learning gains [+M]
  • Higher compliance with MetaTutor's revise-summary prompt is associated with larger proportional learning gains [+M]
  • Higher compliance with MetaTutor's suggest-subgoal prompt is associated with larger proportional learning gains [+M]
