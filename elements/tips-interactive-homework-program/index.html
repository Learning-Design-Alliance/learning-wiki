Teachers Involve Parents in Schoolwork (TIPS) interactive homework program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TIPS is "a researcher and educator-designed, implemented and tested partnership process for teachers to regularly keep families involved in their children's education." Its core mechanism is interactive homework: "teachers provide students assignments that require them to talk to someone at home about something interesting that they are learning in class." Activities are organized by grade level (elementary, middle, high school) and subject (language arts, math, science). Implementation requires no additional staffing, training, or technology.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must regularly assign interactive homework that requires students to talk to someone at home about class learning

Constraints¶

  • Optional professional development is a paid one-day workshop costing $5,000

Target Learners¶

  • elementary students (third and fourth grade in the evaluated study)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • family involvement in mathematics, reading, and science homework

Claims¶

  • TIPS students report significantly higher family involvement in reading and science homework than non-participants [+W]
  • TIPS participation raises family-reported involvement in mathematics homework (ES = +0.49) [+W]
  • End-of-fourth-grade mathematics achievement significantly favors TIPS students over non-participants [+W]
  • TIPS participation of any duration raises student-reported family involvement in mathematics homework (ES = +0.82) [+W]

Related Elements¶

Examples¶

  • Use interactive homework assignments that require students to discuss class learning with someone at home
