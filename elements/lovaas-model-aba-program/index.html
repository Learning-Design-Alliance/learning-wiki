Lovaas Model of Applied Behavior Analysis: intensive early behavioral intervention program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Lovaas Model is "a type of behavioral therapy that initially focuses on discrete trials: brief periods of one-on-one instruction, during which a teacher cues a behavior, prompts the appropriate response, and provides reinforcement to the child." Children receive an average of 35 to 40 hours of intervention per week combining in-home one-to-one instruction, facilitated peer play, classroom inclusion, and generalization activities; parents are trained in instructional techniques and the intervention generally lasts about three years. It was developed at UCLA under O. Ivar Lovaas and is delivered through the Lovaas Institute.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A team of three to five trained instructors delivers the majority of teaching, with trainers, a case supervisor, and a program director supervising the intervention team

Constraints¶

  • One study estimates initial costs that range between $45,575 and $69,050 annually

Target Learners¶

  • children ages 18 to 42 months with autism or pervasive developmental disorder

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive development
  • communication and language competencies
  • social-emotional development and behavior
  • functional abilities

Claims¶

  • The evidence base for the Lovaas Model is small: only two of 58 reviewed studies met WWC evidence standards [~W]
  • The Lovaas Model has no discernible effects on communication/language competencies for children with disabilities [-W]
  • The Lovaas Model has no discernible effects on functional abilities for children with disabilities [-W]
  • The Lovaas Model has no discernible effects on social-emotional development and behavior for children with disabilities [-W]
  • The Lovaas Model has potentially positive effects on cognitive development for children with disabilities, with an improvement index of +27 percentile points [+W]
