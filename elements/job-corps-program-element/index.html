Job Corps residential education and vocational training program¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Job Corps is a federally funded education and job training program for economically disadvantaged youth ages 16 to 24, most of whom lack a high school diploma or GED certificate. Its core services — academic instruction, vocational training, and residential living services — are provided through centers, with self-paced, open-entry/open-exit enrollment; the average participant spends about eight months and receives more than 1,000 hours of instruction. Services include remedial reading and math, GED preparation, training for about 10 trades at a typical center, job placement assistance, and, for residents, dormitory housing with counseling, health services, social-skills training, and a biweekly living allowance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Participants must be economically disadvantaged youth ages 16 to 24 who meet Job Corps eligibility criteria including citizenship or legal residency, good health, and ability to conform to standards of conduct

Constraints¶

  • The type and number of vocational training opportunities vary across Job Corps centers
  • Some centers offer only a nonresidential version without on-center housing

Target Learners¶

  • economically disadvantaged youth ages 16–24 without a high school diploma or GED

Target Learning Goals¶

  • remedial reading and math
  • GED or high school credential completion
  • vocational trade skills
  • employment readiness

Claims¶

  • Job Corps' effect on completing school is larger for older youth (18–24) than for younger youth (16–17) [+W]
  • Job Corps' completion effect came entirely from GED receipt, with a small statistically significant negative effect on earning a high school diploma [~W]
  • Job Corps substantially increased time spent in education and training relative to the control group during the 48-month follow-up [+W]
  • Job Corps shows no statistically significant or substantively important effect on years of schooling completed (progressing in school) [-W]
  • Job Corps has potentially positive effects on completing school: more youth without credentials earned a diploma or GED [+W]
