# Learning Contracts

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A learning contract is a written agreement — negotiated between learner and instructor — that specifies what the learner will accomplish, how they will do it, by when, and how the outcome will be verified. It converts a course's external structure into a learner-authored plan, making goals, effort, and evaluation criteria explicit and mutually binding.

## Design Implications

Learning contracts support learning primarily by forcing goal specification and self-monitoring: writing down goals, activities, and success criteria is a form of planning that improves self-regulated performance [Self-regulated learning processes such as goal-setting and planning predict achievement.](../claims/self-regulated-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]. Negotiation itself matters — contracts that learners help author increase ownership and intrinsic motivation relative to instructor-imposed plans [Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. Contracts work best when the instructor retains a quality gate: approving scope, checking that goals are challenging and assessable, and verifying evidence against criteria [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured template: goals, learning activities, resources, timeline, evidence of accomplishment, and evaluation criteria
- Instructor negotiation and approval — the contract must be challenging enough and verifiable, not a rubber stamp
- Scheduled [check-ins](check-in.md) for progress monitoring and mid-course revision of the contract
- Clear alignment between the contract's evidence requirements and course or program outcomes

#### Constraints
- Learners with weak self-regulation or little experience setting goals often write vague, unambitious, or unassessable contracts [-M] — without instructor scaffolding and exemplars, the contract becomes a formality
- Contracts impose high overhead; in large-enrollment or short-duration courses the negotiation and monitoring costs can exceed the benefit [-M]
- Overly flexible contracts can let learners avoid difficult content; overly rigid ones eliminate the autonomy that makes them motivating [~M]
- Grading against individually negotiated criteria raises fairness and comparability concerns unless evidence standards are shared across the cohort [~M]

### Target Learners
- Adult and experienced learners who can realistically appraise their own needs and manage their time [Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Independent-study, practicum, capstone, and professional-development contexts where a single fixed curriculum fits poorly
- Novices benefit only with heavy scaffolding — provided goals, exemplar contracts, and frequent instructor review [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-directed and self-regulated learning: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own progress [Self-regulated learning processes such as goal-setting and planning predict achievement.](../claims/self-regulated-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]
- Applied and project-based outcomes: individualized [application](application.md) of skills to authentic work
- Metacognition: making one's goals, strategies, and evidence standards explicit and revisable

### Affordances
- [Autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md) — the contract enacts autonomy by giving learners genuine decision rights over goals, methods, and pace, while the negotiation keeps those choices accountable
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — because success criteria and evidence are specified in advance and revisited at check-ins, the contract functions as a standing feedback and self-assessment instrument
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — the written plan externalizes expectations, reducing ambiguity about what counts as done and well done
- [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) — contracts tie evidence of accomplishment directly to demonstrated competence rather than seat time or uniform tasks

## Examples

**[Academic Choice (Planning, Working, Reflecting)](../strategies/academic-choice-planning-working-reflecting.md)** — Learners plan what they will do, carry it out, and reflect on results; the planning artifact is a simplified learning contract.

**[Action Planning](../strategies/action_planning.md)** — A structured plan of goals, steps, and timelines that serves as the operational core of a learning contract.

**Knowles' andragogical contracts** — Malcolm Knowles' model for adult education: learners diagnose needs, set objectives, select resources and strategies, and specify evidence of accomplishment, iterated with instructor approval (Knowles, 1986).

**University independent-study and practicum courses** — Typical implementation: a proposal template with goals, activities, timeline, and deliverables, approved by a faculty supervisor and assessed at midterm and final checkpoints.
