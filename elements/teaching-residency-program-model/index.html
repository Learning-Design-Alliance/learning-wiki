Teaching residency program (TRP) model: master's coursework plus a one-year supervised residency¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Teaching residency programs are alternative teacher-training models funded under the Teacher Quality Partnership Grant Program. The article describes the model as offering "coursework toward a master’s degree along with a one-year supervised residency under the guidance of a full-time teacher," intended to attract new teachers to the profession and to high-need schools. The study examined components such as required coursework, teacher backgrounds and experiences, and teacher retention across 30 programs.

Claims¶

  • TRPs were created in 2008 under the Teacher Quality Partnership Grant Program to draw highly qualified teachers to high-need schools [+W]
  • The 2010-2015 study examined characteristics of 30 TRPs, including required coursework, teacher backgrounds and experiences, and teacher retention [+W]
  • Novice TRP teachers' district retention rates are roughly the same as those of teachers entering through other preparation programs [~M]
  • TRP participants could accept a stipend in exchange for committing to teach in the same district for at least three years [+W]
  • Novice teachers trained through teaching residency programs are more likely than similar non-TRP teachers to remain teaching in the same district [+M]
  • School-retention rates are similar between TRP-trained and non-TRP novice teachers [~M]
  • The retention comparison covers two cohorts of novice TRP teachers across six districts served by 12 TRPs, with a representative non-TRP comparison sample [+M]
  • TRP teachers who changed schools within their district tended to move to schools with similar low-income shares, a lower percentage of black students, and higher achievement [~M]
