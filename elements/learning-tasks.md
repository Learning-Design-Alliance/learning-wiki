# Learning Tasks

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
A learning task is the integrated unit of work around which instruction is organized: a problem to solve, a case to analyze, a project to complete, or a performance to produce. Rather than decomposing content into isolated topics presented before application, task-centered design places learners in a meaningful, whole task from the start, with supporting information and part-task practice organized around it [Merrill's first principles identify task-centeredness as the core condition for effective instruction.](https://doi.org/10.1109/MTS.2002.1024043) [+M].

## Design Implications

Learning tasks drive learning because they force learners to *use* knowledge rather than merely receive it, promoting integration of facts, concepts, and procedures into usable schemas [Unguided or minimally guided tasks work best when learners have sufficient prior knowledge; for novices, tasks must be strongly scaffolded.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [~S]. Effective tasks are authentic or realistic, sequenced from simple to complex, and paired with feedback and support that fades as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks that are recognizable as authentic to the discipline — with the variability, complexity, and success criteria of real performance
- A task class with multiple, progressively more difficult tasks, so learners see the same underlying structure in varied surface forms
- Available support: [Demonstration](demonstration.md), worked examples, or [Coaching](coaching.md) for early tasks, fading on later ones
- Feedback tied to task and process levels, not just correctness [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Whole tasks imposed on novices without scaffolding overload working memory and produce floundering [Minimally guided instruction is less effective for novices than explicit guidance.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [-S]
- Tasks that are authentic but far beyond current competence cause cognitive overload and disengagement; complexity must be sequenced, not dumped
- Ill-structured tasks without clear success criteria leave learners unable to evaluate their own performance
- Learners with high prior knowledge may find heavily scaffolded early tasks redundant and demotivating [Expertise reversal: guidance that helps novices can hinder more knowledgeable learners.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices, provided early tasks are heavily scaffolded and complexity is carefully sequenced [Example–problem sequences reduce cognitive load for novices.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+M]
- Intermediate learners, who benefit most from task variety and fading support [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners, who need less scaffolding and more task variability to discriminate between situations

### Target Learning Goals
- Integration: coordinating facts, concepts, procedures, and attitudes in realistic performance
- Transfer: applying learned schemas to novel problems within the task class
- Complex skill acquisition: learning whole professional or disciplinary practices, not isolated subskills

### Affordances
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — task sequencing enacts this principle by controlling intrinsic load: early tasks are simple versions of the whole task, and complexity increases only as schemas form
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — learning tasks are the structure on which scaffolds are built; support is high on early tasks and fades across the task sequence [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — a learning task is the canonical active-learning unit: learners produce, decide, and perform rather than receive
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — tasks designed around real audiences and consequential products increase engagement and make quality standards meaningful
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — well-specified tasks make performance criteria visible, so the task itself becomes the vehicle for formative assessment and self-evaluation

## Related Elements

- [Demonstration](demonstration.md) — models expert performance of the task type before learners attempt it

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Four-Component Instructional Design](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — learning tasks are the backbone component; the other three components (supportive information, procedural information, part-task practice) are organized around them
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — authentic tasks are the arena for modeling, coaching, and articulation
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — cases function as analysis-oriented learning tasks

## Examples

**[4C/ID task classes](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md)** — In van Merriënboer's model, a domain (e.g., troubleshooting electrical circuits) is analyzed into a sequence of task classes; each class contains several whole tasks of increasing complexity with fading learner support.

**[Harvard Business School case method](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/Pages/the-hbs-case-method.aspx)** — Each class session is organized around a single business case; students must take and defend a decision, making the case the learning task rather than supplementary reading.

**[Project Lead The Way](https://www.pltw.org)** — K-12 STEM curriculum organized around design challenges (e.g., engineering a prosthetic device) that integrate science content into authentic problem-solving tasks.

**[POGIL](https://pogil.org)** — Process-Oriented Guided Inquiry Learning uses structured data-rich activities as tasks; teams work through models and questions with assigned roles, with the activity itself carrying the instruction.
