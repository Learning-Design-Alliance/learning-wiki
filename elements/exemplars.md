# Exemplars

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)

## Description
An exemplar is a high-quality model of a finished product or performance — an essay, design, proof, or solution — that learners study to internalize quality criteria before producing their own work. Unlike a [Demonstration](demonstration.md), which shows a *process*, an exemplar shows the *product* of expertise, making implicit standards observable and discussable.

## Design Implications

Exemplars make quality criteria concrete: learners calibrate their own standards against a model rather than guessing at what the instructor wants [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]. Their impact multiplies when learners actively analyze exemplars against explicit criteria — annotating, ranking, or critiquing — rather than passively reading them [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Multiple exemplars of varying quality are more powerful than a single perfect model, because contrast reveals the features that distinguish strong work from weak work [Cognitive flexibility theory benefits from multiple cases.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- An authentic, high-quality model of the target product ([Practice](practice.md) tasks should later mirror it)
- Explicit criteria ([Rubrics](rubrics.md) or checklists) so learners know *what* makes the exemplar good, not just that it is good
- An analysis activity — annotation, comparison, or critique — that forces engagement with the model's features
- Ideally, a set of contrasting models ([Non-Examples](non-examples.md)) spanning a quality range

#### Constraints
- A single polished exemplar can anchor learners to one style or approach, producing imitation rather than transfer; multiple varied exemplars mitigate this [Cognitive flexibility theory benefits from multiple cases.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [~M]
- Exemplars studied without criteria or critique tasks yield shallow engagement and an illusion of understanding [-M]
- For highly novel or creative tasks with no established quality norms, exemplars may over-constrain learner exploration [~W]
- Learners with strong prior knowledge gain little from studying models and may benefit more from direct problem solving [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack a mental model of what "good" looks like in a genre or discipline [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners who misjudge their own work's quality; comparing drafts against exemplars improves self-assessment accuracy [~M]
- Less valuable for experts, who already possess internalized standards [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality calibration: internalizing criteria for evaluating products
- Genre knowledge: understanding the conventions and structure of a form of writing, design, or argument
- Transfer: recognizing deep structural features across varied instances [Analogical reasoning improves transfer.](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [+M]

### Affordances
- [Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) — an exemplar applied to problem solving *is* a worked example; both give learners a complete model to study before producing their own
- [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — studying a finished model removes the burden of generating structure and content simultaneously, freeing working memory for understanding quality criteria
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../principles/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — exemplars make expert products visible, the artifact-level counterpart to modeling expert process
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — an exemplar is temporary support; it should be faded as learners internalize criteria and can self-evaluate

## Examples

**[Use Worked Examples](../strategies/use_worked_examples.md)** — Presents a fully solved problem for study before asking learners to solve a parallel one; the solution is the exemplar.

**Comparing and contrasting cases** — Learners study two worked-out cases differing on one key feature and articulate the difference, a structure shown to improve abstract understanding [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M].

**[Calibrated Peer Review](https://calibratedpeerreview.org)** — Learners evaluate sample texts and receive feedback on whether their judgments match expert judgments, directly training quality calibration against exemplars.

**Writing program model texts** — Many composition curricula (e.g., the [National Writing Project](https://www.nwp.org) approach) use mentor texts: published exemplars students analyze for craft moves before drafting their own pieces.
