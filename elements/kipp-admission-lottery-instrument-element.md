# KIPP middle school admission lotteries as a random assignment instrument for estimating long-term charter impacts

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study uses oversubscribed KIPP middle school admission lotteries as "a random assignment instrument" to estimate causal impacts of attendance on college outcomes. The network studied is the nation's largest charter school network, serving "disadvantaged students of color," and the analysis covers "13 KIPP middle schools" with outcomes through the first two postsecondary years. This design addresses selection bias that complicates comparisons of charter and non-charter students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to admission lottery applicant and enrollment data from KIPP middle schools that are oversubscribed, so lotteries determine offers
- Postsecondary enrollment records for lottery applicants over at least two years after expected high school graduation
#### Constraints
- Findings apply to KIPP middle schools serving disadvantaged students of color and to applicants who entered the lotteries; generalization beyond this population is limited by the study's scope

### Target Learners
- Middle school students from disadvantaged backgrounds, primarily students of color

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing 4-year college enrollment and persistence
