# Repeated reading practice procedure

> **Element** · [All elements](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 against) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Repeated reading is an academic practice, without a single developer, that "aims to increase oral reading fluency." In the described implementation, "a student sits in a quiet location with a teacher and reads a passage aloud at least three times," typically with a passage of about 50 to 200 words selected by the teacher. If the student misreads a word or hesitates longer than 5 seconds, the teacher reads the word aloud and the student repeats it correctly; if the student requests help, the teacher reads the word aloud or provides the definition. The student rereads the passage until achieving a satisfactory fluency level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher or other adult available to work one-on-one with the student in a quiet location
- Passages of roughly 50 to 200 words matched to the student's level
#### Constraints
- Cost information is not available about the costs of teacher training and implementation

### Target Learners
- students with learning disabilities in grades 5–12 who have initial word reading skills but inadequate fluency

### Target Learning Goals
- oral reading fluency
- word recognition automaticity
