Functional Approach model: a five-step progression taking students from BICS (oral dialogue) to CALP (written discourse)¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Larsen-Pusey's Functional Approach is a teaching model that bridges the gap between the oral skills of BICS and the Cognitive/Academic Language Proficiency (CALP) needed to use the target language in content classes. It moves students from context-embedded to context-reduced situations and from cognitively undemanding to cognitively demanding tasks, through activities progressing from oral dialogue to edited scripts, reported speech, narrative writing, and dialogue reconstruction. The packet's notional-functional taxonomy and model diagram support its activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Goals must deal with communicative competence in all four modes of language use and be implemented across the curriculum

Constraints¶

  • The packet itself states "a more detailed methodology remains to be developed" for this model

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 bilingual and L2 students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • moving from oral interpersonal communication to academic written discourse

Claims¶

  • BICS and CALP intersect developmentally but are not identical or reducible one to the other [+W]
  • The BICS-before-CALP sequence is typical for immigrant children but not universal: high L2 CALP can precede fluent L2 BICS in certain situations [~W]
  • Immigrant children acquire peer-appropriate conversational fluency in English within about 2 years but require considerably longer (5-10 years) to catch up academically in English [+W]
  • Surface conversational fluency develops within about two years, but academic proficiency in English takes 5-7 years for immigrant students [+M]
