# Belonging

## Description
Belonging is a learner's subjective sense of being a valued member of a learning community — accepted by peers and instructors, and confident that they fit there. It is not an activity but a psychological state that instructional designs can foster or undermine. Because belonging shapes persistence, engagement, and interpretation of difficulty, it functions as a motivational foundation on which other elements depend.

## Design Implications

Belonging uncertainty — doubt about whether one fits in a setting — disproportionately affects students from stigmatized or underrepresented groups and depresses persistence and performance; brief, well-timed interventions that normalize the worry and reframe difficulty as common and temporary improve outcomes, especially for at-risk students [Belonging interventions improve academic outcomes, particularly for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]. Belonging is sustained less by one-off messages than by everyday design: instructor warmth, peer connection structures, and feedback framed as belief in the student's potential. Designs should treat early moments (first sessions, first graded feedback) as high-leverage, since initial attributions of difficulty to "not belonging" compound over time.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early and repeated signals of acceptance: welcoming norms, instructor outreach, and peer-connection structures ([Check-In](check-in.md), [Class Discussion](class-discussion.md))
- Feedback that communicates high standards plus confidence in the student's capacity to meet them
- Normalization of struggle: explicit framing that difficulty and early failure are common and not evidence of not belonging
- Visible representation of diverse role models within the domain community

#### Constraints
- Brief interventions decay without environmental reinforcement; a one-time belonging message in an otherwise cold or competitive climate has little lasting effect [Belonging interventions improve academic outcomes, particularly for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [~M]
- Competitive grading curves and public ranking can actively undermine belonging by framing peers as threats [-M]
- Interventions that single students out or imply they need "fixing" can backfire by reinforcing difference [-W]
- In large or anonymous online settings, absence of social cues makes belonging harder to establish and requires deliberate community design [-M]

### Target Learners
- Students transitioning into new environments (first-year, transfer, first course in a discipline), where belonging uncertainty peaks
- Students from underrepresented or stereotyped groups in a field, who are more likely to attribute setbacks to a mismatch between themselves and the domain [Belonging interventions improve academic outcomes, particularly for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]
- All learners benefit, but effects are largest where belonging uncertainty is highest [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence and retention: staying enrolled and engaged through difficulty
- Motivational orientation: sustaining intrinsic engagement and willingness to seek help
- Participation: contributing to discussion and collaborative work rather than withdrawing

### Affordances
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) — belonging (relatedness) is one of three basic psychological needs; designs that satisfy it support intrinsic motivation alongside [autonomy](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- [Communities of Practice](../principles/communities-of-practice.md) — legitimate peripheral participation frames belonging as the entry condition for learning; newcomers learn by being accepted into practice, not just by consuming content
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — structured peer interdependence creates repeated, purposeful social contact that builds belonging as a by-product of shared work
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — feedback framed as investment in the learner ("I'm giving these comments because I have high expectations") converts assessment from a judgment on fit into a signal of membership

## Related Elements
- [Check-In](check-in.md) — routine social-emotional temperature-taking that surfaces belonging problems early
- [Class Discussion](class-discussion.md) — structured participation gives every learner a legitimate voice, a daily signal of membership
- [Accommodations](accommodations.md) — removing access barriers signals that diverse learners are expected and welcome

## Patterns That Use This Element
- [Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) — social presence is the belonging component of the teaching–social–cognitive triad
- [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) — positive interdependence structures peer acceptance into the task itself
- [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) — depends on participants feeling safe to contribute

## Examples

**Social-belonging intervention (Walton & Cohen)** — A brief reading-and-writing exercise in which students read survey results showing that worries about fitting in are common and fade with time, then write about their own experience for future students. In a randomized study with college students, it raised GPA and halved the racial-achievement gap among Black students years later.

**[PERTS](https://www.perts.net)** — Platform developed at Stanford that delivers and scales belonging and growth-mindset interventions in classrooms, with instructor dashboards for monitoring student experience.

**First-year seminar programs (e.g., [University of South Carolina's University 101](https://www.sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/university_101/index.php))** — Credit-bearing transition courses that combine peer cohorts, instructor mentorship, and explicit normalization of academic struggle; associated with improved retention.
