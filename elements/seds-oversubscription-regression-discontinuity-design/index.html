Regression discontinuity design exploiting oversubscription rationing of SEdS¶

Element · All elements Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The study's identification strategy exploits a program rule: oversubscribed districts must serve the lowest-achieving eligible applicants first, and districts use "a continuous measure of prior academic achievement to determine which eligible applicants will be offered services." This creates a cutoff around which offered and non-offered students are comparable, enabling impact estimation without random assignment.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A district where SEdS are oversubscribed, so that eligibility alone does not guarantee an offer
  • A continuous measure of prior academic achievement used to rank applicants

Constraints¶

  • Estimates are local to the achievement cutoff and apply to oversubscribed districts in Florida, Ohio, and Connecticut

Target Learners¶

  • low-income students in low-performing schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement as measured by test scores

Claims¶

  • Offering SES to eligible applicants near the cutoff in oversubscribed districts has no statistically significant impact on reading or math achievement [+W]
