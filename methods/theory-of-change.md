# Theory of Change

> **Design Method** · [All design methods](index.md)

## Description
A Theory of Change works backward from a long-term outcome, asking at each step what else would have to be true for the next step to follow. The answers divide into two kinds: **preconditions the design can produce**, which become its own chain of intended effects, and **assumptions it depends on and does not control** — a supportive manager, a policy that permits the new practice, a labour market that rewards the credential. Both are written down, and the second kind is what distinguishes the method from a [Logic Model](logic-model.md).

Originating in the evaluation of comprehensive community initiatives, where no single intervention could plausibly be credited with the outcome, it was built for exactly the situation in which a programme cannot get there alone and needs to say so.

## Design Implications

The method's contribution is not the diagram but the assumptions register. A learning design that teaches a capability nobody at work is permitted to use has a dependency it does not hold; stated, that is a finding addressed to the sponsor, and unstated the course looks like the thing that failed. Weiss's original argument for theory-based evaluation was precisely this — that initiatives fail for two distinguishable reasons, a wrong theory and a poor implementation, and that without a stated theory an evaluation cannot tell which happened.

Its second contribution is that it can hold a disagreement. Working backward with several parties in the room surfaces the cases where an employer's desired outcome and a learner's are not the same outcome, and the honest artifact records both rather than averaging them into one. Resolving that tension is rarely the design's to do; recording it is.

The costs are real and frequently unjustified. Where one party wants one thing and the chain is short, a [Logic Model](logic-model.md) produces the same links for a fraction of the effort, and a theory of change run for a single-stakeholder course produces ceremony.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A long-term outcome specific enough to reason backward from — "improved outcomes" supports no backward step
- The parties in the room, or at least their positions honestly represented; the method's distinctive yield comes from disagreement surfacing
- Somebody with standing to record a disagreement as a disagreement rather than resolving it in the write-up
- Tolerance for an artifact that states what the design cannot do

#### Constraints
- The term is used for two different things — a participatory reflective process and a document produced for a donor — and the second has largely displaced the first in practice, which strips out the reflection that gave the method its value [-M]
- Assumptions get logged and never revisited. A precondition recorded once and never checked is worse than an unrecorded risk, because it reads as handled [-M]
- Backward reasoning invites over-specification: chains elaborated to a level of detail nobody can observe, which converts a hypothesis into a plan and makes revision feel like failure [~M]
- Like any programme theory, it is a framework for organising evidence and not evidence; the systematic-review finding that stated theory often goes unused in the evaluation that follows applies here at least as strongly [~M]
- Complex, emergent effects resist the form. Where the outcome depends on other actors' adaptive responses, a precondition tree understates how much is genuinely unknown [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Workshop form:** the parties build the backward chain together, and the process is as much the point as the artifact
- **Analyst form:** a designer drafts and circulates; cheaper, and it loses the disagreement-surfacing the method exists for
- **Assumptions-first:** start by listing what has to be true and only then draw the chain, which resists the tidy-diagram failure mode
- **Contribution-claim framing:** the theory used to structure evidence for a contribution claim rather than an attribution claim, which is the honest posture where the outcome has many causes
- **Nested:** an organisational theory of change into which individual programmes attach, so a course's distal outcome is somebody else's intermediate one

### Target Learners
- Not a learner-facing method
- Its indirect beneficiaries are learners on programmes whose stated purpose is remote from the classroom — employability, health behaviour, civic participation — where the unexamined chain is long and the design's actual reach is routinely overstated
- Where the method surfaces a precondition the learner's environment does not supply, it argues for changing the environment rather than teaching harder, which is a conclusion no learner-side analysis reaches on its own

### Target Learning Goals
- None directly. The method takes the goals as given and interrogates what surrounds them
- It bears most on designs whose goals are instrumental — taught because of what they are supposed to enable, rather than for their own sake

### Instructions
1. **State the long-term outcome,** concretely enough that somebody could disagree with it, and name the parties who want it. Where two parties want different things, write both.
2. **Ask what has to be true immediately before it.** Do not skip to what the programme will do; the question is about the world, not the intervention.
3. **Sort each answer into two piles** — preconditions the design produces, and assumptions it depends on and does not control. The sorting is the method.
4. **Repeat backward** until the chain reaches something the design already claims, usually a capability from [Backward Design](backward-design.md)'s desired results.
5. **Write the assumptions as first-class entries,** each with what would falsify it and who could check.
6. **Mark the warrant on each link** — published, held position, or the design's own reasoning.
7. **Decide what comes back to it, and when.** A theory of change with no revisit point is a document; with one it is a design instrument.

## Examples
- **Comprehensive community initiatives** — the setting the method was developed for, in which many organisations act on one outcome and none can claim it, and the reason the assumptions register exists at all
- **International development programming** — the DFID-commissioned review of the term's use found it covering both a reflective process and a donor deliverable, and is the standard reference for the gap between the two
- **The Center for Theory of Change** ([theoryofchange.org](https://www.theoryofchange.org/what-is-theory-of-change/)) — ActKnowledge's practitioner materials, which carry the backward-mapping and preconditions vocabulary most widely used
