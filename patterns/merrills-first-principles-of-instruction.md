# Merrill’s First Principles of Instruction

## Description
A framework based on five core principles that promote effective learning. These principles emphasize real-world problem-solving, activation of prior knowledge, demonstration, application, and integration. Merrill’s model suggests that learning is most effective when instruction is task-centered and involves authentic, meaningful problems rather than isolated skills or concepts. The five principles include: (1) Problem-Centered (learning is organized around real-world tasks), (2) Activation (learners activate existing knowledge as a foundation for new learning), (3) Demonstration (learners see examples of what they are learning), (4) Application (learners practice applying their knowledge), and (5) Integration (learners reflect and apply new knowledge to their own experiences).

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- - Requires authentic, well-designed tasks that align with real-world applications.
- Needs instructor facilitation or structured materials to guide learners through problem-solving.
#### Constraints
- - May not be suitable for basic factual recall or highly structured learning environments.
- Requires more time and effort to design real-world tasks and scenarios.
- Learners unfamiliar with self-directed learning may struggle without proper scaffolding.

## Design

### Sequence
<!-- Steps with links to elements -->
- Begins with problem-centered tasks, then cycles through activation, demonstration, application, and integration.
- Learning progresses from simple to complex problems with scaffolding.
- Application and integration reinforce retention and real-world use.

### Personalization
- - Allows learners to choose authentic problems relevant to their context.
- Can include adaptive scaffolding and self-paced learning.
- Supports multiple ways to demonstrate and apply knowledge (e.g., projects, case studies, simulations).

## Examples
- - Medical training: Case-based learning for diagnosing patient symptoms.
- Engineering: Solving real-world design challenges through iterative problem-solving.
- Corporate training: Scenario-based simulations for leadership development.

## Impact
- - Research indicates improved knowledge retention and transfer to real-world settings.
- Enhances problem-solving abilities by engaging learners in active, meaningful tasks.
- Encourages higher learner motivation and engagement due to authentic applications.
