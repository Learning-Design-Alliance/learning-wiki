# Fiber Crafting for Proportional Reasoning

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
Proportional reasoning is persistently difficult for learners taught primarily through abstract symbolic ratios. This pattern instead grounds proportional reasoning in fiber crafting, where a personally-defined unit (a knit stitch, a crochet stitch, a fabric fold) is repeated into a larger pattern, and the multiplicative relationship between the unit and the pattern becomes visible and tactile rather than purely symbolic. Three craft instantiations target the same underlying concept through different sensory and material affordances: knitting foregrounds gauge (the ratio between stitch width and height), crochet foregrounds multiplicative row growth (sectors of stitches multiplying outward), and pleating foregrounds hidden-layer ratios (the 3:1 relationship between unfolded and folded fabric length).

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough time for iterative making and unmaking (the source study documented one learner unraveling and restarting a knitting project 12 times to develop a stable sense of her own stitch unit)
- Materials whose properties genuinely constrain the outcome (yarn tension, needle/hook size, fabric spring-back) so that the unit is co-produced by learner and material, not simply specified by the instructor
- A design phase (the source study convened adult crafters — knitters, mathematicians, a fashion designer — to align each craft's natural structure with the target mathematical relationship before running it with youth)
#### Constraints
- This is qualitative case evidence from three focal youth in one camp, not a tested curriculum with measured learning outcomes across a full cohort
- The proportional relationship in each craft is *discovered* through material feedback, not stated up front — a facilitator who intervenes too early to "correct" a stitch or fold may short-circuit the discovery process the pattern relies on

## Design

### Sequence
1. **Knitting a rectangular bag** (gauge): learners cast on stitches, knit rows, and — often through repeated unraveling — discover their personal gauge (stitches per inch, rows per inch), realizing stitch width and height are not equal.
2. **Crocheting a circular bag** (sector growth): learners build a magic-circle foundation and distribute stitches across rows in a multiplicative pattern (e.g., 6, 12, 18 stitches per successive row) to keep the resulting shape flat and circular, adjusting tension when their own stitch counts don't match the needed ratio.
3. **Pleating a fabric bag** (hidden-layer ratio): learners fold fabric into pleats, discovering that each visible pleat corresponds to a 3:1 ratio of unfolded-to-folded fabric length (two hidden layers per visible pleat), and must invent a fixation strategy (e.g., ironing) to make the fold unit persist long enough to build the full pattern.

## Examples
- A 10-year-old knitter unraveled and restarted her project 12 times before recognizing that her stitches were taller than they were wide, then repurposed the resulting extra height as a bag flap rather than reworking the whole piece.
- A 9-year-old crocheter, having distributed stitches unevenly across a row (creating a shape flaw), corrected the resulting circle by deliberately loosening the tension on one stitch to compensate — using material tension itself as a proportional-reasoning tool.
- An 11-year-old, pleating fabric, discovered that pinning alone couldn't hold a fold in place, then invented ironing as a way to "lock in" the 3:1 fold ratio before proceeding to the next pattern unit.
