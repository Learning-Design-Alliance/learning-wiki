# Five "E" Model

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
The Five "E" Model is a popular pattern for implementing [constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) in the classroom, defined by the Biological Science Curriculum Study (BSCS). It sequences a constructivist lesson through five stages: students **Engage** with the topic, **Explore** it directly, **Explain** their experience, **Elaborate** on the concept, and undergo **Evaluate**ion — with evaluation feeding back into the next cycle of lessons rather than only closing the current one.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for genuine student-led exploration before formal vocabulary or explanation is introduced
- A teacher willing to act primarily as facilitator during Explore, providing materials and guidance rather than direct instruction
#### Constraints
- Direct instruction should be minimal during Explore — introducing formal explanation too early undercuts the model's premise that terminology is more meaningful once a learner already has a concrete experience to attach it to
#### Grain Size
lesson

### Target Goals
- Inquiry-driven concept formation, where formal terms and explanations are introduced after (not before) direct experience with the phenomenon

### Target Learners
- Learners for whom hands-on exploration and peer discussion, rather than lecture-first instruction, is the intended path to understanding

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — the model operationalizes both psychological constructivism (individual discovery during Explore) and social constructivism (peer/teacher dialogue during Explain and Elaborate)
- [Sociocultural Theory](../theories/sociocultural-theory.md) — the Explain stage's introduction of formal terminology by the teacher-as-expert, after the student already has an experiential referent, mirrors scaffolded vocabulary acquisition within a zone of proximal development

## Design

### Sequence
1. **Engage** — Students have their first encounter with the topic through questions, discussion, or a problem, connecting it to prior knowledge. This assesses current understanding, establishes the lesson's organizational groundwork, and stimulates anticipation.
2. **Explore** — Students directly explore the topic and materials, individually or (preferably) in groups, building a common understanding and practicing communication about it. The teacher facilitates and provides materials but keeps direct instruction minimal, letting students guide their own inquiry.
3. **Explain** — Students put their experience into communicable form: articulating process, sequence, and results to themselves, peers, or the teacher. The teacher introduces formal terminology only now — replacing a student's informal description (e.g., magnets "stick" to metal) with the correct term (e.g., "an attracting force") — because the student now has an experiential context to attach the term to, and because establishing common language aids both communication and the teacher's diagnosis of misconceptions.
4. **Elaborate** — Students expand on the concept, make connections to related concepts, and apply their understanding to new contexts or real-world examples (e.g., extending an observation about a lamppost's changing shadow to questions about where to plant flowers for sunlight). These extensions often prompt further inquiry and new understandings.
5. **Evaluate** — An ongoing process of assessing understanding throughout all four prior stages, plus a more formal assessment (test, report, presentation, rubric, or checklist) to determine whether learning objectives were met. Evaluation is not necessarily the lesson's endpoint: its results can reveal gaps needing further enrichment and can directly shape the design of future lessons, giving the whole model a cyclical structure in which questions lead to answers that lead to further questions.

### Affordances
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — sequences discovery before formal explanation, consistent with a constructivist view of concept formation
- [Bloom's Taxonomy](../theories/blooms-taxonomy.md) — the stages naturally progress from lower to higher cognitive levels, from initial engagement (Remembering/Understanding) through Elaborate and Evaluate (Applying, Analyzing, Evaluating, Creating)

### Personalization
- Explore-stage group size and scaffolding can be adjusted for learners needing more structure, without changing the overall five-stage sequence
