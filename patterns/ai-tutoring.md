# AI Tutoring

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 413 claims (185 for, 173 mixed, 55 against) · 282 studies (105 causal, 60 review, 38 design, 28 theoretical, 19 quant-synthesis, 17 associational, 15 qualitative), `q1`–`q4` · 58 of 282 report an effect size · 397 claims rest on one study

## Description and scope

Tutoring delivered by software rather than a person: step-based intelligent tutoring systems that track a learner's work on each step and give hints and feedback, and conversational tutors built on large language models that answer, question and explain in dialogue. The decisions it covers are how much the tutor gives away (answers, hints, Socratic questions), how it keeps the learner doing the thinking, how it checks understanding rather than accepting a correct-looking answer, how it guards against over-reliance and wrong answers, and when it refers the learner to a human tutor, mentor or instructor.

It draws on three older literatures that conversational AI tutors inherit: **pedagogical agents** (animated or embodied on-screen characters that explain, model, give feedback and motivate), **teachable agents** (learning by teaching a computer agent that learns from the learner, as in Betty's Brain, and the protégé effect), and **computer-supported collaborative learning (CSCL)**, where software scripts, scaffolds or takes part in a learning conversation as a peer or partner.

The study configurations below are evidence; the response-dependent policy is an **untested design proposal**. Use it to gather better evidence about a learner group and a tutor configuration, not to certify that a tutor "works" or that a learner has mastered something because the tutor says so.

**What the evidence does and does not test.** The wiki's strongest evidence is on *step-based intelligent tutoring systems* against conventional instruction: three meta-analyses (Ma et al. 2014, k = 107; Kulik & Fletcher 2016, 50 evaluations; [VanLehn 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369)) agree on a moderate advantage over classroom instruction, textbooks and other computer-based instruction and no detected difference from one-to-one human tutoring, with much smaller effects on standardized than on locally developed tests. For *LLM tutors* the wiki records a handful of randomized studies, mostly single-session and immediate: an unguarded GPT-4 interface raised practice scores and lowered later unaided exam scores, while a guardrailed tutor removed the harm without a detected gain ([Bastani et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)); a research-designed physics tutor beat an in-class active-learning lesson on an immediate post-test ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6)); over five weeks, a structured tutor raised a post-test over a holdout group free to use consumer chatbots, in a small preregistered field experiment ([Yang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.10939); 86 randomised, 46 analysed); and with about 2,400 adults practising GRE items, AI tutoring and expert human tutoring each beat no tutoring by similar amounts in roughly one session each (Northcutt et al. 2026). No study recorded here follows an LLM tutor across a whole course to a delayed or transfer outcome. Pedagogical-agent evidence rests largely on reviews of older multimedia experiments; teachable-agent evidence on one meta-analysis of learning by teaching and a few small studies; CSCL evidence here is two meta-analyses of collaboration scripts by one research group (Vogel et al. 2016; [Radkowitsch et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-020-09316-4)): a small effect on domain learning and a larger one on collaboration skills, against unscripted collaboration, with no AI partner in the studies. So the configuration this page defends is narrow: a tutor that works step by step, withholds final answers by default, escalates help contingently, is judged on unaided and delayed performance, and has a named human route.

[Mastery learning](mastery-learning.md) decides when a learner moves on; [self-regulated learning](self-regulated-learning.md) concerns the learner's own planning and monitoring; [practice with simulated people](practice-with-simulated-people.md) covers AI role-play partners and simulated patients or clients; [mentor support in self-paced online learning](mentor-support-in-self-paced-online-learning.md) covers the human side of the handoff. This pattern is about the tutor's moves inside a task.

## Inputs: establish the design brief

| Field | Record or elicit |
|---|---|
| Learner and starting observation | Task-specific starting knowledge (`novice`, `intermediate`, `advanced`, or `?`); an unaided attempt at a representative task, with reasoning; prior use of AI tools and how the learner says they use them (for answers, hints, checking). Preserve unknowns. |
| Context and activities | Setting and mode (`classroom`, `online-instructor-led`, `online-self-paced`, `workplace-clinical`); whether a human tutor, mentor or instructor is reachable and how fast; time per session and number of sessions; the exact tutor configuration: model or system, system prompt or tutoring rules, answer-withholding rules, hint ladder, problem-specific content given to the tutor, agent persona or voice, logging. |
| Learner-valued goal | Ask what the learner wants from the tutor (finish the assignment, pass the assessment, be able to do this at work) and why. Record disagreement with the designer objective: a learner who wants answers and a designer who wants independent skill want different tutors, and the evidence below says which one each will get. |
| Designer objective | The capability (`procedure`, `concept`, `complex-skill`, `judgement`), whether the domain is well structured (steps can be checked) or ill structured, and which aids are permitted when the capability is assessed. |
| Outcome | Separate measures for assisted performance during practice, unaided performance at the end, a delayed unaided measure and, where it matters, transfer to a changed task. Say whether the test is developed locally (close to the tutored problems) or standardized. Add a reliance or verification measure if over-reliance is a risk. |

A request to "add an AI tutor" does not specify these inputs. Ask for them before choosing a tutor configuration or predicting a gain. Check first that the tutor's answers on the course's own problems are correct: in the wiki, LLM-generated hints were wrong, too general or gave the answer away about a third of the time in one coding study, and [expert reviewers discarded a quarter to nearly half of generated hints](../claims/genai-hints-effective-but-require-validation.md) [~M] in another. Do not take a learner's stated intention as a forecast of how they will use the tutor: among 98 adolescents, [learning-oriented intentions did not predict enacted help-seeking](../claims/intent-enactment-gap-genai-help-seeking.md) [~M], while those who asked for "just the final answers" did request more answers. Read the log as well as asking.

## Sequence and conditional policy

1. **Elicit an unaided attempt first.** Before the tutor opens, ask for the learner's own attempt and reasoning on a representative task. Keep it short and record it as a learning exposure. This is the baseline against which tutored practice is later read.
2. **Configure the tutor for the objective, not for helpfulness.** For a structured task, set the tutor to work step by step, to withhold final answers by default, and to give problem-specific hints (teacher- or designer-written content in the prompt, or a validated hint bank). Ground it in the course's own materials (slides, notes, worked solutions) and have it say which one it is drawing on; a tutor answering from general knowledge invents content (see the grounding row below). Start contingent: the least help that moves the learner, beginning with a cue or leading question, more only after a failed attempt, less after success. Do not pass identity labels about the learner (group membership, a disability or motivation label) into the tutor's prompt; personalise from the learner's work instead. Test the tutor on the course's own problems before learners see it, and do not judge it by how helpful its turns are rated: [turns that leaked the answer were rated more helpful and followed by less independent work](../claims/per-turn-leakage-coupling-primary-base.md) [~M]. Include in that test a learner who insists on the answer: in a teacher-configured K-12 tutor, [direct requests for answers doubled the rate of direct answers (26.1% against 12.5%)](../claims/student-pressure-shifts-ai-toward-answers.md) [~M], although the tutor kept scaffolding in most turns. Show the learner the tutor's answer or a short summary of its reasoning, not a full reasoning trace: in a preregistered experiment with 559 adults, [full traces lowered AI-assisted reasoning performance against answer-only (d = −0.54) and summary traces (d = −0.48)](../claims/full-traces-impair-ai-assisted-reasoning-performance.md) [-M] while [raising trust in the AI](../claims/trace-exposure-raises-trust-and-hedonic-appeal.md) [~M].
3. **Tutored practice, dose by session.** Early sessions (the first one or two in a unit): 20–40 minutes of tutored step-based practice with hints available and an explanation request at each step. Middle sessions: raise the threshold for help (a hint only after an attempt; no worked step without a learner explanation) and add prompts to check the tutor's claims. Late sessions: tutor off for at least one unaided practice set before the assessment. Fade per skill on the learner's own performance rather than by calendar where the tutor can track skills: in a step-based geometry tutor, [fading worked steps adaptively, skill by skill, beat fixed fading and plain tutored problem solving](../claims/adaptive-fading-worked-examples-benefits.md) [+W] (on the delayed test in the classroom replication). Move a learner on when unaided work on a changed problem meets the agreed criterion, not when tutored practice does. The session doses are a proposal; that one claim tests fading inside a step-based tutor, and none tests a dose or a fading schedule for a conversational AI tutor.
4. **Read the log, not only the score.** Record what was asked (answer, hint, explanation, check), how many hints, whether the full answer was given, and how often the learner verified the tutor. Where the tutor tracks skills, read accuracy per skill, not overall: [overall percent correct can stay flat while each skill's accuracy rises, because later tasks are harder](../claims/constant-error-rate-rising-skill-correct.md) [~M], and a skill with no rising curve points to a fault in the tutor's model of the skill, as [log analysis found in an algebra tutor](../claims/learning-curve-revealed-coefficient-one-flaw.md) [+M] and as [a data-derived model that improved mastery when used to redesign a geometry unit](../claims/datashop-model-improved-geometry-mastery.md) [+M] illustrates. Watch for learners who never signal confusion: in one tutor deployment [a "passive consumer" pattern was visible only in longitudinal analytics](../claims/itas-behavioral-archetypes.md) [~M], not in any single session. Use the table below.
5. **Hand off to a human on named triggers.** Repeated impasse at the same step, a learner asking for the answer repeatedly, a tutor error the learner accepted, distress, or a question the tutor cannot answer. Name who takes over and how fast. As a proposed default, change approach after a second failed turn at the same impasse and hand off after about three: in 6,630 impasse turns with guided LLM tutors, [next-turn recovery fell from 39.7% at onset to 26.2% at the third turn and 18.8% from the sixth, about 13% lower odds with each turn](../claims/recovery-declines-with-impasse-depth.md) [~M], and help-seeking impasses fell most steeply once the first help had failed (an association in logs, not a test of a trigger). Two studies recorded here support human involvement alongside software; none tests a specific trigger.
6. **Reobserve unaided and later.** Score an unaided, changed task at the end of the unit and again after a delay, separately from tutored practice. If tutored practice is high and unaided performance is not, treat it as a configuration problem, not as learning.

| Observation | Discriminating probe | Proposed action, with its uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Tutored practice scores high, unaided scores flat or lower | Compare the log: share of turns asking for the answer or receiving it; give one matched unaided item mid-unit. | If answer-taking dominates, tighten answer-withholding and require an attempt before a hint. This is the pattern Bastani et al. observed with an unguarded interface; the guarded configuration removed the harm but did not show a gain, so expect no more than parity until an unaided measure shows otherwise. |
| Learner asks for the answer repeatedly, or skips | Ask what they are trying to get done and by when; check whether the task is beyond reach with the current hints; check where in the session it happens. | If the task is out of reach, step back (a worked example or a simpler step) rather than release the answer. If the goal is completion under time pressure, negotiate the goal; refer to a human mentor if it persists. Expect drift: [within a session, requests shifted from conceptual and procedural help toward answers and verification](../claims/within-session-hs-shifts-toward-closure-srl-increases.md) [~M], so hold the answer-withholding rule late in a session, not only early. Expect it most from learners who are struggling: in 482 biology students' trace data, [the lowest-scoring quartile opened worked solutions more often while hint use did not differ by quartile](../claims/solution-access-differs-hints-do-not.md) [~M] (small effect, associational). The branch itself is untested. |
| Learner accepts a wrong tutor answer | Ask them to check the step against a source or a second method. | Add a short reflection or verification prompt; warning labels alone did not reduce reliance in one study recorded here. Do not rely on learners checking unprompted: in one classroom log [verification was 16% of requests on average and the median learner made none](../claims/instrumental-help-seeking-procedural-low-verification.md) [~M], although most had said they wanted the AI to check their understanding. Log the error and fix the tutor's content. |
| Learner rarely asks for help but fails unaided | Compare hint use with errors; ask whether they avoided help. | Expect it: [most students in one course said they sometimes avoided asking for help when they needed it](../claims/students-avoid-help-seeking-despite-need.md) [~M]. A warning that the tutor can be wrong raised hint requests in one classroom study; a metacognitive help-seeking tutor [improved some kinds of help-seeking](../claims/help-tutor-improved-in-tutor-help-seeking.md) [~M] but [produced no domain learning gain](../claims/help-tutor-no-domain-learning-gains.md) [~M] in another. Prompt help at impasse rather than waiting to be asked: [proactive feedback raised feedback uptake](../claims/propact-feedback-increases-feedback-uptake.md) [+M] in a within-subjects pair-programming study, and in a randomised distance-education experiment with 120 learners a tutor that reached out raised use of [lexical](../claims/proactive-tutoring-increases-lexical-help-use.md) [+M] and [metacognitive help tools](../claims/proactive-tutoring-increases-metacognitive-help-use.md) [+M] whether the tutor was a person or the computer, while [use of the conceptual help tool did not change](../claims/no-tutoring-effect-on-conceptual-help-use.md) [~M]. Still an untested branch for learning outcomes. |
| Learner stops asking the tutor questions | Look back at what the tutor answered when they did ask: relevant, correct? | Route open questions to a human or a curated source and fix the tutor's grounding. In AutoTutor, answers to student questions were often irrelevant or wrong, and [student questions extinguished quickly](../claims/autotutor-student-questions-extinguish.md) [-M]. |
| Correct with hints at the first step, stuck later | Probe the later step alone. | Escalate the hint ladder for that step only; change approach (address the error) rather than repeat the same question: in one tutor-log study [a scripted question repeated after a failed one halved the odds of recovery](../claims/repeating-scripted-question-lowers-recovery.md) [-M]. If the impasse persists past about three turns, hand off (step 5). |
| Fluent explanation in dialogue, weak on a changed problem | A changed problem without the tutor. | Add varied unaided practice; the dialogue may have carried the reasoning. Untested branch. |

## Choosing configurations from evidence

- **Whether to expect a step-based tutor to beat ordinary instruction.** [Intelligent tutoring systems improve achievement over non-individualised instruction](../claims/adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes.md) [+S]: Ma et al. (107 effect sizes, 14,321 participants) and Kulik & Fletcher (50 controlled evaluations, median 0.66 SD; random-effects g = 0.50, 95% CI [0.40, 0.59]) both find a moderate advantage. [The overall weighted effect was g = .41 under random effects](../claims/its-meta-analysis-overall-effect-0-41.md) [+M]. [By comparator](../claims/its-beats-large-group-cbi-textbooks-not-human-tutoring.md) [~M], tutoring systems beat large-group human instruction (g = .44), individual computer-based instruction (g = .57) and textbooks or workbooks (g = .36), but showed no significant difference from small-group (k = 4, g = .05) or individual human tutoring (k = 5, g = −.11); equivalence was not tested. In Kulik & Fletcher's set, [tutored students outscored controls in 46 of 50 evaluations, by more than 0.25 SD in 39](../claims/its-beat-controls-in-92-percent-of-evaluations.md) [+M], with [a median of 0.66 SD (random-effects g = 0.50)](../claims/its-meta-analysis-median-es-066-sd.md) [+M]. For school STEM, [AI-enabled personalised instruction gave g = 0.455 across 99 effects from 32 randomised trials](../claims/ai-personalized-stem-overall-effect-g0455.md) [+M]; its subgroup of [comprehensive courses (g = 2.16)](../claims/comprehensive-courses-largest-subject-effect.md) [~M] is an outlier, not a forecast. Against no instruction at all the effect is larger, [g = 1.23 across nine effect sizes in Ma et al.](../claims/its-versus-no-treatment-controls-g-1-23.md) [+M], which is why the comparator has to be named. Within the same classes, [ninth-graders gained more from tutor-based than from paper-based practice on matched problems](../claims/tutor-practice-greater-learning-gains-than-paper.md) [+M] (counterbalanced, 54 students, no effect size printed).
- **Human tutoring is the ceiling to compare against, and it is lower than often said.** [Human tutoring had a medium effect over no tutoring](../claims/tutoring-effectiveness-comes-from-scaffolding-and-feedback.md) [+S]: VanLehn's review found d = 0.79 for human tutoring and d = 0.76 for step-based tutoring systems against no tutoring (abstract only). In a small study in the same claim (11 tutor–student pairs), students learned as well when tutors only prompted them to construct explanations as when tutors also explained and gave feedback, which is one reason this page defaults to eliciting rather than telling. An editorial reports a study in which [pedagogically expert biology tutors who reverted to explaining produced less deep learning than more interactive tutors who scaffolded](../claims/pedagogical-experts-less-deep-learning-tutoring.md) [+W], and in a real programming course [an LLM agent that elicited drew more reflection messages from students than one that told](../claims/elicit-agent-more-reflection-messages-lad.md) [+M] (a process measure, not learning). [AutoTutor's gains were virtually equivalent to experienced human tutors](../claims/autotutor-gains-equivalent-to-human-tutoring.md) [+M] in typed or spoken physics tutoring, with no effect size printed, and [AutoTutor gains of 0.3 to 0.8 sigma over reading text for the same time](../claims/autotutor-learning-gains-0-3-to-0-8-sigma-vs-reading.md) [+M] ([about one letter grade over reading a textbook](../claims/autotutor-improves-learning-one-letter-grade.md) [+M], in a review's summary of a dozen experiments). Not every synthesis finds parity with human tutors: a review reports that Steenbergen-Hu and Cooper found [tutoring systems moderately effective but less effective than human tutoring](../claims/its-moderate-effect-less-than-human-tutoring.md) [~M], while still ahead of classroom instruction, reading and homework. For LLM tutors, a randomised study of about 2,400 adults practising GRE items found [AI and expert human tutoring both raised gains over no tutoring](../claims/ai-human-tutoring-beat-control-gre-gains.md) [+M] (AI by 6.15 percentage points, about 1.5–2 more of 27 items), [pooled AI tutoring statistically equivalent to human tutoring](../claims/pooled-ai-tutoring-equivalent-gre-learning-gains.md) [+M] within ±0.25 SD, and [one open model individually equivalent to the human tutors at far lower cost](../claims/gemma-4-31b-equivalent-human-tutoring-918x-cheaper.md) [+M]; but [human tutors stayed ahead of the pooled AI tutors in every verbal domain, while the best AI tutors led in quantitative ones](../claims/best-ai-tutors-lead-quantitative-not-verbal-domains.md) [~M] (descriptive). That was roughly one session per learner on an immediate test. So "as good as a human tutor" is not a safe forecast; "better than the usual alternative" is the safer one.
- **The test decides the size of the effect.** [Effects were large on locally developed tests and small on standardized ones](../claims/test-type-moderates-its-effects.md) [~M]: average ES 0.73 against 0.13 (g 0.62 against 0.09) across Kulik & Fletcher's evaluations. Plan an outcome that is not written by the tutor's designers, or expect a smaller effect on it. [The largest effects in that set](../claims/darpa-digital-tutor-largest-its-effects.md) [+M] (ES 1.97 and 3.18) came from one system for Navy technicians compared with longer classroom courses on locally developed tests; they are outliers, not a forecast. The same gap shows within one system: Cognitive Tutor Algebra students [outscored peers by about 0.3 SD on standardized tests and by 0.7 to 1.2 SD on problem-solving tests](../claims/cognitive-tutor-algebra-standardized-test-advantage.md) [~W], and a teacher-comparison study in Moore, Oklahoma, which met What Works Clearinghouse standards, [found about 0.4 SD on a standardized end-of-course test](../claims/cognitive-tutor-moore-oklahoma-outcomes.md) [+M]. For LLM hints in a non-randomised physics course, [hint use was associated with exam performance only on items that matched the homework](../claims/ai-hints-aligned-exam-performance-only.md) [~M]. Whether the AI is allowed in the test matters as much: in a meta-analysis of generative AI in programming education, [the learning effect was g = 0.76 when AI tools were permitted in the assessment and g = −0.06 (not significant) when they were not](../claims/exam-environment-moderates-genai-learning-effects.md) [~M] (11 effect sizes). And short-term gains can fade: [benchmark gains from one tutoring programme did not persist to statewide mathematics tests a year later](../claims/air-tutors-short-term-gains-did-not-persist.md) [~M] (a matched-comparison evaluation; its claim page does not describe an AI tutor).
- **An unguarded LLM tutor can raise practice and lower learning.** In a randomized trial with nearly a thousand high-school mathematics students (2,848 observations), [a standard GPT-4 interface raised assisted practice scores by 0.137 (out of 1) over a textbook-only control mean of 0.28](../claims/gpt-base-improves-assisted-practice-performance.md) [~M], and [lowered the later unaided exam by 0.054, about 17% of the control mean](../claims/gpt-base-harms-unassisted-exam-performance.md) [-M]. [A guardrailed tutor with teacher-designed, problem-specific hints raised practice more (0.361)](../claims/gpt-tutor-improves-practice-more-than-gpt-base.md) [+M] and [removed the exam harm (−0.004, not significant) without a detected exam gain](../claims/gpt-tutor-exam-effect-null.md) [~M]. In a separate experiment, [costs concentrated among participants who used the AI for direct solutions](../claims/persistence-costs-concentrated-among-direct-solution-users.md) [-M] (61% of the AI group; d = −0.36 against controls on later solving, cross-sectional within the AI arm), while hint users resembled controls.
- **A research-designed LLM tutor against active learning.** In a crossover randomized trial in an undergraduate physics course, [students learned more from an AI tutor than from an in-class active-learning lesson on the same content](../claims/ai-tutor-beats-active-learning-post-test.md) [+M] (median post-test 4.5 against 3.5; N = 142 and 174), [a regression effect of 0.63 after controls, which the authors call an underestimate because of a ceiling](../claims/ai-tutor-regression-large-effect-size.md) [+M], in [a median of 49 minutes against a 60-minute class](../claims/ai-tutor-less-time-on-task.md) [+M], with [the advantage holding above and below 40% on the pre-test concept inventory](../claims/ai-tutor-advantage-holds-subpopulations.md) [+M] and [higher self-reported engagement (4.1 against 3.6 of 5) and motivation](../claims/ai-tutor-engagement-motivation-higher.md) [+M]. The outcome was an immediate post-test of one lesson; the trial says nothing about retention or a whole course. Over five weeks, in a preregistered field experiment, [students randomised to a structured AI tutor scored about 6.6 points higher on a 55-point post-test than holdout peers](../claims/structured-ai-tutor-raises-post-test-scores-field-experiment.md) [+M] (46 analysed of 86 randomised), and [the advantage held although 21–42% of the holdout used consumer chatbots in any week](../claims/ambient-consumer-ai-did-not-close-tutor-gap.md) [+M]: the comparison was a structured tutor against the chatbots learners already have. Within the same students, [voice and text weeks gave statistically equivalent weekly mastery, while voice roughly doubled turns and questions at 2.8 times the cost](../claims/voice-text-mastery-equivalent-interaction-transformed.md) [~M].
- **Answer-giving is not always worse on an immediate test, which is why the horizon matters.** In a 50-person experiment, [an unrestricted chatbot produced larger immediate learning deltas than a Socratic chatbot (d = 0.80) or an adaptive exercise system (d = 0.85)](../claims/unrestricted-chatbot-higher-learning-delta-than-socratic-and-adaptive.md) [~M]; in a proctored experiment with 211 undergraduates, [any generative AI during a 35-minute learning phase raised an immediate unaided test by 7.2 points (0.28 SD)](../claims/genai-access-raises-immediate-test-scores.md) [+M]. In writing, [open AI collaboration gave the best supported writing and bounded support the best independent Week 8 writing](../claims/genai-supported-performance-independent-divergence.md) [~M]; in creativity, [free ChatGPT use raised originality on the assisted task (d = 0.79) and the advantage vanished unaided (d = 0.04)](../claims/chatgpt-free-use-originality-gains-vanish-unassisted.md) [~M], and in another randomised experiment [ChatGPT raised the quality and originality of solutions to an ill-defined problem](../claims/chatgpt-improves-quality-elaboration-originality-pit.md) [+M], measured on the assisted task only. Not every unaided measure falls: in a preregistered online experiment with 2,238 adults, [practising cover letters with an AI tool improved later writing without AI (d = .38 against practice without AI), and the gain held a day later](../claims/ai-practice-improved-writing-skill-persisted-one-day.md) [+M], although [the AI group spent less time and typed about a quarter as many keystrokes](../claims/ai-practice-less-effortful-fewer-keystrokes.md) [~M]; the practice followed a lesson on five writing principles, and the horizon was one day. In peer feedback, [gains with AI assistance diminished once it was removed](../claims/genai-performance-gains-diminish-without-assistance.md) [-M] and [students struggled to do the task alone](../claims/ai-assistance-peer-feedback-overreliance.md) [-M] (one study, reported by two sources). The syntheses agree that the pooled number mostly measures assisted performance: [a meta-analytic g = 0.7 for generative AI is argued to be task success rather than learning](../claims/genai-meta-analysis-g-0-7-performance-not-learning.md) [~M]; a STEM meta-analysis had [extreme heterogeneity (I² = 96%), a prediction interval spanning harm to benefit, and no overall effect after adjusting for publication bias](../claims/genai-learning-effects-heterogeneous-conditional.md) [~M]; and in a meta-analysis of 29 experiments on higher-order thinking [instructional method did not significantly moderate the effect](../claims/genai-instructional-method-moderator.md) [~M]. Read immediate gains as provisional until a delayed unaided measure agrees.
- **Contingent help, and how much to give away.** [Computer-based scaffolding showed g = 0.46 across 144 STEM studies](../claims/scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S], and [contingent scaffolding beat fixed or absent support](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M] in small tutoring studies. In an LLM tutor comparison, [a tutor that escalated and faded on what the learner did gave away the full answer in one session in sixteen, against one in six for a question-only tutor and three in five for an answer-giving one](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-surrenders-full-answer-least.md) [+M]. [Answer-revealing turns were followed by less independent student work on every tutor model tested](../claims/leakage-reduces-next-turn-independence-all-bases.md) [-M]. In a logic tutor with 113 undergraduates, [adaptive scaffolding policies beat a non-adaptive control on the post-test](../claims/adaptive-icap-scaffolding-improves-posttest-logic-tutor.md) [+M]. How a model is trained changes how much it gives away, though these results are benchmark or simulated-student evaluations with no learners: [guardrail post-training cut leak-or-overhelp turns from 0.35 to 0.03](../claims/tactutor-guardrail-flags-improve-accept-leak.md) [+M], and [reinforcement-learning alignment without a directness reward cut leakage from 27% to 13% and raised Socratic scaffolding](../claims/grpo-without-directness-raises-socratic-scaffolding-effectiveness.md) [+M], but in another tutor [alignment collapsed teaching-knowledge explanations from 18% to 6.5% of sentences](../claims/alignment-shifts-mkt-collapse-exploratory-surge.md) [~M] in favour of questions. A tutor tuned only to withhold can stop explaining; test both. A live deployment shows the same trade with learners: in a K-12 mathematics tutor, [removing a verbose guidance section cut answer giveaways by about 85% and cognitive engagement by about 18%](../claims/math-agent-guidance-limit-tradeoff.md) [~M].
- **Feedback content.** [Feedback averages d = 0.48 (435 studies) but more than a third of feedback interventions in an older meta-analysis reduced performance](../claims/feedback-improves-learning.md) [~S]; information-rich feedback is the moderator. [Praise for intelligence after success undermined later persistence](../claims/feedback-praise-reduces-learning.md) [-S], so a tutor's encouragement should name the process, not the person. [ChatGPT-generated hints produced significant gains over no hints](../claims/chatgpt-hints-significant-gains-versus-control.md) [+M] in an online algebra experiment (N = 274, crowd workers), but [GPT-4 feedback was targeted for 66% of errors and too general, wrong or answer-revealing for about 35%](../claims/gpt4-feedback-66-percent-targeted-35-percent-flawed.md) [~M], and on real student data [it passed simulated-student helpfulness checks only about 35% of the time](../claims/gpt-feedback-passes-automated-helpfulness-35-percent.md) [-M]. Two results support the default of cueing before telling and of giving feedback during the step: a review of human tutoring reports that [cueing with leading questions tends to have higher impact than other feedback](../claims/subtle-cueing-feedback-higher-impact.md) [+M], and within one adaptive tutor [adding automated real-time feedback improved learners' progression on a four-level understanding scale (d = 0.73, as a review reports it)](../claims/real-time-feedback-its-solo-progression-advantage.md) [+M]. "During the step" need not mean "at the first error": in a randomised spreadsheet-tutor experiment, [a tutor that let learners notice and correct their own errors before stepping in gave better delayed transfer (74% against 60%) than one that flagged each error at once](../claims/intelligent-novice-error-correction-better-transfer.md) [~M]. Hints need not come from an LLM: [hints derived from past students' solution paths were correct over 80% of the time, and logic students solved more proofs with them in a pilot](../claims/hint-factory-mdp-hints-effective.md) [+W] (as a review reports it). For second-language writing, [GPT-4 feedback and human tutor feedback produced no detected difference in progress over six weeks](../claims/gpt4-feedback-no-superior-linguistic-progress-enl.md) [~M] (48 students, quasi-experimental; equivalence not tested). Emotional support is not a substitute for cognitive help, nor has it been shown to add to it: in a classroom experiment with 42 seventh-graders, [an LLM giving mindful, reflective support and one giving cognitive support produced the same algebra gains and the same fall in maths anxiety](../claims/its-reduces-executive-state-ma-improves-algebra.md) [~M].
- **Over-reliance is not fixed by a warning.** [A brief metacognitive reflection prompt cut acceptance of incorrect AI advice (62.4% to 39.7%; OR = 0.40)](../claims/reflection-reduces-incorrect-ai-advice-acceptance.md) [+M], whereas [warning labels alone did not reduce reliance](../claims/genai-warning-labels-insufficient-reliance.md) [-M] in the medical-education studies a review reports. Learners can spot an error and follow the system anyway: [80.7% detected flawed recommendations yet deferred](../claims/surface-validation-works-substantive-validation-fails.md) [-M]; [7 of 15 students failed a task with plausible falsehoods despite checking](../claims/checking-coexists-with-unresolved-error-zhang.md) [-M]. Where verification was measured, [checking the AI's claims against sources predicted gains](../claims/checking-ai-claims-predicts-gains.md) [+M]; within a randomised trial with 318 university students, [trying first, verifying and correcting the AI's errors were associated with higher exam scores, and copying its solutions was not](../claims/proactive-critical-ai-engagement-higher-exam-scores.md) [+M] (an association within the AI arm, not a randomised contrast of behaviours). The reflection prompt worked selectively: [students kept following correct advice and rejected more incorrect advice](../claims/reflection-selective-not-blanket-ai-rejection.md) [+M]. In the same study, [open ChatGPT support raised students' final confidence over deciding alone, and reflection reduced the boost](../claims/chatgpt-support-increases-final-confidence.md) [~M]. How the tutor presents itself moves trust more than accuracy: [full reasoning traces raised trust without raising performance](../claims/trace-exposure-raises-trust-and-hedonic-appeal.md) [~M], and a review reports that [users' confidence in model answers exceeded the models' own, and explanations reflecting the model's confidence narrowed the gap](../claims/calibration-gap-configuration-dependent.md) [+W], so have the tutor state its uncertainty. A short AI-literacy intervention did not stop the reverse problem, [an assistant carrying the user's own errors into its advice](../claims/intervention-did-not-reduce-error-propagation.md) [~M] (60 participants; a null test, not equivalence); test the tutor for agreeing with a learner's wrong step.
- **Guided prompting of the learner's AI use.** [Theory-guided five-step prompting improved open-ended explanations over unprompted AI access and slides](../claims/structured-prompting-improves-open-ended-ai-learning.md) [+M] (95 undergraduates, construction engineering; 12.41 against 10.45 and 9.68).
- **Human in the loop.** In a preregistered randomized trial with about 900 tutors, [students whose human tutors had real-time AI suggestions were 4 percentage points more likely to master the session's topic](../claims/tutor-copilot-raises-exit-ticket-mastery-4pp.md) [+S] (9 points for students of lower-rated tutors, per a review). In a quasi-experiment with 635 middle-school students, [adding human tutors to AI tutoring raised relative MAP growth by 61% within the analysis bandwidth](../claims/human-ai-tutoring-improves-map-growth-over-ai-only.md) [+M] (the full-sample 26% was not significant) and [time on task by 25%](../claims/human-ai-tutoring-increases-time-on-task.md) [+M]. Within that study, sending human tutors proactively to lower-performing students is not yet shown to beat letting students call for help: [at the assignment cutoff the proactive and reactive groups did not differ significantly](../claims/didc-cutoff-null-proactive-benefit-increases-below-cutoff.md) [~M], and [skill proficiency rose in both while the gap between them widened, in favour of the reactive group](../claims/human-ai-tutoring-skill-proficiency-gap-widens.md) [~M], which started higher.
- **Pedagogical agents: the content and the voice matter more than the face.** A review reports that [a meta-analysis of 43 studies found agents enhance learning against no agent](../claims/pedagogical-agents-enhance-learning.md) [+M] (no effect size printed). [AutoTutor's talking head added little beyond the content of the dialogue](../claims/autotutor-content-matters-not-talking-head.md) [~M]; [effects are attributed mainly to the agent's voice](../claims/agent-effects-due-to-voice-not-visual-presence.md) [~M], with transfer rather than recall improved; [iconic and realistic appearance did not differ](../claims/apa-appearance-null-effect.md) [~M]. In one 2×2 experiment with 72 undergraduates, [a high-competency learning companion improved application (d = 0.46)](../claims/high-competency-pal-improves-application.md) [+M], [a low-competency one raised self-efficacy (d = 0.49)](../claims/low-competency-pal-raises-self-efficacy.md) [~M], and [a proactive companion improved recall over a responsive one (d = 0.78)](../claims/proactive-pal-improves-recall.md) [+M]; [the high-competency companion was also liked more (d = 0.91)](../claims/high-competency-pal-more-positive-attitudes.md) [+M], and [a responsive companion did not improve attitudes](../claims/responsive-pal-no-attitude-benefit-no-interaction.md) [~M]. Reviews of older agent studies add that [animated personas beat static ones and the voice contributes, sometimes more than the animation](../claims/animated-persona-and-voice-effects.md) [+M]; that [gains are clearer for motivation than for learning](../claims/apa-affective-gain-stronger-than-cognitive.md) [~M]; that [mere human-like presence shows no effect, while likeable agents raised motivation and transfer](../claims/no-evidence-mere-visual-presence-social-cue-hypothesis.md) [~M]; that [facial expressions helped attitudinal learning but hurt procedural learning](../claims/nonverbal-effects-depend-on-task-type.md) [~M]; and that [speech and gesture saying the same thing aided recall more than complementary ones](../claims/speech-gesture-redundancy-better.md) [+M]. One review reports that [students rated agents of their own ethnicity as more credible and engaging, while agent ethnicity did not change learning in another study](../claims/same-ethnicity-agents-more-credible.md) [~W]. More agents are not better: [adding a second writing agent raised reported overload](../claims/second-agent-increases-cognitive-load-writing.md) [~M]. The most recent meta-analysis in the wiki puts the overall benefit low: [learning with a multimedia agent beat learning without one by g+ = 0.20](../claims/pedagogical-agents-small-overall-learning-benefit.md) [+M] (32 effect sizes, N = 2,104), [2D agents tended to do better than 3D ones (g+ = 0.38 against 0.11)](../claims/2d-agents-tend-more-effective-than-3d.md) [~M], and [most agent features, including gesture, motion and voice, did not moderate the effect](../claims/agent-characteristics-mostly-not-moderators.md) [~M]. So budget for the dialogue, not the character.
- **Teachable agents and learning by teaching.** [Preparing to teach and then teaching gave g = 0.56 across 28 studies, larger when the teaching was interactive](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-tutor-learning.md) [+S]. [Adding a teachable agent to a fourth-grade science kit improved gains on curriculum and reasoning tests (d = 1.1 and 0.93)](../claims/ta-improves-basic-and-added-value-gains-fourth-grade.md) [+M] in a two-year quasi-experiment with seven classrooms. With an LLM, [students who taught a ChatGPT agent outscored a video group on knowledge](../claims/chatgpt-teachable-agent-knowledge-gains.md) [+M] (n = 20 and 21; the printed η² = 0.74 overstates an effect the paper's own figures put nearer d ≈ 0.7) but [not on code correctness](../claims/chatgpt-teachable-agent-no-correctness-gain.md) [~M], though [scored higher on code clarity and readability](../claims/chatgpt-teachable-agent-code-clearness-readability.md) [+M] (low rigour, small groups). Betty's Brain, the best-studied teachable-agent system, shows why a teachable agent needs its own monitoring: in one classroom study [18 of 40 students made very little progress teaching Betty while 16 taught a correct or nearly correct map](../claims/betty-brain-classroom-performance-dichotomy.md) [~W], in a video study [77% of the feedback from Betty and the mentor agent was ignored](../claims/betty-brain-feedback-ignored-77-percent.md) [-W], and [students who succeeded combined reading, map editing and quizzing in focused sequences and gained more](../claims/more-successful-students-higher-gains-focused-actions.md) [~W] (associational). Scaffolding inside the agent changes what learners do before it changes what they learn: in a four-hour Betty's Brain study, [three scaffolding conditions produced different strategy use but no difference in learning gains](../claims/scaffolding-alters-strategy-use-not-learning-gains.md) [~M]. The protégé effect itself has no claim of its own in the wiki.
- **Collaboration scripts and AI in groups.** Scripting who does what in a collaboration helps: [CSCL scripts raised domain learning over unscripted collaboration](../claims/cscl-scripts-small-positive-domain-learning-effect.md) [+M] (d = 0.20 across 33 effects in Vogel et al.; g = 0.24 across 54 independent effects in Radkowitsch et al., the later and larger pool from the same group), with [a larger effect on collaboration skills](../claims/cscl-scripts-large-effect-collaboration-skills.md) [+M] (d = 0.95, 0.65 without two outliers; [g = 0.72 in the update](../claims/cscl-scripts-medium-positive-collaboration-skills-effect.md) [+M]) and [no significant effect on motivation (g = 0.13)](../claims/cscl-scripts-motivation-non-significant-meta-analysis.md) [~M]; [only subject discipline moderated the effect on collaboration skills, and study duration did not](../claims/further-moderators-discipline-duration-measurement-level.md) [~M]. An AI partner that runs a script inherits this evidence only by analogy: none of these studies had one. The nearest test recorded here is one experiment, reported by journal editors, in which [a conversational agent's prompts improved outcomes for computer-science students individually and for each pair as a whole](../claims/conversational-agent-prompts-improve-individual-and-dyad-outcomes.md) [+W] (no effect size printed), while [students found its automatic interruptions disruptive and reacted to them differently](../claims/agent-interruptions-perceived-disruption-varies.md) [~W]. In the collaboration-scripts moderator analyses, [prompting transactive activities](../claims/transactivity-moderator-not-significant.md) [~M], [the script level](../claims/script-level-moderator-not-significant.md) [~M] and [extra content support](../claims/content-support-moderator-not-significant.md) [~M] did not significantly moderate effects, but each test had low power (8–16%), so "no detected difference" is not "no difference". In a preregistered online experiment (315 participants), [a tutor plus LLM peers produced the highest unaided accuracy (about 65% against 42% for control)](../claims/tutor-plus-peers-strongest-unassisted-math-accuracy.md) [+M]; [the tutor alone also beat control, while error-making LLM peers alone did not reach significance](../claims/tutor-alone-significant-peers-alone-not-math.md) [~M] (exploratory pairwise tests), so the tutor carries most of that result.

Do not rank these results against one another: comparators (no tutoring, textbooks, classroom instruction, active learning, an unguarded chatbot), outcomes (practice, immediate unaided, standardized, transfer) and learners differ. A precise abstention names the missing comparison, outcome or observation and the next way to resolve it.

## Fitting the design to a situation

Ask for four facts when the brief omits them: **is the task structured enough for the tutor to check each step?**, **what is assessed, with what aids, and when (immediately, at the end of the course, later at work)?**, **is a human tutor, mentor or instructor reachable, and how quickly?**, and **what will the tutor actually say, tested on the course's own problems?**

## Illustrative design instance and observation record

*Illustration only, invented, not a tested course.* An online, self-paced introductory statistics module for working adults, with a mentor who answers within one business day. The designer's objective is that each learner can choose and run the right test on a new data set and explain the result; one learner says she wants to pass the module quickly while working full time. Agree that the module assessment is unaided and proctored, and that a delayed check a month later matters to the programme. The first activity asks each learner to choose a test for a short scenario without help. The tutor is configured to work step by step, never to give the final test choice or computed value, to give problem-specific hints written by the course team, and to ask the learner to check its claims against the course notes. Sessions one and two: about 30 minutes of tutored practice; sessions three and four: hints only after an attempt; session five: an unaided practice set with the tutor off. The log flags a learner who asks for the answer three times on one step, or accepts a tutor statement later marked wrong, and sends a message to the mentor.

Record: **unaided first attempt → tutor configuration and content actually used → per-step requests (answer, hint, explanation, check), hints given, answer released or not → tutor errors and whether the learner accepted them → handoffs to a human and their response time → unaided score on a changed task at the end → delayed unaided score**. Preserve the learner's stated purpose and any change in it. An agent may summarize this record, but must not fabricate missing inputs or assign numerical mastery probabilities without a calibrated model.

## Elements and limits

[Hints](../elements/hints.md), [feedback](../elements/feedback.md), [immediate feedback](../elements/immediate-feedback.md), [scaffolding](../elements/scaffolding.md), [fading scaffolding](../elements/fading-scaffolding.md), [Socratic questioning](../elements/socratic-questioning.md), [self-explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md), [worked examples](../elements/worked-examples.md), [pedagogical agent signaling](../elements/pedagogical-agent-signaling.md), [peer teaching](../elements/peer-teaching.md), [collaborative problem solving](../elements/collaborative-problem-solving.md) and [metacognitive reflection](../elements/metacognitive-reflection.md).

This pattern is scoped to the tutor's moves within tasks and the handoff to people. It does not cover AI grading, AI-generated course content, proctoring or AI role-play as a practice partner (see [practice with simulated people](practice-with-simulated-people.md)). The wiki records no randomized comparison of an LLM tutor across a whole course with a delayed or transfer outcome (the longest is a five-week field experiment with 46 analysed and an end-of-period post-test), no test of a handoff trigger, and no test of fading a conversational tutor's help across sessions (the one fading result is for worked steps in a step-based tutor); no trial of grounding a tutor in course materials against an ungrounded one with a learning outcome; and no test of withholding learner identity labels from a tutor's prompt beyond the feedback-bias audit it rests on. Much of the newer LLM-tutor evidence here is benchmark or simulated-student evaluation of the tutor's outputs, which says what a tutor does, not what learners learn. The diagnostic branches above remain to be tested with actual learners.
