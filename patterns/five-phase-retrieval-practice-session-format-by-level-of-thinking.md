# Five-phase retrieval practice session crossing question format and level of thinking

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
The study's session design: read a passage, do a distractor task, answer mixed-format retrieval questions, do a second distractor, then take a final test. The article states the procedure "involved five phases: study phase, first distractor task, retrieval practice phase, second distractor task and final test phase", with 10 MCQs and 10 short-answer questions, each split into five lower-order and five higher-order items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reading passage (980 words on Problem Solving) read for 15 minutes.
- A 1-minute word search puzzle as distractor, whose function the article states is to clear working memory and prevent mental rehearsal.
- Retrieval practice of 10 MCQs and 10 short-answer questions answered within 15 minutes, each set containing five lower-order and five higher-order items.
- A short-answer grading rubric: one point for a fully correct response, half a point for a partially correct one.
- A final test of 20 MCQs similar to, but slightly modified from, the practice questions, given immediately or after three weeks.
#### Constraints
- Delivered with online tools (Google Doc and Google Form Quiz) under a signed integrity declaration.
- No restudy or rereading control condition was included.

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduates enrolled in a Cognitive Psychology course with MUET Band 3 or above English proficiency

### Target Goals
- Reading comprehension of course content measured at lower-order and higher-order levels of thinking
