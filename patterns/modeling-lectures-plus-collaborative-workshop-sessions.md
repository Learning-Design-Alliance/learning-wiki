# Technical mathematics course structure combining expert modeling lectures with collaborative workshop sessions

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern describes the structure of the experimental technical mathematics classes tested in the study. "During the formal instruction, the teacher modeled problem solving sixty percent of the time and used the whole class for group problem solving," while "Collaborative small group problem solving was then done by the students forty percent of the time," with the teacher acting as a coach. Three of five weekly periods were allocated to teacher modeling of solutions using feasible heuristics and describing expert thought processes; the remaining two periods placed students in teacher-assigned groups working on more difficult, application-oriented problems, with the teacher roaming, questioning, scaffolding, and fading assistance as students became more proficient.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must be selected so they can be solved with an appropriate strategy and contain mathematical concepts relevant to the course material
- Teachers must roam among groups, ask students what they are doing and why they selected a method, and provide scaffolding when knowledge is lacking
#### Constraints
- The study took place during ten weeks of a fifteen-week semester in community college technical mathematics classes; the authors recommend more time be allotted for lab sessions in future implementations

### Target Learners
- community college vocational students in industrial technology programs

### Target Goals
- problem-solving skill in technical mathematics
- ability to talk through and defend mathematical solutions
