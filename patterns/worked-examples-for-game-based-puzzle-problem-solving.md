# Before-the-problem complete worked examples for a game-based puzzle task

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study, `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A worked example intervention inserted between two rounds of a commercial computer puzzle game. The article argues "The design or structure of work ed examples plays an important role in their effectiveness" and lists six design dimensions; its own examples were "being given before the problem", "being complete", "not including fading procedure", "using integrated text and diagrams", "using visual-visual instruction", and "using steps". Players studied them after a first game session and before a second.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worked examples positioned on six dimensions the article names: before vs. after, complete vs. incomplete, backward vs. forward fading, text vs. diagrams, visual vs. verbal, and steps vs. subgoals
- A game-based problem-solving task (SafeCracker) played before and after studying the examples
#### Constraints
- The authors report the resulting improvement was small compared to expert performance
- This design omitted fading, verbal instruction and subgoals, which the authors propose adding in future study

### Target Learners
- Adults: undergraduate or graduate students playing a computer puzzle game (SafeCracker)

### Target Goals
- Content understanding and problem-solving strategies (retention and transfer) in a game-based puzzle task
