# Positive Behavioral Interventions And Supports

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 21 claims (17 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 27 studies (11 causal, 6 quant-synthesis, 6 review, 2 theoretical, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q4` · 7 of 27 report an effect size · 16 claims rest on one study

## Description and scope

A reusable, school-wide policy for behaviour as something taught: **a leadership team chooses three to five positively stated expectations → defines what each looks like in every setting → teaches them like academic content and re-teaches them when data show a need → acknowledges students who meet them, specifically and often → corrects briefly and without exclusion where it can → reviews discipline data on a fixed cycle → adds a daily small-group support (Tier 2) for students who do not respond → adds an individual, function-based plan (Tier 3) for those who still do not respond → checks how faithfully each tier is running**. The intended change is behavioural: fewer office referrals, in-school and out-of-school suspensions and days lost to them, and more time in class (`behavioural` outcomes, `pre-k-12`). Gains in achievement are, at most, indirect.

How it sits beside its neighbours, each of which owns its own model: the [Effective Classroom Management Plan principle](../principles/effective-classroom-management-plan.md) holds one teacher's plan for one room, which is what the universal tier asks of every classroom; the [Functional Behavior Assessment principle](../principles/functional-behavior-assessment.md) holds the individual plan this pattern places at Tier 3; the [Social-Emotional Learning pattern](social-emotional-learning.md) teaches named social and emotional skills, where this pattern teaches and acknowledges setting-specific expectations; and [Self-Monitoring](../principles/self-monitoring.md) is where acknowledgment can hand over to the student. This page owns the school-wide, tiered configuration and does not restate those models.

**What the claims in this wiki test.** The relationship is tested by one group-randomized effectiveness trial: 37 Maryland elementary schools, 21 trained in school-wide PBIS (SWPBIS) and 16 comparison schools, with four years of outcome data on 12,344 children, reported in four articles (Bradshaw et al. 2010, 2012 and 2015; Waasdorp et al. 2012) that the wiki records across about 25 claims. Against the comparison schools, children in SWPBIS schools were [33% less likely to receive an office discipline referral](../claims/swpbis-reduces-office-discipline-referrals.md) [+M] (AOR 0.67), but [out-of-school suspensions did not differ](../claims/swpbis-no-effect-suspensions.md) [-M] (AOR 0.97, CI 0.69–1.36; equivalence not tested). Teacher-rated [aggressive and disruptive behaviour](../claims/swpbis-reduces-disruptive-behavior.md) [+M] (ES 0.12), [concentration problems](../claims/swpbis-reduces-concentration-problems.md) [+M] (ES 0.08), [emotion regulation](../claims/swpbis-improves-emotion-regulation.md) [+M] (ES 0.11) and [prosocial behaviour](../claims/swpbis-improves-prosocial-behavior.md) [+M] (ES 0.17) all moved in the expected direction by small amounts, and [the effects on referrals and school services were largest for children rated at-risk or high-risk at baseline](../claims/swpbis-effects-greatest-at-risk-high-risk-children.md) [+M]. [School-level achievement gains did not differ](../claims/swpbis-no-significant-achievement-effect.md) [~M]. The claim pages do not say which tiers the trained schools ran; the trial measured fidelity with the School-wide Evaluation Tool, on which [all trained schools reached and kept the 80% threshold](../claims/swpbis-training-high-fidelity-set.md) [+M]. One quasi-experiment, Gage et al. (2025), recorded across four claims, compares PBIS schools with propensity-score-matched schools on 2020–21 federal discipline data: schools running at least the universal tier with fidelity did not differ from their matches on any exclusion outcome, and the 40 schools running all three tiers with fidelity had about half the out-of-school suspensions. Read together, and only for what they tested: whole-school PBIS in elementary schools lowers referrals and teacher-rated problem behaviour by a small amount, most for children already at risk; a difference in out-of-school suspensions appeared only where the intensive tier ran with fidelity, in a different study and an unusual year. One pre-post study of 37 students with emotional disturbance (Benner et al. 2008) without a comparison group is recorded across five claims. The other claims bear on single parts (taught expectations, universal classroom management, reward design, empathic discipline) or on school-based exclusion programmes in general. No claim here tests PBIS in secondary schools or compares the tiers with each other by random assignment. Each step below says which kind of evidence it has.

## Inputs: establish the design brief

Record what is unknown (for example, referrals that were never written down) rather than assuming it. A request to "implement PBIS" does not specify these. Ask what the data show, which outcome matters and how much staff time exists before choosing expectations, an acknowledgment system or tier entry rules.

## Sequence and conditional policy

The sequence is the earlier page's, with doses and decision rules added. The whole package, as the Maryland trial trained it in elementary schools, has a randomized test (above); single steps do not. Steps 1–3 and 7 have a neighbouring or partial claim; step 8 rests on the one quasi-experimental comparison; steps 5, 7 and 9 also draw on the trial's subgroup and fidelity findings; the doses, timings and entry rules are **untested proposals** unless a claim is named.

1. **Form a team and read the data (before the year starts; one or two half-day meetings, then monthly for 45–60 minutes).** An administrator, three to six staff across grades and roles, and where possible a family member and, in secondary schools, students. Disaggregate the data by student group from the first meeting. Evidence status: untested proposal; [tracking discipline data by student group can show disparate practice](../claims/exclusionary-discipline-harms-and-disproportionality.md) [+W] (a policy report citing research, no effect reported).
2. **Choose three to five positively stated expectations and build a setting matrix** ("be safe, be respectful, be responsible", with two or three observable behaviours for each setting: classroom, corridor, cafeteria, playground, bus, online). Evidence status: [developing and using clear behaviour expectations is rated as strong evidence in a practice guide resting on 14 studies](../claims/clear-behavior-expectations-strong-evidence-14-studies.md) [+M] (teacher-delivered interventions in grades K–5; the number of expectations and the matrix form are the earlier page's, untested).
3. **Teach the expectations like content.** Proposed dose: in the first week, a 10–15-minute lesson per setting, taught in that setting, with modelling, a non-example, and practice; in weeks 2–6, a 5-minute booster two or three times a week; re-teach after each long break and within a week of any month in which data show a setting or time spike. Early in the year the adult models and students rehearse; later, students lead the re-teaching and new students are taught by peers. Evidence status: [universal classroom management practices reduce problem behaviour](../claims/universal-classroom-management-reduces-problem-behavior.md) [+M] (12 classroom studies, K–12, not school-wide PBIS); the dose is an untested proposal.
4. **Acknowledge expected behaviour, specifically and often.** Specific verbal acknowledgment naming the expectation met, from every adult, in every setting; a school-wide token or recognition only if the team wants one. Proposed ratio, untested: about four acknowledgments for each correction, checked by brief observation. Across sessions, move from frequent and tangible (early primary, early in the year) to intermittent, social and privilege-based (later in the year, older students), and from adult-given to student self-monitoring. Evidence status: [expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with an already-interesting task, while verbal praise does not](../claims/rewards-undermine-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~S]; [autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation and engagement](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. Both come from laboratory and meta-analytic work on interest in tasks, carried here to school acknowledgment systems by extrapolation.
5. **Correct briefly, privately and without exclusion where possible.** Name the expectation, re-teach it, and return the student to the activity; decide in advance which behaviours are handled in class and which go to the office. Prepare teachers to treat misbehaviour as a relationship moment. Evidence status: [a brief empathic-discipline exercise for teachers reduced suspensions](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]: middle-school maths teachers in randomized field experiments; the preregistered replication's overall effect was small and marginal, its effect on the gap between student groups larger. The claim's table counts 3 distinct studies. Expect this step to take hold last: in the Maryland trial, [the fidelity subscale for responding to behavioural violations was the one of seven that did not change significantly across all four years, differing only between year 1 and year 4](../claims/swpbis-behavioral-violations-subscale-mixed.md) [~M], and [out-of-school suspensions did not differ from comparison schools](../claims/swpbis-no-effect-suspensions.md) [-M]; a school that wants fewer suspensions has to change its response rules, not only add the universal tier.
6. **Review data on a fixed cycle (monthly, 45–60 minutes).** Look at where, when, who and what: setting, time, grade, student group, behaviour category. Each review ends with one or two changes (re-teach a setting, add supervision, adjust acknowledgment) and names students for Tier 2. Evidence status: untested proposal.
7. **Tier 2 for students who do not respond.** Proposed entry rule, untested: two to five office referrals in a term, or a teacher nomination backed by classroom data. Default support: [Check In/Check Out](../strategies/check-incheck-out.md), a 2–3-minute morning [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) with one adult, a point card with the school's expectations rated each period, and a 2–3-minute check-out with feedback, the card going home. Review at 4–6 weeks: a student who meets the daily point goal (for example 80% of points) on most days for four consecutive weeks moves to self-monitoring and then exits; a student who does not moves to step 8. Evidence status: untested proposal for the rule and the dose; the CICO page's own claims are carried from belonging, empathic discipline and feedback work, not from CICO trials. The children this step is for are the ones the school-wide system already helped most: in the Maryland trial, [at-risk children in SWPBIS schools were less likely to receive an office referral than at-risk children in comparison schools (41.3% against 46.5%)](../claims/swpbis-reduces-odr-at-risk-high-risk.md) [+M], [less likely to be referred to special education (23.2% against 27.0%)](../claims/swpbis-reduces-special-education-referral-at-risk.md) [+M] and [less often received counselling for behaviour](../claims/swpbis-reduces-counseling-services.md) [+M] (29.8% against 35.7%), each with a small printed d (−0.12 to −0.15). So read Tier 2 nominations after the universal tier has run for a term; some students the baseline data would have named no longer need it.
8. **Tier 3 for students who still do not respond: a function-based individual plan** with family involvement, built from a [functional behaviour assessment](../principles/functional-behavior-assessment.md) and integrated with any IEP and [accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md). Evidence status: [schools running all three tiers with fidelity had fewer out-of-school suspensions, days lost and incidents than matched non-PBIS schools](../claims/pbis-three-tiers-fidelity-fewer-oss-pandemic.md) [+M], and the authors [attribute the reduction to the intensive tier](../claims/pbis-three-tiers-fidelity-fewer-oss-pandemic.md) [~W] (their interpretation, not a tested contrast); [intensive interventions should be planned from data and guided by evidence-based practices](../claims/intensive-interventions-data-based-evidence-guided.md) [+W] (guidance, no study); for students with emotional disturbance in self-contained classes, [externalizing and total problems fell over a year of teacher PBIS training](../claims/pbis-reduces-externalizing-total-problems-ed-self-contained.md) [+M] (pre-post, 37 students, no comparison group).
9. **Check fidelity before judging results (once or twice a year with a fidelity tool, such as the TFI the earlier page names).** Report each tier's fidelity beside its outcomes; a tier running below fidelity is fixed before it is judged. Evidence status: [teachers' knowledge and skill in PBIS interacted with changes in student problem behaviour](../claims/tkss-fidelity-ancova-interaction-problem-behavior.md) [+M] and [low-fidelity teachers tended not to see large improvements](../claims/tkss-fidelity-ancova-interaction-problem-behavior.md) [~W] (the same 37-student study); in the matched comparison, [at least Tier 1 fidelity alone showed no effect on exclusions](../claims/pbis-tier1-fidelity-no-effect-pandemic-disciplinary-exclusions.md) [~M]. Fidelity is attainable with training: in the Maryland trial [all trained schools reached and kept the 80% threshold on the School-wide Evaluation Tool, and no comparison school consistently did](../claims/swpbis-training-high-fidelity-set.md) [+M], and [staff ratings of school-wide, non-classroom, classroom and individual support systems all improved](../claims/swpbis-training-improved-ebs-staff-reports.md) [+M] (d 1.08–1.71). Those are measures of the system being in place, not of students' behaviour.

**Across years (the schedule is untested; the earlier page says full effects typically take 2–3 years):** the Maryland trial read its outcomes over four years, and its teacher-rated effects were small differences in growth rather than early drops: [bullying](../claims/swpbis-reduces-growth-teacher-reported-bullying.md) [+M] and [peer rejection](../claims/swpbis-reduces-growth-peer-rejection.md) [+M] rose more slowly in SWPBIS schools, while [both rose over the four years in both kinds of school](../claims/bullying-rejection-increase-approaching-middle-school.md) [~M]. Effects were stronger for the youngest: [children who started in kindergarten gained most on prosocial behaviour and emotion regulation](../claims/swpbis-strongest-kindergarten-exposure.md) [+M], and [the rejection effect was the one grade-cohort interaction found, with older comparison-school children's rejection rising most](../claims/swpbis-earlier-exposure-stronger-rejection-effect.md) [+M]. So judge the system over years, and start it in the earliest grades a school has. In year 1, the universal tier and the data cycle; Tier 2 begins once the data cycle names students reliably, usually in the second term; Tier 3 is built in year 1 for the few students already in crisis and becomes systematic in year 2. Expectations are re-taught every year, and the acknowledgment system is reviewed each spring with students.

## Fitting the design to a situation

**The three facts that most change the decision:**

- **Whether the school can run the intensive tier, not only the universal one.** In the one matched comparison recorded here, schools with only universal (or universal and targeted) fidelity did not differ from their matches; schools with all three did. If unstated, ask: who will conduct functional assessments and write individual plans, and how many students need one now?
- **Which outcome matters and how severe it is.** School-based programmes reduced milder exclusions more readily than out-of-school suspension or permanent exclusion. If unstated, ask: is the target referrals, in-school exclusion, out-of-school suspension, a gap between groups, or climate?
- **The age of the students and who sets the acknowledgment.** Concrete, frequent tokens suit young children in the earlier page's guidance; with adolescents, rewards seen as controlling risk the interest the school wants. If unstated, ask: what grades, and will students help write the expectations and the recognition?

## Choosing configurations from evidence

- **The whole system against matched schools.** One quasi-experiment, recorded on four claims (1 distinct study): schools implementing PBIS were matched by propensity score to schools that were not, and discipline counts were compared for 2020–21. [Schools implementing all three tiers with fidelity had significantly fewer students with out-of-school suspensions, fewer days missed and fewer incidents](../claims/pbis-three-tiers-fidelity-fewer-oss-pandemic.md) [+M]: 40 schools against 152 comparison schools, IRRs 0.50, 0.45 and 0.41 (d = −0.71, −0.65, −0.84). [Schools with at least Tier 1 fidelity showed no significant effect on any of five exclusion outcomes](../claims/pbis-tier1-fidelity-no-effect-pandemic-disciplinary-exclusions.md) [~M], and [the four fidelity conditions did not differ on in-school suspensions or students with only one out-of-school suspension](../claims/pbis-tier1-fidelity-no-effect-pandemic-disciplinary-exclusions.md) [-W]. [The authors attribute the reduction to the intensive tier](../claims/pbis-three-tiers-fidelity-fewer-oss-pandemic.md) [~W], which is their reading, not a contrast they tested. A pandemic year, a quasi-experiment and one study: carry it to "the intensive tier, run with fidelity, is where the matched difference appeared", no further.
- **The universal tier's parts.** [Clear behaviour expectations](../claims/clear-behavior-expectations-strong-evidence-14-studies.md) [+M] rests on a federal practice guide's rating of 14 studies of teacher-delivered interventions in grades K–5 (the claim records the rating, not the studies' effects). [Universal classroom management reduces problem behaviour](../claims/universal-classroom-management-reduces-problem-behavior.md) [+M]: a meta-analysis of 12 K–12 classroom studies, mean classroom effect size 0.80 (ICC .05) or .71 (ICC .10), range −0.05 to 1.74. Both test classroom practices, not a school-wide system.
- **Students with emotional disturbance.** One pre-post study of 37 students in self-contained classes whose teachers had a year of PBIS training, no comparison group: [externalizing and total problems fell](../claims/pbis-reduces-externalizing-total-problems-ed-self-contained.md) [+M], [three syndrome scores fell](../claims/pbis-reduces-externalizing-total-problems-ed-self-contained.md) [+W], [the share meeting clinical criteria fell (total problems 76% to 62%)](../claims/pbis-reduces-externalizing-total-problems-ed-self-contained.md) [+M], and [teacher knowledge and skill interacted with the change](../claims/tkss-fidelity-ancova-interaction-problem-behavior.md) [+M]. Without a comparison group, maturation and regression cannot be ruled out.
- **Acknowledgment design.** [Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement, while verbal praise does not](../claims/rewards-undermine-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~S] (two competing meta-analyses and reviews; the claim's table counts 8 distinct studies) and [autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S] (8 distinct studies in its table, one meta-analysis with an effect). They measure interest in tasks, mostly in laboratories, not behaviour in schools; they bear on how acknowledgment is given, not on whether PBIS works.
- **Discipline responses.** [The brief empathic-discipline exercise](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]: in 2016 suspensions were 4.8% against 9.6% for students of treated and control teachers (31 teachers, five schools); the 2022 preregistered replication (66 randomized teachers, 5,822 students) found a small, marginal main effect and narrowed the Black and Hispanic versus other gap from 10.6 to 5.9 percentage points. It is a teacher-mindset exercise that complements PBIS, not part of it.
- **School-based exclusion programmes in general.** [School-based interventions reduced exclusion by a small amount](../claims/school-based-interventions-small-significant-reduction-exclusion.md) [+M] (67 evaluations, SMD 0.104), and [severe sanctions were less responsive than milder ones](../claims/exclusion-type-moderates-intervention-effect.md) [~M]. These pool many programme types; they set expectations for size, not for PBIS.

Do not read a fall in suspensions in one school after adoption as an effect of PBIS: the [41% drop at one middle school](../claims/pbis-suspensions-decreased-41-percent.md) [+W] has no comparison. Do not read a null result as equivalence, and do not judge a tier that was not running with fidelity. A precise abstention names the missing comparison (the universal tier alone against usual practice, randomized, over two or more years, on referrals and suspensions) and the local observation that would stand in for it: the school's own disaggregated data before and after, read beside its fidelity scores.

## Illustrative design instance and observation record

*Invented illustration, not a tested programme.* A 600-student middle school (`adolescent`, `classroom`, `term-plus`) has 140 out-of-school suspensions a year, most for "defiance", and Black students are suspended at three times the rate of others. The table changed the default in three places: students co-wrote the expectations ("be ready, be kind, be honest", kept from the school's existing values) and chose privileges as acknowledgment rather than tokens; all teachers took the brief empathic-discipline exercise in September and "defiance" was redefined as three observable behaviours; and because the target was out-of-school suspension, the counsellor and a behaviour specialist began function-based plans for eight students in the first term instead of waiting for Tier 2 to run. Expectations were taught in 10-minute lessons per setting in the first week and re-taught in the corridor after the October data showed a spike there; Check In/Check Out started in November with two teaching assistants as mentors. A student group said they wanted to stop being "sent out for nothing"; the school's objective was fewer suspensions. They agreed to report both the total and the gap, and to report removals from class beside suspensions. The claims cover matched schools in one year and teachers in middle-school maths classes, not this school; these are local choices to observe.

Record: **expectations and setting matrix → lessons taught, where and when → acknowledgment form and observed ratio → referral categories and their definitions → monthly data by setting, time, group and category → changes made at each review → Tier 2 entry, card completion and exit → Tier 3 plans, the function each assumes and the student's response → fidelity by tier → year-end suspensions, days lost, removals from class and the gap between groups**. Keep students' and families' stated aims and any change in them. An agent may summarise this record but must not fabricate missing inputs or predict an individual student's response from a school average.

## Elements and limits

[Clear structure](../principles/clear-structure.md), [reinforcement](../elements/reinforcement.md), [behaviour-specific praise](../strategies/behavior-specific-praise.md), [feedback](../elements/feedback.md), [check-in](../elements/check-in.md), [Check In/Check Out](../strategies/check-incheck-out.md), [functional behaviour assessment](../strategies/functional-behavior-assessment.md), [accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) and [belonging](../elements/belonging.md). Theories: [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) (reinforcing expected behaviour rather than punishing the unwanted), [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) (expectations learned by modelling) and [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) (acknowledgment that informs rather than controls, supporting [autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md)); the long-term aim of self-management connects to [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md). Neighbouring principles: [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md), [Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms](../principles/culturally-responsive-classroom-norms.md) and [Wise Feedback Across Difference](../principles/wise-feedback-across-difference.md) for corrections given across difference.

This pattern is scoped to a school-wide, tiered system of taught and acknowledged expectations with data-based decisions. It is not an academic intervention, not a single classroom's plan (see the classroom management principle) and not, by itself, an answer to trauma or severe emotional need, which the intensive tier and other services must address. Whether the universal tier reduces exclusions against usual practice, how long a school takes to show it, and for which students, remains untested in the claims recorded here; the one matched comparison found its difference only where all three tiers ran with fidelity.

## Related Patterns
- [Social-Emotional Learning](social-emotional-learning.md) — teaches named social and emotional skills; PBIS teaches and acknowledges setting-specific expectations, and the two are often run together
- [Direct Instruction](direct-instruction.md) — PBIS borrows its instructional logic (model, practice, reinforce) and applies it to behavioral expectations
- [Cooperative Learning](cooperative-learning.md) — prosocial expectations taught in Tier 1 create the behavioral foundation that collaborative structures require
- [Constructive Alignment](constructive-alignment.md) — shares the systems logic of aligning stated goals (expectations), activities (teaching), and consequences (acknowledgment)

## Examples
- **[PBIS Technical Assistance Center](https://www.pbis.org):** The national OSEP-funded center provides implementation guides, fidelity measures (e.g., TFI, SAS), and state-level implementation data for thousands of U.S. schools.
- **Maryland Safe and Supportive Schools (MDS3):** A statewide initiative combining school-wide PBIS with climate measurement; randomized evaluation showed improved climate and reduced suspensions in high-need schools.
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO):** A widely used Tier 2 program in which a mentor greets the student each morning, reviews goals, and closes the day with feedback and points shared with family — a structured daily [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) cycle.

## Description
Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS) is a multi-tiered framework for establishing the social culture and behavioral supports needed for all students to succeed. Rather than reacting to misbehavior with escalating punishment, schools define a small set of positively stated expectations, explicitly teach them like academic content, and consistently acknowledge students who meet them. Support is organized in three tiers: universal expectations for all students (Tier 1), targeted small-group support for students at risk (Tier 2), and individualized intensive interventions (Tier 3). The framework is implemented at the whole-school level, with teams using behavioral data (e.g., office discipline referrals) to guide decisions.

## Implications

PBIS treats behavior as a learned skill rather than a character defect, applying the same instructional logic to expectations as to academics: define, model, practice, and reinforce. This grounding in [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) — reinforcement of desired behavior rather than punishment of undesired behavior — is combined with explicit teaching so that students know exactly what "be respectful" looks like in each setting. Consistent, predictable acknowledgment systems build a sense of [belonging](../elements/belonging.md) and school climate, and randomized trials show school-wide implementation reduces office discipline referrals and suspensions [Bradshaw et al. RCT evidence.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018125) [+S]. Because punitive discipline disproportionately harms marginalized students, pairing PBIS with empathic rather than exclusionary responses to misbehavior further reduces suspensions [Brief empathic-discipline intervention cuts suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A defined set of 3–5 positively stated, school-wide behavioral expectations
- Explicit teaching of expectations in each setting (classroom, hallway, cafeteria), with modeling and practice
- A consistent acknowledgment/reinforcement system for expected behavior
- A behavioral data system (e.g., office discipline referral data) reviewed regularly by a leadership team
- Administrative commitment and sustained staff buy-in; partial implementation weakens effects
- Tier 2/3 supports (check-in/check-out, individualized plans) for students who don't respond to universal supports

## Design

### Sequence
1. **Define expectations** — Leadership team drafts 3–5 positively stated expectations (e.g., "Be safe, be respectful, be responsible") with clear [structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)
2. **Operationalize** — Specify what each expectation looks like and sounds like in every school setting
3. **Teach** — Deliver explicit lessons at the start of the year and re-teach after breaks; model and practice expected behavior like academic content
4. **Acknowledge** — Reinforce expected behavior consistently through a school-wide acknowledgment system with frequent, specific [feedback](../elements/assessment.md)
5. **Monitor** — Collect and review behavioral data (referrals, acknowledgment ratios) on a regular cycle
6. **Tier 2 support** — Provide targeted group interventions (e.g., check-in/check-out with daily adult [check-in](../elements/check-in.md)) for non-responders
7. **Tier 3 support** — Conduct functional behavioral assessments and build individualized support plans with family involvement

### Affordances
- [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) — the acknowledgment system operationalizes reinforcement of desired behavior instead of punishment of undesired behavior
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — explicitly defined, taught expectations remove ambiguity about what is required in each setting
- [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) — a predictable, positively framed school culture strengthens students' sense of being known and valued
- [Autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md) — mature implementations shift from tangible rewards toward self-monitoring and student ownership of behavior

### Personalization

**Young learners (K–5):** Use concrete, frequent acknowledgment (token systems, praise) with heavy modeling and role-play; expectations must be taught in small, observable steps.

**Adolescents:** Shift from tangible tokens to social recognition, privileges, and self-monitoring tools; involve students in defining expectations to protect autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

**Students with trauma histories:** Emphasize predictability and relationship-building over compliance; pair Tier 2 supports with regulation skills rather than purely consequence-based systems.

**Students with disabilities:** Tier 3 plans should be function-based (addressing why behavior occurs) and integrated with IEP goals and [accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md).

**Culturally diverse schools:** Audit discipline data for disproportionality; combine PBIS with empathic discipline training so corrections are delivered relationally, not punitively [Brief empathic-discipline intervention cuts suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M].
