# Jigsaw Method

## Description
A collaborative learning strategy where students work in small groups to become experts on different aspects of a topic, then teach their peers, followed by a whole class synthesization

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires structured group work and equal participation. Instructor must ensure all students are actively engaged.
#### Constraints
- If students do not fully engage with their expert role, knowledge transfer may be weak. Requires monitoring of group interactions.

## Design

### Sequence
<!-- Steps with links to elements -->
1. Students are divided into "expert groups", each learning a different piece of the topic. 
 2. Experts reconvene into "jigsaw groups", where they teach their assigned concept to peers. 
 3. Whole class synthesizes information. 
 4. Instructor facilitates discussion and corrects misconceptions.

## Examples
- - High school history: Groups analyze different primary sources, then share findings. 
 - Medical education: Students specialize in different medical conditions, then teach peers.

## Impact
- Studies show higher retention and collaborative engagement in jigsaw classrooms compared to traditional instruction (Aronson, 2000). Improves social and cognitive skills.
