# Cognitive Load Reduction (CLT) Scaffolding Approach

## Description
An instructional approach that minimizes extraneous cognitive load while optimizing intrinsic and germane load for better learning efficiency. This method emphasizes worked examples, segmentation, dual coding (text + visuals), and gradual fading of support as learners develop expertise.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires structured instructional materials that align with CLT principles.
#### Constraints
- Over-scaffolding may lead to passive learning; Poorly designed multimedia can create split-attention effects.

Lesson, Unit

## Design

### Sequence
<!-- Steps with links to elements -->
1. Learners begin with guided worked examples.
 2. Scaffolding gradually fades to independent problem-solving.
 3. Multimedia and dual-channel learning support is used strategically.
 4. Learners transition to open-ended, self-directed tasks.

## Examples
- - Math education: Worked examples with step-by-step problem breakdowns.
- Medical education: Progressive reduction of guidance in patient diagnosis.
- Programming courses: Gradual shift from structured coding exercises to independent projects.

## Impact
- Research suggests higher knowledge retention, reduced cognitive load, and improved transfer of learning (Sweller, 2011).
