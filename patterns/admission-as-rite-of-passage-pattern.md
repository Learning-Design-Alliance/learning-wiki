# Admission structured as a rite of passage combining rituals and constructivist therapy

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
The article's organizing pattern treats residential admission as a rite of passage rather than an event, integrating Van Gennep's phases with constructivist therapy. The author's stated conjecture is that "rituals and constructivist therapies comprise a complementary means of helping youths to adjust to residential treatment." The pattern frames admission as separation from a former context, experimentation with new ways of thinking and behaving, and completion marked by ritual or celebration of the youth's arrival and goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Admission treated as a gradual therapeutic process in which workers examine needs, form relationships, involve significant social supports, and celebrate arrival
#### Constraints
- The article notes Menses and Durrant's rite-of-passage view was limited by its holistic treatment of residential treatment, and that rituals' importance depends on whether the context is forced or natural and the meaning attached

### Target Learners
- youths entering residential treatment and their families

### Target Goals
- experiencing admission as a comfortable transition
- developing new meanings about self and problems
