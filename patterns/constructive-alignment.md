# Constructive Alignment

## Description
Constructive alignment, developed by John Biggs, holds that effective teaching requires three components to point in the same direction: intended learning outcomes (ILOs) stating what learners should be able to *do*, teaching and learning activities (TLAs) that require learners to enact exactly those verbs, and assessment tasks (ATs) that measure the same performance. Because learners construct their understanding from what they actually do during instruction, misalignment — teaching one thing, assessing another — produces learning that follows the assessment rather than the stated outcomes. The pattern treats the course as a system to be designed, not a set of topics to be covered.

## Implications

The pattern rests on a constructivist premise: learners cannot acquire an outcome they never practice. If an outcome says "analyze," but lectures only transmit content and the exam only asks for recall, learners rationally adopt a surface approach — doing the minimum the system actually rewards. Alignment works by making the rewarded behavior and the intended behavior identical, harnessing the powerful "backwash" effect of assessment on study behavior [Assessment practices that provide feedback during learning improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. When activities require learners to actively construct and apply knowledge rather than receive it, outcomes improve [Active learning outperforms lecture on exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M], and giving learners visible purpose and autonomy within aligned tasks supports intrinsic motivation [Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Intended learning outcomes stated with explicit action verbs (analyze, design, critique), not topic lists ("know about," "understand")
- Teaching activities that give learners repeated practice enacting those verbs ([application](../elements/application.md), [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [case studies](../elements/case-studies.md))
- Assessment tasks that elicit the same verbs under valid conditions ([assess performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
- Criterion-referenced grading: standards defined by the outcome, not by rank ordering students against each other
- A course-level (not lesson-level) design perspective, since alignment is a property of the whole system

#### Constraints
- Outcomes written at low cognitive levels ("list," "define") align the whole system to surface learning — alignment amplifies whatever level the outcomes specify, good or bad [-M]
- Long checklists of granular outcomes fragment learning and encourage strategic compliance rather than integrated understanding [~M]
- Even well-aligned high-stakes summative grading can pull learners back toward surface strategies when grade stakes dominate [~M]
- Difficult to sustain across program-level curricula: individual course redesigns drift apart without program-wide coordination
- Redesign is time-intensive and often provokes resistance when it removes predictable, reproducible assessment formats

#### Grain Size
Course or program — alignment is a system property; a single aligned lesson inside a misaligned course changes little.

### Target Goals
- Deep approaches to learning: learners engaging with meaning rather than reproducing content
- Higher-order outcomes: analysis, evaluation, design, argumentation — outcomes that cannot be assessed by recall alone
- Transfer and application of knowledge to novel, authentic problems
- Transparent expectations that support learners in self-monitoring progress toward stated standards

### Target Learners
- Higher education and professional education, where Biggs developed and validated the framework
- Learners prone to surface strategies under assessment pressure — alignment redirects strategic effort toward intended outcomes
- Competency-based and accreditation-driven programs that must demonstrate outcome attainment

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — learners construct understanding from what they do; alignment ensures the "doing" matches the intended outcome
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) (Zimmerman) — explicit outcomes and criterion-referenced standards give learners the information needed to monitor and direct their own effort
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) (Sweller) — a coherent, aligned structure reduces extraneous load created by guessing what the teacher really wants

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) — alignment's reliance on assessment "backwash" can shade into reward-driven compliance; behaviorist framing predicts learners optimize for the measure, which is exactly the surface-learning risk when outcomes are poorly chosen
