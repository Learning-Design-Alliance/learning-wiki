# Filmed standardized task units as a research pattern: 20-minute common tasks with varied latency, filmed to permit within- and between-teacher comparison and study of observer variation

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's core methodological pattern is presenting each of six teachers with the same tasks under controlled conditions and filming 20-minute sessions so that inter- and intra-teaching comparisons can be analyzed by diverse observers. Teachers performed two filmed tasks with a two to three day latency between receiving instructions and doing the task, and one task with no delay, allowing systematic variation over content and latency of tasks. The films serve as a permanent record; the article states "In a very real sense, the films are the data." Anecdotal reports were written for all filmed tasks and two additional tasks including snack time, and validity is to be ascertained by comparing teachers across tasks, films and anecdotal reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Common tasks across teachers to minimize difficulty of focusing on teaching and reaction variation
- Filming and anecdotal recording so observers can study both teacher performance and observer variation
- Teachers with relatively similar groups of children, physical facilities and group history
#### Constraints
- Film processing delays limited initial use; only two films were processed initially, with twelve being processed
- The relationship of style to behavioral effects in children outside the classroom was not attended to either concurrently or longitudinally

### Target Learners
- preschool (Head Start) children

### Target Goals
- studying contrasting teacher styles and observer variation in describing teaching
