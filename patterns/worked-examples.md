# Worked Examples

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
Worked examples is a reusable sequence for studying a solved model before attempting similar problems, with the amount of guidance adjusted to the learner's current knowledge and task. It describes a design choice, not an observed effect on every outcome.

## Design

### Elements Used
- [Worked Examples](../elements/worked-examples.md)
- [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)
- [Fading](../elements/fading.md)

### Sequence and Adaptation

1. Establish what the learner already knows about the **specific task** and what the design is trying to improve: initial procedure, independent application, conceptual understanding, or transfer.
2. When an example-first sequence is warranted, show a solved instance and make its decisions visible. For a novice on a structured task, put this before a similar independent attempt; an example delivered only *after* a failed attempt is a different configuration.
3. Observe the learner's next attempt and reduce support when the learner can carry the relevant steps independently. Full examples, completion problems, and independent problems are possible configurations; this page does not supply an empirically established universal fading threshold.
4. Measure the intended outcome at the stated horizon. Immediate success, later retention, and transfer are different observations.

### Context

- **Target learners:** especially learners who lack a usable solution schema for the current structured task. General expertise is an imperfect substitute for task-specific prior knowledge.
- **Target goals:** initial procedural performance and movement toward independent problem solving. Conceptual transfer can warrant comparison with a problem-first design followed by consolidation.
- **Requirements:** a representative solution and an opportunity to observe subsequent independent performance.
- **Constraints:** a full example can become redundant for learners who already know the procedure; evidence from a short immediate test cannot establish a delayed benefit. Prior knowledge, task difficulty, and example type may interact.

## Design Decisions

### Should the initial task begin with a worked example or a problem?
- **Default:** for novices on a structured task where immediate independent performance matters, begin with an example. In one four-arm circuit-troubleshooting experiment, example-only and example→problem conditions outperformed problems-only and problem→example conditions on the immediate test — [sequencing claim](../claims/worked-example-problem-sequences.md) [~M].
- **Changes when:** the goal is conceptual understanding or transfer and the design includes later instruction that uses learners' attempts → consider problem solving before instruction. A synthesis favors that order against instruction first for those outcomes; its grade 2–5 and domain-general-skill subgroups instead favored instruction first — [productive failure claim](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [~S].
- **Tested with:** secondary novices troubleshooting circuits in the example-first experiment; varied ages and domains in the problem-first synthesis.
- **Not settled:** the two sources did not directly compare these exact lesson configurations on the same learners and measures. Do not infer that the circuit study establishes a delayed transfer effect.

### Must examples alternate with practice problems?
- **Default:** choose an example-first sequence when that is the supported choice; in the four-arm experiment, examples alone and example→problem pairs did not differ detectably on its short immediate test — [sequencing claim](../claims/worked-example-problem-sequences.md) [~M].
- **Changes when:** independent application is the instructional goal → include opportunities to demonstrate and assess it, then adjust support based on performance. The experiment does not identify the best number or spacing of attempts.
- **Tested with:** a short secondary-school circuit-troubleshooting intervention.
- **Not settled:** no detected difference is not equivalence. The experiment cannot settle whether pairing, fading, or later practice benefits delayed retention or transfer.

### Does an example given after an initial problem have the same effect as one given first?
- **Default:** for a novice in the four-arm circuit task, put the example before the initial attempt when choosing between these orders. Problem→example pairs did not outperform problems only on the immediate test; both trailed example-only and example→problem — [sequencing claim](../claims/worked-example-problem-sequences.md) [~M].
- **Changes when:** a problem-first phase is deliberately used to elicit varied attempts before instruction addressing those attempts → evaluate it as the distinct problem-solving-then-instruction design, with a conceptual or transfer outcome — [productive failure claim](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [~S].
- **Tested with:** short circuit troubleshooting for the four-arm result; heterogeneous domains and durations in the synthesis.
- **Not settled:** the circuit experiment did not test a full productive-failure design. Its null for problem→example versus problems only is not proof of equivalence.

### Full examples or completion problems for a learner with prior knowledge?
- **Default:** for lower-prior-knowledge learners facing simultaneous-equation tasks, one study's follow-up contrast favored full examples on the immediate posttest (reported p=.05) — [prior-knowledge claim](../claims/lower-prior-knowledge-learners-score-higher-with-full-than-completion-worked-examples.md) [~M].
- **Changes when:** prior knowledge or task difficulty differs → inspect both. A three-way interaction was reported, while the simpler prior-knowledge × example-type posttest interaction was not significant — [interaction claim](../claims/prior-knowledge-worked-example-task-difficulty-three-way-interaction-on-algebra-posttest.md) [~M].
- **Tested with:** college students solving simultaneous equations, with full versus completion examples and easy versus difficult tasks.
- **Not settled:** a fixed high-prior-knowledge → completion rule; the high-prior-knowledge follow-up difference was not significant (p=.22), and the task-difficulty cells differ. No delayed outcome was established in this study.

### When should full examples be withdrawn?
- **Default:** look for independent performance on the relevant task and progressively give the learner more responsibility — [fading support claim](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
- **Changes when:** guidance is redundant for a learner with an established schema → reduce it; when the learner still cannot make the next step, retain support — [expertise reversal claim](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M].
- **Tested with:** mostly structured problem-solving tasks in the cited claims and reviews.
- **Not settled:** the wiki evidence here does not define a validated threshold or a universal rate of fading.
