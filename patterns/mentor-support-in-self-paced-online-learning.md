# Mentor Support in Self-Paced Online Learning

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 93 claims (49 for, 38 mixed, 6 against) · 75 studies (15 associational, 13 causal, 12 review, 11 quant-synthesis, 9 qualitative, 8 theoretical, 7 design), `q1`–`q4` · 10 of 75 report an effect size · 83 claims rest on one study

## Description and scope

Support for learners who work through online courses on their own schedule, from mentors, coaches or instructors who are not lecturing but are available to help: answering questions, giving feedback, helping learners plan and keep pace, and reaching out when a learner stalls. The decisions it covers are how much contact is scheduled and how much is on demand, what triggers outreach (inactivity, missed milestones, failed assessments), what a contact does (content help, feedback on work, planning and self-regulation, encouragement, referral), and how human mentors and automated or AI support divide the work. Grain size is a course, or a programme of self-paced courses with one mentor across them.

It applies the [community of inquiry principle](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) (teaching presence and social presence when there is no class meeting), the [self-regulated learning principle](../principles/self-regulated-learning.md) (planning, monitoring and help-seeking that a self-paced learner must do alone), [instructor accessibility](../principles/instructor-accessibility.md) and [social presence](../principles/social-presence.md). The course-shape decisions (template, module structure, discussion design) belong to [online course design](online-course-design.md); this pattern is the people-and-outreach layer on top of a course. The study configurations below are evidence; the response-dependent policy is an **untested design proposal**. Use it to gather better evidence about a programme's learners, not to certify that a learner is "at risk" or "self-regulated".

**What the evidence does and does not test.** The wiki's causal evidence comes from four bodies of work, none of them a self-paced competency-based programme: (1) proactive **student coaching and advising** in US colleges (one randomized trial of a commercial coaching service, two matched comparisons of a city coaching programme, and a practice-guide synthesis of eight advising studies), which moved persistence by a few percentage points and did not detectably move completion or grades; (2) **automated outreach** (a randomized chatbot outreach trial in two large online undergraduate courses, a null text-messaging trial for college transition, and a pre–post check of analytics-triggered feedback emails); (3) **planning prompts** at the start of MOOCs (field experiments that raised completion); and (4) **self-regulation supports** in online and computer-based environments (meta-analyses of SRL interventions and metacognitive prompts). Evidence on teaching and social presence in online courses is correlational and qualitative. In the wiki, no study compares mentor contact schedules or doses, tests when to hand a learner from automated help to a person, or measures a mentor model inside a self-paced competency-based programme; the competency-based evidence recorded here is descriptive completion data. Treat those as open questions with proposed defaults, labelled below.

## Inputs: establish the design brief

| Field | Record or elicit |
|---|---|
| Learner and starting observation | Prior online or self-paced experience, prior knowledge of the course domain (`novice`, `intermediate`, `advanced`, or `?`), work and caring commitments, the learner's own plan and expected pace, and their first-week activity (logins, first submission). Preserve unknowns; do not infer readiness from demographics. |
| Context and activities | Mode (`online-self-paced`, `online-instructor-led`, cohort or rolling enrolment), whether there are deadlines or only an end date, what help exists (mentor, course instructor, tutoring, peer forum, AI tutor or chatbot), each one's hours and response time, and the mentor's caseload. |
| Learner-valued goal | Ask what the learner wants from the course or programme and by when (a credential, a promotion, a skill for current work, finishing quickly). Record disagreement with the designer objective, for example a learner who wants speed when the programme wants mastery. |
| Designer objective | What counts as success: completion, pace, mastery of the competencies, or all three; which assessments are judged by a person (performance tasks, simulations, labs) and which are automated. |
| Outcome | Separate measures for activity (logins, submissions), progress (milestones, course completion, term-to-term persistence), learning (assessment results, judged by an instrument independent of the help given) and uptake of support (who used which help, and how often), each with a horizon. |

A request to "add mentoring" or "improve retention" does not specify these inputs. Ask for them before choosing a contact schedule, an alert threshold or a predicted gain. Check first that outreach can say something specific (which activity to do next, what a failed attempt shows), and that someone has the time to answer the replies it produces.

## Sequence and conditional policy

1. **Before or in the first week: a planning contact.** Ask each learner to write an open-ended plan for how they will work through the course, the obstacles they expect and an if–then response to each (about ten minutes, once). A mentor reads it and replies personally within the first week. Contact early: in one continuing-education programme, online attrition during the orientation week was twice that of the first week of on-campus classes. The planning activity is evidence-based (see below); the personal reply is a proposal.
2. **Weeks 1–4: scheduled check-ins plus signals.** Proposed default, untested: a short scheduled contact at each of the first two milestones (about one a fortnight), each naming what the learner has done and the next concrete step. In parallel, define the signals that will trigger outreach (for example days without activity, a missed milestone, two failed attempts at one assessment) and write the thresholds and who answers the flags into the programme's records before launch. Dropout does not concentrate at a single lesson in most online courses, so watch progress throughout, not at one checkpoint.
3. **Whenever a signal fires: triggered outreach with specific content.** Send a message that names what the data show and the specific activities or help to use (not a general "we noticed you've been away"), and offer a person. Automated outreach can carry routine reminders and course-specific nudges; a mentor takes any reply that concerns planning, motivation or a failed performance task. Record what was sent, to whom and what the learner did next.
4. **On demand: divide content help.** Routine questions and practice hints can go to automated or AI help available at any hour. Feedback on graded performance work (labs, cases, simulations, written analyses) comes from a mentor or instructor, and a mentor reviews work-in-progress where the platform lets them see it. Hand off from AI to a person when the learner asks for one, repeats an answer-seeking request without progress, fails the same task twice, or raises anything about pace, wellbeing or withdrawal (proposed rules, untested).
5. **Middle to late course: fade scheduled contact, keep triggered contact.** Once a learner has met two consecutive milestones on their own plan, move them to milestone-only contact; keep signal-triggered outreach for everyone. Learners in one study wanted feedback outreach more often than twice a term, so do not fade to nothing.
6. **Reobserve and audit.** At each milestone check progress and learning separately, and at term end check who used which help and who received triggered outreach, by subgroup, against outcomes. Revise thresholds and messages from that audit rather than from mentor impressions alone.

| Observation | Discriminating probe | Proposed action, with its uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| No activity for the agreed number of days early in the course | Short message asking which of: unclear what to do next, too little time this week, content difficulty, reconsidering the course; check whether the learner ever started a first task. | If time or planning: revisit the plan and its if–then obstacles. If content: point to the specific resource or a mentor session. If reconsidering: a person contacts them. The categories are a proposal, not a validated diagnosis. |
| Steady activity but repeated failed attempts on one assessment | Look at what the attempts got wrong; ask the learner to explain their approach to one item. | Mentor feedback on the work, with a revise-and-resubmit, rather than more practice of the same kind. If attempts show answer-guessing, address strategy before content. |
| Heavy use of an AI tutor, requests shifting toward "just give the answer" | Ask the learner to solve one comparable item without the tool; compare with assisted performance. | If unaided performance lags: a person discusses how to use the tool (attempt first, ask for hints), and practice includes unaided items. Assisted performance alone does not show learning. |
| Learner on pace but reports feeling isolated or unsure of standards | Ask what they would want from contact (feedback, a peer, a person to check in). | Add a personal check-in or a peer connection. Presence evidence in the wiki is correlational; this branch may change satisfaction without changing completion. |
| No reply to outreach | Check whether messages are delivered and read; try a different channel or a person. | Escalate to a person after one unanswered automated message. Whether escalation helps is untested in the wiki. |
| Fast progress, high scores | Check that assessments are independent of the help given. | Reduce scheduled contact and offer stretch work; do not withdraw access to help. |

## Choosing configurations from evidence

- **What to expect from proactive human coaching.** [InsideTrack coaching has potentially positive effects on persistence](../claims/insidetrack-coaching-potentially-positive-persistence.md) [+M]: in the Bettinger & Baker randomized trial (seven lotteries, 3,527 college students), 66.4% of coached students were retained at 12 months against 61.4% of the comparison group (effect size 0.13). At 24 months [degree completion did not differ detectably](../claims/insidetrack-coaching-no-discernible-attainment-effect.md) [~M] (35.2% vs 31.2%, effect size 0.11, p < .10, 1,346 students). The service [cost about $390–$500 per student per semester](../claims/insidetrack-coaching-cost-per-student-semester.md) [~M]. Plan for a persistence gain of a few percentage points, not for coaching to fix completion on its own.
- **Coaching in another programme, with weaker designs.** [Success Boston Coaching has potentially positive effects on progressing in college](../claims/success-boston-coaching-potentially-positive-progressing-in-college.md) [+M]: two matched comparisons of Boston public-school graduates (second-year enrolment 83% vs 75%, effect size 0.30, and 82.6% vs 78.3%, effect size 0.17). The scale-up study found [no effect on cumulative GPA](../claims/linkow-2019-null-gpa-effect.md) [~M] (2.37 vs 2.36, effect size 0.01). Coaching moved enrolment, not grades, in both programmes recorded here. A WWC practice guide pooled eight studies and found [comprehensive, integrated advising improves progression, achievement and degree attainment](../claims/comprehensive-integrated-advising-improves-persistence-and-degree-attainment.md) [+M], with no effect size printed on the claim page; "integrated" advising bundles several services, so it does not say which part did the work.
- **Automated outreach in online courses.** In a randomized trial in two large online undergraduate courses (1,568 and 915 students), [chatbot outreach raised the share earning an A or B by four percentage points](../claims/chatbot-outreach-increases-ab-grades.md) [+M] (from 61%). Effects [differed by course](../claims/chatbot-completion-effects-differ-by-subject.md) [~M] (fewer D, F or withdrawals in one, fewer withdrawals in the other), [concentrated in the middle of the grade distribution](../claims/chatbot-quantile-effects-middle-distribution.md) [~M], and [did not carry to other courses or next-term enrolment](../claims/chatbot-no-spillover-medium-term-effects.md) [~M]. Part of the effect ran through people: [the chatbot raised tutoring attendance by about two percentage points](../claims/chatbot-increases-tutoring-attendance.md) [+M] from baselines of 7% and 2.4%, carrying about 18% of the grade effect. So outreach that connects learners to human help is one configuration with a tested benefit; expect it to be course-specific and short-lived.
- **Generic messaging is not enough.** [Text-message college-transition messaging did not increase persistence](../claims/gear-up-text-messaging-no-persistence-impact.md) [-M] (GEAR UP evaluation), and the authors suggest, without testing it, that [broad advisor caseloads limited what the messages could lead to](../claims/gear-up-advisor-caseload-limited-effectiveness.md) [~W]. Outreach is only as useful as the help behind it.
- **Analytics-triggered feedback emails.** In one course, models trained on one semester [identified at-risk students in the next with AUC above 0.8](../claims/cross-semester-at-risk-prediction-high-accuracy.md) [+M], and after a feedback email naming the activities they had not used, [more than 30% of flagged students visited those activities within two weeks](../claims/relational-feedback-email-increases-activity-visits.md) [+M]. That is a pre–post count with no comparison group and no learning outcome. [Respondents wanted the feedback more often than twice a semester](../claims/at-risk-students-satisfied-prefer-frequent-feedback.md) [+W], but only 9.27% of flagged students answered the survey.
- **Do not take a dashboard vendor's retention figures at face value.** The Course Signals early-warning system's reported retention (93.24% vs 69.4%) [may be explained by confounding](../claims/course-signals-confounding-number-of-classes.md) [-M]: students who took more courses both met the system more often and persisted longer. Georgia State's advising system [paired prediction with advising capacity and grants, and reported narrowed graduation gaps](../claims/gps-advising-equity-gap-narrowing.md) [~W]; those are institution-reported outcomes with no comparison group, and the claim credits the advising capacity, not the prediction.
- **Prediction models can miss some learners more than others.** On a large open-university dataset (25,690 students), standard models [had unequal true-positive rates across subgroups](../claims/baseline-ml-models-biased-tpr-oulad.md) [-M] by sex, age, deprivation and disability. Audit a model's misses by subgroup before letting it decide who is contacted.
- **Planning prompts at the start.** In three HarvardX MOOCs, [open-ended planning prompts at the start increased completion by 29%](../claims/planning-prompts-increase-mooc-completion.md) [+M] (relative, no standardized effect size) and [certificate payment by 40%](../claims/planning-prompts-increase-certificate-payment.md) [+M]; the effect was [largest for students enrolled in traditional schools](../claims/planning-prompt-effect-larger-traditional-school-students.md) [~M], and [plans focused on specific times forecast lower completion](../claims/time-focused-plans-forecast-lower-mooc-completion.md) [~M]. Across 94 tests, [if–then implementation intentions improve goal attainment](../claims/implementation-intentions-improve-goal-attainment.md) [+S] (d = .65), and in two randomized online-course experiments (17,963 learners) an eight-minute plan-and-obstacles activity made learners in countries classed as individualist 32% and 15% more likely to complete, with no effect for those in countries classed as collectivist; the text analysis found it worked when the obstacle named was predictable and surmountable (work or family), not when it was lack of time. Use the obstacle the learner names, not their country, to decide what follow-up they need.
- **Self-regulation support online.** Across 15 studies (5,519 participants), [SRL interventions in online and blended environments had a pooled effect of d = 0.65 on learning outcomes](../claims/srl-interventions-moderate-effect-online-blended.md) [+M]; [metacognitive prompts in computer-based environments](../claims/metacognitive-prompts-improve-learning.md) [+S] raised learning by g = 0.40, with larger effects when prompts were task-specific, adaptive and paired with feedback, and a 35-study review of online and MOOC settings found prompting effective but too varied for one effect size. In six MOOCs (4,831 learners), self-reported [goal setting and strategic planning predicted reaching one's goal](../claims/goal-setting-strategic-planning-predict-mooc-goal-attainment.md) [+M] (54% higher odds per SD of goal setting), while [help seeking predicted lower odds of certification](../claims/help-seeking-negative-predictor-mooc-goal-attainment.md) [~M] (27% lower per SD). That last result is associational; learners who struggle are more likely to seek help, so it is not evidence that help harms, but it does say that help-seeking alone does not carry a struggling learner through.
- **Who gives feedback on graded work.** In a randomized comparison with 35 students, [those given instructor feedback improved their lab scores more than those given AI-generated feedback](../claims/instructor-feedback-greater-lab-score-improvements-than-ai-feedback.md) [+M] (group coefficient −4.97 for AI, p = 0.04). The sample is small and the design is coded `r1`; treat it as a reason to keep people on performance-task feedback until a programme tests otherwise. Feedback in general [improves learning by a medium amount on average (d = 0.48), most when it carries information about how to proceed (d = 0.99), and more than a third of feedback interventions reduce performance](../claims/feedback-improves-learning.md) [~S]: the content of a mentor's message matters more than its existence.
- **AI help and unaided performance.** Reviews of one randomized trial report that [unrestricted GPT-4 access raised assisted practice by 48% but left students 17% worse once it was withdrawn](../claims/gpt4-access-harms-learning-after-withdrawal.md) [-M], and in 112 students' sessions with an LLM, [help-seeking shifted toward answer- and verification-seeking as a session went on](../claims/within-session-hs-shifts-toward-closure-srl-increases.md) [~M]. In K-12 AI-based instruction a review reports [AI-generated information combined with teacher intervention associated with better performance, participation and confidence](../claims/ai-plus-teacher-intervention-student-benefits.md) [+M], while [real-time alerts sometimes overloaded teachers' attention](../claims/ai-alerts-can-overload-teacher-attention.md) [~M]. These support step 4's hand-off rules as a direction; none tests a hand-off rule.
- **Presence and personal contact.** In a national survey of undergraduates moved online, [personal instructor messages showed the largest association with satisfaction](../claims/personal-messages-largest-satisfaction-effect.md) [+M] (68% vs 47% satisfied; motivation a major problem for 33% vs 53%). A meta-analysis summarised in a book chapter [verified the importance of student–instructor, student–student and student–content interaction in distance learning](../claims/three-interaction-types-verified-meta-analysis-distance-learning.md) [+M]. In an online case-based graduate course, [co-regulation correlated more strongly with the three presences than self-regulation did](../claims/co-regulation-stronger-presence-relationships-than-self-regulation.md) [+M] (47 students, ρ = .65 with social presence). Distance students said [their needs centre on course support and communication with tutors](../claims/online-students-learning-needs-support-communication.md) [+M], and online teachers said [community online requires deliberate, planned effort](../claims/online-community-requires-conscious-effort.md) [+M]. None of these measures completion or learning; they support scheduled personal contact as a satisfaction and motivation measure.

Do not rank these results against one another: comparators (no coaching, matched non-participants, no messages, the same course before the email), outcomes (retention, grades, completion, activity visits, satisfaction) and populations differ. A precise abstention names the missing comparison, outcome or observation and the next way to resolve it.

## Fitting the design to a situation

Ask for four facts when the brief omits them: **what is the mentor's caseload and response time?** (outreach without capacity behind it is the configuration that failed), **is success completion, pace, or mastery, and how is each measured?**, **which work is judged by a person?**, and **who, by the programme's records, uses on-demand help now?**

| If the brief says… | Then change… | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Learners new to online or self-paced study, or in their first term | Put the planning contact and a personal reply in the first week, and the first scheduled check-in before the first milestone | [Orientation-week attrition online is twice that of on-campus first weeks](../claims/orientation-week-attrition-double-online.md) [~M]; [Planning prompts raised MOOC completion](../claims/planning-prompts-increase-mooc-completion.md) [+M] |
| Experienced adult learners in work, choosing their own pace | Keep the plan-and-obstacles activity, and let the learner choose check-in frequency after the first milestone; keep triggered outreach | [Implementation intentions worked when obstacles were work or family, not lack of time](../claims/implementation-intentions-improve-goal-attainment.md) [+S]; fading schedule is untested |
| Secondary-school or younger learners | Route alerts to a teacher or family as well as the learner, and act on grades and attendance signals | [Ninth-grade early-warning systems on grades and attendance were followed by improvements](../claims/ninth-grade-early-warning-systems-improvements.md) [+W] (a research agenda's summary); [parent text alerts about missing homework raised performance](../claims/parent-text-alerts-incomplete-homework-raise-performance.md) [+M] |
| The goal is completing a credential, not one course | Plan coaching across terms and measure persistence each term; do not expect completion to follow from one term's support | [Coaching raised retention at 12 months](../claims/insidetrack-coaching-potentially-positive-persistence.md) [+M]; [no detectable effect on degree completion at 24 months](../claims/insidetrack-coaching-no-discernible-attainment-effect.md) [~M] |
| The goal is mastery judged on performance tasks (cases, labs, simulations) | Keep a person on feedback for graded performance work and on debriefing; use automated help for practice | [Instructor feedback raised lab scores more than AI feedback](../claims/instructor-feedback-greater-lab-score-improvements-than-ai-feedback.md) [+M]; [Feedback, analytics and structured reflection strengthen learning in simulations](../claims/analytics-feedback-reflection-strengthen-learning-gains.md) [+W] (a review's summary) |
| Online, self-paced, asynchronous, no class meetings | Use a consistent course layout with goals, due dates and feedback turnaround stated, and schedule personal messages rather than waiting for learners to ask | [A standard template heightened teaching presence in accelerated online courses](../claims/course-template-supports-learner-presence.md) [+W]; [Personal messages had the largest association with satisfaction](../claims/personal-messages-largest-satisfaction-effect.md) [+M] |

| Little learner time each week | Keep contacts short and specific: name the one next activity; avoid plans phrased only as times | [Time-focused plans forecast lower completion](../claims/time-focused-plans-forecast-lower-mooc-completion.md) [~M] |
| Mentor caseload is large or mentor hours are limited | Use automated outreach for reminders and routing, reserve mentor time for replies and for learners with repeated failure; set alert thresholds against what mentors can answer | [GEAR UP messaging did not raise persistence; broad caseloads may have limited it](../claims/gear-up-advisor-caseload-limited-effectiveness.md) [~W]; [alert threshold changes reshape advisor workload](../claims/threshold-change-governance-impact.md) [~W] |
| No mentor at all; automated or AI support only | Use chatbot outreach that links to whatever human help exists (tutoring, office hours) and expect course-specific, short-lived effects | [Chatbot outreach raised A/B grades](../claims/chatbot-outreach-increases-ab-grades.md) [+M]; [no spillover or next-term effects](../claims/chatbot-no-spillover-medium-term-effects.md) [~M] |

| High stakes (licensure, certification) | Keep a person on judgments that certify competence, and audit any risk model's misses by subgroup before using it to decide who gets contact | [Standard prediction models had unequal true-positive rates across subgroups](../claims/baseline-ml-models-biased-tpr-oulad.md) [-M] |
| Health professions programme | Add career or programme coaching that connects learners to the programme's next steps, and evaluate it against a comparison group | [Career coaches appeared to expand exposure to and completion of health sciences programmes](../claims/coaching-expanded-program-exposure-completion.md) [+M] (associational) |
| Teacher education with clinical or field assignments | Give probing feedback that asks for evidence from the learner's own students, with a required revise-and-resubmit, and expect uneven progress | [Probing feedback with revise-and-resubmit moved one candidate, not another](../claims/probing-feedback-revise-resubmit-evidence.md) [~W]; [instructor access to work-in-progress enabled coaching](../claims/web-folders-enable-coaching.md) [+W] |

| Concern about equity of who uses help | Make the first contact proactive for everyone, track uptake and outcomes by subgroup, and do not target outreach by group membership | [Only about 10% attended optional drop-in tutorials](../claims/drop-in-tutorials-improve-p1y-marks.md) [~M]; [chatbot effects differed by gender in one course](../claims/chatbot-gender-heterogeneity-microeconomics-women.md) [~M]; [voluntary chatbot use was bimodal](../claims/chatbot-engagement-bimodal-continuous-versus-superficial-users.md) [~M] |

## Illustrative design instance and observation record

*Illustration only, invented, not a tested programme.* A self-paced online certificate in health informatics for working adults, with rolling enrolment, a mentor with a caseload of 150, case- and simulation-based performance tasks, and an AI tutor for practice questions. In week 1 each learner writes a plan with two expected obstacles and if–then responses, and their mentor replies within three working days. Milestones are set by the learner (one module every two weeks on average). Outreach fires after ten days without activity or two failed attempts at one performance task: the first message is automated and names the next activity and the mentor's booking link; a second unanswered message goes to the mentor. Performance tasks are scored and given feedback by the mentor with one resubmission. One learner says she wants to finish quickly for a promotion; the programme wants mastery of each competency. Agree a pace that keeps resubmission time, and check at the end that completion, scores on an independent final case and help uptake were recorded separately and by subgroup.

Record: **learner plan and named obstacles → contacts scheduled and actually made, with dates → signals fired, thresholds and who answered → message content and channel → learner's next action and time to it → help used (mentor, AI tutor, tutoring) → candidate interpretations and confidence basis → milestone progress and assessment results at the stated horizon**. Preserve the learner's stated purpose and any change in it. An agent may summarize this record but must not fabricate missing inputs or assign numerical risk probabilities without a calibrated, subgroup-audited model.

## Elements and limits

[Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md), [goal setting](../elements/goal-setting.md), [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) and [learning analytics feedback](../elements/learning-analytics-feedback.md).

This pattern is scoped to support around self-paced online courses and programmes: planning contact, triggered outreach, feedback on work, and the division between people and automated help. Course design itself, peer-learning formats, tutoring as instruction and the design of an AI tutor's dialogue need their own configurations and evidence. The causal evidence recorded in the wiki comes from campus and online college courses and MOOCs, mostly in the United States, measured on persistence, grades and completion over one or two years; it does not test contact frequency, hand-off rules or any mentor model inside a competency-based programme, and the diagnostic branches above remain to be tested with actual learners.
