# Threading, Weaving, Patternmaking

## Description
A three-phase instructional sequence operationalizing [Critical Speculative Design Pedagogy](../theories/critical-speculative-design-pedagogy.md) within a standard science unit. In the source study it replaced a conventional 8th-grade physics unit on light waves with the driving question "How can and does light serve some communities and not other communities?" **Threading** cultivates sociopolitical consciousness through close, emotionally open annotation of texts that center Communities of Global Majority (e.g., racial bias in AI-driven dermatology, medical racism and melanin, workplace inequity in solar energy). **Weaving** has learners connect insights across multiple readings and their own cultural/community knowledge to recognize a pattern of bias as systemic rather than isolated. **Patternmaking** has learners produce a speculative, transdisciplinary artifact — a multimedia "Visioning Board," a poem, a visual narrative — envisioning a more just, restorative future, deliberately substituting these forms for the standard causal-explanation or conceptual-model culminating assessment.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A credible, well-documented case of embedded bias in the unit's science content, sourced from multiple readings so weaving has real cross-source synthesis to do
- Assessment flexibility: annotation and culminating tasks must accept visual, emotional, and aesthetic expression, not just propositional science content, since the pattern's evidence is that this flexibility is what reveals the heterogeneity of learners' engagement
- Space for intergenerational or out-of-classroom collaboration (a family member, a community member) on the culminating artifact, which the source study found deepened the final patternmaking work
#### Constraints
- Requires a teacher willing to explicitly name their own positionality and cede some epistemic authority (the source study's teacher described trying to "remove myself from that position... as the holder of the power or holder of the knowledge")
- Evidence base is a small ethnographic case study (13 students with full pathway data, 2 focal cases); no comparison condition or quantified learning-outcome measure
#### Grain Size
Unit (one physics unit; reduced from 75 to 28 minutes per session under COVID constraints in the source study, suggesting the sequence is compressible)

## Design

### Sequence
1. **Threading** — Learners closely read and annotate sociopolitical science texts, using open-form annotation (highlighting, questions, personal/emotional reactions in the margins) rather than propositional summary alone. See [Text Underlining and Annotating](../elements/text-underlining-and-annotating.md).
2. **Weaving** — Learners connect insights across the threaded readings and their own cultural/community knowledge (e.g., linking melanin biology to skin-cancer health disparities to sunscreen-marketing gaps) to build a systemic, not individual, account of the bias.
3. **Patternmaking** — Learners produce a speculative multimedia artifact (a Visioning Board, poem, or visual narrative) that names the injustice, articulates a refusal of it, and imagines a restorative future — optionally in collaboration with a family or community member.
