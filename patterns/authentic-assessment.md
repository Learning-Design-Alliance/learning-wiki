# Authentic Assessment

> **Pattern** · [All patterns](index.md)

## Description
Authentic assessment asks learners to demonstrate competence by performing the kinds of tasks experts actually do — designing, arguing, diagnosing, writing for real audiences, solving ill-structured problems — under conditions that resemble real practice. Instead of measuring isolated knowledge with decontextualized test items, it evaluates the quality of a performance or product against public criteria, typically via [rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md) or expert judgment. The problem it solves is the transfer gap: learners who score well on tests often cannot apply knowledge when the context, format, or stakes change [~M].

## Implications

Authentic assessment rests on the argument that assessment should model the target performance, not merely sample it: if the goal is transfer to real contexts, practice and evaluation must occur in those contexts [Wiggins's foundational critique of standardized testing.](https://doi.org/10.2307/1179495) [+M]. Because authentic tasks are typically open-ended and criterion-referenced, they shift assessment from ranking to improvement — feedback on drafts and performances drives revision, making the pattern a natural vehicle for [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Real or realistic audiences also raise the stakes and quality of student work [Authentic audiences improve student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks that genuinely resemble disciplinary or professional practice, not just "real-world flavored" test items
- Public, explicit criteria ([rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md)) shared before work begins
- Opportunities for [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) and revision during the process, not only judgment at the end
- Judgment capacity: instructor time, peer review structures, or external evaluators to assess complex products

#### Constraints
- Scoring is less reliable and more labor-intensive than objective tests; inter-rater agreement on complex performances is difficult to achieve [~M]
- Difficult to align with standardized accountability systems and large-enrollment logistics
- Authenticity can be superficial — a "realistic" wrapper on recall tasks measures no more than a conventional test [-M]
- Complex tasks introduce construct-irrelevant variance: language proficiency, background knowledge, and resource access can drive scores as much as target competence [~M]
- Higher cognitive load of ill-structured tasks can disadvantage novices without substantial scaffolding [~M]

#### Grain Size
Unit or course — authentic tasks typically anchor an extended sequence of preparation, drafting, feedback, and revision rather than a single lesson.

### Target Goals
- Transfer of learning to novel, ill-structured situations
- Integration of knowledge and skill across topics (performance requires coordination, not isolated recall)
- Self-regulated revision and quality standards ([reflection](../elements/reflection.md) against criteria)
- Disciplinary practices: argumentation, design, inquiry, professional communication

### Target Learners
- Learners preparing for professional practice (education, nursing, engineering, business) where performance competence matters more than test-taking
- Intermediate and advanced learners with enough domain knowledge to handle ill-structured tasks productively
- Less effective for absolute novices, who benefit first from structured, low-complexity practice before open-ended performance

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Situated Learning](../theories/situated-learning.md) (Lave & Wenger) — knowledge is inseparable from the context of its use; assessment should therefore occur in authentic practice contexts
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — learners build understanding through active, meaningful tasks rather than reproduction of transmitted content
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) (Deci & Ryan) — autonomy, relevance, and competence feedback in authentic tasks support intrinsic motivation

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) (Sweller) — ill-structured authentic tasks can overwhelm novices; means-ends search in open problems imposes extraneous load, so scaffolding or worked examples are needed before full authentic performance
- Direct instruction research — for well-defined procedural knowledge, explicit instruction with objective testing is more efficient and reliable

### Claims
#### Supporting
- [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S] — formative use of assessment information drives learning gains
- [Authentic audiences improve student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M] — real audiences and purposes raise effort and quality
- [Feedback improves learning outcomes.](../claims/feedback-improves-learning.md) [+S] — criterion-referenced feedback during authentic tasks supports improvement

#### Contradicting
- [Checklist-based evaluation is ineffective in online contexts.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [~M] — mechanical rubric application can substitute compliance for judgment

## Design

### Sequence
1. **Define the performance of understanding** — identify what experts in the discipline actually do and design a task that requires it ([application](../elements/application.md))
2. **Establish criteria publicly** — share [rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md) or exemplars before work begins so quality standards are transparent ([assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
3. **Prepare through structured practice** — build component skills with [scaffolding](../elements/scaffolding.md) and [worked examples](../elements/worked-examples.md) before the full authentic task
4. **Draft / perform with feedback** — learners produce work-in-progress and receive [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) from instructors, peers, or authentic audiences
5. **Revise** — learners act on feedback, treating assessment information as input to improvement ([reflection](../elements/reflection.md))
6. **Final performance / exhibition** — present the product or performance to a real or simulated audience ([authentic audiences](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))
7. **Evaluate against criteria** — judge the final work with rubrics, self- and peer-assessment, and instructor judgment

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — authentic assessment enacts this principle by making assessment information actionable: criteria are public, feedback arrives during the process, and revision is expected rather than penalized
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — the pattern gives student work a genuine audience and consequence, which raises investment and quality
- [Constructive Alignment](constructive-alignment.md) — authentic assessment closes the loop between intended outcomes and assessed performance, ensuring the assessment task itself practices the target competence
- [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — authentic tasks require learners to produce, decide, and perform rather than select or recall

### Personalization

**Novices:** Decompose the authentic task into staged sub-tasks with heavy scaffolding early; use [case studies](../elements/case-studies.md) of expert work as models before independent performance.

**Learners with some background:** Increase task ambiguity and reduce scaffolds; require learners to define aspects of the problem themselves, as experts must.

**Learners with anxiety or low confidence:** Allow low-stakes rehearsal performances and private feedback before public exhibition; emphasize growth against criteria rather than comparison to peers.

**Diverse cohorts:** Offer choice of authentic task context or product format (where the assessed competence permits) so that language background and resource access do not masquerade as competence differences; provide [accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) such as extended time on complex productions.

**Learners with language or learning differences:** Provide rubrics in plain language, allow oral or multimodal demonstrations of competence, and separate the assessment of communication mechanics from the assessment of disciplinary reasoning where the latter is the target.

## Related Patterns
- [Constructive Alignment](constructive-alignment.md) — authentic assessment is the assessment half of alignment; outcomes, tasks, and criteria must point at the same competence
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — authentic tasks provide the realistic practice context in which modeling, coaching, and fading unfold
- [Case-Based Learning](case-based-learning.md) — cases supply the realistic problem contexts that authentic assessments often draw on

## Examples

**Teacher education — edTPA:** Pre-service teachers plan, teach, and video-record a real lesson sequence, then analyze their own teaching against standardized performance criteria scored by trained external evaluators. [https://www.edtpa.com](https://www.edtpa.com)

**Writing across the curriculum — National Writing Project:** Students write for genuine publication and community audiences, revising through peer response cycles rather than submitting single drafts for grades. [https://www.nwp.org](https://www.nwp.org)

**Engineering — capstone design courses:** Teams deliver a working prototype to an actual client sponsor, assessed by faculty and practicing engineers against professional design criteria.

**Medical education — OSCEs (Objective Structured Clinical Examinations):** Students perform standardized patient encounters assessed with structured checklists and global ratings — a hybrid that adds scoring reliability to authentic performance.
