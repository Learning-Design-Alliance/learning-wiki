# Think-Pair-Share

## Description
A cooperative learning strategy where students first think about a question individually, then pair with a peer to discuss their thoughts, and finally share their ideas with the class. This method promotes active engagement, verbal processing, and peer learning, helping learners articulate their understanding.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires clear discussion prompts, instructor facilitation, and a structured time frame for each phase.
#### Constraints
- Some students may dominate the discussion, while others may remain passive. Works best when all learners are actively engaged and encouraged to contribute.

## Design

### Sequence
<!-- Steps with links to elements -->
1. Instructor presents a question or problem.
 2. Students think individually and formulate a response.
 3. Students pair up and discuss their ideas.
 4. Groups share their responses with the larger class.

## Examples
- - Middle school science: Students discuss a real-world problem before engaging in a class debate. 
 - University-level business course: Learners evaluate ethical dilemmas using Think-Pair-Share before presenting solutions.

## Impact
- Studies show increased engagement and comprehension compared to passive lecture (Lyman, 1981). Enhances critical thinking and verbal processing.
