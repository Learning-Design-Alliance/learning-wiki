# Wave-equation-first lesson sequence: intuitive phenomenon, simple sine wave, differential equation, then quantum substitution

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This pattern sequences the opening of the quantum mechanics course: begin with an intuitive wave phenomenon such as water ripples described by A(r) = A₀ sin r/r, move to the mathematically simpler sine wave A(x,t) = A₀ sin(kx−ωt) familiar from standing waves, derive the classical wave differential equation, then substitute the quantum relations for photon energy and de Broglie wavelength to obtain the Schrödinger equation. The author notes "A computer software like Mathematica or Matlab can help the lecturer to simulate this wave during the course."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students have learned standing waves in high school and are acquainted with imaginary numbers from calculus
#### Constraints
- Not all students can quickly understand the wave equation in sinusoidal or differential form, which motivates starting from the intuitive phenomenon

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- transition from classical-mechanics thinking to quantum-mechanics thinking
