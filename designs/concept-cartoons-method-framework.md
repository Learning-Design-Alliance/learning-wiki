# Concept cartoons© as a visual, cognitive-conflict-based method for inducing conceptual change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Concept cartoons© present an everyday situation visually with dialog bubbles from 3–5 fictional characters whose differing views create conflict that leads discussants to argumentation. The article states "Individual statements of these characters support formation of conflicts which lead discussants to argumentation", aiming to induce cognitive conflict that disturbs students' current expectations and drives conceptual change. Most character views are naïve but appear logical, and one is usually scientifically acceptable; statements are drawn from research on the most common children's views.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The method requires pictures with everyday situations and dialog bubbles whose statements derive from researched preconceptions of the topic
#### Constraints
- The article notes conceptual change is a very complex process and students' ideas are very resistant to change

### Target Learners
- fourth-grade primary school students

### Learning Goals
- scientifically acceptable explanations of natural phenomena
- argumentation and reasoning with evidence
