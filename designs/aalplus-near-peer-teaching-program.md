# AaLplus near-peer teaching program for preclinical basic medical skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
AaLplus (living anatomy plus) is a compulsory near-peer teaching program at Heidelberg University that "teaches basic medical skills, especially history taking, physical examination, and venepuncture, to preclinical students" across four semesters (320 students/year, 9h/semester, 30 hours total). Sessions occur in small groups of 10 to 12 participants led by teams of two tutors, using standardized patients, checklists, dummies, and case vignettes, and end with a formative OSCE with structured in-depth feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained peer tutors who complete 40 to 50 hours of preparatory teaching training before teaching
- Small groups of 10 to 12 participants to allow active participation and hands-on practical learning
- Standardized patients selected, trained and supervised within the MediKIT program
#### Constraints
- The program is embedded in Heidelberg's longitudinal Family Medicine curriculum and aligned with other curricular blocks, so its structure is tied to that curriculum
- Because the course is compulsory, no control group could be formed to compare the program's effect between participants

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students in their first four semesters

### Learning Goals
- History taking (structured anamnesis)
- Physical examination techniques
- Practical skills such as venepuncture, hand disinfection and blood pressure measurement
