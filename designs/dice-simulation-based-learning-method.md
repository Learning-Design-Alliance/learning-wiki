# DICE: a problem- and simulation-based learning method combining design, interpretation, computation, and estimation of a scientific investigation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
DICE is an engaging learning method that enables students to use simple simulations to design, analyze, and interpret a realistic epidemiological study. The acronym represents the learning steps involved, but not in order, and the method combines problem-oriented learning with Monte Carlo simulations. The overall aim is to promote statistical reasoning in the health sciences by combining medical and statistical knowledge in designing epidemiological studies. The article states that "The use of DICE as a learning tool combines problem-oriented learning [10,11] with simulations [12]."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in small groups to plan, generate, analyze, interpret, and communicate their own scientific investigation with simulations
- Access to statistical software such as Stata or R to generate and analyze simulated data
#### Constraints
- The effectiveness of DICE in conveying statistical concepts in epidemiology has not been formally evaluated yet
- The proposed simulation method is practically limited to only a few numbers of parameters, as each additional variable increases the complexity of the DGM exponentially

### Target Learners
- Graduate students in medical sciences, including public health and epidemiology

### Learning Goals
- Fundamental statistical concepts such as sampling variability, error probabilities, and the construction of statistical models
- Combining substantive medical knowledge and statistical concepts
