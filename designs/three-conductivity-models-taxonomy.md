# Three models of conductivity: macroscopic, Drude, and band structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes conductivity instruction around three distinct, sometimes contradictory physical models. The macroscopic model treats the system "as if it had no underlying structure" using charge, current, and voltage. The Drude model treats matter as ions and free classical electrons moving through a lattice. The band structure model is a semi-classical quantum description with de-localized electrons moving within or between bands. Students are expected to know when and how to use each model, much as thermodynamics, kinetic theory, and quantum statistics relate to each other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need to be able to distinguish between the commonly used, though sometimes contradictory, physical models and use them where appropriate
#### Constraints
- The band structure model is fundamentally semi-classical: band diagrams are predicated on the wave nature of electrons but depict electrons as particles, and the shift from spatial to energy-spatial diagrams is potentially confusing

### Target Learners
- upper-division engineering students in a modern physics/quantum mechanics course

### Learning Goals
- distinguishing and appropriately applying macroscopic, Drude, and band structure models of conduction
