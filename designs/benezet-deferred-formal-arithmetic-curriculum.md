# Benezet's deferred formal arithmetic curriculum: reasoning, estimation, and self-expression before grade 6

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A grade-by-grade curriculum (Table 1) that defers formal arithmetic drills until grade 6 while building numbers, telling time, measurement, and estimation through everyday activities such as reading, games, and license plates. Teachers were told to 'soft-pedal the mechanical arithmetic al drills and to concentrate on reasoning, estimation, and on self-expression', giving children many books to read and encouraging oral English in telling the stories of those books.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must 'teach any arithmetic as the need comes up but not on the plane of formal drill' (Berman 1935, quoted in the article's footnote 5).
#### Constraints
- Teachers new to the experiment have steered clear of all arithmetic, a failure mode the article flags in footnote 5.

### Target Learners
- K–6 elementary students

### Learning Goals
- reasoning and estimation
- verbal expression and problem-solving skills
- numeracy learned in context
