# Documents Model and Online Research and Comprehension Model as the framework for sourcing throughout online inquiry

## Description
The study is informed by two theoretical models. The Online Research and Comprehension Model frames online inquiry as five recursive, reciprocal processes: "specifying information need and locating, critically evaluating, synthesizing, and communicating online information". The Documents Model holds that "readers can construct two types of representations when reading multiple texts: an intertext model and an integrated mental model", with source-to-content and source-to-source links connecting source information to content and across documents. The intervention and its scoring rubrics were designed around these models, treating sourcing as applicable in every inquiry phase.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sourcing instruction should be structured to follow the phases of online inquiry so that source information is attended to in each phase
#### Constraints
- The Documents Model was initially developed in the context of interpreting historical documents, and the article applies it to online health information
