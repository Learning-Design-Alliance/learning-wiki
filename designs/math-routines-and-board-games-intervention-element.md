# Daily mathematical routines program with five logic challenge types and mathematical board-game sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Two classroom interventions were implemented with 3rd-grade classes in Abitibi-Témiscamingue. The first was "a school program which involved daily routines in mathematics (1-Sudoku, 2-chess, 3- find an intruder in a group of objects, 4- match replacing, 5- solving adaptation of Raven's matrices)" lasting two months (N=48). The second had children "play mathematical games two hours per week (N = 33)" for two months; a control group (N=38) received no particular intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Daily scheduling of the logic-challenge routines, or two hours per week of mathematical board games, sustained over two months.
#### Constraints
- The study participants were from the largest city in Abitibi-Témiscamingue; no rural students were involved, limiting generalization.

### Target Learners
- 3rd-grade primary school students

### Learning Goals
- mathematical problem solving
- school motivation
