# Symptom-centered case series organized around a chief complaint with eight disease categories

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a case-series design that “systematizes clinical data from patients presenting with this primary complaint,” organizing cases “into eight categories: joint ankylosis, coronoid process elongation, joint disorders, medial pterygoid muscle spasm, osteomyelitis, impacted maxillary teeth, joint infections, and joint tumors.” Rather than teaching disease-by-disease, the pattern starts from one chief complaint and requires residents to traverse multiple etiologies that share the same presentation, cultivating differential diagnosis across TMD and non-TMD causes. It was implemented in weekly two-case discussions with groups of 3–4 residents working from anonymized patient portfolios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cases must share one authentic chief complaint and span multiple diagnostic categories
- Anonymized patient portfolios with comprehensive medical/dental histories
- Faculty supervision with structured debriefing using assessment rubrics
#### Constraints
- Developed and piloted for the single complaint “limited mouth opening” in a TMJ specialty rotation; generalization to other complaints is proposed, not tested

### Target Learners
- dental resident trainees in standardized residency training

### Learning Goals
- TMD differential diagnosis and clinical reasoning from a chief complaint
