# A vertical homework-to-research course structure: familiar problems teach the tool, then harder curriculum problems, then research-level results

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course was organized as a three-stage 'vertical' progression woven with cross-cutting themes: '(i) First use familiar problems from introductory physics and/or math courses to learn basic Mathematica©R commands and programming methods.' '(ii) Then use those techniques to probe harder Physics problems at the junior-senior level' to motivate new skills, and '(iii) Finally, extend and expand the programming experience in order to obtain results comparable to some appearing in the research literature.' Cross-cutting themes compared similar computational methods across topics, with emphasis on 'breadth over depth' and consistent emphasis on visualization of both analytic and numerical results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students with some prior programming experience; the article notes the majority of majors have reasonable programming experience by the end of their sophomore year
#### Constraints
- The emphasis was 'breadth over depth' rather than extensive code writing; topic selection reflected the instructor's own research interests in quantum mechanics and semi-classical methods

### Target Learners
- junior-senior undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- programming and computational skills in an integrated symbolic-numerical language
- applying computational methods to core physics coursework and research literature
