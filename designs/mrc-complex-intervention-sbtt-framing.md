# Medical Research Council framework for complex interventions as the organizing framework for a simulation-based team training program

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article applies the Medical Research Council framework for complex interventions to structure the development, implementation, and evaluation of a simulation-based team training program in pediatric departments. It argues the intervention "meets the criteria for classification as a complex intervention" because it spans four departments over a year, targets multiple behaviors including teamwork, leadership, communication, and decision-making, requires specific facilitator skills, and serves a diverse recipient group of doctors and nurses with varying clinical experience. The framework's elements named are a feasibility study, development overview, implementation, and evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Intervention delivery requires trained simulation facilitators with specific skills, maintained equipment, and stakeholder collaboration across departments
#### Constraints
- The protocol states that implementing the project over a one-year time frame makes it challenging to identify other factors potentially influencing the measured outcomes

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals
