# Train orders-of-magnitude estimation and Fermi-question modeling as core physics skills for complex systems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course makes quantitative estimation and elementary modeling through Fermi questions its first learning objective, practiced in tutorials and assessed on the exam. The authors state that "estimates of orders of magnitude and elementary modeling through Fermi questions are crucial skills in the education of physicists," and that this is especially relevant for tackling real-life problems and complex systems such as climate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Class activities and exam exercises built around Fermi questions and elementary modeling, with worked examples in supplementary material
#### Constraints
- The authors report that most students never had to do this kind of reasoning before the course, and grade dispersion probably reflects this difficulty

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- orders-of-magnitude estimation
- elementary physical modeling
- applying physics to everyday and societal problems
