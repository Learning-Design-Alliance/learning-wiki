# Give real-time graphing activities substantially more time than a single short lab episode when targeting graph-interpretation gains in non-majors courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's null result and stated limitation motivate allocating extended time to real-time graphing treatments: the robot activity "was too little and too short", occupying half an hour of 30 hours of semester lab work, and students drew only two graphs. The authors state that in the future more time would be spent on the treatment activity, so implementers should plan repeated or extended sessions rather than one brief encounter.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A lab setting where students sketch kinematic graphs and receive instantaneous feedback by observing the corresponding motion, as in the robot activity
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a single null result from one two-hour session; the article shows more time is needed but does not identify an optimal duration

### Target Learners
- non-science majors in introductory conceptual physics labs

### Learning Goals
- interpretation of kinematic graphs
