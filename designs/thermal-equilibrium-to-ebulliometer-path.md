# A vocational-anchored physics learning path: three laboratory sessions plus a final participated lesson leading from thermal equilibrium to a professional instrument

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The path starts "by characterizing thermal equilibrium, gave some elements of calorimetry, analyzed temperature in a system where a constant amount of heat is supplied and finally utilized the Malligand ebullioscope". It comprised three 3-hour laboratory activities (9 hr total) and a 3-hour final participated lesson with data analysis and guided questions, with individual reports for assessment. The first lab used a detailed worksheet; later labs required reports without worksheets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A professional device routinely used in the students' future career as the anchoring instrument
- Access to a physics laboratory (the school itself lacked one, so the first lab was held at the Department of Physics)
#### Constraints
- PLS suggests 15 hr or more and joint planning with teachers; this path was 12 hr and teachers did not participate in initial educational choices

### Target Learners
- Second-class vocational secondary students aged 15-16 in viticulture and enology

### Learning Goals
- Thermal equilibrium and heat capacity
- Calorimetry and changes of state
- Understanding the functioning of the Malligand ebulliometer and alcoholic-strength measurement
