# Longitudinal near-peer teaching with formative OSCE and structured feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curricular pattern in which more experienced students teach basic clinical skills to junior students over multiple semesters, culminating in a compulsory but unmarked formative OSCE. Tutors "serve as assessors, using standardized checklists for rating and subsequently giving in-depth feedback to examinees", pointing out completed and missing checklist points and giving proposals for improvement. The program combines theory, hands-on practice, and tutor feedback in a consecutive curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A rigorous tutor selection process and 40 to 50 hours of preparatory tutor training
- Standardized checklists for OSCE rating and feedback
- Faculty supervision of tutors and standardized patients during lessons and OSCEs
#### Constraints
- The formative OSCE is unmarked, so students face no negative consequence from failing
- Rotation time per station is limited (5 min examination, 3 min feedback, 1 min change), which the authors note prevented standardized patients from giving feedback

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- Basic medical skills: history taking, physical examination, practical procedures
- Receiving and acting on structured performance feedback
