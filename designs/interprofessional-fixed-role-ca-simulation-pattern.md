# Interprofessional team simulation with fixed roles and deliberately high-complexity scenarios for non-technical skill training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This session-level design organizes residents into stable interprofessional teams (four to seven members, at least one resident from each year of pediatrics and nursing) who repeat the same simulated scenario with roles fixed across repetitions. The scenario was deliberately demanding: ventricular fibrillation was "purposefully chosen to increase the degree of technical and non-technical skill difficulty", since a high-complexity, low-frequency situation demanded more from the team in leadership, communication, task management, and teamwork. Before each simulation, residents decided who would be leader and other roles; the leader was the higher-degree medical resident and roles were maintained through all scenarios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stable team composition across repeated scenarios
- Simulator orientation on operation and limitations before each simulation
- Trained instructors delivering structured debriefing
#### Constraints
- The article notes residents were not used to frequent simulation training and some worked together clinically but not regularly on the same team

### Target Learners
- Pediatric medicine and nursing residents in interprofessional teams

### Learning Goals
- Leadership, communication, task management, and effective teamwork in emergency care
