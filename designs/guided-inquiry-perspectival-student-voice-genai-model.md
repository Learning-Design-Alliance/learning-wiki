# Conceptual model positioning students as co-designers of course policies through guided inquiry, perspectival thinking, and student voice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conceptual model (Figure 1) positions students not only as learners but as co-designers of course structures and policies. It integrates guided inquiry as the activity frame with perspectival thinking from situated learning theory and principles of student voice, so that "perspectival thinking, informed by situated learning theory and principles of student voice, helps students examine AI policy guidelines from multiple stakeholder perspectives." The model treats the GenAI policy itself as both an artifact for student engagement and a pedagogical tool, complementing top-down policy analyses by centering student agency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A guided inquiry learning environment with curated resources and structured individual-then-collaborative policy design tasks
- Instructor and institutional support for multi-stakeholder discussion
#### Constraints
- The study reframes policy as a social process rather than a fixed institutional document; co-design does not imply instructors or students alone bear responsibility for policy development

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in technology ethics coursework

### Learning Goals
- AI literacy
- perspectival thinking about AI governance
- student voice in policy formation
