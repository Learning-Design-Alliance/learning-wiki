# Four-activity active-learning package embedded in a flipped A&P lecture

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article designed four recurring in-class activities layered onto pre-posted flipped materials: a one-minute paper identifying the muddiest point, two to three group-based activities in groups of four, a partial outline completed in pairs after a ten-minute mini-lecture, and at least four clicker questions answered with colored response cards. The activities were "designed in a way to be suitable for all types of learning styles in order to increase the active participation of students in class". The instructor circulated, answered questions, and posted explanations for muddiest points on Blackboard the same day.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course materials (outlines, notes, narrated PowerPoint slides, animations) posted on Blackboard one week before each class
- Same instructor and same content coverage and exam schedule across conditions
#### Constraints
- Studied in a four-credit content-intensive A&P I course at a community college; the lab component was unchanged

### Target Learners
- Community college allied health majors in Human Anatomy and Physiology I

### Learning Goals
- Exam performance across Bloom difficulty levels
- Conceptual understanding and misconception correction
- Course satisfaction and retention
