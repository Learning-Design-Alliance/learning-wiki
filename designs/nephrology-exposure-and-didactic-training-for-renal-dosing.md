# Internal medicine house-staff should receive more nephrology clinical exposure and formal didactic educational training during residency to individualize medication dosing to renal function

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residency programs provide 'more nephrology clinical exposure and formal didactic educational training during residency' so house-staff can individualize therapy to patients' renal function and prevent dosing errors. It grounds this recommendation in its own finding of poor awareness and knowledge across all post-graduate year levels, and in the shortage of nephrologists amid a growing CKD population treated mainly by generalists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residency curricula must include both nephrology clinical exposure and formal didactic educational training on renal dose adjustment
#### Constraints
- Electronic drug prescribing systems 'do not often provide guidance on the need for dose modification', so such tools cannot substitute for trained awareness

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine house-staff (PGY1 through PGY3 and greater)

### Learning Goals
- Awareness of which medications need dose adjustment in chronic kidney disease
- Knowledge of the GFR level at which a medication needs adjustment
