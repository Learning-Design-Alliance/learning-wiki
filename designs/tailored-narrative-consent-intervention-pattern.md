# Narrative-based consent intervention combining hierarchical modular information, vignettes, pictures, teach-back, peer discussion and a neutral presenter

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level design for improving research-participants' understanding: information is delivered as a story in the local language with a PowerPoint presentation, using "A hierarchical and modular approach to providing information", vignettes, colourful pictures, asking participants to repeat explanations in their own words, peer discussion, and a presenter from outside the research team. In this article the pattern was instantiated as a fertilizer-trial story in ChiChewa that mapped randomisation, double-blinding and placebo onto farmers, numbered paper draws and identical fertiliser bags.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explanations must cover how each procedure will be implemented, its justification, and its personal implications
- Delivery in the participants' local language with everyday examples familiar to the population
#### Constraints
- Tested only as a pilot after the parent trial had ended, among women already familiar with the trial procedures

### Target Learners
- low-literacy adult clinical trial participants in Malawi

### Learning Goals
- understanding of randomisation, double-blinding, placebo use and their personal implications
