# Introduce group work with peer evaluation early in medical education, using the same evaluation system across years

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing peer-evaluated group work in the first year and continuing it with the same three-domain evaluation system (degree of prior learning, contribution to group discussion, cooperative attitude) into professional education. It states that "emphasizing teamwork trainings in the early years of medical school can improve students' academic and professional life" and that peer evaluation should be part of those trainings. The longitudinal correlations across years support this continuity design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Detailed instructions for peer evaluation must be provided to students, as instructions were suggested to strengthen reliability
- Peer evaluations should be anonymous to reduce social-relationship bias
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted at a single medical school and the book review group work may be unique to Mie University
- As an observational study, cause-and-effect relationships could not be concluded

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students
- preclinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- teamwork and communication skills
- preparation for group work (prior learning)
- cooperative attitude
- academic achievement
