# Blended pedagogical model combining peer instruction, group problem solving, and direct instruction with explicit growth-mindset framing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a lesson-level instructional pattern used in physics-major introductory mechanics: "each instructor used a blended model of direct instruction, peer instruction, and group problem-solving pedagogy", delivered in a SCALE-UP classroom with pre-reading quizzes, clicker questions, facilitated peer discussion, and modeled mathematical sense making. Instructors explicitly explained the value of conceptual understanding, active engagement, and a growth mindset, and conceptual presentations preceded quantitative problem solving. The authors credit this confluence for the atypical positive outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teaching team including a graduate teaching assistant and trained undergraduate learning assistants to facilitate discussions
- Explicit first-day and ongoing explanation of the pedagogical rationale and growth mindset to students
#### Constraints
- The article states the investigation was not a controlled study and does not claim these methods necessarily lead to the same results

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics
- expertlike attitudes and beliefs about physics
- mathematical sense making and problem solving
