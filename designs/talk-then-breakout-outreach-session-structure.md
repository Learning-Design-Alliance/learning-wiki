# Introductory talk followed by small-group interactive breakout sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The outreach events follow a two-part structure: a short whole-group introductory talk that defines networks, shows diverse everyday examples (the Tube, Facebook, food webs, the Web), and deliberately introduces a small amount of jargon, followed by small-group breakout sessions of 30–45 minutes in which 3–5 students per volunteer explore one module in depth. The authors note that "we wanted the students to think independently (and in small groups) a nd to investigate diﬃcult, open-ended problems instead of problems with neat answers". Students often presented their findings to the other groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An introductory talk of 20–30 minutes to establish shared terminology before breakouts
- 1–2 facilitators per breakout session with 1–2 additional helpers
- Open-ended problems rather than problems with neat answers
#### Constraints
- Sessions worked best when shortened; a 6-hour event was probably too long for students
- Group-size balancing was needed when most students picked the same topic

### Target Learners
- School students aged 13–16

### Learning Goals
- Independent and small-group investigation of open-ended problems
- Experiencing how a scientist tackles a problem
