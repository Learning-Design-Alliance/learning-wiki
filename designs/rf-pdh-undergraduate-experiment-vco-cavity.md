# Radio-frequency undergraduate experiment for Pound-Drever-Hall locking using a voltage controlled oscillator and coaxial cavity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A senior undergraduate experiment in which the primary objective is to frequency stabilize a voltage controlled oscillator to a cavity resonance at 800 MHz using the Pound-Drever-Hall method. It uses a commercial voltage controlled oscillator, a λ/4 coaxial transmission-line resonator with adjustable coupling loops, and an integrating control circuit. The article states the technique is "commonly applied to stabilize lasers at optical frequencies" and that RF equipment makes the study "inexpensively, and free from eye hazards."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A commercial voltage controlled oscillator whose central frequency approximately matches the cavity resonant frequency and whose modulation bandwidth greatly exceeds the Pound-Drever-Hall modulation frequency
- A resonating cavity with rotatable coupling loops allowing under, critical, and over coupling to be observed
#### Constraints
- The unloaded quality factor of the cavity, QU ≈ 700, is small compared to literature values, which the authors attribute primarily to the brass cartridges

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate physics students
- new graduate students and researchers learning about Pound-Drever-Hall locking

### Learning Goals
- understanding frequency stabilization and the Pound-Drever-Hall technique
- familiarity with modular radio-frequency components such as mixers and splitters
- basic feedback control loops
