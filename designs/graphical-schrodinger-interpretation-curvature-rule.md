# Graphical interpretation of the Schrödinger equation: curvature set by the difference between particle and system energy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course reformulates the one-dimensional time-independent Schrödinger equation graphically: the curvature of the wavefunction is given by a simplified constant k times the difference between particle energy and system (potential) energy. The article states "Die graphische Interpretation der Schrödinger-Gleichung kann man qualitativ beobachten und anwenden"; curvature opposite in sign to the wavefunction "ergibt eine Exponentialkurve"-type rules distinguish sinusoidal from exponential regions. Students sew together continuously differentiable wavefunction pieces that start and end at zero, then square the wavefunction to obtain probability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first command the toolbox concepts of energy, probability and curvature of graphed curves before using the graphical interpretation.
#### Constraints
- All constants are simplified into a single constant k, and the rules are applied to the one-dimensional square potentials used in the course.

### Target Learners
- university students in a conceptual quantum physics course
- future physics teachers

### Learning Goals
- graphically interpret the Schrödinger equation
- qualitatively understand bound states, ionisation and tunneling
