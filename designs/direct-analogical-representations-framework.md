# Direct vs. analogical representations: a framework for classifying simulations and games as complementary multiple representations

## Description
The article classifies its two instructional tools using a two-category representational framework. Simulations are referred to as "direct representations" of physical phenomena "since they include elements that map directly on such how such phenomena happen in real life," while educational games are referred to as "analogical representations" inspired by Gentner's Structure-Mapping Theory, in which "analogical reasoning enables learners to map relationships from a familiar or fictional domain onto a target concept." The framework frames the two modalities as complementary strengths that can be sequenced when teaching abstract physics phenomena.
