# Barrier-to-design backward mapping from documented PER barriers to learning goals, activity prompts, and rubric evidence

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The module is organized around six recurrent learner barriers drawn from physics education research: superposition as physical splitting, basis-change errors, coherent-superposition/mixture conflation, finite-sample misconceptions, ordered-operation reasoning, and notation ambiguity. "Documented barriers determine the learning goals, which in turn determine the activity prompts and assessment evidence." Table I makes the alignment explicit, and the article argues the structure is portable: instructors adapting it for another topic can identify barriers from the literature, state the design response as a prompt, and specify which assessment item evidences each goal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Barriers must be documented in the literature and addressable within the chosen scope without formalism beyond introductory quantum mechanics
#### Constraints
- Barriers requiring density matrices, multi-qubit entanglement, or full Hamiltonian time evolution were excluded as outside the scope of a single class meeting

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- distinguish coherent superposition from classical mixture
- predict basis-dependent measurement probabilities
- interpret finite-sample variability without state change
- communicate reasoning in consistent Dirac notation
