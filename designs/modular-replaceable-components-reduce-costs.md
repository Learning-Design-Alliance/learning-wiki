# Use modular replaceable components to reduce long-term simulation training costs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article demonstrates that designing simulation models with modular and replaceable components significantly reduces long-term cost per training session. The stomach and duodenum components, costing 100 US dollars, were intentionally designed as modular and replaceable units, as they are more susceptible to wear from repeated suturing practices, while reusable components (liver, gallbladder, pancreas) accounted for 400 US dollars of the budget.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of which components are susceptible to wear from repeated practice
#### Constraints
- The modular design applies only to the expendable components; the reusable components still represent the majority of the budget

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in under-resourced and remote regions

### Learning Goals
- affordable access to high-fidelity surgical simulation training
