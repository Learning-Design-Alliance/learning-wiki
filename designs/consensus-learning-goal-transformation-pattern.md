# Consensus-based learning-goal setting as the first step of a large lab course transformation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The transformation process started by establishing new learning goals through multi-stakeholder input: individual interviews with 10 physics faculty, an online survey to College of Engineering and Applied Sciences faculty with over 90 responses, and three round-table meetings with 18 faculty participants to build consensus. The article states "The outcome of this process was the following set of ﬁve broad learning goals and associated assessment instruments." Notably, "No goals included reinforcing physics concepts"; goals instead targeted experimental practices and epistemology, each paired with a named assessment instrument.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Input from both the physics department and the engineering college (CEAS) faculty, gathered via interviews and an online survey
#### Constraints
- The resulting goal set must be tractable and the course sustainable even as many different faculty members rotate teaching the course

### Target Learners
- first-year engineering and physical science majors in a large introductory physics lab course

### Learning Goals
- alignment of students' epistemology of experimental physics with the expert view
- scientific communication skills such as making presentation-quality graphs
- set-like reasoning when evaluating measurements
