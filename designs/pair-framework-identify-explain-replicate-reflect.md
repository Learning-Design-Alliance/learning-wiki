# The P-A.I.R. framework: a four-step structured AI-use cycle of identify, explain, replicate, and reflect

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
P-A.I.R. is a structured pedagogical approach designed to transform AI use into an active learning cycle rather than an answer-generating tool. Its "central mechanism ... is problem replication": after prompting an AI system to explain a challenging concept and solve a target problem, the student requests new, similar practice problems, attempts them independently, and only then compares solutions with the AI. The iterative process of "identify, explain, replicate, reflect" is designed to promote retrieval practice and elaborative engagement. In the study it was introduced at the start of the semester with a standardized prompt, and students were encouraged, though not required, to apply it to challenging homework or practice quiz problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured prompt standardizing the four-step interaction with an AI tool of the student's choice
- An optional reflection entry per instructional unit submitted via the course learning management system to support metacognitive engagement
#### Constraints
- Students were encouraged but not required to apply the framework
- Reflections were not analyzed in the present study
- The study relies on self-reported perceptions with no pre-intervention measure, so perceived gains cannot be attributed to the framework with certainty

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in algebra-based introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of physics
- Independent problem-solving skill
- Metacognitive engagement
