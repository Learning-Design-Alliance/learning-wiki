# PHYS 2150 photovoltaics CURE: a three-phase, in-person experimental physics CURE partnered with a national laboratory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
PHYS 2150: Experimental Physics 2 is a one-credit, second-year lab course at CU-Boulder transformed from a traditional prescriptive modern physics lab into a CURE in which students investigate how temperature and illumination wavelength affect the external quantum efficiency of metal-halide perovskite solar cells. The course runs in three roughly semester-third phases (Research Onboarding, Data Collection and analysis, Investigating Team Project Question), structured around a course-level research question set with a science mentor and team-level questions students generate from the aggregated class dataset. The article states that "the course emphasizes the practice of generating new questions from existing data, which is a common approach in many areas of physics and astronomy." Students use custom-built J-V and EQE apparatus, weekly Colab notebooks, and teamwork norms, serving roughly 200 students per academic year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A science mentor, defined as an active researcher in the CURE's domain who collaborates with the instructional team and students, provides scientific direction, and supplies materials such as experimental samples and analysis tools
- Equipment and materials accessible within existing departmental resources, scalable to all enrolled students, and supportable across semesters without substantial modification
#### Constraints
- The project design required that student participation be necessary rather than tasks automatable or better done by another research team
- The parameter space (wavelength, temperature, fabrication method) was too large for the partner lab to explore in reasonable time, making large student contribution critical and not artificial

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate physics, astrophysics, and engineering physics majors

### Learning Goals
- designing and executing an experimental plan
- using Python for basic experimental data analysis
- iterating on proposals and experimental design based on feedback
- working collaboratively on a shared research question
