# Layered course organization combining lectures, recitations, peer tutorials and office hours with individual and collaborative homework

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course was structured as "lectures, recitations, tutorials and oﬃce hours", with contact time broken up so the whole class met for lectures while recitations ran in four roughly equal sections and tutorials in six sections led by advanced undergraduates for closer interaction. Homework came in two kinds: individual homework and "collaborative" homework in groups of four dealing with technically advanced applications in sequentially ordered exercises. A student commented these assignments "fosters camaraderie and team work among peers and felt more eﬀective than individual homework assignments."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Co-instructors and advanced undergraduate teaching assistants to staff the recitation and tutorial sections, plus specially designed conceptual exercises for tutorials
#### Constraints
- The pattern was implemented in a large-enrollment (225-student) freshman course; the article does not report a test of the structure's separate effect

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science and engineering freshmen in large-enrollment courses

### Learning Goals
- problem-solving skill
- conceptual understanding through collaborative and individual homework
