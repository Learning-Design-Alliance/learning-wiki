# Research-shaped homework: self-contained undergraduate problems combining multiple methods, data representations, and workflows with AI permitted as a partner

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A homework design in which assignments are "self-contained pieces" of professional research practice, combining several methods, data representations, and workflows rather than routine applications of one known method. In KT1, ten such sheets covered literature work, modeling, plotting, detector interpretation, derivations, analysis-style measurements, and scientific communication, with AI explicitly allowed and students responsible for verifying results. The instructor tested candidate tasks beforehand and found a single prompt did not reliably yield a complete solution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must be tested so that a single prompt does not reliably yield a complete solution
- Students must remain responsible for verifying whether AI output makes physical sense
- Assignments need extended time (1.5 weeks per sheet in KT1)
#### Constraints
- The article treats resistance to a single prompt as design experience, not a durable property of the assignments, since models improve drastically

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students (fifth-semester bachelor students in the KT1 cohort)

### Learning Goals
- research practice skills such as modeling, data interpretation, and scientific communication
- foundational nuclear and particle physics knowledge
