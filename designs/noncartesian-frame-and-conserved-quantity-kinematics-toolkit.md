# Non-Cartesian frames plus conserved quantities as a two-idea pedagogical toolkit for early kinematics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes instruction around two ideas that normally appear after 2D kinematics: frame choice and conserved quantities. It states that "Choosing a good reference frame can simplify a solution dramatically" and that "If there are conserved quantities, it may be a very powerful problem-solving tool." The chase problems serve as a playground where both ideas are applied without calculus, giving students early exposure before energy and momentum conservation are formally introduced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students familiar with concepts of uniform motion, vectors and basics of relative motion
#### Constraints
- The conserved quantity in the chase problem (vX + uZ) has no transparent physical meaning, unlike energy or momentum

### Target Learners
- pre-calculus introductory physics students
- senior students who have taken an ODE course (for the brute-force treatment)

### Learning Goals
- problem-solving with conserved quantities
- flexible choice of reference frames
