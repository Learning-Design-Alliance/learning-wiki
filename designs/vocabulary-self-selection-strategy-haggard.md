# Vocabulary Self-Selection strategy as an autonomy-building, student-generated word-selection approach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Vocabulary Self-Selection (Haggard) is an interactive-learning instructional strategy in which students choose the words they want to learn from their own reading, guess meanings from context in collaborative groups, negotiate definitions with the teacher, and record words on index cards. The article describes it as "based on the self-selection of words during reading", using student-generated rather than preselected word lists, and states it "makes students independent over time". In the study it was implemented in groups of four over 24 sessions with a board vocabulary graph and card games.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students read texts and self-select words they believe should be learnt
- Group discussion and negotiation of word meanings with teacher facilitation
- Recording selected words, sentences and guessed meanings on a vocabulary graph and index cards
#### Constraints
- The study applied it with pre-intermediate Iranian EFL learners aged 14-16 in groups of four

### Target Learners
- pre-intermediate Iranian EFL learners aged 14-16

### Learning Goals
- vocabulary knowledge
- learner autonomy
- word consciousness
- inferring meaning from context
