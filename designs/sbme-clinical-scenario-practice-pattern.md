# Clinical-scenario simulation practice with web-based preview and teacher-guided debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This lesson-level pattern replaces demonstration-first practice with scenario-based simulation: students preview content via web-based learning, then practice clinical operations on molds or a human phantom within clinical scenarios, discussing and deciding the next action while the teacher guides them to find, think about, and solve problems themselves. The article states that "The practice began with clinical scenarios, in which the teacher added the content of practice," and that the "Muke." app was interspersed throughout the whole SBME process. Four printed cases (wound closure, infected dressing change, drainage-tube removal, CPR) each pair training items such as aseptic concept, doctor–patient communication, and teamwork with a preparation and teaching process ending in discussion and feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinical teacher leads each session of about 10 students
- Web-based preview material (the Muke app) available between study and practice sessions
- Molds or a human phantom for the clinical operations
- Debriefing and feedback after practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes standard assessment tools for SBME are still missing and a uniform measurement way is full of limitations

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate junior medical students learning surgery

### Learning Goals
- Surgical practical operation skills
- Aseptic concept
- Doctor–patient communication
- Humanistic care
- Teamwork
