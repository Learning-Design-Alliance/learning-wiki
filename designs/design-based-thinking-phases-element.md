# Design-based thinking operationalized through Capobianco et al.'s iterative phases

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article interprets design-based thinking as an "iterative, problem-solving process that integrates inquiry, reflection, and collaboration", unfolding in phases: problem scoping and information gathering (identifying stakeholders, criteria, constraints, assumptions and trade-offs), solution formulation (brainstorming, identifying materials and metrics), solution production and performance (building and testing prototypes or conceptual models), communication and documentation (sharing designs, comparing results, incorporating feedback), and optimization (refining and retesting). In the authors' courses, students propose conceptual solutions rather than build physical models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students engage in cycles of reflection, iteration, and improvement, balancing trade-offs, constraints, and assumptions
#### Constraints
- Due to resource constraints students do not create actual physical models; they propose solution approaches or conceptual models

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate engineering-major physics students

### Learning Goals
- Design thinking: stakeholder identification, metrics and criteria definition, iteration justification, and feasibility studies
