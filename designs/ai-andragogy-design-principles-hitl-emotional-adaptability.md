# Three learner-derived design principles for AI systems supporting andragogy: human-in-the-loop, emotional design, and adaptability

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study distills adult learners' interview perspectives into three design-principle themes with six sub-themes: human-in-the-loop (building shared mental models between humans and AI; enabling human-AI co-creation), emotional design (calibrating an appropriate level of AI reliance; empathetic communication), and adaptability (continuous adaptation; interoperability). Learners wanted AI that keeps "learning about the individual learners it is interacting with," guides skepticism of its own outputs, communicates empathetically, adapts after deployment, and works across platforms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI systems must involve human input, oversight, and decision-making alongside AI, with interpretable explanations of system behavior and learner control over generative processes.
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from perceptions of a small sample of degree-seeking adult learners on the East Coast of the US and are inherently subjective; the authors call for empirical measurement of effectiveness in future research.

### Target Learners
- Adult learners (typically 25 years or older) in online learning environments

### Learning Goals
- Self-directed, experience-based, problem-centered adult learning aligned with personal and professional goals
