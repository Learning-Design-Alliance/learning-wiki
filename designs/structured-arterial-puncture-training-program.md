# Structured arterial puncture training program combining theoretical instruction, simulator practice, and debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A training session of at least 1 hour delivered by a single instructor to groups of approximately 7 students, combining "theoretical instruction, simulation-based practice, and debriefing." It includes a reviewed slide deck, individual hands-on practice on an artificial arm simulator until self-reported confidence, peer-to-peer exercises, a standardized communication script practiced in pairs, and a structured debriefing following French simulation guidelines. The program was routinely implemented in the department and evaluated against usual heterogeneous bedside teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single instructor, an artificial arm simulator with palpable pulse and visible blood return, reviewed slide deck and communication script, and time of at least 1 hour per session
#### Constraints
- The training was not delivered by certified simulation instructors as recommended by the French National Authority for Health, which the authors state may have affected debriefing quality

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students performing their first arterial puncture

### Learning Goals
- Radial arterial puncture technique, patient communication, and procedural confidence
