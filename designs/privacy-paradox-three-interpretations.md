# Three interpretations of the privacy paradox guiding instructional design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on Kokolakis (2017), the article organizes the privacy paradox—the inconsistency between stated privacy concerns and observed behavior—into three interpretations: (1) surveys are the wrong methodology and most people have only vague concerns, so behavior should be monitored; (2) users lack information about how their data are captured, stored and used, so invisible data flows should be made visible; (3) the paradox exists as a positive privacy vs. utility trade-off. The authors used these interpretations to determine their instructional approach, translating them into letting participants experience first-hand the exposure of data they were unaware of having exposed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness interventions should address factual knowledge of how technologies operate, not only lists of do's and don'ts
- Relevance must be tied to personal situations where sensitivity and visibility of data are at stake
#### Constraints
- The theoretical debate on the existence of the privacy paradox is still open; different interpretations may explain different users
- Conceptualizing privacy risks is inherently challenging, depending on technical, cultural and subjective factors

### Target Learners
- young Internet users aged 11–20

### Learning Goals
- privacy awareness
- informed decisions about personal data disclosure
