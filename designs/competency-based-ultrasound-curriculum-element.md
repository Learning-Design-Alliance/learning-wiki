# Competency-based ultrasound curriculum combining theoretical classes and phantom-based hands-on practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a curriculum built for residents from non-ultrasound specialties, in which "The competency-based curriculum combined theoretical and practical training". The theoretical component comprises four one-hour classes covering foundational knowledge and typical images of liver, cardiovascular, thyroid and breast diseases; the practical component adds two one-hour scanning sessions, the second with "hands-on practice using an ultrasound phantom". Assessment uses an image interpretation questionnaire and a two-radiologist scan score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residents must have completed undergraduate medical education and undergone a minimum of six months of training at the hospital
#### Constraints
- Ultrasound-specializing residents were excluded because they receive a more comprehensive ultrasound training program
- The evaluation instruments were developed based on preliminary research and have not undergone formal validation

### Target Learners
- Standardized training residents from non-ultrasound specialties (internal medicine, surgery, pediatrics, general practice, emergency, anesthesiology)

### Learning Goals
- Ultrasound image interpretation accuracy and speed
- Basic ultrasound scanning technique for liver, abdominal vessels and other common organs
