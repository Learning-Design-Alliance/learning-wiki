# Science-fiction story-writing exercise with checkpoint discussions, peer critique, and a five-criterion rubric

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A semester-long activity in which each student selects a peer-reviewed journal article related to course topics and writes a science-fiction story based on its science. Four discussion-group meetings serve as checkpoints with points lost for missing work, anonymized drafts from other groups are critiqued twice, and "approximately 15% of the homework points for the course were devoted to the final story grade and successful completion of the checkpoints." A rubric grades science understanding, integration of the science, clarity, plot, and characters, in that order of importance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A grading rubric distributed at the start of the semester, scheduled checkpoint discussion days, and a homework-point incentive for the story and checkpoints.
#### Constraints
- In both implementations students perceived the activity as misaligned with a physics course, and in spring 2013 two students did not submit a final draft.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students in an electricity and magnetism course

### Learning Goals
- understanding the science of a peer-reviewed journal article and imagining its applications and consequences
