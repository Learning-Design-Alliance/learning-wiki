# Cognitive-energetic model grounding of NAT: synergistic engagement of cognitive mechanisms, energetic pools, and executive functions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article grounds NAT in the cognitive-energetic model, which "posits that ADHD involves deficits across three interrelated levels: cognitive mechanisms, energetic mechanisms, and EFs". NAT's efficacy is interpreted as synergistic: NCT trains core cognitive efficiency, progressively structured handwriting tasks challenge and regulate the child's arousal, activation, and effort pools, and phased teacher positive feedback modulates the effort pool via reward sensitivity. Enhanced cognitive efficiency alone is described as insufficient for transfer; it must be supported by an optimal energetic state and deployed under executive control within a meaningful academic context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simultaneous engagement of all three levels — cognitive training, scaffolded ecologically valid tasks, and motivational feedback — rather than EF training in isolation
#### Constraints
- The article states enhanced cognitive efficiency alone "is insufficient for far-transfer" without optimal energetic state and meaningful context

### Target Learners
- Children with ADHD

### Learning Goals
- Executive function efficiency
- Transfer of trained cognitive skills to academic tasks such as Chinese handwriting
