# Coordinated activity system model linking physics content, teaching and learning theory, and community fieldwork

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a course model whose three components — physics content study, readings on teaching and learning physics, and practical fieldwork teaching — form "the three interacting components of the course as the vertices of a triangle," each co-constructing the others. Components are temporally aligned (the same week students study a concept, read about difficulties with it, and teach it), and the structure is deliberately flexible so activities adapt to local context. The model is offered as transferable to other science domains by substituting content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Upper-division students with prior physics coursework; weekly class sessions plus two to four hours per week of supervised fieldwork at community sites
#### Constraints
- The article notes such systems may depend on a single individual and may not adapt when that individual leaves; sustaining the course after the founding instructor's departure remained uncertain

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors
- graduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual mastery of electricity and magnetism
- theories and practices of teaching physics
- metacognitive and epistemological development
