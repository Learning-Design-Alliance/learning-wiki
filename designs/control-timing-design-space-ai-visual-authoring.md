# Control timing as a design dimension for AI-assisted visual authoring: pre-, mid-, and post-generation control

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces "control timing" as a key design dimension in AI-assisted visual authoring, operationalized through three interaction paradigms that differ in when teachers intervene relative to AI output. Pre-generation control means users "specify intent solely through natural language prompts before generation"; mid-generation control means users "inspect and confirm an explicit layout structure before the system completes generation"; post-generation control means users "directly modify AI-generated visuals after generation through object-level edits". The framework holds task content constant and varies only the timing of human control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The paradigms require a multi-stage generation pipeline with distinct points (intent specification, intermediate planning, localized refinement) at which control can be exercised.
#### Constraints
- The article instantiates and evaluates the three paradigms only for primary mathematics word problems in a single-session study.

### Target Learners
- primary mathematics teachers (Grades 1–6)

### Learning Goals
- creating pedagogically correct instructional visuals for arithmetic word problems
