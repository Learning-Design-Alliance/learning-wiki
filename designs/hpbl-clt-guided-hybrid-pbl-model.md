# Hybrid PBL as a cognitive-load-theory-guided combination of problem-based learning with minimal lecturing and guided questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
HPBL is the article's modified PBL model in which "problem-based learning method, some guided questions, minimal lecturing and a tutor were used", combining small-group problem work with brief mini-lectures, real-world trigger examples, and end-of-lesson corrective feedback and summarization. The authors designed it on cognitive load theory and Vygotskian scaffolding so that guidance in the zone of proximal development reduces extraneous load and split attention while preserving PBL's germane-load benefits. It was operationalized with a floating tutor, group leaders and scribers, and Patient Information Sheets across five lesson phases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator/tutor to guide small groups and deliver brief lectures and corrective feedback
- Structured lesson plans with induction, acquisition, practice, closure, and assessment phases
- Trigger problems and Patient Information Sheets for the target topics
#### Constraints
- The article limits generalization to courses of similar content and level and an 8-week intervention
- Students unfamiliar with PBL may experience anxiety and low confidence at the start

### Target Learners
- undergraduate junior nursing students in large classes

### Learning Goals
- higher-order cognitive skills such as analysis, synthesis, and evaluation of pediatric nursing problems
- long-term retention of declarative knowledge
