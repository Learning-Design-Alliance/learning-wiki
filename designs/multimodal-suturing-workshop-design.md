# Multimodal suturing workshop design combining online preparation, expert demonstration, hands-on practice with feedback, and end-of-session competency evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's literature review identifies a set of design characteristics for suturing education: "interprofessional learning, multimodal content delivery that incorporates technology, opportunities for repeated review, direct faculty feedback, and repeated hands‐on practice with return demonstrations." The redesigned workshop implements these: preparatory lectures and videos in Canvas, live demonstration streamed to iPads, individual foam-model practice with faculty guidance, and OSCE-verified competency at the end of the session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must complete online preparatory materials and practice knot-tying before attending the in-person session, so faculty can focus on laceration repair techniques
#### Constraints
- Creating such a workshop entails significant financial investment in supplies, space, faculty time, and student costs; students may present with a wide range of background experience

### Target Learners
- student nurse-midwives
- novice midwifery learners

### Learning Goals
- perineal laceration repair skill
- suturing confidence and competence
