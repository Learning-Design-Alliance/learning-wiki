# Expert-panel human-review workflow for AI-generated MCQ drafting in a high-stakes exam

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured workflow in which LLMs (ChatGPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro) received the full blended-learning curriculum and generated MCQs via sequential prompting, followed by expert-panel review. The panel "accepted 82% of the questions", applied "limited expert refinement" to retained items, and excluded items needing substantial revision; 30 AI questions (18 ChatGPT, 12 Gemini) entered the exam. The authors position it so "AI-generated MCQs serve as template, while humans remain responsible for quality control".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human expert panel review of all generated items before exam use
- Provision of the full curriculum and learning materials to the LLMs for generation
#### Constraints
- 18.2% of initially generated MCQs had to be eliminated, showing the necessity of human oversight
- A visible bias underestimating FM competency was observed during human evaluation, e.g. distractors suggesting referrals for abdominal ultrasound, a common FM task

### Target Learners
- fifth-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Family Medicine clinical decision-making and clinical reasoning assessment
