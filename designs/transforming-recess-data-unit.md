# Transforming recess unit: a mathematics data-management unit in which students survey, graph, and present findings to change school practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-week interdisciplinary mathematics unit (two hours a day, five days a week) in which Grade 4 students designed surveys about playground use, represented results with tally charts, frequency tables, and double bar graphs, and presented findings to peers, teachers, the principal, and the school council. The unit connected local curriculum topics of “frequency, graphs, percentage, and measures of the centre of a distribution of values” to students' own issues, aiming to transform the social terrain of the playground.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher and researcher collaboration to design the unit around issues students identified
- Curriculum connections to data management and mapping
- Opportunities for students to present results to audiences with power to act
#### Constraints
- The article notes some students' marginalized identities were further reinforced in the unit and quieter voices were not heard

### Target Learners
- Grade 4 elementary students in a linguistically diverse urban school

### Learning Goals
- data management (survey design, graph representation, ratio, percentage)
- measures of centre
- comparing two related sets of data
