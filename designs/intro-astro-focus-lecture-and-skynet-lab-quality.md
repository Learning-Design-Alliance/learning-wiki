# Instructors seeking to boost students' science attitudes in introductory astronomy should concentrate on improving the quality of their lectures and Skynet-based telescope labs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This principle states that, of all course components, only the perceived helpfulness of in-class lectures and of Skynet-based labs correlated with students' astronomy and STEM attitudes, so instructors should invest effort there. The article's own words: "an instructor’s best bet for boosting attitudes with our Intro Astro curriculum is to concentrate on improving the quality of their lectures and of the Skynet-based telescope labs that they offer." It rests on the subset regressions showing weak but significant positive correlations for those two components' Helpfulness Indices and null results for all others.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The course must include an in-class lecture component and at least one Skynet-based lab presented and integrated in a way perceived as helpful to students' understanding
#### Constraints
- Findings are correlational and causal direction cannot be disentangled
- Other curriculum components may still improve traditional learning outcomes even though their attitude effect was not detected

### Target Learners
- introductory astronomy students at 2-year community colleges through Research I universities

### Learning Goals
- positive attitudes towards astronomy and STEM fields
