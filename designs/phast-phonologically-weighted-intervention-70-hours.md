# PHAST Reading Program adaptation: 70-hour phonologically-weighted decoding strategies intervention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention was an adaptation of the PHAST Reading Program delivered in small instructional groups of 5-8 students; "These groups received 70 hours of intervention (typically 45ŌĆō60 minutes daily) during the school year, as an add-on to their regular language arts and reading curriculum". "The PHAST intervention was designed to remediate decoding deficits and teach effective word identification strategies", combining direct instruction of letter-sound correspondences, phonological analysis and blending, four word-identification strategies, repeated varied practice, and later comprehension strategy work. Approximately 75%-80% of activities were devoted to decoding and word identification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experienced research teachers with a detailed scripted Scope and Sequence, weekly monitoring by a senior research teacher, monthly fidelity observations, and daily lesson diaries
#### Constraints
- Formal comprehension instruction was added only for lessons 44-70, with only 5-10 minutes per lesson allotted to comprehension activities

### Target Learners
- Children with reading disabilities in grades 3-4 in urban public and charter schools

### Learning Goals
- Decoding and word identification
- Transfer of strategies to uninstructed texts
- Reading comprehension
