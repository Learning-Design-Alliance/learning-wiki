# Hard scaffolding: static advance supports that reduce free-riding and cognitive load in cooperative learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article draws on Saye and Brush's concept of hard scaffolding as 'static supports that are devised in advance based on typical student difficulties with a task', implemented here as a paper-based worksheet. It sits within a Vygotskian framing in which scaffolding enables a novice to accomplish tasks beyond unassisted efforts and peer interaction facilitates cognitive growth. The authors argue that integrating well-structured scaffolding into cooperative learning minimizes the free-rider effect and promotes individual accountability, and they cite Saye and Brush's finding that hard scaffolding decreased learners' cognitive loads and fostered more active engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolds must be designed in advance based on typical student difficulties with the task
