# Frankfurt ‘whisper exam’: a case-based collaborative summative examination using key-feature questions completed in pairs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The whisper exam is a summative assessment in which third-year medical students complete “four clinical case vignettes comprising a total of 20 key-feature questions” in pairs, discussing at low voice and agreeing on a joint final response for each item. It is administered digitally on tablets using the tExam application with EX3 scoring software, with 60 minutes examination time and a cut score of 12 of 20 correct answers. The format was designed to assess clinical reasoning and transfer of knowledge from the preceding laboratory course in hygiene, microbiology and virology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation in pairs, with seatmates from the preceding laboratory course as partners
- A preceding case-based laboratory course covering the assessed learning objectives
- Digital examination infrastructure (tExam application and EX3 scoring software)
#### Constraints
- Passing or failing is assessed at the team level, which the authors note raises legal questions for multicenter adoption
- Examination time limited to 60 minutes according to 3 minutes per KFQ

### Target Learners
- Third-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Clinical decision-making in hygiene, microbiology and virology
- Collaborative competencies and consensus-based decision-making
