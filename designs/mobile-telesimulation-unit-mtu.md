# Mobile telesimulation unit (MTU): a portable, self-contained unit combining telecommunication and mobile simulation for remote skills training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The MTU is a portable, self-contained training unit developed through an iterative design process to deliver simulation-based medical education to rural and remote practitioners. It consists of an inflatable rapid deployment tent outfitted with a laptop, monitor, camera, speaker, microphone, and a portable wireless internet hub, enabling 2-way communication between trainees and an off-site mentor who has similar simulation supplies and setup. Off-the-shelf and low-cost equipment was deliberately used to keep the design accessible and practical. In this study it housed 20-minute chest tube insertion training sessions with deliberate hands-on practice and mentor feedback, both remotely and face-to-face.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires 2-way communication between trainees and an off-site skilled mentor, with similar simulation supplies and setup on both sides
- Requires reliable internet connectivity, which the authors note may be limited in rural and remote areas
#### Constraints
- In this test-of-concept study a portable rapid deployment tent was used rather than the eventual goal of a self-contained vehicle
- Training sessions were conducted in areas with reliable, high-speed internet access

### Target Learners
- rural and remote acute care practitioners
- medical students

### Learning Goals
- high-acuity low-occurrence procedures such as chest tube insertion
