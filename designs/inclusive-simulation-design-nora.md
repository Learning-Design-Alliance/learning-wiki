# Inclusive simulation design: involving all professional groups from the outset to align NORA simulation training with clinical practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's central framework for improving simulation training for non-operating room anaesthesia procedures: "inclusive simulation design, defined as involving input from all professional groups from the outset". It holds that NORA simulations fail to prepare teams unless disciplines such as anaesthesia contribute to scenario design, so that realistic task interactions, haemodynamic challenges and shared understanding can be trained. The authors argue engagement of all disciplines should be an ongoing, iterative improvement process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Active input from all professional groups involved in the NORA team, particularly anaesthesia, in designing scenarios and debriefing
#### Constraints
- The authors do not present their findings as the definitive solution for optimal NORA simulation scenario design; evidence is single-centre and qualitative

### Target Learners
- multidisciplinary emergency NORA teams (anaesthesia, radiology, neurology, nursing)

### Learning Goals
- shared understanding of roles and responsibilities
- multidisciplinary task collaboration
- team communication during time-critical procedures
