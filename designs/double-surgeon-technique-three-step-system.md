# The Double-Surgeon Technique: a three-step graduated delegation system for training the patient-side surgeon in robotic gastrectomy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The DST is a surgical educational system in which both a console-side surgeon and a patient-side surgeon work actively during robotic gastrectomy, with the patient-side surgeon progressively taking on tasks. Its "primary concept underlying the DST is to educate assistants as future surgeons". Training proceeds in steps: first vascular clipping and stapling, then handling an energy device, then active field development combining both, culminating in suprapancreatic lymph node dissection. The article presents it as an educational approach that uses robotic surgery opportunities to build laparoscopic skill.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A console-side surgeon who guides the trainee, develops tissue deployment initially, and can take over delicate tasks
- Trainees who begin with the simplest reliably performable techniques before advancing to energy-device dissection and active intervention
#### Constraints
- The technique is currently uncommon in Japan
- Unreliable clipping of blood vessels may lead to bleeding during the first step

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees (assistant surgeons) in gastrectomy

### Learning Goals
- endoscopic surgical techniques for laparoscopic and robotic gastrectomy, including lymph node dissection
