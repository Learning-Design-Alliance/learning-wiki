# Multimodal delirium assessment education combining didactic lectures, scenario-based case discussions, and one-to-one bedside mentoring with immediate corrective feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This quality improvement pattern delivered delirium assessment training in three components: "didactic lectures equipped nurses with evidence‐based knowledge", case discussions of challenging scenarios identified through pre-test error data, and "one‐on‐one bedside mentoring provided personalized, hands‐on instruction from an expert nurse" who observed assessments and gave immediate corrective feedback. Sessions were embedded in routine ward meetings and scheduled around individual shift rotations. It was well received, particularly among new nurses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An expert nurse with specialized delirium training available to deliver lectures, lead case discussions, and conduct one-on-one bedside mentoring
- Participation embedded in routine ward meetings to reach high participation rates
- Scheduling of mentoring sessions according to individual shift rotations to ensure comprehensive coverage of all bedside nurses
#### Constraints
- This study was conducted at a single centre, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to other healthcare settings
- Adherence to the intervention could not be tracked, as informed consent was waived for individual bedside nurses
- The possibility of a Hawthorne effect cannot be excluded, as nurses may have altered their behaviour because of the awareness of being observed

### Target Learners
- Intensive care unit nurses
- Newly recruited or transferred ICU nurses

### Learning Goals
- Accurate and reliable bedside delirium screening using the Intensive Care Delirium Screening Checklist (ICDSC)
