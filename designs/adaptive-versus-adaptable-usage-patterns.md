# Usage-pattern categorization of AI-powered personalized pedagogies: adaptive recommendation versus adaptable on-demand help

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distinguishes two usage patterns for GAI-powered personalized feedback, building on Du et al (2023) and Lai (2015): "adaptive learning or personalized recommendation—wherein the system proactively creates learning pathways or resources based on learner data; and (b) adaptable learning or on-demand help—wherein learners autonomously request just -in-time explanatory or problem -solving assistance." The framework treats who controls the feedback flow (system-initiated versus learner-initiated) as the pedagogically decisive variable, and the two experiments operationalize it as compulsory recommendation versus autonomous on-demand help. Its purpose is to explain heterogeneous achievement and autonomy effects across achievement groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A learning system capable of generating personalized feedback (GAI-powered hints, explanations, or practice) so that either system-initiated recommendation or learner-initiated help-seeking can be implemented
#### Constraints
- The framework was tested in high school physics over five-week interventions; the article notes variations in subject, educational level and intervention duration have impeded cross-study comparability

### Target Learners
- high school students in STEM (Grade 10 physics)

### Learning Goals
- physics academic achievement
- learner autonomy and self-regulated learning
