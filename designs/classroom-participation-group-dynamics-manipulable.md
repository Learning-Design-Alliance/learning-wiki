# Participation patterns as sociological group dynamics that can be deliberately manipulated

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing on Fassinger (1995), the article presents the view that student silence or involvement is largely a matter of sociological group dynamic, with peer expectations and norms shaping who talks. Karp and Yoels' concept of the rate buster and patterns of participation, in which a talker group forms and nonparticipants expect and enforce it, motivate the program's design choices such as random cash-draws and avoiding participation patterns. The author explicitly positions Cash-Participation as rebutting the claim that classroom dynamics lie beyond instructor control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Deliberate instructor manipulation of the classroom dynamic, including random reward draws and varied opportunity rounds
#### Constraints
- The article offers the author's own classroom experiences as the rebutting evidence rather than a systematic test of Fassinger's claim

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in introductory science courses

### Learning Goals
- Broadening participation beyond a small group of talkers
