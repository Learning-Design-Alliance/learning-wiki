# PER-based reform pattern for a large upper-division lecture course: interactive engagement plus applications and conceptual emphasis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course-level design pattern combining interactive engagement techniques (3-person consensus groups for peer instruction with clickers, about 5 questions per 50-minute class, collaborative problem-solving sessions staffed by trained facilitators, interactive simulations), content chosen via faculty interviews, and emphasis on "reasoning development, model building, and connections to real world applications" across lecture, homework, and exams. Homework was conceptually difficult but only moderately difficult mathematically.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All aspects of the course, including homework and exams, must address conceptual understanding
- trained facilitators who help students figure out answers rather than telling them
#### Constraints
- Collaborative problem-solving sessions were voluntary; attendance was encouraged by advertising their value and making homework difficult to complete alone

### Target Learners
- engineering majors in an upper-division modern physics course

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics
- reasoning development
- beliefs about the relevance and coherence of physics
