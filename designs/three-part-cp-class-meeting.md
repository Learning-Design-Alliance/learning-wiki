# Three-part class meeting: compact exposition, think-pair-share analysis, paired whole-problem solving

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Each weekly meeting follows a recurring functional sequence: a compact conceptual exposition naming the paradigm and its applicability conditions, interactive think-pair-share problem analysis, and paired whole-problem solving with instructor circulation. The article prints the timing shape: "roughly 15–20 minutes introduce the week's conceptual structure; most remaining time is devoted to interactive analysis of problems; and approximately 25 minutes near the end of the second meeting are used for paired problem solving." The parts map to increasing ICAP engagement levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The share phase must require more than naming an algorithm: students must point to structural clues, state the brute-force baseline, and defend improvements against peer and instructor questions.
#### Constraints
- Exact timings are adjustable; the article treats the functional sequence, not the specific minute counts, as the important feature.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate competitive programming students in large-enrollment introductory courses

### Learning Goals
- problem classification and paradigm recognition
- articulating solution reasoning
- collaborative algorithmic problem solving
