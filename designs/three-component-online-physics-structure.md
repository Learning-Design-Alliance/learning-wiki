# Three-component online course structure: asynchronous E-lecturing, synchronous problem-solving, and in-person laboratory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course design model restructuring large introductory physics courses into three components: asynchronous E-lecturing (55-minute recorded lectures with accompanying lecture-note tasks), synchronous problem-solving sessions (1 hour 55 minutes, mandatory or choice-based attendance), and in-person laboratories requiring 80% completion to pass. The authors state this model "is more suitable for the student population at CSI because a large percentage of students in all 4 different introductory physics classes are working (full or part-time) while taking a full-time course load." The model is implemented on Blackboard LMS with six structured tabs and OER materials at zero cost to students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A learning management system (Blackboard) with structured unit modules
- Instructor-designed assignments updated every semester
- Manual digital grading with detailed feedback
- OER textbook or equivalent zero-cost materials
#### Constraints
- Designed for introductory-level physics service courses for non-physics majors
- Laboratory component must be conducted in person
- Requires instructor time for hand-grading all assignments

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate non-physics majors in introductory physics courses
- Working students (full or part-time) taking full-time course loads

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual physics knowledge
- Problem-solving strategies
- Critical and analytical thinking
- Hands-on laboratory skills
