# Robotic telescopes and citizen-science platforms (Faulkes Telescopes, Las Cumbres Observatory, Agent Exoplanet) extend classroom exercises with student-acquired data

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The SalsaJ platform is complemented by remote observing tools in radio and optical wavelengths and by a webcam system for local data acquisition, so pupils can "acquire their own data (images or spectra) in the classroom through a web interface". The Faulkes Telescopes, a pair of 2-meter-mirror telescopes, are open to the school community, and the Agent Exoplanet web-based photometry of Las Cumbres Observatory invites students to analyze ground-based transit datasets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Web interface access to remote observing tools
- Webcam plugins developed by the Polish partner (CFT, Warsaw) for local acquisition
#### Constraints
- The Faulkes Telescopes are open to the school community only "under special circumstances"

### Target Learners
- middle and high school students

### Learning Goals
- acquiring and analyzing one's own astronomical data
- measuring techniques of star brightness changes
