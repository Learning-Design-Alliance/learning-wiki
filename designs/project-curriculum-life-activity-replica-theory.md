# Project curriculum theory: each phase of school work should be as far as possible a replica of a life activity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The organizing theory of Wells' project curriculum holds that "each phase of school work shall be so far as posible a replica of a life activity". Children are organized as families, the year's work is dramatized, and doll families provide motivation even for arithmetic. Proponents extend this to seeking life activities through which all skills, information, problem-solving training, and attitudes are developed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires school work to be organized as life activities such as families, in which children take assigned roles across a full school year
#### Constraints
- As reviewed, the theory was implemented only in grades 1-3 of one Trenton, N. J. school; the grades 4-6 course remained theoretical

### Target Learners
- children in the first six grades of elementary school

### Learning Goals
- school skills
- information
- problem solving
- fundamental attitudes
