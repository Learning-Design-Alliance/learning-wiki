# IT 341 multi-section assessment-format study with deliberately varied final exam formats

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An empirical study of 115 undergraduates in four sections (three in-class, one distance learning) of a Computer Networking core course (IT 341) at George Mason University, Fall 2016. The authors "drastically changed the exam structure in each section": section 001 used essay format only, section 002 used MCQ and essay, section DL used MCQ, essay, FIB and numerical problem, and section 004 used MCQ. Assessment data were drawn from the Blackboard Learn learning management system and analysed with Pearson correlation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All sections shared equivalent content, home/lab assignments and teaching methodologies on a common syllabus, with equal assessment time and the same grading rubric.
#### Constraints
- The online section's students had to meet on campus for proctored midterm and final exams; different instructors taught some sections.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate Information Sciences and Technology students in a core networking course

### Learning Goals
- evaluating student learning performance across varied assessment formats in computer networking
