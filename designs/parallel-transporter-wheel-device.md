# A frictionless parallel-transporter wheel as a thought-experiment device for measuring angle excess on a sphere

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter introduces an imagined instrument: a notched protractor wheel on a frictionless axle that stays fixed in orientation while its carrier walks. Walking a closed path on an asteroid, the wheel's final reading equals the accumulated angle, which the chapter links to area: "you can calculate the area of any shape just from the interior angles, as long as its sides are segments of Great Circles." It also grounds the Foucault pendulum discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A frictionless mounting so the wheel keeps its orientation; the chapter notes a real one "would be impossible to actually build for use on Earth"
#### Constraints
- Realizable only as a thought experiment or dream device; on Earth the Foucault pendulum substitutes

### Target Learners
- school students learning spherical geometry

### Learning Goals
- understand parallel transport and holonomy
- relate angle excess to enclosed area
