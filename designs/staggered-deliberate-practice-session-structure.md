# Staggered deliberate-practice session structure: three 1-hour sessions over three days with readiness assessments before progression

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The deliberate practice condition divided three hours of training into "three separate 1-hour sessions over three consecutive days," each targeting a specific step of the procedure with hands-on practice, immediate feedback, and readiness assessments at the end of each session. Students worked in small groups of 4–6 participants to allow effective supervision and individualized feedback, and those not meeting competency standards received tailored guidance and additional practice before advancing. This staggered structure was designed to let participants consolidate learning and progressively build skills.

#### Requirements
- Standardized models simulating real-life trauma scenarios; structured checklists assessing accuracy, safety, and procedural steps; instructor availability for immediate feedback
#### Constraints
- The article notes the distributed schedule itself may have contributed to the group's superior performance, and calls for future studies to separate the effect of time distribution from teaching method

### Target Learners
- first- to third-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- stepwise mastery of trauma procedures (airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure)
