# Telehealth education needs standardised definitions, trained educators, standardised authentic scenarios and placement practice opportunities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This principle holds that telehealth rapport-building should be formalised, fully integrated and explicitly taught rather than left ad hoc, because the study found disparate definitions, untrained educators and inconsistent scenarios undermining graduate preparedness. The article argues "simulation‐based Learning curricula (SBLC) offer an effective means of delivering training", conditional on scenario choice, protocol standardisation and authentic modalities. Guidelines must balance institutional flexibility with specific competencies for midwives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardised definitions of telehealth in midwifery care
- Specific training for clinical educators
- Focused scenario development and standardised educational resources
- Skills training in rapport-building in digital environments
- Opportunities to practise in clinical placements
#### Constraints
- Guidelines must balance flexibility for institutions to design context-suited curricula with directive detail on how telehealth encounters should be conducted

### Target Learners
- undergraduate midwifery students
- new midwifery graduates
- clinical educators

### Learning Goals
- rapport-building in telehealth consultations
- woman-centred communication in digital care settings
