# Pragmatic weekly low-fidelity RCDP curriculum with rotating scenarios covering shared objectives and a formative capstone assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A four-week repeating simulation curriculum: three 60-minute small-group RCDP training sessions followed by a 30-minute individual formative traditional simulation-based assessment. Four realistic scenarios (hemorrhagic shock, status epilepticus, opioid intoxication, mixed respiratory failure) each embedded the same three clinical problems in variable contexts and order, so learners attending only one or two sessions were still exposed to all key tasks. Delivery used a basic portable manikin, pre-recorded sounds, an app-based monitor, and existing training material to minimize costs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios designed from reviewed learning objectives, entrustable professional activities, and local rapid-response consultation data
- Fellow facilitators trained via a 60-minute interactive workshop and paired with the core fellow for their first session
- A 22-task checklist aligned with learning objectives for the formative assessment
#### Constraints
- The authors state it was impossible to determine how much of assessment performance was attributable to RCDP sessions, clinical exposure, concurrent teaching, or prior competencies; no pre-test was performed and RCDP was not compared with traditional simulation

### Target Learners
- specialty residents from medicine, surgery, and anesthesia programs
- medical students on ICU rotations

### Learning Goals
- management of circulatory shock, hypoxemic respiratory failure, and altered level of consciousness
