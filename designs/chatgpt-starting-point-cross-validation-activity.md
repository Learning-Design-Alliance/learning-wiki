# ChatGPT-as-starting-point with mandatory cross-validation against academic sources

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A challenge-based learning activity design in which students use ChatGPT as a starting point for research and then verify its outputs. In the biology course activity on gut microbiota, students gained quick insight from ChatGPT and "were tasked with validating the information derived from ChatGPT by cross-checking it with at least two academic, reliable, verifiable, and current sources." In the mathematics course, ChatGPT acted as a virtual research assistant for scikit-learn topics, with sample prompts provided to guide the research process. The design pairs the tool's immediacy with an explicit validation requirement intended to engage critical evaluation of AI outputs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction to validate ChatGPT outputs using formal academic sources
- Access to ChatGPT during the activity
#### Constraints
- Critical-evaluation survey items (Q11–Q13) received the lowest scores despite the explicit validation instruction, suggesting the design did not strongly foster critical evaluation skills

### Target Learners
- university students in general-education STEM courses across multiple campuses, online delivery

### Learning Goals
- disciplinary research skills (e.g., microbiota and nutrition; scikit-learn machine learning algorithms) and critical evaluation of AI-generated information
