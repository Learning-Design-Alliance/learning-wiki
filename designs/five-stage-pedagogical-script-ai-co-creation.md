# 5-Stage Pedagogical Script for one-on-one AI co-creation sessions

## Description
A structured session protocol tutors follow to move clients from an "Oracle" mindset (expecting answers) to a "Process Partner" mindset (co-creating solutions). The article states "Sessions followed a structured protocol (Figure 2) designed to shift clients from an 'Oracle' mindset (expecting answers) to a 'Process Partner' mindset (co-creating solutions)," with stages: Problem Framing (5–10 min), Tool-Task Mapping (3–5 min), Iterative Co-Prompting (20–30 min), Deployment & Verification (10–15 min), and Ethical Reflection (3–5 min). Tutors adapt pacing and tool selection to individual clients, and each session ends with a facilitator-verified summary documenting tools used and next steps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors withhold tool suggestions until the problem is clearly scoped in Stage 1
- Tutors calibrated by observing two sessions and co-facilitating two before leading independently
- Ethical Reflection reframed as a required session-end checklist because it was the easiest stage to compress
#### Constraints
- Debugging or deployment sometimes consumed the final minutes, making Stage 5 the easiest to compress
- Clients often arrived with vague goals requiring additional scoping time

### Target Learners
- Mixed-ability clients: students, faculty, and staff with self-selected authentic tasks

### Learning Goals
- Prompting as specification, requirement decomposition, output verification, and ethical reflection on AI use
