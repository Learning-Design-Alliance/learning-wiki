# Progressive low-to-high fidelity scalpel skill training sequence: demonstration, part-task practice on inanimate objects, low-fidelity wax simulation on a colleague's foot, then real callus and public patients

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a commonly used structured progression for teaching scalpel skills in podiatry programs. Teaching typically begins with theoretical content and demonstration, followed by part-task training on inanimate objects such as oranges, soap, cork, potatoes, fleecy web, wax or candles. Training then progresses to low-fidelity simulation such as debriding wax from wax impregnated materials attached to a colleague's foot, then to debriding actual callus from fellow students, and finally to treating members of the public in the university clinic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to inanimate practice materials, wax impregnated simulation bases, willing peer partners, and a university clinic with patients
#### Constraints
- The article notes many students were too young to have developed significant skin pathology, limiting practice on real callus from peers

### Target Learners
- novice undergraduate podiatry students

### Learning Goals
- safe and competent scalpel handling and callus debridement technique
