# The 1994 Kigali Falcon 50 crash analysis as a ready-to-use undergraduate physics case study with per-section exercises

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A physics-based analysis of the April 6, 1994 shooting down of the Rwandan presidential Falcon 50, built from expert reports, witness statements and publicly available information, and "designed to be understandable using undergraduate-level physics." It covers the aircraft approach trajectory, speed reconstruction, interception position, lateral deviation, missile launch site, trajectory and type, with supplementary material including "additional details and possible studies." Each section provides at least one idea of exercise for students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the publicly available source documents (expert reports, witness accounts, recordings) and the supplementary material
#### Constraints
- The analysis rests on stated assumptions (e.g. standard instrument approach procedure, constant deceleration) and the free-fall scenario is retained only as a bounding case

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- inference from uncertain real-world data
- model building and hypothesis discrimination
- understanding the role and limits of scientific expertise in judiciary processes
