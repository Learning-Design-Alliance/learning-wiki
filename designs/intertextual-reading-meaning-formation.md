# Intertextual reading as producing new meanings by setting relationships among texts

## Description
The article frames intertextuality as texts sharing with and cooperating with other texts, and defines intertextual reading as "producing new meanings by setting up relationships among the thoughts and ideas of the texts" (Akyol, 2010), running two or more texts at the same time to get meaning. On this account, reading involves discussing texts, and intertextual reading and meaning formation allow the reader to think intertextually and develop alternative perspectives. The study operationalizes this framework through four connection categories applied during language instruction.
