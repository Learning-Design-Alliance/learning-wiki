# One-dimensional polymer segment model linking configurational entropy to rubber's thermomechanics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's model treats a rubber polymer as a 1-D chain of fixed-length segments contributing ±a to the total length L. Counting configurations yields the configurational entropy, "a measure of the polymers' freedom of motion", which falls as stretching pulls polymers taut; combined with the First Law this gives tension increasing with temperature, and for small extensions "the tension is linear in L, like Hooke's law". Students use the same model to predict adiabatic temperature changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students derive the tension-temperature relation and complete the analytic prediction for the adiabatic temperature change before undertaking the lab activity.
#### Constraints
- The linear tension-extension prediction is derived only for small extensions, via the binomial expansion.
- The adiabatic temperature-change calculation assumes the temperature change is much smaller than the initial temperature.

### Target Learners
- second- and third-year undergraduate students in an advanced thermal physics course

### Learning Goals
- relate microscopic molecular configurations to macroscopic thermodynamic behavior
- apply the First Law of Thermodynamics and both microscopic and bulk definitions of entropy
