# Two-day small-group SBE workshop combining standardized patients, free simulators, and low-cost eye models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reusable workshop design for teaching a clinical specialty to preclinical medical students: a small group (limited to five participants) over two weekend days, using standardized patients, free-to-use web simulators, and low-cost physical eye models to teach anatomy, pathology, examination skills, and procedures. The authors state the group was "limited to 4–5 to allow for maximal individual learner attention as well as time and resources for practice" and that learners "practiced in groups of 2–3 aiding each other to allow for collaborative learning."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator with specialty expertise and a simulation education expert involved in design and delivery
- Access to standardized patients and the low-cost or free simulator resources listed
- Both-day attendance by participants with pre- and post-questionnaire completion
#### Constraints
- Designed and evaluated for second-year medical students who have first-year lecture knowledge but no completed clinical rotations
- The authors caution that "every medical school curriculum, schedule, and priorities are different and the same SBE solution may not be a good fit everywhere"

### Target Learners
- Second-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- Ophthalmology knowledge, examination skills (slit-lamp, ophthalmoscopy, tonometry, retinoscopy), and confidence with eye conditions
