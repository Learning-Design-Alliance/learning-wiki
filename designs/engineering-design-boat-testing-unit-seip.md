# Engineering Design boat-testing unit in a first-grade SEIP classroom

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An instructional unit developed by the teacher Krysta and the first author, part of a unit on Engineering Design whose goals were developing appreciation for iterative design and understanding physical variables affecting designs. Students read a fictional story, agreed to help a mouse cross a deep river, and groups built boats or bridges, drawing, building, testing, identifying improvements, and retesting in 75-minute sessions. Data came from videotaping each of the five sessions; the analyzed third session used a water-filled vat and toy mouse.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A water container for testing, student-built prototypes, and whole-class prediction and discussion of testing results
#### Constraints
- The analyzed episode involved a beginner/intermediate SEIP classroom of 21 first-graders speaking seven different first languages

### Target Learners
- first-grade emerging bilingual students in a Sheltered English Immersion Program

### Learning Goals
- iterative engineering design
- understanding physical variables affecting floating and stability
