# Diode laser spectroscopy teaching-lab experiment with student-assembled grating spectrometer

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A teaching-lab experiment in which students assemble the grating and lens of a grating spectrometer viewing a 658 nm Thorlabs HL6501MG diode laser, with the laser, beamsplitter, slit, prism, and camera pre-aligned on a 12-inch breadboard. The article states that "By using a low-power visible laser, this experiment is suitable for use in an open lab environment, where students assemble the spectrometer optics themselves as part of the project." A camera sends a live spectrum to a large monitor, giving a real-time view used for mode observation, temperature and current studies, and ECDL tuning. Appendix 1 lists all parts with 2025 prices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The left-side optical elements (laser, beamsplitter, slit, prism, camera) are pre-aligned with Torx-head bolts students cannot adjust, leaving grating and lens alignment to students; a handout walks students through the alignment procedure in detail because many enter with no previous optics experience.
#### Constraints
- Bare diode lasers have undesirable properties as stated: relatively low output power, ill-determined wavelength, difficult tuning, and poor beam quality; the threshold current varies substantially with temperature and from device to device, so Figure 14 cannot accurately predict power versus current for all devices.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students in a teaching lab, many entering with no previous optics experience

### Learning Goals
- Laser physics and optical resonators
- Grating spectroscopy and wavelength resolution
- Optical imaging and scientific image analysis
- Semiconductor physics and LED-to-laser transition
- Optical feedback for laser stabilization
