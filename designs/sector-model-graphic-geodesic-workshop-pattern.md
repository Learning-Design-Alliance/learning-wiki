# Sector-model workshop pattern: graphic geodesic construction with pencil and ruler teaches general relativity without going beyond elementary mathematics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This lesson-level pattern replaces solving the geodesic equation with hands-on graphic construction: learners draw geodesics by joining flat paper sectors so that the line remains locally straight. The article describes "a novel approac h that is focussed on geometric insight and makes do with elementary mathematics as ta ught in school", aimed at advanced high school students, undergraduates in physics minor or teacher-education programmes, and as a supplement to standard textbooks to strengthen geometric insight.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sector model worksheets (provided or self-constructed) covering the curved region, plus ruler and pencil
#### Constraints
- The workshop models use a deliberately coarse resolution, so accuracy is not high; suited for qualitative considerations
- The constructed lines are spatial geodesics, not light rays; light deflection is illustrated only by analogy

### Target Learners
- advanced high school students (grades 10 to 13, age 16 to 19)
- undergraduate students, especially physics minor and physics teacher education

### Learning Goals
- understand geodesics as locally straight lines
- understand how gravitational light deflection arises
- strengthen geometric insight into curved spaces and spacetimes
