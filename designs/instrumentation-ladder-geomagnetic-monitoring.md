# Instrumentation ladder: a tiered hierarchy linking research-grade observatories, intermediate-cost community magnetometers, and smartphones as engagement entry points

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an integrated hierarchy for participation in geomagnetic and space-weather studies. At the top are research-grade observatories; the middle tier holds "intermediate-cost community magnetometers (e.g., HamSCI Personal Space Weather Stations)"; smartphones sit at the bottom as "high-engagement entry points" for participation. The structure is meant to align citizen science with open-data protocols and Nature-of-Science pedagogy, transforming low-cost sensing from novelty into epistemically grounded inquiry feasible for introductory college laboratories, and to support a hybrid network combining calibrated instruments with distributed citizen participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Calibrated instruments must anchor the network, with citizen-hosted mid-quality magnetometers calibrated against observatory records and explicit data-quality and reporting frameworks adopted by projects.
#### Constraints
- Under realistic field conditions smartphones provide conceptual access and metacognitive insight but not quantitative resolution, so they cannot substitute for absolute total-field instruments in research or operational monitoring.

### Target Learners
- introductory college Earth and space science students
- citizen-science volunteers

### Learning Goals
- understanding geomagnetism and space weather
- Nature of Science conceptions
- data literacy and quantitative reasoning
