# Outdoor in-situ gamma-ray survey with Web-GIS result sharing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A 4-hour outdoor activity in which students wear the NaI(Tl) detector in a backpack and perform 180 s acquisitions at sampling points chosen to cover different ground coverage types (asphalt, grass, brick), recording GPS coordinates, photos and measurement IDs. Results are converted to K, U and Th concentrations via the Window Analysis Method and shared as a kml file on an open access Web-GIS platform, letting students "discuss the results, understand how radioactivity is spatially distributed, and how it relates to the ground coverage type".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-loaded map of measurement points in Google Maps, the GammaTOUCH app, and adherence to an acquisition procedure of taking notes of all relevant experimental conditions
#### Constraints
- In the outside environment it is impossible to manage all experimental parameters; vegetation, buildings, morphology, soil humidity and weak sources needing longer acquisition times can interfere with the measurement

### Target Learners
- pre-collegiate students in groups of 5–8

### Learning Goals
- designing and performing in-situ γ-ray spectrometry measurements
- critical understanding of unplanned factors in field surveys
- spatial thinking about radioactivity distribution

- Ground Coverage Dependent Radionuclide Concentrations [+M]
