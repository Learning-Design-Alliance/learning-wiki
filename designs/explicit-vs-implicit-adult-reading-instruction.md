# Explicit skills instruction versus implicit extensive reading as competing approaches to adult literacy instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames adult literacy instruction as divided between two approaches. The explicit skills instruction approach "directly teaches adults how to decode and comprehend," operationalized through the SRA/McGraw-Hill Direct Instruction Corrective Reading program, which uses "a graduated sequence of steps," massed practice, immediate specific feedback, scripted lessons, and unison responding. The implicit approach "encourages students to read text that is of interest to them without directly teaching them decoding and comprehension skills," operationalized as Extensive Reading with self-selected high-interest/low-vocabulary books, sustained silent reading, teacher read-alouds, and book discussion, aligned with andragogy and self-directed learning. The study tested both approaches alone and in combination.
