# Video-game model of physics learning based on balancing skill and challenge, clear goals and feedback, and repetition without penalty

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a pedagogical model that has students work on learning physics the way they work at video games, drawing on the observation that good games "require focus, a balance of skill level and challenge, clear goals and feedback, as well as the opportunity to repeat the task at hand until it is no longer difficult." It is operationalized through three principles: balancing skill level and challenge, providing clear goals and feedback at every stage, and allowing repetition without penalty. In practice this means defining what it means to reach the next level, giving unlimited attempts at a perfect score with slightly changed problems, giving feedback along the way, and rewarding winning with guaranteed high scores and bonus points for on-time 100%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Unlimited attempts without penalty, with problems changed slightly on each attempt
- Clear definition of what it means to get to the next level
- Feedback provided along the way
- Guaranteed high scores toward the final grade so that winning is worth the students' time
#### Constraints
- The stringent requirement that every student pass all homework assignments at 100% imposes a restriction on the difficulty level of the problems, as the authors state

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate engineering students in first-year calculus-based introductory physics courses

### Learning Goals
- Mathematical toolkit for physics problems
- Symbolic closed-form problem solving
- Conceptual understanding and problem-solving skills in mechanics and electricity and magnetism
