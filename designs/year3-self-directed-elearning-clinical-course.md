# Year-3 self-directed e-learning course combining reflective self-assessment and clinical video analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-credit-hour e-learning course run for six months during the third-year curriculum at a South Korean medical school, in a "self-directed learning format". Students completed two activities: writing a reflective paper on their OSCE performance after self-rating 14 areas on a 5-point scale, and analyzing patient encounters from online clinical videos provided by the Korean Consortium for e-Learning in Medical Education, writing reports on at least six of over 40 clinical presentation topics. A one-hour face-to-face orientation preceded the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the consortium's shared online clinical video repository of patient-encounter clips of approximately 10 minutes each
- A one-hour face-to-face orientation from the course director explaining the course purpose and website
#### Constraints
- The article notes that students used the videos to prepare for the exam even though they "were not intended to teach students test-taking skills"

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- Self-directed learning and clinical performance competencies assessed in the OSCE
