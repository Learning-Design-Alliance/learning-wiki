# Empirical model of individual, parental, and institutional factors affecting mathematics education doctoral students' academic performance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structural equation model in which five independent factors (fear of delay, student engagement, parental support, teacher support, and facilitating conditions) influence two intermediate factors (stress level and well-being), which along with the direct paths determine academic performance. The article states the model distinguished that five independent factors "can significantly influence two intermediated factors (namely stress level and well-being), and further influence the academic performance of mathematics education doctoral students". It was estimated with PLS-SEM on questionnaire data from 147 Indonesian doctoral students, with R2 of 0.651 for academic performance, 0.298 for stress level, and 0.592 for well-being, and nine of fourteen hypotheses supported.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questionnaire measurement of the seven constructs plus academic performance; measurement model must pass reliability (composite reliability and Cronbach's alpha above 0.60) and discriminant validity (Fornell-Larcker and HTMT below 0.90) before structural estimation
#### Constraints
- Conclusions are based on a voluntary sample of mathematics education doctoral students in Indonesia, limiting generalizability to other higher education systems; only individual, institutional, and parental level variables were included

### Target Learners
- doctoral students in mathematics education
