# Five-stage scaffolding design between planning and translating in the Hayes and Flower writing model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article designs a scaffolding sequence inserted between the planning and translating-plans-into-text stages of the Hayes and Flower writing model. It comprises five stages: "Concept mapping, modes of concept representation, translation among different modes of representation, multiple representations, and multimodal representation." Concept mapping trains hierarchical content arrangement; mode identification introduces text, picture, diagram, graph, equation, table and photo modes; translation and multiple-representation stages build flexibility across modes; multimodal representation integrates modes into cohesive writing reviewed by peers or experts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students first produce a planning outline of an assigned sub-topic before entering the scaffolding stages
- Writing is reviewed individually by peer review or expert review at the multimodal stage
#### Constraints
- Tested only with pre-service physics teachers on the topics of electromagnetism and waves and optics in small classes of about 17 students

### Target Learners
- pre-service physics teachers

### Learning Goals
- writing physics teaching materials
- physics conceptual understanding
- translating among representation modes
- making multiple representations
