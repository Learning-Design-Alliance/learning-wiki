# CLEIMS: extended immersive continuing simulation contextualising workshops and seminars

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
CLEIMS (Clinical Learning through Extended Immersion in Medical Simulation) places learners in an accurate, continuing simulation of a realistic clinical story, interspersed with just-in-time seminars and workshops on topics the simulation raises; after each activity students return to the simulation and continue management with newly acquired skills. Teams of four or five include a randomly chosen registrar, manage live trained simulated patients, complete real documentation, handle overnight on-call via text messages and phone calls, and receive SP feedback, video debriefing and a wrap-up session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires trained simulated patients, facilitators, high-fidelity mannequins, interactive internet modules, and is resource-intensive in academic and facilitator time
#### Constraints
- The article notes the program is quite resource-intensive, and its cost-effectiveness was awaiting a formal cost-benefit analysis

### Target Learners
- senior medical students in a graduate-entry program preparing for the junior doctor role

### Learning Goals
- prescribing and medication safety
- resuscitation and emergency management
- clinical reasoning and knowledge application for intern practice
