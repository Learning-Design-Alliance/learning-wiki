# Near-peer instructor training program combining didactics, cooperative reciprocal peer teaching, and hands-on prosection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An eight-session elective training program, run at the end of the second semester or start of summer break, that prepares top first-year anatomy students (grade 85/100 or above) to assist dissection teaching the following year. Content includes "the basics of didactics and pedagogic approaches and teaching methods", constructivist learning theory, active learning, facilitation skills, reflective writing, feedback methods, and advanced cadaveric dissection. The program is "based on a principle of cooperative learning, with reciprocal peer teaching, where students alternate roles as teacher and student", with peer evaluations via student-developed rubrics and a culminating CBL session or mini-lecture plus prosection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants are selected from students who graduated their anatomy class with a grade of 85 (out of 100) or above
- Providing ongoing verbal and written peer evaluations was a requirement for the participants
#### Constraints
- Due to financial restraints, only the top training program graduates (four to six annually) could be employed as NPIs

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students
- sophomore near-peer instructors

### Learning Goals
- anatomy dissection and prosection skills
- didactic and teaching skills
- providing effective feedback
