# Use all-multiple-choice exams with scratch notebooks to assess whether students spontaneously value problem-solving strategies like drawing diagrams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that a multiple-choice exam with a scratch notebook reveals students' problem-solving strategies in a more "native" form, because students only write what they think is helpful when no partial credit is available. Since "there was no partial credit given for drawing the diagrams or doing the scratchworks", students who drew diagrams must have valued them for solving problems. This turns an ordinary multiple-choice final into a novel assessment of spontaneous strategy use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an exam notebook or scratch booklet provided to each student
- students informed that only the chosen option counts for the grade
#### Constraints
- The measure counts only the number of problems with diagrams or scratchworks, not their quality; scratchwork quantification does not distinguish a short from a long scratchwork

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- spontaneous use of effective problem solving strategies such as diagram drawing
