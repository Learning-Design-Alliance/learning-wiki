# Add a brief SA-focused educational component (web module plus follow-up group discussion) to simulation-based team training to strengthen its effects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supplementing simulation-based team education with a small, systematic SA-focused intervention: a web module followed by group discussion in preparation for an in situ simulation session. The authors state that "a small educational intervention, consisting of a web module with follow-up group discussion in preparation for an in situ simulation session, may increase the positive effects of simulation based education". In their randomized study this combination improved TEAM leadership, task management and total scores relative to simulation training alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A web-based SA module completed before training, an instructor-facilitated reflective discussion, and an SA-focused debriefing integrated into the simulation session
#### Constraints
- The effect was demonstrated for team performance subscales only; no significant effect was found on SAGAT-measured SA or the ABCDE checklist, and the authors note it is too early to draw definitive conclusions about which educational intervention is most effective for optimizing team SA

### Target Learners
- interprofessional intensive care teams
- healthcare teams training in acute care settings

### Learning Goals
- team performance (leadership, task management) in acute critical situations
