# Sociomathematical norms for designing mathematically good computational models as a mechanism for integrating programming with elementary science

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper proposes that teachers can integrate agent-based programming with science by emphasizing mathematizing and measurement, and by cultivating sociomathematical norms — normative classroom criteria for what counts as an acceptable mathematical solution or a "good" model — which "regulate classroom discourse and influence the learning opportunities that arise for both the students and the teacher". Three norms developed in Phase II: Inventing Measures, Approximations, and Predictions. Each norm's development paralleled new computational practices in students' ViMAP modeling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher-led development of classroom-wide conventions, connected to out-of-computer embodied and physical modeling experiences so commands like step-size become experientially real
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated in one 3rd-grade classroom over 7 months with a teacher-researcher partnership; the teacher's framing of accuracy was her intuitive conceptualization, which researchers did not intervene to align with epistemologists' view that models are incomplete

### Target Learners
- elementary (3rd grade) students

### Learning Goals
- computational thinking
- scientific modeling of motion
- measurement and multiplicative reasoning
