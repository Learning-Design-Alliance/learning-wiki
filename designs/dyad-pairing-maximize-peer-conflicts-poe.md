# Dyad composition pairing low-scoring with higher-scoring students to maximize peer conflicts, combined with POE simulation tasks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this design pattern, students are assigned to dyads to work on simulation-based predict-observe-explain tasks with pairing deliberately arranged so that "students of low scores being paired up with students of relatively higher scores to maximize the chance of peer conflicts," alongside friendship patterns to ensure partners could work together for a prolonged period. Each task required students to jointly make a prediction, explain it, run the program to test it, and reconcile any discrepancy between prediction and observation in the microworld, with responses recorded in worksheets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-test results available to inform pairing
- Friendship patterns considered so partners can work together for a prolonged period
- Worksheets structuring predict-observe-explain responses
#### Constraints
- The categorizations of engagement and change were arbitrarily defined for crude comparison between dyads

### Target Learners
- Grade 10 high school science students

### Target Goals
- Conceptual change in mechanics
- Resolving discrepancies between predictions and observations
