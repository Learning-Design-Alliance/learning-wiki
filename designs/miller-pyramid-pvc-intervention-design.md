# Miller Pyramid-based five-phase educational intervention design for first PVC skill acquisition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article structures its PVC educational intervention as a synthesis of instructional theories proposed by the Miller Pyramid, with four steps: "(1) Knows: acquisition of theoretical concepts and skills (2) Knows How: connecting what students already know or can do with what is to be newly learned... (3) Shows How: demonstrations... in a simulated laboratory environment (4) Does: opportunities for students to practice what they have learned and receive instructor mentoring". The intervention was implemented in five phases: intervention design, theoretical information (two face-to-face parts of 60 and 120 minutes), pre-test and demonstration with an IV arm task trainer, the group-specific intervention, and a post-intervention survey two weeks later.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students without prior PVC training practicing under researcher supervision in a skills laboratory with separate simulation rooms
#### Constraints
- Blinding was not implemented and was deemed impractical and ethically challenging, acknowledged as a notable limitation

### Target Learners
- first-year nursing students

### Learning Goals
- peripheral venous catheterization knowledge and psychomotor skill acquisition
