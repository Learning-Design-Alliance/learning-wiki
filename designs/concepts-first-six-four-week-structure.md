# Concepts-first course structure: six weeks devoted to learning concepts followed by four weeks of practice on complicated problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A quarter-level course organization pattern in which all core concepts are covered before complicated problem solving begins. The treatment section "worked to learn the ideas over the ﬁrst 6 weeks of the 10-week quarter and then used the ﬁnal 4 weeks to apply those principles to solve the analyti- cally (and algebraically) complicated physical problems that physicists prize." The structure was implemented within an active-learning course using identical curricular materials to the control, so the sequence, not the materials, is the manipulated design feature.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course materials must support separation of conceptual instruction from complicated multi-step problem solving across the quarter
#### Constraints
- Tested in one quarter of introductory classical mechanics at UC Davis with non-random section assignment
- The author later taught a watered-down 8-weeks-concepts/2-weeks-problems version in which the grade gap for underrepresented groups was about 1/2 rather than 1/8 of the standard-lecture gap

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of mechanics
- problem-solving with complicated analytic problems
