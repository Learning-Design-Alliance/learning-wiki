# Relational bridge: AI-enabled pedagogical accompaniment as temporary, directional, bounded micro-mediation toward human engagement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The framework defines AI-enabled pedagogical accompaniment as "a structured process that supports learners in clarifying goals, articulating questions, and identifying next steps, while consistently orienting interaction toward teachers, peers, and communities of practice." Its core, the relational bridge, is a sequence of micro-mediations with three properties: temporality (interaction limited in scope and duration), directionality (oriented toward concrete next steps involving human actors), and boundedness (constrained by pedagogical purposes and ethical safeguards). Effectiveness is assessed by observable transitions toward participation, agency, and responsibility, not by engagement with the AI itself. The bridge is explicitly designed to be temporary and to avoid open-ended conversational loops.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human-in-command governance: adults retain responsibility for educational decisions, relational boundaries, and ethical oversight, with explicit thresholds for escalation or hand-off to human actors.
#### Constraints
- The framework explicitly rejects technocentric and substitutionist models; the system is not conceived as an autonomous empathic subject or surrogate for human care, and does not provide curricular instruction, psychological counseling, or affective companionship.

### Target Learners
- Learners in technical and vocational education facing challenges of self-efficacy, orientation, and continuity of engagement

### Learning Goals
- Orientation, reflection, and decision-making; articulation of goals and questions; transition toward human educational relationships
