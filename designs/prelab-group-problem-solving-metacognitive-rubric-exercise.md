# Weekly pre-laboratory cooperative group problem-solving exercise with metacognitive self-monitoring rubric

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the studied course, the first 60 minutes of each laboratory section were devoted to a cooperative group problem-solving exercise on context-rich problems framed in biology or health science contexts, followed by an individual metacognitive reflection. Students filled out a rubric adapted from Yerushalmi et al.'s self-diagnosis scaffolding, rating understanding of problem description, solution construction, and logical progression as "Full/Partial/None" and noting strengths and struggles. Groups of 2–3 (or paired pairs in Spring 2017) spent approximately "30 -50 minutes to solve the problem" and could consult instructors, textbooks, and notebooks. This exercise supplied the context in which learning orientations were elicited and classified.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor review of the problem solution after the exercise, submission of the metacognitive reflection for completion credit, and conceptual follow-up questions for fast-finishing groups.
#### Constraints
- The exercise was implemented in a traditional lecture-laboratory format with no recitation section; open resources were permitted because a significant number of students arrived underprepared.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life science majors in introductory algebra-based physics laboratory sections

### Learning Goals
- problem-solving skills, metacognitive self-monitoring, and teamwork in physics
