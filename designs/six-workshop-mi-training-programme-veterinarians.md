# Stretched-out 6-month MI training programme with six workshops, between-workshop practice and coded feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An educational concept for training motivational interviewing skills in cattle veterinarians, delivered as study groups of four to eight persons meeting at six workshops over 6–7 months, totalling 36 hours. Between workshops participants read 30–70 pages of the MI handbook and recorded one practised conversation uploaded for coding and feedback. Skills were evaluated with role-play conversations with professional actors coded using the MITI V.4.2.1 manual. The article states veterinarians "gathered in study groups of four to eight persons (six groups in total) at six workshops, comprising in total 36 hours and spread out over a period of 6–7 months".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants need opportunities to record professional conversations for practice and feedback, which the article reports participants found difficult
- Feedback on recordings and transcripts, which participants rated as the most valuable training component
#### Constraints
- Time was the main reason participants gave for not completing the course, and the authors state a prolonged course is unlikely to be attractive to veterinarians in VHHM
- Some participants commented the programme had not supplied enough training to master the new skills

### Target Learners
- practising cattle veterinarians involved in veterinary herd health management

### Learning Goals
- client-oriented communication skills for supporting farmer behaviour change in herd health management
