# Three-day iron oxide nanoparticle synthesis, surface-stripping, and characterization laboratory protocol

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A three-day advanced undergraduate laboratory in which Day 1 is nanoparticle synthesis, Day 2 is sample preparation and surface treatment, and Day 3 is characterization. Students use air-free chemistry to synthesize and separate iron oxide magnetic nanoparticles, then "modify the nanoparticle surface using a chemical stripping agent" (iodomethane), and characterize size, morphology, and magnetic response with SEM, DLS, and a magnetophotometer. The protocol follows a published one-pot two-part synthesis modified to align with undergraduate laboratory expectations, and detailed protocols are provided in supplementary Instructor and Student Manuals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students require prior general laboratory safety training and at least one semester equivalent of general chemistry
- A trained laboratory assistant must be present throughout the procedure
- Access to SEM, DLS, and MAP instrumentation and a fume hood, glove box, and Schlenk line
#### Constraints
- Equipment access limited which measurements students could perform themselves in the pilot
- The cohort's mixed response on rigor led the authors to include variations in the Instructor Manual to increase complexity

### Target Learners
- Upper-division undergraduate students in inorganic chemistry or materials science
- Potentially graduate students in an experimental methods course

### Learning Goals
- Stoichiometric reagent calculation
- Air-sensitive chemistry techniques (glove box, Schlenk line)
- Nanomaterial characterization and data analysis
- Scientific communication and data visualization
