# A four-step methodology for bridging arts and STEM: identify core concepts, connect them to the arts, design activities, and assess

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a step-by-step methodology to integrate professional skills into technical classes through the arts. Its aim is to "enrich the learning of technical topics by integrating professional skills through the arts, rather than by adding separate humanity-related subjects to the curriculum as an 'additional'". Step 1 identifies the technical core concepts of the subject; Step 2 finds nuanced connections between those core concepts and forms of art; Step 3 designs teaching activities that foster critical thinking, creativity, active learning, or teamwork; Step 4 assesses the activities before, during, and after the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Core concepts identified in Step 1 must truly be the basis for the subject, requiring experienced instructors
- Assessment in Step 4 must take place before, during, and after the course
#### Constraints
- Connections in Step 2 should serve the nuclear ideas selected in Step 1, avoiding shallower or easier-to-find metaphors with secondary concepts
- Rigid templates that do not foster rhizomatic learning should be avoided

### Target Learners
- computer science students at technical universities

### Learning Goals
- technical concepts such as information entropy and network security
- professional skills such as critical thinking, teamwork, and oral presentation
