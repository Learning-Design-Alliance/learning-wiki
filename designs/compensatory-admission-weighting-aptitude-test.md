# Use a compensatory admission procedure weighting an aptitude test so applicants can offset weaker school-leaving grades with strength on the test

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes and evaluates a compensatory local admission procedure in which "a ranking formula was used that is given in the methods section", combining the school-leaving GPA with the TMS score so that "potentially able applicants to compensate for poor school-leaving grades by strength in the TMS as an additional measure of merit". Unlike additive credit schemes, the compensatory formula lets strength on one instrument offset weakness on the other. The study's regression and continuity results support this weighting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The aptitude test must be administered and scored (TMS standardised with mean 100, SD 10) and combined with the school-leaving GPA in a ranking formula
- Only better than average TMS scores were considered in the formula as implemented
#### Constraints
- The article states the observed absence of a clear GPA relationship "may be restricted to the study groups and cannot be generalised at this stage"
- German law requires the school-leaving grade to carry substantial weight in admission decisions

### Target Learners
- Applicants to undergraduate medical training with a wide range of school-leaving grades

### Learning Goals
- Selection of applicants with potential for academic success and continuity in the medical program
