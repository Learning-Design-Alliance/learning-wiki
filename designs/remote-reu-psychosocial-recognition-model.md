# Integrated model of self- and other-recognition linking REU experiences to SoB, self-efficacy, identity, and career decisions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Fig. 5 synthesizes its interview findings with prior literature into an integrated model: mentees' experiences of self-recognition (performance/competence, producing potentially new results, more knowledge) and other-recognition (being seen as reliable and trustworthy, scientific conversation, approachable mentor) boost SoB and self-efficacy, which lead to physics and researcher identity and finally a positive view of a research career and future career decisions. As the article states, "Figure 5 portrays an integrated model that shows how various elements of the REU experience contributed to growth in mentees' SoB, self-efficacy, and leads to physics and research identity". The model is aligned with Bandura's self-efficacy definition and with Hazari's physics identity framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors must provide autonomy and ownership and support mentees' confidence for the recognition pathways to operate as described
#### Constraints
- Derived from a small qualitative sample of ten mentees and eight mentors in remote summer-2020 physics REU programs; the article notes the lack of any of the factors can result in a mediocre undergraduate research experience

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics REU mentees

### Learning Goals
- Development of sense of belonging, self-efficacy, physics and researcher identity, and informed career decision-making
