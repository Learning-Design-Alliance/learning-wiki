# Modular IMR-SS training framework integrating teaching, hands-on practice, and assessment phases

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose a modular curriculum framework for immersive mixed reality surgical self-training that "tightly integrates teaching, hands-on, and assessment phases, effectively bridging the gap between virtual simulation and physical practice." Guided by the Cognitive Affective Model of Immersive Learning, the teaching module follows a Preparation–Exploration–Exercise–Reflection cycle with the procedure decomposed via expert-led cognitive task analysis; the hands-on module pairs patient-specific 3D-printed models with holographic digital twins; the assessment module tracks process errors and breaches, not just final outcomes, and closes the loop with real-time multimodal feedback. The authors argue this structure may explain performance benefits where less structured MR training attempts did not.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A theory-driven, expert-validated curriculum; expert-defined accuracy and safety thresholds; spatial tracking of instruments and phantom to synchronize virtual and physical contexts
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated for one classic spine procedure; the authors state future work must develop IMR-SSs for more complex surgeries and validate generalizability across procedures

### Target Learners
- novice medical students in surgical skills training

### Learning Goals
- surgical procedural skill acquisition
- safe and accurate instrument handling
