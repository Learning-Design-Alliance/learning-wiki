# Classroom teachers and teaching assistants — not only researchers — can deliver effective phoneme awareness instruction in kindergarten

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study's central design choice was to have phonological awareness instruction delivered "by kindergarten teachers and their teaching assistants" in ordinary low-income, inner-city classrooms, rather than by outside researchers. The significant post-intervention advantages on phoneme segmentation, letter knowledge, reading, and invented spelling indicate classroom staff can implement this instruction effectively. This supports embedding phoneme awareness training in regular kindergarten practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Kindergarten teachers and teaching assistants deliver the instruction in their own classrooms over an extended period (11 weeks in this study)
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from low-income, inner-city kindergarten classrooms in an urban district in upstate New York

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten children in low-income, inner-city schools

### Learning Goals
- Phoneme awareness, letter name and letter sound knowledge, beginning reading, and invented spelling
