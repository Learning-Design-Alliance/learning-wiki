# UML as a common language for discussing software design, analogized to Feynman diagrams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The APC uses the Unified Modelling Language in a lecture combined with paper-and-pencil exercises to establish "a common language for discussing software design issues which goes beyond writing down code or pseudo-code". UML concepts are discussed as analogous to developing formulae or Feynman diagrams to simplify understanding a physics problem, and UML diagrams are then consistently used to present more advanced topics throughout the school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Only the UML subset needed to discuss design issues on paper or blackboard is presented, given its detailed formal definition
#### Constraints
- The lecture presents only what is needed for pencil-and-paper design discussion, not the full formal definition

### Target Learners
- physicists without formal computer-science education

### Learning Goals
- structured discussion of software architecture and design
