# Night-laboratory station rotation with three simultaneous activities for large non-major classes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A laboratory structure in which a large non-major class is divided into sections of about 18 students meeting one night every other week, each section subdivided into three groups of six rotating through three stations: the 16-inch telescope, small telescopes on an adjacent rooftop, and a self-guided naked-eye activity. The article reports that "these groups rotated through three activities in each clear night," with a separate set of cloudy-weather labs provided for cloudy nights. Four clear-night activities (each with three parts) and five cloudy-weather activities were designed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A staffed observatory with at least three simultaneous activity stations
- Both a clear-night and a cloudy-night laboratory prepared for any given night
- Graduate and undergraduate teaching assistants to run the stations
#### Constraints
- Only six people fit in the 16-inch telescope dome at a time
- Equipment difficulties on the main telescope put that station behind schedule
- Teaching assistants had difficulty keeping track of who had done which lab

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate non-science majors in a 70-80 student introductory astronomy class

### Learning Goals
- Astronomical observing skills and reduction of common astronomical misconceptions such as moon phases
