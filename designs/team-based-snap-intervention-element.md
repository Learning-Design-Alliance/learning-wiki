# Team-based SNAP intervention for community nurses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A multi-component service-level intervention to build generalist community health nurses' capacity for brief lifestyle (SNAP) assessment and intervention. It comprised a one-day training program with "role-plays with simulated clients (actors), group discussions and activities", integration of standard assessment tools and prompts into routine initial visits, a local service referral directory, and support resources such as written action plans, guides, tape measures and pedometers. A seconded site nurse supported local implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One-day group training focused on the 5As model, motivational interviewing and goal setting
- Standard assessment tools embedded in routine initial client visits
- A local referral directory and implementation resources for nurses and clients
#### Constraints
- Referral component was limited by lack of local services, long waiting lists and transport difficulties
- Time pressures from an acute-care-focused model of care threaten sustainability

### Target Learners
- Generalist community health nurses in community health services

### Learning Goals
- Brief lifestyle risk factor assessment and intervention skills
- Motivational interviewing and goal setting
- Confidence and self-efficacy in preventive care
