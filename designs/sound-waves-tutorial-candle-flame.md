# Sound Tutorial: a one-hour group-learning activity using candle-flame video, graphed data, and two-candle phase-difference reasoning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The sound Tutorial is a one-hour University of Washington-style group activity that begins with the motion of a candle flame in front of a loudspeaker. Students "begin by predicting and then viewing a digitised video of the motion of a single candle due to a sound wave coming from a large loudspeaker", then graph previously collected VideoPoint data to find the oscillation period, sketch displacement-time graphs for two candles to develop phase difference, and extend to many candles in a Gedankenexperiment to find wavelength. It emphasizes the wave as a propagating event rather than a moving unidirectional force.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students had already received traditional lecture instruction on the basic physics of sound; the Tutorial encompassed one hour of instruction in addition to that
#### Constraints
- Due to time limitations during the Tutorial, students are not asked to take the video data themselves

### Target Learners
- university engineering students in the second semester of introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- describing sound wave propagation as a propagating event
- connecting observations, mathematical descriptions, and physical properties of waves
