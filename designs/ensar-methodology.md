# ENSAR: the Experience-Name-Speak-Apply-Repeat methodology for teaching abstract concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
ENSAR is a five-component pedagogical methodology in which learners first experience a concept through 3D materials and models, then learn its correct name, speak about it in structured peer-to-peer sentences, apply it creatively, and repeat it at home. The article describes it as integrating "elements from numerous recognised learning theories", rooted in experiential learning, constructivism, active learning, inquiry-based learning, and spaced repetition. The components must be applied in a fixed order, starting with experiencing and moving to naming.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The five components must be applied in the correct order: the teacher must always start by experiencing, move to naming, and continue with the rest in order
#### Constraints
- The article states the methodology is designed for complex concepts requiring abstract thinking and claims applicability across subjects, but its reported validation is limited to introductory quantum computing with the Qureka! Box

### Target Learners
- middle school, high school, and pre-graduate students
- professionals outside engineering or science fields
- general audiences

### Learning Goals
- foundational quantum computing concepts such as superposition, entanglement, and quantum states
- sustainable knowledge acquisition and long-term retention of abstract concepts
