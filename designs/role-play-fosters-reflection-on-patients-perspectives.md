# Combine ambulatory clerkship with peer role-play so students can reflect on outpatients' perspectives

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues for pairing clerkship encounters with role-play because reflection is otherwise rare: "it is often difficult for medical students to reflect on their patients because of limitations in time, space, and teaching staff." The role-play exercise "allowed students to attend to patients' narratives", and role-play fosters reflection of patients' perspectives in undergraduate medical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Real outpatient encounters during the clerkship whose narratives can be re-enacted
- A facilitated role-play and group discussion session after the encounter
#### Constraints
- The research does not suggest that peer role-play is necessary to learn patients' narratives

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in ambulatory clerkships

### Learning Goals
- reflecting on patients' perspectives and attending to patients' narratives
