# Hybrid productive failure with analogical comparison: schema abstraction through comparing contrasting models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a hybrid learning design that combines productive failure's two phases (Exploration and Generation, then Consolidation and Knowledge Assembly) with analogical comparison scaffolds. In the exploration phase students compare two highly contrasting computer models that share deep structure, which the authors argue activates structural prior knowledge so that teacher-led consolidation elicits "the cognitive process of schema abstraction" and assembly of new knowledge. The design was operationalized as the Two Models condition, where students "were instructed (i.e., scaffolded) to write out similarities and differences of the models" across climate and non-climate domains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two computer models implementing the same underlying complexity principles but from contrasting surface domains
- Explicit scaffolding prompting students to write out similarities and differences between the models
- A teacher-led consolidation phase presenting correct solutions and targeted concepts
#### Constraints
- The study included no non-PF and no non-computer-model control conditions, so the design's advantage over those alternatives is not tested here
- The schema abstraction account is the authors' proposed explanation for the far transfer finding, not a directly measured process

### Target Learners
- ninth-grade secondary science students

### Learning Goals
- weather and climate concepts
- complex systems principles such as feedback, emergence, and tipping points
- far across domain transfer of structural concepts
