# FLIGHT's five-component support model combining scholarships, mentoring, case management, workshops, and student detail reports

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
FLIGHT organizes its services around five components: "The intervention included five components aimed at increasing the likelihood of students applying for, being accepted to, and enrolling in college." These are a pre-paid college scholarship for students who remain in good academic standing, one-on-one mentoring from adult volunteers, intensive case-management services from school staff, academic and college preparation workshops for students and their parents, and a year-end Student Detail Report of grades, attendance, test scores, and intervention status provided to parents, teachers, and guidance counselors. The model extends the TSIC® base with more intensive academic, attendance, and behavioral monitoring and added workshops.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained adult volunteer mentors meeting students a minimum of 15 sessions per year for at least 30 minutes; an assigned student advocate case manager; dedicated school-day space for meetings and workshops.
#### Constraints
- Cost information for FLIGHT was not reported in the reviewed study or on the TSIC® website; the WWC could only identify preliminary cost components.

### Target Learners
- eligible low-income middle and high school students in grades 6 through the first three semesters of college

### Learning Goals
- college application and enrollment
- academic success and graduation on track
