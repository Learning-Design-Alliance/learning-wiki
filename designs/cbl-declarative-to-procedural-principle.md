# Use case-based e-learning to transform declarative knowledge into procedural knowledge

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's stated aim for its case-based e-learning is that "declarative knowledge" be transformed "into procedural knowledge, so that students are already accustomed to procedural algorithms for the patient encounter". This is achieved by interactive questions that link new content to previously learned textbook knowledge while training clinical reasoning, with a question-driven feedback system acting as checkpoints at each step of the case.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive questions with argument-based feedback at each case step
- links to reading material and lectures for basic knowledge
- OSCE or portfolio evaluation of procedural and translational knowledge
