# Multicomponent participatory ergonomics training program for geriatric nurses (2-day basic seminar plus two follow-up sessions)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A workplace training program combining knowledge transfer on body postures in nursing, body awareness training and physical exercises, ergonomic practical instructions at the bedside and bathroom, and reorganization/redesign of the work environment. The article states: "To reduce stressful trunk postures, we designed a 2-day basic seminar and two follow-up training sessions." The seminar used pictorial material to sensitize participants to postures, analysed work situations interactively, and had participants compare tasks at low versus raised bed height and standing versus sitting on a stool. Follow-up sessions offered advice and support during an early shift, supported by telephone interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All employees and managers of the participating wards had to attend the basic seminar
- Height-adjustable beds had to be available exclusively in the participating facilities during the study phase
#### Constraints
- Follow-up training sessions were offered only to test participants
- The program was aimed primarily at increasing awareness of body postures rather than at reducing low back pain

### Target Learners
- geriatric nurses and nursing assistants in nursing homes

### Learning Goals
- awareness of frequent bending and physical strains during care work
- practical ergonomic work methods at the bedside and in the bathroom
