# AI Peer experimental framework with a modified Force Concept Inventory pre-test

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A website-based experimental framework in which students complete a 30-question modified FCI pre-test, explain their reasoning for wrong answers, discuss each with an AI Peer prompted to correct the misconception, then take the original FCI as post-test. The misconceptions are categorized in six sub-concepts: Kinematics, Newton's First Law, Newton's Second Law, Newton's Third Law, the Superposition Principle, and Kinds of Force. The article states "The framework can be extended to other domains and other AI systems", and code is released at the project GitHub repository.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-quality pre- and post-test pair assessing the same concepts via different questions, and a personalized prompt supplying each student's wrong answers and explanations
#### Constraints
- Post-test was singular and immediately after treatment, so durability of gains is unknown; students had prior exposure to the post-test about 2 months earlier without answers

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory courses with concept inventories

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding measured by concept inventories
- misconception remediation
