# Shard elevator ride smartphone acceleration dataset with AIRIS-structured student guide

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete classroom task in which a smartphone recorded vertical acceleration during a downward elevator ride in the Shard (London) from floor 34 to the ground floor, held vertically against the elevator wall so only the y-component is meaningful. The dataset is released with the paper: "the dataset is provided with this paper, and the same AIRIS structure applies to any phenomenon that can be measured, modelled, and interpreted". Students predict, delegate visualization and integration to AI, then interpret and reflect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- No access to the Shard or any moving vehicle is required because the dataset is supplied
- Conceptual knowledge of acceleration, numerical integration, and kinematic relations must be established through prior instruction
#### Constraints
- The sensor data required correction for a systematic offset, determined as the mean acceleration during the constant-velocity interval from 15 s to 20 s (a_offset = 0.042 m/s2)

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- characterizing motion from real acceleration data
- relating a-t, v-t, and s-t representations
