# A five-stage, 90-minute didactic activity combining a low-cost magnet-in-tube experiment with explicit NOS reflection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A classroom activity in which students investigate which materials fall slowly through a copper coil versus a PVC cylinder, using 11 materials including rubber pieces, neodymium and ferrite magnets, coins, and an aluminum seal. It follows five timed stages, from activating prior knowledge via a mind map on "theory" to a synthesis discussion, guided by the question "How does a scientist know whether an experiment confirms a theory?". Students use cell phones as stopwatches and choose which materials to test and in what order.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A copper coil, PVC cylinder, and paired magnetic/non-magnetic materials of similar dimensions
- Students with devices usable as stopwatches; care with fragile neodymium magnets and phones
#### Constraints
- The details of electromagnetic induction require abstraction and mathematical proficiency beyond most high school students, so the phenomenon's effects are deliberately not visually evident

### Target Learners
- Third-year high school students (ages 17-18)
- Potentially first-year high school students and undergraduate courses

### Learning Goals
- Reflecting on the relationship between theory and experiment
- Argumentation grounded in empirical evidence
- Hands-on experience with observation, questioning, systematization, and peer discussion
