# Rapid cycle deliberate practice (RCDP): short practice–feedback–rewind cycles with immediate correction of performance gaps

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
RCDP is a simulation technique aligned with the principles of deliberate practice but characterized by shorter cycles of practice—feedback—"scenario rewind"—practice again. Unlike traditional manikin-based simulation, it immediately addresses performance gaps instead of waiting until the end of the scenario, interrupting scenarios with multiple pauses for brief, task-oriented feedback and repeated practice of the same scenario section until performance is adequate. In this article it was implemented as fellow-facilitated, low-fidelity small-group sessions during ICU rotations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators trained in the debriefing approach (here, a 60-minute interactive workshop plus paired supervision for first sessions) and a standardized debriefing script
#### Constraints
- The authors state the evidence base is inconsistent: not all studies comparing traditional and RCDP-based simulations demonstrated greater skill development, higher satisfaction, or confidence gains, and a meta-analysis highlighted inconsistencies in learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- non-critical care specialty residents
- medical students on ICU rotations

### Learning Goals
- key clinical tasks for initial care of acutely deteriorating inpatients, including airway management, hemodynamic instability, and hypoxia management
