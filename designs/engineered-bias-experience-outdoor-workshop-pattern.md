# Deliberately engineered failure experience embedded in an outdoor activity, bracketed by pre-briefing and debriefing discussions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The workshop pattern combines a one-hour large-group primer on decision-science concepts, a guided outdoor activity in which near-peer leaders were secretly instructed to "intentionally get lost along the way", and a large-group debrief revealing the deception and connecting the experience to clinical practice. The authors state the discussions "allowed for consistent dissemination of information across all groups" and "collective processing of the experience".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A primer session introducing the targeted concepts before the experience
- Near-peer guides briefed in advance to enact the decision-making error
- A post-experience debrief in which the design is revealed and processed collectively
#### Constraints
- Hiking was chosen because it is "relatively low cost, widely accessible, and relatively low consequence for participants"
- The study could not isolate the outdoor component's contribution from the discussions

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- recognizing decision-making biases such as authoritative bias, confirmation bias, overconfidence bias, bandwagon effect and halo effect
