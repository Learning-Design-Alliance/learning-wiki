# Peer-led simulation program with three calibrated clinical scenarios and debriefing-weighted session structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-hour-per-case simulation program for PGY-I residents, taught by volunteer PGY-II residents, built around three scenarios (acute myocardial infarction, multiple injuries, aspiration pneumonia) developed through alpha and beta testing with prior PGY-I input and expert review. Each scenario repeated "case presentation, team discussion, simulation training, and debriefing" (1, 3, 5, and 8 minutes) three times with role rotation, using tools such as SimMan 3G, a monitor, an emergency cart, and a cardiac defibrillator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteer PGY-II residents as teaching staff, who received debriefing education before participating
- Scenarios calibrated for difficulty, validity, accuracy, and effectiveness through alpha and beta testing
- Simulation equipment including high-fidelity patient simulators and real equipment
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a single facility over three one-week orientation periods before clinical practice

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate-year-I medical residents entering clinical practice

### Learning Goals
- Initial evaluation and management of the three scenario conditions
- Reporting to senior doctors
- Team-based acute care roles
