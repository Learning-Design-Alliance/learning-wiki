# Light-touch AI literacy warning about LLM persuasion and selective information

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A brief pre-conversation warning telling users that LLMs "can be 'prompted' to persuade or manipulate" and "may present information in a biased or selective way, emphasizing one side of an issue." The general version was shown identically worded in both studies; Study 2 added a specific version disclosing which direction the model was instructed to argue. It is a minimal, text-only intervention shown before a conversation with an LLM assigned to persuade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivered before the user converses with an LLM; the conversation must last at least three exchanges in the evaluated design
#### Constraints
- Tested only on political attitudes on contested issues with compelling arguments on both sides; the authors state effectiveness may differ in domains involving factual beliefs, e.g. conspiracy theories

### Target Learners
- Adult U.S. online panel participants (CloudResearch Connect)

### Learning Goals
- Recognizing that AI systems can be prompted to persuade and may present information selectively, creating resistance to AI persuasion attempts
