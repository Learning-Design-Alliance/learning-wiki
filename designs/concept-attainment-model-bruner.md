# Concept attainment model: teaching specific concepts by comparing and contrasting positive and negative examples

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The concept attainment model, historically linked with Jerome S. Bruner and his associates, is "intended to teach specific concepts by comparing and contrasting examples that contain the concept and that do not contain the concept". It is built from Bruner's work on categorizing, which "helps to reduce the complexity of environment and necessity for concept learning". The model was used as the experimental treatment in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must present examples that contain the concept and examples that do not, for comparison and contrast.
#### Constraints
- The article describes the model's application to specific physics concepts in class IX; effectiveness evidence comes from this single study.

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade students (14-15 years old)

### Learning Goals
- Concept learning and categorizing in physics
