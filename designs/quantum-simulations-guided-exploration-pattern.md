# Research-based interactive simulations with guided-exploration activities for abstract quantum concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The 17 simulations use interface-design principles from previous studies, with common interactive components such as play controls, radio buttons, tick boxes and sliders, a simple startup screen to encourage exploration, and a "Step-by-step exploration" view explaining key points with text and animated highlighting. They depict "simplified, idealized situations" to reduce complexity and cognitive load, and aim to help students connect physical, graphical and mathematical representations. Each simulation comes with an accompanying activity promoting guided exploration and sense-making, with scaffolding from simpler to more complex situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities include an initial question asking students to freely explore the simulation and note what they found; solutions are available to instructors
#### Constraints
- The simulations require no mathematical prerequisites apart from elementary understanding of probability and basic linear algebra with 2x2 matrices

### Target Learners
- introductory-level university quantum mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- linking multiple representations
- comparing and contrasting situations
- collecting and interpreting data
- testing local hidden variable theories
