# Street-lights-as-standard-candles field activity using a DSLR camera and free software

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A hands-on activity in which learners photograph a line of street lights along a straight road, measure their apparent brightness with image-processing software, and infer distances via the inverse-square law, treating the lights as standard candles. The article states it "is suitable as a high school class project as part of a physics course, or as an astronomy club activity" and uses a 660 m road in Heidelberg as its worked example. Reference distances come from the Google Maps measuring tool, akin to testing one distance indicator against another.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A long straight road with street lights of a perspective-robust type
- A DSLR camera saving raw-format images
- Free image-processing software for brightness measurements
#### Constraints
- Street lights whose light shines downward from a horizontal surface are ruled out because perspective changes apparent luminosity

### Target Learners
- high school physics students
- astronomy club members

### Learning Goals
- understanding the standard candle method of astronomical distance measurement
- understanding the inverse-square law
- introduction to astronomical photometry
