# Ensure smooth transitions from early intervention programs into school-based special education programs when children age out of EI

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that continuity of services in the presence of need benefits child development, and that its findings "support the need to ensure smooth transitions between EI programs and enrollment in school-based programs for children who demonstrate educational disabilities when they age out of EI." A prior sequence analysis within the same data system found children receiving both EI and special education had better outcomes than those receiving only one. Barriers documented in prior literature include lack of resources, changing diagnoses, and communication barriers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continuity of services in the presence of need; coordination between EI programs and school-based programs at the age-3 transition
#### Constraints
- Barriers documented include lack of resources, changing diagnoses, and communication barriers

### Target Learners
- Infants and toddlers with developmental delays or disabilities transitioning out of Part C early intervention at age 3

### Learning Goals
- Maintaining developmental and academic gains across the early intervention to preschool/school-age transition
