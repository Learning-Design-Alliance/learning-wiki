# The novella Infra Draconis as a teaching resource for ultra-cool substellar objects (brown dwarfs)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Georgi Gurevich's 1958 novella Infra Draconis follows a space flight to an ultra-cool object called infra, too small to sustain fusion; "The infras are analogues of the real-world brown dwarfs (BDs)." The article states the novella "is suitable to introduce the ultra-cool sub-stellar mass objects, to underline their high spatial density, and the possibility that they may harbor life" either on planets or within their own atmospheres, connecting to real objects Luh-16 and W0855.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators should connect the fiction to real-world analogues such as Luh-16 and WISE J085510.83−071442.5, noting their actual temperatures place them above the liquid water regime
#### Constraints
- The fictional nearest infra at seven light days away is far closer than any known brown dwarf; the article notes the real nearest binary BD Luh-16 is at about 2 pc

### Target Learners
- high school students
- amateur astronomers

### Learning Goals
- brown dwarfs and substellar objects
- habitability and subsurface oceans
