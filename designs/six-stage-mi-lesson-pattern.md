# Six-stage MI-based lesson pattern with dominant-intelligence grouping at the expression stage

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reusable lesson-level instructional pattern built on Gagne's instructional events, in which students first work in mixed groups and then express understanding in groups of similar dominant intelligence. The six stages are self-reflection, concept introduction, question formulation, SPS-based concept deepening, expression of understanding matched to dominant intelligence, and conclusion. The article states "The characteristic of this learning strategy lies in step (5), in which students were grouped based on the dominant type of intelligence."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A MI questionnaire administered before learning to identify each student's dominant intelligence type
- Activities at stages 1–4 that involve all MI aspects, with stage 5 activities matched to students' dominant intelligences
#### Constraints
- The article reports the strategy was applied in junior high school science lessons over 12 weeks and does not claim effectiveness outside that setting

### Target Learners
- junior high school students

### Learning Goals
- development of multiple intelligences
- science process skills
- mastery of science concepts
