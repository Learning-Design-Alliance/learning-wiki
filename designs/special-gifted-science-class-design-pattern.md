# Special-class design combining broadened science curriculum, university collaboration and intensive foreign-language teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Nis special physics class is a whole-programme design for gifted pupils built on five stated measures: "(i) focus on the natural science, on physics in particular", provision of laboratory equipment and PCs with virtual experiments and Internet access, close collaboration with the University (guest teaching by docents, assistants and mentors), collaboration with similar EU and Eastern European projects, and more intensive foreign language teaching, especially English. Its goals are high-quality education, a perspective for continuing education, and conveying initiative and enthusiasm. The class grew from 7 to about 16 pupils per generation, which the authors explain by attractive curricula, guest lecturers, laboratory work, excursions and awards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- University collaboration providing guest teaching and mentors
- basic laboratory equipment and PCs with virtual experiments and Internet access
- a selection system for pupils that is established and properly implemented
#### Constraints
- Physics syllabi differ so much across classes after the first year that mid-schooling comparisons are only sensible after the 4th year; students and teachers judged the curriculum content too large

### Target Learners
- gifted high-school pupils with special abilities in physics

### Learning Goals
- advanced physics and science knowledge
- problem-solving and higher-order thinking
- preparedness for further science education
