# Assessment without examinations: weight moved to in-class exercises, reflective writing, and a defended team project that resist unattributed automation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern rebuilds grading around tasks a language model cannot quietly ghost-write, on the bet that "an assessment a model can silently complete is a weak assessment." The Spring 2026 grade comprised in-class exercises done in the room in a fixed hour (20 percent), weekly projects that may be reflection, programming, or a mix (40 percent), and a team project (40 percent), with no midterm or final. Reflective writing carries a first-person account of the student's own process, and in-class exercises were graded complete-or-incomplete on submission with AI scores advisory only.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class exercises require a fixed scheduled hour in the room and produce an artifact on the spot
- Reflective writing must be required to be in the student's own voice
#### Constraints
- The author's own students, asked to design fair grading for such a course, mostly reinvented proctored or handwritten assessment, the strongest internal challenge to this structure
- The author makes no causal or learning-outcome claims for the structure

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate computer science students

### Learning Goals
- artificial intelligence fundamentals
- engineering judgment and supervision of AI tools
- reflective practice
