# A 7-stage design-based research process for developing and evaluating an initial teacher education health literacy intervention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article structures its whole research program around a 7-stage design-based research (DBR) approach, adapted from Reeves’ model and organized into 4 sequential studies: a systematic review, a qualitative focus group study, a pilot study, and a controlled trial. The stages move from needs analysis and initial design principles, through co-design and pilot testing, to revision, modification, controlled-trial evaluation, and final refinement of design principles. The authors state that "The proposed methodology underpinning the research program follows a 7-stage design-based research approach, with the 7 stages outlined in 4 sequential studies." DBR is chosen because it assumes real-world practice is contextual and interventions must be flexible to the needs of the group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stages must proceed sequentially: needs analysis (systematic review plus stakeholder interviews) must produce initial design principles before unit co-design, and pilot data must inform revised principles before the controlled trial
#### Constraints
- The design is specific to one initial teacher education unit in a Bachelor of Education (HPE) program at the University of Sydney, with the controlled trial extending to additional Australian universities

### Target Learners
- final-year health and physical education preservice teachers

### Learning Goals
- health literacy and electronic health literacy capabilities
- health literacy teaching practices in HPE
