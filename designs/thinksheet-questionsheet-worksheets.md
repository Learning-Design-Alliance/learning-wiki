# Thinksheet and questionsheet worksheets for integrated reading and writing instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The thinksheet is a three-step worksheet modified from the WIRC project's thinksheet (Englert & Raphael, 1989) to suit ELL students. Step 1 asks students to read a chunk of the story, identify one or two important ideas, and paraphrase the main ideas. Step 2 asks students to select the most important ideas and reflect on them. Step 3 asks students to write a summary and reflection. An additional worksheet called a questionsheet was created for this study, providing students with space to work cooperatively on their questions about vocabulary, grammar, and the story. The worksheets serve as a cognitive model while students read and write, helping each other achieve both "process goals" and "content goals."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers are provided with orientation materials about paraphrasing, questioning, and summarizing, answer keys to the student worksheets, and synonym lists.
#### Constraints
- The thinksheet and questionsheet were designed so as to allow teachers to "step-in" when explaining, correcting, and scaffolding and to "step-back" when giving students time to work individually or as part of a group.

### Target Learners
- Korean middle school EFL students at beginning, intermediate, and advanced proficiency levels

### Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension
- Summary writing
- Paraphrasing
- Reflection writing

### Claims

- [Students at different proficiency levels used the thinksheet differently, with advanced students revisiting prior steps and beginning students failing to complete or use them as intended](../claims/proficiency-differences-thinksheet-use.md) [~W]
