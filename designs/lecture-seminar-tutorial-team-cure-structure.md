# CURE course structure of shared lecture, cohort seminars, team tutorials, and research teams of four

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a large-enrollment CURE around four recurring components: a shared 50-minute lecture for all cohorts, a 50-minute student-led seminar per research cohort, a one-hour weekly tutorial per research team with its TA, and about six hours per week of team research and homework. The authors state that "a clear course structure is instrumental to help undergraduate students be productive with their original research", and that this added structure makes students more productive than individual apprentice-style experiences. Cohorts of 8-40 students each pursue one overarching research question, with teams exploring individual parts of it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A TA-to-student ratio of 1:4 and one instructor for every 1-4 cohorts
- Instructors need a strong research background in the CURE's research field and experience analyzing the data given to students
- A research question with multiple subprojects and relevant scientific data
#### Constraints
- The research topic works best when it is non-time-critical and exploratory, complementary to but detached from the experiment's main science goals

### Target Learners
- mostly first-year undergraduate students in physics, computer science, math and statistics, and engineering, with no prior coding or physics knowledge required

### Learning Goals
- conducting original scientific research in physics data analysis
- analyzing large physics data sets using Python
- scientific communication and teamwork
