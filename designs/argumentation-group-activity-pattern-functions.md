# Argumentation-based group activity pattern for teaching a mathematics unit

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The pattern organizes a mathematics unit as repeated small-group argumentation activities: the class is divided into small groups that work through researcher-prepared activities, defend claims in group discussion, and share defended claims with the whole class. As printed, "the experiment group was divided into seven groups totally as 6 groups of four each and 1 group of six each and they dealt with the activities prepared by argumentation method throughout the function subject and did 9 activities totally during the practice". Students who doubted a claim tried to confute it by raising a new claim, with constant teacher interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities prepared in advance for argumentation
- small groups of about four to six students
- classroom time for discussion and whole-class sharing of claims
#### Constraints
- The article states "This study is limited to 9th-grade students and "Functions" subject" and notes generalization would require larger samples

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade high school mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- science process skills
- attitude toward mathematics
- conceptual understanding of functions
- willingness for argumentation
