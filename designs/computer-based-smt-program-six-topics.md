# Computer-based stress management training program with six self-paced topics delivered via weekly e-mail prompts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article developed a self-paced computer-based stress management training (SMT) program consisting of behavioral, communication, and cognitive technique sections, each divided into two topics: "problem-solving and time management skills", "assertion and delegation skills", and "cognitive restructuring and causal attribution skills". Learning followed a two-phased approach of skill acquisition and practice, grounded in social learning theory's outcome and efficacy expectancies. Participants received weekly e-mails (encouragement, congratulations, application enhancement) through a workplace coordinator, and completed the program during working hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual IDs and passwords for program and questionnaire access
- A workplace coordinator to relay weekly e-mails between participants and the program author
- Delivery during working hours at an intended pace of one lesson per week
#### Constraints
- Communication with the program author occurred entirely by e-mail with no face-to-face interaction
- Completion required roughly 30 minutes per topic; finishing in 1–2 days was judged insufficient time to apply skills

### Target Learners
- full-time employees in research and development divisions of a manufacturing company

### Learning Goals
- stress management knowledge
- coping skills including problem-solving, assertion, delegation, cognitive restructuring, and seeking social support
