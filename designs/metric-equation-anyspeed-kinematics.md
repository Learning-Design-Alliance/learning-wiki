# Anyspeed kinematics via the metric equation with traveler-time as the invariant

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article introduces the metric equation as "a space-time extension of Pythagoras’ Theorem, written so that traveler-time is the invariant", letting students learn most of what they need about traveler time at high speed before tackling multi-frame relativity. From it follow three corollary variables (gamma, proper velocity, and proper or 'felt' acceleration) and an equation set, patterned after the classical one, exact for unidirectional motion at any speed. The approach sticks to one map-frame with its tangible Cartesian map and synchronized clocks, in the spirit of J. Bell.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- More sophisticated use of the metric equation is needed before students can predict measurements with traveler yardsticks at high speed
#### Constraints
- The anyspeed equation set is exact only for unidirectional motion; the non-defining equalities complicate if motion is polydirectional
- Comparing clock rates requires that simultaneity have specified meaning, which choice of a map-frame provides

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- students with only one course in physics

### Learning Goals
- understanding motion at any speed
- preparation for special relativity and space-time geometry
