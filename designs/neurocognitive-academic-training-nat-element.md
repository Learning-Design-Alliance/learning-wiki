# Neurocognitive-Academic Training (NAT): a low-frequency multicomponent intervention integrating NCT, teacher positive feedback, and scaffolded Chinese handwriting practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
NAT is a school-deliverable multicomponent intervention for children with ADHD that "integrates neurocognitive training (NCT), teacher positive feedback, and scaffolded academic tasks". Each 45-minute session combines a 5-minute warm-up, 25-30 minutes of NCT via the Focus Pocus platform with a dry-electrode EEG headset, a break, and a 3-6 minute handwriting module, delivered twice weekly over 10 weeks across three progressive phases. The 20-session protocol achieved symptom, EF, on-task, and handwriting gains despite lower frequency than conventional NCT programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained teachers delivering standardized positive feedback during NCT and around handwriting tasks
- Computerized NCT platform with adaptive difficulty (Focus Pocus) and EEG-monitoring headset
- Scaffolded handwriting tasks using grade-appropriate, curriculum-aligned characters with mastery criteria for phase progression
#### Constraints
- The integrated design "does not allow us to disentangle the specific contribution of each component" to outcomes
- Generalizability to older children facing complex compositional tasks may be constrained since about one-third of participants were preschoolers

### Target Learners
- Children aged 5-9 with clinically diagnosed ADHD in kindergarten Year 3 to primary school Year 2

### Learning Goals
- Inhibitory control and working memory
- ADHD symptom reduction
- On-task behavior during academic tasks
- Chinese character handwriting construction, accuracy, and directionality
