# PETSP-ML: a four-stage classroom process model for explanatory thinking in machine learning, adapted from CRISP-DM

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
PETSP-ML (Promotion of Explanatory Thinking Standard Process for Machine Learning) is a process model the authors developed "to promote explanatory thinking in a structured manner", adapted from the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM). It reorganizes CRISP-DM's six phases into four classroom-aligned stages: Task Understanding, Data Phase (merging data understanding and preparation), Model Understanding, and Model Training and Evaluation, with an iterative structure encouraging students to revisit earlier stages. Its deliberate departure from professional practice is making model understanding a distinct, prerequisite stage before model training and shifting focus away from performance metrics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Model Understanding is treated as a distinct, prerequisite stage before model training, reflecting the pedagogical priority of building conceptual foundations before procedural skills
#### Constraints
- CRISP-DM's original formulation is not directly suited to educational settings, where objectives, constraints, and prior knowledge differ substantially from industrial projects

### Target Learners
- K-12 students learning machine learning

### Learning Goals
- explanatory thinking about how and why ML systems behave as they do
- understanding of training data, algorithmic mechanisms, evaluation metrics, and sources of error or bias
