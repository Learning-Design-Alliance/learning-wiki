# Nonverbal communication game quantifying stereotype- and interaction-driven adjustments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-player digital game board with a 3 × 3 grid on which participants guide a partner (a role-blind confederate) to a hidden acorn by moving a bird avatar. Only the participant knows the location and only the partner can collect the object, driving dyads to "achieve mutual understanding by aligning on a limited but idiosyncratic number of strategies." Dyads solved trials well above chance (89.8 ± 11.7% against a chance level of 7.7%), indicating participants generated communicative behaviors that, despite their novelty, could be mutually understood. The game measures stereotype-driven adjustment (time spent in the object square) and interaction-driven convergence across eight blocks with alternating presumed partners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A role-blind confederate performing both presumed-partner roles so that adjustments reflect stereotype beliefs rather than partner behavior
#### Constraints
- Minimizes linguistic conventions, so findings may not generalize to verbal communication with its prosodic and lexical cues

### Target Learners
- adolescent participants in dyadic social interaction studies

### Learning Goals
- measuring real-time communicative adjustment to partner beliefs and interaction dynamics
