# Flipped-classroom mechanism: shifting initial content acquisition to pre-class activities frees class time for application-oriented practice with instructor facilitation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes that flipped classrooms work by reordering instructional flow: learners "review core concepts before class via videos or readings, whereas in-class time is used more often for problem solving, case discussion, and skills practice". Instructors act more as facilitators, giving real-time feedback and scaffolding based on learners' preparation and in-class performance. The review presents this as a mechanistic interpretation consistent with, but not directly tested by, the pooled effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must access preparatory materials (e.g., videos or readings) before class
- Class time must be used for discussion, practice, and application, with instructor facilitation and feedback
#### Constraints
- The mechanism is the authors' interpretation; the meta-analysis did not directly test mediating processes, and effects are context dependent given very high heterogeneity

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students
- Residents and postgraduate medical learners

### Learning Goals
- Theoretical knowledge
- Procedural/technical skills
- Self-directed learning ability
- Case analysis/problem-solving ability
- Learner satisfaction
