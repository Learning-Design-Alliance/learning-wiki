# Pilot introductory physics for the life sciences course in the NEXUS project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The setting is the pilot year of an introductory physics course for undergraduate biology majors in the NEXUS project, with weekly lectures, labs, and discussion built around group problem-solving tasks connecting chemistry, biology, and physics. "This course is unusual in that biology and chemistry are required as prerequisites, and students are therefore expected and encouraged to draw on their knowledge from these other disciplines." The course included an extensive chemical bond energy thread using potential-energy graphs and CLUE curriculum simulations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Biology and chemistry prerequisites, so that students enter with disciplinary knowledge they can be asked to draw on.
#### Constraints
- Pilot-year enrollment was about 20 students per semester, and the authors note case-study limits on generalizability.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology and life science majors

### Learning Goals
- building cross-disciplinary coherence among physics, biology, and chemistry around energy and thermodynamics
