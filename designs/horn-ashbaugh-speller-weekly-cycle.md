# Horn-Ashbaugh Speller weekly curriculum: distributed practice and retrieval practice with pretesting, self-testing, and spaced review

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Speller's curriculum assigned a new word list each Monday with a pretest, self-testing study routines on Tuesday and Thursday (look, visualize, cover, write from memory, check, repeated three times), diagnostic tests on Wednesday and Friday that also assessed the list from one month prior, and a spelling notebook tracking errors and progress. The review characterizes these as implementations of "distributed practice" and "practice testing" (retrieval practice), two techniques "now regarded as evidence-based and highly robust". The pattern also included reviewing difficult words at the start of each school year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires daily instructional time devoted to spelling, weekly word lists, and teacher-administered pretests and diagnostic tests with corrective feedback
#### Constraints
- The review judges the Speller overall obsolete: its visualization-heavy drill routines rested on an incomplete view of spelling's cognitive mechanisms, and it lacked grade-level benchmarks and systematic error diagnosis

### Target Learners
- 1st–8th grade students

### Learning Goals
- correct spelling of assigned word lists
- development of a spelling conscience and motivation to spell correctly
