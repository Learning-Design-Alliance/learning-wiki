# Self-diagnosis and correction of mistakes as a scaffolded learning activity requiring extrinsic motivation for non-intrinsically motivated students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames self-diagnosis and correction of mistakes as a form of "explicit scaffolding support and guidance" that helps students learn from their mistakes. It posits that students who are not intrinsically motivated may need extrinsic motivation, such as explicit reward, to engage in this reflective practice. The framework draws on the observation that many advanced students, like introductory students, do not automatically exploit their mistakes as a learning opportunity without such scaffolding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be asked to self-diagnose and submit corrected solutions before receiving the official solutions
#### Constraints
- The intervention was tested over two midterm exams in one course, and the authors caution it does not necessarily follow that students have become adept at self-monitoring skills from just two such interventions

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students
- advanced STEM students
