# Use a simple induction demonstration to teach both Earth-field measurement and how electric power is generated from rotational motion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's design principle is that a single unsophisticated demonstration—rotating a coil in the Earth's magnetic field—serves a dual learning goal: measuring the field and illustrating electricity generation. The authors state "This will also enable the students to learn about how electric power is gene rated from the rotational motion", while the same setup measures the Earth's field. The idea is explicitly not original but is chosen for its accessibility, using no sophisticated devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- basic laboratory equipment only: a wooden coil, belt-drive rotation system, voltmeter, rheostat and compass
#### Constraints
- the coil and handmade belt system may require some craftsmanship, which once made, can be used for years

### Target Learners
- students in an elementary physics laboratory

### Learning Goals
- Faraday's law of induction
- generation of electric power from rotational motion
- measurement of the Earth's magnetic field
