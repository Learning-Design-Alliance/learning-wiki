# Treat internal energy as including chemical energy, not only thermal energy, in IPLS thermodynamics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the NEXUS/Physics course, the First Law of Thermodynamics is taught with internal energy comprising both thermal and chemical energy, so internal energy changes may appear as chemical reactions rather than only temperature changes. This aligns the physics treatment with how biology courses teach the First Law, where chemical reactions are central. A consequence the authors accept is that total internal energy is undefined, but "only changes in internal energy have physical significance."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have a model of chemical energy in terms of potential and kinetic energy before this treatment
#### Constraints
- This is a departure from traditional introductory courses that may include an explicit expression for internal energy; total internal energy is undefined because the zero of potential energy can be placed anywhere

### Target Learners
- life sciences majors in introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- connect the First Law of Thermodynamics to chemical reactions
