# Tri-annual teacher survey operationalizing dosage, adherence, instructional quality, and support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A teacher self-report survey administered "three times annually during the 2024–2027 study period" to all participating grades 6–8 teachers, designed to take no longer than 30 minutes per administration. The team chose self-reporting as "the least burdensome and most reliable strategy" given the logistical and fiscal challenges of direct observation in a national study. Items are drawn where possible from nationally validated surveys and operationalize dosage (units taught, percentage of class time using the core curriculum), adherence (skipping, supplementing, modifying), instructional quality (frequency of teacher and student instructional practices), and support (professional learning hours, usefulness ratings, and planning collaboration).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teacher self-report items from nationally validated surveys and aggregation of responses to create individual implementation measures comparable across districts using different programs
#### Constraints
- The report acknowledges the challenges related to the reliability of self-report data and notes locally gathered fidelity data cannot be relied on because districts use different programs and measures

### Target Learners
- grades 6–8 mathematics teachers in participating districts

### Learning Goals
- measuring curriculum dosage, adherence, instructional quality, and professional learning support for impact models
