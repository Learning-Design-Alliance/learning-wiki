# Integrated longitudinal PBL curriculum design for pharmacology and CPT education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a curriculum redesign in which pharmacology is integrated with pathophysiology, microbiology and neurosciences across the second and third years, with a new fifth-year CPT course, all "based on the principles of PBL" and focusing "on solving written patient cases in working groups under the supervision of clinical pharmacologists". It replaced a discipline-based lecture programme and used 10% fewer teaching hours (129 vs 144 h), yet was associated with better prescribing competencies. The design integrates content and context across the whole curriculum rather than concentrating CPT in one or two courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of PBL modules throughout the medical curriculum starting as early as possible; content and context of teaching matter more than teaching hours
#### Constraints
- The integration of training sessions throughout the medical curriculum can be challenging for medical schools with few CPT teachers; evidence comes from one medical school with a historic control, limiting generalizability

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- rational prescribing knowledge, skills and attitudes (effective, safe and low-cost prescribing)
