# Three-part simulation-based breaking bad news curriculum for OB/GYN residents

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 3-hour curriculum combining a 15-minute palliative medicine didactic on the SPIKES protocol, four standardized patient encounters (previable PPROM, surgical complication, cancer diagnosis, intrauterine fetal demise) with faculty debriefing using the PEARLS tool, and a 1-hour patient panel discussion. The design was "guided by Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory" and incorporated "principles of deliberate practice" with immediate faculty feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- OSCE rooms with audiovisual equipment and 2-way mirrors
- Four trained standardized patients
- Four faculty facilitators with expertise in delivering bad news
- Community panelists recruited through advocacy organizations
#### Constraints
- Designed for OB/GYN residents within a 2-year simulation curriculum during protected education time

### Target Learners
- OB/GYN residents from PGY 1 to PGY 4

### Learning Goals
- Deliver bad news using the SPIKES protocol
- Respond effectively to patient emotions with empathetic statements, reflective listening, and supportive silence
