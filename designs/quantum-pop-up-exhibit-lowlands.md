# Quantum: The pop-up exhibit — a 13-panel bilingual travelling exhibit on quantum science and technologies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A bilingual (English and Dutch) pop-up exhibit designed in 2016 by the Institute for Quantum Computing, Waterloo, and borrowed for the festival. It 'comprises 13 panels which use text, pictures, two videos and four interactives' covering quantum computers, sensors and networks, superposition and wave-particle duality. Visitors entered freely with no mandatory route, typically spending around 20 minutes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Panels, videos and interactives must be transportable and set up at an informal venue where visitors can enter and engage voluntarily
#### Constraints
- No mandatory walking route; visitors chose if and when to engage
- The authors note the exhibit's breadth may have introduced too much novelty at once, potentially overwhelming visitors

### Target Learners
- Adult festival visitors aged 16 and older, with no prior quantum knowledge required

### Learning Goals
- Understanding of core quantum science concepts (superposition, wave-particle duality) and the impact, importance and opportunities of quantum technologies
