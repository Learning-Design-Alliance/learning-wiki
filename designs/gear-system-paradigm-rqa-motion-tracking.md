# Gear-system problem-solving paradigm with motion tracking and recurrence quantification analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An experimental paradigm in which participants predict the turning direction of a target gear given a driving gear, using static images of gear systems varying in size, number of pathways, and presence of extraneous gears. Participants' finger motion is tracked (Optotrak at 100 Hz), angular-velocity time series are extracted, and recurrence quantification analysis computes "percent-recurrence, percent-determinism, mean line, max line, and entropy" used as predictors of strategy discovery. Strategy use (force tracing vs. alternation) is coded from video and think-aloud protocols.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Motion tracking of the dominant hand during problem solving
- Think-aloud coding of strategies by observers (inter-observer agreement 90% in Study 1)
#### Constraints
- Displays are completely static except the target gear after responding; participants never see the interlocking gears move

### Target Learners
- college students

### Learning Goals
- predicting gear-system behavior; discovering the alternating turning-direction relation
