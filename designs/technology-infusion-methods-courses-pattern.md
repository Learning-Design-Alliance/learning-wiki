# Technology infusion into methods courses as an alternative to a stand-alone technology course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When a stand-alone educational technology course was eliminated, the program replaced it by purposely infusing "technology-integration assignments, objectives, and lessons into methods courses," aligned to the ISTE-T standards. By fall 2015, 16 methods courses across five programs had been infused. The approach places responsibility for teaching technology integration on content-methods instructors rather than educational-technology experts, supported by specialist PD.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor professional development and ongoing specialist support
- Standard syllabi with technology-infused objectives and assignments
#### Constraints
- Instructors hired for content-methods expertise had varying, though generally quite limited, technology capabilities, and some did not implement the agreed-upon technology outcomes as designed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate teacher candidates in early childhood, elementary, secondary, special, and English language learner education programs

### Learning Goals
- integrating technology into future classroom instruction
