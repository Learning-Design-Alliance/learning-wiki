# PTA Feedback Study: a double-blinded randomized controlled trial of patient feedback embedded in a patient teaching associate programme

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Patient Teaching Associate (PTA) Feedback Study is "a randomized, controlled, assessor- and patient-blinded, single-centre exploratory trial with two parallel groups after six student consultation sessions". Sixty-six third-year medical students in their first clinical year from two universities see the same community patients living with chronic illness in groups of three, in an after-hours general practitioner clinic adapted as a daytime teaching clinic. Students are randomized in equal proportions to written patient satisfaction feedback plus usual multisource feedback, or usual multisource feedback alone, with outcomes assessed at baseline and after one semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Block randomization using computer-generated random numbers with allocation numbers kept in sealed containers and concealed in sealed opaque envelopes
- Written consent obtained from all participants
- Baseline assessment battery (demography, baseline RICS and MISS-21 after the first consultation) completed at enrolment before randomization
#### Constraints
- Because of the nature of the feedback, student participants will not be blinded to the group membership
- There will be no control group for patient assessors
- The trial is recruiting participants by invitation only

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students in their first clinical year at Monash University and Deakin University

### Learning Goals
- Clinical consultation skills
- Patient-centred approach to consultation
- Professionalism in ambulatory care
