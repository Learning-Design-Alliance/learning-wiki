# AI-integrated analogy-generation survey activity on the Morse potential curve

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 14-question open-ended online extra-credit activity (worth 1% of the course grade) administered via Qualtrics in Week 11 of a first-semester calculus-based physics course with Spring 2024 enrollment N = 1446. Students explained the Morse potential curve themselves, to second graders, and in preferred contexts, then repeated the tasks with generative AI, compared paired responses side by side, and reflected on perceived ease and usefulness. AI tasks were designed to align with three UNESCO-derived criteria for responsible AI use: non-critical accuracy, verifiability via course materials, and student responsibility through critiquing AI content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a generative AI platform and course materials students can use to verify AI outputs; the Morse potential curve must already have been introduced in class
#### Constraints
- Data analysis retained 839 of 1243 respondents after removing 404 incomplete responses; 25 AI responses were excluded as contextually irrelevant due to improper prompting

### Target Learners
- first-semester calculus-based physics students, primarily future engineers

### Learning Goals
- explaining the Morse potential curve through spontaneous and self-generated analogies and critiquing AI-generated explanations
