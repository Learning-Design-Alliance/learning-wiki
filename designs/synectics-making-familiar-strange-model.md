# Synectics instructional model — 'making the familiar strange' version for vocabulary teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Synectics instructional model, adapted for education by William Gordon (1961), is a problem-solving and creative-thinking model based on constructivist philosophy in which learners form personal connections between new and existing knowledge. The article applies the 'making the familiar strange' version, whose steps are describing the topic, creating direct analogies, describing personal analogies, identifying compressed conflicts, creating a new direct analogy, reexamining the original topic, and evaluation. It uses metaphors, analogies and contrasts to stimulate creative thinking and encourage active, collaborative participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher guides students through the seven steps, ensuring participation of every student and voting to select analogies and compressed conflicts
#### Constraints
- The article examines the model only for foreign language vocabulary teaching in English preparatory classes over 16 class hours

### Target Learners
- pre-intermediate university English preparatory-class students

### Learning Goals
- foreign language vocabulary learning and retention
- creative thinking through analogies and metaphors
