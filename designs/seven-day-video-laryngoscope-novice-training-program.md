# Seven-day structured simulation training program preparing novices for video-laryngoscope intubation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A training program in which volunteers with no prior endotracheal intubation experience received theoretical instruction on airway anatomy, indications, contraindications, and video-laryngoscopy principles, then watched demonstrations by anesthesiologists with "at least 10 years of clinical practice in airway management" and engaged in hands-on manikin practice. It ran "over a period of 7 days, with each training session lasting approximately 4 hours per day," chosen to let participants absorb theory and become familiar with equipment before the timed comparison.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants with no prior endotracheal intubation experience
- Theoretical instruction followed by expert demonstration and hands-on practice
- A simulated airway manikin with adjustable difficulty settings
#### Constraints
- The study simulated only normal airways and did not simulate difficult airway scenarios
- Simulated patients cannot replicate real physiological conditions such as secretions, bleeding, or mist

### Target Learners
- Medical students and resident physicians with no prior intubation experience

### Learning Goals
- Performing endotracheal intubation with video laryngoscopes
