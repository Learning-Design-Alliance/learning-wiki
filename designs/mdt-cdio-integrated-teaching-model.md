# MDT-CDIO integrated teaching model: four-phase closed-loop clinical learning cycle with multidisciplinary role assignment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article integrates the multidisciplinary team (MDT) model with the Conceive–Design–Implement–Operate (CDIO) framework, which "embodies the translation and adaptation of competency-based engineering education principles into the medical education context." Teaching proceeds through four phases: Conception (open-ended cases requiring differential diagnosis), Design (five-student groups with assigned clinical roles conducting a 30-minute simulated MDT consultation), Implementation (physician critique of student plans, bedside care, surgical participation, and an "MDT Full Process Log"), and Operation (patient follow-up and weekly debriefing sessions). The MDT component adds collaborative decision-making across orthopedics, radiology, rehabilitation, anesthesiology, and nursing to address the single-discipline limitations of CDIO alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multidisciplinary teaching team of attending physicians and senior staff from orthopedics, radiology, rehabilitation, anesthesiology, nursing, and other relevant disciplines
- A joint workshop before commencement to standardize instructional objectives and feedback mechanisms for each CDIO phase
- A standardized clinical teaching database of open-ended cases
#### Constraints
- 18% of students reported difficulties integrating diverse expert perspectives during the initial training phase
- 12% of students suggested increasing hands-on clinical practice time to better apply theoretical knowledge

### Target Learners
- orthopedic clinical internship students
- medical students in clinical internship phase

### Learning Goals
- orthopedic theoretical knowledge
- clinical operational skills
- interdisciplinary collaboration
- clinical reasoning
- communication and consensus-building
