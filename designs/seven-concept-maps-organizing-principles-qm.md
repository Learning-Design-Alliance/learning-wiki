# Seven concept maps modeling the organizing principles of basic quantum knowledge

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The framework's answer to which concepts and structures act as organizing principles in basic QM is "seven concept maps, intended as models of the organizing principles of quantum knowledge needed to account for the results of measurement and time evolution both at a qualitative and quantitative level". Each map displays a general pathway of solution from the required task (predicting a measurement outcome or time evolution) through the entities, procedures and prediction tools to the outcome. The top-level nodes are the intertwined concepts of measurement, time evolution, state, observable, eigenstate and superposition, relations between observables, and their formal representations, with prediction tools of superposition analysis and commutation-relation analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Using the maps requires knowledge of a formal representation of the eigenstate concerned, and the context emerges when energy, change of basis or degeneracy examination is involved
#### Constraints
- What happens to systems during the measurement process is not included in the maps, so they can be adapted to different approaches and interpretive stances
- Convenient shortcut methods such as the uncertainty relation and the expectation-value formula are not included among the organizing principles because the information they give is partial and applicable only in limited cases

### Target Learners
- upper-undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- predicting results of measurement and time evolution at qualitative and quantitative levels
- overcoming knowledge fragmentation in quantum mechanics
