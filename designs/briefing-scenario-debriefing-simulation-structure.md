# Three-phase simulation session structure: briefing, scenario, and reflective debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article structures simulation-based education in three sequential phases: a briefing that presents the scenario, learning objectives, roles, and a psychologically safe orientation; a scenario phase of approximately 15 minutes; and a debriefing phase of 35–40 minutes. The debriefing, described as "the cornerstone of the simulation process," used Gibbs' reflective learning cycle's six stages to guide discussion, foster reflective thinking, and give performance feedback. In this study the pattern was applied to a postpartum hemorrhage case with a Mama Natalia mannequin strapped to a trained standardized patient.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator to lead the debriefing and guide reflective discussion
- Learning objectives and equipment presented during briefing
- Scenario designed to achieve the stated learning objectives
#### Constraints
- The scenario phase "should not last more than 15 min" per the article
- Poorly designed simulation "can promote negative learning" per the article

### Target Learners
- Third-year ordinary diploma student nurse-midwives

### Learning Goals
- Preventing and managing postpartum hemorrhage, including active management of the third stage of labor
