# The Question Cycle model for organizing CCS-based class sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The question cycle is the bulletin's model for organizing in-class CCS teaching: the instructor poses a question, students discuss it in small groups and enter answers, the class histogram is displayed without revealing the correct answer, and the instructor moderates discussion of the reasoning offered. The bulletin calls it 'a powerful model for organizing CCS-based teaching during in-class meetings'; Figure 1 diagrams it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be answerable through a few minutes of small-group discussion, with answers aggregable into an instant class-wide histogram
- The instructor must withhold which answer is correct while volunteers explain the reasoning behind each answer
#### Constraints
- If formative assessment is taken seriously, the instructor must be prepared to adjust or abandon any existing lesson plan and extemporize
- The model requires class-management skill in soliciting and moderating discussion and directing students' attention

### Target Learners
- students in large university lecture courses (50 or more students)

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding and reasoning rather than recall of answers

- [Ccs Answer Commitment Boosts Attention](../claims/ccs-answer-commitment-boosts-attention.md) [+M]
