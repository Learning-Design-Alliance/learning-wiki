# Home thermal-inversion simulator for teaching damped harmonic motion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A low-cost home experiment in which a transparent container with temperature-controlled bottom and top ends simulates an isobaric troposphere; smoke introduced through an inlet oscillates under temperature inversion and is modeled as a damped harmonic oscillator. The article states the setup 'builds upon the idea that a transparent container whose ends are covered by a good thermal conductor may serve as a simulation of an isobaric troposphere'. Students collect data, fit damping frequency and smoke density, and compare with a numerical simulation. Equipment includes temperature probes, a smoke source, a heat source, and video tracking software.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A transparent container with thermally conductive ends, temperature sensors, a smoke inlet, a heat source, and video capture to track smoke dynamics
#### Constraints
- The container is not hermetically sealed so pressure is assumed constant and equal to atmospheric; the model assumes constant air viscosity and negligible gas density change over the experiment timescale

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate STEM students in introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- understanding damped harmonic motion
- applying physics to environmental phenomena such as temperature inversion and air pollution
