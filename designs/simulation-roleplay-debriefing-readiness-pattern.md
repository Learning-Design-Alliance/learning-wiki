# Learner-centered readiness pattern combining simulation practice, role-play, scenario analysis, peer feedback, and reflective debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The module's instructional design cycles students through active, learner-centered strategies around each station theme: clinical scenario analysis of realistic skills-based cases, structured small-group discussions with checklist-based peer observation, simulation-based skill practice on models and with standardized patients, and instructor-led reflective debriefing. Students alternated between "practitioner" and "observer/evaluator" roles, which the authors state "encouraged self-assessment, critical thinking, and peer feedback skills". Debriefing is described as helping learners close the gap between knowledge and performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios developed or standardized from previously validated pediatric OSCE stations
- Instructor-led debriefing at each simulation station
- Peer observation using structured checklists
#### Constraints
- Some weeks covered two themes due to content density and time constraints

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate dental students preparing for clinical rotations

### Learning Goals
- clinical decision-making
- communication skills
- teamwork
- technical procedural skills
- self-assessment and peer feedback
