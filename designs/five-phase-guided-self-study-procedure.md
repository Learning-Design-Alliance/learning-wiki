# Five-phase guided self-study procedure for higher education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operationalizes guided self-study (G-SS) as a structured procedure in which "students are supported by lecturers or tutors during G-SS", in contrast to free self-study where students are self-reliant. Drawing on Landwehr and Mueller, it defines five phases: a preparatory phase in which students receive a learning assignment with clear-cut learning objectives; realization stages of tutor coaching and checking; presentation of learning outcomes in a plenum session; reflection on the learning process by students and tutor; and peer feedback on presentations and learning processes. The study instantiated this as an eight-day cycle per clinical case, with six cases aligned to module contents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor or lecturer guides preparation, presentation, reflection and feedback phases
- Clinical cases aligned with curriculum contents and clear-cut learning objectives
- Scheduled sessions (90 min at day 8) for presentation, reflection and feedback
#### Constraints
- The article reports no standardized procedure for G-SS existed at BFH before this study, and empirical evidence for the underlying theoretical model was still lacking

### Target Learners
- first-semester undergraduate physiotherapy students

### Learning Goals
- practical physiotherapy skills such as examination protocols, massage techniques and gait analysis
- independent acquisition of knowledge and skills
