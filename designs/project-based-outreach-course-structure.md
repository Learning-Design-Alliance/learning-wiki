# Three-phase project-based outreach course structure: short hands-on challenges, a self-chosen group project, and a public show

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A semester-long course pattern in which a driving problem — how to engage the public with the fundamentals of physics — organises thirteen three-hour sessions in three phases: "two starting sessions with very small projects, nine sessions devoted to a large project, and two sessions to prepare a public show". Accompanying presentations and exercises on outreach facets run throughout, and the course ends with a one-hour show to an audience of about 200 to 300. The article offers the description so the course "can be easily duplicated in other universities".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two teachers experienced in outreach to mentor students
- At least two classrooms, since students need room and silence for videos, experiments and rehearsal
- A set of multimedia tools corresponding to about $3000 in initial budget, plus a modest flexible budget for project-specific equipment
#### Constraints
- Maximum of about twenty students per year for practical reasons
- Grades are attributed by teachers only, which the authors note is perhaps not optimal since a good project-based evaluation may also involve student self-evaluation and the audience

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- communication and didactical skills
- teamwork and project management
- opening students to new forms of outreach and their role as scientists in society
