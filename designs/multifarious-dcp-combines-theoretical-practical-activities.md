# Disability awareness programs in recreational settings should combine theoretical and practical activities including simulation, information, and interactive discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's DCP combined ten theoretical-practical activities including disability simulation sports, Paralympic Games information via lectures and videos, drawing activities, and end-of-day interactive discussions. The authors state the program's multifarious nature and the camp's enjoyable environment were key to its success. They recommend such programs be implemented in recreational and educational settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs need to be planned rather than happening spontaneously; content and duration appear to play a key role
#### Constraints
- Some activities like shooting a basketball from a wheelchair proved too difficult for children aged 11 years old; age of participants and choice of activities should be considered

### Target Learners
- children without disabilities aged approximately 11 years in camp settings

### Learning Goals
- positive attitudes toward inclusion of peers with disabilities
