# Tear-up paper blank as a low-tech random sampling algorithm for Monte Carlo teaching experiments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A paper blank with two areas painted in radically different colors is torn into small pieces; each piece acts as one random point. The author writes that the experimenter "paints two areas with radically different colors (the simplest examples are white and black), and then randomly breaks (tears) it up into small pieces." Ambiguous pieces can be assigned to either group or broken in half, since "it does not matter for a large number of pieces after all." Puzzle assembly is optional illustration only.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Painting two areas in contrasting colors before tearing
- counting the resulting pieces rather than assembling them
#### Constraints
- The puzzle-assembly illustration is described as time-consuming but absolutely optional

### Target Learners
- school and university physics students

### Learning Goals
- estimating Pi
- verifying geometric area formulas
- understanding random sampling
