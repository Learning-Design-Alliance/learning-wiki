# Progressive ISLE-iOLab lab module structure moving from instrument introduction to full inquiry cycles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The modules, "of 4 to 10 hours", begin with an introduction to the iOLab instrument and basic measurement tasks, then progress to ISLE-based activities combining the device with basic lab materials. Each module follows the observational-testing-application experiment cycle, includes "review questions to be solved in groups to consolidate the concepts and skills acquired", and introduces growth mindset through deliberate practice with immediate feedback and explicit goals. Modules covered mechanics, electric field and potential, and DC circuits in 11th- and 12th-grade classrooms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to iOLab devices and basic laboratory materials
- Group work structures and whiteboards for collaborative explanation
- Learning goals set across conceptual knowledge, technical skills and the ISLE process
#### Constraints
- The article notes interventions were scheduled after theory was introduced in class, differing from the ideal ISLE model, and that limited class time constrained the modules

### Target Learners
- High school physics students in 11th and 12th grade

### Learning Goals
- Developing and testing multiple explanations
- Using scientific equipment and analyzing data
- Estimating physical quantities
