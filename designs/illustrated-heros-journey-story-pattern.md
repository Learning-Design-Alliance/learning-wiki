# Minimal-integration illustrated hero's-journey story woven through an existing lecture sequence

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a reusable course-level design: an illustrated story following a hero's-journey plotline, told in the third person to avoid anthropomorphizing E. coli, with each lecture opening with a few story slides before traditional lecture content. The implementation was deliberately minimal: "existing lectures were simply reordered to match the story chronology and story slides only took a few minutes from each lecture." The story was revisited across three course parts, exposing students to the full storyline multiple times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A story plotline students find broadly familiar (the hero's journey)
- Illustrated story materials and, optionally, an introductory video available before the course
#### Constraints
- A more extensive integration of a story into the course is possible but could require substantial effort, even with the materials available

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life-science students in a microbial physiology and biochemistry lecture course

### Learning Goals
- attitudes toward a detail-intensive science topic
- functional knowledge of metabolism: applying details to specific situations
