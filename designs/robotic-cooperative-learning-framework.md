# Robotic cooperative learning framework: hands-on robotics within cooperative learning cultivates STEM interest and self-efficacy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a framework in which robotics used as a mechanical platform inside cooperative learning pedagogy creates a nurturing instructional environment that builds positive attitudes toward STEM. Its core ideology is that "students learn difficult concepts when they can play", implemented as a curriculum that is "90% hands-on laboratory classroom style". Hands-on activity is argued to promote self-efficacy, which sustains interest and supports further STEM pursuit, connecting critical thinking, STEM interest, and self-efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cooperative teams of three or four members with rotating roles (builder, parts specialist, programmer, tester) so all members experience each phase of robotic construction and programming
- A hands-on laboratory format in which students immediately apply lecture content
#### Constraints
- The article states it is difficult to determine whether the robotics, or robotics coupled with cooperative learning, promoted the interest
- The authors state the findings are premature and require further analysis of the metrics, scales, and assessments

### Target Learners
- sixth-grade middle school students
- undergraduate service-learning students mentoring middle schoolers
- girls and underrepresented minorities in STEM

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- STEM interest and self-efficacy
- robotic construction and programming skills
- collaboration
