# Highly structured course design: daily and weekly active-learning exercises with frequent low-stakes formative assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The highly structured course design assigns "daily and weekly active-learning exercises with the goal of providing constant practice with the analytical skills required to do well on exams," in contrast to low-structure courses based on lecturing and two or three midterms plus a comprehensive final. Its components, added in implementation order, were clicker questions in peer-instruction format, weekly online peer-graded practice exams, open-book reading quizzes on basic vocabulary and concepts, class notes summaries aimed at metacognition, and in-class group exercises with random-call participation replacing Socratic lecturing. Moderate structure added clickers and practice exams; high structure added reading quizzes and group exercises.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires frequent low-stakes assessment points (clicker questions, practice exams, reading quizzes) that together represent a minority of total course points, with exams remaining the primary determinant of grade
#### Constraints
- The study population was sophomores who had completed a chemistry prerequisite, so failure rates were lower than at many other institutions before any intervention

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate biology majors in large introductory gateway courses

### Learning Goals
- Higher-order cognitive skills at the application and analysis levels of Bloom's taxonomy, and reduced course failure
