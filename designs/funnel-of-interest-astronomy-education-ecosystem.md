# The funnel of interest as an organizing model for the astronomy-data-in-the-classroom ecosystem

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes thinking of the many programs using astronomy data in classrooms as a funnel ordered from least to most interested participants. At the top, citizen science reaches many people who need little technical skill; moving down, working with real data, contributing real data, and original research each reach fewer people but demand deeper understanding and technical skills such as programming. The author states the model can be organized "as a “funnel” of interest, from least to most interested", with the most engaged participants moving down toward the bottom, which is grad school. All parts of the funnel are described as important and valid.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs at each level must be aware of where they sit in the funnel and of other programs' positions so participants can be referred appropriately
#### Constraints
- The author notes the model may not be a one-dimensional continuum; other dimensions could include level of student involvement or degree of instrumentation building

### Target Learners
- K-12 students and teachers
- undergraduates
- citizen scientists and the public

### Learning Goals
- participating in authentic scientific inquiry
- understanding how science really works
- developing technical and research skills
