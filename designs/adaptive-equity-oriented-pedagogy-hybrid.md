# Adaptive equity-oriented pedagogy: a hybrid of democratic, assessment-driven, strengths-based, multimodal, and game-based instructional strategies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central framework is an "adaptive equity -oriented pedagogy" that hybridizes specific components of democratic, assessment-driven, strengths-based, multimodal, and game-based pedagogies. Instructors collect weekly ungraded pre- and post-tests, interest surveys, and anonymous course-feedback forms, then adjust practices such as modeling skills, in-class practice, low-stakes feedback, and feedback incorporation. Multimodal instruction is described as a synthesis of "PowerPoint lectures, simulations, role -playing, dialogues, technology, music production, and art" within a universal design for learning framework, and game-based mechanics let students redo graded assignments and earn points as teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must collect ongoing student data (weekly pre-/post-tests, surveys, anonymous course-feedback forms) and use it to adjust instruction each week
- Students must have voice in co-constructing classroom guidelines, assessment models, and their projects
- Graded assignments and assessments must be redoable, and collaborative point thresholds must be reachable for students to pass each lesson
#### Constraints
- The study oversampled historically underrepresented groups, so the samples are not representative of other higher education institutions
- The treatment course was taught before the control course to mitigate instructor rehearsal effects

### Target Learners
- diverse undergraduate students in higher education, including historically underrepresented and first-generation students

### Learning Goals
- mastering rigorous course objectives (94% or higher)
- applying course concepts through a community-organizing project
- growth mindset and reduced stereotype threat
