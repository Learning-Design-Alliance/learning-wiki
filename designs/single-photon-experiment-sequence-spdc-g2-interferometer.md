# Single-photon experiment sequence: SPDC, second-order correlation parameter, and single-photon interferometer with quantum eraser

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A sequence of three quantum optics experiments, commonly used together in undergraduate courses to demonstrate particle-wave duality of photons, all using a similar apparatus. In the first, "students set up detectors that measure pairs of entangled photons created by the process of spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC)". The second uses the entangled pairs as a heralded single-photon source and a beam splitter to measure the second-order correlation parameter g(2), distinguishing quantum anti-correlation from classical correlation. The third sends heralded single photons through a Mach-Zehnder interferometer and finishes with a quantum eraser using half-wave plates and a 45-degree polarizer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 405 nm laser, non-linear crystal, two or three detectors, coincidence-counting electronics, a LabVIEW computer interface, and for the third experiment a Mach-Zehnder interferometer with a piezo-actuated mirror, half-wave plates, and polarizers.
#### Constraints
- In this study the experiments were implemented outside of a course setting with lab guides adjusted to the researchers' logistical constraints.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students
- students in beyond-first-year lab courses

### Learning Goals
- understanding particle-wave duality of photons
- distinguishing quantum from classical models of light
- single-photon interference
