# Connect theoretical derivation, hands-on experiment, and open data analysis to teach quantum correlations through authentic scientific practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper proposes a complete pedagogical framework in which students move from hypothesis and modeling through experiment to data analysis, culminating in potential falsification of a classical model in the sense of Karl Popper. The authors state that "it provides a complete frame- work linking theoretical derivation, experimental im- plementation, and open data analysis." This makes the experiment well suited for undergraduate teaching, letting students directly observe violations of a Bell-type inequality in measured data and develop both intuition for quantum mechanics and a rigorous scientific mindset.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a polarization-entangled photon source, or the provided open dataset as a substitute
- A derivation of the inequality accessible at the introductory level
#### Constraints
- The authors note their demonstration does not address experimental loopholes such as the perfect anti-correlation assumption, and recommend discussing loophole-closure strategies with students

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students
- Introductory physics course students

### Learning Goals
- Engage in the complete scientific process from hypothesis to falsification
- Develop intuition for quantum mechanics and a rigorous scientific mindset
