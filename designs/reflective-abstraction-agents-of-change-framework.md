# Reflective abstraction constructs and agents of change framework

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's framework combines Piaget's reflective abstraction constructs — interiorization, coordination, encapsulation, and generalization — with Dubinsky's added construct of reversal, and pairs them with four agents of change that can initiate reflective abstraction: individual, peer, instructor, and curricular. The article defines each agent, e.g., "Peer: A classmate challenges or questions an individual." It uses this framework to design the experimental calculus curriculum on limits and to code student protocols.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum activities, peer group work, instructor debriefing, and individual exercises must each be present so each agent can challenge or question students
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied to the concept of limit in first-semester college calculus at a community college

### Target Learners
- first-semester college calculus students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of the limit concept
