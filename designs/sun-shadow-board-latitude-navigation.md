# Sun shadow board navigation: shadow direction gives cardinal directions and noon shadow length gives latitude

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The navigational model the lesson teaches: at local noon the Sun transits the meridian due south, so a gnomon's shadow points north, and the shadow's length indicates the observer's latitude. The article explains that "the shadow of the gnomon at noon can be calibrated to any latitude by aligning the tip of the shadow with a circle"; on following days a shorter shadow means the position is too far south and a longer one too far north. The Vikings probably used this technique, likely with a wooden disk in a bucket of water, to sail along latitude.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Readings are taken at local noon, when the shadow points north.
- A board with concentric rings and a gnomon of adjustable height; at sea it was put inside a bucket with water to cancel out ship movements.
#### Constraints
- The calibration assumes a negligible change of the Sun's declination.
- For ﬁve to seven day journeys, the allowed period is between end of May and beginning of July.

### Target Learners
- students aged 12-16 in middle or secondary school

### Learning Goals
- explain how the Sun can be used to maintain a course along latitude
- determine cardinal directions from the position of the Sun
