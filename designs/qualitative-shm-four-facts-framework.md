# Qualitative SHM analysis framework built on four elementary kinematics and mechanics facts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes analyzing simple harmonic motion qualitatively using only knowledge students typically have before SHM is introduced. It rests on four listed facts: "The gradient of a velocity-time curve is acceleration", Newton's second law, Hooke's law, and mechanical energy conservation. From these, eight qualitative results about the motion are derived and used to sketch the velocity-time curve, whose shape is largely determined by the gradient-acceleration fact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must already have been exposed to elementary kinematics, Newton's second law, Hooke's law, and mechanical energy conservation before the analysis is introduced
#### Constraints
- The approach cannot determine the exact value of the period and cannot claim the curve to be sinusoidal

### Target Learners
- physics students who are not mathematically well prepared

### Learning Goals
- understanding key features of simple harmonic motion
- graphical analysis of motion under a varying force
