# NEXUS/Physics IPLS lab curriculum organized around five threads with open-ended investigations of living-systems physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-semester (11 weeks per semester) laboratory curriculum accompanying a reformed second-year IPLS course, built around physics at cellular scales (Brownian motion, diffusion, charge screening, fluid flow, fluorescence) and open-ended experimental design. The development "focuses on five main threads: modeling; experimental design/protocol development; error analysis; technical lab skills; and interdisciplinary thinking." Students use research-grade equipment (inverted microscopes with CCD cameras, USB spectrometers) and modern analysis tools such as NIH's ImageJ.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Labs must accompany a reformed course taken after calculus, biology, and chemistry prerequisites
- Explicit, extensive support on technical skills so students can spend time on open-ended design and data interpretation
#### Constraints
- Preliminary results come from two small test classes (maximum N = 31); replication in large-enrollment courses with new TAs remains to be seen

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate life sciences (IPLS) students, mostly pre-med

### Learning Goals
- quantitative modeling of living systems
- experimental design and error analysis
- interdisciplinary physics-biology thinking
- technical lab skills with modern equipment
