# Introduce general relativity in the English secondary curriculum at Year 13, where students can recognise curvature as the source of gravitational force

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article concludes that GR and its associated geometry are "better suited to the A-Level curriculum rather than lower year groups", because a deeper understanding of curvature as the source of the gravitational force is not seen until Years 12 and 13. Placing GR at Year 13 "aligns with students' ability to recognize curvature as the source of gravitational force and matches the current placement of gravity study in the curriculum". Younger year groups appreciated curvature effects in activities but showed little grasp of spacetime curvature in their descriptions of gravity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Placement assumes students have prior exposure to geometry and gravitational-orbit content, which the article notes lower year groups lack
#### Constraints
- A one-off intervention alone does not imbue a deeper understanding of GR; the authors suggest a multi-stage intervention may prove more beneficial for dispelling force misconceptions

### Target Learners
- Year 13 (A-Level) secondary school students

### Learning Goals
- understand gravity as spacetime curvature
- recognise curvature as the source of gravitational force
