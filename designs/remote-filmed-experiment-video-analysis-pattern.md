# Remote experimental procedure combining a filmed apparatus demonstration with student video analysis and conceptual questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern in which a laboratory experiment is filmed and presented to a remote student audience, who then analyze the recorded data and answer conceptual and calculation questions. The experimental model "incorporates conceptual questions on oblique projectile motion and the practical application of the concept". Students received the spring constant, initial spring compression, projectile mass, and flight time and range, and used accessible software (Tracker, OriginLab, Excel) for video analysis and curve fitting. In this study software use was optional but encouraged for one class and required for the other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recording environment controlled to minimize external factors such as wind and vibrations, with a white background and metric references for motion analysis
#### Constraints
- A simplified version of the procedure was provided to the civil engineering class due to time constraints imposed by the academic calendar

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- oblique projectile motion
- conservation of mechanical energy
- experimental data analysis
