# PTEBL: problem-, team- and evidence-based learning approach to Stop the Bleed training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
PTEBL is a three-session teaching approach combining problem-based learning (1.5 h), team-based learning (2 h), and evidence-based learning (0.5 h) within a four-hour Stop the Bleed course. The PBL session poses scenario questions; the TBL session has students practice skills in small teams with role-played scenario simulation and peer critique; the EBL session follows "a) developing an answerable question, b) finding the best available evidence, c) evaluation the evidence and d) applying the evidence to a patient care decision." The article applies it to teach hemostasis skills while explicitly training teamwork.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single instructor experienced in educational reform and certified in Stop the Bleed
- Small groups in which each student performs at least one tourniquet placement and plays scenario roles
#### Constraints
- Teamwork gains may not benefit every STB action, since building a team in real life is difficult and single practice cannot reflect the method's effectiveness

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students in a five-year training program

### Learning Goals
- Hemostasis techniques (direct finger compression, packing, tourniquet placement)
- Teamwork and comprehensive emergency response competence

### Claims
- Ptebbl Improves Teamwork Skills Stb [+M]
- Ptebbl Noninferior Hemostasis Skills [+M]
