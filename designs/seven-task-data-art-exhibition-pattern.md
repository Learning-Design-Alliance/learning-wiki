# A seven-task authentic learning pattern culminating in a public data-art exhibition develops technical and presentation skills in computing students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The pattern divides students' work into two phases with seven tasks: topic and data selection and analysis, research and art inspiration, design conceptualisation, proposed solution, visualisation creation, exhibition curation, and reflection. By integrating these tasks, students develop technical skills and gain practical experience presenting to a public audience, bridging academic learning and professional practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prominent public exhibition space must be arranged in advance, with deadlines set early and printing costs covered
- The task is personalised so every learner has their own dataset, and separated into stages with synchronous activities including art inspiration and Five Design-Sheets design sketches
#### Constraints
- The paper states it takes longer to organise than a traditional module, and the tutor needed extra help sessions to get struggling students through

### Target Learners
- third-year BSc Computer Science computing students

### Learning Goals
- technical visualisation and programming skills
- presenting work to a public audience
- creative design thinking
