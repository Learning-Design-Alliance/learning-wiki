# The Physics in Films course framework: four goals for improving scientific literacy through film-based analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors organize their course around four stated goals: to "motivate students to think critically about science information presented in ﬁlms", to help students distinguish physical laws from pseudoscience, to encourage understanding of how science works and how accepted theories are verified by the scientific method, and to help students learn where the borderline between tested and untested physics ideas lies. The framework extends the traditional physical science core beyond classical physics to relativity, quantum mechanics, astronomy and cosmology, using films as the motivating medium. It differs from earlier film-based approaches, such as Dubeck's, by being mostly quantitative and drawing on a broad array of genres rather than only science fiction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A broad range of film genres and topics, homework film viewing, and quantitative in-class analyses integrated with lectures and demonstrations
#### Constraints
- Designed for the non-major introductory level; keeping the course up to date with new releases is an acknowledged challenge

### Target Learners
- non-science majors in general education physical science courses

### Learning Goals
- scientific literacy
- critical thinking about media portrayals of science
- understanding the scientific method
