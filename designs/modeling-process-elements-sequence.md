# The modeling process as a teachable sequence of objective-setting, variable/parameter definition, explicit assumptions, scenario-based sensitivity analysis, verification, and transparency

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents mathematical modeling as a flexible, nonlinear, iterative process with broadly agreed fundamental elements. Models, as "functional representations and simplifications of complex biological systems," are built for a target audience beginning with a clear objective; scenarios are translated into equations; variables and parameters are defined; assumptions are made explicit; and "[t]he model should be tested for its sensitivity to changes in variable and parameter inputs through multiple scenarios." Each prototype is verified against the model's objectives, and the whole process is kept transparent for modelers and audience alike. The course taught this sequence explicitly and assessed it.
