# Teachers can foster resilience in regular classrooms by providing supportive environments that develop protective factors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "the teacher can foster the resiliency characteristics among at -risk students by helping them acquirea protective mechanism through providing supportive environment and developing protective factors contributing towards resilience." Teachers do this by providing purposeful activities and opportunities that encourage students to apply their skills, providing explanations, encouraging students to elaborate their responses, appreciating and applauding successes, and providing support during task learning. The study demonstrated this in a three-month intervention with at-risk secondary students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers who provide a supportive environment and develop protective factors contributing towards resilience
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated at secondary school level only, with the selected protective factors, over a three-month intervention delivered by a researcher rather than a regular teacher

### Target Learners
- Non-resilient at-risk of failure students at secondary school level

### Learning Goals
- Academic resilience and protective factors such as self-esteem, self-efficacy and internal locus of control
