# Performance–Play–Dialog (PPD) model of game-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The PPD model is the article's explanatory framework for game-based learning designed around inquiry. Performance is the primary thrust, encompassing development of understanding in the subject domain and construction of self-identity; it is realized through the sub-constructs of play and dialog, which stand in dialectical relation — "3OD\ VXVWDLQV GLDORJ DQG GLDORJ LQIRUPV SOD\". The model holds that "WKH SULPDU\ WKUXVW RI OHDUQLQJ LV GULYHQ E\ SHUIRUPDQFH WKDW HQFRPSDVVHV WKH GHYHORSPHQW RI XQGHUVWDQGLQJ LQ WKH VXEMHFW GRPDLQ DQG WKH FRQVWUXFWLRQ RI VHOI-LGHQWLW\". Play is mediated by embodied engagement with a material digital game world via an avatar, while dialogism, a key Bakhtinian idea, fosters internally persuasive discourse that allows students to construct new ways of meaning. The model underpinned the design and classroom enactment of the Legends of Alkhimia curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A coherent, single, substantial digital game enacted as a full curriculum or curricular unit rather than a short embedded resource
- Teacher-facilitated classroom dialog following game play, in which student voices are heard, valued, and harnessed in service of student learning
- A developmental trajectory of becoming within a professional domain, instantiated through incremental game levels
#### Constraints
- The model was developed and evaluated in the domain of lower secondary school chemistry; the article states its principles are applicable to effective design of game-based learning in general but reports evidence only from chemistry

### Target Learners
- lower secondary school science students (14-year-olds)

### Learning Goals
- learning chemistry through inquiry
- developing identity as a chemist
- epistemological understanding of the nature of science
- scientific argumentation and critique
