# Course-specific RAG as an intermediary layer between independent study and formal academic support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that course-specific RAG systems occupy an intermediary space between independent study and formal academic support: rather than replacing educators, they offer "private,immediate,andcourse‐specificguidance" as a low-friction first point of support before students engage lecturers or tutors. The account holds that help-seeking barriers extend beyond availability of services to confidence, anxiety, fear of judgement, and perceptions of whether questions are appropriate, so an accessible private channel can lower the threshold for initiating help-seeking while preserving the pedagogical role of educators. This framing informed both Beacon's design objectives and the study's evaluation questions on trust, learning support, and help-seeking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Responses must be grounded in approved teaching materials and remain aligned with module learning outcomes, so guidance is educationally appropriate rather than unrestricted.
#### Constraints
- The article states course-specific AI is not a substitute for effective teaching or student support services, and the findings come from a single institution's computing modules.

### Target Learners
- higher education students reluctant to seek help through conventional channels due to anxiety, low confidence, or fear of judgement

### Learning Goals
- initiating help-seeking
- accessing academically appropriate guidance
- building confidence before engaging formal academic support
