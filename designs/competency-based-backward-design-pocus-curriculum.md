# Competency-based backward design for POCUS curriculum development

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a four-step backward design process for building ultrasound curriculum: competencies are first designated from professional society guidelines and institutional needs, then learning objectives are established, assessments are designed to align with those objectives, and educational activities are planned last to maximize success on the assessments. The authors state "In the four-step backward design process, the first step is to designate and characterize competencies that trainees must acquire". They offer this as a model other institutions can adapt for EFAST and other POCUS curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Review of professional society guidelines and assessment of the educational needs of the particular institution to establish competencies
#### Constraints
- Competencies and activities were tailored to the anticipated needs of SHSU COM and its community, so transfer to other institutions requires local needs assessment

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical education students (pre-clerkship osteopathic medical students)

### Learning Goals
- Clinical point-of-care ultrasound competence, specifically the EFAST examination
