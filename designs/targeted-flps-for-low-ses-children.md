# Design family literacy programs for low-SES children as targeted programs focused on a limited set of activities and skills, restricted to one (training) context

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The meta-analysis concludes that low-SES children benefit most from family literacy programs that are targeted rather than comprehensive: "children benefit from targeted programs that focus on a limited set of activities and skills and that are restricted to one (training) context." The authors describe this as a "less is more" pattern emerging from several moderator effects. It applies to program developers designing interventions for families with low socioeconomic status.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should focus on a limited set of activities and skills rather than a comprehensive mix.
#### Constraints
- Effects on follow-up measures were marginal (d = 0.16), so the principle rests mainly on immediate posttest results; the formal-activities and L2-training comparisons were based on only one study.

### Target Learners
- Children aged 0–6 from low-SES families

### Learning Goals
- Comprehension-related emergent literacy skills
- Code-related emergent literacy skills
