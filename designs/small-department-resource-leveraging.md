# Small physics departments should utilize every available resource — technology adoption, nearby-institution collaborations, and cross-department joint efforts — to build a rigorous program under constraints

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A small department should, as the authors put it, 'develop a program that utilizes any and all the resources that we could draw upon': adopt computer technology to stretch limited laboratory capacity, build research and course collaborations with nearby institutions, and pursue joint programs with other departments or schools. At Manhattanville this approach balanced service teaching and the major and produced a small program of rigor and vitality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willing partners at nearby institutions and in other departments or schools, and continued support from the college administration.
#### Constraints
- Lack of critical mass in both the faculty and the students, and lack of significant development funding, limit major growth in facilities and faculty size.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in small physics programs at liberal arts colleges

### Learning Goals
- Rigorous physics training comparable to larger programs despite limited staffing and funding
