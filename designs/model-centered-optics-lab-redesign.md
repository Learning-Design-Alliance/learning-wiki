# Model-centered redesign of optics labs: retaining apparatus while reframing guides around modeling, prediction, and systematic error

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The transformed optics labs retain existing physics content and apparatus but restructure lab guides around modeling. In the polarization lab, 'students now develop a model of polarized light using Jones matrix formalism,' enabling quantitative fitting of data to determine parameters of elliptical polarization. The article states 'the equipment is identical to the previous lab, but the focus is now on modeling the experimental system, predicting results, comparing with data, and uncovering and modeling systematic error effects.' This reframing was chosen because the original lab gave students equations without a predictive model, leaving little ability to make predictions or discuss systematic error.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictive model of the optical system so students can make qualitative or quantitative predictions and discuss observable systematic error effects
#### Constraints
- The previous lab's biggest limitation 'was not in the apparatus, but in the level of understanding the lab promoted'

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- modeling the physical system
- modeling the measurement system
