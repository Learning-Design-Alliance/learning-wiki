# A five-phase problem-solving process enacted in heterogeneous learners' community groups structures PBL for secondary mathematics lessons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's PBL implementation organised each lesson around a flowchart of problem processes and grouped students heterogeneously. As printed, "the PBL group process adopted consisted of five phases namely (i) identify the problem (ii) make assumptions (iii) formulate a model (iv) use the model and (v) evaluate the model." The 42 experimental students formed six groups of seven, seated semi-circularly so the teacher could walk across groups, with group members presenting solutions and peers critiquing them while the teacher acted as facilitator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students grouped heterogeneously by prior performance; seating arranged so the facilitator can move between groups; ill-structured tasks with multiple solutions; teacher orientation on PBL before implementation
#### Constraints
- The rigid school timetable allowed only two 40-minute periods per week and no extension of classroom activities beyond closing time

### Target Learners
- Senior Secondary School year one Further Mathematics students (mean age about 15 years)

### Learning Goals
- Indices and logarithms, algebraic equations, sequences and series; problem solving and conceptual understanding in Further Mathematics
