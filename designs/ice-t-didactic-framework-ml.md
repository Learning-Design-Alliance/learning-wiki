# ICE-T: a didactic framework integrating intermodal transfer, computational thinking, and explanatory thinking for ML education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
ICE-T is a multi-faceted didactic framework for teaching machine learning whose name mnemonically captures its components: Intermodal Transfer grounded in Bruner's enactive-iconic-symbolic principle, Computational Thinking operationalized through the Use-Modify-Create progression, and Explanatory Thinking supported by the PETSP-ML process model. The article states the framework "unifies the three didactic facets described above into a coherent, actionable framework for ML education". The three facets are treated as orthogonal dimensions of a single learning unit: EIS modes structure how a concept is represented, UMC structures the degree of student autonomy, and PETSP-ML structures the temporal sequence, with which combinations are realized left as a deliberate educator design decision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators deliberately decide which combinations of representational mode, UMC stage, and process phase are realized at each point of a learning unit
#### Constraints
- The connection between ICE-T and trust calibration is, at this stage, theoretical and has not itself been evaluated; its empirical grounding is limited to the K-12 landscape

### Target Learners
- K-12 students with little or no prior exposure to programming

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of machine learning
- computational thinking
- explanatory thinking about ML systems
- calibrated trust in AI systems
