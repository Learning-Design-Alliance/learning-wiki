# A qualitative-observation ball-and-bottle laboratory built on minimal instructions, whiteboard data plotting and a plenary discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Groups of three to four students drop a ball with a water bottle balanced on top and investigate how the bounce heights of both objects depend on the amount of water, using invented scales and no numerical measurement. The article states the implementation "incorporates three distinct areas of experimental skills development: observation, method development, and note keeping". Instructions are deliberately minimal, students plot their data on whiteboards, and a concluding plenary discussion covers plotting practice and the underlying physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of 3–4 students, an area of "at least 4 × 4 m per group" with sufficient ceiling height, and access to a whiteboard or similar large writing surface per group
#### Constraints
- The article states the number of students that can be facilitated in a session "will depend on the space available and 'teacher' experience"

### Target Learners
- introductory university physics students

### Learning Goals
- observation and qualitative description of experimental behaviour
- experimental method development and adjustment
- laboratory note keeping
