# Schoolwide coherence through coordinated adoption of a structured direct instruction program

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Ackerman Elementary achieved the highest coherence ratings among the 11 field study schools by adopting a Direct Instruction program providing scripted lessons and matching assessments in mathematics and reading for grades one through six, supplemented by grade-level literacy leaders and classroom libraries. Teachers met in within-grade and across-grade teams, often twice weekly, to coordinate curriculum, instruction, and assessment. The report notes "The fully articulated instruction and assessment scheme provided by the Direct Instruction program, along with schoolwide implementation of the literacy/library supplement, were leading reasons for Ackerman's high rating on instructional program coherence."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A fully articulated instructional and assessment scheme
- Ongoing whole-school staff development for implementing the program
- Grade-level literacy leaders to support peers
#### Constraints
- Not every school would agree with Ackerman's decisions to adopt Direct Instruction and to focus its program so much on improved test scores

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students in grades one through six in high-poverty urban schools

### Learning Goals
- Raising reading and mathematics achievement through a common scripted instructional framework
