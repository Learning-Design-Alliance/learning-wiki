# Three design principles — authentic learning, deliberate practice, and scaffolding complexity — guiding iterative design of telementoring sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's instructional design was guided by three theoretical principles: deliberate practice (effortful, goal-directed skill development and refinement, supported by expert feedback under conditions of high standards and psychological safety), Herrington's principle of authenticity (learning opportunities reflecting real-world settings and tasks reflecting the complexity of real-life practice), and scaffolding complexity within Merrill's first principle of instruction (the gradual increase in task difficulty alongside the progressive reduction of mentor support). These principles shaped a two-cycle iterative design, evaluation and re-design of telementoring sessions following initial in-person POCUS training.
