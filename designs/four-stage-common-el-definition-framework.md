# Four-stage framework for a common English learner definition: identify, classify, establish an English-proficient standard, and reclassify

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper organizes the entire EL definitional process around four stages adapted from a National Research Council panel report: "1) identify a student as a potential English learner; 2) classify (confirm/disconfirm) a student as an English learner; 3) establish an 'English-language proficient' performance standard on the state/consortium English language proficiency (ELP) test"; and 4) reclassify to former-EL status through multiple exit criteria. The framework is intended to help consortium member states "systematically review and document existing practices" and align instruments, policies, and practices within states, across states within a consortium, and across consortia.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consortium member states must explicitly define their approaches to the four key steps and establish mechanisms to move toward sufficient convergence or alignment over time
#### Constraints
- The framework is adapted from an NRC (2011) panel report on comparability of state procedures for identifying, classifying, and reclassifying English learners
- Fulfilling the common definition requirement is described as presenting substantial challenges requiring a carefully coordinated, multiyear effort proceeding in stages

### Target Learners
- K-12 students potentially or actually classified as English learners

### Learning Goals
- English language proficiency sufficient to access academic content instruction and participate fully in society
