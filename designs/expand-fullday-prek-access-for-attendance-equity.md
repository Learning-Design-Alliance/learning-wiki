# Expand access to full-day pre-k, prioritizing students and families facing the greatest logistical and economic barriers to attendance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that policymakers increase full-day pre-k availability because it better fits families' daily lives and raises attendance, particularly for groups with historically lower attendance. It states that "Increasing opportunities for full- day programs is especially beneficial for these families" with fewer economic resources, and that districts should consider which students benefit most when deciding program location and enrollment processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Full-day pre-k is more expensive for districts to offer than half-day pre-k, so expansion requires additional investment in teachers and classes.
#### Constraints
- The article states full-day programs address some, but not all, logistical barriers; families with limited economic resources still face challenges such as childcare, transportation, and healthcare access.

### Target Learners
- pre-kindergarten three- and four-year-old students
- families with fewer economic resources
- Black students and other groups with historically lower attendance

### Learning Goals
- regular pre-k attendance
- kindergarten readiness
- equitable access to early learning
