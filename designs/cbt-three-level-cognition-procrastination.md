# CBT account of academic procrastination as cognition maintained across three levels

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames academic procrastination as grounded in cognitive structures that CBT discusses "in three dimensions, as false automatic thoughts, intermediate beliefs, and core beliefs." Within this framework, people procrastinate because of established ways of thinking, and change requires replacing irrational beliefs with rational ones at all three levels. The program's effectiveness was, in the authors' view, tied to replacing cognitions at each level, with cognitive, affective, and behavioral techniques used together.
