# fMRI-compatible simulated instrument landing decision task

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A simulated Cessna 172p instrument landing task built on a modified version of FlightGear, rendered via projection onto an fMRI head-coil mirror and flown with an fMRI-compatible joystick. Pilots flew ILS approaches to SFO runway 1L across six fog-density variations, making a land/do-not-land decision at the 200 ft decision height, with control trials requiring continued descent without a decision. The article states "The landing decision task was a simulation based on Kennedy et al., (2010) with design consultation from IFR & CFII/ATP pilots who worked in the laboratory."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pilots with current FAA Medical Certificate Class III or higher and at least 500 total flight hours
- A 15-minute practice session on laptop plus in-scanner practice before data collection
#### Constraints
- Flaps, throttle, and elevator trim were pre-set, so pilots primarily controlled ailerons; the task compressed a real approach to roughly 30-second trials

### Target Learners
- licensed instrument-rated pilots

### Learning Goals
- instrument-approach landing decisions under low-visibility conditions
