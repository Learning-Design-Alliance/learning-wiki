# Hands-on, practice-based training sessions combining interactive videos, collaborative activities, and real-time Q&A

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The training sessions were designed as experiential rather than didactic: "Interactive videos, collaborative group activities, and real-time Q&A sessions were included in each training session". Participants gained practical experience integrating the tools, and the authors attribute the programme's positive short-term response to this hands-on, practice-based approach to digital pedagogy. The Exit Ticket activity, collected on Wooclap, showed Wooclap and Padlet were the most memorable and likely-to-be-implemented tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each four-hour session must embed interactive videos, collaborative group activities, and real-time Q&A so faculty gain practical experience with the tools.
#### Constraints
- The article links lower adoption of Representation-linked tools such as Edpuzzle and Mindomo to time constraints.

### Target Learners
- medical faculty members new to UDL and Web 2.0 tools

### Learning Goals
- practical skill in integrating interactive technologies into teaching
