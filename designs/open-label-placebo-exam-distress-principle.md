# Offer open-label placebos as a transparent, non-deceptive option to counteract acute exam-related distress in students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article supports administering an openly labeled placebo — pills explicitly stated to contain no active ingredient — taken twice daily for three weeks before a high-stakes exam. In its trial this regimen "can significantly reduce exam-related distress, as indexed by a reduction in perceived stress, negative mood, fatigue and confusion", while producing no general test-score benefit. It is presented as an ethical alternative to deceptive placebo administration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must be informed and consent that the pills contain no active ingredient; the intervention relies on open communication as an integral part of the OLP rationale.
#### Constraints
- No general improvement in test performance was observed; performance benefits appeared only in students with a strong general belief in the benefit of medication.
- Effects were found for exam-related distress of limited duration and may not translate to prolonged stress.
- Participants were not blinded to treatment, which the authors note may have introduced reporting bias.

### Target Learners
- healthy university students facing exams
- individuals undergoing periods of increased demand

### Learning Goals
- reducing exam-related stress, fatigue, and confusion
- supporting psychological well-being during assessment periods
