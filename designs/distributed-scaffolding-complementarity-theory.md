# Distributed scaffolding and complementarity between material and teacher scaffolds

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Distributed scaffolding is the framework in which support for complex project-based learning is spread across multiple tools, routines, activities, and the teacher, forming a system that simultaneously supports all students in a classroom. The article stresses that "the agents, tools, and resources must complement one another, so that they all work together in a system of scaffolding". Material scaffolds address anticipated difficulties and can be faded in printed tools, while teacher scaffolding supplies the responsive, contingent support that static materials cannot. The study tests this framework by examining how two teachers' moves complemented or merely replicated fading material scaffolds in the Scientist's Journal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support distributed across tools, activities, and the teacher must be integrated so the components complement rather than duplicate one another
#### Constraints
- Material scaffolds in printed tools provide a fixed set of prompts identical for all students and cannot tailor support to individual learners' needs in the moment

### Target Learners
- sixth-grade middle school science students in mixed-ability classrooms

### Learning Goals
- science practices such as hypothesis writing and data interpretation
- physics concepts of forces, motion, and energy
