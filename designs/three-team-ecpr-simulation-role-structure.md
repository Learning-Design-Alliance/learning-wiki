# Three-team role structure for multidisciplinary eCPR simulation (airway, medical resuscitation, cannulation)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The simulation organizes participants into "three primary teams, each with specific personnel assigned to distinct roles to ensure a coordinated and effective resuscitation and cannulation process": an airway team (leader and assistant), a medical resuscitation team (medication nurse, recorder, two chest compressors, resuscitation leader), and a cannulation team (preparation assistant, cannulator, wire assistants). Roles are assigned by training background, with cannulator and wire assistant roles reserved for those who perform them clinically. Minimum staffing of three resuscitation and two cannulation participants is specified for limited enrollment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- At least three participants for medical resuscitation and two for cannulation when enrollment is limited; role assignment based on training background
#### Constraints
- Cannulator and wire assistant roles were performed by participants who assume these roles in real clinical scenarios (fellows and attendings)

### Target Learners
- Multidisciplinary resuscitation teams including physicians, nurses, pharmacists, respiratory therapists, and advanced practice providers

### Learning Goals
- Demonstrate quality team dynamics, including specifying roles and utilizing close-looped communication

- [The eCPR simulation significantly increased comfort with cannulation procedures, eCPR candidacy criteria, and postcannulation resuscitation](../claims/ecpr-simulation-domain-comfort-gains.md) [+W]
