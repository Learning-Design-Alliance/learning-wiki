# MATLAB Simulink-based CSL workflow with tiered assignments and Google Classroom video submissions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A computer simulation-based learning implementation for an electric circuit analysis course in which students study circuits with "MATLAB® – a computer simulation program for engineering" and upload recorded presentation videos to "Google Classroom, a learning platform that aims to simplify creating, distributing, and scoring assignments." Performance-based assignments were designed weekly by the course lecturer; week 1 assignments were randomly chosen at the same difficulty level, and thereafter students who made at least the least score could choose among three difficulty levels (Normal, Difficult, Very Difficult) illustrated by D.C. circuits with independent and dependent sources. Weekly analytical mistakes were discussed with the class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must upload a recorded PowerPoint presentation video to Google Classroom each week
- Assignments must be available in three difficulty levels with different score levels
- The course lecturer must tailor the video content and discuss circuit analytical mistakes with the class each week
#### Constraints
- The study describes this workflow for a first-year electrical engineering course at a single institution (RMUTP) during the COVID-19 shutdown

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate electrical engineering students

### Learning Goals
- electric circuit analysis skills in MATLAB Simulink
- self-efficacy in circuit analysis assessment
