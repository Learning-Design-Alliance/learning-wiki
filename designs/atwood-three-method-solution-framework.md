# Three-method treatment of the Atwood's machine: Newton's second law for particles, centre-of-mass dynamics, and energy conservation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes the Atwood's machine solution around three complementary methods. Method 1 is the traditional free-body analysis giving a = g(m2−m1)/(m1+m2) and T = 2gm1m2/(m1+m2); Method 2 treats the system as a whole via the centre of mass, where "the tension in the string is now an internal force"; Method 3 uses conservation of energy. The article states the latter two methods "are appropriate to the more complex problem of the rope", making the familiar problem a bridge to variable-mass systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Method 1 assumes a massless string over a frictionless pulley of negligible mass and m2 > m1
#### Constraints
- Newton's second law for particles (equation 1) is, in principle, not valid for variable mass systems, as the article reminds teachers
