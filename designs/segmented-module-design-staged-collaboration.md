# Use a segmented module design to enable staged, scalable inter-university collaboration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a segmented approach to module design that allowed collaboration between universities on some parts of a module and independent working on others. The authors state that "The segmented approach to the module design allowed collaboration between universities on some points in the module and independent working on others" and that sharing "could be scaled up or down with little interruption to delivery patterns." This staged pathway made it easier to progress toward full virtual student exchange while respecting institutional constraints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Modules at collaborating institutions must share matched learning outcomes on common subject matter
#### Constraints
- Formal modification of module learning outcomes, delivery, and assessment took a year to complete at one institution
- Timetabling placed module sessions on different days and time zone differences removed potential for shared lectures with Flinders University

### Target Learners
- Final-year undergraduate and postgraduate environmental health students

### Learning Goals
- Community needs assessment and intervention design
- Inter-institutional collaborative learning
