# Student-led peer-assisted mock OSCE (MOSCE) at the University of Ottawa

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A yearly, voluntary, student-led mock OSCE launched in 2015 to give third-year students experience with clerkship-level stations. In it, "first- and second-year medical students (M1 and M2) acted as SPs, fourth-year (M4) students served as examiners, and M3 students were the examinees". It comprised 5 stations assessing history-taking, physical examination, counseling, and management, mirroring the faculty-run format of 10-minute stations including 2 minutes of examiner feedback, with M4 examiners using a checklist and global rating scale. Cases were designed by students and reviewed by a faculty advisor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student volunteers in three roles (M1/M2 standardized patients, M4 examiners, M3 examinees)
- Student-designed cases and checklists reviewed by a faculty advisor
- Administration less than a month before the higher-stakes third-year OSCE
#### Constraints
- The article states the student-designed stations mean the MOSCE construct "may be called into question"
- Participation did not affect subsequent OSCE scores in this study
- Evaluated at a single center, limiting generalizability

### Target Learners
- Third-year undergraduate medical students (clerkship)

### Learning Goals
- Preparation for clerkship-level OSCE stations in history-taking, physical examination, counseling, and management
