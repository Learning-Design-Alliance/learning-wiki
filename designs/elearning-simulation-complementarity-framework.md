# Complementary-modality blending framework: e-learning as cognitive preparation and extension of simulation-based training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The editorial organizes simulation and e-learning as complementary modalities within medical education: simulation provides "a controlled, versatile, and dynamic environment with opportunities for repeated practice", while e-learning offers "modular, scalable content that can be accessed remotely". E-learning primes knowledge before simulation and extends it afterwards through debriefing and quizzes, and the blend "complements patient-based training, supports adaptive learning, and enhances readiness for clinical practice".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration requires curricular planning to identify gaps, establish distinct learning objectives and instructional strategies, and balance cognitive, metacognitive, and psychomotor skills
#### Constraints
- The editorial states simulation and e-learning "should remain complementary adjuncts to real patient-based clinical training in medical education"

### Target Learners
- medical and healthcare students
- healthcare professionals in clinical training

### Learning Goals
- technical and non-technical clinical skills
- clinical reasoning
- professional competence and teamwork
