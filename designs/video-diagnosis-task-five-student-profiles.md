# Video-based student-profile diagnosis task with eye tracking (11-min geometry lesson, five marked students)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's core element is an 11-min video of an eighth-grade introductory geometry lesson from a German high school, containing a whole-class question-answer scene (bottom-up drivers assumed) and an individual seatwork scene (top-down drivers assumed). Five students marked with letters B, E, K, P, T each represented one empirically identified profile (strong, struggling, overestimating, underestimating, uninterested) from a latent profile analysis. Participants watched the video under eye tracking, then assigned each student to one profile and wrote down the indicators they had used, with a second-choice option for possible confusions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must first be introduced to the student characteristics and the five profiles, and watch a short trailer to become familiar with the lesson and classroom.
#### Constraints
- Only the strong, struggling, overestimating, and underestimating profiles were identified by latent profile analysis in percentages of 26%, 21%, 12%, and 9%, with a fifth intermediate low-interest group (31%) treated as comparable to the uninterested profile.

### Target Learners
- novice and expert (in-service) teachers

### Learning Goals
- diagnostic judgment of individual student characteristic profiles from observed engagement
