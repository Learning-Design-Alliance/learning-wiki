# Traditional group-based (TRAD) control training: 8 days of lectures and simulator practice offsite, usually in a hotel

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The control arm is the traditional in-service training approach common in Nigeria: "8 days of lectures with practice sessions on simulators, off-site the participants' workplace, usually in a hotel." It uses the Nigerian BEMONC package and clinical observation checklists with MamaNatalie/NeoNatalie manikins, and covers the same clinical topics as the intervention arm. Unlike the LDHF arm it has no onsite post-training practice sessions, no peer practice coordinators, and no mobile phone support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must travel offsite for the 8-day course, removing providers from clinical practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes this approach is costly in provider and opportunity costs and may leave untrained team members unable to support implementation

### Target Learners
- Maternal and newborn health providers in control facilities in Kogi and Ebonyi States

### Learning Goals
- BEmONC knowledge and skills in day of birth care
