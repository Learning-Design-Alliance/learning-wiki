# Digital storytelling as a multimodal narrative writing genre with seven elements and three production stages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Digital storytelling (DS) is defined as "the newest version of the ancient storytelling", telling a story using multimedia elements such as images, music, and narrated voice. The article describes seven elements (point of view, a dramatic question, emotional content, gift of voice, the power of soundtrack, economy, and pacing) and three stages: preparation (topic selection, storyboarding, script writing), production (choosing non-verbal elements), and presentation. It positions DS as a learner-centered setting in which students construct meaning from their own experiences using multiple modes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Script writing must be treated as the most essential part, since if the script writing is ignored the final product cannot be truly effective
#### Constraints
- DS does not negate the traditional writing process; it follows process writing steps such as drafting, editing, revising, and sharing

### Target Learners
- EFL English-major preparatory students

### Learning Goals
- narrative writing skills
- multimodal expression and meaning-making
