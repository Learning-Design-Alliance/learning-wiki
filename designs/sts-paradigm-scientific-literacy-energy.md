# STS (Science, Technology and Society) paradigm guiding scientific literacy through energy conservation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article frames its teaching under the STS paradigm, choosing the perspective of scientific literacy in which students comprehend and cope with a variety of science-related situations rather than being trained as potential scientists. Science is taught embedded in a social and technological milieu connected to students' worlds and practical experiences. In practice, the classes emphasized playing with batteries, lamps and wires "to illustrate the idea that energy can be stored on a battery and" changed from one form to another, feeding debates about sustainability and saving energy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teaching anchored in students' interests and everyday technological and social worlds, with hands-on materials such as batteries, lamps and wires
#### Constraints
- The article states that its emphasis at LL was not on creating and testing hypotheses about electricity, but on illustrating the idea that energy can be stored and transformed

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students, including those with learning difficulties or ADHD

### Learning Goals
- Scientific literacy: comprehending and coping with science-related situations, starting with energy conservation
