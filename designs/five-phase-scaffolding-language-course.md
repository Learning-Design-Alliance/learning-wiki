# Five-phase scaffolding instructional design for language courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article applies He's (1997) five-component scaffolding model to an emergency language course: building the scaffold (pre-testing to determine learning situation), entering the context (using movies or pictures to create emergency situations), independent exploration (teacher guidance gradually decreasing as student ability increases), collaborative learning (group work and discussion), and evaluation (student presentations with timely teacher feedback). This sequence structures an 8-week course across four emergency-event teaching units. The article describes it as a design in which "the teacher's guidance gradually decreases as the students' problem-solving ability increases until the students are able to complete the tasks independently."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher capacity to design graduated learning units and provide timely feedback
- Pre-testing to determine students' starting point
#### Constraints
- Validated only for a foreign-language (English) emergency language course with Chinese undergraduates; the article notes other language types (Mandarin, dialects, sign languages) were not studied

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students of teaching Chinese to speakers of other languages

### Learning Goals
- Emergency language proficiency in English
- Professionalism as emergency personnel
