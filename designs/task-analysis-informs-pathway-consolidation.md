# Consolidate duplicated educational pathways into a single curriculum when task analysis shows extensive task sharing across programme levels

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This principle holds that pre-service curriculum design should be grounded in measured task performance of graduates in real service settings. The Mozambique Ministry of Health used a task analysis survey of task frequency by cadre and facility type to identify gaps and duplications across six health worker cadres. Where the two levels of a cadre shared a common body of knowledge and skills, the article deemed it appropriate to craft "a single educational pathway" rather than maintaining parallel programmes of different lengths.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Task analysis data on actual task performance frequency across cadres and facility settings
- Curriculum documents and scope-of-practice statements to generate the task and knowledge instruments
- Content experts to verify content validity of the instrument items
#### Constraints
- The article notes respondents could not be said with certainty to be proportionately representative of actual providers in each facility and care setting
- Findings addressed the Mozambican health system context and six specific cadres

### Target Learners
- pre-service nursing and maternal/child health nursing students
- mid-level health worker trainees

### Learning Goals
- clinical competencies matched to the tasks graduates are expected to perform in their assigned health facilities
