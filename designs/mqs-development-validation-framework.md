# Framework for developing, validating and implementing multiple-choice question sequences guided by a research-validated tutorial

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a framework in which a multiple-choice question sequence (MQS) is developed by taking advantage of "the guided inquiry-based learning sequences" in a corresponding research-validated Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorial (QuILT). Development draws on the QuILT's learning objectives, guided sequences, scaffolding, and pre/posttest data, then iterates through faculty feedback and think-aloud student interviews before in-class implementation via clickers with peer discussion. The framework treats sequencing, not just individual question quality, as the object of validation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing individually validated multiple-choice questions or a validated tutorial on the same topic to guide sequencing
- Iterative refinement with faculty feedback and one-on-one student think-aloud interviews
#### Constraints
- Developed and evaluated in upper-level undergraduate quantum mechanics courses with roughly 15-25 students per year at a large research university

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- foundational quantum mechanics concepts including Hilbert vs. physical space, state preparation, measurement, and superposition vs. mixture
