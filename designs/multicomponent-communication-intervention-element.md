# Multi-component communication intervention (DTT + JASP+EMT+SGD + caregiver training) for preverbal preschoolers with autism

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 36-session adaptive intervention delivered over 4 months combining three components: 'therapist-implemented DTT with the child to teach foundational skills', therapist-implemented JASP+EMT+SGD during play and routines, and caregiver training to implement JASP+EMT+SGD. Sessions occurred two clinic days and one home day per week (45–60 min), with three caregiver workshops. The intervention was adaptive: caregiver practice time increased with caregiver skill fluency and child DTT decreased as foundational skills were mastered. All families also received an iPad with Proloquo2Go and caregiver SGD programming training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Children must be preverbal or at first-words stage (fewer than 20 different spontaneous words in a 20-min language sample) with a confirmed autism diagnosis
- Master's-level therapists meeting fidelity criteria for all components; the same therapist implements all components for a family
#### Constraints
- Components were implemented at lower doses than previous studies of DTT and clinician-delivered JASP-EMT+SGD, which the authors state may have impacted effectiveness

### Target Learners
- preschool children with autism (ages 3–5) who are preverbal or minimally verbal

### Learning Goals
- joint attention
- social communication
- spoken language
- foundational skills (imitation, receptive language, SGD use)
