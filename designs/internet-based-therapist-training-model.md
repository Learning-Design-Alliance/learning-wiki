# Internet-based training as a scalable alternative to workshop-plus-supervision training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that clinically-rich training websites — carrying detailed treatment information, a library of complete acted treatments with commentaries, formative knowledge tests, ongoing feedback with certificates, and refresher courses — can replace the conventional two-stage workshop-plus-supervision model. In internet-enhanced training the website replaces the initial workshop and a supervisor with good generic supervisory skills provides "web-centred supervision" by referring repeatedly to the website, a role likened to a facilitator in guided self-help. Internet-alone training involves no workshop and no supervision, making it far less costly and immensely scalable but likely less effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- For internet-enhanced training, the supervisor needs good generic supervisory skills, familiarity with the disorder and general class of psychotherapy, and intimate knowledge of the website
#### Constraints
- The article predicts internet-enhanced training would be as effective as conventional training, if not more so, but this is a prediction, not a tested result; internet-alone training is expected to be less effective given the absence of case supervision

### Target Learners
- Geographically dispersed trainee and practising therapists seeking training in psychological treatments

### Learning Goals
- Acquiring knowledge of a treatment and skill at implementing it
