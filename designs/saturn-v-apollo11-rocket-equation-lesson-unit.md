# Saturn V / Apollo 11 calculation lesson unit for grades 10–13 (90–180 minutes)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A teacher handout and worksheet-based lesson unit in which students "berechnen ausgehend von der Raketengleichung verschiedene Parameter der dreistufigen Mondrakete Saturn V unter dem Einfluss der Schwerkraft". It builds on an earlier unit on flying a rocket to the ISS but targets higher grades because of the mathematical demands. Stated learning goals include becoming familiar with the Apollo program, graphing the launch flight profile, computing velocities of a multi-stage rocket with realistic figures, and determining key rocket-flight parameters. Materials are worksheets, pen, calculator, and internet access; duration is 90–180 minutes depending on task selection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior knowledge of mechanics fundamentals (mechanical fundamental equation, momentum, force) and differential calculus, as the handout states: "Die Schülerinnen und Schüler benötigen für diese Aktivität bereits Vorkenntnisse zu dem Grundlagen der Mechanik"
- Worksheets must be copied in advance and calculators available
#### Constraints
- The unit only introduces basic concepts of rocket propulsion and performs a partly realistic calculation; several simplifying assumptions are made (vertical ascent, constant exhaust velocity and propellant mass flow, constant g)
- A realistic treatment of a rocket launch would exceed the difficulty level feasible for school teaching

### Target Learners
- students in grades 10–13 (Sekundarstufe II)

### Learning Goals
- applying the rocket equation to a real multi-stage rocket
- graphing a launch flight profile
- understanding the Apollo program and rocket propulsion basics
