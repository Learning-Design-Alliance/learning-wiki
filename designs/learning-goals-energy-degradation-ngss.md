# Learning goals for energy degradation and the second law of thermodynamics grounded in teachers' productive ideas and aligned with the NGSS

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces learning goals for energy degradation and the second law of thermodynamics that (1) "represent a sophisticated physics understanding of these concepts," (2) "originate in ideas that teachers already use," and (3) "align with the NGSS." The goals build on five identified teacher ideas: energy can be present but inaccessible; can lose usefulness as it transforms within a system; can lose usefulness as it disperses; tends to end as thermal energy; and its usefulness depends on the objects involved in the scenario. They support teachers integrating disciplinary and everyday understandings of energy, connecting first-law conservation with second-law degradation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Building on teachers' existing productive ideas rather than treating their ideas as misconceptions to be replaced
- Alignment with NGSS statements on energy availability, concentration changes, and uniform spreading
#### Constraints
- None of the observed teachers demonstrated a complete understanding of energy degradation and the second law; some teacher ideas (thermal energy useful in some situations; usefulness depends on scenario objects) go beyond what the NGSS articulates

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers in energy-focused professional development

### Learning Goals
- Understanding energy degradation, availability, and usefulness
- Understanding the second law of thermodynamics
- Integrating disciplinary and everyday understandings of energy
