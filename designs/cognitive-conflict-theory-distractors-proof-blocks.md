# Cognitive conflict account of learning from misconception-based distractor lines

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's rationale for distractors is cognitive conflict: distractor lines tied to common misconceptions about proof by induction should make students "experience a state of cognitive conflict", resolved when they figure out or are told which proof line is correct, helping them assimilate the knowledge. The article reports this account had not previously been applied to learning to write proofs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Distractor lines must relate to documented common student misconceptions, and selecting a distractor must produce targeted feedback explaining why it is incorrect.
#### Constraints
- In the article's own randomized experiment, the predicted learning benefit of distractors over no distractors was not statistically significant.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer science students in a discrete mathematics course

### Learning Goals
- writing mathematical proofs by induction
