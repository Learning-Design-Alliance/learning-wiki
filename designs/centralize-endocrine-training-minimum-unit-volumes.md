# Concentrate endocrine surgery training in units with minimum annual volumes of 100 thyroid, 50 parathyroid, 15 adrenal, and/or 10 GEP-NET operations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is that endocrine surgical training should be centralized in high-volume units, because operative exposure depends on unit caseload. It states: "Training in endocrine surgery should be performed in units that perform a minimum of 100 thyroid, 50 parathyroid, 15 adrenal, and/or 10 GEP-NET operations yearly." This follows from its findings that resident exposure in many countries falls below delegate expectations and that adrenal learning curves of roughly 30 cases cannot be completed at low annual volumes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient annual unit caseload in the relevant procedure types to provide fellows the recommended performed-operation minimums (e.g., 50 thyroid operations for fellows)
#### Constraints
- The article notes the recommended adrenal volumes are well over the workload of many units, reinforcing the need to centralize training in a small number of units

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- endocrine surgery fellows

### Learning Goals
- acquiring operative competence in thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal, and GEP-NET surgery sufficient for independent practice
