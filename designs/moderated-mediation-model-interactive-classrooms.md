# Moderated mediation model of learning efficiency, classroom atmosphere, network environment, and grade level in remote teaching evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes and validates a moderated mediation model in which classroom atmosphere mediates the effect of rural students' learning efficiency on teaching evaluation of urban-rural interactive classrooms, while network environment reliability and student grade level act as moderators. The authors report that "classroom atmosphere is the mediating variable, and Network environment and grade level are the moderating variables." The account is grounded in self-efficacy theory and Piaget's cognitive development theory, with grade level operating as a second-stage moderator of the indirect path.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement of learning efficiency, classroom atmosphere, network environment, and teaching evaluation via validated questionnaires adapted to the rural interactive-classroom context
#### Constraints
- Developed and tested in the cultural and educational context of Chinese rural Hope Primary Schools, which the authors state may restrict broader applicability
- Grade level was dichotomized into high and low groups for the moderation analysis

### Target Learners
- Rural primary school students (grades 1–6) in Chinese Hope Primary Schools participating in urban-rural interactive classrooms

### Learning Goals
- Improving teaching evaluation of remote interactive instruction
- Understanding how learning efficiency, classroom atmosphere, and network conditions shape remote learning outcomes
