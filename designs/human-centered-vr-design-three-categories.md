# Human-centered VR design framework of communication, form of reality, and presence applied to surgical simulator design

## Description
The article applies a human-centered VR design framework whose "High-level principles for human-centered VR design fall into three categories: communication, the form of reality, and presence." Communication requires prioritizing the UX with simple, transparent user-technology interaction to foster presence; form of reality concerns placement on the reality-virtuality spectrum and input/output hardware choices; presence manifests from the user's interpretation of immersion and is maximized by minimizing breaks in presence and optimizing world stability and depth cues. The authors used this framework to guide iterative refinement of the SurgiSim microscope UX/UI.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An iterative design process with regular end-user (surgeon) feedback to learn differences between VR and physical environments
#### Constraints
- Fundamental differences between physical and virtual worlds, such as gravity and the physical constraints of the Oculus headset and controllers, create challenges to the user's sense of presence

### Target Learners
- surgeons and surgical trainees using VR simulation

### Learning Goals
- surgical rehearsal skills
- operating microscope maneuvering
- microsurgery skills
