# The 'learn, see, practice, prove, do, maintain' model as a pedagogical framework for procedural skill training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The curriculum is structured around the 'learn, see, practice, prove, do, maintain' model (attributed to Sawyer and colleagues), which sequences procedural education through six phases: learners "completed online comprehensive preprocedural education modules and assessments on paracentesis and ultrasound (learn), viewed a video of the procedure properly performed (see), practiced the procedure in a simulation laboratory with faculty guidance and deliberate practice (practice), and were assessed completing the simulated procedure (prove)". Once a competence standard is demonstrated, learners perform live procedures under supervision (do), then return to simulation and case reviews at intervals (maintain). The authors state this model "creates a more systematic and reliable way to teach procedural skills in order to ensure competency" and organize the article's curriculum phases end to end.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Phase progression gated by demonstrated minimum competence before live supervised performance
#### Constraints
- Applied here to a single bedside procedure with resident learners; the article presents it as a framework the authors followed rather than a tested comparison

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine and medicine-pediatric residents of all training years

### Learning Goals
- Systematic acquisition and maintenance of invasive bedside procedural skills
