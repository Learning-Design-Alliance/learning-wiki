# Guided problem solving: presenting each laboratory experiment as an experimental problem solved in graded stages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This lesson-level pattern combines 'problem-solving' and 'guided design' modes: an experiment is presented as an 'experimental problem' with guiding procedural instructions, and each problem is "presented as a collection of simple smaller experimental stages, which are interdependent or hierarchical in time". Students follow tasks woven in succession so the problem unfolds stepwise toward the solution. A 20-minute introductory demonstration precedes independent solving of the problem for the remaining laboratory time, without major curriculum or content changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An introductory demonstration presented to each small group for 20 minutes as a prelude to the problem
- A carefully designed student handout, instruction sheet and answer paper for each experimental problem
- Reading material on measurement, data analysis, significant figures and error analysis prepared in advance
#### Constraints
- The free laboratory ambiance is not open-ended or exploratory; students are told the final outcome of each part of the work and guided with respect to the possible method of solving the problem

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- higher secondary to M.Sc. physics students
- teachers in training programmes

### Learning Goals
- independent thinking in experimental physics
- experimental problem-solving abilities
- procedural and conceptual understanding
