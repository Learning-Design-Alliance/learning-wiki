# Audio Articulation Method (AAM) model lesson structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Audio Articulation Method, proposed by Demirezen (2004), is a pronunciation teaching model "designed to fill this gap in the field of pronunciation teaching" by rehabilitating fossilized segmental errors within a single class hour. Its basic steps involve the teacher identifying the problematic core sound, preparing a corpus of 50-100 words including that sound and its nearest pair, singling out minimal pairs, developing tongue twisters and idiomatic chunks, and stimulating further awareness through experiential practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher identifies the problematic core sound of the target language
- Corpus of 50-100 words including the problem sound and its nearest pair
- Minimal pairs extracted for practice
- Tongue twisters, proverbs, idioms, mottoes, or cliche expressions developed in chunks
#### Constraints
- Designed for students studying English Language Teaching at university level
- No reliable diagnostic tool for determining whether errors are fossilized
- Fossilization issue excluded from the scope of this study

### Target Learners
- University-level EFL students
- Turkish EFL learners with segmental pronunciation difficulties

### Learning Goals
- Rehabilitation of fossilized segmental pronunciation errors
- Awareness of phonological forms
