# Contextual teaching and learning as a framework of six fundamental characteristics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article adopts contextual teaching and learning as its theoretical framework, defined as teaching that enables pupils to "employ their academic understandings and abilities in a variety of in – and out –of–school contexts to solve simulated or real –world problems". The framework comprises six fundamental characteristics: problem-based curriculum, multiple contexts, self-regulated learning, anchoring in students' life contexts, authentic assessment, and interdependent learning groups. These characteristics were used to develop the curriculum and assessment materials for the experimental treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum and assessment materials must be designed with the fundamental characteristics of contextual teaching and learning in mind, as the article's experimental treatment did
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied here to a postsecondary distance-education agricultural biotechnology course; the article notes few studies have focused on teaching science in the context of agriculture at the postsecondary level

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students in agricultural and life sciences courses

### Learning Goals
- mastery of scientific concepts related to agricultural biotechnology
- application of scientific information in contextual settings
