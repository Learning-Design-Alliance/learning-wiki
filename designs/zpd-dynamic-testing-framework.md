# Dynamic testing grounded in Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, with IRT modelling of change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents dynamic testing as an assessment approach drawing "upon Vygotsky's theory of the zone of proximal development (ZPD)". Dynamic tests "examine the changes that occur during an assessment by incorporating feedback and training into the testing phases, providing information about the individual's ZPD". Because classical gain scores are criticized for ceiling effects and regression to the mean, the study uses item response theory models that "enable estimating the probability of solving an item correctly, based on the child's ability and the item difficulties", giving a more favourable reliability of gain scores in dynamic testing contexts.

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- Gain-score interpretation depends on item difficulty range; the authors note four-point gains from 1 to 5 items can mean something different than gains from 13 to 18 points on a 20-item test

### Target Learners
