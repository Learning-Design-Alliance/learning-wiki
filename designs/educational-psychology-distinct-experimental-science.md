# Educational psychology as a distinct experimental science whose standard course takes the learning process as its backbone

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that educational psychology deserves status as a distinct discipline because education is a significant field of application of psychological truth, comparing it to agriculture: "Experimental pedagogy is just as important and just as possible as experimental farming." It holds that the learning process should form the backbone of standard courses, studied experimentally rather than a priori, preferably in the school system, with constant application in laboratories or schools and differentiation of courses by the type of educator being prepared.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments carried out throughout the entire field of education, ideally under school conditions normal for scientific investigation
- Laboratory training of instructors in pedagogical apparatus such as Binet tests before laboratory work is attempted
#### Constraints
- The author admits much laboratory work is at present inexact and subject to constant revision, and that mental phenomena elude absolutely exact measurement
- Articulating experimentation with the public school system presents difficulties the article acknowledges, though it sees no other wholly adequate means of arriving at pedagogical truth

### Target Learners
- College and university students preparing to be teachers, supervising school officers, normal school instructors, and educational investigators

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the laws of the learning process and their experimental investigation
- Applying psychological principles to school subjects and teaching methods
