# A 4-hour, three-session PFA training program adapted from WHO guidelines with lecture, simulation, and role-playing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a Psychological First Aid training program developed from WHO's Psychological First Aid guide for field workers and its facilitators' guide, tailored to local disaster types and culture. It consisted of "three sessions with a total duration of four hours" delivered face-to-face using lectures, video presentations, crisis event simulations, group discussions, role-playing, and scenario-based activities. Session 1 introduces PFA and the BEST model (Behaviour, Emotion, Somatic, Thinking); Session 2 teaches the Look, Listen, Link action principles; Session 3 adds a 30-min earthquake role-play, ethical principles, self-care, and relaxation techniques. Delivery was by five experienced nurses trained in a disaster nursing master's program under a disaster-nursing supervisor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers with systematic PFA training and clinical nursing experience, guided by a disaster-nursing supervisor
- Classroom setting supporting group work and role-play scenarios
#### Constraints
- Content was tailored to the regional disaster and cultural context (scenarios inspired by the 2008 Wenchuan earthquakes)

### Target Learners
- Registered community nurses without prior PFA training

### Learning Goals
- PFA knowledge (definitions, core and action principles, self-care)
- PFA skills in observing, listening, linking and terminating assistance
- Self-care awareness during disaster response
