# DST as a multimodal middle ground that facilitates community-of-practice formation and shared knowledge construction for EFL students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames digital storytelling as an educational technology that "establishes a meaningful and inclusive multimodal learning context" in which EFL students with different life experiences, sociocultural backgrounds and proficiencies negotiate concepts. Drawing on communities-of-practice theory, the authors argue the videomaking process encourages students to construct shared knowledge, gives them autonomy, and creates a context for collaboration, with DST serving as a tool for adapting prior beliefs about STEM-A to a video format. In this study the framework explains why video-producing groups showed the largest disciplinary literacy gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in groups on videomaking tasks tied to inquiry-based astronomy content
- Teachers should be ready to enter a community of practice with their students and co-construct disciplinary knowledge
#### Constraints
- The mechanism account is the authors' interpretation of a small-sample case study; time spent on video production and group composition varied across groups

### Target Learners
- EFL students aged 10-16 learning STEM content in English

### Learning Goals
- Astronomy (STEM-A) disciplinary literacy in English
- Conceptual understanding of Moon phases and the Solar System
