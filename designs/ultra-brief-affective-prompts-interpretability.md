# Use ultra-brief affective prompts to enrich the psychological interpretability of passive physiological data at minimal participant burden

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's design guidance is that a single open-ended affective question, answered in the participant's own words, can complement passive wearable sensing by supplying psychological context the sensors cannot recover. Responses were extremely short — the median was three words — yet still associated with within-person variation in sleep and activity outcomes. The article concludes that "ultra-brief affective prompts enrich the psychological interpretability of passive physiological data at minimal burden", positioning this as a lightweight alternative to infrastructure-intensive sensor-language models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single open-ended concern prompt integrated into an existing study protocol (here, a bimonthly ecological momentary assessment delivered via a study mobile application), paired with concurrent wearable outcome data aggregated over the same two-week window.
#### Constraints
- The concern prompt is negatively valenced by construction, selectively surfacing worry and distress; the authors state findings should be read within that scope.
- The bimonthly interval is a design constraint representing a middle point in the burden-versus-sensitivity tradeoff, not an optimum established by the study.
- Associations are formative and exploratory; none survived correction for multiple comparisons.

### Target Learners
- university students
- first-year university students in longitudinal health monitoring studies

### Learning Goals
- psychological context for physiological monitoring
- early detection of distress
- timely clinical outreach
