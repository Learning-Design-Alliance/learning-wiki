# Make school improvement teacher-led rather than administration-driven

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report argues that school-improvement initiatives should be led by teachers rather than imposed top-down by administrations. In Brockton, a Restructuring Committee of veteran teachers framed the work, designed the literacy goals, and won over colleagues; the report states "the literacy initiative was teacher led, and this was undoubtedly a key factor responsible for the success that was achieved." It contrasts this with districts that expect compliance with central-office orders rather than building genuine buy-in.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness of site and district leadership to accept a gradual approach rather than demand immediate widespread adoption

- Initially the teachers' union objected and supported teachers who refused to participate; the strategy relied on working with the willing
