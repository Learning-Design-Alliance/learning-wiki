# Guided human–AI collaboration workflow with five components and AI-use logging

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An instructional component in which the experimental group received "explicit instruction in a guided human–AI collaboration workflow built around five interrelated components": problem framing and prompt design; generating alternative outlines, arguments, and claims; iterative drafting and revision; verification and triangulation of claims and citations; and responsible-use regulation. Students used a generative AI assistant for idea development and structural scaffolding alongside a language-feedback tool, and each major assignment included an AI-use log documenting prompts, outputs consulted, and accept/reject decisions with justifications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a generative AI assistant and a language-feedback tool
- Explicit instruction in the five workflow components
- An AI-use log per major assignment documenting prompts, outputs, and accept/reject/justify decisions
#### Constraints
- The instructional model is contingent on scaffolding quality, metacognitive readiness, and institutional governance
- Without structured workflows and accountability mechanisms, risks of over-reliance and superficial learning are substantially elevated

### Target Learners
- Saudi undergraduate EFL students in a required academic writing course

### Learning Goals
- Academic writing proficiency
- Digital critical thinking and AI literacy
- Responsible and ethical AI use
