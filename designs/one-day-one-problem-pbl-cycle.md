# The one-day, one-problem PBL cycle at Republic Polytechnic

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A compressed PBL variant in which "students work on one problem each day", each day covering a single subject. A class of "five teams of five students, meets three times under the guidance of a tutor": problem presentation with prior-knowledge activation and learning-goal formulation, a short self-study period, a second meeting with tutor scaffolding, a longer self-study, then team presentations and whole-class discussion. Problems, materials and assessments are accessed through a laptop-based e-platform.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An e-platform providing access to the problems, learning materials, and assessments, with each student having a laptop computer
- A tutor who meets the class three times during the day to monitor progress and provide scaffolding if needed
#### Constraints
- The rationale is framed for polytechnic students who are generally young and "generally not been exposed to PBL before"

### Target Learners
- post-secondary polytechnic students, typically 17 years of age

### Learning Goals
- subject-specific knowledge construction through problem analysis, self-study, and collaborative elaboration
