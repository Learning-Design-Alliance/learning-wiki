# Weekly instructional cycle combining whole-class fluency instruction with ability-differentiated small-group practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The program organizes instruction as a five-day weekly cycle: "The first two days of each instructional week are devoted to direct instruction and guided practice activities for the whole class" focused on fluent reading, prosody, and oral comprehension. On the next two days, students reading at or above grade level practice fluency independently while below-grade-level students receive small-group instruction emphasizing phonics and word attack skills; the fifth day is used for assessment-based identification of struggling students and goal setting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use of the Fluency Formula™ Assessment System on the fifth day to identify struggling students and set instructional goals
- Teachers model fluent reading and lead repeated, partner, and choral reading practice
#### Constraints
- In the evaluated study, low initial-ability students received four days a week of instruction while high initial-ability students received only two days of whole-class instruction

### Target Learners
- first- through sixth-grade students reading below, at, or above grade level

### Learning Goals
- reading fluency
- prosody
- phonics and word attack skills
- oral comprehension
