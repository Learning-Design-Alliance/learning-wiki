# Drama sessions structured in three stages: warm-up, impersonation, and assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes drama activities as organized in a recurring three-stage session structure. In the warm-up stage students do physical and rhythm activities to get ready to express themselves and socialize; in the impersonation stage the selected activity is performed; in the assessment stage students reflect on their feelings to build awareness and empathy. The study's 8-week test-group program followed these stages meticulously.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants, a setting where players can comfortably express themselves, a subject/content, and a drama leader who provides continuity for the process
#### Constraints
- The leader should avoid going too far with the games and tiring the participants unnecessarily

### Target Learners
- 7th-grade students in Turkish language courses

### Learning Goals
- Turkish verbal skills
- speech anxiety reduction
- communication skills
