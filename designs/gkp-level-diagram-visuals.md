# Level-diagram visuals (Figs. 1–14) as the core instructional component conveying GKP encoding, errors, and decoding

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The learning material is built on a series of fourteen figures using level diagrams in which a shaded cell marks the active level, green marks the code space, and grey cells mark possible errors yielding the same syndrome. The diagrams carry the whole conceptual progression: two-level encodings (Fig. 1–3), padded encodings (Figs. 4–7), syndrome ambiguity (Fig. 8), the uncertainty principle (Fig. 9), 2D grids with X and Z errors (Figs. 10–12), and the continuous-variable generalisation (Figs. 13–14). The authors state the material is "guided by a series of visuals specifically designed to enhance intuitive understanding of the continuous variable (CV) and GKP encoding".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators use the diagrams consistently, indicating an active level by a shaded cell and referring to the corresponding state by the shaded cell index
#### Constraints
- The authors note Z errors appearing to affect only |1L⟩ can be difficult for learners, possibly requiring a change of basis beyond the transition diagram

### Target Learners
- CS-background learners new to quantum computing

### Learning Goals
- intuitive understanding of CV and GKP encoding
- visual understanding of X and Z error correction
