# Multi-level practice teaching system: linked lab experiments, team course projects, contests/certification/research projects, and graduation practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because object-oriented programming "has lots of knowledge points and wide applications", the authors set up a multi-level practice system with four stages. Lab-room experiments verify each knowledge point while being deliberately connected so that "the back experiments are to perfect the front experiments, and eventually students can get a small application system". Course projects are "slightly larger and difficult topics" completed by teams of 3~5 students who present their work for comprehensive evaluation. Students then enter programming contests (ACM, Baidu star, TopCoder, Oracle Think Quest), the China Rank Certificate Test, and undergraduate research projects, and finally do graduation practice in software companies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiment content designed with front-to-back association so exercises accumulate into an application system
- Team organization (3~5 students) with comprehensive evaluation of each team's project presentation
- Training and encouragement structures for contests and certification participation
#### Constraints
- The article states the system's benefits prospectively and reports no measured outcome for it

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer science majors across all four years

### Learning Goals
- comprehensive practical and innovative programming ability beyond isolated knowledge points
