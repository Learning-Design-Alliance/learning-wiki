# Wright State University Introduction to Data Science course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An Introduction to Data Science course co-developed and co-delivered by one MIS and one CS professor at Wright State University in summer 2015, structured in three parts — data hacking, mathematics and statistics foundations, and predictive analytics — with "Approximately 40% of the course was devoted to data hacking, 20% to math and statistics foundations, and 40% to predictive analytics", and practice in analyzing real-life datasets carried out through interactive labs and a course project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Laboratories with real data are used in each part of the course to practice the discussed concepts.
#### Constraints
- Designed for students whose majors do not require courses in probability theory, multivariable calculus, or calculus-level statistics, so the structure builds technical skills from the ground up.

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate and graduate students across business, liberal arts, and engineering and computer science colleges

### Learning Goals
- Data manipulation, management, visualization, and preprocessing; probability and statistics foundations; running and interpreting predictive analytics models
