# Regular cooperative exposure to complex procedures through dual surgeon operating prevents skill fatigue and supports rural workforce retention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that in rural centres lower case volumes contribute to "skill fatigue and loss of confidence to perform complex operations", and that prolonged periods without practice cause deterioration of technical and non-technical skills. Dual surgeon operating is presented as a mechanism that doubles each surgeon's exposure and creates a cooperative environment for shared decision-making, maintaining technical precision for both participating surgeons and making rural posts rewarding enough to aid recruitment and retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A cooperative environment with regular exposure to complex surgery
- Willingness of the surgical department to support the additional surgical resource
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is only limited information about the impact of two-surgeon operating on clinical outcomes and patient safety to date

### Target Learners
- Rural general surgeons maintaining an extended scope of practice

### Learning Goals
- Skill maintenance and further development in a low to medium case volume environment
- Confidence maintenance for complex procedures
