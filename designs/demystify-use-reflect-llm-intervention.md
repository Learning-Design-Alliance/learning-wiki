# Demystify, use, and reflect: a structured LLM-intervention course design positioning LLMs as tools for supported learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course design centers on a structured intervention that works by "demystifying LLMs, how they work, their capabilities, and their probabilistic nature and limitations, alongside guided use through prompting strategies and iterative interaction". Rather than mandating or banning LLM use, the course leaves the decision to each student, requires students to understand, explain, and acknowledge AI-generated contributions, and adds a post-assignment reflection survey capturing prompt counts, role conceptualization, and verification strategies. This study extends the intervention by examining how its effects vary at the subfield level rather than only in aggregate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to an LLM tool (institutionally licensed Microsoft Copilot Chat was available, though not restricted to a single tool)
- Post-assignment reflection surveys must be administered immediately after task completion to capture interaction
#### Constraints
- The course did not explicitly encourage LLM use; the decision of whether and how to use them was left to each student
- Findings are tied to the specific assignments and instructional practices of a single course context

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate computer science students in a required post-CS1 problem-solving course

### Learning Goals
- Informed and responsible LLM use
- Effective prompting and iterative interaction
- Assessing the reliability of LLM-generated responses
