# The briefing–scenario–debriefing (BSD) cycle as a design scaffold for distributing guidance, feedback, and representational work over time

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The editorial describes the briefing–scenario–debriefing cycle as a widely used framework for structuring simulation instruction: a prospective phase prepares learners, an in-action phase supports performance, and a retrospective phase renders experience accountable and generalisable. Conceptually, "the BSD cycle is less a fixed script than a design scaffold for distributing guidance, feedback, and representational work over time". Briefings translate curriculum goals into attentional priorities; debriefings convert concrete experiences into general principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Briefings must translate curriculum goals into attentional priorities and familiarise learners with the simulation environment; debriefings must convert experiences into generalisable insights
#### Constraints
- The BSD cycle is only one of many ways to embed simulations within broader instructional designs

### Target Learners
- professional education learners across domains

### Learning Goals
