# Flipped statistics course combining LMS-mediated pre-class multimedia with in-class peer instruction and group problem-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern organises a statistics course into two stages: pre-class activity on a Moodle-based LMS and in-class interactive group work. In pre-class activity, "students listened podcasts, watched videos, took online quizzes, and did self -assessment through the Exelsa", with 20 screencast videos covering 4 topics uploaded to YouTube and embedded in the LMS. In class, peer instruction with Desmos polling opened 3 of 4 meetings, followed by group problem solving with the instructor circulating to check and challenge thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An LMS hosting sequential pre-class materials (podcasts, videos, quizzes, self-assessment)
- Internet access for students to reach all online materials
- Video and audio production tools (screencast, recording, editing software)
#### Constraints
- Students reported difficulties when internet connection was inadequate
- Students perceived insufficient time to solve all in-class problems
- One meeting without peer instruction used a brief lecture instead

### Target Learners
- third-year pre-service English language teachers in a statistics course

### Learning Goals
- basic descriptive statistics, central tendency, dispersion and the normal curve
- preparation for statistical reasoning in research (skripsi/thesis)
