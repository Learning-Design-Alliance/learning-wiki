# Participatory design as a three-phase user-involvement approach to instructional re/design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participatory design, imported from fields such as cognitive ergonomics and health promotion, aims to ensure active participation of users in design decisions that affect them. The article states that a participatory design process consists of three phases: "analysis", "design", and "implementation" — participants first analyze the current situation and its shortcomings, then generate ideas for re/design, and finally devise an implementation plan. In this study the approach was adapted for secondary students co-designing their courses with their teacher.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants analyze the current situation and assess its shortcomings taking their own needs and desires into account before designing changes
#### Constraints
- The article notes participatory design in school contexts is fairly new and that it found hardly any prior classroom studies of its effects

### Target Learners
- 10th-grade pre-university secondary students

### Learning Goals
- Improving the fit of instruction to students' perceptions and desires
- Improving congruence between teachers' and students' interpretations of instruction
