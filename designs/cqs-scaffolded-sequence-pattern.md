# Clicker question sequence design: scaffolded validated questions building on each other with whole-class discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern in which research-validated multiple-choice questions are sequenced so that they build on each other and systematically target a coherent set of learning goals derived from common student difficulties. The article notes that "since the CQS questions build on each other, they typically take less time to complete than if the questions were not part of a sequence." After each vote the instructor displays results and leads a full class discussion, providing immediate feedback and scaffolding. The pattern was applied to measurement uncertainty in two-state quantum systems in a junior-/senior-level QM course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions validated through iterative review and think-aloud interviews to minimize unintended interpretations
- Coverage prioritized toward conceptual knowledge and common difficulties, avoiding burdensome calculations to fit allotted class time
- A response system and instructor-led discussion after each question
#### Constraints
- The Peer Instruction small-group discussion component was largely forgone in the online administration due to difficulty adapting it
- Complex multiple-choice formats may increase student cognitive load, though the authors and others have used them for parsimony across multiple facets of a concept

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students in quantum mechanics courses

### Learning Goals
- understanding measurement uncertainty, well-defined observables, and compatible vs. incompatible observables in two-state quantum systems
