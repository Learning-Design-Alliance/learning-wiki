# Task-based language teaching and its three-phase task procedure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Task-based language teaching treats in-class tasks as the center of second language learning, with meaning paramount and outcomes assessed. For design and sequence, the article describes Ellis's three principal phases: "'pre-task,' 'during task' and 'post-task.'" Pre-task options include a similar task, observing a model, non-task preparation activities, or strategic planning; the pre-task phase acts as an advance organizer. The study operationalized this as in-class tasks, out-of-class written assignments as during-task activities, and in-class sharing as post-task activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must keep meaning primary, connect to real-life activities, prioritize task completion, and use task outcome as the basis of assessment, as the article lists from Skehan.
#### Constraints
- The article notes TBLT's mainly inductive approach to language structure might be problematic for adult learners, who are described as better achievers learning from information about the language system.

### Target Learners
- Tertiary-level advanced EFL students

### Learning Goals
- Advanced English grammar structures used in written production
