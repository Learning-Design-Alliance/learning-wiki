# FLS-based laparoscopic box-simulator training program with five timed, penalty-scored tasks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 150-minute simulator training program delivered in two sessions separated by a one-week interval, using five tasks mimicking the validated Fundaments in Laparoscopic Surgery (FLS) exercises: peg transfer, cutting, passing, intracorporeal knot, and suture. Scoring subtracts baseline time and penalties from accomplishment time, so "higher scores mean better performance", and scores are expressed as a percentage of expert scores. The guide-wire exercise was slightly modified to render it more difficult.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students naive to surgical technique serve as their own controls via pre-training time and score collection; expert instructor averages provide the scoring reference.
#### Constraints
- Only the guide-wire exercise was modified from the published FLS tasks; performance in simulators does not necessarily reflect surgeon performance in real procedures, per the authors.

### Target Learners
- First- and second-year medical students with no prior surgical training

### Learning Goals
- Basic laparoscopic psychomotor skills: peg transfer, cutting, wire passing, intracorporeal knot tying, and suturing
