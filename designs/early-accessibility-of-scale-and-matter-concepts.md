# Rationale that scale and nature-of-matter concepts are accessible to young learners

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article advances a developmental rationale for teaching subatomic concepts early: at ages 7-12, children are curious and open, not yet believing physics is "hard", and are drawn to the unusual and unexpected. The authors report that learning research "suggests that concepts of small scale and the particulate nature of matter are accessible to young learners," and that greater emphasis early in learning should be placed on scale and the nature and properties of matter and energy. This framework grounds the project's design choices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A literature review of relevant learning research and a pilot assessment study were used to establish the rationale
#### Constraints
- Contradicted by early reviews claiming particle physics is too abstract for this age group; the authors' resolution is empirical, via their pilot study

### Target Learners
- children aged 7-12

### Learning Goals
- understanding of scale, matter, and subatomic concepts as foundations for later science learning
