# Three-stage SPOC+PBL blended course structure: online preparation before class, PBL case discussion during class, online consolidation after class

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's blended neurology course divides teaching into three stages. Before class, teachers release preview materials (PPT and flash GIFs of nervous system anatomy) on the Xuexitong platform so students "can watch videos repeatedly, post difficult questions online". During class, teachers use PBL with students as the main body: offline teaching of localization diagnosis methods, group discussion of cases with typical neurological signs, and online in-class exercises. After class, students complete online review of difficult parts and teachers post homework and extended reading. This structure reserves class time for detailed explanation and application rather than anatomy review.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must fully prepare online video content and publish online interactive test questions
- An online teaching platform (Xuexitong) for releasing materials and collecting questions
- Students need strong self-learning and problem-solving skills
#### Constraints
- The article states there is still a lack of unified syllabus to standardize online neuroanatomy teaching content, so preparation takes much time and effort
- The study applied this structure to one chapter (Anatomy, Physiology, and Localization Diagnosis of Nervous System Lesions) over four class hours

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate clinical medicine students

### Learning Goals
- neuroanatomy review and localization diagnosis of nervous system lesions
- clinical reasoning and application of textbook knowledge to practical problems
