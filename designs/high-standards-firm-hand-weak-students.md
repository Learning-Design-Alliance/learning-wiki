# Hold weak students to high, firmly enforced standards rather than lowering them

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The program's faculty believe this cohort "will respond better to a firm hand and high expectations" than to catering to student desires, arguing that "Lowering standards only communicates disbelief in student abilities to succeed." The publication requirement operationalizes the high standard: only A work is publishable, academic dishonesty immediately disqualifies a project from publication, and borderline projects are graded B unless other faculty confirm excellence. Students who persist and repeat a research project the following semester have always earned As and produced publishable work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty willing to enforce consequences (honor referral, publication disqualification) and to invest heavy mentoring time
#### Constraints
- Some faculty considered the publication-disqualification penalty for dishonesty too harsh and recommended preserving the publication possibility as a growth opportunity

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate freshmen with weak academic preparation

### Learning Goals
- Quantitative problem solving
- Work habits, self-esteem, and confidence
