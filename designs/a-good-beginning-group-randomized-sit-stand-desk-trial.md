# A Good Beginning: a two-and-a-half-year group-randomized trial of sit-to-stand desks in grade-three classrooms

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A prospective, two-armed, group-randomized trial in which two grade-three groups (age 8–9) in one Leiden primary school were compared: an experimental group receiving sit-to-stand desks and a control group using regular desks. The article states the trial "started in 2017 and will be completed in 2019", with outcomes assessed at baseline (T0) and five follow-up sessions (T1–T5) alternately in winter and summer over three academic years. Allocation was by coin toss after baseline assessment and informed consent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Children must follow regular primary education in third grade, have signed informed consent from parents/caregivers and the child, and be physically able to stand without serious health issues or injuries
#### Constraints
- Blinding is not possible for children, teachers and parents/caregivers, and assessor blinding proved difficult to uphold
- The study involves only one school, with potential limited external validity
- The sample of 38 children (19 per group) is most likely too small for detecting a significant difference in sitting time

### Target Learners
- primary school children aged 8–9 (grade three)

### Learning Goals
- academic performance (arithmetic, orthography, reading comprehension)
- sedentary time reduction at school
