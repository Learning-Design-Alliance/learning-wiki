# The Digital Café: a drop-in public-access computing and learning space

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Digital Café is a two-hour weekly drop-in program in a suburb of Vancouver, BC, offering free Internet, computers, and just-in-time volunteer tutor support for tasks such as email, job applications, online government forms, and social media. It began in 2008 as a Community Access Program site. The room was often full before the 3 p.m. start time with adults between 35 and 75, mostly new immigrants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteer tutors to provide one-to-one just-in-time support; a sociable, informal atmosphere (coffee, tea, snacks).
#### Constraints
- The article notes the program operated on a shoestring and that participants' security of access rested precariously on the availability of working computers and volunteer tutors.

### Target Learners
- Adults aged 35-75, mostly new immigrants, referred by employment or social service agencies or the library

### Learning Goals
- Everyday computer tasks, email, job applications, government forms, and social media use
