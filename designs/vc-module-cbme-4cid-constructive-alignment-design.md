# The module's instructional design integrated competency-based medical education, the 4CID model, and constructive alignment under Kern's six-step model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article frames its virtual consultation module with a design framework combining competency-based medical education with Kern's six-step curriculum development model. The "4CID model in SBE encompasses the following: (i) structuring learning tasks, (ii) offering supportive information, (iii) providing procedural information, and (iv) focusing on part-task practice". Constructive alignment consolidated teaching–learning and assessment strategies with the VC competencies, and the module was implemented and evaluated as Kern's steps 4, 5, and 6.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Outcome competencies must be explicitly articulated (derived from the VC-EPA competency framework) so syllabus content and learning opportunities can be aligned to them
#### Constraints
- The article describes the framework as applied to a pilot module for senior clinical students; transfer to junior students or other curricula is stated as requiring further work

### Target Learners
- Year 4 and 5 clinical medical students and fresh graduates not yet in housemanship or residency

### Learning Goals
- Virtual consultation competencies: initiating a VC, history-taking and communication, observational examination, patient management, ethics and professionalism, and concluding a VC
