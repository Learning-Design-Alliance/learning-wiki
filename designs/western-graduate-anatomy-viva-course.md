# Graduate anatomy course with four blocks contrasting feedback-based and quiz-based oral viva assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A graduate-level anatomy course at Western University for Master of Clinical Anatomy and Master of Clinical Pathologists' Assistant students, with full-body dissection over eight months and four formative oral vivas worth 20% each plus a 20% cumulative final viva. Blocks 1 and 4 used feedback-based formative assessment with weekly or daily conversational assessment and individualized written or dictated feedback; Blocks 2 and 3 used quiz-based formative assessment with set questions and numerical grades. The small annual class size of about 15 students enabled the individualized approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small annual class size of about 15 students, which the authors state made the feedback-based change possible
#### Constraints
- Oral vivas are difficult to employ in large class sizes with outsized student-to-instructor ratios, per the article

### Target Learners
- graduate anatomy students in course-based master's programs

### Learning Goals
- anatomy knowledge and application
- non-technical discipline-independent skills such as communication and autonomy
