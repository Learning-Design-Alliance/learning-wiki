# Cooperative Problem Solving (CPS): Heller's cooperative-grouping implementation of Polya's solving strategy for physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
CPS is a problem-solving teaching method in which students work in cooperative groups on text-enriched physics problems following a shared solution scheme. The article defines problem solving as "the ability of one person to cope with a problem, the latter being a new situation which requires elaborating previous knowledge and experience to achieve the solution". The method combines role differentiation within the group, division of the solving process into steps (focusing, description, planning, execution, evaluation), and a shared database of enriched problems produced by teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cooperative groups with a distinction in roles among members
- Text-enriched (rich context) problems prepared in advance
- A solution scheme dividing the work into separate steps
#### Constraints
- The article reports that preparing rich context problems requires a lot of effort
- Teachers suggested implementation in final high-school classes where student ripeness eases group management

### Target Learners
- high-school physics students (IV and V year)

### Learning Goals
- physics problem-solving skills
- critical evaluation of obtained results
