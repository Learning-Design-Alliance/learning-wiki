# Small rural districts should pool resources through cross-district consortiums to attain capacity no single district could achieve alone

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report's first lesson is "Working Stronger Together": by bringing together small, rural districts, the consortium "amassed and leveraged resources that individual districts likely would not have attained by working independently." These previously unavailable resources provided critical capacity and helped alleviate challenges in connecting students and families with resources—two common issues in rural schools. The principle applies the community schools strategy where economies of scale are unavailable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Districts with similar demographics and challenges willing to share ideas and resources; a formal structure for initiative management and collaboration.
#### Constraints
- The report notes cross-district consortium formation remains underutilized and understudied.

### Target Learners
- Students in small rural districts

### Learning Goals
- Academic achievement, attendance, and access to social and mental health supports
