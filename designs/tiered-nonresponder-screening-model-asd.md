# Tiered model in which broad-based tier-two interventions are delivered before diagnosis and non-responders receive additional screening and augmented interventions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose, based on their findings, a possible new "tiered" model for interventions such as First Step in which "broad-based groups of children with a variety of behavioral or developmental problems are not only provided with a generic tier-two intervention, but 'non-responders' are then provided with additional screening" designed to select children who might benefit from tier-two interventions augmented with components of specific evidence-based disorder interventions. The model leverages the finding that a broad-based intervention benefited children at risk of ASD without targeting the diagnosis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated broad-based tier-two intervention deliverable before formal diagnosis
- Additional screening administered to non-responders to select augmented interventions
#### Constraints
- The article offers this as a possible model suggested by the evidence, not one tested in this study

### Target Learners
- preschool children with challenging behavior, including those at risk for ASD

### Learning Goals
- prosocial academic behaviors
- school success skills
- reduction of ASD-related symptoms and impairment
