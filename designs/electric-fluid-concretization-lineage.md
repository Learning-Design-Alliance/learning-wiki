# Historical concretization lineage: from electricity as spirit, through electric fluid theories, to the water-flow analogy for electric current

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents a historical account in which electricity was first regarded as immaterial and was progressively described as a material entity. It traces Cardan's 'fatty and glutinous humor', Gray's 'electrical virtue', Du Fay's two classes of electricity, Watson's and Franklin's fluid theories, Volta's circuit, Ohm's mathematical treatment, Faraday's definition of current, and finally the water-and-pipe analogy in modern textbooks. The authors use this lineage to promote a History of Science approach for first-year undergraduates and in-service physics teachers, showing that the water analogy that describes a current in a wire was 'materialized'.
