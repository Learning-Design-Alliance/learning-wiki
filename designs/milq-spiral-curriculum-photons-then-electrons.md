# Spiral curriculum: first photons in an interferometer, then electrons at a conceptually higher level

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The milq course is organized as a spiral curriculum in two main parts. First, the quantum behavior of photons is examined with the Mach-Zehnder simulation, showing that light may show both wave and particle aspects in the same experiment and that no classical model suffices. Then electrons are studied, mainly via the double-slit experiment, treating "the same topics as with photons, but on a conceptually higher level", with the probability interpretation formulated via the wave function, which remains only an abstract entity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Key experiments with the accompanying simulation software in both parts
#### Constraints
- The discussion remains qualitative throughout; advanced topics close the course (measurement process, Schrödinger's cat and its resolution by decoherence)

### Target Learners
- Upper-secondary physics students (grade 13 Gymnasium)

### Learning Goals
- Qualitative grasp of quantum behavior, Born's probability interpretation, and limits of classical models
