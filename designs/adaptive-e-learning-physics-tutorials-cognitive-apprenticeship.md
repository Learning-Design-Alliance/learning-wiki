# Research-based adaptive interactive e-learning physics tutorials based on cognitive apprenticeship

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Three adaptive tutorials (Newton's second law, conservation of energy/work-energy theorem, conservation of angular momentum), from a set of twenty covering mechanics, electricity and magnetism, were developed via cognitive task analysis and iterative student interviews. Per the article, "The e-learning tutorials are developed to model and coach students to learn physics content and develop useful skills and wean students as they develop self-reliance." Each tutorial starts with a quantitative overarching problem broken into research-guided multiple-choice sub-problems whose incorrect options elicit common difficulties and trigger difficulty-specific feedback and help sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should first attempt the overarching problem themselves, then work sub-problems in order, making and articulating predictions before selecting answers
#### Constraints
- Performance was evaluated via paired problems on the same concepts in different contexts; tutorial lengths varied (7, 17, and 19 sub-problems)

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students in algebra-based and calculus-based courses

### Learning Goals
- expert-like systematic problem solving
- conceptual understanding of Newton's second law, energy conservation, and angular momentum conservation
