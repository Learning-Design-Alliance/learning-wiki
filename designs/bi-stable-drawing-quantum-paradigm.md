# Bi-stable drawings as an inherently dual representation of quantum objects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a pictorial paradigm in which a quantum object is represented by a bi-stable drawing, a picture that creates ambiguity between two distinct shapes and induces a spontaneous, stochastic alternation between two mutually exclusive perceptual states. The author argues this gives an inherently dual representation that everyday objects lack, letting spectators grasp superposition, random collapse and the observer effect through a direct sensorial experience rather than by analogy to classical objects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The drawing must remain intact; cutting out a single shape destroys the bistability that represents the quantum object
#### Constraints
- The analogy cannot reproduce the stability of a quantum state after collapse, since perception keeps swapping between alternatives
- The drawings only represent dual states with equal probabilities of being measured

### Target Learners
- high school students
- general population

### Learning Goals
- understanding quantum superposition
- understanding random collapse and the observer effect
- understanding wave-particle duality
