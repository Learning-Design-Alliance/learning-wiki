# Three-phased grounding script for distance student groups

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A collaboration script that "provides a format for carrying out a grounding discussion among students who have to work together at a distance and who do not know each other". It divides grounding into an input phase, in which participants individually make their knowledge and objectives explicit; a discussion phase focused on differences and inconsistencies between members; and a consensus phase in which the group resolves differences and summarizes shared knowledge. Its theoretical rationale draws on small-group work, brainstorming, and CSCW research, on the idea that students should negotiate common goals and build detailed models of each other's knowledge, skills, and motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students who do not know each other and work together at a distance on a joint assignment
- A discussion forum or comparable environment in which the scripted discussion can take place
#### Constraints
- The script pushes but does not force students to interact in a particular way, so unintended use is possible
- Too much control in the dialogue can diminish motivation and endanger learner autonomy

### Target Learners
- international distance-education students in small groups of three to six

### Learning Goals
- establishing common ground about group members' knowledge, objectives, and qualities before collaborative work
