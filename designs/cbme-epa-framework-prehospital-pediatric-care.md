# Competency-based medical education with entrustable professional activities as a structured framework to define, assess, and operationalize competencies in prehospital pediatric care

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In competency-based medical education, the gap between competencies and observable workplace tasks is addressed by entrustable professional activities, which are units of professional practice that can be entrusted to a learner once sufficient competence has been demonstrated. Incorporating EPAs into emergency medicine training aligns assessment with authentic clinical performance, supports a graduated increase in trainee autonomy, and contributes to improved patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful adaptation to the specific challenges of prehospital medicine, particularly the rarity of critical pediatric cases
#### Constraints
- Various EPAs for emergency medicine have been proposed, but their definitions and implementation remain heterogeneous and inconsistently applied across settings; EPAs have not yet been formally implemented in Germany

### Target Learners
- EMS physicians and trainees in physician-based EMS systems

### Learning Goals
- Translate abstract competency frameworks into observable clinical activities
