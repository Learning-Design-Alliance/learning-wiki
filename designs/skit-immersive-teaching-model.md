# Script Killing Immersive Teaching (SKIT) model: full-process task chain, role responsibility binding, and dynamic NPC intervention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
SKIT is a murder-mystery game–based, role-play scenario simulation approach in which rotating residents play patients, family members, doctors, and nurses under instructor guidance. The article describes three core mechanisms: "a full-process task chain (pre-hospital-in-hospital-ward full cycle), role responsibility binding (clear division of labor to avoid bystander effect), and dynamic variable intervention (NPC creates real conflicts)". Scripts are written by experienced teaching physicians and release key information in stages contingent on learner actions, with debriefing after play.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scripts compiled by experienced teaching physicians covering representative, typical, professional emergency cases
- Instructor guidance for dynamic intervention and debriefing
- Role assignment with clearly defined hierarchical tasks
#### Constraints
- Does not address technical procedural skills, per the authors' limitations
- Implemented over a 3-month emergency rotation in this study

### Target Learners
- Non-emergency resident physicians rotating through the emergency department

### Learning Goals
- Emergency clinical decision-making
- Interprofessional teamwork and communication
- Triage and disposition planning
