# Modified teaching model of multiple spaced exposures to high-fidelity simulation built on Kolb's experiential learning cycle

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops a modified version of a previously used teaching model in which each 2 h guided activity includes a briefing, a 20 min high-fidelity simulation, a first debriefing and a 30 min skill reinforcement session, followed by a second simulation of the same scenario and a final debriefing. Kolb's Experiential Learning Theory structures the phases, a "spaced learning" approach repeats a more complex scenario 15 days later, and scaffolding-inspired personalized faculty support runs through debriefing and reinforcement. The model was applied in an Italian graduate intensive care nursing course and evaluated with Kirkpatrick-level outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Graduate nurses enrolled in an intensive care program; very realistic scenarios, high student engagement, educators with deep clinical experience, and serious organizational efforts are stated as required for graduate-level simulation
- A dedicated room for the three-step debriefing and video recording of sessions
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a single Italian course with a small sample, explorative nature, and no control group, so the authors call for caution in generalizing to other educational settings

### Target Learners
- Graduate nurses enrolled in intensive care specialization courses

### Learning Goals
- Managing life-threatening conditions such as acute respiratory failure
- Advanced technical and nontechnical critical care nursing skills
