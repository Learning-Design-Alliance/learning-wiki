# Observational learning and self-regulated learning as the theoretical basis for role-based online peer assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts observational learning and self-regulated learning as the theoretical basis for its online peer assessment activities, in which students act as reviewer, author, or both. As reviewers, students "provide suggestions based on evaluation criteria, inspect and learn from others via observational learning, and then make adjustments to their own work"; as authors, they receive peer feedback and revise. Peer feedback is treated as formative scaffolding, and the process is framed as developing self-observation and self-judgment sub-processes that help learners align behavior with performance goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must assume reviewer and/or author roles with access to evaluation criteria and peer feedback during the review process
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied in a business plan writing course for fourth-year university students over approximately two months

### Target Learners
- fourth-year university students in entrepreneur management elective classes

### Learning Goals
- business plan writing quality
- self-regulated learning skills
- critical evaluation of peers' work
