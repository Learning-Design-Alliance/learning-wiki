# San Francisco Department of Early Childhood and Early Learning for All network

## Description
The San Francisco Department of Early Childhood (DEC) is a dedicated local governing agency for early childhood, reporting to the mayor, formed in 2013 and renamed in 2022 when it merged with First 5 San Francisco. It uses city funds, including Proposition C commercial rent tax revenue generating $180 million to $220 million annually with 85% dedicated to ECE, to incentivize providers to join its Early Learning for All network. In 2025 the network included 174 centers and 363 family child care providers; members must meet DEC's quality standards, accept subsidy children, and provide data, and in return receive supplemental funding, wage supplements, professional development and coaching, and family referrals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Providers must meet DEC's quality standards, which are higher than most federal and state requirements
- Providers must agree to accept children receiving child care subsidies and provide data regularly to DEC
- Dedicated local tax revenue
#### Constraints
- San Francisco is the only case study site able to raise teacher qualifications and compensation at scale, due to its local commercial tax

### Target Learners
- Children birth to age 5 in San Francisco
- Families with low and moderate incomes

### Learning Goals
- Equitable access to high-quality preschool and child care
- Workforce qualification and compensation improvements
