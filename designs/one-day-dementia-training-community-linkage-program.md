# One-day interactive dementia training program with clinical sessions, take-home tools, and community service engagement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-day training for primary care physicians and a staff member, delivered by two UNC medical school faculty (a memory disorders specialist and a geriatrician) with a Project C.A.R.E. family consultant and AAA staff. Sessions covered "evidence-based dementia screening, differential diagnosis, clinical assessment, pharmacological treatment, management of behavioral symptoms, caregiving, and community resources", were primarily discussion-based with cases, and included examples of cognitive tool administration and scoring. Participants received a binder of tools and articles, a laminated pocket card with billing codes and Project C.A.R.E. contacts, online materials, and bi-monthly e-newsletters. Overall satisfaction averaged 4.7 out of 5 (N=19).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physicians encouraged to bring an office staff member to foster team-based dementia care
- Follow-up support including e-newsletters and post-training outreach from Project C.A.R.E. personnel
#### Constraints
- Recruitment of interested physicians was the most challenging aspect; low acceptance rates suggest the strategy may need alteration to appeal to a larger audience
- Some physicians without significant geriatric populations did not feel training was warranted; the weekend day-long time commitment was reportedly off-putting

### Target Learners
- Primary care physicians
- Primary care office staff such as physician assistants, nurse practitioners, nurses, and medical assistants

### Learning Goals
- Dementia screening, diagnosis, and management competency
- Use of cognitive assessment tools
- Referral to community dementia care resources
