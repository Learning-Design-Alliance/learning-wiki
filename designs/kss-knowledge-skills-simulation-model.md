# The Knowledge, Skills, Simulation (KSS) model: a staged 28-day emergency training framework combining preliminary knowledge training, scenario simulation, and face-to-face skill practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The KSS model is a three-component emergency training framework for pediatric clinical workers comprising knowledge, skills, and simulation. The article states that "The KSS model includes three components: knowledge, skills, and simulation", delivered as "four 7-days" plus a 14-day practice period totalling 28 days. Trainees "establish theoretical schemata through preliminary knowledge training, and finally consolidate the effect through face-to-face skill practice", with long-term centralized scenario simulation in a practice-while-watching format and one-on-one departmental guidance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A three-level supervision system at hospital, section, and individual level, with hospital-level emergency skills upgrading teams supervising the simulation stage
- Training content designed by emergency and nursing experts and based on available literature and the 2019 ILCOR recommendations
#### Constraints
- Developed and tested at a single tertiary children's hospital; the authors state future research should explore the model with different participants and at other hospitals or institutions

### Target Learners
- Pediatric medical workers including nurses, doctors, and technicians in a tertiary children's hospital

### Learning Goals
- Basic life support and advanced life support emergency knowledge
- Teamwork, leadership, communication, and condition communication ability in resuscitation
