# Multisystem mapping: a hypothetical mechanism of mirror discrimination learning for letters

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a hypothetical learning model in which mirror invariance for letters is slowly overcome at school through multisensory-motor mappings between the systems representing letters. Training activities are built "based on our 'multisystem mapping' hypothetical mechanism of mirror discrimination learning for letters", aiming to "maximize mappings between systems representing letters". Beyond the auditory, visual, and writing-gesture systems traditionally involved in school letter representations, the design adds tactile perception and 'perception for action' activities to expand mappings towards the somatosensory and dorsal systems respectively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must pair multiple letter-representation systems (auditory, visual, writing gestures, tactile, dorsal) within the same sessions, using mirror-letter pairs in training groups.
#### Constraints
- The article labels the mechanism hypothetical; it is the model the intervention was designed from, not a directly tested mechanism in this protocol.

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers (first graders, 5-7 years old)

### Learning Goals
- Mirror discrimination for letters and reading fluency
