# A 1-credit team-based practical module in which teams of three solve a real problem with a quantum approach under real-data and demonstration requirements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
After the 3-credit course, weeks 13–16 form a 1-credit practical module in which "The students are asked to form teams of three and solve a real problem using any quantum approach." Common requirements are that "real input data is required" and "at least one quantum approach shall be demonstrated", with comparisons of quantum versus classical or among quantum approaches encouraged but quantum advantage not required. Weekly class sessions combine progress reports from rotating team members, expert tips from researchers or engineers, and a free Q&A session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Real input data (e.g. real finance portfolios, protein structures, medical image databases); at least one demonstrated quantum approach; a report well written in LaTeX with well-presented figures.
#### Constraints
- Teams "do not necessarily show quantum advantage over classical method", though showing it is described as better and potentially publishable.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students working in teams of three

### Learning Goals
- apply quantum approaches to real industry problems in optimization, finance, machine learning, chemistry and biology
