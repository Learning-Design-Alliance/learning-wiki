# OruMbya: webinars and cultural activities connecting sky stories across three continents

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
OruMbya (Orum, sky in Yorubá, and Mbya, a Brazilian Guarani ethnicity) is a project to 'celebrate Astronomy as the fuel of life', preserving stories of the stars across South America, Europe and Africa through scientific and cultural activities. In its first realisation, hosted by the cultural centre 'Remanescentes da Tia Ciata' and the centenary Observatory of Valongo, it ran five webinars connecting teachers, students, Indigenous, Quilombolas and Favelas representatives and astronomers, plus a plantation workshop of indigenous and Afro-Brazilian species with 15 children from a nearby favela, two online extension courses of 2 hours each for teachers, and two promotional videos.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnership among a cultural centre, an observatory and a university (UFRGS), and adaptation to varying social-distancing rules.
#### Constraints
- The project was developed under three possible degrees of COVID-19 impact on social distancing (none, moderate and total), which changed during its nine months of development; the plantation workshop was restricted to 15 children to meet distancing measures.

### Target Learners
- Teachers, students, Indigenous, Quilombolas and Favelas community representatives in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

### Learning Goals
- Cultural astronomy knowledge across Yoruba, Afro-Brazilian and Indigenous cosmogonies
- Social inclusion and sustainable development awareness
