# Classroom footprinting activity sequence: guided discussion, preliminary thermodynamic calculation, hands-on boiling measurement, and scope reflection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A classroom activity in which students estimate the footprint of an everyday item, starting with guided discussion such as "What is the carbon footprint of a mug of hot water?", then a preliminary Q = mcΔT calculation using the carbon intensity found earlier, then measurement, where students "boil water to refine their initial calculations" with timers, stove information, and Kill-A-Watt meters, and finally reflection on which processes the footprint definition includes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students are ready to footprint when they have background in energy, the back-of-the-envelope climate calculation, and "some experience working with data, uncertainty, and spreadsheets"
- For a first footprint, choose an item students know well, that is not too complicated, and that has at least one solid published study to draw from
#### Constraints
- These activities are not prescriptive and may be adapted to different courses and settings

### Target Learners
- college students in an energy course
- undergraduate statistical physics students

### Learning Goals
- quantitative estimation of carbon footprints
- awareness of the definitional choices behind footprint results
