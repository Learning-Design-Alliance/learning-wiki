# Virtual community of students and faculty across disciplines as a vehicle for knowledge transfer

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes creating a virtual community of students and faculty as the organizing framework for transferring knowledge from physics and mathematics to engineering applications. It is built on a Blackboard course-management system and Web-based communication, extending the educational impact of real-time laboratories. Within it, virtual classes let physics, mathematics, and engineering instructors "communicate with each other and work with the same students simultaneously", and students are monitored across departments, "creating a comm unity that cares about their academic success." The authors predict this mutual enrichment will increase retention and graduation rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Blackboard e-Education platform and Web-based communication systems as vehicles for instructional delivery
- Instructors of physics, mathematics, and engineering working with the same students simultaneously
#### Constraints
- The system is implemented at a single commuter institution (New York City College of Technology); the retention and graduation benefits are stated expectations, not measured results

### Target Learners
- STEM and engineering technology students at an early point in their academic careers

### Learning Goals
- Transfer of physics and mathematics knowledge to engineering problem-solving applications
