# Two idealized instructional approaches to fostering adaptive strategy use: explicit demonstration versus implicit generation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article contrasts two idealized learning environments for fostering adaptive use of strategies. The explicit approach reduces cognitive load by demonstrating and practicing strategies with explicit efficiency criteria, building a strategy repertoire and routine execution. The implicit approach capitalizes on the generation effect: children analyze number characteristics, categorize problems as "easy problems", "smart problems" or "other problems", generate their own strategies, and compare their efficiency, with the teacher acting as a mentor who avoids presenting official strategies or preferences. Both environments used the same problems and strategy comparison from the beginning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be able to generate the target strategies correctly; the article states a necessary condition for the generation effect is that students are able to generate the target information correctly
- The teacher must avoid setting socio-mathematical norms that implicitly privilege particular strategies
#### Constraints
- The generation effect does not hold for strategies too complex for students to generate, such as indirect subtraction and simplifying for third graders

### Target Learners
- primary school students (third grade, 9–10 years old)

### Learning Goals
- adaptive use of computation strategies in multi-digit addition and subtraction
