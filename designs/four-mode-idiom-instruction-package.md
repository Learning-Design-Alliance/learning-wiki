# Four-mode idiom instruction package (movie clips, sentence-use, PowerPoint definitions, role-play)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study’s treatment consisted of four ways of presenting the same 100 idioms over eight one-hour sessions, with 10 to 12 idioms taught per session per group. The Movie Group watched short clips from Everyday English Idioms on YouTube and guessed meanings; the Sentence-use Group wrote sentences with corrective feedback; the Definitions Group worked through 100 PowerPoint slides showing each idiom and its definition; the Role-play Group practiced and performed short conversational stories containing the idioms. All groups received the L1 (Persian) equivalent of each idiom.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The same idioms must be taught through each tool to keep conditions comparable; teaching/learning time was kept the same across groups.
#### Constraints
- Participants were 47 volunteers aged 15–22 from two language institutes, homogeneously upper-intermediate/advanced, all sharing Azeri L1 and Persian L2.

### Target Learners
- upper-intermediate/advanced adolescent and young-adult EFL learners

### Learning Goals
- idiomatic competence (comprehension and use of English idioms)
