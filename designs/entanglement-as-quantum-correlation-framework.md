# Entanglement as a quantum correlation beyond classical physics, detected by a Bell test on joint outcomes of independent parties

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The demonstration's explanatory framework holds that "entanglement between two quantum particles can be operationally defined as a kind of additional correlation between the quantum states of the particles that cannot be explained by the laws of classical physics," detectable in a Bell test by analyzing joint outcomes of independent parties. Coherent superpositions arise because "indistinguishability of pathways leads to coherent superpositions," and Alice's measurement projects the partner particle's state, reducing Bob's possible outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors introduce the two-particle quantum state, coherent superposition and projective measurement on a whiteboard or slides before defining entanglement operationally
#### Constraints
- The simulated Bell test uses a single measurement direction, unlike real quantum key distribution where measurements are carried out along random orientations

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade Chilean high-school students
- high-school students in other contexts

### Learning Goals
- operational understanding of quantum entanglement
- understanding coherent superposition and projective measurement
