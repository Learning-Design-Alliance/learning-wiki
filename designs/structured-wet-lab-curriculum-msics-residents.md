# Structured wet-laboratory curriculum for ophthalmology residents (foam suturing then goat-eye steps before supervised operating)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A stepwise pre-operative skills curriculum introduced in March 2013 at the study institute, which residents had to complete before entering the operating theater. It begins with hand–eye coordination training, described as "suturing on foam under microscope (3-step, Leica microscope) for two classes", followed by goat-eye practice of "the scleral tunnel, entry into anterior chamber and capsulotomy for 6 weeks (12 Goat's eye each)", then two months of wet-laboratory training before supervised surgery and regular monthly training of 6 h/month during the first two residency years.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residents complete the wet-laboratory curriculum before being allowed to operate on patients
- Continued monthly wet-laboratory training of 6 h/month during the first two years of residency
- Supervised operating in the theater after wet-laboratory training
#### Constraints
- The article notes the cost involved in setting up and maintaining the wet laboratory as a pitfall

### Target Learners
- Ophthalmology residents (first- to third-year) in a postgraduate training program

### Learning Goals
- Hand–eye coordination and microsurgical skills for manual small-incision cataract surgery
- Mastery of cataract surgery steps (scleral tunnel, anterior chamber entry, capsulotomy) before live surgery
