# Run-off length as an accessible observable for sticking versus separation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The activity's core measurement design: students record the run-off length, "defined as the vertical distance from the lip to the point where the water stream visibly detaches from the solid surface". This observable is easy to define, easy to measure, and clearly related to the strength of sticking, allowing quantitative comparison of velocity and wettability effects without extracting ejection angles or fluid-dynamical modeling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A waterproof millimeter scale adhered vertically to the outer wall of the cup
#### Constraints
- The activity does not attempt a full quantitative comparison with theoretical predictions for ejection angle or critical Weber number

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- measuring and interpreting flow separation quantitatively

### Claims
- Flow Velocity Reduces Teapot Runoff Length [+M]
- Wettability Reduces Teapot Sticking [+M]
