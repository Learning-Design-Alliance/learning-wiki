# Four-stage coaching cycle applied to teaching history of visual arts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article adopts the Parsloe and Wray conception of coaching as "a process furthering implementation of training and development and consequently improvement of the trainee's competence and professional skills", structured in four basic stages: goal setting, reality check, courses of action, and will to act. In History of Visual Arts classes, students set a goal such as mastering the historical periods of visual arts, assess their initial knowledge via questionnaires and self-study, seek courses of action in collaboration with the professor coach, and then carry out planned activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A professor acting as coach with whom students seek courses of action in collaboration
- An initial knowledge assessment (questionnaires and self-study) to establish the reality check stage
#### Constraints

### Target Learners
- higher education students in art and design programs

### Learning Goals
- knowledge of historical periods of visual arts
- representative artists of each period and their best-known works
