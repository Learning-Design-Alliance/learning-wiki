# Five-session PBL implementation with ill-structured acid-base problem scenarios

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An instructional element in which first-year pre-service teachers in five small groups worked through an ill-structured, real-life problem scenario about children drinking corrosive substances over five sessions: analysing the scenario, listing key issues, defining the problem state, forming and revising hypotheses, deciding on the best solution, and presenting a report. The article states "The teaching of the acid-base topic was presented in five sessions through the PBL in the experimental group", with the researcher acting as facilitator who guided "with questions without giving any information".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ill-structured problems reflecting real life, presented through scenarios, selected as critical to PBL success
- Small-group learning arrangements with active participation of each student
#### Constraints
- Implemented with first-year pre-service teachers newly acquainted with the approach, in the acid-base chemistry topic only

### Target Learners
- first-year pre-service science teachers

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking disposition
- perception of problem-solving ability
- acid-base chemistry content
