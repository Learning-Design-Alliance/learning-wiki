# Proactive intensive form-focused instruction (FFI)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Proactive intensive FFI is a type of form-focused instruction in which the teacher selects demanding target forms before instruction and treats a limited number of features in depth. In this study it comprised three components: "metalinguistic explanations, i.e. explicit explanations of the grammar", "repeated form-focused tasks", and "explicit corrective feedback on form", delivered within free discussion classes over nine sessions targeting nine verb tenses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must be embedded in meaning-focused communicative classes, integrating form with meaning as the article's FFI framework prescribes.
- A limited set of target forms must be selected prior to instruction and treated intensively across repeated tasks.
#### Constraints
- The study tested it only with intermediate and advanced adult Persian-speaking EFL learners in a four-week free discussion program.

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and advanced adult EFL learners

### Learning Goals
- Grammatical knowledge of English verb tenses
