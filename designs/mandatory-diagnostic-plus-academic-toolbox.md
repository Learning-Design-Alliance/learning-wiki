# Mandate registration for at-risk students, pair diagnostic assessment with feedback, and build an academic toolbox honed through multiple application opportunities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors attribute the SSC's success to three design characteristics: compulsory registration (since students rarely seek out free support services voluntarily), diagnostic assessment with individual feedback that makes students aware of their own strengths and weaknesses, and an academic toolbox of cognitive, metacognitive, and self-governance skills practiced repeatedly. As they state: "We believe that the following characteristics of the SSC underlie its success as a student support tool: mandatory program registration, diagnostic assessme nt with feedback, and development of an “academic toolbox” of skills honed through multiple application opportunities." The assessment feedback also framed academic performance as changeable with effort, which the authors connect to Dweck's growth mindset.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough time for attitudes toward mandatory attendance to become positive (the article's program duration was 14 weeks)
- Instructors who provide guidance and constructive feedback throughout the semester, allowing relationship building
#### Constraints
- Mandatory course registration and payment of associated fees were not initially well received by most students, though attitudes became more positive with time

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students on academic probation or otherwise at risk

### Learning Goals
- Awareness of one's own learning strategy strengths and weaknesses
- Adoption of more sophisticated cognitive and metacognitive strategies
- Personal responsibility for learning
