# Lecture demonstration combining simulation and hands-on optical experiments for teaching signal-processing mathematics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 7 studies (3 causal, 2 review, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 7 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper's teaching pattern pairs numerical simulations with physical optical demonstration experiments so students can see abstract operations concretely. Optical implementations include a Mach-Zehnder interferometric arrangement for amplitude addition, a converging-lens setup demonstrating the convolution theorem, a 4f setup for the Abbe-Porter experiment and spatial filtering, and joint-transform correlation recording and replay setups. The author reports that "This method of teaching using simulation and hands-on experiments can stimulate the curiosity of the students" and that student feedback "has been quite encouraging".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Optical components: collimated laser beam, grating transparencies, beam-splitters, converging FT lenses, and a photographic plate or digital camera for recording joint Fourier transforms
#### Constraints
- The optical demonstrations require coherent optics laboratory equipment, which the paper describes in its setups but does not claim is available in all teaching contexts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate electrical, electronics and communication engineering students

### Learning Goals
- understanding convolution and correlation
- understanding spatial filtering and the convolution theorem
