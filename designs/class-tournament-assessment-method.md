# Class tournament assessment method with staged team questions and self-/peer-assessment questionnaires

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A summative assessment format in which a class is randomly divided into teams that compete through staged physics tasks: 1-point simple open questions, 2-point multiple-choice questions requiring explanations, a 3-point open question versus 2-point multiple-choice risk choice, 4-point calculus and practical experimental tasks, and a 10-point common extra task. Final marks combine the group percentage result (weight 0.5), questionnaire-based "subject matter contribution" (0.3) and "communication skills" (0.2), with a median-based algorithm penalizing inconsistent self- versus peer-assessments downward to "reduce a risk of „collusion” among the students".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two independent teachers monitored each stage to verify "verity and integrity of the student evaluation"; students were familiarized with the scoring algorithm before the tournament.
#### Constraints
- The authors state organizational difficulties concern predominantly the teacher: preparing and conducting a tournament is "by far more conceptually demanding and therefore time-consuming" than traditional testing, and a full-scale event may need two or three times a single lesson hour.

### Target Learners
- final-year K-12 high-school physics students

### Learning Goals
- physics content knowledge in electricity
- scientific reasoning
- group-work and communication skills
- honest self- and peer-assessment
