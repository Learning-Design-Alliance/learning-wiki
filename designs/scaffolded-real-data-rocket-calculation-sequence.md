# Scaffolded task sequence from a simple momentum analogy to full Saturn V parameter calculations with real telemetry data

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The unit's task set is ordered from simple to complex: an astronaut 'shot-put' in weightlessness applies the rocket equation to a 10 kg mass thrown at 10 m/s; next students plot the Apollo 11 flight profile from a printed telemetry table; then they compute first-stage propellant mass flow, end-of-burn velocity and thrust; then second-stage tank heights from propellant masses and densities; then Earth-rotation velocity at Cape Canaveral added to orbital speed; and finally the gentleness of the trans-lunar injection burn. Each task uses realistic figures the handout attributes to Orloff (2000).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worksheets with the data tables and the stated physics/mathematics prerequisites
#### Constraints
- The calculations rest on the unit's simplifying assumptions (vertical ascent, constant w and µ, constant g)

### Target Learners
- students in grades 10–13

### Learning Goals
- applying the rocket equation with realistic parameters
- interpreting real mission telemetry
- estimating geometric and rotational quantities
