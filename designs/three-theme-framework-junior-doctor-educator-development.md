# Three-theme framework: pedagogical toolbox, professional legitimacy, and clinical teacher identity.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's qualitative content analysis yielded three overarching themes describing junior doctors' experiences of the pedagogical internship track. The article states: "We identified three main themes: Theme 1, Building a pedagogical toolbox... Theme 2, Establishing professional legitimacy... Finally, Theme 3, Becoming a clinical teacher." This framework links skill acquisition (simulation, feedback, orchestration), social recognition (credibility, networks, influence), and identity formation (confidence, taking on teaching roles) as interconnected dimensions of early-career educator development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Qualitative content analysis per Graneheim and Lundman of semi-structured interviews with former participants
#### Constraints
- Findings based on ten former participants at a single Swedish non-university hospital; perspectives of current interns, students, and other professional groups were not included

### Target Learners
- Junior doctors in postgraduate medical training

### Learning Goals
- Understanding how structured teaching programs shape teaching skills, professional legitimacy, and educator identity
