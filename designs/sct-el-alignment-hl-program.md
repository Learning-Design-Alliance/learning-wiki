# Social Cognitive Theory and experiential learning theory jointly underpin the Healthy Living program design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The HL program was underpinned by Social Cognitive Theory combined with experiential learning theory. The article states "The activities were underpinned by Social Cognitive Theory (SCT)", mapping SCT constructs (knowledge, self-efficacy, observational learning, reinforcement, modelling, environmental support) onto Kolb's EL phases: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation and active experimentation. Table 1 aligns art, storytime, discovery and physical-activity activities with these constructs and four key messages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must provide concrete hands-on experiences plus reflection, conceptualisation and real-world application phases
#### Constraints
- The alignment is a design rationale; the study did not test SCT constructs as outcomes

### Target Learners
- children aged 0-10 years attending a children's museum with their caregivers

### Learning Goals
- healthy eating knowledge and behaviour
- physical activity participation
