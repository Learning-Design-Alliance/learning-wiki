# Use computer-supported multi-sound notation and vocalization programs instead of block flute accompaniment when teaching songs in primary music education.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Instead of using the block flute as an accompaniment instrument in teaching songs, using multi-sound accompaniment prepared in the computer is more effective" across all five evaluated singing behaviors. It argues this contributes "easy application and higher success rate" and can fill the gap of rarely available multi-sound instruments in Turkish primary schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computer supported notation and vocalization program with a multi-sound arrangement of the target song, used through aural-song training method
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single song ("Bir Dünya Bırakın") taught to 5th, 6th and 7th graders in Burdur primary schools over 15-minute sessions

### Target Learners
- Primary school students in grades 5-7

### Learning Goals
- Singing songs accurately in melody, rhythm, lay-rhythm, intonation and as a whole
