# Sequential simulation as a linked patient-journey design with team-based participation and interprofessional exposure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article defines sequential simulation as a design that "integrates environments, actors, and scenarios to recreate clinical pathways and the entire patient journey, deepening understanding of the healthcare system and patient needs". In mCLEIMS, the sequence was longitudinal at the curriculum level: students repeatedly encountered patient needs, system transitions, and team-based decision points, with simulation patients, facilitators, and nonphysician professionals maintaining continuity of clinical narrative across stations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation patients, facilitators, and nonphysician healthcare professionals maintain continuity of clinical narrative, role expectations, and feedback across stations
#### Constraints
- Fragmented scenarios with limited continuity may not adequately reflect real-world patient care, per the article's rationale for sequential design

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical clerkship

### Learning Goals
- understanding the healthcare system and patient needs
- teamwork
- integrated patient care
