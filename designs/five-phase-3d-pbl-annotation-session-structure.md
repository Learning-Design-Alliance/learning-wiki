# Five-phase integrated 3D-PBL-annotation session structure for spinal surgery teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The experimental teaching followed five structured phases: pre-session preparation with clinical cases and endoscopy videos distributed one week ahead; session initiation with self-organized groups of 8–10 students and designated leaders; instructor-guided exploration using Complete Anatomy 3D manipulation and QuPath annotation; facilitated discussion with group presentations and cross-group interrogation; and a conclusion with faculty synthesis and resolution of contentious concepts. The design embeds "guided inquiry, self-directed learning, and interactive discussion" within a technology-augmented PBL workflow.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Advance distribution of cases and videos one week prior
- Groups of 8–10 students with a designated leader
- Access to Complete Anatomy and QuPath during sessions
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in spinal surgery (lumbar disc herniation) teaching at a single institution

### Target Learners
- undergraduate clinical medicine students in surgical rotations

### Learning Goals
- clinical case analysis
- spatial anatomical understanding
- collaborative problem-solving
