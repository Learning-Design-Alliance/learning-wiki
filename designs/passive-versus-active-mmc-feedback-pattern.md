# Two interventions of increasing complexity built on MMC output: passive wide dissemination versus active in situ simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The trial contrasts two interventions layered on standard Morbidity and Mortality conferences. Passive feedback widely disseminates the MMC agenda, decided areas for improvement, and monthly monitoring of progress by e-mail and/or paper to all health professionals in the unit. Active feedback additionally broadens the choice of events analysed (using trigger-detected potential adverse events) and adds the in situ simulation programme. The article describes them as "two interventions of increasing complexity": passive feedback is simple to implement with limited expected impact but very good exportability, while active feedback has a more important expected effect but more limited exportability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Passive feedback requires a local MMC coordinator to choose the case and manage monthly monitoring of implementation of previous objectives
#### Constraints
- The article states active feedback's exportability is more limited to date, though simulation training is developing rapidly in other institutions

### Target Learners
- all medical and paramedical professionals in hospital wards

### Learning Goals
- appropriation of corrective measures from MMC analyses and error prevention
