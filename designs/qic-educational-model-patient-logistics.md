# Quality improvement collaborative (QIC) as an educational model combining learning sessions, change packages, extranet sharing and on-site support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes the QIC as an improvement-and-education method in which, per Øvretveit et al. as quoted, "a collaborative [that] brings together groups of practitioners from different healthcare organizations to work in a structured way to improve a specific aspect of the quality of their service." Most QICs use the IHI Breakthrough methodology. The studied QICs' educational approach organized learning sessions taught by change experts and subject experts, supported by change packages (booklets describing ten distinctive phases with PowerPoint files and communication tools), a collaborative extranet, and QIC staff site visits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Series of structured meetings to learn best practices, quality methods and change topics, and to share experiences of local changes
#### Constraints
- The studied QICs focused on patient logistics: Advanced Access aimed to reduce outpatient access time to a maximum of two days; Process Redesign aimed to reduce throughput time for admitted patients by at least 20%

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in hospitals participating in quality improvement collaboratives

### Learning Goals
- identifying and resolving bottlenecks in patient flow
- balancing demand and supply
- redesigning care processes
