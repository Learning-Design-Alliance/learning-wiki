# Acoustic–quantum analogy: the straw pipe as a formal model of the infinite square well

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The framework maps the acoustics of a pipe open at both ends onto a particle in an infinite square potential well: both systems are described by waves confined in a finite region, and the D'Alembert equation for straw pressure is "formally identical to the stationary Schrödinger equation for a particle in an infinite square potential well, a first approximation of the hydrogen atom potential". Pressure mode shapes mirror the wavefunctions of the energy levels, boundary conditions are the same (pressure or wavefunction zero at the boundaries), and quantization of k yields frequency quantization in the pipe and energy quantization in the well. The analogy serves as a conceptual bridge before formal treatment and grounds discussion of the Copenhagen interpretation and Heisenberg uncertainty relations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both systems must be describable in terms of waves and spatially confined; the analogy is introduced after students have measured the straw's quantized spectrum
#### Constraints
- The infinite square well is only a first approximation of the hydrogen atom potential; the sine-only solution is not the most general form, though the article states this does not alter the eigen-states or eigenvalues

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary students

### Learning Goals
- understanding why energy quantization arises in bound quantum systems
- introducing the wave function and its probabilistic interpretation
- a first glimpse of the Heisenberg uncertainty relations
