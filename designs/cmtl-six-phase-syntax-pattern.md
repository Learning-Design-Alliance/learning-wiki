# Six-phase lesson sequence for contextual mathematical thinking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern with six ordered syntax steps: (1) presentation of contextual problems; (2) asking critical and analytical questions; (3) individual and group investigation; (4) presentation and discussion; (5) reflection; and (6) a higher-order thinking test. Each step has specified teacher and student activities and an allocated time, moving from lower-order questions that "lead to a higher-order thinking questions" and from individual problem-solving to group discussion and public presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contextual problems presented via pictures, stories or videos that enable critical, analytical and creative thinking
- Worksheets for individual investigation followed by group discussion
- Time allocations per step as set out in the syntax table
#### Constraints
- Designed and tested for middle school mathematics (Grade VIII geometry topics)

### Target Learners
- Grade VIII junior high school students

### Learning Goals
- higher-order thinking ability in mathematics
