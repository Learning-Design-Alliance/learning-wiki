# Integrative and interdisciplinary learning modules are effective for broadening students' perspectives on engineering and its role in society

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that integrative learning modules, designed to connect curricular content with career expectations and engage holistic problem-solving, effectively broaden first-year students' perceptions of engineering. The Water Module required students to "explore controversial social issues and evaluate how these issues can influence the success of an engineered solution," integrating engineering calculations with humanities-based data sources like stakeholder interviews and government documents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Module must be broken down into components and learning tasks throughout the semester requiring data collection and thorough analysis
#### Constraints
- Students self-selected into courses, so differences cannot be solely linked to content and pedagogy
- Professor style, gender, practices, research, and department could have influenced results
- One course alone may not solve limitations in the entire curriculum

### Target Learners
- First-semester, first-year college students of any major

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the engineering profession's interdisciplinary nature and societal impact
- Developing holistic approaches to solving complex problems
