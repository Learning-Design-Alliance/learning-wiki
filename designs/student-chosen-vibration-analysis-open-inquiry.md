# Open-inquiry lab task pattern: student-chosen everyday periodic signal analyzed with smartphone accelerometer and DFT

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The task follows "a skills-based approach to laboratory work, emphasizing the role of open inquiry and learning experimental skills such as experimental design and data analysis over cookbook-style closed instructions". Students pick a periodic signal at home or on campus (e.g. washing machine spin-dry, phone vibration alarm, own heart rate), design the measurement, collect accelerometer data via phyphox, and analyze it with a DFT in the notebook. The design and data collection are left open for students to decide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a smartphone with an accelerometer and the phyphox app, and the Jupyter notebook with Python analysis tools
#### Constraints
- Example contexts are intentionally relatively simple so attention stays on the data-analysis method; the task suits distance-learning and on-campus settings

### Target Learners
- beyond-first-year university physics students

### Learning Goals
- experimental design
- data analysis
- digital signal processing
