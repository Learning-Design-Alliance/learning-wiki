# Set of commercial video educational games used with a linguistic approach over 36 sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention consisted of commercial games arranged into a 36-session sequence: Upwords, which "increases the English vocabulary of learners and helps them to create sentences"; strategy games (Quarto, Quoridor, Pentago, Ab alone); the Civilization and Anno series presenting historical content through English; and Scribblenauts, in which "a name and an adjective appears after writing each combination". Students played them at home during the summer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students needed access to a personal PC at home; for those without one, the school computer site was used on Mondays for two hours
#### Constraints
- Games were played at home "due to the lack of equipment in the school's computer site"

### Target Learners
- 2nd grade male high school students (ages 16-17) learning English

### Learning Goals
- English vocabulary, sentence creation, spelling, and recalling of textbook concepts
