# Ainsworth's DeFT framework applied to video motion analysis: complementary, constraining, and integrating functions of multiple representations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article uses Ainsworth's DeFT (Design, Functions, Tasks) taxonomy, in which "two or more external representations are used simultaneously". It maps three functions onto video analysis: representations complement each other (stroboscopic imaging plus motion diagrams), constrain each other's interpretation (the familiar real motion sequence constrains the less familiar strobe picture), and support integration of corresponding information (decomposing two-dimensional motion into one-dimensional movements with time-position and time-velocity graphs).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be able to switch between representations as needed and view them in combination on one device
#### Constraints
- Multiple representations can increase cognitive load, so the environment must manage load carefully

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of uniform motion
- representational competence in kinematics
