# Social judgement theory with judgement analysis as the basis for capturing and teaching expert prognostic judgement policies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The intervention's design rests on social judgement theory, which "assumes that judgements (prognostic decisions) result from the integration of different types of information, known as 'cues'". Judgement analysis captures an expert's 'judgement policy' using a multiple regression procedure to calculate the relative weights the expert attaches to different cues. The authors previously applied it to expert palliative care doctors and now teach the resulting cue weights to students, an approach used successfully outside palliative care to train student populations to make decisions more aligned to experts'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prior judgement analysis study identifying which cues experts weight most heavily, used as the content of the training
#### Constraints
- High levels of discrimination and consistency "do not guarantee accuracy", so expertise measures are evaluated alongside agreement with expert estimates

### Target Learners
- UK medical students in the penultimate or final year

### Learning Goals
- Aligning prognostic decisions with expert judgement policies
