# Applying the linguistic theory of humour to grammar teaching through metaphor, pun and ambiguity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's organizing framework is the application of the linguistic theory of humour to explaining a difficult grammar topic. The researchers "worked in applying the linguistic devices of humour: metaphor, pun, and ambiguity", using these devices to explain inherent and non-inherent adjectives and to design "humorous, funny and incongruous activities which evoke laughter". The theory holds that humour arises from incongruity that learners perceive and resolve, and that resolving it focuses attention on the instructional content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must be able to perceive and resolve humour; the article states effectiveness "depends on the students' ability to perceive and then resolve the incongruity in a humorous instruction"
- Humour must be relevant to the course content and appropriate in form
#### Constraints
- The method "is very much dependent on the teacher's creativity" and "takes the most time and creativity on the part of the teacher"
- Styles of humour are culture-dependent, so humour must fit the learners' culture

### Target Learners
- EFL university students learning English grammar

### Learning Goals
- Understanding inherent and non-inherent adjectives
- Retention and recall of grammar rules
