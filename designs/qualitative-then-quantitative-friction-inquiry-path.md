# Inquiry-based friction learning path: qualitative exploration with prediction-test cycles followed by quantitative verification experiments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A laboratory learning path that begins with an inquiry-based exploratory phase in which students predict, test and re-predict the sliding behaviour of common materials, then moves to quantitative experiments verifying Leonardo's laws. The article states "A quantitative laboratory followed the qualitative exploration" using the same materials. Activities use low-cost materials, a detailed worksheet, and small-group experiments in which each student produces a report determining static friction coefficients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sliding friction and Leonardo's laws introduced, discussed and assessed by the teacher in previous school time
- A detailed worksheet guiding the roughly 90-minute qualitative exploration
- Teachers available to provide clarification during animated discussion
#### Constraints
- Wooden-block measurements may be inconsistent due to light load and lack of surface hardness
- Students need more solid training in lab as prerequisite for the quantitative extension

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students, ages 15-19

### Learning Goals
- understanding sliding and static friction
- distinguishing relevant variables (load, contact area, roughness)
- connecting macroscopic friction to microscopic electromagnetic interaction
- measurement and uncertainty evaluation
