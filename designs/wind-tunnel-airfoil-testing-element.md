# Elementary wind tunnel in which students design, test, and revise airfoils

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A demonstration device for the Air and Aviation unit, built at modest cost by Penn State physics graduate student John Huckans, in which 'students construct airfoils of their own design and test them in the wind tunnel' and then change their designs based on observations from initial trials. The project provoked much discussion, including 'ongoing debates about the importance of the Bernoulli force in really keeping planes in the air' among the team and participating undergraduates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A wind tunnel must be built or obtained; the article reports one was constructed at a modest cost by a physics graduate student.
#### Constraints
- Team members report ongoing debate about the importance of the Bernoulli force in keeping planes in the air, so the underlying conceptual explanation remains contested within the project.

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in grades 3 and 4

### Learning Goals
- Understanding lift and flight concepts
- Participating in elements of scientific inquiry through design, testing, and revision
