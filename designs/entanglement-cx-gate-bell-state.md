# Entanglement and the CX gate as taught content in the unit

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The unit treats two-qubit systems qualitatively: a general two-qubit state is a four-component real vector, and "Eine Besonderheit der Quanteninformation ist nun, dass es Zwei-QuBit-Zustände gibt, die sich nicht als TensorproduktvonzweiEin-QuBit-Zuständendarstellenlassen. Wir nennen diese Zustände verschränkt." The CX gate (controlled X) transforms the product state |+⟩⊗|0⟩ into the Bell state, which cannot be visualized by two unit circles — showing that a two-qubit state is more than two single-qubit states.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A coefficient-comparison proof that the Bell state is not a tensor product can be worked out in class as an exercise.
#### Constraints
- The mathematical details of the tensor-product Hilbert space are deliberately not treated; the unit concentrates on the qualitative difference between qubits and bits arising through entanglement.

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary informatics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding entanglement as the qualitative difference between qubits and classical bits
- applying the two-qubit CX gate
