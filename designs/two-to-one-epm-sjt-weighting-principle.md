# Weight foundation programme selection approximately 2:1 in favour of EPM decile over SJT score, and reconsider the role of educational achievements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This principle holds that applicant ranking for the UK foundation programme should give greater weight to EPM decile than SJT score, and should not rely on the educational achievements score for predicting completion. The article reports that "Rather our results would suggest a 2:1 weighting ratio between the EPM decile and the SJT score may be the optimum, if the aim is to rank and advantage candidates most likely to successfully complete the foundation programme." It rests on the finding that EPM decile is roughly twice as predictive per decile as SJT, and that educational achievements adds no incremental value beyond the other two measures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection outcomes must be measured against a completion-type criterion such as ARCP outcome 6, and both predictors must be placed on a comparable metric (deciles) before weights are set.
#### Constraints
- The article cautions the outcome is circumscribed: completion captures only the lower end of performance, and selectors may also wish to advantage applicants likely to succeed in long-term careers, for which academic records may matter.

### Target Learners
- UK medical school graduates applying to the foundation programme

### Learning Goals
- Valid selection and ranking of applicants most likely to successfully complete postgraduate medical training
