# Research-based Bloch sphere tutorial with isometric-projection illustrations and QuVis visualization support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A research-based tutorial on the Bloch sphere developed, validated, and implemented in an interdisciplinary undergraduate course, "Foundations of Quantum Computing and Quantum Information," the only mandatory course in an undergraduate certificate program. It has three sections — construction, measurements, and geometric intuition — and was iterated with think-aloud interviews of four students spanning roughly fifteen hours plus faculty feedback. Its illustrations use "a method resembling isometric projection" with color-coded Cartesian axes, supplemented by computer-generated QuVis graphics students used to verify predictions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administered after traditional lecture-based instruction on the requisite concepts
- Only prerequisites are Calculus I and II
#### Constraints
- Mixed states are outside the scope of the targeted introductory level

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students from all science and engineering majors

### Learning Goals
- constructing and depicting single-qubit states on the Bloch sphere
- identifying measurement outcomes and calculating probabilities
- developing geometric intuition about sets of states
