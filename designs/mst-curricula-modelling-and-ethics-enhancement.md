# Enhance engineering curricula with mathematical modelling methodology and research-and-engineering ethics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is that evolution of engineering curricula, rather than isolated MS&T courses, should include the general methodology of mathematical modelling of material entities, procedures for using mathematical models in conceptual and functional design of measuring systems, strategies of responsible use of AI tools, and elements of research-and-engineering ethics enabling dialogue between engineers and professional ethicists. Scope and complexity should fit the instruction level, being more basic and illustrative at B.Sc. level and more advanced and abstract at Ph.D. level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Changes should be fit to the local educational environment; syllabus changes may serve as an anticipatory step before whole-curriculum reform
- Student use of AI tools should follow the assistant demarcation: the tool helps generate ideas and polish drafts but does not replace students in final decisions or dictate the core contents
#### Constraints
- A considerable inertia of curricula evolution is characteristic of most academic institutions, which the article says cannot justify procrastination

### Target Learners
- B.Sc. to Ph.D. students in measurement science and technology and related engineering fields

### Learning Goals
- mathematical modelling
- responsible use of AI tools
- research-and-engineering ethics
