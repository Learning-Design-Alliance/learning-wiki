# A European CPT core curriculum with clear learning outcomes, early real‐life prescribing experience, and a robust assessment structure should be developed

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that undergraduate CPT education be restructured around a core curriculum with defined outcomes and practical prescribing experience. Specifically, it suggests "a European CPT curriculum should be developed with clear learning outcomes, emphasis on gaining early experience of drug prescribing for real patients in a clinical setting and completing prescriptions" alongside a robust European assessment structure ("European Prescribing License") to ensure outcomes are met. This responds directly to the study's finding of widespread inadequate prescribing competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear learning outcomes for CPT education
- Emphasis on early experience of drug prescribing for real patients in a clinical setting
- A robust assessment structure to ensure outcomes are met
#### Constraints
- Curricular change is described as a slow and demanding process

### Target Learners
- final‐year medical students
- medical graduates entering clinical practice

### Learning Goals
- safe and effective drug prescribing
- knowledge of drug interactions and contraindications
- rational drug selection
