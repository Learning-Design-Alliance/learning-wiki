# Non-standard chain-rule approach to deriving the SHM solution

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An instructional framework for introducing simple harmonic motion as a completely solvable example of Newton's second law, rather than as solving a second order non-homogeneous differential equation. The approach rewrites the acceleration term via the chain rule as v dv/dz, integrates to obtain a conserved-quantity form, and uses inverse trigonometric functions to reach the familiar sinusoidal solution. The author states it 'only requires knowledge of the chain rule for derivatives and of standard integration techniques'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students in a first calculus-based introductory physics course already know the chain rule for derivatives, inverse trigonometric functions, and basic integration methods
#### Constraints
- Presented for the vertical spring-mass system driven by Hooke's Law; the author notes the same equation arises for small pendulum oscillations and partially submerged objects

### Target Learners
- students in a first calculus-based introductory university physics course

### Learning Goals
- derive the SHM solution through mathematical reasoning rather than memorization
- apply calculus and trigonometry knowledge to physics problems
