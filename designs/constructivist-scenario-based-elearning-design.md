# Constructivist scenario-based eLearning design with authentic tasks and embedded reflective questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course is designed on a constructivist view of learning as "an active process of constructing knowledge," using two key design strategies: (1) scenario- and task-based learning activities using authentic cases to support acquiring, linking, integration, and application of knowledge and skills, and (2) activities embedded within content, including revisable reflective questions and conversational style to promote active reflection and cognitive dialogue. The course incorporates aspects of problem-based learning and situated learning with authentic tasks, delivered through 3 interactive modules using rich media that runs on low-specification computers with limited internet speed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive, scenario-based design with authentic cases
- Content conforming to WHO case-management guidelines
- Accessibility on low-specification computers with limited internet speed, with a CD version for areas without internet access
#### Constraints
- No support or assistance was given to participants while taking the course
- Course was originally implemented in Adobe Flash (being reimplemented in html5)

### Target Learners
- In-service health professionals working with undernourished children
- Preservice health science trainees
- Educators and trainers in medical, nursing, and health science schools

### Learning Goals
- Identification and classification of malnutrition
- Assessment and screening of children for malnutrition
- Management of malnourished children using WHO Ten Steps
- Differentiating chronic and acute malnutrition
- Supporting mothers and caregivers to prevent recurrence
