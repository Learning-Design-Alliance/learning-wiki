# Structured student MCQ-writing task with per-module quotas, quality ratings and scaffolding

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reusable course-level design in which each student creates at least two MCQs per topic module and correctly answers at least twenty peer-generated MCQs per module. Each MCQ comprises "a stem, one correct answer and three or four plausible distractors" with required explanations justifying the correct option and distractors. Questions are peer-rated on a six-point scale, tagged by topic, and a 30-minute scaffolding session introduces the rationale and question-writing guidance before implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 30-min instructional scaffolding session covering pedagogical rationale and technical support
- Timetabled class time for authoring/answering within one to two weeks of relevant teaching
- Course credit allocation (here 20% of the final grade) to support compulsory participation
#### Constraints
- Peer feedback via rating and commenting was strongly encouraged but not required, and engagement remained shallow
- The authors note the high cognitive load required for task completion may have seemed excessive to students

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- deep learning including knowledge analysis, evaluation, and creation
- preparation for high-stakes MCQ examinations
