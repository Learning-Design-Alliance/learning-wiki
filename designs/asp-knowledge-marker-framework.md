# Knowledge markers (A/S/P): a lightweight, AI-agnostic course-level labelling scheme distinguishing Application, Structure, and Procedure knowledge

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Knowledge markers are a lightweight, AI-agnostic course-level labelling scheme in which each learning unit carries one primary emphasis: (A) Application knowledge "refers to the ability to implement concrete solutions", (S) Structure knowledge to conceptual understanding and mental models, and (P) Procedure knowledge to systematic approaches such as debugging and verification. The article maps the markers onto Krathwohl's revised taxonomy as orientation and communicates them to students through a screw-tightening metaphor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Labels must be communicated as part of the course culture, paired with explicit explanations and examples such as the screw metaphor.
#### Constraints
- A/S/P is intentionally coarse
- It depends on consistent interpretation across authors
- The marker denotes a primary intent, not exclusivity

### Target Learners
- first-semester engineering students with little or no prior programming experience
- instructors without formal didactic training

### Learning Goals
- implementing concrete solutions (Application)
- building conceptual understanding and mental models (Structure)
- systematic debugging and verification (Procedure)
