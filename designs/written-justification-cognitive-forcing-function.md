# Strategic friction via required written justification as a cognitive forcing function for critical AI uptake

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adapts the HCI principle of strategic friction to academic writing by requiring students to articulate the reasoning behind each AI-suggestion adoption decision. Building on cognitive forcing functions that interrupt fast, heuristic decision making, the intervention is designed to prompt self-explanation and elicit metacognitive monitoring: "requiring justification may also prompt metacognitive monitoring of students' writing and revision processes". It resembles prompted self-explanation, which supports monitoring of understanding and identification of knowledge gaps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must make a separate accept/reject decision for each suggestion and provide a brief written justification for each decision
#### Constraints
- The study design isolates the effect of the requirement, not its precise mechanism; the effect of justification cannot be separated from the time and effort it requires

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate and master's students in a data science course

### Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of AI-generated writing feedback
- selective reliance on AI suggestions
