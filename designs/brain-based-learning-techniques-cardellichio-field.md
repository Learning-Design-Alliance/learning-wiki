# Brain-based learning techniques for encouraging neural branching and association-making

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts four brain-based learning techniques introduced by Cardellichio and Field (1997), designed to encourage students to form associations when learning science: hypothetical thinking, application of symbol systems, analysis of point of view, and web analysis. The article states these strategies should be taught because of "their capacity to stimulate connection-making", and that making connections among elements of the subject matter is "a key process in brain-based learning". A teacher guide with example classroom activities operationalised the four techniques across two eighth-grade science units.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher guide to assist teachers in using the brain-based learning techniques with eighth-grade students
- Classroom activities that prompt students to construct hypotheses, analyse viewpoints, translate between verbal and symbolic representations, and explore relationships between concepts
#### Constraints
- The article frames the techniques as encouraging association-making; the neural account (neural branching, dendrite growth) is asserted from cited neuroscience literature rather than tested in this study
