# Fresh frozen human cadaver model with a 5-task FLS-derived laparoscopic skills curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a cadaver-based training setup in which novices perform 10 repetitions each of 5 tasks "improvised from the Fundamentals of Laparoscopic Surgery (FLS) curriculum": two sharp peg transfer tasks, a simulated appendicectomy, and intracorporeal and extracorporeal knot tying. Participants worked in pairs as operator and camera assistant with scheduled rest breaks and role swaps to allow distributed training, spread over 2 days. Performance was video recorded and scored with a modified GOALS scale by blinded assessors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A Human Tissue Act-approved fresh cadaver laboratory with ethical approval
- A validated assessment scale (modified GOALS) and blinded video assessment
- A live task demonstration plus DVD reference for cognitive preparation
#### Constraints
- The cadaver model lacks bleeding, so iatrogenic bleeding control scenarios cannot be created
- Cost and legal constraints make cadaveric training a precious resource limited to licensed cadaver training centers
- Cadavers require up to 2 weeks of defrosting before use

### Target Learners
- Junior surgical trainees who are novices in laparoscopic surgery

### Learning Goals
- Basic laparoscopic technical skills (peg transfer, appendicectomy, knot tying) to a safe performance level
