# Limiting demonstration attempts as a scarce resource to encourage clustering and discourage brute-force attempts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course makes demonstration attempts a limited resource: lab sessions have a fixed number of demonstration slots and a cap on achievements per attempt. The authors state that "Making the demonstration attempts a limited resource increases their value, and discourages 'waste' in the sense of attempting to pass by brute force". The cap encourages clustering achievements into larger, coherent presentations, and students unable to form synthesis are deliberately disadvantaged. Randomly selected re-checks of passed achievements keep knowledge fresh without allowing passes to be lost.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough lab sessions with demonstration slots to schedule attempts; computer support for the request queue
#### Constraints
- Students unable to form synthesis are at a disadvantage by design; re-checks can pile up and block further progress until passed

### Target Learners
- second-year university programming students

### Learning Goals
- synthesis of course concepts
- continuous engagement with course material
