# Online self-administered strategic mindset intervention for secondary school students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A scalable, cost-effective online exercise (~40 minutes) that adolescents can self-administer in classrooms. It involves "learning about the common challenges that Secondary school students often face; hearing other students'testimonials about how they had used the strategic mindset psychology to overcome these challenges, to make progress, and to learn better"; reading scientific evidence; identifying personal strategy-eliciting questions; and committing to more effective learning approaches. Crucially, it does not train specific strategies but teaches the value of self-prompting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery in a classroom environment with sufficient peer engagement and minimal disruption for fidelity
- Students need some existing repertoire of learning strategies to benefit maximally
#### Constraints
- Did not include training in specific learning strategies; conveyed via slides rather than videos due to technical limitations in some schools

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students (ages 13–17) in public school classrooms

### Learning Goals
- Increasing use of effective learning strategies when studying for exams and improving exam performance
