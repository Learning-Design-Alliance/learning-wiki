# Dual-use NIHSS documentation with standardized and screening checkboxes in electronic health records

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a documentation element addressing the NIHSS's two distinct purposes at the clinical site: screening clinically for neurological deterioration or improvement, and providing reliable stroke severity data for global comparability. It states "To ensure clarity in documentation, nurses and physicians indicate the type of NIHSS assessment used by selecting either a “standardized” or “screening” checkbox in patients’ electronic health records". The standardized version is scored strictly per certification protocol on admission and daily, while the simplified screening version uses all items as a rapid tool throughout the day; when deterioration is suspected (commonly ≥ 4 NIHSS points within 24 h), the standardized version is performed and the physician notified.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All nursing staff complete online NIHSS certification, renewed every one to two years, with eight hours of credited work time as an incentive
- Nurses and LPNs collaboratively conduct the initial NIHSS assessment at shift start to establish a consistent baseline
#### Constraints
- The NIHSS tends to underestimate deficits from right (non-dominant) hemisphere strokes and poorly captures posterior circulation symptoms, so targeted monitoring beyond the NIHSS is specified
- The article notes a prospective analysis of this dual-use documentation may yield insights; outcomes are not yet reported

### Target Learners
- nurses in acute stroke units
- licensed practical nurses in stroke care

### Learning Goals
- standardized and reliable NIHSS scoring
- early detection of neurological deterioration
