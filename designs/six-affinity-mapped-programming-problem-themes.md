# Six affinity-mapped problem themes experienced by space-science students learning to program

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From affinity mapping of open-ended questionnaire responses, the authors identified "a set of emergent themes, each framed as a problem experienced by students": what is good code; managing expectations; learning the basics; finding support; managing larger problems; and learning advanced skills. These six problem statements, drawn from students' uncertainty about good code, surprise at the programming level, difficulty learning Python alongside course material, resource-finding needs, large data and project scope, and advanced techniques, formed the stated basis of the course development work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Derived from affinity mapping of students' open-ended written questionnaire responses, supported by quantitative ratings.
#### Constraints
- Themes reflect one MSc programme's cohorts (students completing the course between 2018 and 2021; 29 respondents of 56 contacted).

### Target Learners
- postgraduate space-science students with physics or engineering backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- developing programming and computational thinking skills to course and industry level
