# Misconception-based distractor design for proof-by-induction block problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates the misconception categories its distractors embody: conflating the inductive hypothesis with the proof goal or failing to bound it, incorrectly identifying base cases, failing to adequately prove base cases, and failure to understand what it means to apply the inductive hypothesis in context. Distractors were shown "visually paired with the corresponding correct line", and selecting one triggered targeted feedback explaining the misconception.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Distractors must be based on documented common student misconceptions from prior research or instructor experience, and be paired with targeted feedback.
#### Constraints
- Designed and evaluated only for proof by induction in an undergraduate discrete mathematics context.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer science students learning proof by induction

### Learning Goals
- writing correct proofs by mathematical induction
