# Tablet-based experimental game theory protocol for mapping cooperative relationships among elementary students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A video game implementing a modified Prisoner's Dilemma for mapping social relationships among elementary school students. As printed: "The video game implements a modified Prisoner's Dilemma where students played anon-anonymous and simultaneous dyadic game once with each of their classmates." Students endowed with ten tokens decide how many to send; sent tokens double, creating a cooperation incentive. The protocol mitigates survey biases (social desirability, cognitive barriers) and captures pre-existing relationships through non-anonymous interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Non-anonymous interaction so students know their partner's identity, to capture pre-existing relationships
- A friendly tablet interface to boost engagement and mitigate cognitive barriers
#### Constraints
- Departs from standard anonymous game-theoretic protocols, so choices reflect relationship history and perceptions, not only intrinsic dispositions

### Target Learners
- elementary school students

### Learning Goals
- mapping cooperative and reciprocal social relationships
- linking social network position to academic performance
