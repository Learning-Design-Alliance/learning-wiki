# School naps can be implemented in ordinary classrooms without specialized resting facilities, because sleeping seated at a desk suffices to boost encoding

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A design principle holding that academic nap interventions need not require beds or dedicated rest rooms: in this study students slept "sitting in chairs, with their heads resting on their forearms on the table" in the school library, and still showed better encoding. The article states this "allows for the development of easily implementable strategies to improve academic performance without needing specialized resting places equipped with beds". This keeps the ecological character of the intervention, with lessons delivered by the regular teacher.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A quiet space such as a library with chairs and tables, plus supervision to ensure a quiet environment
#### Constraints
- Presence of experimenters may have increased sleep onset latency, per the article
- Ecological school settings introduce uncontrolled variables such as timing between classes and examination days

### Target Learners
- high-school students in full-day schools

### Learning Goals
- boosting memory encoding of academic lesson content
