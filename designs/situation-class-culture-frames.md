# Situation and class culture as nested frames explaining variation in sustained curricular reforms

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article analyzes curricular implementation at nested levels or frames of context, from specific tasks up to departmental and institutional structures. Two critical frames are the situation and the class culture: "Situations" refer to the surrounding structure of students working at Tutorials, including rooms, teaching assistants, materials and social setting during recitation sessions, while class culture is the environment of norms, purposes and grading policies in which situations are set. The authors argue tasks are easy to hand off but situations and class culture are harder to prescribe and thus subject to greater variation when faculty change.
