# Top-down, pattern-recognition-first approach to teaching the asymmetric quantum rotor

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article contrasts a "bottom-up approach" of deriving eigenvalues and selection rules from a Hamiltonian with an alternative where "puzzle solving skills are invoked and spectral patterns identified" before molecular properties are discussed. Under the top-down approach students first locate common spectral patterns and then meet the underlying theory, with the aim "that a top down approach is more available to interested parties". The theory content itself (the asymmetric rigid rotor) is over 70 years old; what is new is the method of presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need a catalogue of common spectral patterns and their approximate dependence on rotational constants, as presented in Table 1 and Figures 3–8
#### Constraints
- The approach is developed mainly for near-prolate asymmetric tops, described as "a very common molecular shape"
- None of the theory introduced is new

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in physics and chemistry classrooms
- undergraduate physical chemistry lab students

### Learning Goals
- quantum number assignment in pure rotational spectra
- determination of approximate rotational constants
