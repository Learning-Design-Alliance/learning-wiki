# Prioritize structured continuous professional development over pre-service academic upgrades to close wound-care knowledge gaps

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that because formal in-service training was the sole independent predictor of good knowledge while degrees conferred no advantage, hospitals should establish "structured, mandatory wound care workshops prioritizing aseptic technique, dressing selection, and infection control." It emphasizes CPD as the operational priority over basic pre-service academic upgrades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hospital management commitment to establish structured, mandatory wound care workshops
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single tertiary institution and may have limited generalizability to lower-tier facilities

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses and midwives in tertiary referral hospitals

### Learning Goals
- Evidence-based wound-care knowledge including aseptic technique, dressing selection, and infection control
