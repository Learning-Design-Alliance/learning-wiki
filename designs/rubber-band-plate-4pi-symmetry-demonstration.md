# Physical demonstration experiments for the 4π (720°) rotational symmetry of quaternions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A reusable classroom demonstration pattern for the quaternion 4π rotational symmetry: a square plate fastened to the corners of a box with rubber bands. The paper reports that "If one turns the plate once, then the bands twist to the point where they are not able to be disentangled. Yet if the plate is turned twice and the rubber bands are consequently more twisted, they can however be disentangled with some patience." This makes visible the difference between 2π and 4π rotations, complementing the Dirac-style marked-cup demonstration and Conway and Guy's quaternion machine with a sign indicator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simple materials: a square plate, a box, rubber bands; patience for disentangling after the double turn
#### Constraints
- The paper describes the demonstration as illustrating the angle-doubling; it reports no measured learning effect

### Target Learners
- university-level physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding quaternion rotational symmetry of 4π
- connecting the angle doubling to spin-1/2 behavior
