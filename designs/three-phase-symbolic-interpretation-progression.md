# Three-phase progression for teaching symbolic interpretation with increasing text complexity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The module organized symbolic interpretation into a sequence the authors describe as "three phases that built on one another": first identifying symbols via associations, then making symbolic interpretive claims grounded in textual evidence connected to characters and their worlds, and finally making claims from patterns of evidence across extended texts. Texts increased in complexity in parallel, from everyday images and pop songs to a culturally less familiar lengthier text. Supporting worksheets accompanied each phase.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worksheets scaffolding each phase
- Class discussions providing practice and feedback on sensemaking and interpretive processes
#### Constraints
- The module was implemented in two ninth-grade sections in a suburban school program for students reading below the 30th percentile

### Target Learners
- ninth-grade students in an integrated freshman support program

### Learning Goals
- identifying symbols
- making evidence-based symbolic interpretive claims
- connecting symbols to characters and themes
