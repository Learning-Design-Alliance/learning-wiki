# Mathematical tutorial design: students construct equations from physical shapes and shapes from equations, then confront their pretest answers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Mathematical Description of Pulses tutorial sequences student activity so that learners explicitly graph a string's shape from its equation, sketch the shape after the pulse travels, and are guided into constructing the mathematical form, so that "students not only construct the shape of the string from an equation, they construct an equation from the shape of a string." A later part revisits the pretest equation without guidance, forcing students to generalize and resolve conflicts with their earlier answers. A final part uses video software to model an actual pulse and explore the physical significance of parameters A and b.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Development in a tight cycle with physics education research so materials address specific student difficulties
- Students work through guided worksheets in groups of three or four
#### Constraints
- The tutorial begins with a simplified equation (y(x) = 50/(1+(x/b)^2), b=20 cm) to minimize confusion related to the exponential before returning to the pretest's exponential form

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics university students

### Learning Goals
- functional understanding of a function of two independent variables
- relating equations to physical situations
- constructing equations from graphs and graphs from equations
