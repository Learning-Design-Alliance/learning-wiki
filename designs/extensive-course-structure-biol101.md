# Extensive course structure: a flipped, group-based Biology 101 design with consistent formative assessment and explicit metacognition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Extensive course structure is a flipped large-enrollment nonmajors biology design in which students learn basic content before class through online lectures, reading and watching guides, discussion-board confusion reports, and content summaries. Class time begins with individual and group quizzing using IF-AT scratch cards, followed by just-in-time lectures and instructor-created groups of four to six students completing worksheet activities of about 30 minutes each. Students then revise their content summaries, giving them explicit opportunities to reflect on how their understanding changed. The article contrasts this with a Moderate structure using reflective pauses, real-time writing, and think–pair–share without permanent groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Permanent instructor-created working groups with assigned seating for the duration of the quarter
- Online prelecture materials and instructor-produced videos to support in-class activities
- Carefully designed worksheet activities that elicit preconceptions and guide construction of understanding
#### Constraints
- The article does not know whether the worksheet activities would be as effective if the incentive were shifted from the group to the individual or if performed outside class as homework

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nonmajors in a large-enrollment introductory biology course

### Learning Goals
- Introductory biology content across cell membranes, energy transfers, cell division, genetics, evolution, and ecology
- More expert-like views about biology and learning biology
