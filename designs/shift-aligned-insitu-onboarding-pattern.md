# Shift-aligned, small-group in-situ simulation sessions for rapid onboarding of a large provider cohort under gathering restrictions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A deployment pattern for onboarding many providers to a new protocol when large gatherings are prohibited: short in-situ sessions scheduled immediately before clinical shifts, capped at six participants in their typical roles, with extra providers observing under social distancing, and a train-the-trainers expansion so additional faculty and residents teach subsequent sessions. The authors report this reached "86.7% (39 of 45) residents and 83.6% (46 of 55) of faculty who worked shifts in the ED during this time."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions limited to providers working the oncoming shift
- Volunteer teachers given instruction on teaching methods and observed for a session
- Teachers encouraged to sign up for sessions immediately prior to their own shifts to minimize infectious risk
#### Constraints
- Social distancing guidelines significantly limited the number of providers trainable at one time
- Access to supplies and equipment was unpredictable during the pandemic

### Target Learners
- ED residents and fellows
- Attending physicians
- Intensivists and anesthesiologists training their own departments

### Learning Goals
- Rapid dissemination and onboarding to new clinical guidelines
- Team role delineation and communication before high-risk procedures
