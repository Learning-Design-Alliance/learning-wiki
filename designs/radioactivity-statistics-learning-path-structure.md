# Learning path alternating class lessons, physics laboratory measurements, and computer-lab statistical analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The learning path integrates three settings: teacher-led class lessons presenting nuclear phenomenology, physics laboratory work measuring background and weak-source radioactivity, and computer lab sessions on statistical data analysis. Table 1 lists covered topics across these settings, from discovery of radioactivity and radioactive decay law to Bernoulli, Poisson and Gauss distributions, and tasks such as testing samples of 10, 20, 30, 50 background data and comparing them with a very long measure of more than 1000 data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities planned for the last year of high school after lessons on electromagnetism
- Data downloadable from the Geiger detector into a computer for the computer lab tasks
#### Constraints
- Hardly feasible in the last class due to the graduation exam; the authors suggest revising the presentation of statistical topics in previous years

### Target Learners
- last-year high school students (5a Liceo Tecnologico)

### Learning Goals
- understanding the importance of statistical analysis in modern physics
- nuclear physics phenomenology and radioactive decay
