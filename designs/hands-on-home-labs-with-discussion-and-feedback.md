# Design online labs around hands-on home equipment plus multiple channels for discussion and targeted feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that reliable, engaging, inquiry-based hands-on labs can run outside the classroom when home lab equipment is coupled with an online course design emphasizing teamwork, targeted feedback, and self-regulation skills. Citing research that "multiple channels for discussion and targeted feedback are necessary in an online environment, and labs are no exception", the design replicates the collaborative lab environment through discussion boards and a peer-review system while each student individually collects all data and writes their own report. Having equipment at home lets students tinker and learn by trial-and-error, and instructors can see each student's individual work and questions on the discussion boards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Home lab equipment for each student; discussion boards; a peer-review system; TA and instructor feedback throughout the week
#### Constraints
- The authors state that measuring and quantifying the efficacy of the labs by themselves is challenging and that a more precise study is needed to compare them with residential labs

### Target Learners
- online undergraduate physics students, predominantly adult learners

### Learning Goals
- inquiry-based experimental skills
- data sense-making
- teamwork and peer feedback
- self-regulation
