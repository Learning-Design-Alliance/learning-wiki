# 4Cs Intern Simulation Common Critical Care Curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The 4Cs is a mandatory longitudinal simulation-based curriculum for approximately 60 postgraduate year one interns from seven disciplines, providing "focused instruction on core critical care topics and procedural skills imperative for interns caring for adult patients". Interns attend three four-hour sessions over six months in mixed-discipline groups of four, using Laerdal Sim Man 3G, with scenarios of about 10–15 minutes followed by roughly 45-minute PEARLS-model debriefings led by faculty dyads.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small interdisciplinary groups of learners from seven disciplines attending mandatory sessions
- Faculty dyads with prior co-debriefing experience, with the principal investigator leading all co-debriefings for uniformity
#### Constraints
- Interdisciplinary attitudes, outlooks or stereotypes were not addressed in course objectives and never discussed in scenario debriefings

### Target Learners
- postgraduate year one interns in internal medicine, family medicine, emergency medicine, general surgery, orthopedics, obstetrics/gynecology, and neurosurgery

### Learning Goals
- confidence managing common acute care scenarios, procedural skills, and communication skills
