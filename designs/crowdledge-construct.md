# Crowdledge: emergent knowledge from crowds as the core of Big Data for science learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article deploys dos Santos's construct of crowdledge to give meaning to Big Data interaction as a science-learning medium: "emergent knowledge from crowds, i.e., knowledge provided by crowds' data and/or information spontaneously left (as digital footsteps and tweets)". The authors argue Big Data's essential attribute is not its bigness but the emergent nature of its analytic results (weak emergence, after Bedau), and that crowdledge tools like Google Trends can serve as a prototype experience of scientific research when learners pursue personally chosen questions through search-interest curves.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computational tool exposing crowd data (e.g., Google Trends charts) that learners can interrogate with terms of personal interest
#### Constraints
- Insertion into a school curriculum mischaracterises the environment as a genuine Papertian learning environment, a mischaracterization the methodology sought to ease

### Target Learners
- first-year high-school students (ensino médio) in physics classes

### Learning Goals
- scientific literacy
- understanding of scientific thinking and doing
- scepticism about Science claims
