# Staged case-based teaching that presents clinical information progressively to reduce cognitive load

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention organized complex cardiovascular scenarios into sequential learning steps: an introductory module using problem-based learning principles with common scenarios (e.g., acute chest pain, postoperative low cardiac output), and an advanced module with complex scenarios containing multiple clinical variables and distracting information. "By dividing complex cardiovascular surgery scenarios into sequential learning steps, this approach may reduce cognitive load and provide more opportunities for active reasoning." Interactive questions guided next-step examination choices, diagnosis identification, and management decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Case materials integrating history, examination, laboratory, and imaging data
- Embedded interactive questions and guideline-based explanatory notes
- Progression from common introductory scenarios to complex advanced scenarios
#### Constraints
- The authors state this may reduce cognitive load; the cognitive-load mechanism was not directly measured in the study

### Target Learners
- Cardiovascular surgery trainees in specialized training

### Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and diagnostic decision-making
- Synthesizing complex clinical information
