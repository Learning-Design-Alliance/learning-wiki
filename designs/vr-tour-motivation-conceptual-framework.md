# Conceptual framework linking spatial presence, situational interest, ideal future-self and immersive capability to motivation and learning outcomes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a conceptual framework in which four VR-related variables — immersive capability, spatial presence, situational interest and the ideal future-self — interact to shape motivation and learning outcomes in online VR tour lessons. It was operationalised through hypothetical learning trajectories such as "Spatial presence (SP) positively influences situational interest (SI)", tested and refined through two-level DBR cycles. The framework guided lesson design with the presentation, practice and production method. The article states it "did not directly assess the impact of immersive capability due to learners' use of provided low-cost VR spectacles".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VR tour lessons with repeated design-test-analysis cycles and learner feedback to refine the hypothetical learning trajectories
#### Constraints
- Immersive capability was not directly assessed because all learners used low-cost VR spectacles with the same immersive level
- The theories developed are context-specific and cannot be readily generalised to all other contexts

### Target Learners
- adult online language learners

### Learning Goals
- English vocabulary learning
- sustained motivation in online language learning
