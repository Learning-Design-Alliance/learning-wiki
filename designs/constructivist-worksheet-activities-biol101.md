# Constructivist worksheet activities that elicit preconceptions then guide group knowledge construction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Biol 101 worksheet activities follow "the basic tenets of constructivism": each activity first elicits student preconceptions about a topic, then guides students through a logical progression of ideas in which they make predictions, analyze data, and explain their reasoning. Groups of four to six students complete the activities in class after individual and group quizzing on their out-of-class preparation, and grades on the activities are given based on group performance. Examples include the osmosis activity addressing preconceptions about equilibrium through a hyponatremia case study and the lactase activity connecting genetics to human evolution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups formed before the quarter to maximize diversity of science background, year in school, and gender
- Students arrive having prepared basic content out of class
- Activities designed so students construct understanding on their own rather than being guided didactically through them
#### Constraints
- The authors do not know whether the activities would be as effective with individual rather than group incentives, or if performed outside class as homework

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nonmajors in introductory biology

### Learning Goals
- Constructing conceptual understanding of biology content built on prior ideas
- Applying concepts to real-life scenarios and data interpretation
