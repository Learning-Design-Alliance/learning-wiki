# Tailored web-based e-module on benzodiazepine prescribing for experienced GPs, grounded in Self-Determination Theory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A web-based, tailored continuing-medical-education program developed by a multidisciplinary team at Ghent University, focusing primarily on "avoiding initial BZD prescriptions and using psychological interventions as an alternative". Rather than merely transmitting knowledge, "it sought to address to GPs’ ambivalent attitudes and perceptions", and, drawing on Self-Determination Theory, it "aimed to fulfil basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness of participants".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Must address GPs’ ambivalent attitudes, perceptions and self-efficacy beliefs rather than focusing only on knowledge acquisition
#### Constraints
- Authors were involved in developing and providing the e-module
- Developed for and evaluated with Flemish GPs in Flanders, Belgium; participation was voluntary

### Target Learners
- Experienced practicing general practitioners (GPs) in Flemish primary care

### Learning Goals
- Avoiding initial benzodiazepine prescriptions
- Using psychological, non-pharmacological interventions as an alternative to prescribing for sleep problems
