# Service-learning integrated with cross-age peer teaching as a course-embedded pedagogical model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's service-learning program (SLP) integrates community service with required course activities: undergraduates in a non–majors biology course were trained in three modified lab sessions and then taught a multilevel plant genetics case-study curriculum to middle or high school students during class time. The authors define service-learning as "a pedagogical method that integrates community service with required course activities to enhance student attainment of course-specific learning objectives while simultaneously meeting the needs of community partners." Unlike outreach, the peer teaching was done by nonexperts, was a course requirement, and related to the university course curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Undergraduates must be trained by faculty experiencing the curriculum as learners before teaching, and the curriculum must be topically aligned with the university course content
#### Constraints
- The study was quasi-experimental with no control group of undergraduates who did not participate in the SLP, which the authors state limits their conclusions

### Target Learners
- non–science-major undergraduates in an introductory biology course
- middle and high school students

### Learning Goals
- genetics and molecular biology concepts
- plant reproduction and Mendelian genetics
- PCR and DNA gel electrophoresis
