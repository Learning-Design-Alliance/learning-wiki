# Five-category artifact taxonomy for evaluating AI-generated images

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes visual inspection of AI-generated images around a taxonomy of five artifact categories drawn from a written guide on distinguishing AI-generated images from authentic photographs. The training "was structured around five categories of artifacts": anatomical implausibilities (hands, eyes, teeth, body structure, identity inconsistencies), stylistic artifacts (cinematization, plastic textures, hyper-real detail), functional implausibilities (dysfunctional objects, malformed text and logos), violations of physical constraints (inconsistent lighting, shadows, reflections, perspective), and sociocultural implausibilities (historically or culturally incongruent scenarios). Each category was taught with concise explanations paired with multiple visual examples, and the same taxonomy coded analysts' free-text reasoning in the qualitative analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explanations paired with multiple visual examples per category, with visual callouts highlighting regions of interest
- Examples drawn from contemporary diffusion models so the taught cues match the artifacts current generators produce
#### Constraints
- The article cautions analysts against over-reliance on any single cue, since artifact visibility varies with scene complexity, pose, image resolution, and viewing time
- Real photographs can contain non-generative artifacts such as compression and focus inconsistencies, so the presence of an artifact does not by itself imply an image is AI-generated

### Target Learners
- intelligence analysts
- professionals whose verification decisions carry operational weight, such as journalists and content moderators

### Learning Goals
- distinguishing real photographs from AI-generated images
- articulating detection reasoning using specific artifact categories
