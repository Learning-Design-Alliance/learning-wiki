# Two-hole cylinder kit with interchangeable wood and brass ballasts that switches between normal and paradoxical behaviour

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because the original Collection cylinder's irregular lead ballast “non si presta a essere usato per scopi didattici”, the authors built a wooden cylinder (Rc = 45 mm) with two holes of radius Rh = 16 mm, one coaxial and one offset by 0.5 Rc, plus two inserts of wood and brass (ρh = 8.5 kg/m3). Inserting the brass piece in the central hole and the wood piece in the offset hole gives normal rolling downhill; reversing them makes the cylinder stay in equilibrium on the incline, showing the paradox. The kit serves as an exhibit for studying centre-of-mass properties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A ballast denser than the cylinder body placed in the off-axis hole, with static friction sufficient for rolling without slipping
#### Constraints
- The original Collection cylinder, due to the irregular shape of its lead ballast, is not suited to didactic use

### Target Learners
- early secondary-school (liceo) students
- general public visitors to the Collection

### Learning Goals
- understanding centre of mass and static equilibrium on an inclined plane

### Claims
- Ballasted Cylinder Equilibrium Maximum Incline [+M]
