# Bloom taxonomy and Miller pyramid as a combined design framework for competency-aligned e-modules, evaluated via the Kirkpatrick model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a design framework in which Bloom taxonomy organizes cognitive learning objectives and the Miller pyramid ensures translation into practical competence within e-modules. As printed, "Bloom taxonomy guided the organization of learning objectives within the module," while the Miller pyramid was integrated "to ensure that learning translated into practical competence." Effectiveness was evaluated with the Kirkpatrick model at levels 1 (reaction) and 2 (learning) across cognitive, psychomotor, and affective domains, following the Kern 6-step approach for curriculum development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject-expert and educational-technologist collaboration for content creation
- Expert content and technical validation using a structured checklist
- Alignment of modules with specific CBME competencies and matching assessment tools
#### Constraints
- Evaluation covered only Kirkpatrick levels 1 and 2; behavior change and long-term impact were not examined
- Findings come from first-year MBBS foundational subjects (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry) at one south Indian tertiary medical university

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate medical (MBBS) students

### Learning Goals
- Cognitive competencies from remembering to analysis
- Psychomotor (manipulation) skills
- Affective/professional attitudes (responding)
