# Historical-context-plus-hands-on-simulation pattern for teaching ancient navigation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The unit pairs historical narrative (Minoans, Phoenicians, Pytheas) with simulated reconstructions of the ancient night sky so students practise the navigators' own methods. Introductory videos and a story-based introduction prepare the field, then students reproduce Bronze Age techniques: deriving circumpolar ranges from latitude and building a stellar compass. The article notes the activity "could be combined with other forms of acquiring knowledge like giving oral presentations in history, literature or geography highlighting navigation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Star charts of both celestial hemispheres, the city/latitude table, compasses, and the bright-stars Excel spreadsheet
#### Constraints
- Designed for a 90-minute session; the supplemental field activity depends on weather and a site with a good view to the horizon

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students aged 14–19 in geography, history or astronomy lessons

### Learning Goals
- understanding how apparent stellar motion supports direction-finding and latitude estimation
- appreciating the navigational achievements of ancient civilisations

- Pole Height Equals Observer Latitude [+M]
