# Scaffolded progression of blend-building graph problems from single-graph interpretation to multiple variables and situations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes its problem set as a complexity ladder: start by interpreting the physical correlates of salient points on a single graph (position vs time first, since it requires fewer levels of inference), then match multiple graphs to multiple situations, then use multiple graphs of different variables for one situation, and finally match graphs to multiple situations and multiple variables at once. As with any complex skill, the author recommends scaffolding by beginning with a simple example and then increasing the level of complexity a step at a time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems should begin with simple examples and increase complexity step by step
- Early problems should use position vs time graphs before acceleration graphs that require physical principles like Newton's second law
#### Constraints
- The most complex problems require managing many concepts at once, so the author advises starting with simple graphs such as straight lines

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics students

### Learning Goals
- interpreting the physics coded in graphs
- seeing multiple graphs as multiple perspectives on one situation
- checking consistency among graphs
