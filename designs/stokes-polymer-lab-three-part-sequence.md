# Three-part Stokes-analysis lab sequence: derive Malus's law, then measure unoriented and oriented polymer samples

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The lab is structured in three parts: students first derive and verify Malus's law with no sample to become convinced of the Stokes method's utility, then repeat intensity-versus-angle measurements with an isotropic (non-birefringent) sample, and finally with an anisotropic birefringent sample. The article states students compare intensity "1. in a setup without a sample; 2. with an isotropic (non-birefringent) sample; 3. with an anisotropic birefringent sample." Each part builds from known to unknown optical behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A vertically oriented linearly polarized laser, linear analyzer, polymer samples, and light intensity detector on an optical rail, as in the article's setups
#### Constraints
- The article notes the birefringence quantification part requires a two-week extension with sample thickness and laser wavelength measurements

### Target Learners
- undergraduate general physics students, mostly non-physics science majors (juniors and seniors)

### Learning Goals
- understand Stokes analysis and Mueller matrices
- understand birefringence and molecular origins of optical anisotropy
- apply matrix calculations and computer programming to optics
