# 28-hour second-year doctor-patient communication course (HannibaL, MHH) with simulated patients, role play and OSCE-aligned observation sheets

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 28-hour, seven-session doctor-patient communication course in the second year of the HannibaL curriculum at Hannover Medical School, taught in groups of ten students. Its learning objectives are 'basic skills in taking patient histories, disclosing diagnoses (breaking bad news), and the use of shared decision-making (SDM) in these contexts', assessed at two of six OSCE stations; simulated patients join three of the seven sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups of ten students to allow repeated role play within the group and with the simulated patients
- A pool of about 80 trained simulated patients and observation sheets combining the Calgary-Cambridge Guides criteria with those of Füeßl and Middeke
#### Constraints
- The article notes that implementing practice-oriented teaching in small groups using SP 'requires significant personnel, organisational and financial resources'

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- taking patient histories
- disclosing diagnoses (breaking bad news)
- shared decision making
