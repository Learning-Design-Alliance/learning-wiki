# The Marble Game: a prototypical kinetic Monte Carlo framework for stochastic processes across the STEM disciplines

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The marble game is a two-box stochastic model in which marbles jump between boxes with rate constant k due to random thermal motion, simulated with a ten-sided die and implemented by students in Excel. The article describes it as "a prototypical kinetic Monte Carlo (kMC) method that can be used to solve the master equations for stochastic processes (Markov chains)", isomorphic with a reversible first-order chemical reaction, and applicable to diffusion, osmosis, ion permeation and other molecular-level processes across STEM.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A ten-sided die, ten marbles and two boxes for the physical class activity, and a blank Excel spreadsheet in which students write and implement their own algorithms
#### Constraints
- The introductory sequence of Modules 1-3 is required before the approach can be applied to other STEM areas, taking about 3 weeks of a 3-4 credit-hour course

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life science majors
- introductory STEM students

### Learning Goals
- quantitative scientific modeling
- stochastic (kinetic Monte Carlo) simulation of molecular-level processes
- algorithmic thinking and finite difference methods
