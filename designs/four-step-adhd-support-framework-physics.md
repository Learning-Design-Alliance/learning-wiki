# A four-step framework for supporting students with ADHD in introductory physics courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes its recommendations into four strategies instructors can use to support students with ADHD in introductory physics: initiating open dialogue about students' needs, abilities, and interests; scaffolding course content to help students stay on track; providing course resources in multiple formats to allow options in how and when students learn; and demonstrating understanding that accommodations promote equity. The framework is grounded in the viewpoint of one student with ADHD who took SCALE-UP style introductory physics at a large southeastern research-intensive university. The article argues the strategies center students with ADHD while likely benefiting all students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors initiate the strategies themselves rather than waiting for students to disclose needs, since students with disabilities can find it daunting to talk about their disabilities with a new instructor
#### Constraints
- The article states all suggestions are generally applicable beyond introductory physics courses, but the second and third suggestions are particularly important in introductory physics because of its emphasis on conceptual learning over rote memorization
- The views presented come from one student with ADHD, and the article recognizes that each student's experience is unique

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students with ADHD in introductory physics courses

### Learning Goals
- success in introductory physics through accessible, inclusive instruction
