# ISS contest as a constructivist STEM educational project using PBL, PrBL, IBSE, ESD, EDP, and ICT

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ISS contest functioned as a STEM educational project implemented through constructivist methods, principally Problem and Project Based Learning (PBL & PrBL), which enhanced Inquiry Based Science Education (IBSE), Education for Sustainable Development (ESD), the Engineering Design Process (EDP), and Information & Communication Technologies (ICT). Students developed innovative experimentation proposals for the International Space Station within seven thematic areas, working in teams with technical tutors from research centres, industries, and universities. The pattern connects school learning to authentic space research contexts, motivating students through real-world scientific challenges and competitive project development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Secondary school students in teams of up to three per school, preferably steered by mentors from accredited institutions
- Technical tutorship from third parties with specific skills such as research centres, industries, universities
#### Constraints
- The contest was targeted at pupils of fourth and fifth years of high schools (ages 17-19) in Italy
- None of the winning projects was ultimately tested on board the ISS

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students (ages 17-19)

### Learning Goals
- STEM competences
- Scientific inquiry skills
- Problem-solving
- Teamwork
- Innovation in space science
