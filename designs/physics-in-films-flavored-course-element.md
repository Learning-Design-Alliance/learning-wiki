# Physics in Films course with themed 'flavors' and in-class electronic personal response system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Physics in Films is a general-education Physical Science course built around popular films: one or two brief clips are shown each day, augmented by live demonstrations, while complete films are viewed at home as homework. The authors extended the pilot into versions "nicknamed ﬂavors—of the course whereby each ﬂa-vor used a particular genre or theme of movies", including Action/Adventure, SciFi, Superhero, Modern Physics, Astronomy, Pseudoscience and Metaphysics. An electronic personal response system records student responses to instructor questions, provides quizzes with immediate feedback, and captures perception and attendance data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supply of popular film clips matched to the physical science concepts to be taught
- An in-class electronic personal response system for quizzes, feedback and perception data
#### Constraints
- The project was young, about 24 months old at the time of writing, and the traditional teaching mode was still used in some sections

### Target Learners
- college non-science majors in general-education physical science courses

### Learning Goals
- basic principles of physical science
- understanding of the scientific method and the distinction between science and pseudoscience
