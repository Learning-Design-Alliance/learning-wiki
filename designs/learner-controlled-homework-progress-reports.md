# Learner-controlled homework with structured progress reports (planning, monitoring, adjusting) replacing collected problem solutions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course replaced collected problem-set solutions with a learner-controlled homework system: students chose from 50-75 suggested problems per unit, organized by learning outcome and sorted by difficulty, and submitted weekly reports instead of solutions. Each report paired self-evaluation of problem difficulty with scaffolded reflection: prompts on goals before practice (Planning), reflection on effectiveness during or after (Monitoring), and next steps (Adjusting). Reports could be completed on a single handwritten page and credit was given for submission regardless of quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need a suggested problem list organized by learning outcome and sorted by difficulty so they can locate appropriate practice
- Reports must be low-burden (a single handwritten page) and credit given for submission to sustain participation
#### Constraints
- Most students engaged only superficially with the reflective components despite guided prompts and class discussion about learning

### Target Learners
- junior life science majors in algebra-based introductory mechanics

### Learning Goals
- self-regulated learning (planning, monitoring, adjusting)
- problem-solving practice tailored to individual needs
