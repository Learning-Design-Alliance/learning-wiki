# Polya's four-step heuristic problem-solving framework

## Description
The article uses Polya's framework, in which problem-solving is "the process used to solve a problem that does not have an obvious solution" and teaching it means teaching "strategies, or heuristics". The four-step process is: 1) understand the problem, 2) devise a plan, 3) carry out the plan, and 4) look back. The experimental group's lesson plans were built on these heuristic steps together with Shireen (2006) guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must be organized as a sequence of heuristic steps applied to problems, per the Polya (1945) guidelines used to build the lesson plans
#### Constraints
- In this study the heuristics had to be preceded by exposition of facts, rules, formulas and procedures before students could use them
