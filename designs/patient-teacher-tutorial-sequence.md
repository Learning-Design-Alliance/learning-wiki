# Patient-Teacher-Tutorial (PTT) sequence: a three-phase framework for outpatient clinical learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The PTT sequence is a framework for organizing medical student learning in outpatient settings, extending models such as the One-Minute Preceptor and SNAPPS. It comprises "three sequential phases: the medical student's encounter with a Patient; their review of the patient case with the Teacher and, the specific subject of this paper, an independent Tutorial phase where the student can reinforce the learning from the patient and clinical preceptor." The patient encounter activates prior knowledge and sets a learning agenda; the brief teacher phase identifies gaps; the tutorial phase exploits the still-present motivated state to cement learning, addressing the "opaque or hidden" conceptual knowledge that time-pressured preceptors cannot cover.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A relevant patient encounter and a preceptor discussion precede the tutorial phase
- A self-contained, brief learning resource must be available immediately after the encounter
#### Constraints
- The authors postulate it is "a third-best educational option behind learning from patients and learning from preceptors" and could be deleterious if it reduces patient contact or preceptor time

### Target Learners
- third-year medical students on outpatient clinical rotations

### Learning Goals
- declarative knowledge (facts and concepts) tied to recently seen patient conditions
- lifelong learning habits of focused reading after patient encounters
