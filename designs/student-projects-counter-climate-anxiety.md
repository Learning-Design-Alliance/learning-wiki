# Use student-chosen group projects on climate solutions to counter feelings of helplessness about climate change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because climate-change lectures often present problems without solutions, students may feel the answers escape them, leading to distancing and anxiety. The authors write that "We believe these projects offer a partial remedy to this by showing the students they can use their skills in physics to develop a meaningful project in a short time frame (12 hours in class)." Students choose their own topic and investigate it via simulations, case studies, literature reviews, or experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Start project brainstorming early in the course, use a Project Description form, and have instructors review forms to narrow overly broad topics
- Keep projects low-cost and do-it-yourself, supported by department lab infrastructure and a small annual budget
#### Constraints
- Projects are limited to a 12-hour in-class time frame; the authors report 39% of students would have preferred more project time

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- research methodology
- scientific reasoning
- independent investigation of environmental physics problems
