# Near-age peer mentor summer outreach intervention with course counseling and financial aid assistance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention assigns recent graduates to near-age peer mentors recruited from undergraduates and graduate students at a local tier-one research university. Mentors reached out via text, phone, email, and Facebook, offering "Course Counseling and Advising" about registration, majors, placement exams, and "Financial Aid Assistance" including outstanding obligations, forms, living arrangements, and work-study. All treated students were contacted at least twice by email and phone; interested students took part in follow-up meetings in person or by phone or Skype. Mentors received one hour of training from CAP staff with templates and roadmaps for conversations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors were trained for one hour by CAP staff in basic strategies for youth development and mentoring and provided with templates and roadmaps to guide conversations
- Mentor caseloads were kept to less than forty students per mentor
#### Constraints
- Operated within four public secondary schools (two urban and two rural) in Central Michigan as part of the larger CAP quasi-experimental intervention

### Target Learners
- graduating 12th grade students preparing to enroll in postsecondary institutions

### Learning Goals
- successful transition and matriculation to college
- navigating course registration and financial aid processes
