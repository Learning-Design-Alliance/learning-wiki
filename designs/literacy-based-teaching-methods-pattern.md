# Literacy-based teaching methods pattern: reading-strategy instruction, writing-process instruction, and written recasts layered onto an established communicative curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson/unit-level design in which an established communicative second language curriculum is extended with three literacy-based components: explicit reading-strategy instruction, instruction in the five-step writing process, and written corrective recasts during drafting. The reading strategies "included the use of scanning, skimming, guessing, predicting, cognates, phonological awareness, identifying grammar structures, using a dictionary, summarizing, using graphic organizers, and connecting the reading to the participant's background knowledge." Students practiced each strategy on a small portion of an extended reading before completing it independently, and wrote key information on a graphic organizer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One teacher trained in the literacy-based methods delivers instruction; the article arranged three training meetings plus a separate meeting on test administration and scoring
- An extended reading matched to learner proficiency and tied to the initial instruction by topic, vocabulary, and grammar structures
- A writing assignment of at least five paragraphs using a shared prompt, with the experimental group expected to use the writing process
#### Constraints
- The teacher could provide recast feedback on only a few sentences per student per approximately 47-minute class period
- The study was limited to four classes of beginning-level Spanish students aged 12–14 whose first language was not Spanish

### Target Learners
- beginning-level (novice proficiency) middle-school second language learners, ages 12–14

### Learning Goals
- second language vocabulary and grammar achievement
- reading comprehension strategy use
- written production in the second language
