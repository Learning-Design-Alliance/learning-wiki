# Blended-learning EBM course pattern: block workshop plus patient-based quasi-journal-club tutorials with facilitator role

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's BL delivery combined a one-day block workshop covering all EBM concepts with remaining tutorials used for patient-based presentations: students identified a patient, took a history, and critically appraised an article applied to that patient, presented in a "quasi-journal club delivery method". Tutors acted as facilitators promoting peer-to-peer learning rather than leading structured activities. Both groups covered the same ten two-hour sessions of EBM content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A one-day block workshop covering the EBM concepts otherwise delivered in tutorials
- Clinical rotations in which students can identify patients applicable to their assigned topic
- Facilitators able to promote peer discussion rather than lead structured activities
#### Constraints
- The article notes feasibility in a large teaching hospital remains uncertain, and few facilitators seem to have both the clinical and EBM expertise desired by students

### Target Learners
- second-year graduate-entry medical students

### Learning Goals
- competency in the five steps of the EBM process, including critical appraisal and application of evidence to clinical scenarios
