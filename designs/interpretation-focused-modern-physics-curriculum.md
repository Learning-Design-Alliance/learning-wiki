# Interpretation-focused modern physics curriculum (INT-1 and INT-2 implementations)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A research-based 15-week large-lecture modern physics curriculum for engineering majors that makes quantum interpretation a topic unto itself. It uses a 'spins first' sequence, interpretive discussions, single-photon and Mach-Zehnder experiments, twice-weekly problem-solving sessions with Learning Assistants, an anonymous online discussion board, and an end-of-semester reflective essay. The article reports it "augmented a number of standard topics (e.g., the uncertainty principle, atomic models) with interpretive discussions that had been missing in prior courses." Students were never evaluated on their preferred interpretations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Twice-weekly problem-solving sessions staffed by co-instructors and undergraduate Learning Assistants who facilitate discussion
- Interpretation-themed clicker questions interspersed with lectures on the new experimental topics
#### Constraints
- Promotes a Matter-Wave perspective while not evaluating students on preferred interpretations; special relativity was omitted to make time for the new material.

### Target Learners
- Engineering undergraduates in a calculus-based introductory modern physics course

### Learning Goals
- Distinguish interpretive perspectives on quantum phenomena; recognize limitations of classical intuitions; develop sophisticated views of quantum uncertainty
