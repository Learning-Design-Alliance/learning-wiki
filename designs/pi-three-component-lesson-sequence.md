# Processing Instruction lesson sequence: explicit information, processing-strategy warning, then structured input activities moving from sentence to discourse

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements PI as a three-part lesson sequence: a lecture on the target structure and its meaning, awareness-raising about counterproductive processing strategies, and structured input activities. The activities "used a combination of referentially oriented activities and affectively oriented activities" and "operated from sentence to longer stretches of discourse as they progressed." In this study the structured-input component comprised five activities taught over one and a half hours across three sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured input activities must be designed so learners attend to the targeted grammatical form rather than lexical cues such as time adverbials
#### Constraints
- The study taught only one function of the present perfect, consistent with VanPatten's guideline to present one thing at a time

### Target Learners
- college sophomores learning English as a second language

### Learning Goals
- interpreting and producing the English present perfect aspect
