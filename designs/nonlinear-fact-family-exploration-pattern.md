# Non-linear fact-family exploration with partner matching, flip books, and products-grid activities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention organized facts practice as exploratory activities rather than sequential drill: students matched product cards with factor-pair cards in tabular arrangements, tested one another with homemade fact flip books, and inspected “interesting nines products relationships on a 10x10 products grid.” Fact families were addressed in non-linear fashion, with sevens and eights explored before fours and sixes, and only two sequential sevens facts worked on at a time to build confidence before fitting the family together as a complete set 1-12.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions of 30 minutes, one-week intervals, replicated systematically across classrooms by the researcher
#### Constraints
- A week was missed on two occasions at each school for state testing and spring vacation, extending the timeframe to twelve weeks

### Target Learners
- Grade 3 students in diverse suburban elementary schools

### Learning Goals
- automaticity with single-digit multiplication facts
