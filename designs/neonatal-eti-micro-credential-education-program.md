# Micro-credential-based comprehensive educational program for acquiring and maintaining neonatal endotracheal intubation skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops a comprehensive educational program for neonatal ETI built on micro-credentialing, "the use of short, focused credentials that provide in-demand skills". It includes video-laryngoscopy assisted coaching, just-in-time training, frequent mannequin practice, rapid cycle deliberate practice, mental skills training and deep slow breathing, with YouTube educational videos, an ETI instruction card, a performance checklist, and a quality improvement assessment framework. The authors state "Our program includes a standardised and structured approach to learning, instruction, supervision, practice, assessment, and credentialing".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adoption of a micro-credential format based on the Australian National Framework for developing micro-credentials
- Organisational implementation and assessment as a quality improvement project using pre-defined deliverables and outcomes
#### Constraints
- The effectiveness of the entire program has not been measured; the authors state well-designed studies are needed to evaluate its impact on patient outcomes and trainee wellbeing

### Target Learners
- neonatal and paediatric trainees
- senior fellows and consultants
- nursing staff and nurse practitioners

### Learning Goals
- acquiring and maintaining neonatal endotracheal intubation skills
- attenuating the acute stress response during the procedure
- monitoring competency in the organisational context
