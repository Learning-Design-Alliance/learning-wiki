# Iterative three-session PBL block structure cycling background, hypothesis development, and experimental design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course organizes each problem into a repeating block: a background session, a hypothesis-development session where students "began to design experiments to test the hypothesis", and a final session fleshing out design details. Because students had "three opportunities to discuss hypothesis development, experimental design and analysis during sessions with their facilitators", the structure deliberately spaces practice of the target skills across problems. Written and oral assignments add further articulation practice. This grain makes skill-building iterative rather than one-shot, with each block addressing a different biomedical problem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups with a facilitator to guide discussion and identify learning issues between sessions
- Students research background literature independently before each session
#### Constraints
- Developed around COVID-19 problems during the pandemic; case material for research-focused biomedical graduate students generally required modification from existing repositories

### Target Learners
- first-year doctoral students in biomedical sciences

### Learning Goals
- hypothesis development
- experimental design
- data interpretation and statistical analysis planning
