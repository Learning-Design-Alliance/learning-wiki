# Assess cognitive skills in schools for refugee children and include cognitive activity training within occupational therapy rehabilitation programs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools serving refugee children routinely assess cognitive skills and embed cognitive activity training within occupational therapy programs. The conclusion states that "cognitive skills should be assessed in schools for refugee children and cognitive activity training to be applied within the scope of rehabilitation should be included in occupational therapy programs for this population". This rests on the trial's finding that a school-based CAT program improved cognitive, academic, and quality-of-life outcomes in trauma-exposed refugee adolescents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-based delivery with therapist supervision; assessment of cognitive skills to identify children with cognitive vulnerability
#### Constraints
- The authors note the study was conducted at a single center in Istanbul, which may restrict applicability, and that further research is needed on feasibility, scalability, and long-term sustainability of school-based implementation

### Target Learners
- refugee children and adolescents in school settings

### Learning Goals
- cognitive skill development
- academic performance (reading speed and handwriting)
- quality of life
