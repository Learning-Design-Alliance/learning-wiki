# A real-exoplanet-data teaching sequence for Kepler's third law and Newton's gravitation in high school

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a roughly 3-hour teaching sequence developed in Chile in which groups of three students share one computer and analyze real exoplanet data. The authors state that "a teaching sequence was developed in Chile using real exoplanet data from the Open Exoplanet Catalog and NASA's Eye on Exoplanets webpage." Activities include an Excel spreadsheet with orbital period, semimajor axis and mass data for the solar system and 59 extrasolar systems, guiding questions, and plotting exercises that avoid complex equations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selected planetary systems must host at least two planets, orbit a single star, and provide complete orbital information (mass, period, semimajor axis)
#### Constraints
- Ideally groups of three students share one computer, totaling about 3 hours

### Target Learners
- high school students in Chile

### Learning Goals
- applying Kepler's third law and Newton's law of universal gravitation to current astrophysics research
- data analysis through plotting
