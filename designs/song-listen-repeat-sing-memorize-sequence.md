# Song-based pronunciation lesson sequence: lyrics preview, repeated listening, part-by-part repetition, group singing, memorization

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's experimental treatment followed a fixed sequence of activities built around the songs in Song Time 3: learners first read the lyrics and ask about new words, then listen to the song twice, repeat it part by part, sing along with the music several times, and finally memorize it in groups. The article states "The researchers stopped the song part by part and asked the students to repeat carefully", and that "the students listened to the songs and tried to recognize and copy the intonation and stress in the words and sentences". The teacher did not sing; the recorded music carried the pronunciation model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded songs with music as the pronunciation model, lyrics distributed to learners, and class time devoted to the song activities (20 minutes per 90-minute session in the study)
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only with beginner-level female learners aged 9-12 in an Iranian private institute over about five weeks

### Target Learners
- young beginner EFL learners (children aged 9-12)

### Learning Goals
- segmental pronunciation
- intonation
- stress pattern recognition
