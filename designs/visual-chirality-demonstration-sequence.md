# Visual demonstration sequence for chirality: mirrors, gloves, and knots

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's instructional pattern moves from the plane mirror question, to the glove demonstration, to knots, because these visually display chirality. It notes "A common demonstration is to show that a left-handed glove, when turned inside-out, becomes a right-handed glove," illustrating that two reflections (inside-out and left-right) are related by rotation, and that left-right, top-down, and front-back reflections are all related by rotations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Objects whose chirality is visually apparent, such as mirrors, gloves, twisted rope, and knots
#### Constraints
- The demonstrations support the classical-physics treatment and do not replace the quantum-mechanical parity formalism

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- recognize chiral versus achiral objects
- understand that reflections cannot be achieved by rotation
