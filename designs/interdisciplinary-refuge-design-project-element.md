# Interdisciplinary refuge design project: three collaborative meetings, fieldwork and stakeholder panel over a 12-week semester

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A 12-week authentic interdisciplinary learning experience in which nursing, architecture and landscape students enrolled in concurrent separate subjects collaborated on designing women’s refuges. "The students from all disciplines met on three formal occasions of three-hour sessions throughout the 12-week semester as well as negotiated out of class meetings." The first meeting formed mixed-discipline teams working on refuge-user case studies, the second involved fieldwork with service providers at a local council, and the third was a presentation of design guides containing ten best-practice principles to a stakeholder panel. Nursing students shared knowledge from four-week placements in women’s health services to support the design students’ understanding of user needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation of all three disciplines as a simulation of a disciplinary consultation team, with stakeholder access for fieldwork and panel presentation
#### Constraints
- Nursing and architecture subjects were electives while landscape was core, so the cohorts differed in motivation and prior exposure

### Target Learners
- third-year nursing students
- master’s architecture students
- third-year landscape design students

### Learning Goals
- understanding the needs and perspectives of refuge service users
- designing safe refuge spaces
