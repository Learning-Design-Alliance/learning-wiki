# Tiered simulation course with a basic block and two refinements so students at different levels can stop after different achievements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course is structured into a basic block (setting up and visualizing the simulation) and two further refinements aimed at more advanced students: quantitative extraction of the perihelion motion, and error/uncertainty analysis. The article states "The course is structured such that students at diﬀerent levels can stop after diﬀerent achievements", with canonical stopping points so less ambitious students can stop after the basic course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructions and templates can be cut down or left out depending on available time and the numerical/computational versatility of the students.
#### Constraints
- The article notes that for most students a detailed study of the uncertainties of the numerical simulations might be too technical.

### Target Learners
- high school students from 10th to 13th grade

### Learning Goals
- understand the role of differential equations in theoretical physics
- implement Newtonian dynamics numerically
- test and optimize computer codes
- visualize numerical results
