# Homogeneous ability grouping by screening test for catch-up literacy sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Fresh Start groups pupils for small-group teaching according to scores on its own screening assessment, so that each group is homogeneous in reading ability. The report's key conclusions state that "Pupils must be grouped according to the reading scores obtained from a Fresh Start screening test" and that "Each group has a homogeneous level in terms of pupil reading abilities". FS claims the closer the homogeneity within groups, the faster individual pupils' progress, and emphasises homogeneity more than group size, while recommending small groups and optional 20-minute one-to-one sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An initial screening test of phonics and simple word recognition conducted individually by the teacher
- Re-grouping after the entry test so pupils share modules or reading age
#### Constraints
- The homogeneity claim is the programme developer's assertion reported in the teacher's guide, not a finding tested in this trial

### Target Learners
- Low-attaining secondary readers in catch-up groups

### Learning Goals
- Reading accuracy and fluency through level-matched phonics instruction

### Claims

- [Fresh Start pupils made more progress in literacy than control pupils after 22 weeks, with an overall effect size of +0.24](../claims/fresh-start-22-week-effect-024.md) [+W]
