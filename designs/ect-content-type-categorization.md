# ECT categorization: partitioning physics instructional content into Concepts, Examples, and Theories

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes "ECT categorization", partitioning physics teaching material into three content types: Concepts (qualitative overviews using "words, sketches, diagrams and/or graphs"), Theories (rigorous mathematical descriptions of the phenomenon), and Examples (fully worked solutions serving as "road maps" for problem types). The authors treat the categories as independent for the investigation while acknowledging content may contain aspects of multiple types, and use this partitioning to define the six possible content sequences tested on the McLEAP platform.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content must be classifiable into Concept, Example, and Theory types by the instructor or material designer
#### Constraints
- The categories are not fully orthogonal: the article notes Concept and Theory may both be categorized under conceptual learning, and that results would be stronger if the content types were fully orthogonal

### Target Learners
- first-year university STEM students

### Learning Goals
- quantitative problem-solving in introductory physics
