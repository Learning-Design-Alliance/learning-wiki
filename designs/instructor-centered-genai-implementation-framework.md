# Instructor-centered framework for translating institutional GenAI guidance into course implementation via three mediating factors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose a framework that guides the translation of institutional guidance to course implementation by placing the instructor at the center of the process and considering mediating factors (Figure 3). The first factor is the instructor's current expertise in GenAI use and broader AI literacy, along with the training needed to implement GenAI in instruction; in CS this includes understanding model limitations in code generation and recognizing security vulnerabilities in AI-generated code. The second factor is discipline or domain, since implications of GenAI use differ sharply between theory-heavy courses such as algorithms and applied ones such as software engineering. The third factor is the nature of instruction and required pedagogical knowledge, as GenAI use varies across lecture-based, lab-based and project-based courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor AI literacy and training to implement GenAI in instruction
- Accounting for whether the course is theory-heavy or applied
- Pedagogical knowledge matched to lecture-based, lab-based, or project-based instruction
#### Constraints
- The framework is proposed, not empirically tested in this study; the authors state the transition to productive GenAI use will be an arduous task and substantial revisions to existing CS pedagogical practices will be needed

### Target Learners
- Higher education students in computing courses

### Learning Goals
- Responsible and productive GenAI use aligned with course learning outcomes
- Preservation of problem-solving skills while supporting technical skill development
