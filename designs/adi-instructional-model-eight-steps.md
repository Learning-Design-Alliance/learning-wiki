# The Argument-Driven Inquiry (ADI) instructional model with eight interrelated steps

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
ADI is an inquiry-based instructional model that gives "a more central place to argumentation and the role of argument in the social construction of scientific knowledge while promoting inquiry." It comprises eight interrelated steps, from identification of the task and research question through developing a method, generating a tentative argument with claim, evidence and justification, an argumentation session, reflective discussion, writing an investigation report, double-blind group peer review, and report revision. In this study five ADI laboratory investigations (density, germination, light, power of lamp, shade) were run over ten weeks with groups of three or four students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in groups to design their own method, generate arguments including claim, evidence and justification, and complete individual written reports that are peer reviewed and revised.
#### Constraints
- The study's teacher was experienced and taught during four months about the ADI instructional method before treatment.

### Target Learners
- Eighth-grade students in public elementary school science classes

### Learning Goals
- Scientific literacy
- Scientific inquiry practices
- Argumentation and scientific writing
