# QR-collection gymkhana combining Problem Based Learning and Game Based Learning with authentic bioinformatics problem solving

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A lesson-level pattern in which student teams collect 8 QR-encoded protein sequences scattered around the campus botanical garden and use them as starting material for a bioinformatics study. The article states "The active learning methods Problem Based Learning (PBL) and Game Based Learning (GBL) have been chosen as training strategies in this intervention proposal." Students then run BLAST identification, ortholog searches, sequence alignments and phylogenetic tree construction, and write an individual scientific report, with teachers acting as facilitators rather than lecturing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have access to molecular databases, BLAST and Seaview in a computer science classroom
- Teams require a map of the sampling area and a QR reader; theoretical content must be taught in course lectures beforehand
#### Constraints
- The activity spans two non-consecutive days for groups of about 20 students, with approximately 160 students per year divided into eight groups

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate Biology students

### Learning Goals
- Sequence identification with BLAST
- Searching molecular databases for orthologs
- Multiple sequence alignment and phylogenetic tree construction
- Scientific report writing
