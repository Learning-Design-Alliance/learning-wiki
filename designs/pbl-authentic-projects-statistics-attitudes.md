# Use project-based learning with authentic, student-defined projects in statistics courses to improve attitudes towards statistics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that statistics teaching replace textbook-based instruction with project-based learning in which students select their own variables, craft research questions, and collect and analyze their own data in small heterogeneous groups, presenting results to peers. The quasi-experiment supports this: "the project based learning increased students' attitude towards statistics in the intervention group", while the control group showed no significant change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects drawn from real-life, familiar contexts with statistical content
- About one month for students to prepare projects
- Heterogeneous groups of about three students with end-of-term presentations
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study with two 8th-grade classes in a single urban middle school in Trabzon; students were not randomly assigned

### Target Learners
- 8th grade middle-school mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- Positive attitudes towards statistics, statistical literacy
