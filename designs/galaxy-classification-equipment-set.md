# Equipment set for the K–12 galaxy-classification implementation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The K–12 implementation uses a defined equipment set: color images one per learner, starter images such as a big book of galaxies, a final handout with the Hubble tuning fork and official classifications, physical models (pinwheel, frisbee disk, donut-and-ball, balls, rugby ball), a wide-angle light source to show shadows as 3D-to-2D projection effects, and videos or the Galaxy Zoo website. The article notes the Atlas of Galaxies "is physically large and very visible."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Color images labeled with galaxy identifier and image source, with multiple examples of each galaxy type
- a wide-angle light source for demonstrating projection effects via shadows
#### Constraints
- The concluding remarks are very full and can easily take more time than remains in a typical 50-minute class period

### Target Learners
- K–6 learners primarily
- K–12 classrooms

### Learning Goals
- galaxy morphology and color
- 3D-to-2D projection effects
- astronomical imaging
