# CDIO-ILB structure: three same-semester courses (one foundational at 50%, two supporting at 25% each) around an industry-driven PBL project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An integrated learning block (ILB) is formed by the interconnectivity of at least two courses; at Sheridan's School of MEET it combines three engineering courses offered in the same semester with a unified mission and learning outcomes. The block's deliverables are based on designing an engineering system or subunit in a project-based learning environment, with the design course as the "Foundational Course (50%)" and two supporting courses at 25% each, coordinated by a CDIO-ILB coordinator. An ILB committee with course leads, the coordinator, the Associate Dean, and industry invitees governs the block.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses selected for an ILB must have synergies and interdependencies, agreed through intradepartmental discussion
- An ILB committee including course leads, coordinator, Associate Dean, and industry invitees
#### Constraints
- The article notes very few schools have experimented with ILBs, and prior blocks generally used two courses or non-engineering courses

### Target Learners
- first- to third-year Bachelor of Engineering students

### Learning Goals
- integrating technical course content around real-world engineering design
- CDIO competencies (Conceive, Design, Implement, Operate)
