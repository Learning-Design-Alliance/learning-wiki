# Three-stage team-based PBL teaching procedure moving from problem definition through self-directed inquiry to simulation-based integration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's teaching procedure divides each scenario case into three stages: Stage I defines "healthcare problems and needs", explores prior knowledge, and forms assumptions about mechanisms; Stage II defines learning issues and devotes a 45-minute step to "Self-study & application of nursing skills"; Stage III integrates new knowledge with prior knowledge in a 50-minute scenario simulation followed by evaluation and reflection. Teams of 9–10 students (six teams total) work through timed steps with instructors as facilitators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students arranged in learning teams of 9–10 with mentors serving as facilitators
- Scenario cases aligned with highly ranked causes of death and injury
- Instructors trained as PBL seed teachers
#### Constraints
- Validated in a two-credit, 18-week elective Emergency Care course for senior nursing students at one Taiwanese medical university

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- core nursing competencies including assessment, emergency-care skills, communication, and teamwork and collaboration
