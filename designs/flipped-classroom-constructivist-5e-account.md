## Description
The article frames flipped teaching as grounded in constructivism, described as a "problem-solving approach to learning" in which students actively create knowledge through social interaction while educators act as learning facilitators. It maps the flipped classroom onto the 5E Instructional Model — engagement, exploration, explanation, elaboration, and evaluation — arguing that shifting passive knowledge acquisition to the pre-class phase and dedicating class time to active, collaborative, problem-based learning fosters deeper understanding and higher-order thinking skills.
