# Five topics for implementing healthcare simulation in patient safety programmes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organises its simulation training programme around "the main topics identified for the successful implementation of healthcare simulation in patient safety programmes: technical skills, nontechnical skills, assessment, effectiveness, and system probing". This five-topic framework, drawn from a consensus process the article cites, structures what each simulation session addresses: scenarios may be clinical using a patient simulator or interpersonal communication simulations, and each session ends with participants and observers evaluating and proposing solutions to identified problems. The framework anchors the programme's claim to place in situ simulation at level 4 of the Kirkpatrick model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions must be designed so that the most appropriate type of simulation for each MMC case is discussed by the simulation team, health services researchers, the local MMC coordinator, and the ward health manager
#### Constraints
- The framework is applied to simulation in the patient's clinical environment within hospital patient safety programmes

### Target Learners
- inter-professional hospital ward teams

### Learning Goals
- patient safety competences spanning technical skills, nontechnical skills, assessment, effectiveness, and system probing
