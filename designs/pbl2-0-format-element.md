# PBL 2.0: a revised problem-based learning format with reduced objectives, pair/trio-led discussions, and no lectures

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
PBL 2.0 is a revised PBL format implemented in the 11-week Brain and Mind course at Weill Cornell, meeting "for 90 min two times per week" in 10 groups of 10 second-year students with faculty facilitators. Learning objectives were "reduced through deletion and consolidation (from 10 to between 4 and 6)"; pairs/trios led interactive discussions using talking points posed to the group, with lectures, PowerPoint, and extensive handouts prohibited, and computers banned on the first day to preserve problem-solving. Facilitators corrected errors and managed time without dominating sessions, and grading emphasized contributions to discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty and student orientations on the new format and amended evaluation expectations
- Facilitators instructed to evaluate students on contributions to group problem solving and discussion stimulation rather than stock lectures
- The same amount of classroom time as the prior format
#### Constraints
- Twenty percent of respondents reported no adherence at all to the model
- Some long-serving faculty facilitators had difficulty transitioning to the new model
- Evaluated in a single second-year medical neuroscience course without randomization

### Target Learners
- second-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- problem-solving skills
- acquisition and discussion of biomedical learning objectives
- interactive group learning of neuroscience, neuroanatomy, pharmacology, clinical neurology, and psychiatry topics
