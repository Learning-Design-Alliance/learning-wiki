# Two-day induction bootcamp combining ten simulation skills sessions for specialty-transition trainees

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The bootcamp is a compact induction format delivered over two days at the transition into a new specialty role, here ten 60-minute skills sessions "delivered by either a senior ENT registrar or an ENT consultant". Sessions cover technical skills (airway, rhinology, mastoidectomy, bronchoscopy, tracheostomy), non-technical skills (NOTSS, communication, simulated ward round) and technology-supported practice (HoloLens, Touch Surgery). Sessions "included the use of different simulated environments, models and technology to provide a safe environment for trainees to practice", pairing simulation training with didactic learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions delivered by instructors with prior experience in the skills session(s)
#### Constraints
- Artificial mannequins introduce artifacts that impede realism and full transfer of skills to the bedside, per the article's cited literature

### Target Learners
- Novice first-year specialty trainee registrars (ENT ST3) entering higher surgical training

### Learning Goals
- Technical and non-technical skills for managing common ENT presentations in ward, elective and emergency settings
