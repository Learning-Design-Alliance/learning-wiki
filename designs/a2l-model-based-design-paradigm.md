# Model-based design paradigm for creating instructional items

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The model-based design paradigm is the project's procedure for developing items. It starts from findings of cognitive research — alternative conceptions, expert–novice differences, cognitive overload, hierarchical analysis — and three premises, including that "Proficient problem solving requires structured knowledge". From a cognitive framework it derives concept-based problem solving, then uses "a “web” or “matrix” of cognitive goals and habits of mind to create instructional materials" for the project's materials, producing items that shift instruction toward beneficial mental processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Development starts from cognitive research findings and the three stated premises about cognition and problem-solving proficiency.
#### Constraints
- The report states "There is no cookbook for developing items"; practice, testing, observation, and reflection are the most useful guide, and copying superficial features of successful items is the most common pitfall.
