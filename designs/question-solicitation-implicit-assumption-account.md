# Question solicitation scripts differ in whether they assume students already possess questions — the proposed mechanism behind invitational phrasing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that solicitation scripts convey different assumptions: the 2020 script, "What. . . questions do [you all] have. . . ?", "contains an implicit assumption: that students already possess questions at the time of the solicitation", while the 2019 formats probe whether no questions exist before proceeding. The authors speculate a perceived perfunctory intent could make questions seem unwanted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The solicitation must be a verbal address by the instructor to the whole class that explicitly inquires whether students have questions
#### Constraints
- The supposed implicit assumption cannot currently be measured: "there is no way to measure this supposed implicit assumption via an assessment tool or other means, because no such tool currently exists"
- The authors note more research is required to confirm which features influence student questioning

### Target Learners
- students in large-enrollment introductory physics courses
- students from groups traditionally underrepresented in physics

### Learning Goals
- increase student questioning during class
- promote equitable classroom participation
