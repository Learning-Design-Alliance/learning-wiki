# Multi-part inquiry pattern: three independent data-type analyses converging on one phylogeny question

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A laboratory/discussion pattern in which students work through three stand-alone activities—DNA pseudogene sequences, amino acid sequences of a functional protein, and chromosomal maps—each challenging them to propose a hypothetical phylogram for the same set of species. The article states that "by engaging several types of data addressing the same underlying question, we demonstrate to students how scientists use multiple lines of evidence to support or refute hypotheses." Parts can be done separately, simplified, or extended with quantitative parsimony analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actual scientific datasets from closely related species
- Group work setting (laboratory, discussion, or other group work)
- A postactivity discussion to draw connections across the three parts
#### Constraints
- The authors note activities are best preceded by a lecture on systematics, though a short primer might suffice depending on student background

### Target Learners
- postsecondary majors-level introductory biology students
- secondary-level biology students
- nonmajors

### Learning Goals
- phylogeny, cladistics, and systematics
- hypothesis-making from molecular data
- understanding multiple lines of evidence in science
