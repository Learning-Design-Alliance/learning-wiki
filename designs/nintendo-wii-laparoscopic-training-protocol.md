# Structured Nintendo Wii training protocol using three eye-hand coordination games

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A four-week video-game training intervention built on the Nintendo Wii console, whose wireless Remote is "a handheld pointing device, able to detect movement in three dimensions". Residents trained 60 minutes a day, five days a week, for four weeks, using both hands, on three games chosen for high demands of eye-hand coordination, movement precision, depth perception and 3D visualization: Wii Sports Tennis, Wii Table Tennis, and Battle at high altitude. Training was conducted under supervision in a dedicated room alongside the laparoscopic simulator station.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants with low prior video-game experience (less than 1 hour a week in the last 10 years) and no prior laparoscopic simulator experience
- Supervised training in a dedicated room with Wii consoles
#### Constraints
- Studied as a pilot in post-graduate I-II year surgical residents with low laparoscopic experience; effects on later learning-curve stages untested

### Target Learners
- Post-graduate I-II year surgical residents

### Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic basic skills
- Eye-hand coordination and economy of instrument movement
- Virtual cholecystectomy performance
