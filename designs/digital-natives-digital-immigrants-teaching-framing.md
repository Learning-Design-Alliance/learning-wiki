# Digital natives versus digital immigrants as a framing for redesigning university science teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article adopts Prensky's digital natives/immigrants distinction as its contextual framework: over 90% of admitted students are under 20 and grew up in computational environments with permanent access to Wikipedia and smartphone applications, having spent fewer than 5000 hours reading books but more than 10000 hours gaming and 20000 hours watching television. The authors argue teachers, mostly over 30, are digital immigrants who must adapt, capture digital natives' attention, and introduce concepts moving "de lo estático a lo dinámico, de lo puntual a lo continuo, de lo escrito a lo visual", shifting from being the sole information source to guides and mentors who help students evaluate online information quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must adapt to the new environment and speak students' own language; the university should emphasize learning (what students do) over teaching (what professors do)
#### Constraints
- The framework is presented as contextual motivation for the course design, not as a tested hypothesis in this article

### Target Learners
- university students under 20 (digital natives) in science careers

### Learning Goals
- information-quality evaluation
- early exposure to research methodologies
