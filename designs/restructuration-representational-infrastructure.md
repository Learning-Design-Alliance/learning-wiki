# Restructuration: transformation of a discipline's representational infrastructure as a methodology for making complex domains learnable

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Restructuration, as the article presents it, arises from changes in the representational infrastructure of a domain: "the adoption of new symbolic or computational forms that alter how learners engage with and make sense of a domain." It goes beyond curricular repackaging, redefining what it means to understand something and enabling new forms of reasoning, learning, and cultural practices previously inaccessible. The article proposes designing new representational forms for QIS—such as agent-based models or interactive simulations—so that foundational concepts like complex numbers, vector spaces, or linear algebra can be engaged without a large body of prerequisite knowledge, potentially allowing learning at a much earlier age.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained attention to how students make sense of QIS concepts, using data collected on how learners think, reason, and develop understanding
- Iterative design, experimentation, and theoretical refinement over years, which the article calls high-risk, high-yield research
#### Constraints
- Meaningful restructuration in QIS will not result from curricular repackaging alone
- The article states this constitutes a conceptual transformation that may take years to achieve

### Target Learners
- Pre-college learners, including students typically considered too young to engage with quantum content

### Learning Goals
- Reasoning with QIS concepts across quantum computing, communication, and sensing
- Engaging with foundational concepts like complex numbers, vector spaces, or linear algebra without full prerequisite mathematics
