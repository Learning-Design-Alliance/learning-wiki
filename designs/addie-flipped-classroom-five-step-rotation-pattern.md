# Five-step ADDIE-based flipped classroom pattern for a clinical rotation (pre-class materials, group discussion, bedside practice, case reporting, end-point assessment)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-to-rotation-level pattern in which each ADDIE phase is operationalised: analysis via admission assessment of knowledge base; design via distributing texts, videos, cases and 3–5 guiding questions before class; development via targeted questioning and assigned tasks; implementation via bedside practical teaching and special case discussion; evaluation via end-of-training assessment. The article reports the pattern ran "a total of 38 class hours" over weeks two to four of the rotation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers prepare and distribute teaching resources (PPT, video, cases, literature) on a social platform in advance
- A teaching team including subject lead teachers and ADDIE specialists
- Access to patients for bedside questioning, physical examination and case discussion
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in a one-month neurology residency rotation with 90 residents at a single hospital; the authors state the limited sample size, one-month duration and single Mini-CEX outcome measure may affect the results

### Target Learners
- medical residents in standardized specialty training

### Learning Goals
- independent learning and clinical thinking
- theoretical knowledge and clinical skills
- communication, collaboration and humanistic care
