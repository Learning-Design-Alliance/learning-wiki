# KCSE-targeted drill module built from exam questions expanded into large randomized drillsets with graded difficulty

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The tutor-web module targeting the KCSE national exam was built by taking questions from an example exam and writing a computer program for each question, generating "drill sets of 100 drill items for each question." When some exam questions proved far too easy or far too difficult, new batches of deliberately easy and harder drills were added, and items extending the national curriculum were included, designed to be self-explanatory but requiring new thinking, with detailed answers provided after incorrect responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computer program per exam question to generate drill items in large numbers
- Random-number generation of items to avoid direct repetition
- Detailed explanations shown immediately after an answer is chosen
#### Constraints
- Initially all secondary school drills were based on a single exam, and some of those exam questions were far too easy or far too difficult
- The tablet reward is expensive, so curriculum-extending drills were added to require considerable work

### Target Learners
- secondary school students preparing for the KCSE national exam in Kenya

### Learning Goals
- passing the KCSE national exam and general mathematical thinking beyond rote learning
