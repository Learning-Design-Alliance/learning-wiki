# Time-pressured picture-cued writing tasks elicit obligatory article use for measuring implicit knowledge.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A time-pressured picture-cued writing test was used to measure learners' implicit knowledge of English articles. Students wrote a coherent story of at least 150 words based on 10 sequential pictures, with word prompts given for each picture to elicit noun phrases with English articles. The Target-Like-Use formula (Pica, 1991) was applied to calculate accuracy of use. Three different sets of sequential pictures were used for pretest, Posttest 1, and Posttest 2 to investigate whether written CF helps learners improve their linguistic accuracy in a new piece of writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sequential pictures with word prompts eliciting obligatory occasions for 'a(n)' and 'the'
- Time pressure of 30 minutes
- Target-Like-Use formula applied for scoring
#### Constraints
- Writing tasks, even when pressured, cannot afford a convincing measure of implicit knowledge because they allow for controlled processing and monitoring for accuracy (Shintani & Ellis, 2013)

### Target Learners
- low-intermediate EFL learners

### Learning Goals
- implicit knowledge of English articles
