# SCIG patient training should ensure high patient confidence by the final training session while keeping overall training duration concise

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that SCIG training for patients with primary immunodeficiency should be designed so that patients feel confident by their final training session, while keeping the overall duration of training concise. The authors state that "while thorough training with a skilled professional would facilitate greater confidence in SCIG use, the overall duration of training should ideally be concise to ensure the benefits outweigh the burden of time-consuming training." This principle is grounded in the finding that higher post-training confidence was associated with better PROs across multiple domains, while longer total training time was associated with lower ease of training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training delivered by a skilled professional to build patient confidence in SCIG self-infusion
#### Constraints
- The exact amount of SCIG training, training location, and ease of training will differ at a patient level

### Target Learners
- Patients with primary immunodeficiency transitioning to SCIG self-administration
- Parent/caregiver proxy respondents for pediatric patients

### Learning Goals
- Developing confidence and competence in SCIG self-infusion technique
- Understanding infusion parameters and ancillary supplies
