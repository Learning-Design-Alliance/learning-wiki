# First-order luminosity-scaling formula for the circumstellar habitable-zone distance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article derives a first-order expression for where an Earth-like planet would orbit a star: the habitable-zone distance in AU equals the square root of the stellar luminosity in solar luminosities, because "Of ﬁrst order, this distance scales with the luminosity of the star." Activity 2 uses it to estimate the habitable-zone distance of Kepler-62 before students compare it with a sophisticated model calculation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The luminosity of the target star relative to the solar luminosity, as tabulated for Kepler-62 in the activity.
#### Constraints
- First-order approximation only: the article notes the habitable zone is not a well-defined range and depends on many properties that allow water to be present in liquid form, and the formula neglects the stellar surface temperature.

### Target Learners
- Students aged 12-16 in middle and secondary school

### Learning Goals
- Understanding that the size and extent of a habitable zone depends on the luminosity of a star
