# The VSRT additive interferometer's beat-signal power is mathematically identical to the complex visibility amplitude of a cross-correlation interferometer

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article derives, for the two-dimensional single-plane case, that the amplitude of the VSRT's beat signal for N incoherent sources equals the magnitude of the complex visibility for that baseline. It concludes that "the power of the beat signal with the VSRT interferometer is identical to the amplitude of the complex visibility for that baseline for any arrangement of sources." This equivalence is what allows the table-top additive instrument to teach the same visibility-function concepts as professional cross-correlation arrays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The two feeds use different mix-down (local oscillator) frequencies so that combining the added signals produces a beat signal of about 500 kHz
- Sources are incoherent, so powers are added
#### Constraints
- The discussion is simplified to a two-dimensional situation in which the source structure and antennas are located in a single plane, with one-dimensional baselines and source structure

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and astronomy students

- understanding the Visibility Function and its relation to source structure
