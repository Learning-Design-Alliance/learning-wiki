# Astro-animation course: a 15-week, jointly-taught art-science collaboration between MICA animation students and NASA Goddard scientist mentors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An undergraduate elective at the Maryland Institute College of Art, jointly taught by an astrophysicist and an animator, in which student groups of two or three are randomly assigned a research topic and a mentor scientist and produce animations presented at a NASA GSFC screening. The course "takes place for 15 weeks with three credits awarded for science and three credits for animation", beginning with astronomy and transitioning to an animation studio. Scientists present topics such as black holes, supernovae, dark matter, and gravitational waves; students visit NASA GSFC to present storyboards, and finished films are posted on AstroAnimation.org.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to collaborating scientists who volunteer as mentors and present their research topics to the class
- A venue and scientist audience for a final screening of the student animations
#### Constraints
- Student films are free-form artistic responses constrained by scientific rigor, deliberately different from NASA's accurate graphic visualizations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate animation students at an art and design college

### Learning Goals
- understand basic concepts of astrophysics and the possibilities and limitations of scientific research
- learn that science is a way of thinking as much as a body of knowledge
- translate scientific concepts into animation reaching a broader public
