# Computer-based multiplicity counting as the core instructional pattern for introducing entropy and the second law

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a thermal physics unit around a computer counting microstates of a simple model system: students tabulate multiplicities of interacting Einstein solids, observe that some macrostates are overwhelmingly more probable, and then derive entropy and temperature from entropy-vs-energy graphs. The authors write that the computer "makes it practical to do the calculation for systems large enough to display the irreversible behavior of macroscopic objects", linking the statistical definition of entropy to the second law without sophisticated mathematics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computational environment able to compute binomial coefficients (spreadsheet, stand-alone program, or graphing calculator)
#### Constraints
- The authors proved the approach's irreversibility argument only for two weakly coupled Einstein solids, though they argue similar counting applies to other macroscopic systems

### Target Learners
- first-year calculus-based introductory physics students
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- understand how the second law arises from the statistical definition of entropy
- connect the statistical and classical approaches to entropy
