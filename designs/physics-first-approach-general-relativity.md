# The 'physics first' approach to teaching general relativity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A curricular framework in which "the simplest physically relevant solutions to the Einstein equation are introduced first, without derivation, as curved space-times whose properties and observable consequences can be explored by a study of the motion of test particles and light rays." The four steps are: exhibit the simplest spacetime geometries first; derive predictions via test-particle and light-ray orbits; apply predictions to realistic astrophysical situations; then motivate and solve the Einstein equation. This reordering of the deductive 'math first' sequence brings students to interesting physical phenomena as quickly as possible and connects to classical mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An intermediate course in mechanics is the single essential physics prerequisite; some acquaintance with special relativity is useful but is developed de novo
#### Constraints
- The approach does not follow the logically appealing deductive order; it may not be indicated when there is enough time for the deductive order, when students already have significant preparation in differential geometry, or when the mathematics is of central interest

### Target Learners
- junior/senior undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- understanding basic physical phenomena of general relativity such as black holes, cosmology, and experimental tests
