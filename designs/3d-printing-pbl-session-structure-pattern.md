# Combined 3D printing + PBL teaching session structure: case-based model printing, question posing, and group discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's experimental teaching intervention follows a repeating session structure that couples a physical 3D-printed vascular model with problem-based learning. The lecturer selects clinical cases per the teaching plan, presents complete clinical and imaging data, and "posed questions based on the characteristics of the disease"; participants take part in printing the 1:1 intracranial vascular model, then hold group discussions in which they list clinical characteristics and propose treatment methods, consulting books and communicating about problems found. Students are the main body and teachers play an assistive role.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- typical clinical cases with complete clinical and imaging data; pre-printed 1:1 vascular models validated by senior professors; group discussion time with access to reference materials
#### Constraints
- applied to cerebrovascular disease teaching in neurosurgery; relies on models that cannot reconstruct small vessels, skull base nerves, or other brain tissue structures

### Target Learners
- medical interns (residents and fourth-year students) in neurosurgery clinical rotations

### Learning Goals
- clinical problem-analysis and problem-solving skills
- learning interest and motivation
- understanding of cerebrovascular disease diagnosis and treatment
