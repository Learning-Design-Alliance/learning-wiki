# Peer-led mock OSCE intervention (student-designed and student-administered mock Objective Structured Clinical Examination)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A formative assessment activity in which students conceptualize and administer a mock OSCE before the summative exam. Twelve students spent four hours designing five stations with scenarios and checklists, then acted as examiners for sixteen examinee peers, with each six-minute station followed by "one additional minute for immediate feedback". A supervising professor "corrected conceptual errors without altering the structure of the stations". The element was evaluated in a randomized controlled trial against a no-intervention control group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supervising professor to allocate station topics and rectify content-specific inaccuracies
- An online course covering OSCE history, station and checklist design, and exam organization
- Station themes drawn from the semester's covered course content
#### Constraints
- The professor did not modify station structure, only inaccuracies in medical knowledge
- Seven students randomized to the intervention could not attend due to academic scheduling conflicts and were reassigned to control

### Target Learners
- Fifth-semester medical students taking their first summative OSCE

### Learning Goals
- Clinical skills practice (history taking, physical examination, communication)
- Familiarity with OSCE format and reduced test anxiety
