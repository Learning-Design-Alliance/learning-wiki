# Coffee-filter terminal-velocity lab activity for comparing linear and drag models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A mechanics lab activity in which "students drop stacks of coffee filters, use a position sensor to identify regions in which the stacks travel at terminal velocity, and then evaluate whether the terminal velocity is linear or quadratic with the mass of the stack." Students decide what range of position-versus-time data is sufficiently linear and quantify their uncertainty, then use fitting tools from an invention task to compare the two possible models by chi-squared. The two models look very similar if the uncertainty is too large or the range of masses is too small, so students must improve their experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must decide what 'sufficient' or 'reasonable' means, with feedback from the instructor
- The two models must look similar under large uncertainty or a small range of masses to require iteration
#### Constraints
- The activity is offered as a demonstration of the operationalization of the goals, not a coherent description of the course transformation; it builds from activities already carried out in traditional labs to save costs

### Target Learners
- students in first-semester, calculus-based introductory mechanics

### Learning Goals
- identify, minimize and/or quantify sources of statistical uncertainty
- decide what and how much data are gathered to produce reliable measurements
- plot a residual graph and calculate the chi-squared statistic to compare data to a model
- design, carry out, and improve an experiment to test competing physical models
