# Calculate first, then cross-check with a physical model: students verify computed longitudes against the Longitude Clock

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This lesson-level pattern pairs symbolic calculation with a concrete visual model so each checks the other. In Activity 1, "The students calculate the time diﬀerence and the resulting longitude by applying the equations below. The results are then cross-checked with the Longitude Clock (paper or app version)." The article also offers the reverse focus as a variation: the teacher may start with the Longitude Clock and use the calculations as the cross-check instead, and may demonstrate the first example for students who struggle with the equations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The Longitude Clock (cardboard or app version), worksheets with the time-reading table, and the equations Δt = 12 h − TST and λ = Δt · 15°/h.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the clock's precision illustrates the procedure but its time resolution is too small to determine longitude with the precision needed for actual navigation.

### Target Learners
- Students aged 14–19 in middle or secondary school

### Learning Goals
- Applying the longitude equation to time readings
- Connecting symbolic calculation to a visual model of Earth's rotation

### Claims
- Longitude From Time 15 Degrees Per Hour [+M]
