# Gaze-based diagnosis of content-related student difficulties for misconception-driven adaptive feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An adaptive educational system framework in which eye tracking data collected during problem solving is used to diagnose not only that a learner needs help but which specific content-related difficulty they have, enabling misconception-driven feedback during the solution process. The article states that "to the best of our knowledge, there is no system that makes diagnosis about the problem-solving strategy and that is able to predict specific content-related student difficulties on the basis of gaze data." The framework rests on the eye-mind hypothesis linking gaze direction to cognitive processing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- RTA protocols or equivalent coding to establish difficulty categories
- expert rating to determine relevant AOIs
#### Constraints
- The link between gaze data and cognitive processes is ambiguous, as longer looks may reflect struggle or knowledge integration

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- identifying student difficulties in graphical differentiation
