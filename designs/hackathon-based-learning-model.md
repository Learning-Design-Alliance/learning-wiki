# Hackathon-based learning model: time-bounded, radically collocated, flat-hierarchy collaboration events that reframe the classroom as team-driven social learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Hackathons are "time-bounded collaboration events that bring together disparate groups to work intensely toward shared goals". A key feature is "radical collocation", and the classical classroom "is completely reframed as an interactive, team-driven social event, with self-directed learning". Teachers become facilitators who adapt the environment on demand, and teams balance beginners and experts so all can meaningfully contribute.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Radical collocation: team members work in close proximity, and nearly continuously during the project duration
- A shared goal with all participants collaborating rather than competing, ensuring everyone has a vested interest and can meaningfully contribute
- Highly engaged facilitators who adapt the learning environment to meet participant needs on demand
#### Constraints
- Hackathons are resource intensive in terms of physical infrastructure, hardware and software access, and the need for highly engaged facilitators
- The short project horizons and focus on solutions can limit design choices and hinder deep thinking about the problem
- The flat hierarchy and open access model boosts creativity, but may lead to gaps in required subject matter expertise

### Target Learners
- participants from high school students to faculty across physics, engineering, biophysics, and statistics

### Learning Goals
- big-data analysis and community coding skills
- interdisciplinary collaboration and teamwork
- design reasoning and problem identification in a human-centered context
