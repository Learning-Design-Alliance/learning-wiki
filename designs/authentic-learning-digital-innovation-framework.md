# Authentic learning environment framework for cultivating digital innovation skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article applies the Herrington et al. (2010) authentic learning framework as the theoretical basis for teaching digital innovation skills, arguing that developing 21st-century skills "can be enhanced by an authentic learning environment in which students must be engaged in an inventive and realistic task that provides opportunities for complex collaborative activities". The framework comprises nine elements coded A1–A9: authentic contexts, authentic tasks, expert performances and modelling, multiple roles and perspectives, collaborative learning, reflection, articulation, coaching and scaffolding, and authentic assessment. Table 1 maps each element to concrete course-design implementations such as NGO partnerships, blogs, peer review and a semester-long capstone project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Real-world tasks with industry or community partners
- Assessment that evaluates the whole collaborative process
#### Constraints
- Group work and collaboration require ongoing monitoring to facilitate conflict resolution

### Target Learners
- Higher education Information Systems students

### Learning Goals
- 21st-century skills including critical thinking, creativity, collaboration and digital innovation capability
