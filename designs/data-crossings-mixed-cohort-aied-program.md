# Data Crossings: a year-long mixed-cohort, no-code AI education program with four integrated components

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Data Crossings is an NSF-funded (ExLENT initiative) year-long AI education program that intentionally integrates non-STEM undergraduates and adult learners in shared instructional and project-based environments. It is "organized around four integrated components delivered over an academic year": an Explorations no-code course, parallel Experiential Learning Studios, team-based capstone projects, and in-situ experiential learning including internships or the RAISEuP fellowship, plus workshops on transferable "superskills". The first cohort comprised 19 participants (12 undergraduates, 7 adult learners) selected from more than 200 applicants, with applicants holding formal STEM training in computing excluded by design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection criteria prioritizing programmatic fit, motivation for non-technical AI engagement, and readiness for mixed-cohort learning rather than technical proficiency
- No-code and low-code tools so participants can explore datasets and interpret model outputs without programming syntax
- Human facilitation through guided discussion, structured reflection, and peer interaction
#### Constraints
- Implemented within a single institutional and regional context
- Cohort size is modest and participants self-select through application, which the authors state limits generalizability
- Post-program learning outcomes for the first cohort were not yet available at the time of writing

### Target Learners
- Non-STEM undergraduate students from humanities, social sciences, arts, and business
- Adult learners and career-transitioning professionals

### Learning Goals
- AI literacy and data literacy without coding
- Ethical reasoning and socio-technical judgment
- Applied AI in policy, organizational, and community contexts
