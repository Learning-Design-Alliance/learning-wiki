# Hybrid multimodal simulation as the ideal architecture for orthopaedic surgical training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's organizing framework is that an ideal surgical simulator combines physical and virtual elements: physical models provide realistic haptic feedback with real instruments, while computer visualization and AR add objective performance assessment, guidance, visibility of hidden structures, and spatial sound. Citing a literature review, the authors state that "an ideal simulator should be multimodal, combining haptic, visual and audio technology to create an immersive training environment." HipSim instantiates this framework for open surgery, where interaction forces are too large for conventional haptic interfaces.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physical components that mimic the mechanical properties of human tissue so trainees can develop force-feedback memory
#### Constraints
- Conventional haptic interfaces cannot render the large forces of open surgery tool-bone interaction, so haptic equipment would be limited to reamer-bone interactions while soft-tissue interaction remains physical

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees and novices in orthopaedic procedures

### Learning Goals
- technical surgical skills rehearsal outside the operating theatre
- realistic haptic and perceptual experience of surgery
