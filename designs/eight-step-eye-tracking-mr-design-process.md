# Eight-step empirically driven, user-centred design process for MR learning environments in STEM experiments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design framework in which MR applications for hands-on STEM experiments are derived from empirical comparison of novice and expert approaches. "Our framework consists of eight subsequent steps using eye -tracking data and interviews to trace the experimental problem-solving approach of novices compared to experts." Steps are: (1) divide participants into novices and experts via a conceptual knowledge test; (2) capture two experimentation phases (overview-building and problem-solving) with eye-tracking; (3) semi-structured interviews on approaches, mental models and wishes for the MR application; (4) joint evaluation of knowledge, models, eye-tracking and interviews, producing areas of interest, gaze heatmaps and learning paths; (5) MR design based on step 4, applying CTML principles to avoid cognitive overload; (6) implementation; (7) usability study with think-aloud and a system usability scale; (8) revision, with steps 7-8 repeatable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The experiment must be conducted by both experts and novices to analyze and compare their approaches and to find similarities and differences.
- A conceptual knowledge paper-pencil test covering domain- and problem-specific topics and knowledge about suitable model representations, used to divide the sample.
- Eye-tracking capture during the experiment to trace learning paths and areas of interest of both groups.
#### Constraints
- The framework is proposed for the focused realm of virtually enhancing physical hands-on experiments in STEM where students are given a problem to solve, not as a widely applicable framework for all educational MR applications.
- Design of visualizations is decided in steps 4 and 5; the usability study focuses on computer-human interaction rather than domain-specific learning.

### Target Learners
- Novice university students in STEM laboratory experiments

### Learning Goals
- Problem solving in hands-on STEM experiments, e.g. understanding the function of capacitors and coils in an electric circuit
