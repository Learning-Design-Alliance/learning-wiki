# Backward design combined with a four-phase instructional framework (Introduction, Presentation, Practice, Application) for planning iVR learning units

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's planning framework starts from desired learning outcomes and works "backwards" to identify objectives, skills, assessment criteria, and finally a sequence of learning activities. Activities are grouped into "the four instructional phases of Introduction, Presentation, Practice, and Application," a framework the authors say is common across instructional design models and integrates easily with Backward design. Each phase carries guiding principles: Introduction activates prior experience and sets expectations, Presentation distinguishes need-to-know content, Practice scaffolds skills with cues and feedback, and Application mimics real-world tasks. FlowTrainer's LLM interaction follows this hierarchy when generating lesson plans.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning activities must first be developed and stored in a library by VR developers, framed via Backward design from overarching learning outcomes.
#### Constraints
- Configurable parameters of activities are limited to timing, message, and hint settings in the current version.

### Target Learners
- Adult manufacturing trainees learning MIG welding basics

### Learning Goals
- Outcome-aligned lesson planning for skill training
- Welding safety, equipment, wire-change maintenance, and Tee joint welding skills
