# Three targeted skill areas for QIST higher education: Theory & Analytics, Computation & Simulation, Experiment & Real World

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article classifies the skills learners should achieve in QIST higher education into three areas that are engaged by different teaching approaches across all eight CF domains. As printed, "we see possible targeted skills in three main areas: Theory & Analytics, Computation & Simulation and Experiment & Real World." Theory & Analytics covers derivation, proof and calculation (e.g. Dirac notation); Computation & Simulation covers programming in languages and libraries such as Qiskit, Pennylane, OpenQASM and Q#; Experiment & Real World covers planning setups, device handling, calibration and data analysis. The areas are explicitly not a classification of concepts — many concepts, such as quantum teleportation, engage several or all three.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators consider targeted skills after choosing concepts, since plain knowledge transfer such as a purely traditional lecture is not effective
#### Constraints
- Not a classification scheme for concepts; some topics may be limited to developing only a subset of the three skills

### Target Learners
- University QIST students from diverse disciplinary backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- Derivation and proof, programming and simulation, and experimental planning and data analysis in QIST
