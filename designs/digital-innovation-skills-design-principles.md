# Nine design principles for teaching digital innovation skills through authentic learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A pattern for designing learning environments that develop digital business innovation skills, refined over three DBR iterations at a South African university. The final principles are: "Ensure collaboration takes place", "Provide opportunities for students to find their own solutions", "Implement a working digital innovation in a business", "Incorporate a design-thinking ethos", "Ensure that students utilise interdisciplinary skills", "Implement a formal process of reflection", "Allow for experimentation", "Ensure that tasks culminate in a capstone project", and "Partner with entrepreneurs". Each principle is mapped to authentic learning elements (A1–A9) and skill sets (S1–S8) from the Kereluik et al. framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Industry partners willing to host student digital innovation projects
- A blended learning environment supporting online and face-to-face collaboration
- Assessment rubrics covering the entire process, not just the end product
#### Constraints
- Conducted at a single university, limiting generalisability of the findings
- The study's design and approach may not apply to other contexts or disciplines

### Target Learners
- Information Systems students in higher education

### Learning Goals
- Digital innovation skills
- Collaboration
- Design thinking
- Interdisciplinary problem-solving
