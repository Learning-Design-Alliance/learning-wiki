# English-motivated versus concept-motivated ASL signing styles for STEM signs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distinguishes two ASL signing styles for STEM vocabulary. English-motivated (EM) signs connect concepts through English words and "generally fall into three categories" of borrowed signs, initialized signs, and fingerspelling. Concept-motivated (CM) signs use visuospatial depiction; the article states "the iconicity of CM signing is more closely matched to the scientiﬁc or technical meaning of STEM signs". In the lessons, CM videos used three-dimensional, visuospatial depictions of content while EM videos used a more linear signing style with more fingerspelled terms. The article situates both on an embodied-learning spectrum, treating sign production by learners as more embodied than observation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fluent ASL signers with sufficient knowledge of ASL and STEM content to perceive the visuospatial information conveyed in CM signs
#### Constraints
- The CM style relies on more conceptual knowledge to understand the added visuospatial information in the signs, which the EM style does not

### Target Learners
- deaf and hard of hearing (DHH) students learning STEM through ASL

### Learning Goals
- STEM conceptual understanding
- accessible language of instruction in ASL
