# GTA preparation course integrating pedagogy, physics content, and professional development in an Orientation-plus-Follow-Ups structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-semester course for first-year PhD GTAs, taught every Fall since 2013, whose goals are to help graduate students develop learner-centered teaching practices, give and receive feedback, manage classroom dynamics, and identify transferable skills. It is "structured in two parts: Orientation and Follow-Ups", with the Orientation a series of workshops comprising approximately 70% of contact hours before teaching duties, including Microteaching and Lab Simulation practice, followed by one-hour Follow-Up meetings every ~2-3 weeks during the semester. All class meetings are interactive, using think-pair-share, group discussion, and problem-solving activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course instructor with significant GTA experience and involvement in PER and educational development
- Classroom observations of GTAs during the semester, used with permission as discussion examples
#### Constraints
- Developed for first-year PhD GTAs in a physics school at a single institution

### Target Learners
- first-year PhD graduate teaching assistants in physics

### Learning Goals
- learner-centered teaching practices
- classroom management
- giving and receiving feedback on teaching
- transferable professional skills
