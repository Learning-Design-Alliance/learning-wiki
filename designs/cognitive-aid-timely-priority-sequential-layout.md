# Interactive cognitive aid screen design pattern: timely information, timer-triggered priority, and sequential-versus-alternative button layout

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article articulates three user-experience design criteria applied when building the app's screens. First, "timely information": each screen gathers only the information necessary for the current phase of the PALS algorithm, communicated both visually and acoustically, aiming to reduce load on the team leader's working memory through multichannel communication. Second, "priority": actions triggered by timers, such as epinephrine administration, take precedence over other on-screen actions. Third, "sequential versus alternative choices": sequential actions are displayed as vertically aligned rectangular buttons, while alternative choices are organized as horizontally aligned square buttons. This pattern makes a complex, time-critical guideline algorithm navigable under high cognitive load.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content must be decomposed into screens matched to phases of the target algorithm
- Timer-triggered critical actions must be given display priority
#### Constraints
- Developed and evaluated specifically for pediatric cardiac arrest management by team leaders in high-fidelity simulation

### Target Learners
- clinicians leading resuscitation teams (pilot participants were pediatric residents)

### Learning Goals
- guideline-adherent execution of resuscitation actions in sequence and timing
