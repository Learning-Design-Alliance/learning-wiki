# A four-part test battery measuring tension, perseverance, environmental assimilation, and aggression

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a battery administered to 87 summoned students to probe factors behind intelligence–scholarship discrepancy. It included "a modification of the Downey will-profile test" in which a phrase was written at normal, rapid, and slow rates to yield tension and perseverance quotients; an abbreviated Rosanoff, Martin, and Rosanoff High Standard Frequency Test scored by agreement with responses of 100 eminent men of science; and three story-based deletion tests for fear, sex, and aggression, with the aggression time divided by total time of the three tests.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires subjects able to write at normal, rapid, and slow rates for the Downey-derived quotients
- Requires the normative response distribution of a group of 100 eminent men of science for scoring the frequency test
#### Constraints
- The aggression timing measure's intended arousal effects were reportedly not achieved according to subjects' introspections
