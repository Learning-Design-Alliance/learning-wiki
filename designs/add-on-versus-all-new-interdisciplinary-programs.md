# Add-on versus all-new approaches to building interdisciplinary programs, imported from bioinformatics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing lessons from bioinformatics, a now well-established discipline that combined biology, computer science, mathematics and medicine, the article names two program-development approaches. The "add-on approach" extends an existing program, such as computer science training plus biology electives, building on its strengths and rigor. The "all-new approach" combines all disciplines in one interdisciplinary curriculum, creating a new discipline unbound to existing conventions but carrying increased administrative burden across multiple deans and schools. As the article concludes, "Both approaches can succeed, and which one is better suited depends on the local circumstances."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Crucial for success is strong support and buy-in from all involved departments
#### Constraints
- The all-new approach carries a likely increased administrative burden and difficulties training students with diverse educational backgrounds, and must be evaluated against the add-on alternative
