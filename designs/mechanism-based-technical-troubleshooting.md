# Mechanism-based technical troubleshooting as target competence

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines mechanism-based technical troubleshooting as the intended competence: learners "observe device outputs, recognize abnormal waveforms or alarm patterns, relate these observations to possible technical or physical mechanisms, and decide which verification step would be appropriate." It contrasts this with output-oriented reasoning that stops at alarms or abnormal values, and structures all scenarios around an observation–hypothesis–verification–interpretation sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need prior theoretical instruction on device operating principles and a safe, repeatable environment for fault practice
#### Constraints
- The simulator is limited to interpreting simulated device behavior and does not replace workplace-based learning

### Target Learners
- biomedical device technology students
- future biomedical technical staff

### Learning Goals
- structured technical reasoning
- relating device outputs to underlying mechanisms
- selecting verification steps
