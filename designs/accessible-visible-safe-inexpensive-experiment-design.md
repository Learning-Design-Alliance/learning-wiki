# Design introductory measurement experiments that are conceptually clear, visible, tabletop-sized, safe, and inexpensive

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates its design requirements for a student experiment: the concept must be comprehensible to non-science and non-engineering majors, the optical path in air must be visible, the experiment must fit on a tabletop, students must conduct it safely without difficulty, accuracy must not sacrifice clarity for precision, and the setup must be inexpensive. It reports satisfying all these requirements, which the authors say no previously published speed-of-light experiment met together.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-cost, explicitly documented electronics for the pulsed laser and detector
- Laser output chosen to balance safety against beam visibility; the article says safety glasses are ill-advised to skip, though the beam "does not seem to necessitate such safety precautions at this time"
#### Constraints
- The authors could not find prior experiments satisfying all requirements, especially because most simpler ones require large space

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-science and non-engineering majors
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- direct measurement of a fundamental physical constant
- independent experimental work with basic instrumentation
