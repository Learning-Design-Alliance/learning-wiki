# SPICE curriculum formative assessments: Rules Task, Debugging Task, and Engineering Task with cross-domain rubrics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
SPICE (Science Projects Integrating Computing and Engineering) is "a three-week middle school curriculum unit in which students redesign a schoolyard using surface materials that minimize post-rainstorm runoff while satisfyingdesignconstraints(e.g.,cost,accessibility)." Within it, three formative assessments were scored by CoTAL: a Rules Task with a nine-subscore categorical rubric expressing rainfall-absorption-runoff conditionals, a Debugging Task with five subscores for identifying errors in a fictional classmate's block-based code, and an Engineering Task scored 0-4 on recognizing unfair design comparisons due to differing rainfall inputs. Rubrics explicitly tag science (SCI), computing (COM), and engineering (ENG) domains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessments and rubrics developed through ECD-aligned participatory design between teachers and researchers, targeting NGSS performance expectations
#### Constraints
- The Engineering Task rubric is a single 0-4 multi-class score, unlike the multi-label subscore rubrics of the other two tasks

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary and middle school students in Earth science and engineering design

### Learning Goals
- Cross-domain conceptual knowledge connecting science, computing, and engineering, including conservation of matter, conditional logic, debugging computational models, and fair tests
