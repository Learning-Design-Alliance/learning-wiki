# Efficient one-hour unit test format: three quantitative question groups plus a ten-part True/False conceptual question

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An assessment artifact for calculus-based introductory physics in which "a unit test is composed of 4 questions" — three free-response quantitative questions with 3 to 5 subparts, each on a central topic, and a conceptual question with 10 True/False subparts. "Each unit test is 4 pages long, with one question per page", and the "same design is used for test 1, test 2, and test 3", mirroring the unit's chapters so a wide variety of topics fits in one hour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Quantitative questions must carry 3 to 5 subparts on a central topic and the conceptual section 10 True/False subparts, with one question per page
#### Constraints
- Material covered in unit 4 is typically tested in the comprehensive final exam rather than a one-hour unit test

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based engineering physics (PHYS 104) students

### Learning Goals
- assessing quantitative problem-solving skills
- assessing conceptual understanding across a full unit
