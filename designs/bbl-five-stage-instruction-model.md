# Five-stage model of brain-based learning instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a five-stage instructional sequence attributed to Alsati (2004) and Marji (2010): preparation, acquisition and directed/indirect learning, elaboration, memory formation, and functional integration. Each stage has a defined role, from briefing the topic and forming neural relations through lecture and visual tools, to integrating the learner in class activities for "the deep comprehension", encoding what was learned, and finally using learning for enhancement and expansion through questioning. The researchers adopted these stages when planning and implementing the teaching of electrochemical cells and acids-bases interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must plan each stage explicitly; the memory formation stage is supported by factors such as "good nutrition, the adequate rest and link age to the prior learning"

- The stages are presented as a framework adopted by the researchers for chemistry topics; the article does not test the stages separately

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade chemistry students

### Learning Goals
