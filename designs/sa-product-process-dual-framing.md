# Dual framing of situation awareness as product and process

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article treats situation awareness as both product and process: observable behavior is scored as the product via behavioral markers, while cognitive processes are accessed through video debriefing and gaze-overlay review. The authors state the approach "is grounded in a dual understanding of SA: as a process, informing skill development; as a product, enabling performance assessment and gap identification."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to eye-tracking recordings and a structured debriefing in which residents revisit observed undesirable events and reconstruct their decision making.
#### Constraints
- The debriefing component is a pedagogical process rather than a measurement instrument and generates no quantitative scores.

### Target Learners
- anesthesiology residents

### Learning Goals
- situation awareness as a cognitive process
- guided reflection on decision making
