# Integrated techniques for Multidisciplinary Science: a proposed course connecting quantum theory to widely used physical and life science techniques

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A proposed undergraduate course built on physics but connected to chemistry, mathematics, and biology, organized around four clusters of widely used techniques: imaging and microscopy, NMR and ESR, optical and vibrational spectroscopies, and luminescence techniques. The author writes, "I propose a course titled “Integrated techniques for Multidisciplinary Science ” and focused on most advanced techniques commonly applied in the physical and life sciences", so that students learn the basic science behind each technique and apply it to real problems in areas such as medicine, pharmaceuticals, clean energy, and food.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have completed the introductory physics and chemistry classes before enrolling
- In each topic, students should learn the basic sciences behind the technique, use it in laboratories, and apply it to a real problem
#### Constraints
- The author states one course is insufficient to cover the advanced techniques associated with all aspects of modern life and technologies, and positions it as a step toward a comprehensive multi-disciplinary STEM degree program

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science majors who have completed introductory physics and chemistry

### Learning Goals
- deeper understanding of quantum mechanical principles such as particle-wave duality and diffraction
- hands-on technical skill and data analysis with widely used scientific techniques
