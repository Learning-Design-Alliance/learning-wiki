# Six-step DICE activity sequence from identifying a health problem to presenting an abstract

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
DICE includes 6 major steps that cover the major stages of a scientific investigation: (1) identifying the health problem, (2) designing an investigation including power and sample size calculations with simulations, (3) generating a unique sample of data, (4) analyzing the sample according to the plan, (5) interpreting the findings carefully, and (6) writing a short abstract to be presented in class. The second step is divided into three parts that can be repeated to calibrate power and sample size. The article notes that "The second step—designing an investigation including power and sample size calculations with simulations—is further divided into 3 parts, which can be repeated to calibrate the power and sample size of a study before moving on to step 3."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient time, with the first 2 steps requiring most of the time
- A common seed set before generating the analytical sample so all groups replicate the study under the same conditions
#### Constraints
- Implementing DICE is resource-intensive and should not be done in a short time frame (eg, less than 1 hour)

### Target Learners
- Graduate students in public health and epidemiology working in small groups of 3-5

### Learning Goals
- Study design, power and sample size reasoning, data generation, model estimation, interpretation, and scientific communication
