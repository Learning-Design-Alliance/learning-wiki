# Cinema Fermi Problems: Fermi problems whose data are extracted from a movie's dialogue and visuals

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Cinema Fermi Problems are the article's name for Fermi problems built on film scenes: 'The students have to extract data from the spoken dialogue and the visual pictures.' The authors state their goal is to train students in critical thinking and reasoning, so class problems are more sophisticated than simple warm-ups, and the calculations are often as intricate as those in an algebra-based introductory physics course. The authors screened hundreds of movies and worked tens of such problems for the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A movie scene must present extractable quantitative information in dialogue or visuals; the authors screened hundreds of movies to find usable scenes.
#### Constraints
- The calculations are 'often quite intricate, similar to those encountered in a regular, algebra-based introductory physics course.'

### Target Learners
- non-science general education undergraduates in physical science courses

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking and reasoning
- extracting and estimating quantitative data from media
