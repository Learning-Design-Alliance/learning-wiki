# Two-layer model of competitive programming competence separating algorithmic problem-solving from contest performance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that competitive programming skill comprises two analytically distinct layers. Layer 1 is algorithmic problem-solving competence: representing problems, recognizing paradigms, decomposing, implementing, debugging, and explaining. Layer 2 is contest-performance competence: triage, time allocation, risk management, and team communication under contest constraints. The article states the difference is analytical rather than absolute, noting "A student may understand dynamic programming but be slow at recognizing it", and assigns Layer 1 to introductory courses and Layer 2 to an advanced capstone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction and assessment must target each layer separately, since a single contest score conflates the two layers with problem-set sampling, prior exposure, and affective response.
#### Constraints
- The article presents the separation as analytical rather than absolute; expert contestants integrate both layers seamlessly.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory competitive programming courses after a data structures and algorithms prerequisite

### Learning Goals
- algorithmic problem-solving competence
- contest-performance competence
