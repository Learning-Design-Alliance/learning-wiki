# Inquiry-based activity sequence: reformulate an everyday question, model it, estimate semi-quantitatively, and compare alternative solution methods

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A lesson-level pattern in which a humorous everyday question is reformulated in scientific terms, modelled with explicit simplifications, answered through semi-quantitative estimation, and then cross-checked by comparing alternative solution methods. The article states it "encompassing several essential steps of the scientific process: reformulating the question in scientific terms, modelling the situation, semi-quantitative mathematization, and comparing different solution methods". Successive questioning lets new questions emerge, illustrating the open-ended nature of inquiry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A relatable, real-world context with publicly available technical data
- Willingness to accept crude modelling simplifications as the price of pursuing the investigation
#### Constraints
- The article notes the analysis is not easily verified experimentally; publicly available data are precise at best to one significant digit

### Target Learners
- third-year Bachelor physics students
- secondary school students (for the simpler jet-height version)

### Learning Goals
- reformulating everyday questions in scientific terms
- Fermi-type estimation
- comparing alternative solution methods
- metacognitive skills and higher-order thinking
