# Two-week otolaryngology simulation and human factors module with entrustable professional activities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-week undergraduate otolaryngology rotation combining entrustable professional activities with six small-group human factors discussions on "compassionate care, effective communication, resilience building, teamwork and professionalism" and three simulations: suturing, flexible nasendoscopy and grommet insertion. Simulations used a Laerdal Airway Management Trainer, an Ambu aScope, a novel ear manikin with an operating microscope, and were delivered in a clinical procedure room alongside standard teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior undergraduate students commencing clinical otolaryngology learning after two or three preclinical years
- A shared common lecture series in otolaryngology prior to the module
- A clinical procedure room with equipment similar to everyday clinical equipment
- Senior clinical staff to observe and evaluate entrustable professional activities
#### Constraints
- Delivered within a two-week subspecialty rotation alongside standard teaching

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate medical students on clinical attachments

### Learning Goals
- otolaryngology knowledge and technical skills
- non-technical skills such as communication, teamwork and professionalism
- preparedness for the hospital and operating theatre environment
