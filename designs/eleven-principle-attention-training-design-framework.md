# Eleven-principle theory-driven design framework for attention-training interventions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes intervention design around 11 scientific insights and corresponding design strategies drawn from educational psychology and cognitive science: seamless classroom integration, reduced need for transfer, motivation-enhancing framing, strong attentional sets, self-monitoring of attention, external accountability, positive reinforcing feedback, distraction recovery, environmental constraints on distraction, an optimal auditory environment, and shaping classroom culture and shared norms. The authors state that “Effective interventions are rarely built from a single insight; they succeed by combining multiple, mutually reinforcing design choices,” and that no existing attention-training intervention had integrated these principles into a single tool. The framework guided the Focus Coach's two-year iterative development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Principles are individually supported by prior research; the framework assumes students value attention as a skill worth developing and that interventions minimize teacher burden to be sustainable.
#### Constraints
- The article notes restrictive strategies such as device bans can fail, and that the framework was developed and evaluated in K-12 settings with adolescents.

### Target Learners
- K-12 students across grade levels and subject areas

### Learning Goals
- attentional focus and self-regulated learning during academic tasks
