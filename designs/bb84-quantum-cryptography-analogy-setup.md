# Low-cost BB84 quantum cryptography analogy setup for hands-on quantum key distribution labs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A hands-on quantum key distribution teaching apparatus using the BB84 protocol, built from Thorlabs components for "a bit less than$4,000" and called "the quantum cryptography analogy setup". A pulsed laser with two detectors after a beam splitter "simulates as if it was single photon" detection. Students "get the experience of really using the protocol to distribute the key between 2 parties", then see how an eavesdropper scrambles the key and can be detected by comparing their parts of the key.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commercially available Thorlabs optics components (pulsed laser, beam splitter, detectors) assembled into the analogy setup.
#### Constraints
- The setup uses "a pulsed laser and it's not single photon detectors"; single-photon detection is only simulated, which the educator says students doing demonstrations do not mind.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in QISE courses

### Learning Goals
- hands-on experience with quantum key distribution via the BB84 protocol, including eavesdropper detection
