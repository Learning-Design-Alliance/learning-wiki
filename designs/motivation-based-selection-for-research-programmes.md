# Select students for extracurricular research programmes aimed at cultivating physician-scientists based on motivation rather than a GPA threshold

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends shifting ERP selection from grades alone toward motivation, because intrinsic motivation for research, perceptions of research, and curiosity are prerequisites of future research involvement and were comparable across GPA groups. It argues "motivation should be given importance in selecting students for ERPs, allowing students with a GPA lower than seven to participate within such programmes as well". Selection should use a model in which GPA is not a threshold for entry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The ERP's pre-eminent goal is to develop an academic mindset and cultivate future physician-scientists.
- Insight into applicants' motivation for research, e.g. via questionnaires or motivation letters.
#### Constraints
- The <7 group had significantly higher ERP drop-out odds and lower third-year GPA.
- Evidence comes from one cohort of 59 students at a single Dutch academic medical centre.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- research involvement and development of future physician-scientists
