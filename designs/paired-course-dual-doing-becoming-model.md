# Paired course model combining academic skill building (doing) with lifelong learning development (becoming)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A six-credit paired course design in which under-prepared first-year students are simultaneously enrolled in a reading support course and a content course, so that reading skills and study strategies taught in the reading course are applied directly to the content course material. The article frames this as a dual approach addressing both "academic skills, labeled as 'doing', and lifelong learning, called 'becoming'". Instructional time in the reading course was divided equally between the two strands, and the content course remained a traditional lecture-based course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Under-prepared students identified through low SAT critical reading scores
- Faculty coordination to structure assignments applying reading-course strategies to the paired content course
#### Constraints
- Evaluated at a single commuter campus with a small sample; the authors note findings are preliminary and warrant future replications

### Target Learners
- under-prepared first-year full-time college students

### Learning Goals
- college reading and study skills
- lifelong learning dispositions
- success in a content course
