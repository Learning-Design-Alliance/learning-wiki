# Informal learning through guided astronomy project work in place of lectures

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The IAYC's core instructional pattern replaces timetabled teaching with three-week, participant-driven research projects culminating in a written report. As the article puts it, "instead of offering lectures or timetabled classes like other science camps, the IAYC encourages informal learning through guided, astronomy-related, project work", which the authors liken to project-based or cooperative learning. The goal is not novel science but bridging cultural barriers while fostering curiosity and intuition for how research is carried out, and letting participants experience failure as part of the scientific method.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leaders provide a range of project ideas adaptable to participants' previous experience, engaging and relevant for modern-day astrophysics
- Participants may suggest their own project ideas, and final reports are written in LaTeX
#### Constraints
- Determining the success of a project is subjective; for exploratory projects objective goals are less straightforward

### Target Learners
- Young people aged 16-24, including those from non-STEM backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- Working scientifically on one's own
- Understanding how research is carried out
- Critical and independent thinking
