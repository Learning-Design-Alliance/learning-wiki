# Time simulation sessions toward the end of the academic year to engage learners in their zone of proximal development

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article intentionally scheduled the session near the end of the PGY-1 year so learners would face scenarios they were unlikely to have experienced independently, engaging them in their zone of proximal development. Learners were encouraged to look to each other and other resources, and a chalk talk provided a cognitive framework as guidance. The article cites prior work showing inexperienced learners in such simulation find the experiences valuable, instructional, and motivating.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios that learners are unlikely to have experienced prior, particularly in leadership roles
- Support structures such as peer guidance and a didactic cognitive framework
#### Constraints
- It was not expected that learners would effectively and independently complete tasks without assistance

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine interns near the end of their PGY-1 year

### Learning Goals
- Leading code response teams
- Managing post-ROSC patients
