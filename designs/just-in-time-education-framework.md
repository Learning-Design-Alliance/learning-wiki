# Just-in-time education (JITE) as a framework for integrating technosciences and humanities in pre-university education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Just-in-time education (JITE) is an instructional approach, enunciated in 1992 and drawing on JIT manufacturing, in which students tackle complex multidisciplinary projects for which they have been given only part of the needed knowledge. The author proposes JITE experiences at end-of-semester, end-of-school-year and end-of-pre-university milestones so that 'JITE is envisaged in the context of nanotechnology, etc., to not only impact the teaching and learning of science and mathematics but also of humanities and social sciences.' Its purpose is to give future technoscientists and non-technoscientists a common understanding of the social, ethical, legal and political issues raised by nanotechnology and its convergence with other technologies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects must span at least two, preferably more, scientific and mathematical disciplines
- Only part of the necessary information should have been imparted in regular coursework before the project begins
- Students must undertake both single-member and team projects
- Guidance by teacher teams drawn from a diverse array of disciplines
#### Constraints
- In countries such as the US it could entail prolongation of the school year with possibly deleterious effects and higher school taxes; in countries such as India and China JICE activities would have to be reduced to accommodate JITE without lengthening the school year

### Target Learners
- Secondary (pre-university) students, both future technoscientists and non-technoscientists

### Learning Goals
- Identify disciplines intersecting a complex problem and synthesize information across them
- Assess socioethical significance of technoscientific developments
