# Teach three different quantum SDKs (Qiskit, PennyLane, Ocean) for comprehensive and fair coverage

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's curriculum deliberately teaches three frameworks: Qiskit from IBM, PennyLane from Xanadu, and Ocean from D-Wave. The authors state they have "yet to encounter a course on quantum computer programming that offers and discusses three different SDKs". They argue this gives comprehensive coverage, fair and balanced presentation of tools, in-depth discussion of diverse applications (PennyLane's machine-learning integration, D-Wave's optimization focus), and lets students "understand the strengths and limitations of each SDK".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must present a fair and balanced view of the available tools rather than defaulting to the most prominent framework
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge they plan to compare the approach to other methodologies in the future to determine whether it is superior; the multi-SDK structure's advantages are argued, not measured

### Target Learners
- Advanced university students (M.Sc./Ph.D.) in quantum computer programming courses

### Learning Goals
- Understanding strengths and limitations of multiple quantum SDKs
- Diverse perspectives on quantum programming applications including machine learning and optimization
