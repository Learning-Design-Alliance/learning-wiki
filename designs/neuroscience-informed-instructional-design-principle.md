# Design instruction using educational neuroscience to develop attention, working memory, and mood

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that because attention, working memory, and mood are foundational and interrelated for learning, teachers should draw on educational neuroscience when designing instructional models. In the discussion it states that "teachers are encouraged to utilize the neurocognitive-based model (5P) since it has been found to be capable of developing the attention, working memory, and mood of students", and that teachers should provide sufficient opportunities for students to develop these domains. The principle rests on the study's randomized comparison showing 5P outperforming 5E.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher understanding of how the brain receives and saves information; an instructional model whose syntax deliberately targets attention, working memory, and mood
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single six-week study in Thai Grade 9 science classes; the mood effect was the smallest of the three outcomes

### Target Learners
