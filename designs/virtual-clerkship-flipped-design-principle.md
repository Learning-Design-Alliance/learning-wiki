# Design virtual clinical rotations around flipped-classroom case-based interactive sessions with asynchronous preparatory work aligned to national curriculum guidelines

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article converted a traditional 4-week clerkship into a 2-week virtual rotation employing "the flipped classroom model with a total of 22 hours of synchronous case-based interactive learning" plus recommended asynchronous preparatory work. Sessions addressed salient clinical scenarios per American Academy of Neurology guidelines, with slide design following Mayer's multimedia learning theory. This design achieved shelf scores comparable to the traditional format.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Purpose-built educational materials aligned with national (AAN) curriculum guidelines
- Asynchronous preparatory work assigned before synchronous case sessions
- Interactive case-based synchronous sessions rather than passive lectures
#### Constraints
- Outcomes assessed only on the Neurology NBME shelf exam; generalizability to other clerkships is limited per the article
- Skills requiring hands-on practice were outside the virtual rotation's scope

### Target Learners
- Third- and fourth-year medical students on a core clinical clerkship

### Learning Goals
- Neurology clinical knowledge base as measured by NBME shelf examination
- Clinical reasoning skills such as lesion localization and differential diagnosis generation
