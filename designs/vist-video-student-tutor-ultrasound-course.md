# ViST course: two multimedia-designed ultrasound teaching videos plus trained student peer tutors with faculty back-up

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ViST course combines two specially produced videos (15 min AU, 18 min TTE) with small-group practical exercises supervised by six trained student tutors, with one ultrasound-experienced clinical teacher present only as a back-up. Videos follow "11 of the 12 principles of multimedia learning (with exclusion of the pre-training principle)", use a two-picture design pairing the ultrasound image with the examiner's hand position, and allow rewinding and speed control. Students share devices in alternating examiner/patient pairs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Six students trained as tutors by two experienced physicians before course implementation
- Videos produced with multimedia-production support and externally evaluated by Skills-Lab members before use
#### Constraints
- Videos were shown only in class with no further viewing opportunities, to match teaching-time exposure of the faculty course
- Neither pathologies nor clinical examination findings were taught in the videos

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students without previous ultrasound knowledge

### Learning Goals
- basic and advanced abdominal ultrasound and transthoracic echocardiography practical skills
