# DIL: digital individual learning combining the Magical Garden adaptive math game with body-and-mind attention exercises

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
DIL (Digital Individual Learning for body-and-mind) is an individual preschool training paradigm built on the theoretical understanding of self-regulation and early math as developing interdependently. The article states that "DIL had two components: an adaptive, interactive math game and a set of attention-enhancing body-and-mind activities." The Magical Garden game, played on tablets with headphones, includes a teachable agent the child teaches early math, with adaptive difficulty and feedback; the 12 body-and-mind activities train attention, breath control, avoiding distractions, body control, and metacognition through metaphors matching the game's characters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators introduce and scaffold the body-and-mind exercises at each child's level, and the game is administered online with tablets and headphones
#### Constraints
- Implemented one hour per day during the six-week intervention; the trial found no measurable effects on the targeted skills

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged four to six

### Learning Goals
- executive functions including auditory selective attention
- self-regulation
- early math skills
