# hinotori™ Surgical Robot System with A-Lap Mini assessment in a simulated infant abdomen

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The hinotori™ Surgical Robot System (Medicaroid Inc., Kobe, Japan) comprises "a master console and three robotic arms equipped with a 10-mm, 30° videoscope and two 8-mm ports", with eight-axis arms and a docking-free architecture enabling compact port placement. In this study it was evaluated against conventional 5-mm laparoscopic instruments in a 1,400-mL transparent acrylic training box (Endowork Pro II) simulating the abdominal cavity of a 6-month-old infant, with suturing accuracy automatically quantified by the A-Lap Mini computer-assisted device across five scored components (maximum total 25).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the compact operative space configuration with two 8-mm ports and a 10-mm 30° videoscope as configured in the study
#### Constraints
- The simulation box cannot fully replicate the dynamic anatomy and tissue properties of actual pediatric abdominal cavities, particularly in neonates
- No comparison with other robotic systems such as da Vinci®, Versius®, or Senhance™ was included

### Target Learners
- Board-certified pediatric surgeons with minimal or no prior robotic experience

### Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic and robotic suturing and peg-transfer skills in confined pediatric-like operative spaces
