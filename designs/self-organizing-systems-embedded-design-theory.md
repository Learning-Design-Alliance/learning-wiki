# Self-organizing systems approach to course design, with embedded design as the pivotal principle

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design framework derived from Bain's work on a self-organizing K-12 school and extended to higher education, in which "Self-organizing systems theory involves the application of six principles that enable change from the ground up": simple rules, embedded design, similarity at scale, emergent feedback, dispersed control, and a schema to guide action. Embedded design is treated as "the overarching and pivotal principle that guides application of all others". In this study it was applied to a pre-service inclusive education course to build deep, coherent pedagogical content knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course content valued as important must be embedded at all levels of the course design, not delivered once
#### Constraints
- The article tests only the embedded design principle, not the other five principles, and in a single course at one university

### Target Learners
- pre-service primary (elementary) teachers

### Learning Goals
- pedagogical content knowledge about inclusive education and differentiation
