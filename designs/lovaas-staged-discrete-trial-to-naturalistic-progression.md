# Staged progression from discrete trial training to naturalistic instruction and generalization

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Lovaas Model organizes instruction as a staged progression. "During the first stages of the intervention, the main method of teaching is discrete trial training", and "As children progress, therapists gradually increase the emphasis on instruction in less structured or more natural settings, such as peer play and classrooms." Early stages target reducing behaviors that interfere with learning and teaching prerequisite skills such as requesting, imitation, and following instructions; instruction then advances to expressive and receptive language, then social and conversation skills, and finally pre-academic and observational learning skills, with programming for generalization to preschool and community settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained one-to-one instructors, trainers, and supervisors delivering 35 to 40 hours per week of intervention
#### Constraints
- The intervention generally lasts about three years, beginning with children ages 18 to 42 months

### Target Learners
- young children with autism or pervasive developmental disorder

### Learning Goals
- prerequisite skills such as requesting and imitation
- expressive and receptive language
- social and conversation skills
- pre-academic skills and generalization
