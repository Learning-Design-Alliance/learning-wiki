# Integrate environmental health e-learning modules into medical curricula to strengthen preventive competencies in primary care

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that the e-learning module's significant improvements in knowledge and self-reported preventive behaviors "support the integration of environmental health training into medical curricula and highlight the potential of scalable e-learning interventions to strengthen preventive competencies in primary care." This principle rests on the module's measured gains: a mean 55.56 percentage-point knowledge improvement and 2.13-point behavior improvement, both at P<.001, with high satisfaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic evaluation of pedagogical innovations to measure impact and guide improvement
- Collaborative development by educators, clinicians, and audiovisual specialists
#### Constraints
- Gains measured immediately post-training; long-term retention and clinical practice application not assessed
- Monocentric setting limits external validity

### Target Learners
- family medicine residents
- primary care health professionals

### Learning Goals
- identify household EDC exposure sources
- provide evidence-based preventive counseling on EDCs
