# Fear of negative evaluation account: voice-based AI practice raises participation by building knowledge mastery and lowering speaking anxiety

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q4` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's explanatory framework holds that "interaction with a knowledgeable voice-based AI discussion partner stimulates greater class participation by elevating a student's mastery of knowledge and lowering their anxiety about speaking up in the classroom." It draws on the Fear of Negative Evaluation (FNE) tradition, in which anxiety about being judged suppresses engagement, and identifies two mechanisms: greater domain mastery raising self-efficacy, and rehearsal reducing social anxiety about public speaking. The study's findings on comfort speaking up and perceived learning are read as substantiating this argument.
