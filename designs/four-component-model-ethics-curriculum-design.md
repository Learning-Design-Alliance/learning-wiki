# Four Component Model of ethical decision-making as a design framework for ethics curricula

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article adopts Narvaez and Rest's Four Component Model, in which "all four must be present for an individual to ultimately exhibit ethical behavior": moral sensitivity, moral judgment, moral motivation, and moral implementation. Historically ethics education has focused on moral judgment because it is easiest to influence and measure, risking students who reason well on case studies but fail to recognize novel issues. QEP's case study template deliberately challenges students to consider all four components, from identifying conflicting stakeholders to identifying barriers to follow-through.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricular materials intentionally designed with all four components in mind, not only moral judgment
#### Constraints
- No ethics intervention can guarantee ethical behavior; sharpened reasoning skills may even be used to rationalize morally-dubious behavior in moral grey areas

### Target Learners
- quantum students and professionals

### Learning Goals
- ethical decision-making and ethical behavior, not just ethical reasoning
