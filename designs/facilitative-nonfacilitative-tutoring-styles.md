# Two defined PBL tutoring styles: facilitative versus non-facilitative tutoring

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study defines two contrasting tutoring styles for PBL small groups, operationalized in an eight-item protocol covering forming, storming and norming phases. The facilitative tutor "offers orientation and explanation", intervenes actively in intra-group processes if required, and offers corrective feedback; the non-facilitative tutor "is participative and delegates", intervenes only in acute necessity, and does not offer corrective feedback. The styles were defined in accordance with Walsh and Tuckman and verified by video-analysis after tutor training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors must be trained to reliably enact each style, verified by video-analysis, and external observers must confirm proper application
#### Constraints
- Defined for PBL sessions in a hybrid dental curriculum with content-expert tutors in endodontics

### Target Learners
- dental students in their first clinical semester

### Learning Goals
- hypothetico-deductive reasoning and endodontics knowledge via problem-based learning
