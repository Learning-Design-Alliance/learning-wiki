# Surgical preference card educational intervention for operating room nursing students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-session educational intervention in which operating room nursing students learn pre-, intra- and post-operative care for common general surgeries using surgeon-specific preference cards. As the article describes it, "the necessary preparations for participating in general surgeries (cholecystectomy, appendectomy, inguinal hernia, and laparotomy) were taught step by step in the pre-, intra-, and post-operative phases using surgical preference cards in two 1-hour training sessions." Card content (position, prep and drape, incision, instruments, equipment, sutures, dressing) was extracted through face-to-face interviews with three general surgeons and content-validated by 12 surgeons and faculty members. The control group received usual lecture training in two 1-hour sessions by the same instructor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cards must reflect each surgeon's actual preferences, extracted via interviews and content-validated by surgeons and faculty
- Delivery by the same instructor with separate times and locations to prevent information leakage between groups
#### Constraints
- The trial used a two-session, two-hour intervention, which the authors say may be insufficient for lasting change
- Convenience sampling from a single university may limit generalizability

### Target Learners
- Fourth, sixth, and eighth-semester operating room nursing students with no prior operating room experience

### Learning Goals
- Preparation for scrub and circulating nurse roles in general surgeries
- Knowledge of surgeon-specific instruments, sutures, positioning and procedure steps
