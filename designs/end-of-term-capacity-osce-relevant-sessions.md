# Allocate skills-lab staff and material capacity to OSCE-relevant sessions at the end of term and reduce advanced-session offerings late in the term

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A resource-planning principle derived from the observed attendance patterns: because OSCE-relevant voluntary basic sessions are heavily used late in the term, capacity should follow demand. The authors state that "Most of the capacity of training staff and material should be allocated to the last weeks of the term for OSCE-relevant skills-lab training sessions", with fewer resources mid-term and reduced advanced sessions at term end, permitting cost reductions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attendance data or knowledge of when summative skills assessment occurs in the curriculum
#### Constraints
- Derived from a retrospective, descriptive single-faculty study; the authors note it does not establish the causal structure of attendance

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clinical skills training

### Learning Goals
- clinical-technical skills acquisition aligned with OSCE assessment
