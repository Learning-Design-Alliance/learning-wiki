# 3D-printed patient-specific instrumentation (PSI) cutting guide for UKA tibial saw cuts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A patient-specific tibial cutting guide for medial UKA, 3D printed in medical grade Nylon (PA 2200) from a CT-based pre-operative plan. The guide "was designed to be used in the same manner as the Oxford Phase III conventional instruments", with a distal portion manufactured to fit the conventional ankle clamp, and delivered the planned cut through a standard minimally invasive incision. In the sawbone study it raised novice trainee accuracy to expert level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-operative CT-based 3D plan of the intended tibial cut
- Use with a video demonstration or training, since unfamiliar users made large sagittal errors on first use
#### Constraints
- Results apply only to the two guide designs tested and cannot be generalised
- In vivo soft tissue makes PSI positioning more difficult than in the sawbone model

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees with no prior UKA experience
- low-volume UKA surgeons

### Learning Goals
- accurate tibial component positioning in unicompartmental knee arthroplasty
