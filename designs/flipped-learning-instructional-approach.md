# Flipped learning as an instructional approach inverting classroom-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes flipped learning as an instructional approach in which "the traditional concept and idea of classroom-based learning is inverted": new content is first mastered through structured activities in the individual learning space, and face-to-face group time is used for active learning strategies and concept application. In this trial the approach was operationalised via the four pillars of F-L-I-P (flexible environment, learning culture, intentional content, professional educator), a brief seminar, individual learning assignments including a written debriefing, and student-conducted debriefings in the second training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Proper planning and organisation with adequate learning guidance for the individual learning phase; the article states flipping a class by merely having learners read additional material does not necessarily result in flipped learning
#### Constraints
- The article notes the theoretical teaching approach designed for the individual learning space might, according to constructive alignment, be objected to as inappropriate, an objection the authors overrule

### Target Learners
- 3rd-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Non-technical skills (planning and problem-solving, teamwork and leadership, team orientation) in emergency simulation training
