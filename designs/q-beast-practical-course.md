# Q-BEAST practical course on experimental evaluation of quantum computing systems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Q-BEAST is a lab course ('Praktikum') combining theoretical instruction with hands-on experimentation on real on-premise quantum systems at LRZ. The course is 'a combination of lectures, vendor talks, structured assignments, and projects', organized into four modules with seven assignments and two final projects, targeting benchmarking and performance evaluation skills. Students work in groups of two or three, with assignments taking one to two weeks and the final project three weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students access real quantum backends and HPC resources through the Munich Quantum Software Stack and work in groups of two or three
#### Constraints
- Class size was limited to a maximum of 15 students to manage workload and support, aligned with quantum resource allocation slots

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students from computer science, physics, data science, and robotics

### Learning Goals
- experimental evaluation and characterization of quantum computing systems
- benchmarking techniques
- hybrid HPC-QC workflow design
