# Peer Instruction as a real-time feedback cycle structured by vote proportions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Peer Instruction, developed by Eric Mazur, is a student-centered approach in which class progression depends on real-time student responses to ConcepTests, described as "multiple-choice conceptual questions". The article's Figure 1 algorithm structures each question by the proportion of correct answers: below 35% the concept is revisited, between 35% and 80% students engage in 2-3 minutes of peer discussion trying to convince each other and then revote, and above 80% a brief lecture of about 10 minutes addresses remaining misconceptions. The approach "emphasizes basic concepts, has students commit to a conception, provides a setting for peer discussion and has instructors explicitly address misconceptions".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must pose ConcepTests with peer discussion (3-4 per class in this study) and be willing to reshape instruction to focus on concepts
#### Constraints
- The vote-threshold branches (35% and 80%) are the article's own implementation heuristic, not a tested optimum

### Target Learners
- first-semester community college students in algebra-based physics

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of mechanics
- addressing misconceptions
