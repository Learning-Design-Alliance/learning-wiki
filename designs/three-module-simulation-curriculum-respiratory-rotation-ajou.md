# Three-module simulation curriculum (HFS, SP, VR) in a respiratory medicine rotation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A three-module simulation sequence delivered during a one-week respiratory rotation for fifth-year medical students: HFS using Laerdal's SimMan for an acute respiratory distress scenario in groups of 2–3, SP-based one-on-one interviews with a standardized tuberculosis patient, and VR using Sim-X and Oculus Quest 2 for a sepsis scenario. The article states "The VR-based simulation utilized Sim-X and Oculus Quest 2," and each session was followed by a 30-minute group debriefing with instructor feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a high-fidelity simulator (Laerdal SimMan), trained standardized patients, and VR equipment (Sim-X and Oculus Quest 2).
- A 30-minute group debriefing including instructor feedback after each simulation session.
#### Constraints
- All students completed the three modalities in the same fixed sequence (HFS → SP → VR), raising the possibility of order effects.
- The three modules targeted different clinical objectives (acute respiratory distress, tuberculosis interview, sepsis), which may confound modality comparisons.

### Target Learners
- Fifth-year medical students (undergraduate medical education)

### Learning Goals
- Identifying signs of acute respiratory distress and recognizing early signs of sepsis
- Focused history-taking, physical examination, and patient-centered communication
- Clinical decision-making and step-by-step clinical reasoning
