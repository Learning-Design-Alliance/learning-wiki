# The article introduces a six-step modeling framework for physics laboratory investigations (Fig. 1)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper develops a framework for modeling at the upper-division level, portrayed graphically as interconnected steps: "construction of a model of a real-world physical process", "making predictions about the behavior of the physical system", "creating a model of measurement tools", "using the measurement model to interpret the data and understand the limitations of the measurements", "mak e comparisons between the data and predictions", and "model reﬁnement". It extends prior modeling curricula in two ways: it emphasizes modeling both the physical process and the measurement tools, and it treats modeling as applying known principles to specific phenomena rather than uncovering new basic principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning goals must be articulated in sufficient detail to be integrated into the classroom and assessed
#### Constraints
- The framework is presented for upper-division laboratory courses as preparation for research; the authors note the primary goal at this level is usually not to uncover new basic principles

### Target Learners
- junior- and senior-level physics majors in an advanced laboratory course

### Learning Goals
- modeling of physical systems and measurement tools
- connecting conceptual and quantitative reasoning within experimental work
