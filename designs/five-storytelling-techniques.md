# Five techniques for helping students build the storytelling tool

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article enumerates five classroom techniques: scaffold building a story tied to blended symbols and based on principles; foster coherence by telling a story in graphs with multiple variables; remove the scaffold with problems requiring both a story and fundamental principles; use essay questions to get students to think about their thinking; and blend two independent stories to reconcile personal experience with physical reasoning. Variation — "changing situations and perspectives to see how the results change" — helps students focus on process rather than answers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stories must be physically correct, based on fundamental principles, and mathematizable — students should learn to tell the story "in a physically reliable way".
#### Constraints
- For the scaffold-removal multiple-choice problem, the article notes there is some scaffolding in the choice and ordering of the questions, and full value requires a class discussion drawing out how students solved it.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students (undergraduate)

### Learning Goals
- building physically reliable stories tied to blended symbology
- understanding mechanism and synthesis
