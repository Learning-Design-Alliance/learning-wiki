# Small-group rotation with peer scoring and feedback in the comprehension step of skills training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level design pattern for the comprehension phase of procedural skills teaching: residents work in small teams and rotate through the roles of reciting the procedural steps, demonstrating the operation, and observing with a scoring sheet, with immediate feedback from peers and the supervising physician at each stage. The article describes "dividing each batch of 12 to 16 residents into four groups of 3 to 4 people each, with one instructing physician for every two groups", ensuring each resident completes a recitation, a demonstration, and one to two scoring observations per procedure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams of 3 to 4 residents with rotation through recitation, demonstration, and scoring-observer roles
- One instructing physician for every two groups
- A scoring sheet used by the observing resident to provide checks, reminders, and noted issues
- Immediate feedback from the supervising physician and team members on problems or deficiencies
#### Constraints
- Described and evaluated in the context of OB-GYN basic skill training with batches of 12 to 16 residents

### Target Learners
- first-year medical and surgical residents

### Learning Goals
- procedural skill mastery
- peer feedback and evaluation skills
- metacognitive reflection on strengths and weaknesses
