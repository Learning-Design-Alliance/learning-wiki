# Skill-focused curriculum design: task-specific modifications to reduce cognitive load and target a single procedural objective

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article modified a comprehensive anesthesiology resident curriculum into a skill-focused version targeting one objective: successful US-guided cannulation. Key modifications included removing US physics content "in order to shorten the training duration and reduce cognitive load", using only large-gauge (16G) needles, teaching only the out-of-plane approach with DNTP, and activating an on-screen midline guidance indicator. Table 1 contrasts the two curricula across goal, content scope, duration, cognitive load, teaching context, outcome, and educational efficiency dimensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation model and handheld US device with an on-screen midline guidance indicator are needed to implement the curriculum as described.
#### Constraints
- The curriculum exclusively emphasizes the out-of-plane approach with DNTP and large-gauge needles, which "may not reflect typical clinical practice".

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical clerks
- Postgraduate year (PGY) residents

### Learning Goals
- US-guided peripheral intravenous cannulation
- Needle tip visualization under ultrasound
