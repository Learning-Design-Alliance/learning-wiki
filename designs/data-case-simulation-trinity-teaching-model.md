# The 'data-case-simulation' trinity practice teaching model for interdisciplinary medical education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a three-dimension practice teaching model organized around data, case, and simulation, in which students use open-source datasets for case analysis with clinical index modeling and statistical thinking. The authors state that the 'Data-case-simulation' trinity practice teaching model 'enables students to utilize open-source datasets for case analysis, employing clinical index modeling and statistical thinking.' It is grounded in situated learning theory and positions virtual simulation as an advanced training stage complementing other methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to public datasets (TCGA, GEO, UK Biobank) and a modular simulation system spanning data preprocessing to result display
#### Constraints
- Positioned as a complement deployed alongside, not as a replacement for, traditional clinical simulation methods such as manikin-based scenarios or standardized patients

### Target Learners
- fourth-year clinical medicine undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- interdisciplinary data literacy
- disease-centered bioinformatics and statistical analysis skills
