# ADDIE-based infection control education program combining lecture, skills training, standardized patient simulation, and debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The researchers developed an infection control education program using the "analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation (ADDIE) model". Both groups received a 50-minute lecture on standard precautions and 30 minutes of skills training (hand hygiene, PPE including N95 masks and gloves, subcutaneous injection). The SP group added simulation orientation, a 40-minute simulation run in teams of four with half participating and half observing, and a 40-minute Description-Analysis-Application debriefing (180 min total); the PRP group completed 40 minutes of peer role-play instead (120 min total).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Third-year students at the start of clinical practice were chosen based on prior research suggesting an intermediate-level module before complex scenarios
- Standardized patients trained in three steps with scripts, scenario algorithms, and final practice with non-participating students
#### Constraints
- The program used only one infection control scenario
- SP and PRP sessions ran at different times and places to avoid exposing participants to the program beforehand

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate nursing students beginning clinical practice

### Learning Goals
- knowledge and awareness of standard precautions
- infection control performance including hand hygiene, PPE use, and safe injections
