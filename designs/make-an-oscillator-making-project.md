# The 'Make an Oscillator' making project in the Tufts computational physics course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The oscillator project is the course element the article analyzes, 'included in the Spring 2019, Spring 2020 and Spring 2021 offerings of the course; each of these iterations involved 18 - 30 students.' Students make physical oscillators from provided materials, share prototypes, characterize motion experimentally (e.g. with Tracker), and build a computational model of one oscillator, comparing and refining both experiment and model. All groups submitted executable Jupyter notebooks, documentation, reports and self-assessments; Slack served as the communication platform.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Python and Jupyter as the recommended computation environment
- Teams of 3-4 students with group work as the majority of class time
#### Constraints
- The 2019 implementation analyzed here was 'most thoroughly documented through video recording and not disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic'

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students with some programming experience

### Learning Goals
- computational modeling of physical phenomena
- experimental characterization of oscillatory motion
