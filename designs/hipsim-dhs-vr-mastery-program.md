# HipSim — a stepwise mastery-learning VR simulation program for the dynamic hip screw procedure with LC-CUSUM-based advancement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
HipSim is a virtual reality dynamic hip screw (DHS) training program on the TraumaVision simulator, designed as 'a time-dispersed mastery-learning program with 2–3 training days of a maximum 4 hours of training per day'. Participants advance stepwise through three competency levels, from repetitive K-wire placement in the same patient to the entire DHS procedure, with clinical variability of 'up to 24 different scenarios (different patients and fracture patterns)' introduced at CL1.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must be 1st-year orthopedic residents who have performed fewer than 10 hip fracture-related surgeries under supervision.
- Advancement to the next competency level is granted only when LC-CUSUM signals competency; a minimum of 39 procedures was required to complete all 3 CLs, with a maximum of 50 procedures per level.
#### Constraints
- The article states VR simulation is 'currently neither suited to teaching the participants the entire procedure including reposition of the fracture and soft-tissue handling nor does it encompass the overwhelming complexity of performing the procedure in the operating theatre'.
- The article states the results of HipSim 'should not and cannot be used as a summative assessment of the participant's capabilities to become a competent surgeon'; it is a formative program with summative evaluation of each simulation.

### Target Learners
- 1st-year orthopedic residents with fewer than 10 hip fracture surgeries

### Learning Goals
- mastering the dynamic hip screw procedure
- hand–eye coordination and working in 3 dimensions from 2-dimensional visual information with haptic feedback
