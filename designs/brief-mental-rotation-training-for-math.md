# Use brief mental rotation training with an object completion task to support early elementary math learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates that a single 40-minute session of mental rotation practice using an object completion task — children "see two parts of a flat shape and then point to one of four pictures that shows the shape as a whole" — improved calculation performance in 6- to 8-year-olds. It offers an existence proof that spatial training per se can improve math, supporting instructional exploration of spatial cognition in early math.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Children are 6-8 years old, an age when calculation skills are developing but not mastered
#### Constraints
- Training was very brief; effects were narrow, appearing mainly on missing term problems
- No general improvement in spatial ability on the PMA Spatial Relations subtest

### Target Learners
- children aged 6-8 in early elementary grades

### Learning Goals
- addition and subtraction calculation
- missing term problem solving
