# Sociocultural model of workplace-based peer-supported faculty development

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes a systems-perspective, sociocultural conceptualisation of workplace-based peer support (Fig. 1), adapted from O'Sullivan and Irby's and Steinert et al.'s faculty development models. It holds that an acceptable and effective strategy requires that "three integrated factors are accounted for: the context and culture in which the programme takes place; the characteristics of the participants, whom we propose are ‘contributors’, and the educational design of the programme." All elements are described as interdependent, with design factors impacting engagement, which is in turn impacted by participant characteristics and workplace context and culture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational design processes attended to, including evaluation throughout, particularly process evaluation during and after implementation
- Participants take responsibility for their own learning as contributors rather than assuming only education experts provide feedback
#### Constraints
- Attempts to change organisational culture bring risks and challenges; developing a supportive culture takes time and patience in complex health care workplace cultures

### Target Learners
- Clinician teachers and clinical supervisors in health care workplaces

### Learning Goals
- Improving clinical teaching and supervision practice through peer-supported reflection and feedback
