# SST as a closed-loop 'scenario experience—practical operation—reflection and optimization' learning model for clinical thinking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames situational simulation teaching as an immersive approach that replicates highly realistic clinical environments using high-fidelity mannequins, assistant-played family members and nurses, pre-class preparation, a timed scenario with evolving patient deterioration, instructor debriefing, and post-class personalized feedback. The authors state that "SST fosters a 'scenario experience—practical operation—reflection and optimization' closed-loop learning model," positioning it against traditional passive lectures for training decision-making under pressure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior instructors trained in SST who can independently guide residents
- High-fidelity mannequins with externally controlled vital signs and trained teaching assistants to advance the scenario
- Structured pre-class materials and quizzes plus post-class evaluation and feedback
#### Constraints
- In this study the model was implemented with a single clinical scenario (hypovolemic shock) in one emergency medicine department

### Target Learners
- second-year emergency medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- clinical thinking and rapid critical illness assessment
- emergency decision-making and team coordination
- doctor-patient communication and ethical decision-making
