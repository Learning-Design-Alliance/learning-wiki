# Clinical Scenario Simulation Course (CSSC): a standardized Briefing–Facilitation–Debriefing simulation program for ANE management

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
CSSC is a simulation-based in-service training program for ANE management delivered across the five participating hospitals as part of routine quality control. The article describes that "The course followed a standardized “Briefing–Facilitation–Debriefing” structure", comprising a pre-briefing to clarify objectives and roles, scenario enactment in a simulated clinical setting, and an instructor-led debriefing on event recognition, mitigation, teamwork, and standardized reporting/documentation. Scenarios span specific ANEs (falls, unplanned extubation, medication errors, needlestick and transfusion errors) and broader emergencies such as elopement and self-harm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery by designated simulation teams using a combination of online and offline formats, typically 5–6 class hours annually
- Standardized core elements (learning objectives, scenario content, debriefing focus) based on a shared framework
#### Constraints
- Specific delivery format varied slightly across institutions
- Participation was not mandatory for all nurses

### Target Learners
- actively employed hospital nurses, including novice nurses at risk of ANE-related errors

### Learning Goals
- ANE risk recognition, emergency team response, standardized reporting and documentation, post-event analysis, and psychological support
