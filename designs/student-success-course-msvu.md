# Student Success Course (SSC): a mandatory 14-week noncredit intervention for students on academic probation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SSC is a mandatory, noncredit intervention developed at Mount St. Vincent University for undergraduates placed on academic probation (term GPA between 1.0 and 1.7). Its stated objectives are to identify academic strengths and weaknesses, enhance motivation, and develop effective study skills and personal developmental skills. The article describes it as follows: "The course consists of  36 contact hours across a 14-week academic term, costs the student $350, and is taught by q ualified instructors who hold graduate degrees in Education and/or Psychology". Instruction uses diagnostic assessment (the LASSI, re-administered at the last class), lecture, group discussion, written assignments, presentations, and in-class application exercises, with classes capped at no more than 20 students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mandatory registration for all AP students, with payment of the cost-recovery tuition fee
- Diagnostic assessment with individual feedback, and instructors who hold graduate degrees in Education and/or Psychology
#### Constraints
- The SSC grade does not appear on the university transcript or affect GPA; students who do not pass must remain on AP and re-take the course
- Whether academic gains were maintained over time and whether students persisted to degree completion were not assessed

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students on academic probation (cumulative GPA below 1.7)

### Learning Goals
- Learning strategies and study skills
- Motivation and self-regulation
- Reduced test anxiety and procrastination
- Improved GPA and removal of academic probation status
