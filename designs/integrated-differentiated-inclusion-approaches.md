# Integrated and differentiated approaches as two complementary frameworks for implementing inclusive education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that effective inclusion combines two approaches. The integrated approach introduces innovations across system elements — "the concept (strategy) that defines the model, external preconditions and stages of inclusion; a school that defines the internal prerequisites for inclusion; a community". The differentiated approach is "used in combination with the integrated one in order to identify the internal prerequisites for inclusion: values, beliefs and attitudes of teachers, the competence of educators".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Inclusion is carried out through socialization of students at the public school while identifying students whose educational needs should be differentiated
#### Constraints
- The two approaches complement each other depending on regional differences in inclusive education, determined by the complexity of students' special educational needs and availability of teachers

### Target Learners
- students with special educational needs in general secondary schools

### Learning Goals
- equal access to high-quality education through inclusive school organization
