# Clinical simulation scenario for pleural effusion management structured by the Fabri et al. criteria and covering cognitive, affective and psychomotor domains

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A clinical simulation scenario in which a third-year student is called to assess a 42-year-old man with shortness of breath and pleural effusion, culminating in indicated thoracentesis on the low-fidelity simulator. It was built using the Fabri et al. (2017) structuring elements — "the student's prior knowledge; learning objective; theoretical basis of the activity; preparation of the scenario; and evaluation" — and took into account "the cognitive, affective, and psychomotor domains". The final version includes a simulated patient script, vital signs, a chest X-ray, resource lists, and evaluation via procedure checklist and OSAT.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Construction based on the Fabri et al. structuring elements and on published evidence
- Two specialists in clinical simulation and two medical-surgical lecturers took part in its development
#### Constraints
- The scenario assumes the sitting patient position while ribs sit at the mid-axillary line, an adaptation from the conventional technique communicated to all participants

### Target Learners
- Third-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Indicating and performing thoracentesis in purulent pleural effusion
- Diagnosing and stratifying parapneumonic effusion
- Respiratory physical examination techniques including auscultation
