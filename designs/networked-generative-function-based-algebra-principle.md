# Teach introductory algebra through networked generative, function-based modeling activities rather than rote procedural instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends reform-minded educators enact the algebra curriculum through networked generative activities based on function-based algebra principles, because traditional methods engender rote learning and mechanistic memorization. It reports that the "networked generative function-based approach is one effective alternative method of teaching introductory algebra through a series of modeling activities that leverage diversity and utilize the collective cognitive space of the classroom."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom network technology supporting real-time display of student responses
- Curriculum organized around functions and their multiple representations
#### Constraints
- Implementing reforms in algebra instruction faces institutional and historical obstacles, as the article notes teachers and the public do not lightly accept changes conflicting with expectations about algebra content

### Target Learners
- lower secondary students learning introductory algebra

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of algebraic principles rather than procedural memorization
