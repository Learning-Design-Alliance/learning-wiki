# Dynamic visual search task with spatiotemporally predictable targets

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An online task in which participants search for eight upward-pointing triangle targets that fade in and out among sixteen downward-pointing distractors over roughly fourteen-second trials. Four targets per trial are "spatially and temporally predictable", appearing at fixed onsets within fixed quadrants, while four appear at unpredictable times and locations; participants are not informed of the manipulation. Data, and the experimental code (PsychoPy, hosted on Pavlovia), are available at OSF (DOI: 10.17605/OSF.IO/8UPY5).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants used a personal computer; the experiment did not support phones or tablets
- Normal or corrected-to-normal vision and no diagnosed psychiatric or neurological condition
#### Constraints
- A well-controlled artificial environment used to maximize trial-by-trial learning of regularities, rather than realistic scenes
- Cognitive issues were not controlled for in remote testing

### Target Learners
- Adults aged 20-80

### Learning Goals
- Learning and using spatiotemporal regularities to guide attention during dynamic visual search
