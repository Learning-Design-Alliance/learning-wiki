# Student-designed jerry-can emptying experiments as atypical experiments verifying theoretical emptying-time results

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An atypical experiment designed by three P17 students in which water empties from plastic jerry cans (35 L non-transparent and 20 L transparent) through 1 cm holes, with emptying time measured by stopwatch and compared with the theoretical time from the Bernoulli equation. The students "found that the theoretical emptying time is lower than the experimental one" (8 min 7 s theoretical vs 8 min 40 s experimental for the blue can) and attributed the gap to neglecting water viscosity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simple accessible material: jerry cans, plugs, stopwatch, meter, a scales of 100g precision and a spirit level, plus the theoretical emptying-time formula from the course
#### Constraints
- The theoretical result assumes a perfect or ideal fluid (µ=0), so the theoretical time underestimates the experimental emptying time

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate engineering students in applied industrial biology

### Learning Goals
- applying the Bernoulli equation and emptying-time model to a real flow
- connecting theoretical course results to everyday-life phenomena
