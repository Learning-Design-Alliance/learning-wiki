# Design guidelines for in-class activities in a transformed upper-division course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors summarize design guidelines in Table I that they "empir ically found result in well-received and eﬀective in-class activities." These include that "Questions were ordered from least to most diﬃcult to optimiz e engagement," that "Activities were designed so that continuous work did not exc eed 15 minutes," that mathematical models be tied to phenomena, prior material reused, algebra minimized, and bonus questions challenge advanced students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each activity must be motivated either verbally by the instructor or in the written preamble
- Activities begin with simple introductory questions followed by more complex ones to maintain engagement across a range of prior knowledge
- Activities include work judged just beyond what students are capable of, to prepare them for the feedback period
#### Constraints
- An open-ended worksheet format worked better than multiple-choice type questions in this course
- Long or elaborate algebraic manipulations in activities are minimized so students focus on underlying physical phenomena

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- student engagement
- physical intuition
- quantitative problem solving
