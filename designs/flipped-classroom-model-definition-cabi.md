# The Flipped Classroom Model as a two-component pedagogical model with four implementation components

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines the FC Model as "a new pedagogical model where the instructor shares predetermined digital resources with students through a platform outside the classroom, and related content is also taught through this outside platform asynchronously". Citing Bishop and Verleger (2013), it identifies two significant components: computer technologies such as video lectures, and interactive learning activities. It further lists four components from the Flipped Learning Network (2014): flexible environment, learner-centered detailed content teaching, intentional monitoring of progress with active learning strategies, and a professional educator who provides immediate feedback. In this study the model was implemented with Khan Academy videos out of class and Kahoot quizzes plus group presentations in class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors share predetermined digital resources through an outside-of-class platform
- In-class sessions carry active, collaborative, interactive problem-solving activities
- The instructor monitors students, provides immediate feedback, and assesses outputs
#### Constraints
- The article notes students had difficulty adapting because it is a new approach, citing Chen Hsieh et al. (2017)

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers in higher education

### Learning Goals
- content comprehension and consolidation
- higher order thinking skills
- self-regulated learning
