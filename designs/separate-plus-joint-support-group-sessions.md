# Separate mother and child group sessions with joint practice sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This intervention design pattern combines parallel age-specific group sessions with shared joint sessions. "The skills learnt in both the mother and child sessions were practiced in the joint sessions", so mothers and children each learned relevant content separately (mothers: coping with HIV diagnosis, parenting skills, child development; children: coping, problem-solving, emotional intelligence, social skills) and then applied it together. The joint sessions "gave an opportunity to the mothers and children to practice their s kills and to apply the knowledge they learnt in the intervention sessions in a fun way" and supported relationship development between mother and child.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Separate manuals/content for mother and child sessions plus scheduled joint sessions where skills are practised together.
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in this study only with HIV-affected mother-child dyads in a South African clinic-recruited sample.

### Target Learners
- Mothers and their children aged 6–10 years attending together

### Learning Goals
- Skill practice and transfer to the mother-child relationship
- Communication and adaptive behaviour
