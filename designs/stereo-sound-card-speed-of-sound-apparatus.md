# Stereo sound card time-of-flight apparatus for measuring the speed of sound

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A low-cost apparatus in which two PC microphones separated by a known distance are fed to the left and right stereo channels of a computer sound board via the line-in connection. Sound analysis software displays both channels and the time delay of a sharp pulse between them gives the travel time. The authors note the expensive components "are available in many homes and almost all schools" and that the microphones and extra electronics "can be acquired for less than 50 $."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a computer with a stereo sound board, sound analysis software, two microphones, and an adapter or electronics box to feed each microphone to a separate channel of the line-in input, which does not provide microphone power.
#### Constraints
- The line-in entrance does not provide power, so self-powered microphones with a special mono-to-stereo adapter or a battery-powered electronics box are needed.

### Target Learners
- secondary school students around 13-14 years old
