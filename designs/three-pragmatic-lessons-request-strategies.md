# Three-part pragmatic lesson sequence on Spanish request strategies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An instructional element consisting of three 20-minute classroom sessions (approximately 60 minutes total) on request strategies in Spanish, delivered to the SHL and L2 experimental groups during a spring 2017 semester. The first session was an explicit lesson displaying typical request strategies by level of imposition and explaining the hearer-oriented nature of Spanish requests; the remaining sessions followed a more implicit approach, including categorizing request clips from popular Spanish films. The lessons were adapted from Langer (2013), CARLA (2006), and Martín Ruiz (2011) to student proficiency level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery within existing courses without modifying departmental or course outcomes, so no additional variables arise between control and experimental groups; materials adapted to students' proficiency level.
#### Constraints
- The lessons addressed only request strategies (formal vs. informal situations, imposition levels, indirect and hearer-oriented requests), not pragmatics broadly.

### Target Learners
- college students of Spanish, both heritage and L2, at intermediate to intermediate-high proficiency

### Learning Goals
- selecting pragmatically appropriate request strategies in Spanish according to social distance, power, and imposition
