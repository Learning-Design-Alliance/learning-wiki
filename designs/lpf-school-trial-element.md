# LPF school trial: grade abolishment with standards-based competency grids, learning-development meetings, and formative feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Learning-enhanced Performance Feedback (LPF) school trial was a quasi-experimental intervention in 35 Baden-Württemberg elementary schools beginning in the 2022/2023 school year, compared with 40 control schools. Trial schools abolished written grades and report cards and instead gave "standards-based competency grids" on a 4-point scale from "You still need to work on this" to "You are an expert", at least two oral learning-development meetings per school year, and formative assessment and feedback practices integrated throughout instruction. The grids share many similarities with standards-based grading systems as defined by Welsh et al. (2013) and Guskey and Bailey (2001).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trial schools were required to eliminate written grades from Grade 2 onward; implementation was evaluated on behalf of the Ministry of Education and Cultural Affairs
#### Constraints
- The trial was implemented with minimal requirements: teachers received no comprehensive professional development and no detailed guidance on formative assessment or feedback practices; standardization of features decreased from the first to the third feature

### Target Learners
- elementary school students in Grades 2-3

### Learning Goals
- achievement in reading and mathematics
- subject-specific self-concepts and interests
- well-being and classroom climate
