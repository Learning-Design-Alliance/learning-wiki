# Patient-CT-derived 1:1 3D-printed thoracolumbar fracture models used as PBL trigger material

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention's core artifact is a set of 1:1 scale thoracolumbar fracture models printed from anonymised patient CT data. Clinical cases were selected from the hospital's spinal surgery database, CT data exported in DICOM format, reconstructed with RadiAnt DICOM Viewer, sliced in CHITUBOX, and printed. The models "served as the key 'trigger material' to stimulate problem identification and initiate the inquiry process during the PBL sessions", alongside imaging data, videos, and guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to patient CT data in DICOM format, 3D reconstruction and slicing software, and a 3D printer able to produce 1:1 scale fracture models
#### Constraints
- Patient data were anonymised prior to use and used under a waiver of consent approved by the institutional ethics committee

### Target Learners
- Assistant general practitioner trainees with three years of college education and no prior clinical experience

### Learning Goals
- Understanding thoracolumbar fracture anatomy, classification, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and first-aid handling
