# Five-phase lesson structure for ILSC mathematics learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The ILSC model follows a five-phase lesson syntax: (1) Introduction — organizing the class, conveying group rankings and materials, and explaining tasks; (2) Activity or problem solving — reminding students of prerequisite material and performing teacher-determined activities; (3) Presentation and discussion — groups report results, teacher leads discussion, other groups respond, and the teacher poses questions that allow students to think critically and connect models to symbolic representations; (4) Closing — re-examining what students did and learned, making summaries; (5) Assessment — conducted before and after learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must organize groups, convey rankings, provide materials, and structure the sequence of activities, presentation, and assessment
#### Constraints
- Assessment must occur both before and after learning to measure enhancement

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students working in cooperative groups

### Learning Goals
- Mathematical concept construction, critical thinking, and problem solving
