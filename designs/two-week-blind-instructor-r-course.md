# A two-week remote born-accessible data science course for blind learners taught by blind instructors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's course artifact consisted of "four interactive sessions, each three-hour long, delivered via Zoom and led by a blind professor who's an expert in using R for data science work," totaling 12 hours for up to 10 participants. Materials (setup guide, slides, R scripts adapted from the open-source Data Science in a Box curriculum, and tactile graphics in swell paper) were shared in advance via GitHub. Sessions covered environment setup with VSCode, data science fundamentals, visualization and exploration with ggplot2 and MAIDR, and a do-it-yourself visualization session. The course team included blind primary and co-instructors, a sighted TA, and a sighted supervisor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must be everyday screen reader users with a laptop running Zoom, VSCode, and R, access to a refreshable braille display, and basic programming understanding.
#### Constraints
- The authors call Session 4 an overambitious goal and could not cover all participants' personalized visualization needs within the time frame.

### Target Learners
- blind professionals and graduate students from fields such as cognitive psychology, political science, and computer science

### Learning Goals
- setting up an accessible data science environment
- creating and communicating multimodal data visualizations
