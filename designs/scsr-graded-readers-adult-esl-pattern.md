# Scaffolded silent reading with graded readers: teacher-guided book selection, accountability conferences, reading logs, and journals in a set silent-reading block

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The pattern adapts Reutzel et al.'s scaffolded silent reading for adult ESL: unlike sustained silent reading, "The teacher plays a more active role." In this study the teacher "assisted the participants in selecting graded readers, monitored their progress, and provided them with feedback," while participants kept a reading log and wrote three journal entries for accountability. Students read graded readers in a 20-minute class block with the goal of finishing and selecting a new book weekly, while the teacher conferenced individually and modeled silent reading himself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supply of graded readers at students' independent reading levels across varied topics and genres
- A set block of class time for silent reading with individual teacher-student conferences
- Accountability structures such as a reading log and journal entries
#### Constraints
- ScSR had mainly been used with native-English-speaking elementary students before this study; adult ESL response was the present study's question

### Target Learners
- adult intermediate-level ESL students at a community college

### Learning Goals
- receptive vocabulary growth
- reading comprehension
- positive attitude toward L2 reading
