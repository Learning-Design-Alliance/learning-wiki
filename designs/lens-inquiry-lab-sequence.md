# Lens Inquiry: a full inquiry-cycle lab sequence from demonstrations through student-designed investigations to synthesis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Lens Inquiry is a three-hour inquiry activity on converging lenses and telescopes for non-science majors, structured as demonstrations, vocabulary and question generation, group investigation, poster presentation, instructor-led synthesis, and telescope construction. The timeline in Table 1 allocates the largest block, 55 minutes, to the Focused Investigation, with 20 minutes for Sharing Out and 20 minutes for the Telescope Worksheet. The authors state the idea behind inquiry is to teach science as real research is done: students identify their own questions, design their own experiments, and share findings with peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators to guide groups; the authors state two facilitators plus the instructor was the minimum number they would recommend to give students enough guidance to reach the content goals in the time allotted
#### Constraints
- The authors report the biggest challenge was covering all of the content given the short amount of time available, and suggest splitting the lab into two sessions

### Target Learners
- community college non-science majors in an introductory astronomy lab course

### Learning Goals
- understanding how converging lenses work and how telescopes form images
- science process skills such as planning investigations and controlling variables
