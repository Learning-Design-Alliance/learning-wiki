# Simulation-based training as a mechanism for improving guideline adherence and retaining emergency procedural skills in practising clinicians

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operates on the premise that simulation-based training "gives better outcomes compared to non-simulation and non-intervention education", and that rehearsing protocol-driven management of rare critical events in a controlled setting produces durable behavioural change in practising clinicians — not only trainees. The authors frame acquired skills as retained at least six months, and suggest "certain elements of correct difficult airway management are being retained for longer periods, warranting less frequent repetition of costly full simulations", implying different decay rates for different skill elements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rehearsal on equipment identical to that used in the assessed scenarios
- Structured debriefing connecting scenario performance to guideline learning
#### Constraints
- Retention beyond six months was not evaluated; a cited prior study (Kuduvalli et al.) found knowledge decay and higher protocol deviation one year after mannequin training
- High cost, technical problems, and variety of approaches limit widespread use in low- and middle-income settings, though low- vs high-fidelity comparisons found similar results

### Target Learners
- practising anaesthesiologists
- experienced clinicians with established management preferences

### Learning Goals
- guideline adherence under stress
- procedural skill retention over months
- clinical decision-making in rare critical events
