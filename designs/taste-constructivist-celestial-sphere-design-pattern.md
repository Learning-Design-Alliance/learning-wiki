# TASTE learning module and planetarium sequence built on constructivist design principles with hands-on celestial-sphere models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention is a learning sequence of an 80-minute school module, two planetarium workshops and a dome presentation, built on design principles "related to a constructivist learning perspective". It systematically explains Sun-star similarities and differences, encourages students to "think in and switch between different reference systems: the geocentric point of view and the allocentric point of view", introduces a physical 3D celestial-sphere model step by step, and minimises teacher instruction while students discuss and make mental models explicit. Materials were iteratively refined through two test runs with a shared protocol across countries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities assume no specific prior knowledge and must be accessible to 13-18 year olds
- The school module must fit two 40-minute lessons; planetarium activities must not exceed 4 hours
- All activities are hands-on and presented in the students' native language
#### Constraints
- The dome presentation only shows the geocentric point of view so it can run in both analogue and digital planetariums

### Target Learners
- secondary school students aged 13-17 in Belgium, Germany, Greece and Italy

### Learning Goals
- understanding the apparent daily and annual motion of the Sun and stars
- switching between geocentric and allocentric frames of reference
