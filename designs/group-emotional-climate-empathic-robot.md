# Group-level emotional climate as a mechanism for empathic robot behavior in collaborative learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article defines emotional climate as "the valence state of a group-level emotion at a given time," detected from students' facial expressions, joint attention, or distraction. A positive climate may trigger reinforcement utterances like "we are playing well" while a negative one prompts engagement-maintaining interventions. This group-level empathy mechanism distinguishes the robot's interaction from one-on-one tutor designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Perception components that process facial features and auditory cues to classify group emotional valence
- A Hybrid Behavior Controller that maps emotional climate states to empathic intervention behaviors
#### Constraints
- Behavioral and emotional state changes depend on the same perceptual features that will drive autonomous robot behavior

### Target Learners
- Children in group learning scenarios

### Learning Goals
- Collaborative learning about sustainable development
