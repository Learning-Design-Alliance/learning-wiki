# Programming exercises for absolute beginners in introductory physics should be simple, browser-based, interactive, step-by-step, and integrated into the physics curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that classes where most students are absolute beginner programmers need an approach that is "simple, involving 75 or fewer lines of well-commented code", easy to use with browser-based tools, interactive at a high frame rate for a video-game feel, step-by-step with interaction with intermediate stages of the correct program, and thoughtfully integrated into the physics curriculum, for example by illustrating velocity and acceleration vectors throughout. The seven OSU exercises are designed around these five considerations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- About 75 or fewer lines of well-commented code visible to the student, with no calculus knowledge required
- Browser-based coding tools with real-time bug-finding capabilities and no installations or plugins
#### Constraints
- Proving the value of game-like activities in programming exercises definitively is beyond the scope of the paper; learning gains beyond conventional web interactives remain an unanswered question

### Target Learners
- algebra-based and calculus-based introductory physics students who are weak or absolute beginner programmers, high school or college

### Learning Goals
- Newton's laws, momentum, torque, harmonic motion, energy conservation, and iterative numerical thinking in mechanics
