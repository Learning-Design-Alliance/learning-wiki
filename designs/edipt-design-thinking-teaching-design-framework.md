# EDIPT design thinking as a framework for designing medical course teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article applies the "EDIPT" (Empathy, Define, Ideate, Prototype and TEST) design thinking theory originating at the Stanford University Design School to design teacher activities, student activities and technical tools for histology and embryology teaching. Each phase maps to a teaching-design task: empathy gathers teacher and student needs, define sets reform objectives, ideate explores strategies such as case-based and collaborative learning, prototype implements the flipped classroom, and test evaluates impact for ongoing improvement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need familiarity with the curriculum to design lectures and targeted comments
- Implementation needs network infrastructure and students' enthusiasm and cooperation
#### Constraints
- Not all chapters are suitable for flipped classroom teaching; the two studied chapters belong to the organs and systems module and do not fully reflect applicability across the course

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in basic medical education

### Learning Goals
- Improved teaching quality and student learning experience
- Students' creative learning and overall competencies
