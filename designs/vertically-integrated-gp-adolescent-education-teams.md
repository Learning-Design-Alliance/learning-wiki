# Vertically integrated teaching teams pairing GPs, registrars and medical students to co-facilitate school health education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention took a vertically integrated (VI) approach, forming teaching teams of one GP, one registrar and one medical student, or one GP with one or two medical students, which then co-facilitated 90-minute health education sessions at secondary schools. The rationale is that registrars and students, being closer in age to school students, "may be seen as more approachable by adolescents, which can help to create a bridge between older GPs and adolescents", while GPs bring greater knowledge of adolescent health issues. The authors suggest this mix "may offer a model for building confidence and skills across the medical education continuum."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams must include members at multiple levels of the medical education continuum
- Teams must be large enough to deliver sessions to six or seven small groups at each secondary school
#### Constraints
- Small sample sizes made comparative analysis between participant levels invalid in this evaluation

### Target Learners
- General practitioners
- General practice registrars
- Medical students

### Learning Goals
- Delivering community-based adolescent health education
- Small group facilitation and co-facilitation skills
