# Self-regulation-phase-based session structure for computer-tailored behavior-change education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention organizes its three tailored sessions around the phases of self-regulated behavior change: "The three sessions were arranged according to the self-regulation phases pre-action, action and evaluation of the behavior change". The first (pre-action) session builds knowledge, awareness, attitude and self-efficacy and ends with goal setting and an if-then implementation intention; the second and third sessions support evaluation of progress, coping planning for difficult situations, goal adjustment, and maintenance information. The design draws on self-regulation theory, the Theory of Planned Behavior and the Precaution Adoption Process Model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants monitor their behavior and goal achievement so tailored personal, normative and comparative feedback can be provided at each phase
- Tailored feedback moments across sessions, including reminders two weeks after each visit to evaluate progress
#### Constraints
- The authors state self-regulation tools were not optimally incorporated and used, and the general self-regulation measure may have been too general to detect behavior-specific changes

### Target Learners
- Adults seeking to change dietary behaviors, including lower-educated adults

### Learning Goals
- Self-regulated dietary behavior change: goal setting, action planning, coping planning and maintenance of change
