# Two-lecture foundational probability and randomness course for quantum physics students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A pair of black-board lectures given at the Institute of Quantum Optics and Quantum Information in 2014, converted into a written text for students. Lecture 1 covers Kolmogorov axiomatics, observables as random variables, conditional probability, the strong law of large numbers, joint distributions and the Boole-Bell inequality, and von Mises frequency theory; a second lecture covers further randomness approaches and quantum probabilistic data. The author wrote it because students "asked me permanently about foundational problems of probability and randomness".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need background in quantum information theory, quantum optics, or quantum foundations, where primitive probability treatment causes misunderstandings
#### Constraints
- The text is based on lectures originally presented in traditional black-board form and is addressed to students at IQOQI and other young physicists

### Target Learners
- experiment-oriented physics students and young physicists at IQOQI and similar institutions

### Learning Goals
- understand foundational problems of probability and randomness and the inter-relation between classical and quantum structures
