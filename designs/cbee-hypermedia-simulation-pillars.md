# Two pillars of computer-based engineering education: hypermedia coupled with industrial-strength simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's organizing account is that computer-based engineering education rests on two pillars — hypermedia and simulation — and that "What was lacking so far is their two-way coupling that provides unprecedented flexibility in choosing learning and teaching patterns keeping in sight future real-world problems". Hypermedia offers non-sequential, distributed, interactive, student-centred presentation with adaptive sequencing; simulation, defined by a computer model of a real system and experimentation on it, supplies authentic engineering practice. The IEE is presented as the proof of concept of this coupling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A two-way coupling between the hypermedia theory content and the simulation engine, rather than separate delivery of either
#### Constraints
- The framework is argued for engineering education in STEM and is illustrated only with crystal growth as a case study

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals
