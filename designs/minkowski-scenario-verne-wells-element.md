# Minkowskian-metric teaching scenario with fictional spaceship data and five structured questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A two-part fictional scenario written by the author combining characters from Verne's De la Terre à la Lune (1865) and Wells' Time Machine (1895), the latter appearing as a 'Space Traveller'. Part I presents fictional data (Table 1) on spaceship length and time between light gleams collected by 'moving' and 'fixed' observers, followed by five questions guiding students from noticing relations between l and t, to plotting points, to seeking a 'tape measure' keeping diagonal lengths invariant. Part II, written taking students' responses into account, leads to the transformation of the Minkowskian metric to its quadratic form. The fictional data are not arbitrary but accord with early Special Relativity thought experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work out the mathematical model themselves rather than having it imposed
- Fictional data because large-scale relativistic effects cannot be achieved in a laboratory
#### Constraints
- The author states the scenario does not claim to serve as an introduction of young students to Special Relativity Theory; the point of view is geometrical rather than physical inquiry

### Target Learners
- Greek high-school students (11th and 12th grade) of varied educational levels

### Learning Goals
- formulating a mathematical model for motions and measurement in space-time
- understanding metric as an additional structure on the plane
