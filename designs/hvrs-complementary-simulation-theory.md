# HVRS as a complementary simulation-based learning environment grounded in experiential learning and deliberate practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames haptic virtual reality simulation as a training tool that "combines three-dimensional visualization with tactile feedback," offering students "a realistic, interactive, and repeatable learning environment." It positions preclinical training models, including HVRS, as "structured learning environments grounded in experiential learning, deliberate practice, and simulation-based education principles." The mechanism proposed is that repeated practice in a controlled setting reduces cognitive load during the transition to clinical practice, allowing students to automatize procedural steps before treating real patients. The authors explicitly frame HVRS as complementary to, not a replacement for, conventional preclinical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single instructor following a standard protocol
- Supervision by a faculty member or researcher during training
- Simulator providing immediate on-screen validation of access cavity preparation
#### Constraints
- Cannot completely replace traditional phantom-based preclinical training
- Direct transfer of skills acquired in a virtual environment to real patient conditions may be limited

### Target Learners
- fourth-year dental students treating patients for the first time in endodontics

### Learning Goals
- endodontic access cavity preparation
- fine motor skills and hand–eye coordination
- three-dimensional anatomical perception
- clinical self-confidence
