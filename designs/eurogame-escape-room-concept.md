# Eurogame escape room concept: mandatory sequential physics puzzles combined with optional, arbitrary Eurogame puzzles scored by a victory point system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article combines a sequential escape-room structure, in which mandatory physics puzzles (PPs) must be solved in order, with a European board game layer: optional Eurogame puzzles (EPs) earn victory points (VP) in any arbitrary and independent sequence, allowing self-adjusted challenge levels by student choice. The authors write that "EPs are optional and arbitrary puzzles" and that the VP system lets them "introduce puzzles without a strong topical connection to the subject of interest". A semester-long score list compares groups' VP per run, and PP points also serve as a fallback so players do not end with 0 VP and as a support mechanism by halving points to sell ideas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An e-game master who is available by phone with diluted visits to check the workflow
- Knowledge-transfer documents per physics puzzle so every questionnaire question could be answered by them
- A rulebook explaining the VP system with a list of point values for PPs and EPs
#### Constraints
- The article states the eEERP's processing time of 2.5 hours and required intensity should not be used for Group A students (high-end physics education), at least with the presented knowledge-transfer documents
- The article notes the definition of Eurogames is not rigid or sharp from an academic point of view

### Target Learners
- First-semester veterinary medicine students aged 18-22 (Group C: basic physics knowledge)

### Learning Goals
- Stabilisation, repetition and application of physics knowledge from lectures
- Slight extension of knowledge by exemplification and veterinary-associated cases
- Training of experimental skills and hands-on mentality
- Decision-making and setting priorities by gauging puzzles
