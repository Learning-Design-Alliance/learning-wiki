# Transversality: integrating environmental education as a transversal theme across existing disciplines rather than a standalone subject

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study's framework is rooted in the concept of transversality, which "according to Greenblatt (2015), referred to a description of objects intersection" and "is rooted in generic and stable Thom's Transversality Theorem (1954)". The author adapts this to education via UNESCO-UNEP (1978)'s recommendation that environmental education not become "just another discipline added to those already existing but be introduced as a transversal theme in school curricula". This infusion of EE into social studies in the basic computer-mediated classroom, using GIS as the instructional vehicle, was the study's conceptual application of the transversality theorem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A host discipline (social studies) into which EE concepts can be infused
#### Constraints
- Environmental education does not exist as a standalone school subject in basic and middle schools in Nigeria

### Target Learners
- Junior secondary school pupils in Nigeria

### Learning Goals
- Environmental awareness, ecological knowledge, attitudes, values, commitments for actions, and ethical responsibilities
