# Tracker video-analysis free-fall lab measuring gravitational acceleration and terminal velocity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Experiment 4 has students drop a golf ball, tennis ball and coffee filter from about 1.829 m (6.0 feet), record slow-motion phone video, and analyze it with the tracker video analysis software. Students calibrate with a 1.829 m stick, mark the ball's y-position frame by frame, and fit displacement-time and velocity-time graphs. As the procedure states, "Do the quadratic fitting and calculate the gravitational constant by using the factor in front of the quadratic term. Then, compare (find percentage error) with g = -9.81 m/s2." For the coffee filter, students check whether quadratic fitting fails and extract terminal velocity from a linear fit of the last data points.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tracker software downloaded and installed (compatible with windows/mac/linux); a mobile phone slow-motion video of the dropped object; excel for graphing and fitting
#### Constraints
- Data collection must stop just before the ball hits the ground; the coffee-filter terminal-velocity analysis uses only the last few data points closer to ground

### Target Learners
- introductory physics I students

### Learning Goals
- uniformly accelerated motion, gravitational acceleration measurement, drag force and terminal velocity
