# Educational DSML design framework built on three principles: evidence-centered design, exploratory learning of dynamic processes, and a block-based programming environment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's design framework rests on three principles the authors name explicitly: "An evidence-centered design approach ... to ensure coherence and coverage of domain concepts, relations, and practices in the design of the DSML constructs"; opportunities "for exploratory learning of dynamic processes by creating a discrete-time step-by-step model of continuous dynamic processes"; and "A block-based programming language ... to lessen programming difficulties among novice learners". The framework is implemented as a four-step design process applied across three science domains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Domain analysis (ECD steps 1-2) must be completed before DSML block creation, identifying target audience, content boundaries, prerequisites, known difficulties, and concept relations
#### Constraints
- The design presumes an existing block-based programming environment in which DSML constructs can be embedded

### Target Learners
- elementary, middle, and high school students in K-12 science

### Learning Goals
- synergistic learning of STEM domain concepts and computational thinking concepts and practices
