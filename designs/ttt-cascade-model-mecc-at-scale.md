# Train-the-trainer (TtT) cascade model for delivering MECC training at scale

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The TtT model "involves using a principal trainer to provide training to individuals so that they become MECC trainers, cascading MECC training to those they go on to deliver training to". The article presents it as an established approach proposed as cost-effective, able to deliver training at scale, and sensitive to context through tailoring opportunities. Its success depends on trainers actually cascading training, which the study found often did not occur. The study analysed this model as implemented for MECC across the NENC region of England.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers must cascade training after attending the TtT programme, upon which the model's success depends
- Organizational top-down support extending beyond TtT attendance to enabling cascade with time and resources
#### Constraints
- The study focused on the NENC approach to MECC training and the TtT programme, which may limit generalisability of the findings
- TtT models often report low cascade rates, with as few as 20% of trainers cascading training according to literature cited

### Target Learners
- Adult staff across health care, local authority, and voluntary and community sectors eligible for MECC TtT training

### Learning Goals
- Delivering person-centred behaviour change conversations (MECC) and cascading that training to others
