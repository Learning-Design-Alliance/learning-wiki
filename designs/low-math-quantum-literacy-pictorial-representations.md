# Low-mathematics quantum literacy courses and pictorial representations for quantum-adjacent learners

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For learners in quantum-adjacent roles, the article describes courses that develop basic understanding of QIST concepts such as quantum bits, superposition, and entanglement. Economou, Barnes, and Rudolph "have developed a teaching method for college and high school students that uses Rudolph's pictorial representations to minimize the need for linear algebra to explain QIST concepts such as quantum gates". Related resources include Raymer's quantum literacy course and textbook and an EdX sequence requiring only basic algebra skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses designed for learners with no previous understanding of QIST concepts or only basic algebra skills
#### Constraints
- Aimed at quantum literacy and quantum-adjacent jobs rather than specialist PhD-level quantum work

### Target Learners
- college and high school students, quantum-adjacent professionals, undergraduates with no previous QIST background

### Learning Goals
- basic understanding of quantum bits, superposition, entanglement, and quantum gates
