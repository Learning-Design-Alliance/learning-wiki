# Flipped-classroom course pattern combining online pre-class lectures, readiness pre-tests, case-based group discussion, and same-day feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern structures a course so that "traditional lectures are replaced by interactive sessions such as group discussions, presentations, and answering questions between students and instructors." Pre-class, students receive online lectures (capped at about 30 minutes) and assigned readings, checked by a 10-minute multiple-choice pre-test at each session's start. In class, small groups work through step-by-step epidemiological case studies with tutors rotating among groups; groups present summaries and submit reports that a single tutor evaluates and returns with feedback the same day. The article reports this design was implemented for five weekly sessions with 10 groups of 12 students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-class online lectures and readings must be distributed in advance, with pre-tests focused on the pre-class content to assist preparedness rather than to grade
- Tutors must prepare materials, supervise two or three groups each, and hold a preview conference before each session to ensure uniform facilitation
#### Constraints
- The classroom environment set up for small-group discussion was unsuited to administering individual exams
- Redundant parallel lecturing in a concurrent course may have discouraged engagement in pre-class self-directed learning

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- epidemiology concepts such as descriptive epidemiology, validity and reliability, surveillance, outbreak investigation, and study design
- problem-solving competency against actual public health issues
