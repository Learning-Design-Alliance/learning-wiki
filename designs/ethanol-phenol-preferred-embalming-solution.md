# Select ethanol–phenol as the default soft-embalming solution for surgical skills training programs balancing fidelity with cost, safety, and accessibility

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends ethanol–phenol (EP) as the preferred soft-embalming solution for surgical skills training laboratories, based on its combination of fidelity and practical performance. The authors state that "significantly higher suitability ratings of the EP embalmed donor and the midline stability of EP tissues in mechanical testing alongside the modest cost, safety, and availability concerns" make it "a superior choice for use in surgical skills training." The recommendation weighs both tissue performance and laboratory-relevant concerns such as cost per donor and chemical hazards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An anatomy laboratory with the capacity to perfuse donors with the ethanol–phenol solution and store embalmed donors at appropriate temperature before testing
#### Constraints
- SRF was identified as least recommended due to its proprietary formula and multi-day protocol
- SSS required additional technician steps to prevent pump clogging from consolidated sediment
- ICL tissue moisture made it read as less life-like, especially for electrocautery
- evaluations were made within a 2-week time frame, so stable performance may not persist beyond it
- the four donors were not matched on age, sex, or comorbidities, so solution-specific differences could be attributable to donor-specific traits

### Target Learners
- surgical residents (PGY 1–3) in surgical foundations training

### Learning Goals
- surgical skills training fidelity: chest tube insertion and anchoring, electrocautery, and bone sawing
