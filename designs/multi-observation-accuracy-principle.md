# Design Earth-radius measurement projects around multiple independent observations, because repeated observations improve accuracy over single-pair methods

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is that a student measurement project should gather multiple sunset-time observations from different heights, since "multiple observations can be used to improve the accuracy of the result." It also eliminates the need to observe sunset at sea level, which the article calls "a considerable limitation" of the earlier method. Timing uses synchronized watches and altitudes and latitude come from GPS, so no elaborate equipment is needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tall building with an unobstructed view of the sunset over the ocean or a large lake
- Synchronized watches for all observers and GPS-determined altitudes and latitude
#### Constraints
- Lower heights give smaller sunset-time differences and require more precise timing measurements
- The experiment fails at latitudes where the sun never rises or sets in a day

### Target Learners
- high school students
- student groups in physics

### Learning Goals
- measuring the Earth's radius
- experimental physics practice
- recreating Eratosthenes' method
