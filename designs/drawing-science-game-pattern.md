# Drawing-to-guess board game pattern: drawing scientific concepts as an expression and assessment of cognitive structure within a team race

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The pattern is a lesson-level game structure in which a player draws a scientific concept so teammates infer the term, and each drawing is treated as "the student's cognitive structure", open to interpretation and debate among the group. Team-based board progression with a time limit pushes the designer toward the simplest representation, abstracting from experience to create figurative meaning. Failed words return to the deck so the same student may draw them again later, which the authors say let the game "also functioned as an assessment tool". Monitors and the teacher explain and discuss the meaning of difficult words during play.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contents of the game should largely have been covered in class to support drawing performance
- A teacher or monitor acts as mediator, explaining and contextualizing the words drawn
#### Constraints
- Words of higher difficulty associated with specific science areas caused difficulty for students who did not know the subject

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- meaningful learning of Natural Sciences concepts
- interpretation and abstraction of scientific knowledge
- peer discussion and socialization
