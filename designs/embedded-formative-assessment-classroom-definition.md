# Embedded formative assessment as classroom-embedded activities that make student thinking visible and inform subsequent instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article adopts Black and Wiliam's definition of formative assessment as "encompassing all those activities undertaken by teachers, and/or by their students, which provide information to be used as feedback to modify the teaching and learning activities", tightened to activities occurring in the classroom. It is grounded in a sociocultural constructivist view of learning and Vygotsky's zone of nearest development, requiring teachers to gather data to target instruction. The process includes both student response construction and the instructor's use of that glimpse into student thinking to modify and refine understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must occur in the classroom at moments appropriate for the learners, not as homework or take-home problem sets.
- The instructor must use the collected information to inform subsequent instruction and refine student understanding.
#### Constraints
- The article limits its discussion to classroom use, though it notes the process extends to distance education and Just In Time Teaching activities between class meetings.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- make student thinking visible to teachers and students
- inform and modify subsequent instruction
- increase student metacognition
