# Tripartite Framework of perioperative clinical reasoning (patient-, surgery-, and anaesthetic-related factors)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Tripartite Framework is a cognitive scaffold that structures perioperative clinical reasoning around three integrated factor sets: patient factors (general condition, preoperative diseases, organ-system pathophysiology), surgical factors (site anatomy, approach, risks, anticipated complications), and anaesthetic factors (technique and agent selection, monitoring, management of intraoperative critical incidents). The authors report that it "serves as a 'cognitive scaffold' for junior residents", transforming fragmented knowledge into a systematic workflow that gives practical traction to System 2 analytical reasoning. It was the core intervention of the elective course and is presented as adaptable to bedside teaching and case discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator with substantial clinical experience (the course used tutors with more than 15 years' clinical experience and AMEE ESME certification) to guide script development and analytical reasoning
#### Constraints
- Grounded in the cognitive demands of anaesthesiology; the authors suggest adaptation to similar high-acuity training contexts rather than claiming general applicability

### Target Learners
- Junior anaesthesiology residents (first-year residents in a standardised residency training system)

### Learning Goals
- Perioperative clinical reasoning: synthesising patient, surgical, and anaesthetic factors to formulate anaesthesia plans and manage preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative scenarios
