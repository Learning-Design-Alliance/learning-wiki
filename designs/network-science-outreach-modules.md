# Seven network-science outreach modules with supplementary lesson materials

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors designed seven interactive modules for 30–45 minute outreach sessions with students aged 13–16: climate change and food webs, small worlds and social networks, disease spread and vaccination, Google's PageRank, graph-theory puzzles, the friendship paradox, and structural balance. They state that "Our session plans are designed for modules that last about 30-45 minutes, but they can be adapted readily for other formats". Modules 1–5 were the most successful and are covered in depth in the Supplementary Online Material, which the authors invite others to use and adapt.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 20–30 minute introductory talk defining networks and basic jargon before breakout sessions
- Small breakout groups of 3–5 students per volunteer, led by 1–2 facilitators
- Volunteers and, ideally, participating school teachers to help run sessions
#### Constraints
- Module 6 (friendship paradox) did not work as designed and needed redevelopment
- Content and format must be adapted from one school to another

### Target Learners
- School students aged 13–16 in the United Kingdom

### Learning Goals
- Intuitive understanding of network science concepts (nodes, edges, degree, small worlds, centrality)
- Appreciation of mathematics as ideas, modeling, and problem-solving rather than only calculation
