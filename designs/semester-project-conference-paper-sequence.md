# Semester-long project sequence culminating in a conference-style paper as integrated final synthesis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course design launches the project early in the term and develops it through topic selection, literature review, midpoint feedback, implementation or simulation, and oral presentation, progressively culminating in a conference-style paper. The paper is framed as "an integrated final synthesis rather than an isolated end-of-course assignment," with elements resembling a professional conference submission including a structured abstract, technical background, methods, results, and discussion. A traditional final examination was omitted so the project could function as a capstone-style culminating experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early project launch to give students time to choose topics and revisit ideas as course concepts are introduced
- Application-centered topic anchoring abstract quantum ideas to a recognizable engineering problem
- Analytical or computational implementation rather than literature summary alone
- Oral presentation as a checkpoint before the final manuscript
#### Constraints
- An alternative comprehensive longer project report submitted to the instructor was available if needed

### Target Learners
- graduate engineering students
- undergraduate senior engineering students

### Learning Goals
- quantum mechanics conceptual understanding
- scientific communication
- research readiness
