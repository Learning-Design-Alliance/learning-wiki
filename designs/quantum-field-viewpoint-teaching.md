# Teaching quantum physics through the field viewpoint: the wavefunction as the nonrelativistic limit of a quantum field

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course adopted Hobson's framework in which the electron is a quantized field and the Schrodinger wavefunction "is simply stated as the nonrelativistic limit of a quantum ﬁeld". In this view, the field is non-local and extends through space, the word "particle" refers to a local interaction in which the field deposits quantized energy, and matter and radiation are treated symmetrically. The author reports this removed the need for statements like the electron "behaving" like a wave and explains how "the electron can get across nodes" without artificial tunneling arguments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need access to the Hobson framework materials cited in the course and must reorganize traditional wave-particle exposition around fields
#### Constraints
- The article notes an alternative viewpoint criticizing the excessive reification of quantum fields (Mermin), and warns that students' habit of "Sticking to particles as the real thing will hamper the assimilation of the concept of quantized ﬁelds"

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science and engineering freshmen

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum physics
- distinguishing quantum uncertainty from everyday uncertainty
