# Interprofessional 'back pain' TBL module for medical and physiotherapy students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 2.5-hour interprofessional TBL session on back pain piloted within the Musculoskeletal Sciences block, joining Year 1 medical students with Year 2 physiotherapy students (311 participants). The article states: "Key principles of our TBL design included: prescribed out-of-class preparation, pre-class individual tests, and in-class team tests, immediate feedback, and clinical problem-solving activities." Physiotherapy students were randomly allocated two or three per established medical team of four to six, and each class had an interprofessional facilitator team.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- compulsory pre-class readings and pre-recorded lectures
- an online 10-MCQ IRAT before class
- facilitators trained in TBL facilitation
- shared learning outcomes aligned with both curricula
#### Constraints
- the mismatch in student cohort numbers compromised the TBL format
- physiotherapy students had no prior TBL experience
- held outside regular weekly schedules due to timetabling

### Target Learners
- Year 1 medical students
- Year 2 physiotherapy students

### Learning Goals
- describe vertebral column anatomy and function
- apply spinal cord and nerve anatomy and physiology
- describe mechanisms of radicular pain
- interprofessional collaboration
