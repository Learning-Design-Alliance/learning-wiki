# Embed an EPO program that relies on collaborating scientists into the collaboration's infrastructure and culture from the outset

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A design principle for large science collaborations: outreach that depends on volunteer scientist participation should be a planned part of collaboration structure, not added later. In DES, EPO "was not embedded during the development stage and had to be “shoehorned in” after survey operations were underway," and the authors conclude that "An EPO program which relies on the participation of collaborating scientists should be embedded into collaboration infrastructure and culture from the outset."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support, agreed strategy, and resources (volunteer time and funding) for EPO activities
#### Constraints
- DES's grassroots, bottom-up program produced innovative projects but faced unforeseen challenges because EPO was added after survey operations began

### Target Learners
- general public reached by large science collaborations
- K-12 and informal science audiences

### Learning Goals
- public awareness of and engagement with science and scientists
