# SALT and PEPPER parallel experimentation-based lab conditions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Two parallel lab conditions within one introductory experimental physics course. SALT labs use "springs, pendulums, circuits, and other equipment and phenomena common to an introductory physics sequence" in open-ended, experimentation-based activities with increasing student freedom and a final student-proposed investigation. PEPPER labs follow the same experimentation-based framework and learning goals but replace the equipment with tabletop cosmic ray muon detectors adapted from the MIT CosmicWatch, including detector-testing sessions and student-led projects, with partner particle physicists connecting the work to contemporary research in semester 2.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both conditions require weekly two-hour lab sections with TAs, group work in groups of two to four, and shared learning outcomes covering iterative data collection, evaluation, communication, and collaboration
#### Constraints
- The article states the PEPPER and SALT labels are "not meaningful acronyms and should not be considered as “named teaching methods”", and PEPPER results are not directly relevant to particle physics researchers

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics and engineering students in large introductory lab courses

### Learning Goals
- experimental critical thinking
- iterative experimental design and data collection
- collaboration and communication of experimental outcomes
