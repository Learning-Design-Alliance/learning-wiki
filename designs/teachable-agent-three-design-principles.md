# Three design principles for teachable agents: visible thinking, independent performance with recursive feedback, and student responsibility for the agent

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes teachable agent (TA) design around three principles drawn from the learning sciences: the TA "makes its thought processes visible"; it "exhibits independent performance to produce recursive feedback," grounded in Premack's recursive thought and the prote´ge´ effect; and it engenders social responsibility toward the agent (naming, customization, game rewards). The authors stress the main value is that the principles "bring these positive learning mechanisms together into a single learning technology," instantiated in Betty's Brain.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher, curriculum specialist, or researcher must produce hidden expert maps so the system can generate questions and feedback
#### Constraints
- The principles are not intended to exclude other approaches to TA design; they reflect features that asserted themselves over 15 years of work

### Target Learners
- elementary-school students (studied with 9-10-year-old fourth-graders)

### Learning Goals
- scientific reasoning schemas such as hierarchical class-inclusion reasoning
- science content understanding
