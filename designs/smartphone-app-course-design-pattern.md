# Smartphone-app course design with increasing problem difficulty and spreadsheet macro linkage

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course design pattern observed in the article: after installing the apps, students reproduce teacher-projected examples on their own devices, share results freely (unlike with calculators), and problem difficulty increases over the semester as specified in the course program. Later, software results are linked to spreadsheets: students learn to design macros, including macros that invoke other macros, and use Visual Basic to create or maintain them, so they can design their own equations for the problems they consider hardest, such as computing measures of central tendency from a supplied data set using handyCalc and a macro.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Apps installed on all student smartphones before the second session
- Video projection in the classroom for the initial demonstration
- Course program specifying increasing difficulty
#### Constraints
- Institutional internet links sometimes fail, so students occasionally had to use their smartphone data

### Target Learners
- university students in exact-science courses

### Learning Goals
- relating theories to applications and designing their own equations to solve difficult problems
