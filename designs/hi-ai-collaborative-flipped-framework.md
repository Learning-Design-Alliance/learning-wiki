# HI-AI collaborative flipped classroom framework explained by constructivist learning theory, cognitive load theory, and the technology acceptance model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a human intelligence (HI)-AI collaborative framework layered on the flipped classroom, in which AI provides "real-time information resources and support for instructional process analysis" while teachers design higher-order tasks and give in-depth feedback. Three complementary theoretical perspectives explain the observed patterns: constructivist learning theory, cognitive load theory, and the technology acceptance model (TAM). AI is positioned as a cognitive scaffold that reduces extraneous load and frees resources for instructor-led higher-order activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to free or low-cost AI tools (such as DeepSeek and Kimi) and basic digital infrastructure
- Instructor guidance rules requiring students to verify and rewrite AI outputs
#### Constraints
- Benefits may not be replicable in resource-limited settings where such tools are unavailable
- In teacher-centred systems or contexts discouraging AI reliance, implementation requires tailored adaptation and faculty development
- Tested only in a single pharmacology course with international students from South and Southeast Asia

### Target Learners
- third-year international undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- pharmacological knowledge integration and clinical decision-making
- higher-order clinical thinking
- self-directed learning
