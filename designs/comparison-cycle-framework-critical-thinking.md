# Comparison-cycle framework for quantitative critical thinking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article names and describes a learning framework in which quantitative critical thinking develops through "a process of repeated comparisons and decisions: comparing new data to existing data and/or models, and then deciding how to act on those comparisons". Each cycle involves making a comparison, acting on it (improving data, adjusting or evaluating a model), and reflecting, with analysis tools such as weighted χ2 embodying the statistical tests. The authors simplified the expert decision tree so first-year students could practice it deliberately with targeted feedback, and argue it fits any instructional setting involving acquisition of data and relating it to scientific models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructional setting that involves the acquisition of data and relating that data to scientific models
- Analysis tools that embody appropriate statistical tests, including measurement variability
- Opportunities for students to improve the data or models
#### Constraints
- The study tested it in an introductory physics lab; the authors state they believe effects would be similar in any subject with quantitative data but this was not tested in this article
- The authors do not expect first-year students to reach expert-level thinking processes

### Target Learners
- first-year university students in data-driven science lab courses

### Learning Goals
- quantitative critical thinking about data and models
- deciding how to act on data-model comparisons
