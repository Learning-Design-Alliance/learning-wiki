# Fibered Mach-Zehnder interferometer laboratory set-up with FTS and optical-amplifier stages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A two-part laboratory experiment for third-year Physics students built on a fibered Mach-Zehnder interferometer propagating 1.5 µm light through monomode, polarization-maintaining fibers. Students modulate the optical path difference to do Fourier Transform Spectroscopy of a Fabry-Perot laser and a superluminescent diode, then place Erbium Doped Fiber Amplifiers in one and then both arms. The article states the setup "eliminates the need for optical alignment" and that "All components are oﬀ-the-shelf, i.e. from standard optical catalogues."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Polarization-maintaining fibers so the light field does not fluctuate between non-interfering polarization states
- A fibered polarizer to remove remaining crosstalk between polarizations
- Fibers of varied lengths (1.0–1.3 m) to compensate differing component path lengths
#### Constraints
- Relatively expensive, with a budget of approximately £15,000
- Delay-line amplitude (5 cm total) insufficient to compensate all component path-length differences without custom fibers

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate Physics students
- early graduate laboratory students

### Learning Goals
- familiarity with fibered optics and interferometry
- understanding of Fourier Transform spectroscopy
- understanding of optical amplification and stimulated emission
