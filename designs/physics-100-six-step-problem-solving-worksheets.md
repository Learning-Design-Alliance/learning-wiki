# UBC Physics 100 prescribed six-step problem-solving strategy with graded worksheet prompts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In UBC's Physics 100, a six-step strategy (Interpret the Problem; Identify Relevant Physics; Model: Identify Assumptions and Relationships; Model: Construct a Diagram; Solve the Problem; Error-checking and Sensemaking) was used in lecture and on small-group worksheets. "Steps 2 through 6 were mandatory and each step was graded," and the first five problem-solving sessions were hour-long workshops on each piece of the strategy. Descriptive prompts for steps 1, 2, 3, and 6 were printed on the worksheets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students were familiarized with the strategy through five explicit hour-long workshops, one on each piece of the strategy.
#### Constraints
- The authors found the prompts were not, in general, effective at promoting sensemaking for these context-rich problems.

### Target Learners
- approximately 800 introductory algebra-based physics students in three lecture sections, fewer than 1% intending to major in physics

### Learning Goals
- solving novel real-world problems
- expert-like problem-solving skills
