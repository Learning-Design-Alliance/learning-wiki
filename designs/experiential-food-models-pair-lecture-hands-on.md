# Pair brief didactic nutrition lectures with hands-on experiential activities using visual food models to build sugar literacy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention design combines short lectures with experiential learning activities in which students manipulate food models, following the recommendation that effective adolescent food literacy interventions use "innovative teaching aids, including opportunities for experiential or 'hands-on' learning" and incorporating behavior change techniques from the COM-B model. Each 45-minute session paired a brief lecture (diet quality, then sugar content and label reading) with group activities visualizing diets and sugar content with the models. The trial showed this pairing significantly improved knowledge, label-reading confidence and intention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Food model kits and real nutrition fact labels for the hands-on activities
- Delivery within school Physical and Health Education class time
#### Constraints
- Follow-up immediately after the second lesson meant behavior frequency and self-efficacy did not change; longer-term effects were not assessed

### Target Learners
- Older adolescents (high school students) in Physical and Health Education classes

### Learning Goals
- Sugar literacy: knowledge, awareness, skills and intention to limit added sugar
