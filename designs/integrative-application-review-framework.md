# Integrative application review framework: reviewing de-identified personal statements alone to assess applicant potential rather than pedigree

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A review framework in which the initial admissions review considers only the de-identified personal statement so that assessment rests on the applicant's self-driven narrative rather than on potentially biased metrics. The program states the process "involves reviewing only the PS to focus solely on the applicant narrative" and that it "reduces the emphasis on the applicant's past and places it instead on their present and future, shifting the focus from pedigree to potential." One unblinded committee member verifies baseline requirements; a rubric with open-ended prompts and a seven-reviewer consensus process guide interview selection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Minimum program requirements must be verified by a single unblinded reviewer (minimum 3.0 GPA, Bachelor's in Biological Science, three letters of recommendation, CV, unofficial transcripts)
- Personal statements removed from application, de-identified, and stored centrally
- Reviewers complete mandatory implicit bias training before reviewing and interviewing
- A vetted rubric with open-ended prompts and a recommend-to-interview vote
#### Constraints
- May not completely remove bias introduced in the application process since blinding did not change possible mention of mentor names and institutions in the PS
- Resource intensive at seven reviewers per application
- Lack of generalizability from a single centrally funded, highly specific PhD program

### Target Learners
- PhD applicants to biomedical science graduate programs

### Learning Goals
- Fair and equitable assessment of applicant potential and narrative in graduate admissions
