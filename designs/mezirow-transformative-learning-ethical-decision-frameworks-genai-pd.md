# Transformative learning theory combined with ethical decision-making frameworks as the design basis for GenAI professional development

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article grounds its professional development design in Mezirow's Transformative Learning Theory together with "established ethical decision-making frameworks in educational technology." This combined framework positions ethical reasoning as something educators develop through structured transformation rather than as a bolt-on compliance topic, and it guided how the study designed, implemented, and evaluated a program that "embeds ethical reasoning within a multicultural Global South institution."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional development activities must be structured so that ethical reasoning is embedded within technical GenAI training rather than separated from it
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied in one multicultural Global South higher education institution; the article states the model's adaptability to other contexts and its scalability remain to be investigated

### Target Learners
- Higher education English for Academic Purposes educators

### Learning Goals
- Ethical awareness of generative AI use
- Digital andragogical competence
- Academic integrity, transparency, and equity in AI-supported teaching

- [A GenAI-integrated professional development program produced significant pre-to-post gains in EAP educators' ethical awareness (d = 0.93) and digital andragogical competence](../claims/genai-pd-significant-ethical-awareness-gains.md) [+M] — the programme this framework guided
