# Bypass introductory quantum physics content and introduce quantum computing through linear algebra to reach a wider audience

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that introductory quantum computing content be taught "through linear algebra instead", bypassing the physics route that makes the subject "a relatively harder subject to grasp". Quantum programming tasks are prepared in line with the content so that "task-based programming approach helps participants to learn through experience". The authors argue this facilitates people from diverse backgrounds entering the field and builds quantum awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- participants with basic knowledge in linear algebra and introductory programming experience in Python
#### Constraints
- the approach leaves the correspondence between mathematical constructs and physical phenomena optional rather than taught
- knowledge gains measured correspond to the lowest level of Bloom's Taxonomy

### Target Learners
- STEM undergraduates without physics background
- high school students with prerequisites
- diverse adult workshop participants

### Learning Goals
- basic quantum computing concepts
- basic quantum programming skills
- quantum awareness
