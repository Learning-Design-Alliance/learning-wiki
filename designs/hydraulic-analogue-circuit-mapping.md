# Hydraulic analogue mapping of basic electric circuit elements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A quantitative analogy in which electric circuit elements are mapped onto fluid systems: a long narrow tube is the resistor, governed by the Hagen-Poiseuille equation, with "The hydraulic resistance is given by 𝑅H = 8𝜇𝐿/(𝜋𝑟4)"; an open container with a bottom outlet is the capacitor, with C_H = A/(ρg); and a large low-flowrate container acts as a constant pressure source or hydraulic battery. Pressure difference plays the role of voltage and fluid volume the role of charge, yielding a hydraulic RC circuit with time constant τ_H = R_H C_H.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The cross-sectional area A of the capacitor container must be much greater than that of any resistor in the system; the fluid surfaces in battery and capacitor must be at the same atmospheric pressure to complete the circuit
#### Constraints
- Unlike electrical resistance, hydraulic resistance depends on fluid viscosity, and R_H ∝ r^-4 rather than r^-2; an overflowing hydraulic capacitor is analogous to dielectric breakdown

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding voltage, current, resistance, and capacitance through visual analogy
- alleviating commonly-held student misconceptions about basic circuits
