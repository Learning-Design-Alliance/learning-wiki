# Continue prematurity risk-clinic follow-up into school age with education and psychology professionals on the multidisciplinary staff

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that follow-up of preterm children in risk outpatient clinics should not stop at the preschool years but continue after schooling begins, because academic difficulties emerge at school age even in survivors without severe sequelae. It explicitly recommends that "education and psychology professionals be included in the multidisciplinary staff" conducting this follow-up, so that learning difficulties can be identified and addressed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Follow-up clinics currently tracking preterm infants extend their monitoring beyond early childhood into the school years
- Multidisciplinary staff include education and psychology professionals
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a cross-sectional single-clinic cohort without a term-born control group; the authors list reduced sample size and difficulty retrieving neonatal record data as limitations

### Target Learners
- School-age children born preterm (6-14 years), especially those born extremely preterm

### Learning Goals
- Early identification of reading, writing and mathematics difficulties and cognitive alteration in survivors of preterm birth
