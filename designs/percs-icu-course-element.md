# PERCS-ICU: a 10-hour simulation-based experiential communication course for ICU residents

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
PERCS-ICU is an adaptation of the PERCS educational model developed at Boston Children's Hospital-Harvard Medical School, implemented in 2017 as a mandatory course for 4th-year anesthesia and intensive care residents at the University of Milan. It features "experiential learning methods such as simulations and debriefings of case scenarios in a safe, collaborative, and interdisciplinary learning environment." The course comprises an 8-hour workshop with lectures, three simulated difficult conversations with trained actors observed via closed-circuit television, debriefings with video review and actors' feedback, a photolanguage session, and a 2-hour follow-up 15 days later.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two psychologists and an ICU physician trained in PERCS methodology as facilitators
- trained actors with a background in clinical psychology
- a realistic case scenario presented with images
#### Constraints
- Suspended in 2020 due to the SARS-CoV-2 outbreak
- evaluated at a single residency school in Milan without a control group

### Target Learners
- 4th-year anesthesia and intensive care residents

### Learning Goals
- communicating bad news and end-of-life decisions with families
- exploring attitudes to organ donation
- self-reflection and emotional awareness in difficult conversations
