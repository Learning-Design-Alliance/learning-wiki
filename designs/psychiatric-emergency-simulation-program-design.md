# Psychiatric emergency simulation program: co-designed scenarios, small groups, psychological-safety briefing, and PEARLS debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A program of three psychiatric emergency scenarios — olanzapine post-injection syndrome, non-suicidal self-injury, and non-fatal hanging — co-designed with psychiatric trainees and delivered in the first fortnight of a 3-month rotation. "Simulation training was run over a 2-hour period in small groups of three to six house officers plus three to five trainers", with pre-reading, a briefing on psychological safety, immersive props and actors, and PEARLS debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios developed with psychiatric trainees who had experienced them clinically, in collaboration with simulation experts, senior psychiatric nurses, and consultant psychiatrists
- Small groups with multiple trainers, and registrars and psychiatric nurses acting in the scenarios
#### Constraints
- Designed for house officers at the start of a psychiatric rotation; the scenarios were uncommon events difficult to teach in real clinical environments

### Target Learners
- house officers (junior doctors) beginning a psychiatric rotation

### Learning Goals
- managing psychiatric emergencies such as hanging, self-harm, and post-injection syndrome
- non-technical skills such as situational awareness and trauma-informed care
