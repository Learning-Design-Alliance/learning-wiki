# Three key collaborative structures for changing the dynamics of classroom instruction during standards implementation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report proposes that standards implementation in mathematics rests on three collaborative structures: role-diverse sense-making teams, strategic partnerships with technical assistance providers, and empowered site-based leaders. The authors observed that "one important strategy to guide this change comes from diverse groups of educators working together to create coherence and alignment of effort within a district system." The structures share functions: supporting iterative shared understanding, cross-group collaboration, and adult learning practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All educators and staff across the district system must be involved in the learning agenda; the report states no one can be left out
#### Constraints
- The framework derives from observations of 10 California districts implementing the CCSS-M under the Math in Common initiative, not from a controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- K-8 teachers and district educators in public school districts

### Learning Goals
- Implementing standards-aligned mathematics instruction and shifting classroom instructional dynamics
