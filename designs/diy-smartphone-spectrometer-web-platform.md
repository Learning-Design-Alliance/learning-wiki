# DIY smartphone diffractive spectrometer with free web platform for instant calibrated spectral analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A low-cost diffractive spectrometer with a diffraction grating in a 3D printed holder, attached over a smartphone camera with a rubber band. It 'must be paired with an android phone or a laptop that can access the website' to convert first-order diffracted light into calibrated line spectra, and is calibrated with a fluorescent light source.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An android phone or laptop with browser access to the free spectral-analysis website
- Wavelength calibration with a fluorescent light source before use; afterwards 'the relative positioning of the phone and spectrometer should not be changed, or else the setup will need to be re-calibrated'
#### Constraints
- Limited wavelength resolution prevents distinguishing very close peaks; calibration errors cause minor shifts in peak positions; sensor saturation flattens very strong peaks; the processing environment changes spectral backgrounds
- The article states such DIY spectrometers 'should be used as qualitative rather than quantitative pedagogical tools'

### Target Learners
- high school students (deployed across Junior Colleges in Singapore)

### Learning Goals
- wave optics and diffraction gratings
- quantization of electronic energy levels (Bohr model)
- broadband vs discrete emission spectra
- Heisenberg's energy-time uncertainty principle
- spectroscopy in everyday life
