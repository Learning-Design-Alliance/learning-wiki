# Five-premise design pattern for beginner physics programming exercises: simple, browser-based, interactive, step-by-step, curriculum-integrated

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article carries over five design premises from its classical mechanics predecessor exercises. The authors state that "students need an approach that is (1) simple, involving 75 lines of code or fewer with plenty of comments, (2) easy to use with browser-based coding tools (3) interactive, with a high frame rate to give a video-game like feel", plus step-by-step interaction with intermediate stages and thoughtful integration into the physics curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exercises stay at or under about 75 lines of code with plenty of comments, run in browser-based coding tools, and maintain a high frame rate for a video-game like feel
#### Constraints
- The premises were designed for students with no prior programming experience and illustrated, for example, by velocity and acceleration vectors throughout

### Target Learners
- students with no prior programming experience in introductory physics courses

### Learning Goals
- physics concepts of the host course, such as velocity and acceleration vectors illustrated throughout the exercises
