# Three-phase framework for optimising live tissue training as a simulated learning event (Planning, Delivering, Experiencing)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organises participants' optimisation suggestions into three themes reflecting stages of participation in a simulated learning event: Planning (what LTT should be used to learn and for whom), Delivering (logistics, pedagogic methods, framing of the animal), and Experiencing (clinical realism, contextual realism, real-life survival). The authors map these onto Nestel and Gough's six-stage simulation design — "Our Planning theme is akin to the preparing stage, with the Experiencing theme reflective of the simulating stage" — and combine them with simulation theory and learning theory to generate recommendations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analysis grounded in interview data from learners and educators with prior LTT experience
#### Constraints
- Developed specifically for LTT in surgical trauma training; the authors note theme boundaries are not sharply delineated and sub-categories interact bi-directionally

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate surgical trainees and surgeons attending trauma courses
- Surgical educators and course faculty

### Learning Goals
- Technical surgical skills such as haemorrhage control
- Non-technical skills including team-working, decision-making and stress management
