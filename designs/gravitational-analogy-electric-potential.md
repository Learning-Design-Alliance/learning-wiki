# Gravitational analogy between mechanical and electrical potential energy as a framework for teaching electric potential and voltage

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops an analogy between a mass in a homogeneous gravitational field and a charge in a homogeneous electric field, formalised through potential energy differences. Electric potential is introduced as an object-independent measure, so voltage is defined as potential difference rather than as "energy per charge". Using the analogy for a two-dimensional dc circuit, the electric potential is encoded in the height dimension of a representational model, so that voltage drops can be read off as height differences. The authors argue students' preconceptions of gravitational potential energy support transfer of the energy concept to electric circuits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have intuitive knowledge of the connection between potential energy and height, as typically developed in basic secondary-school mechanics courses
#### Constraints
- The formal analogy to the gravitational potential requires the case of a homogeneous and straight conductor; validity of the field-potential relation presupposes no time-varying magnetic field present

### Target Learners
- Year-8 secondary school students
- Students in higher secondary and undergraduate physics or electrical engineering courses

### Learning Goals
- Understand electric potential and voltage as potential difference in dc circuits
- Relate macroscopic circuit concepts to microscopic electron behaviour
