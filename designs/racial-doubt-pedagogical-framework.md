# Racial doubt as a disequilibrium-based pedagogical framework for teaching about race

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's organizing framework holds that the first step in teaching race-related courses is establishing "racial doubt" that challenges students' preconceived assumptions, stereotypes, and experiences. Drawing on Piaget's "disequilibrium" between current categorical beliefs and new information, the framework holds that doubt opens students to reevaluating assumptions such as that racial categories are distinct, clearly defined, and predictable, and to broader issues like structural inequality. The study operationalizes it through active-learning exercises and measures it as reduced classification confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must create course content that challenges stereotypical views from the onset and foster an intellectually safe environment for discussion
#### Constraints
- The authors state raising doubt is the beginning, not the end, of greater wisdom, and that their findings provide only limited insights into how 'unlearning' affects future learning in other classes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in race and ethnicity courses, including students of privilege and majority who may manifest the most resistance

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking about the social construction of race
- recognizing structural inequality and racial stereotypes
