# CPOAL: a two-stage, three-month pre-training program for newly recruited pharmacists without clinical pharmacy backgrounds

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Clinical Pharmacist Occupational Adaptive Learning (CPOAL) program is a three-month pre-training delivered before China's one-year standardized clinical pharmacist training. Its first stage is two weeks of job awareness training covering "the daily work schedule, personnel management system, basic work content, skill requirements (professional and communication skills), and assessment criteria". The second stage teaches basic clinical diagnosis and treatment processes and common medical software, with clinical pharmacist instructors covering prescription review, patient monitoring, medication education, and pharmacy consultation, and clinician instructors teaching ward rounds, history taking, diagnosis, and follow-up, via centralised lectures and on-site teaching in general medicine wards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior experts in clinical pharmacy or clinical medicine as instructors
- Access to affiliated hospital wards and medical software for on-site teaching
- Delivery before the one-year standardized training
#### Constraints
- Developed for the Chinese pathway in which NRPs are graduates of basic pharmacy programs without clinical medicine training or internships; the article states its design did not draw on training models for nurses, physicians, or clinical pharmacists from other countries

### Target Learners
- Newly recruited pharmacists without a clinical pharmacy academic background

### Learning Goals
- Career awareness and professional adaptability
- Clinical soft skills including communication, adaptability, and clinical reasoning
