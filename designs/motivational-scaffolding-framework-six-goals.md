# Motivational scaffolding framework: six motivationally grounded goals for scaffold design in PBL

## Description
The article's central framework holds that scaffolds in problem-based learning environments should be designed around six motivational goals. As the authors state, "our framework posits that motivation-enhancing scaffolds in PBL should promote these six goals that are broadly supported by motivation theories: establish task value, promote mastery goals, promote belonging, promote emotion regulation, promote expectancy for success, and promote autonomy." Achieving these goals is expected to raise behavioral, cognitive, and emotional engagement, since "motivation is a basis for subsequent engagement." ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolds must be designed intentionally to encourage motivation rather than assuming authentic problems automatically engage students
- Sustained situational interest requires content knowledge and a belief in task value
#### Constraints
- Framework addresses computer-based scaffolds in PBL; teacher scaffolds are acknowledged but beyond the article's scope
- The article synthesizes motivation theories, adopting single terms where constructs differ in nuance, to keep focus on design implications

### Target Learners
- K-12 and university students working on authentic, ill-structured problems in PBL environments

### Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement and motivation during extended problem-based learning
- Deep conceptual learning and problem-solving in authentic inquiry
