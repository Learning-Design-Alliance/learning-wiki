# FCCL in-class session pattern: Q&A reinforcement, application analysis and clinical case analysis in small groups

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's FCCL design organises each 90-minute flipped session for eight groups of eight students around three synergistic components. It reports: "the Q&A reinforced core anatomical knowledge, application exercises built interpretive and reasoning skills and case discussions integrated learning in a realistic clinical context." Sessions follow pre-class video viewing and a closed-book specimen quiz, with the instructor acting as facilitator and students writing reflective journals afterwards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must watch the assigned pre-class videos and complete the pre-class specimen quiz before the session; the article states inadequate preparation would hinder benefiting from in-class activities.
#### Constraints
- The nervous system was excluded from the FC approach and taught traditionally due to its complexity.

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate clinical medicine students

### Learning Goals
- systematic anatomy knowledge across nine organ systems
- higher-order application and analysis skills
- clinical reasoning connecting structure to practice
