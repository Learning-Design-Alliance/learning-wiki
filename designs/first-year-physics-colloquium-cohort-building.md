# First-year exploratory colloquium for cohort-building, identity, and self-efficacy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A weekly, half-credit, pass-fail first-year Physics and Engineering Colloquium emphasizing overarching themes in the physical sciences with a high relational component. The article says a successful experience cultivates "social capital (cohort-building and inclusion), content engagement in the discipline (identity), and active learning in the discipline (self-efficacy)", introducing first-year students to upper-level cohorts and faculty through hands-on investigations. It was required for the physics degree from 2013 and enrollment rose from 19 in 2013 to 36 in 2019.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty consensus and student receptivity for curricular change; implementation carried out for a minimum of three years before evaluation
#### Constraints
- Graded pass-fail on participation, completion of assignments, and written reflection quality; pandemic effects on this high-relational course were unclear

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate physics and engineering students at a liberal arts college

### Learning Goals
- STEM identity and sense of belonging
- self-efficacy for experimental tasks
- cohort-building and inclusion
