# Introductory patient communication training curriculum for medical physics graduate students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An introductory patient communication training curriculum for medical physics graduate students, developed by a medical physicist together with a radiation oncologist, social workers, and two experts in simulated patient interactions. Instruction centered on "a 1.5‐h lecture developed and led by the social workers", and encounters were scored with a rubric "based on the communication rubric developed through the Macy Initiative in Health Communications".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- "Simulated patient encounters require knowledge of how to design the activity, train the actors, and obtain funding to pay for them."
- Instructors with medical interpersonal communication expertise, such as the social workers who led the didactic content
#### Constraints
- Where funding is difficult, the authors note that "alternative formats such as role play between participants or other faculty, may be more viable and should be investigated"

### Target Learners
- medical physics graduate students at certificate, Masters, and PhD levels

### Learning Goals
- foundational patient communication concepts
- effective communication skills for interacting with patients
