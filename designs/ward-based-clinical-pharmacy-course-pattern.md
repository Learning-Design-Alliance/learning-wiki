# Ward-based clinical pharmacy course combining case-based learning, team-based small groups, and supervised patient contact

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course is a patient-centred ward-based placement (2 × 4 hours) on a psychiatric ward, supervised by a teacher practitioner (TP) fully integrated into the ward team, preceded by a 1.5-hour preparation lecture and a 1.5-hour active-learning seminar. Case-based learning is integrated as an active-learning style, and team-based learning in small groups of 3 students is the chosen teaching strategy. Day 1 focuses on taking a medication history and identifying drug-related problems through a supervised patient interview; day 2 focuses on counselling a psychiatric patient, with the TP modelling the counselling while students observe. The course targets final-semester (8th) pharmacy students who have passed a pre-course oral clinical knowledge exam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher practitioner fully integrated into the ward team, with 50% of their work time dedicated to teaching activities
- A friendly and highly supportive work environment comprising the faculty of Clinical Pharmacy, the Psychiatric Clinic and the Pharmacy Department
- Small student groups (teams of 3 students)
- Patients asked for their consent to participate
- Students with prior knowledge in psychiatric and general pharmacotherapy, verified by a pre-course oral exam
#### Constraints
- Ward-based teaching can be performed only in small groups with close supervision, making the course very time-consuming
- Pharmacists who work regularly on wards and are competent in teaching are needed as tutors, a requirement currently not met by the majority of German universities
- The learning exercises mostly engaged the lower and middle levels of Bloom's revised taxonomy (remembering, understanding and applying), with some higher-level (analysing) learning
- The students' total exposure to the clinical setting and direct patient care experience was limited to 8 hours

### Target Learners
- final-year (8th semester) pharmacy students in Germany

### Learning Goals
- improving patient and interdisciplinary communication skills
- identifying and managing pharmaceutical care issues
- counselling patients on drug therapy
- taking medication histories
