# Five-assignment incremental skill-building sequence for phylogenetic tree construction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A semester-long sequence of five assignments that progresses "through the steps of phylogenetic tree construction, beginning with basic skills and culminating with relatively complex tasks." Students first build a character-by-taxon matrix, then map traits onto a skeleton tree, then learn two hand-construction techniques (base-to-tips and tips-to-base) introducing homoplasy and polytomies, and finally construct a large tree from a matrix of 25 hypothetical DNA sequence positions for 13 taxa. Content acquisition is embedded within skill development, and trees omit branch-length information to let students focus on topology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments distributed across the semester with 1 week of advance preparation time
- Small-group and class-wide discussion time in lab with revision allowed before grading
- Low-stakes grading (approximately 4% of total course grade)
#### Constraints
- Trees used omit branch lengths conveying time or character-state change counts, simplifying hand construction

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate biology majors (90% or more of enrollment)

### Learning Goals
- Constructing and interpreting phylogenetic trees from morphological and sequence data
