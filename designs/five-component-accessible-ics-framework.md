# Five-component framework for systematically redesigning introductory CS curricula for students with visual impairments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a curriculum redesign framework integrating accessibility into core curricular design rather than peripheral accommodations. It comprises "five interconnected components": multi-modal screen-reader-compatible learning resources, tactile programming kits supporting hands-on algorithmic thinking, peer mentoring and instructor support structures, accompanying resources for tools and course content, and scaffolded psychosocial interventions addressing stereotype threat and self-efficacy. The framework is grounded in universal design for learning principles and validated through expert consultation with accessibility specialists and disability services professionals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation within standard university environments without specialized technical infrastructure or significant investment
- Instructor preparation time comparable to traditional course development, with documentation and training materials provided
#### Constraints
- The framework lacks empirical validation through systematic implementation and assessment of learning outcomes, student satisfaction, or long-term educational impact
- Design assumes a high-resource university context with 3D printing, dedicated support services, and flexible budgets

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory computer science students with visual impairments

### Learning Goals
- introductory computer science concepts and programming
- equitable participation in collaborative computing education
