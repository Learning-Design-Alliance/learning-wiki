# Online TBL session structure: six true-or-false problems with iRAT, breakout-room team discussion, tRAT and teacher feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's concrete online TBL implementation: 90-minute sessions on the video conferencing platform Webex with Moodle, in which "Each of the online TBL sessions included six problems, each presented as a question with five true or false answers". Students first answered individually (iRAT), discussed each problem in five-student breakout-room teams, entered the group decision as a tRAT vote, and received immediate teacher feedback and clarification in an interactive plenary discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A video conferencing platform supporting breakout rooms and a polling tool; teachers trained to manage breakout rooms, operate the polling tool, and handle results without additional personnel support.
#### Constraints
- Implemented online during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic; course credits were earned based on attendance only, with no grade incentive on RAT scores, which the authors note likely resulted in less team interaction and team building.

### Target Learners
- second-term medical students attending TBL sessions in batches of 60-70

### Learning Goals
- application-oriented problem solving and topic-specific domain knowledge acquisition
