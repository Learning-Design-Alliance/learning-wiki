# Three-section progression structure for a short course on computational modelling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course uses progression as its guiding design principle, moving from familiar to advanced material across three three-hour lectures. "The course is divided into three sections, each of which is delivered in a three-hour lecture (including two ten-minute breaks)." Section one refreshes thermodynamics and numerical-methods fundamentals; section two introduces five classes of thermodynamic models ordered from more-familiar (ideal gas, ideal solution) to more-advanced (SAFT, empirical); section three builds on both to cover computational problems such as root-finding, stability analysis and flash algorithms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content and exercises are structured such that they progressively reach the required level of complexity and sophistication
#### Constraints
- With three three-hour lectures, students had a harder time concentrating by the final hour of each day
