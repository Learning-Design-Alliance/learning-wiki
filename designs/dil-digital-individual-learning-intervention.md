# DIL: an individual digital learning intervention combining body-and-mind self-regulation training with the Magical Garden early maths game

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Digital Individual Learning (DIL) for Body and Mind is an individual, digitally implemented intervention aiming to enhance early maths skills, number sense and self-regulation. It combines a package of 12 body-awareness, breathing and attention activities (including the metacognitive strategies The Bird Breath and Oh, well I can…) administered at circle time with the digital learning game Magical Garden, which includes a Teachable Agent based on learning-by-teaching. DIL was implemented 1 h, 5 times per week for 6 weeks, with 15–30 min per session of individual game play (a minimum of 20 sessions per child) while children wore headphones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preschool staff trained in four 2-h evening sessions covering the rationale for early math learning, self-regulation, scaffolding, and use of Magical Garden
- Teachers scaffolding children's participation, supporting focused attention, metacognition and emotional regulation, and helping with mathematical tasks and the digital device
- Program support for Magical Garden provided by the Education Technology Group at Lund University
#### Constraints
- Individual design with headphones to avoid distractions; game exercises progressively advance in difficulty and are scaffolded by teachers

### Target Learners
- Preschool children aged 4;0–6;0 years

### Learning Goals
- Early maths skills and number sense
- Self-regulation and executive functions
- Attention
