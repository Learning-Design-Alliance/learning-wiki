# Peer-customer mechanism: students pitch problems and serve as customers for other teams' projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Peer-customers is a customer-pairing mechanism for hands-on final projects in which each student pitches one personal problem and a portion of pitches are chosen by student voting and instructor curation. Each development team is paired with a peer-customer who is in the class but not on the team: "students within the class will pitch one problem that they wish someone in the class would make a solution to solve for them." The dissociation forces teams to practice customer needs assessment, testing, and surveying, and the mechanism scales because the number of peer-customers is proportional to enrollment. It was prototyped in an introductory physical prototyping course across two offerings with N=29 and N=35 students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each development team should have exactly one student acting as a peer-customer for another team, so teams retain enough non-customer members during in-class activities
- Peer-customer selection should occur after the initial add/drop period to reduce risk of a customer dropping the class
#### Constraints
- Not a perfect replica of a real-world paying customer
- May not be appropriate for courses focused deeply on customer discovery, need finding, and new product ideation, since the customer arrives with a defined need

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in hands-on design-oriented courses with team final projects

### Learning Goals
- customer needs assessment
- designing solutions for a real client
- system testing against customer-defined metrics
