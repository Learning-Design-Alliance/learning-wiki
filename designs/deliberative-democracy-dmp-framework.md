# Deliberative Democracy and the Deliberative Mini-Public framework

## Description
Deliberative Democracy emerged around 1990 as a "deliberative turn" in democratic theory, responding to a crisis of confidence in how typical democratic systems engage citizens. It operates by convening a Deliberative Mini-Public (DMP) with three core features: "Influence", "Inclusion", and "Deliberation", the latter providing "open dialogue, access to information, respect, space to understand and reframe issues, and movement to toward consensus". Random or stratified selection delivers the mini-public aspect; neutral facilitation, balanced expert witnesses, and senior-leadership sanction are further key features. The article applies DD as an organisational consultation on AI ethics in higher education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sanction by senior leadership with commitment that the DMP's recommendations matter, so the process is not tokenistic
- Neutral facilitation by a person or team with no stake in the outcome
- Random or stratified sampling to deliver the mini-public aspect and mitigate bias
#### Constraints
- The use of DD for an organisational rather than citizen consultation, and for AI ethics, is novel, so established practice evidence is limited
- Who sets the DMP's purpose/agenda may be contentious; arm's-length conduct is advised if framing bias is feared

### Target Learners
- higher education students
- educators and tutors
- institutional leaders

### Learning Goals
- understanding ethical issues of analytics/AI educational technology
- deliberative decision-making and consensus skills
