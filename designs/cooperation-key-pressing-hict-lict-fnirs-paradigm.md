# Adapted cooperation key-pressing paradigm distinguishing HICT and LICT with fNIRS hyperscanning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-task experimental element adapted from a prior cooperation key-pressing paradigm and administered under fNIRS hyperscanning. In HICT, "if |RTleft − RTright |<| R Tleft + RTright |/8, they could receive 1 point" rewarding synchronous key-pressing; in LICT, each participant pressed faster than a personally calibrated standard time derived from practice trials. Each round comprised 20 formal trials with per-trial fast/slow feedback, and neural data were collected from 21 channels covering PFC.R, DLPFC.L, DLPFC.R and TPJ.R.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two NIRSport fNIRS systems; E-prime 3.0 stimulus control; participants refrained from verbal or non-verbal communication
#### Constraints
- The fNIRS device's limited channel capacity conﬁned the study to the PFC and the TPJ.R, and the authors note the generalizability of the ﬁndings may be limited because real-world cooperative situations are often more complex.

### Target Learners
- college students in same-gender dyads

### Learning Goals
- cooperative behavior and neural coordination during joint tasks
