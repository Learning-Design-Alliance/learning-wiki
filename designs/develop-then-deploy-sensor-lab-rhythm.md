# Alternate-week develop-then-deploy lab rhythm with context-rich problems solved via student-built sensor systems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The pattern organizes a semester of labs so that students first develop a measurement system and then apply it. "Over the course of the term, in roughly alternate weeks, students first developed microcontroller-driven sensor systems to perform a given measurement, and then deployed their systems to address a set of similar problems." Problems relate to normal University Physics topics but are context-rich, framed as tasks an actual scientist or engineer would do, with specifications and sensor data sheets provided.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A context-rich concrete preparation that makes the activity feel like authentic scientist or engineer work
- Small, meaningfully chosen student groups
- Whiteboarding sessions at various stages
#### Constraints
- The approach may seem like extra work to students in a traditional lab class, per the authors' pilot experience

### Target Learners
- University Physics students working in small groups

### Learning Goals
- Designing and deploying data-collection systems
- Transferring a constructivist modeling pattern to new sensors and algorithm demands
