# Curriculum design characteristics fostering perceived authenticity: flexibility, live data, disciplinary practice structure, and culminating project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies four curriculum-level design characteristics that together fostered students' sense of authentic learning: curricular flexibility enabling pursuit of interest-aligned explorations, grounding activities in live real-time datasets, structuring lessons around core data science practices, and embedding authentic assessment in a culminating project. As the article states, "The curriculum's flexibility boosted Personal Authenticity by enabling students to pursue data exploration activities and projects that resonated with their diverse interests." Each characteristic maps to one of the four thick-authenticity dimensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Datasets and themes selected based on student interests identified through participatory design research
- Tools supporting the full data science workflow
- A rubric aligned with core data science practices for assessment
#### Constraints
- Tensions exist among the four dimensions; focusing on one form of authenticity may impact another

### Target Learners
- high school students in computer science classes

### Learning Goals
- data science skills and practices
- engagement through personally meaningful inquiry
