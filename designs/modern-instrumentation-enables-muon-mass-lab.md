# Modern instrumentation makes the 1964 spark-chamber muon-mass experiment feasible for instructional laboratories

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 1964 proposal to measure the muon mass from spark-chamber decay tracks was never adopted by instructional lab developers because it required too much specialized technical expertise and photographic-film capture was too cumbersome. The article shows the experiment "can be implemented today with relative ease through the use of modern instrumentation, including CCD-based image acquisition", paired with an easy-to-replicate software simulation for extracting the muon mass from the data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- CCD-based image acquisition to replace photographic-film capture of spark tracks
- Computer-controlled data acquisition so the experiment can run unattended overnight
#### Constraints
- The 1964 prototype was too cumbersome and required too much specialized technical expertise for most educators to copy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in an instructional laboratory

### Learning Goals
- experimental methods of elementary particle physics
- determination of a fundamental particle property from first-hand data
