# Brachistochrone problem framing: among infinitely many paths between two fixed points, the minimum-time curve is a cycloid

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes the laboratory around the classical extremal problem in mechanics: of "infinite number of possible paths joining O to A", students seek the one taking minimum time. Energy conservation gives the speed–height relation (modified for a rolling ball to v = √(10/7)gy, which the authors note does not change the nature of the minimum time curve), and the minimizing curve is stated to be a cycloid with parametric form x = a(φ − sin φ), y = a(1 − cos φ). The experiment lets students verify this extremal result empirically rather than through variational calculus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fixed initial and final points O and A between which paths are compared
#### Constraints
- The full mathematical treatment is described as more apt for sophomore classes; the arclength integral is not evaluable without the exact curve equation except for special cases such as the straight line and cycloid

### Target Learners
- freshman introductory mechanics students
- sophomore physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding extremal problems in mechanics
- connecting Fermat's principle tradition to a rolling-ball experiment
- recognizing the cycloid curve and its tangency properties
