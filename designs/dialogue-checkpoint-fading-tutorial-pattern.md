# Guided inquiry tutorial pattern using hypothetical-student dialogues, checkpoints, and fading scaffolding across problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The tutorial's instructional design pattern combines guided inquiry sequences that build on each other with three recurring devices: dialogues between hypothetical students (Sarah, John, Emily) presenting common incorrect reasoning that learners evaluate, checkpoints after each section for reflection and error correction, and a gradual reduction of scaffolding from a scaffolded 2D problem to a less-scaffolded 3D problem. The authors report that "we introduced a dialogue between two hypothetical students discussing common incorrect choices and reasonings" to address observed difficulties, and that these refinements appeared effective in later interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Difficulties must first be identified via research (interviews, instructor discussions) to target the dialogues' content
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness evidence is qualitative interview observation rather than controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- recognizing applicability of Laplace's equation
- applying separation of variables
- writing and applying boundary conditions
- constructing general and unique solutions with the Fourier trick
