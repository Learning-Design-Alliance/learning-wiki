# MicroBooNE sterile-neutrino undergraduate teaching laboratory experiment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A third-year undergraduate laboratory project, "Study of muon-neutrino oscillations at the MicroBooNE experiment", in which student pairs work two days per week over an eight-week period with a reduced-scale version of real MicroBooNE data and simulation. Students derive neutrino oscillation theory, build Python event selections and optional machine-learning classifiers, apply oscillation weights and chi-squared hypothesis tests, and visualise results for demonstrators. The article notes "A significant advantage of this student project is its ease of implementation at low cost, requiring only a PC and the capacity to develop and run Python code."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reasonable level of Python knowledge, including installing and using packages such as pandas and numpy, plus troubleshooting ability
- 1 GB of storage space and a minimum of 8 GB of memory recommended for running the laboratory comfortably
- Support from laboratory demonstrators; at Manchester five pairs of students were supported by a rotating team of three demonstrators
#### Constraints
- The dataset provided to students is reduced in scale relative to the collaboration's data
- A full treatment of MicroBooNE's systematic uncertainties lies beyond the scope of the exercise; a fixed 15% relative uncertainty is applied as an approximation

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate physics students in mandatory laboratory courses (Bachelor's and Master's)

### Learning Goals
- understanding neutrino oscillation theory
- statistical hypothesis testing and confidence levels
- event selection and machine-learning classification
- data visualisation and research-style analysis practices
