# A subject-based differentiation approach for teaching mathematics to gifted students built on project-based learning, multiple intelligences, and enrichment.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops a differentiation approach for teaching mathematics to gifted middle-school students that modifies content, process, product, and learning environment relative to the national curriculum. Content is defined as "enriched objectives + theme", process incorporates "determining the students' multiple intelligence domains + teachers' strategies + basic skills + research skills + productive skills", and the learning environment combines "creative thinking + multiple-intelligences + different disciplines + project-based". The approach draws on the Williams, Maker, Kaplan, and autonomous learner models and Gardner's multiple intelligences, with particular emphasis on Maker matrix Type III and Type V problems. Students select project topics aligned with their dominant intelligence domains and complete all project stages including problem definition, method, and presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need knowledge of students' dominant intelligence domains (via the Multiple Intelligences Inventory) to assign suitable project topics and teaching strategies.
- Teachers must be informed about creativity, project preparation steps, and project evaluation processes before implementation.
#### Constraints
- The approach was developed and evaluated specifically for mathematics instruction with gifted middle-school students in grades 5–7 in Turkish schools.
- One teacher noted that conducting the same activities with all mathematics subjects could waste time, and that background and readiness levels of teachers, students, administrators, and parents could pose difficulties.

### Target Learners
- gifted middle school students (grades 5-7)
- non-gifted middle school students (grades 6-7)

### Learning Goals
- mathematics achievement (ratio-proportion, tables and graphics, conscious consumer arithmetic)
- academic and social skills
- creative thinking and research skills
