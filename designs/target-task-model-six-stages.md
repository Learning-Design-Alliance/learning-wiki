# The Target-Task Model: a six-stage guided-discovery problem-solving model for teaching science

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Target-Task Model is "an adaptation of the guided discovery method for teaching science", involving presentation of a major problem whose solution requires rules and principles students may not know. It proceeds through six stages drawn from Frost (2004): Pre-task, Task, Planning, Report, Analysis and Practice. In this study it was operationalized as the Target-Task Instructional Model, with students solving mathematical and non-mathematical problems in groups of two to four while the teacher monitors, then presenting written solutions for class critique and teacher-led analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher presents solutions similar to the target task and guides students; the teacher introduces the topic and states objectives before the task stage
#### Constraints
- The model was studied only in senior secondary physics, on current electricity concepts, over four teaching weeks

### Target Learners
- senior secondary school physics students

### Learning Goals
