# Inexpensive transparent-chambered β-type Stirling Engine laboratory apparatus with light gate, oscilloscope, and thermocouples

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The experiment uses an inexpensive β-type Stirling Engine (purchased for £30 each) mounted on a cup of boiling water, with thermocouple probes measuring the temperature difference across the chamber, a light gate detecting wheel rotation via a simple DC circuit, and an oscilloscope measuring rotational frequency. The measured engine parameters include a wheel mass of 6.52 ± 0.01 × 10⁻² kg, radius of 4.35 ± 0.01 × 10⁻² m, n = 3.45 ± 0.01 × 10⁻³ mol of gas, and compression ratio CR of 1.3 ± 0.3. "As the chamber is transparent, students can identify the position of the displacer piston together with the work piston as the engine moves through multiple cycles."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A cup of boiling water as the hot source, thermocouples taped to the top and bottom plates, a light gate powered by a breadboard DC circuit, and an oscilloscope or computer with an appropriate I/O device.
- Sometimes a slight push on the wheel is required to overcome its static inertia before the wheel begins to turn.
#### Constraints
- The article notes alternatives to equipment such as the oscilloscope and the digital thermometer could be employed without affecting the experimental outcome.
- Occasionally, after some use, the Stirling Engines would seize up and either not cycle or cycle slowly despite an obviously large temperature difference.

### Target Learners
- first year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- measuring mechanical efficiency of a heat engine
- using oscilloscopes and DC circuits
- data analysis
