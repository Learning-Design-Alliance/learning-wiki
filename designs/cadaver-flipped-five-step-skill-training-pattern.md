# Two-phase, five-step emergency skill training combining flipped pre-training preparation, cadaver-lab practice with formative assessment, and summative supervision-level assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The program follows a two-phase, five-step structure for procedural skill training: a cognitive phase of reading/visualization and viewing demonstrations, then a psychomotor phase of formative simulator assessment, summative assessment, and performance on patients. Fellows received "literature and video documents along with the departmental standard operating procedures (SOP)" individually before training, then practiced on human cadavers in groups of "a maximum of six fellows with two experienced attending consultants", with formative assessment during training and a summative post-training supervision-level assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-training individual preparation with literature, videos, and departmental SOPs
- Small learner-to-teacher ratio (maximum six fellows with two attending consultants)
- Cadaver laboratory setting for safe repetition of emergency skills
#### Constraints
- The article notes "try and error" for emergency skills should be avoided in direct patient care and performed in simulated training instead
- Retention of emergency skills is time-dependent, with repetition (e.g. every 6 months) likely required to sustain performance

### Target Learners
- ICU fellows in a tertiary care academic hospital

### Learning Goals
- Emergency procedural skills: chest tube insertion, pericardiocentesis, cricothyroidotomy
