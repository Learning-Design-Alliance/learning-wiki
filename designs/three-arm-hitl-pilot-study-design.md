# Three-arm pilot study design for HITL control training in an undergraduate CS laboratory course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a pilot study embedded in a normal undergraduate CS laboratory instructional setting using a standard IDE and an AI coding assistant. The "three-arm design" assigns course sections/lab groups to minimize contamination: Arm 1 is unstructured AI use, Arm 2 is HITL control training, and Arm 3 adds injected drift. All arms complete two instructional lab projects, a common evaluation lab (RQ1, no injected drift, AI access for all), and a traditional assessment (RQ2) with questions on the drift-targeted concepts. Data include planning artifacts, version-control histories, de-identified AI interaction logs, final code submissions, and aggregated assessment results.
