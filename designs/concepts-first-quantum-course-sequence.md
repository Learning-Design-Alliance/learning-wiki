# Concepts-first quantum course sequence: classical logic, then quantum superposition, then applications, ending in a discovery transition to linear algebra

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A semester-long course design (roughly 20 lectures) that defers formal mathematics. "The course begins with the introduction of the shapes as (classical) bits along with boxes representing classical gates", then introduces superposition via the mist representation and the Hadamard gate, measurement and probabilities, entanglement, quantum key distribution, algorithms (Deutsch, Grover), teleportation, and error correction. Only after most of the semester does it transition to linear algebra by omitting shapes and keeping coefficients, so that "Most students can actually find the matrices on their own, drawing from their experience and practice with the pictorial representation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lectures must include in-class practice problems with real-time instructor help; each lecture has associated homework
#### Constraints
- First part of the course restricts states to real, integer coefficients; the Hadamard gate is the only intrinsically quantum gate used throughout

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduates in a QISE minor
- quantum-aware non-majors

### Learning Goals
- quantum circuits and gates
- entanglement and teleportation
- quantum algorithms
- quantum error correction
- foundations for linear algebra
