# Enhanced supervision model: quarterly skills-based supervisory visits by trained district eye coordinators

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention model assigns trained District Eye Coordinators (DECs) responsibility for quarterly supervisory visits to each health facility, each visit focused on one of five primary eye care subjects. DECs were trained over two days with "short didactic sessions followed by interactive sessions to practice and demonstrate competence", used role play for supervisory challenges, and were "coached and expected to demonstrate how they would teach each of five subjects included in the PEC test". Study coordinators phoned DECs quarterly to verify visits occurred.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- DECs trained in enhanced supervisory methods with supervisory monitoring forms, a minimal incentive and transportation money
- quarterly phone contact by study coordinators to ensure supervisory visits were actually made
#### Constraints
- In Tanzania one supervisor left his post halfway through the study and was not replaced, so half of that country's intervention facilities missed the enhanced supervision they should have received

### Target Learners
- primary health care workers at government primary health care facilities in Kenya, Malawi and Tanzania

### Learning Goals
- visual acuity measurement, recognition and management of conjunctivitis, cataract, presbyopia, and severe eye trauma
