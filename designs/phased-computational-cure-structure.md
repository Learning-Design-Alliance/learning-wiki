# Phased structure of a computational CURE from lecture preparation to final analysis presentation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article lays out five phases of a computational materials science CURE: "Lecture(s)", "Discussion section exercises", "Homework assignments", "Submission of raw data", and "Final presentation/paper". Each phase has stated goals, from introducing the research concept through tool familiarization, practice with instructor feedback as a checkpoint, collection of the class dataset, and analysis of individual and class data. The author focuses on use "as a final project, which can be the most impactful to deploy within an existing course".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A detailed lab manual with instructions, ideally "tested with undergraduates before use in class to identify and improve on points of confusion or technical problems"
- Early stages of the project carried out in homework "to ensure that the instructor can point out and correct any potentially serious problems in students' calculations at an early stage"
#### Constraints
- A CURE can be "focused on a single lab session or assignment, take the form of a culminating final project, or even constitute the entirety of a course"
- In some cases teaching-assistant training may also be needed

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level to graduate students in physics and materials science courses

### Learning Goals
- independent research skills
- application of course concepts to novel materials
