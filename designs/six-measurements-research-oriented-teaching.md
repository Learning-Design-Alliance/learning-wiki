# Six main measurements of research-oriented teaching: integrated teaching/case analysis, open resources, interactive mode, course paper, solutions beyond normal, and content research/update

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates its own set of design characteristics for research-oriented teaching: "a. Integrated teaching and case analysis; b. Open resources; c. Interactive mode; d. Course paper; e. Solutions beyond normal; f. Research and update of contents." Integrated teaching composes cases from instructor experience to express live ideas students may retain beyond textbook knowledge. Open resources means openness to references, the internet, and open problems. The remaining measures cover interactive classroom formats, an academic-standard course-paper program, unusual high-quality problem solutions, and research-driven renewal of course content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor research background and experience for composing cases and updating content
- Campus network and reference resources for the open-resources component
#### Constraints
- Case composition "depends on instructor's background and experience"

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students, including large-enrollment introductory classes

### Learning Goals
- Research consciousness, creativity, scientific literacy, and deep understanding of physics
