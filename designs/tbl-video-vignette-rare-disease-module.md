# TBL module on clinical reasoning for rare diseases with scripted video-vignette illness scripts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A team-based learning module for fifth-year medical students built around clinical situations with "hypoproteinemia and asthenia" as clinical starting points, composed of an introduction session, distance preparation on Moodle, and a face-to-face session with individual and team readiness assurance tests and collective action. Illness scripts were presented successively as the teams progressed; the intervention group received "3 video vignettes, each lasting between 5 and 7 min" made with standardized patients and patient testimonials, while the comparator received the same scripts in written format with identical review time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams of 7 to 9 students with one medical instructor per 67 to 98 students, parallel amphitheater sessions, standardized teaching material, and a Moodle platform for submitting team assignments and receiving instructor feedback
#### Constraints
- The video vignettes required elaborated pedagogical material produced with standardized patients; the article notes integrating clinical reasoning teaching modules represents an increased demand on faculty staff, which TBL reduces relative to other formats

### Target Learners
- fifth-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning in the context of rare diseases
- history taking, diagnostic strategy, data synthesis, physical examination, patient information and management
- transversal competencies including listening and questioning skills
