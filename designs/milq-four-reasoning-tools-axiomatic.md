# The four milq reasoning tools as a qualitative mini-axiomatic system for quantum physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The milq approach formulates four qualitative rules — statistical behavior, interference of single quantum objects, unique measurement results, and complementarity — said to contain the basic traits of quantum physics. The article states they "can serve as a support in qualitative discussions about quantum phenomena" and "enable students to predict certain kinds of quantum behavior and help to avoid learning difficulties", forming a kind of "qualitative mini-axiomatic" for quantum physics at high-school level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Introduced by means of key experiments (double slit with single quantum objects; Grangier-Roger-Aspect) in which the rules become particularly evident, so students can test their application in real-life situations
#### Constraints
- The rules lack anything that can only be expressed within mathematical formalism
- Entanglement was not included when the tools were formulated; the article suggests it should possibly be added from today's perspective

### Target Learners
- High-school students (German Gymnasium, grade 13)

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of quantum physics: probabilistic behavior, interference and superposition, measurement, complementarity
