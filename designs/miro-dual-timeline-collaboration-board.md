# MIRO interactive dual-timeline board for collaborative historical sense-making

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course used MIRO as an interactive class collaboration space built around two linked timelines: the instructors' timeline of historical events and a student "response" or "meta" timeline onto which students posted their reflection assignments, with the stated goal "to explore the connection between these two timelines". Students added content they felt brings the history to life, including milestones on an expanded timeline and responses to lectures on sociological issues such as radon contamination. Survey results rated MIRO the least-favored tool, and the authors state its integration will require additional effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students add content to MIRO when not asked for a written response page, so the board depends on ongoing student contribution
#### Constraints
- Students rated MIRO 5.78/10 for effectiveness and only 22% would keep it in person; one student called it too clunky with too much information for a nice-looking timeline

### Target Learners
- graduate and undergraduate nuclear engineering students in a remote course

### Learning Goals
- creatively processing the past in the present for a better future
- connecting lecture content to student interpretations of history
