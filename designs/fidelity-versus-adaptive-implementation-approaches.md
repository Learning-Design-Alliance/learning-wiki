# Fidelity versus adaptive implementation approaches to scaling up educational programs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article contrasts two program-management frameworks for scale-up. Under the fidelity approach, "the teacher's job is to achieve the program ideal envisioned by the program developers," supported by top-down management. Under the adaptive approach, teachers diagnose problems, devise and test solutions, and adapt programs to context, which the authors link to a "context-focused approach to scale-up" requiring teacher control and collaborative work structures. The study tests these approaches empirically as Traditional versus Adaptive READS.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Under the adaptive approach, teachers must be able to recognize what is working and what is not, and collaborate in work structures; the fidelity approach requires adherence to researcher-designed procedures
#### Constraints
- The authors note that program effects often vary by context, so approaches showing promise in efficacy trials may fail to replicate elsewhere

### Target Learners
- elementary school teachers implementing a literacy intervention

### Learning Goals
- teacher learning about literacy instruction
- incorporation of intervention practices into regular classroom teaching
