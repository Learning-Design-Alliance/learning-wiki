# Structure surgical training around a 100-case volume benchmark with mentorship and simulation-based training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that training programs for radical prostatectomy use structured mentorship, case-volume progression, and simulation. It states that "structured mentorship programs and simulation-based training may help young surgeons overcome early technical challenges" and that reaching a threshold of 100 cases may be necessary to consistently attain optimal functional and perioperative results. This follows from its finding that functional and perioperative outcomes, though not biochemical recurrence, improve beyond the initial learning curve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured approach to training coupled with appropriate mentorship and case volume progression
- Supervised learning environments and standardized mentorship programs
#### Constraints
- The 100-case cutoff is a reference threshold; the authors note further investigation is warranted on whether alternative thresholds (e.g., 50 or 200 cases) offer different predictive validity
- All supporting evidence is observational and non-randomized

### Target Learners
- Surgical trainees and young urologists in the initial learning curve for radical prostatectomy

### Learning Goals
- Technical proficiency in robot-assisted and laparoscopic radical prostatectomy
- Consistently attaining optimal functional and perioperative outcomes
