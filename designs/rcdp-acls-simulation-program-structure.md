# RCDP ACLS simulation program structure: standardized three-stage prebriefing, first simulation, video-based formative feedback with rerun, and structured debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's program combines Ludlow's preparation, orientation, and pre-briefing stages with an RCDP cycle: a first simulation, viewing of a best-practice video with instructor formative feedback, a rerun of the same scenario, and a gather-analysis-summarize debriefing. The authors state that "RCDP frequently involves restarting simulations, each time increasing their complexity" and that feedback is "integrated during the exercises for on-the-spot learning and corrections". The program ran over three weeks with teams of 3–4 members.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams of 3–4 members with a high-fidelity simulator (SimMan 3G) and an instructor experienced in simulation education
#### Constraints
- Applying RCDP to complex scenarios can be challenging and may require more time and resources compared to traditional training methods due to its repetitive practice and immediate feedback structure

### Target Learners
- Senior nursing students

### Learning Goals
- ACLS knowledge, self-efficacy, performance skills, and team communication in cardiac arrest resuscitation
