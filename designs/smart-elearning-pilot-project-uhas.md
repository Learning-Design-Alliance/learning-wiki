# SMART e-Learning pilot project: a Moodle-based platform with offline WLAN content delivery for pre-service health trainees

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The SMART e-Learning pilot was inaugurated on 24 May 2018 at the University of Health and Allied Sciences, Ghana, enrolling 675 students, faculty and ICT personnel on Moodle with over 15 undergraduate and postgraduate courses mounted. "The SMART e-Learning pilot project entailed provision of learning content (e-books, links to database articles, distribution of course updates); communication from lecturers to students (sharing lecture materials/resources, academic announcements); online/offline assessments (quizzes, assignments, case scenarios, case studies which are all timed & secure, self-test, scoring component), and creation of discussion for a (students can communicate with lecturers & peers, and interact as a class or in groups) for students and teaching faculty." It delivered materials free of charge through students' existing mobile devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- ICT infrastructure set-up and capacity building for teaching and ICT staff, as in the project's five implementation phases
#### Constraints
- End of semester examinations were not written via the Moodle platform because the university was yet to adopt an e-Learning policy for university-wide examinations

### Target Learners
- pre-service healthcare trainees (nursing, midwifery, public health nursing, radiology) in a public university

### Learning Goals
- access to learning materials, interim assessments, assignment submission, and lecturer-student communication
