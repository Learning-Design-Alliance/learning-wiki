# A shared-vision change model in which faculty discussions of core ideas and practices drive changes to assessments and instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a model of departmental change in which faculty discussions about disciplinary core ideas and scientific practices are the engine of curricular transformation. As the authors state, "Our model for change uses faculty discussions about core ideas and scientiﬁc practices to drive changes to assessments and instruction." The model has three principal anchors: articulating what should be taught, developing aligned assessments, and changing pedagogy to align with the goals. The anchors are interdependent rather than strictly linear.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator (at MSU a PER postdoctoral research associate) to run the faculty discussions
- Faculty participation in developing performance expectations
#### Constraints
- The last two elements of the four-pronged approach (teaching recognition and faculty networks) are facilitated at the college level and outside the paper's scope
- The linear progression of the model is not completely accurate, as each anchor influences the others

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in introductory physics courses

### Learning Goals
- Alignment of introductory course goals, assessments, and instruction with disciplinary practices and core ideas
