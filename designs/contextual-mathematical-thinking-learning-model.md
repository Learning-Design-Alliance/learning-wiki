# Contextual mathematical thinking learning model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A learning model the authors develop by combining constructivism, problem-based learning, contextual teaching and learning, and higher-order thinking strategies. Its six-phase syntax runs from presenting contextual problems through critical/analytical questioning, individual and group investigation, presentation and discussion, and reflection, to a higher-order thinking test. The article states it is "supported by generally standard theories" and shows internal consistency among its components: purposes, syntax, reaction principle and support system.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation follows the six syntax phases with the allocated times (about 10, 10, 30, 20, 5 and 15 minutes)
- Teacher acts as facilitator providing scaffolding to raise students' thinking to a higher-order level
#### Constraints
- Results come from one Grade VIII sample of 78 students on cone, cylinder and sphere material; the authors state results "should be validated by a larger sample size"

### Target Learners
- Grade VIII junior high school students

### Learning Goals
- higher-order thinking ability (analyzing, evaluating, creating) in mathematics
