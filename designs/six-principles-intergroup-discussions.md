# Six principles of intergroup discussions guiding workshop design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines intergroup discussions as discussions satisfying two criteria: the topic concerns one or more dimensions of marginalization, and participants include people who both do and do not belong to the corresponding marginalized group. The authors state that "our designs incorporated the following six principles of intergroup discussions": discussions can be unsafe for marginalized-group members; allies develop through repeated exposure to counterideologies; scholarly work and lived experiences are equally important; discussions must focus on intersections of identities; empathy is critical; and resources must be tailored to the organization's needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitation approach that prioritizes the safety of students from marginalized groups
#### Constraints
- The article notes participation in intergroup discussions can carry emotional, professional, and even physical risks for people from the marginalized group

### Target Learners
- Heterogeneous groups of students and postdocs discussing marginalization

### Learning Goals
- Learning about racism, sexism, and other dimensions of marginalization
- Developing models of allyship
