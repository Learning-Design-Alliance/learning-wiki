# Structure graduate simulation learning as double simulation sessions separated by supervised skill reinforcement and spaced about 15 days apart

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that a theoretically based, 2 h teaching model characterized by a double simulation session with briefing, simulation and debriefing, separated by a supervised skill reinforcement stage, with ideally two participants, and repeated 15 days later benefits graduate intensive care nursing students' learning outcomes. The recommendation rests on the measured performance, self-efficacy and self-confidence gains and on the absence of performance loss across the 15-day interval.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty with deep clinical experience to guide debriefing and skill reinforcement
- A high-fidelity simulator and laboratory setting with video-assisted debriefing
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a small, explorative, single-group study without a control group; the ideal spacing interval remains an open question the authors note in the literature

### Target Learners
- Graduate nurses in intensive care specialization programs

### Learning Goals
- Advanced clinical competence in managing life-threatening respiratory conditions
