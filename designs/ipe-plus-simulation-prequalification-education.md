# Interprofessional education combined with simulation as a pedagogical approach for pre-qualification health professional students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames interprofessional education — defined as occurring "when two or more professions learn with, from and about each other to improve collaboration and the quality of care" — combined with simulation based on real-world situations as a teaching strategy supporting interdisciplinary learning. It argues both tools had predominantly been used in postgraduate training, and that integrating them into pre-qualification education lets nursing and medical students interact early, focusing on communication and teamwork skills within a safe environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation scenarios incorporating aspects and problems important across the spectrum of professions
- Supervisors to aid students at a basic level and facilitate advancement through successive stages of skills acquisition
#### Constraints
- Dispute over when to introduce IPE; conventional wisdom held it was best offered only after licensure, though the authors cite historical evidence for introducing it from the start of professional education

### Target Learners
- Pre-qualification health professional students

### Learning Goals
- Collaborative patient care
- Teamwork and communication competencies
- Positive interprofessional attitudes
