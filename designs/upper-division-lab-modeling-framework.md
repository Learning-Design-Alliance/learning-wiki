# A modeling framework for upper-division labs that treats measurement tools and the physical system as two parallel modeling targets

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The framework divides the full experimental apparatus into two parts, each subjected to a modeling cycle: construction from principles, parameters, and assumptions; use of the physical system model for predictions and the measurement model for interpretation of data; comparison; and four revision pathways (refine either apparatus or either model). The article states it "divides the full experimental apparatus into the physical system (right side of Fig. 2) and the measurement tools (left side of Fig. 2)". It serves both as a descriptive tool for student reasoning and as a curriculum design tool.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Upper-division lab activities with sophisticated measurement equipment whose principles of operation and performance limitations can be modeled
- Students must have access to principles, parameters, and limitations (e.g., manufacturers' data sheets) for both system and measurement models
#### Constraints
- Developed for upper-division physics labs; the authors say earlier frameworks' domain of applicability excluded upper-division labs, and the new framework is motivated by that setting

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- model construction and use in experimental work
- understanding principles and limitations of measurement tools
- connecting predictions with interpreted measurements
