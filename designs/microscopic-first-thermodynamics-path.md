# A microscopic-first instructional path for teaching thermodynamics in high school

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an educational path for high-school thermodynamics that assigns to the microscopic description of physical systems a constantly privileged role. Work, heat and thermodynamic processes are each explained both macroscopically and microscopically, the latter requiring only the laws of motion, general principles of mechanics and simple probabilistic arguments. The authors argue this yields a simple yet deep understanding of thermodynamic laws while emphasizing their generality, from gases to black holes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must already command the laws of motion and the general principles of mechanics, since the microscopic explanations rely on applying these plus simple probabilistic arguments.
#### Constraints
- The microscopic description definitively requires more details than the macroscopic treatment, as the article states.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- understanding the laws of thermodynamics
- distinguishing heat, work and energy
- understanding entropy and the second law
