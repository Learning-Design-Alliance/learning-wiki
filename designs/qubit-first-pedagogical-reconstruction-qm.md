# The two-state (qubit-first) approach as the framework for a pedagogical reconstruction of QM at high-school level

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that the qubit-first approach provides the appropriate framework for a pedagogical reconstruction of Quantum Mechanics at high-school level, bypassing the difficulties of the historical approach. It cites research indicating that such approaches "may introduce QM concepts in an intuitive and accessible manner, avoiding complex mathematical formalisms" while illustrating superposition, measurement and entanglement. Three reasons are given: finite-dimensional systems require only linear algebra, qubits are the most elementary quantum systems exhibiting all key elements, and the approach connects with contemporary quantum information research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Only linear algebra on finite complex vector spaces and basic probability theory are needed, so students need a high-school mathematical background
#### Constraints
- The approach restricts to finite-dimensional systems, justified by the authors' claim of no loss of generality for practical purposes

### Target Learners
- high-school students

### Learning Goals
- understanding key concepts of Quantum Mechanics: superposition, measurement, entanglement, unitary evolution
