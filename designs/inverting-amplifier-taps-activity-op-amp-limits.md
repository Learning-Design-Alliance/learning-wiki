# Inverting amplifier TAPS interview activity on op-amp functional limits

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A hands-on think-aloud problem-solving activity built around "an inverting ampliﬁer circuit (LF356 op-amp) operating outside its recommended voltage and frequency range at a gain of 10." The circuit was preset so the output signal was clipped by the power rails and slightly distorted by the slew rate limit, while remaining recognizable. Students received a circuit diagram, the op-amp data sheet, and the gain equation, and were asked to make the circuit function as predicted, with an optional harder second activity at gain 100. The design deliberately allowed multiple pathways to resolving the issue.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The activity was designed such that there were multiple pathways to resolving the issue, to elicit a large range of modeling behavior.
#### Constraints
- The activity targeted measurement/test apparatus errors (slew rate, bandwidth, clipping) rather than circuit construction errors, to explore a different error space than prior troubleshooting work.

### Target Learners
- junior and senior physics and engineering physics majors who had taken the electronics lab course

### Learning Goals
- reasoning about the functional limits of operational amplifiers
- model and apparatus revision to reconcile discrepancies
