# Electromagnetic damping experimental problem: disc-and-magnet apparatus for determining the ratio of magnetic pole strengths

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A sample experimental problem in which an aluminum disc on a horizontal axle with a wound cord and hanging slotted mass reaches constant angular velocity in the presence of a pair of cylindrical magnets, where the weight's torque is balanced by an electromagnetic damping torque. Two pairs of identical magnets with different pole strengths B1 and B2 and a velocity measurement unit with a detector are provided. Students observe the motion with and without the magnetic field, study terminal velocity versus attached mass for each magnet pair, and "determine the ratio of the magnetic pole strengths of the two pairs of magnets and estimate the frictional torque due to the frictional force at the supports".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two pairs of identical magnets of the same size and shape but different pole strengths
- A velocity measurement unit with a detector for the falling mass
- Apparatus and instruction sheet provided; students assemble the arrangement themselves
#### Constraints
- The frictional torque at the supports is relatively small but present; terminal velocity depends on disc geometry, material conductivity and permeability, mass, magnet pole strength, magnet position and spacing, and frictional torque

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students at the introductory university level

### Learning Goals
- studying variation of terminal velocity with mass
- determining ratios of magnetic pole strengths
- estimating frictional torque
