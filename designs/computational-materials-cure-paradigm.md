# A paradigm for computational materials science CUREs in which students collectively build and analyze a novel class dataset

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article names and describes a paradigm for Course-based Undergraduate Research Experiences in computational materials science. Its defining aspects are that "students learn and use research methods, give input into the project, generate new research data, and analyze it to draw conclusions that are not known beforehand". Students use web-based simulation tools to calculate structures assigned from an instructor-selected materials set, contributing "to a novel class dataset which they analyze", and additionally calculate an individual property of their choice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor must choose a research question answerable by student calculations, since "Generally it is not feasible for the students to select the research question and materials, because deep expert knowledge is required to design an authentically novel research study"
- A simulation tool rich enough to support an exploratory phase beyond the minimum required calculations
#### Constraints
- CUREs have been "rare or absent in materials science and engineering, as well as condensed-matter physics", so the paradigm targets that gap
- Additional individual-property calculations "typically it would require more student knowledge than is attainable in the context of the course to make these additional property calculations constitute reliable research data"

### Target Learners
- undergraduates from sophomore-level to graduate courses in physics and materials science

### Learning Goals
- research methods and computational simulation skills
- course concepts in condensed matter physics and materials science
- sense of belonging in the STEM community and scientific thinking
