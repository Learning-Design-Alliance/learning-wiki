# Blinded quasi-experimental AI-versus-student assessment marking protocol

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A pilot evaluation protocol in which ChatGPT-generated submissions, formatted by an independent research assistant to resemble student work, were mixed with deidentified past student assessments and marked by blinded discipline-expert academics against standardized rubrics. As printed, "33 assessments, across the 5 assessment types, were selected for the study: 18 were AI-generated (experimental group), and 15 were past students’ assessments (control group)". A second-marker consensus process applied when a remark differed by more than 10 marks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Blinded markers with postgraduate qualifications and DH or HIM expertise; standardized rubrics; deidentification of student work
#### Constraints
- Quasi-experimental design without random assignment; limited samples per category; markers' informal AI-authorship judgments were sometimes inaccurate

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and master's-level digital health and health information management students

### Learning Goals
- evaluating assessment validity under generative AI
- academic integrity benchmarking
