# NEXUS/Physics course design: repurposing algebra-based physics around topics shared with the life-science curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A curriculum pattern in which an introductory physics course for life-science majors is designed as a second-year class with prerequisites of two terms of introductory biology, one term of general chemistry, and two terms of calculus. The designers "included topics that we viewed as critical for life-science students where a physics approach could help: chemical energy and bonding, random motion including diffusion, entropy and Gibbs Free Energy, motion of an [object] in fluids, and electrostatics in fluids", while reducing or eliminating traditional topics of less general life-science relevance such as projectile motion, circular motion, gravitation, magnetism, and special relativity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prerequisites of introductory biology, general chemistry, and calculus so students can leverage disciplinary knowledge
- Negotiation of content with biology faculty and biophysicists
#### Constraints
- Designed for the common needs of the broad population of life-science students rather than professional-level applications, which the authors found too broad to guide a one-year course

### Target Learners
- second-year life-science (biology and pre-medical) undergraduates

### Learning Goals
- building understanding of physical mechanism and coherence
- valuing mathematical reasoning in qualitative and calculational situations
