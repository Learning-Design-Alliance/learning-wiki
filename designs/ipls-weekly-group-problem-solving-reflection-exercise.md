# Weekly lab group problem-solving exercise with context-rich problems and written self-diagnosis reflections

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A recurring component of the first hour of each lab section in a first-semester algebra-based IPLS course: lab groups of two or three students cooperatively solve a context-rich problem while the instructor and a learning assistant tutor verbally, then students submit written reflections on which part of the problem-solving process they struggled with, using a rubric adapted from Yerushalmi et al. (2012). As the article reports, "Beginning each laboratory period, a context -rich problem w as introduced to lab groups of two or three students each to work on cooperatively." End-of-semester feedback indicated 37 of 39 students found the exercise useful.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Context-rich problems
- a reflection rubric adapted from Yerushalmi et al. (2012)
- instructor and learning assistant support
- groups of two or three students
#### Constraints
- The exercise was omitted on exam weeks and studied in one course at one medium-size state university

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students, primarily biological, health, and behavioral science majors

### Learning Goals
- physics problem-solving framework development
- self-diagnosis of problem-solving struggles
