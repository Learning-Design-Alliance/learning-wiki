# Facilitating relational reflection: a three-phase debriefing model keeping interprofessional team learning focused on daily clinical practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies a debriefing model it calls "a model of facilitating relational reflection adopted seems to provide ways of keeping the collaboration and learning in the interprofessional team clearly focused". In phase one each team member is invited in sequence to describe actions and interactions, building a collective narrative; in phase two the instructor acknowledges what each member did well, building confidence; in phase three members spontaneously state insights for everyday practice. Instructors use observation notes and probing questions, and reflection is repeatedly related to clinical practice at the delivery unit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video-recorded simulation exercises with instructors taking observation notes during the scenario to anchor the debriefing recapitulation
- Certified, experienced instructors who facilitate individual reflection while relating each performance to the whole team's work
#### Constraints
- The article states that neither imposed structure nor lack of structure in debriefing alone ensured interprofessional collaboration emerged as a reflection topic; the relational focus is what the authors propose made the difference

### Target Learners
- Interprofessional maternity care teams (obstetricians, midwives, nurse assistants)

### Learning Goals
- Emergency teamwork and communication in acute obstetric care
- Transfer of simulated scenario learning to daily clinical practice
