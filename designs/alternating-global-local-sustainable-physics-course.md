# Alternating global climate-change and local building-design topics to teach sustainable physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course alternates two threads: a global perspective on climate change and a specialized one on sustainable building construction, so that the two show shared physics concepts and complementary viewpoints. The authors state that "Sustainable physics concepts are progressively introduced through both a global and a specialized perspective: climate change and building design." Sessions alternate between instructors and topics, and each two-hour unit combines a lecture with problem-solving tutorials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors for the two threads coordinate so that sessions call on each other to create a coherent syllabus
#### Constraints
- The course was offered as an elective to third-year physics students at Paris-Saclay University, typically enrolling around 15 students

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding climate change physics
- understanding sustainable building design
- connecting physics concepts to real-world systems
