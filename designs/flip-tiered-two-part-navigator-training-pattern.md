# Tiered two-part navigator training with continuing education: resource navigators complete a didactic community-engaged curriculum, then advance to patient navigators through case-based role-play

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The FLiP curriculum uses a tiered experiential learning structure in which students first serve as resource navigators, maintaining a community resource guide, and then advance to patient navigators who contact families directly. Part I is a live, interactive virtual didactic webinar covering "cultural humility and trauma-informed care" and community resource access; part II is a 3-hour case-based session with facilitated role-playing, data-collection and texting platform training, and HIPAA expectations. Quarterly 1.5-hour continuing education meetings with guest speakers sustain skill development. This tiering reflects experiential learning theory, cycling students through navigation experiences with ongoing education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must complete both training parts before serving as patient navigators; direct family contact requires a background check and Volunteer Services approval
- A dynamic, web-based community resource guide must be maintained and regularly updated by resource navigators
#### Constraints
- Sessions are held Saturday mornings to accommodate scheduling variabilities across institutions

### Target Learners
- medical students across all four training years

### Learning Goals
- SDoH and food insecurity knowledge
- trauma-informed communication skills
- resource navigation for patient-centered care planning
