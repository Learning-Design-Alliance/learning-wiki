# Research topic–driven learning model for graduate immunology grounded in constructive alignment, inquiry-based and active learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes and implements a "research topic–driven learning" model in which graduate students select research topics aligned with immunological frontiers and, under faculty mentorship, construct their knowledge base around those topics. The design is explicitly grounded in educational theory: "Constructive alignment theory provided the conceptual foundation" and inquiry-based learning informed the course structure as students formulated questions, analyzed evidence and synthesized conclusions from authentic research topics. The model operationalizes the research–teaching nexus in a resource-constrained setting and is presented as a transferable framework for other foundational medical disciplines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty mentorship with one-on-one guidance for topic selection, literature selection and iterative feedback
- Orientation training in literature searching, data extraction and reference management before the course starts
#### Constraints
- Faculty workload increased compared with lecture-based formats, and the article states sustainable implementation will require institutional support such as workload recognition and training in educational methods

### Target Learners
- Graduate (master's and PhD) students in basic medical sciences, specifically immunology

### Learning Goals
- Research competence and innovative thinking
- Mastery of core immunological theory integrated with frontier developments
- Academic communication and self-regulated learning
