# Einstein-First lesson structure combining visual presentation, model-based activities and worksheet work time

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Einstein-First programs used a repeating 45-minute lesson structure divided into three roughly equal components: a PowerPoint presentation explaining Einsteinian physics concepts visually with pictures, video clips, animations and key words; hands-on activities based on the models and analogies described in Parts 1 and 2; and work time. The article states "the last 15-minutes was used to complete worksheets and allow class discussion". The same structure was used across Year 6 to Year 11 programs with presentation style and language adapted to the age group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Models and analogies from Parts 1 and 2 of the series are available for the activity component
- Presentation style and language must be appropriate to the age group
#### Constraints
- Results reviewed are from short programs with post-questionnaires taken soon after completion, so long-term effects are not established

### Target Learners
- school students aged 11 to 16 (Years 6, 9, 10 and 11)

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Einsteinian physics (light, space, time, gravity)
- positive attitudes to physics
