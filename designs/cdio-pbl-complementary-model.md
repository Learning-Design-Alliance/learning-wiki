# CDIO-PBL as complementary coupled educational strategies: CDIO contributes outcomes (goals) and PBL contributes processes (means)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents CDIO-PBL as a combined teaching model in which the conceive-design-implement-operate framework, an engineering education model grounded in a "learning-by-doing" approach, is coupled with problem-based learning. The authors state that "the CDIO teaching model benefits from outcomes (goals) and the PBL teaching method benefits from processes (means)" and that the two are "overlapping and complementary in many aspects." In this study CDIO structured the hands-on skills cycle (conceive scenarios, design curriculum, implement via simulation, operate through end-of-cycle assessment) while PBL structured theoretical learning around real patient cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors who can conceive clinical scenarios, design case-based curricula, run simulation-based implementation, and assess and refine each 2-week cycle
- Requires access to real patient cases and simulation training models
#### Constraints
- The article notes no studies had previously reported this combined model in obstetrical education; evidence here is a single-institution pilot

### Target Learners
- 8-year-program medical students rotating through obstetrics

### Learning Goals
- Clinical theoretical knowledge in obstetrics
- Obstetric procedural skills
