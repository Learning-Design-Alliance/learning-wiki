# Flexible hypertext-based online course design with hierarchical navigation and multiple representations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern describes the flexible online course design the article built on cognitive flexibility hypertext theory: an e-learning process using video, PDF, images, simulations and discussion forums, organized by hierarchical rather than linear navigation. Its printed characteristics include that "The e-learning process contains multiple representations of knowledge through image, video, PDF, simulation, and Flash animation" and that abstract concepts are interconnected to show knowledge in use, with the main module divided into sub-modules and students guided during the process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hypertext and hypermedia learning resources realizing the links between concepts, cases, projects and examples
- Division of the main module into several sub-modules and guiding of online students during the e-learning process
#### Constraints
- The article states this design was experimented only with the Adobe Flash course and must be experimented with other complex and ill-structured domains of knowledge

### Target Learners
- university-level information technology diploma students in e-learning environments

### Target Goals
- acquisition of complex, ill-structured multimedia development knowledge with flexible navigation of learning resources
