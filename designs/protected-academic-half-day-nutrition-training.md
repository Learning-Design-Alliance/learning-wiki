# Deliver formal online nutrition training to residents using protected time already scheduled in mandatory academic half days

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This study embedded a 4-hour online, self-paced course, Nutrition Science for Health and Longevity, into existing mandatory academic half day time rather than adding new curricular demands: 'Four hours during the residents’ academic half day was provided in February 2024 to complete this course'. One hour at each of two sessions 6 weeks apart was protected for the pre- and post-course questionnaires. Because attendance at academic half day is mandatory, this leverages existing scheduled education to deliver formal nutrition training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an approved online nutrition course for clinicians (the Gaples Institute course used here), and coordination with the local academic half day coordinator to slot it into existing mandatory sessions
#### Constraints
- Uptake was low in this study, with only six residents completing the post-course questionnaire (response rate 24%), which the authors say introduces potential non-response bias

### Target Learners
- family medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- nutrition knowledge
- nutrition counselling confidence and attitudes
- dietary counselling practices with patients
