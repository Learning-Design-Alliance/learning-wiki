# High-fidelity simulation session pattern: scenario simulation followed by structured 40-minute debriefing with rotating active-player roles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes each simulation session around a case presentation, a 15–20 minute simulated emergency with a high-fidelity mannequin, and a debriefing in which "The students' performances were discussed with teachers, pointing out adequate and inadequate actions and procedures". Five to ten students participate per scenario, with two as active players and the rest as observers, and "The students' roles changed with each scenario so that all students had the opportunity to be active players or observers". Three teachers run each session: two role-play parents and health team members, one commands the simulator responses. Each student completed seven one-hour topic sessions (7 h total).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-fidelity full-body computerized patient simulator (PediaSIM in this study) programmed to provide realistic physiological responses
- Multiple teachers to role-play parents and team members and to operate the simulator
- A debriefing period with teachers discussing adequate and inadequate actions
#### Constraints
- The study used five to ten students per scenario with only two active players at a time, others observing

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical internship students

### Learning Goals
- Pediatric emergency management: clinical reasoning, communication, attitude, leadership
