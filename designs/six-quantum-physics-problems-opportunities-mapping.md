# Mapping six problems of teaching Quantum Physics to six corresponding opportunities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on seventeen years of classroom experience, the author identifies "six macrocritical aspects that make learning Quantum Physics difficult for adolescents" and pairs each with an opportunity in Table 4: discouraging conceptual intricacy becomes appealing epistemological richness, difficult ontological indeterminacy becomes interdisciplinary study of chance, sci-fi terminology abuse becomes curiosity about basic notions, worrying formalism becomes compact QP formulation, non-linear development becomes unifying explanations, and the Copenhageners' low popularity becomes captivating narration of the QP story.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The mapping presupposes a teacher able to diagnose which critical aspects a given class faces; the author addressed the critical aspects always in the maximum number of three per year.
#### Constraints
- Combinations other than the 2007-2008 and 2008-2009 triplets produced "overall lower marks" in the author's later classes.

### Target Learners
- adolescent students (ages 14-19) in scientific high schools

### Learning Goals
- learning motivation for quantum physics
- understanding of basic quantum physics concepts
