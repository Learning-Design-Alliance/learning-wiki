# In situ cross-over simulation procedure with standardized patient and facilitator-led debriefing for rural team training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study's training procedure (Fig. 1) is an eight-step sequence conducted in participants' everyday emergency departments: briefing and demographics, a facilitator lecture on patient safety and teamwork based on Crew Resource Management and Advanced Trauma Life Support, orientation, randomized scenario order, two approximately 20-minute scripted scenarios (urosepsis co-located; myocardial infarction distributed with telemedicine), each followed by a facilitator-led debriefing focused on medical treatment and teamwork skills. A standardized patient (an experienced nurse actor) and authentic equipment supported realism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator present in the emergency department to supply clinical assessment information the standardized patient could not display, plus a facilitator-controlled vital sign monitor
#### Constraints
- Diagnoses were not randomized across both settings to keep the sample size manageable; only one standardized patient was used, and intraportrayal and interportrayal reliability were not measured

### Target Learners
- rural cottage hospital interprofessional staff

### Learning Goals
- emergency teamwork
- telemedicine-mediated collaboration
- stabilization of deteriorating patients
