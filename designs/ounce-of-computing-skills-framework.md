# Ounce of computing skills: embedding discipline-relevant computing into individual discipline courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that courses in discipline X include the computing relevant to that course's learning outcomes, as used by practitioners in X. It refers to these as an "ounce of computing skills," to highlight the amount of computing to be integrated and to contrast with teaspoon programming languages. The framework holds that the goal has to be to advance students in their disciplines, that only the disciplinary experts can tell us how computing is used in that discipline, and that the disciplinary values are paramount over CS values.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Discipline instructors and computing experts must act as co-designers, with discipline students serving as design informants
#### Constraints
- Restricting computing to CS+X courses alone is contrary to the pervasiveness of computing and will likely result in computing not being a learning goal for most courses in discipline X

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in non-computing disciplines

### Learning Goals
- computing skills relevant to and usable within the student's own discipline
