# Gravitational light deflection: competing Newtonian and Einsteinian predictions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The framework underlying the article's classroom activity: light passing near the Sun's limb is bent by gravity, and two competing theories give different predicted displacements. In 1911 Einstein calculated a deflection of 0.87 arcsec; in 1915, after modifying the Newtonian law of gravitation via the general Principle of Equivalence, "the correct light displacement value was not the same as the Newtonian result, but twice as large (1 .75 arcsec)". The historical expeditions' measurements are evaluated against these two values, and radio interferometry later provided a more accurate measurement method.
