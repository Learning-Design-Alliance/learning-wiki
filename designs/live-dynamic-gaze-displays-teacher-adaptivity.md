# Live dynamic student-to-teacher gaze displays for one-to-one teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Gaze displays are "visualizations of where a student looks while executing a task", produced by eye-tracking apparatus (SMI RED250mobile, 250 Hz) and shown to the teacher in real time on a separate monitor during one-to-one teaching. The novel application is helping teachers spot covert misconceptions — for example, a student looking at the outermost retinal layer while speaking about the innermost — and adapt support accordingly. In this first experiment with live dynamic displays, they did not improve student learning or perceived adaptivity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to interpret the gaze display quickly and act on it simultaneously during the session; eye-tracking calibration with average error ≤ 1.0° per point
#### Constraints
- Teachers in the study expressed doubt in their interpretations and found interpreting and reacting almost simultaneously cognitively demanding; effects over longer periods or with teacher training remain untested

### Target Learners
- optometry students in one-to-one clinical teaching sessions

### Learning Goals
- OCT image interpretation; diagnosing retinal pathology
