# The gas-mileage estimation lesson: a teacher's-guide/textbook chapter introducing dimensional analysis and order-of-magnitude physics in introductory physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A worked lesson that estimates a car's gas mileage through air resistance, Reynolds numbers, and gasoline energy content. The abstract states it "describes one way to intro duce dimensional analysis and order-of-magnitude physics into introductory physics (if only th e syllabus would allow it)" and that it is "part teacher's guide and part textbook chapter". It proceeds from the question of where gasoline energy goes, through drag estimation, to a mileage prediction and a follow-up question about driving 80 mph.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor willing to present the Navier–Stokes equations first to motivate the simpler method, and to interleave the checks and thought experiments as designed.
#### Constraints
- The author notes it fits introductory physics only "if only th e syllabus would allow it"; the mileage result benefits from cancellation of two errors, so the final number is unreasonably accurate by luck.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- general education students

### Learning Goals
- dimensional analysis
- order-of-magnitude physics
- air resistance and Reynolds numbers
