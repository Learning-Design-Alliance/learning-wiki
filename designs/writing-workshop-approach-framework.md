# Writing workshop as a student-centered, process-based approach to teaching writing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The writing workshop is defined in the article as "a process-based approach for teaching writing in a meaningful environment" that teaches writing in a social context. It typically begins with a mini-lesson, followed by writing and rewriting stages, and ends with a sharing or celebration time such as an author's chair. The teacher serves as a guide and facilitator, and the framework highlights the active role of the learner, the social nature of writing, and the recursive rather than linear nature of the writing process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mini-lesson at the beginning of the session
- Sustained writing and rewriting time
- Sharing/celebration time for students to present work
- Teacher acting as guide and facilitator
#### Constraints
- When implemented in ideal form, writing workshops take a large portion of the instructional day

### Target Learners
- Prospective teachers of English
- Primary and secondary EFL students

### Learning Goals
- Writing process skills
- Writing mechanics (punctuation, spelling, grammar)
