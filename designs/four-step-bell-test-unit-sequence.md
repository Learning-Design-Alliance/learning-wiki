# Four-step Bell-test teaching unit moving from discussion of locality and realism to data-driven violation of the CHSH inequality

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a lesson-level sequence for the Bell test that begins with students discussing their own concepts of locality and realism, then introduces dichotomous outcomes and the CHSH quantity, then has students perform all measurements on entangled pairs using the game, and finally compare their initial intuition with the data and analyze the implications of the violation. The article states the sequence is suggested "to enhance their critical thinking", with students discovering implications from their own gathered data rather than being told the result in advance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior teaching of superposition and entanglement; the entangled-pair game rules; enough students and measurements for statistically reliable expectation values.
#### Constraints
- The article assumes "the initial concepts of superposition and entanglement have been already taught beforehand" and focuses only on the Bell test.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- local realism and its assumptions
- Bell inequality violation
- scientific reasoning from experimental data
