# Three-component text-processing lesson model for building comprehension of informational science texts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention operationalizes a lesson model in which "Each instructional lesson consisted of three components: 1) identifying key words and main ideas through text-based discourse, 2) synthesizing information within a single text for summarization and making inferences, and 3) integrating information across multiple texts". Interventionists model components through think-alouds, provide guided practice and corrective feedback, and gradually release responsibility as students show proficiency. The model targets both lower-level and higher-level language processes hypothesized to restrict struggling readers' synthesis of central ideas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventionists use explicit instructional routines, quality feedback, and gradual release of responsibility, supported by semi-scripted lessons and weekly research-team support
#### Constraints
- The article reports the feedback loop for main idea statements was challenging for undergraduate interventionists and required ongoing weekly professional development and script modifications

### Target Learners
- middle grade students (grades 6-8) reading below Basic or Basic on a state reading assessment

### Learning Goals
- identifying key words and main ideas in informational science texts
- summarizing and making inferences
- integrating information across multiple texts
- vocabulary knowledge
