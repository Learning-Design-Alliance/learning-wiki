# The startup model of graduate training: teams of specialists coordinated by shared vocabulary rather than duplicated expertise

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The roadmap proposes organizing graduate QISE training around teams rather than individual generalists: "In a technology startup, the relevant unit is not the individual expert but the team: a collection of specialists who coordinate around a shared goal through a common language rather than duplicated expertise." Under this model, programs produce specialists who know their own domain in depth and can communicate across it, shifting the design criterion from what knowledge a student graduates with to what collaborative configurations a student can participate in effectively. It is offered as the article's first concrete recommendation and as a reframing that separates individual learning needs from collective team capability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared vocabulary and conceptual fluency that lets specialists from different disciplines work productively together without a translator
#### Constraints
- The tension reappears in deciding how much common language is enough and which subfields require minimal fluency

### Target Learners
- graduate students in quantum information science and engineering

### Learning Goals
- cross-disciplinary communication and team-based research capability
