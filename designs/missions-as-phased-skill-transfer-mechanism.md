# International surgical missions as a phased skill-transfer mechanism for newly established open-heart centers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents international surgical missions as a mechanism combining humanitarian service delivery with local capacity building: their targets are "to provide humanitarian surgical treatment to patients coupled with improving skills of the local team". Missions supervise the local team, arrange overseas training placements, and progressively reduce their involvement as local competence grows, as happened when the visiting team "progressively declined the number of visits because the local team continued to acquire skills and competence". The mechanism culminates in local independence, which the pandemic tested when missions could not travel.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A local team trained in various subsections including critical care nurses, theatre nurses, biomedical engineers, and perfusionists apart from cardiac anesthesiologists and cardiac surgeons
- Government commitment as a pre-requisite to such programs
- Advanced diagnostic facilities at the host center
#### Constraints
- The article notes missions have been observed with mixed results; a Nigerian review concluded missions were not able to provide adequate services in terms of reduction and provision of educational skills to the local team

### Target Learners
- Local surgical teams at newly established open-heart centers in sub-Saharan Africa
- Fellowship students from the missions' countries

### Learning Goals
- Cardiac surgical operative skill (CABG)
- Perioperative and critical care competencies across the cardiac team
