# Two structured Jupyter-notebook activities on ultracold atoms and BEC creation designed for upper-division courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors developed a sequence of two activities, each taking students two to three hours plus roughly half an hour of preparation, structured as Jupyter notebooks in which students submit runs to Oqtant, analyze returned data, and answer reflection questions. The article reports that "Each of the activities and preparation activities included explicit learning goals, so the students would know what they were expected to learn from the activities." Activity 1 covers absorption imaging and extracting atom number and temperature; Activity 2 covers BEC creation, bimodal fits, critical temperature, and aspect-ratio comparisons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need basic Python programming knowledge including generating basic plotting commands, using for loops, and slightly adjusting already-written code
#### Constraints
- The activities were designed assuming minimal student prior knowledge of BECs but were somewhat structured due to the complexity of Oqtant

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students in quantum mechanics or beyond-first-year lab courses

### Learning Goals
- analyzing absorption-imaging data
- understanding BEC formation and quantum behavior
