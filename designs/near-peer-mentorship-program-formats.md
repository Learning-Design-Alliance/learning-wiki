# Four program formats for near-peer mentorship: unstructured mentor groups, university club, semester class, and summer early start program

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide describes "four ways to structure your mentorship program" — unstructured mentor groups, university club/student organization, semester class, and summer early start program — listing pros and cons of each and rating each from 1 to 5 on time commitment and cost. Choice depends on the community's goals, size, funding, and level of organizational support; for example, semester classes ease scheduling and allow credit incentives, while summer early start programs are expensive (over $15,000) but effective at creating lasting communities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The leadership team should discuss target audience, goals, format, and group dynamics before proposing the program to department leadership and applying for funding
- A semester-class format requires content developers, a TA and often an LA, plus university paperwork to list the class for credit
#### Constraints
- This chapter does not include information on how to start or run a semester class or pre-arrival program; the guide defers this to a future chapter
- Summer early start programs are expensive (over $15,000) and require substantial organizer time across multiple university offices

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students, including incoming first-year and transfer students
- graduate and senior undergraduate mentors

### Learning Goals
- navigating college and the STEM major
- building mentoring relationships and community
