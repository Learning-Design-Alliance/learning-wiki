# Model-comparison lab activities require linearly related variables, competing models, simple data collection, and messy data

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When choosing a lab activity to meet iterative experimentation and quantitative fitting goals, the authors specify a reusable configuration of activity features. They needed "an activity that involved two continuous and linearly-related variables; two reasonable models that could be compared; relatively simple data collection so that students could focus on the analysis and iterate; and relatively messy data that would warrant iteration and reflection." This pattern makes the data genuinely ambiguous, forcing students to improve the experiment to distinguish the two models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two reasonable models that could be compared
- Data messy enough to warrant iteration and reflection
#### Constraints
- The features are stated as the authors' design choices for their own mechanics activity, not as tested conditions

### Target Learners
- first-semester introductory mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- collect data and revise an experimental procedure iteratively and reflectively
- evaluate the process and outcomes of an experiment quantitatively
