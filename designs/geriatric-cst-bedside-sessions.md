# Geriatric clinical skills training: four 2.5-hour bedside sessions with real patients plus a self-preparation textbook

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention was a geriatric clinical skills training (CST) of "four 2.5-hour teaching sessions" covering central geriatric assessment domains (activities of daily living, cognition, mobility, social situation, nutrition, medication, continence, hearing and vision screening, and a consolidating complete geriatric assessment). Each session began with a 45-minute interactive introduction, then students worked in small groups of four to five with real patients at the bedside, supervised by a clinician experienced in geriatric medicine, using only validated instruments (MMSE, BADL) with a textbook for self-preparation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A textbook with detailed instructions on performing and interpreting the validated instruments, distributed before the first session
- Supervision by a clinician experienced in geriatric medicine
- Small groups of four to five students working with real patients at the bedside
#### Constraints
- Students did not do ward work during the intervention period
- Evaluated at a single academic institution with several teachers, limiting generalizability

### Target Learners
- First-year clinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- Geriatric assessment skills (cognition, mobility, ADL, nutrition, medication, continence, sensory screening)
- Attitudes toward elderly patients
- Geriatric knowledge
