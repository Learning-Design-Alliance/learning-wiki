# Use the real night sky as a live background for teaching observational astronomy with a guided laser pointer

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's guiding principle is that teaching of basic observational astronomy concepts can use the real night sky itself as the display surface, with a laser pointer beam perceived as touching the celestial dome at the chosen star. The authors write that "The teaching of basic concepts of observational astronomy can take advantage of these perceptions using the real night sky as a live blackboard," and built Stellector to project sky dynamics and representations directly onto the open sky. Because such activities demand open skies away from tall buildings and illuminated areas, they are mainly employed in non-formal educational environments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sufficiently dark environment so the laser light path is visible through atmospheric dispersion and Rayleigh scattering
- Open skies far away from tall buildings and highly illuminated areas
#### Constraints
- These activities are mainly employed in non-formal educational environments due to their intrinsic outdoor nature

### Target Learners
- general public
- school and university students in non-formal settings

### Learning Goals
- basic concepts of observational astronomy
- apparent motions of the celestial sphere
- constellations and their cultural meanings
