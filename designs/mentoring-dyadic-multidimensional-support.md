# Mentoring as a dyadic relationship spanning cognitive, motivational and emotional dimensions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article builds its design on a concept of mentoring "understood as dyadic relationship between mentor and mentee" that can support learners' learning processes. A holistic view includes cognitive, motivational and emotional dimensions while ensuring learner autonomy and self-determination; because the research literature presents mentoring as inhomogeneous, the article systematizes it via functional criteria, including support for the transfer of scientific expertise. The prototype focuses on students' domain-specific knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- optimal conditions for effective learning processes, including frequent and high-quality feedback, practice and consolidation of what has been learned
#### Constraints
- the prototype development currently focuses on knowledge of the students, not the full multidimensional scope
- individual face-to-face mentoring requires a lot of time and effort and cannot reach all students in higher education

### Target Learners
- higher education students supervised by a small number of lecturers

### Learning Goals
- domain-specific learning processes, especially knowledge
