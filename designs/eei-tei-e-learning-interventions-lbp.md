# Experimental vs traditional e-learning interventions for physiotherapists' low back pain management

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Two three-module (30 min each) e-learning interventions, both based on the same LBP clinical guidelines, were compared in the RCT. The EEI "contained interactive modules with the inclusion of menus, videos, simulated clinical situations, metaphors, voice-overs and quizzes", let participants control their pace, and demonstrated guideline application pragmatically; the TEI was lecture-based, theoretical, without interaction or clinical examples, and pace-controlled only by watching and pausing. This nested qualitative study evaluated participants' experiences of both interventions' content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Developed by a multidisciplinary expert team of academics and clinicians and based on recent LBP clinical guidelines and a prior pilot study
#### Constraints
- The TEI provided no interaction or clinical examples and did not allow pace control beyond watching and pausing

### Target Learners
- Licensed physiotherapists in Belgium and France actively managing patients with low back pain

### Learning Goals
- Improving knowledge, attitudes, and beliefs toward a biopsychosocial, guideline-consistent approach to LBP management
