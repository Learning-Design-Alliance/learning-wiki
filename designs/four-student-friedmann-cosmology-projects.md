# Four student cosmology projects based on numerical Friedmann-equation calculations with EXCEL instructions and supplementary materials

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes four student projects that use numerical solutions of the Friedmann equations to compute ages and distance factors. The authors write: "We use these numerical calculations in four student projects that help to build their understanding of high-redshift phenomena and cosmology." The projects are written in four parts, assume familiarity with Ryden's textbook presentation, include a Matter Only test case with an analytical solution for debugging, and are available online and as supplementary materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students are assumed familiar with the theory presentation in Ryden's textbook; advanced students complete the project in about six hours while less advanced students need instruction on creating and using parameters in EXCEL
#### Constraints
- The numerical solution is limited to unique single-valued functions when swapping axes, and the paper advises avoiding epochs prior to 10^-32 seconds when inflation and quantum gravity play an important role

### Target Learners
- undergraduate astronomy students taking classes such as ASTR-326

### Learning Goals
- understanding of high-redshift phenomena and cosmology
- computing ages and distances back to the time of the Big Bang as professional astronomers do

- Numerical Friedmann Reproduces Wmap Age Horizon [+M]
