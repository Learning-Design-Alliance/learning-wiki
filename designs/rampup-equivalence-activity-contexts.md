# RAMPUP uses six contextualized activity structures to elicit convergent, relational, and transformational equivalence reasoning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief illustrates equivalence reasoning through six RAMPUP contexts that overlap with but are not identical to typical school topics: mazes (connected components as equivalence classes), sliding puzzles, graphs of networks, the mean as point of balance, functions as alphabetic codes (integers modulo 26), and perspective drawing. As stated, "This section offers examples from observations of RAMPUP field trials and analysis of written student work." Table 3 maps student utterances in each context onto the three framework approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must be designed so multiple reasoning approaches can surface; for example, the mean activity elicits transformational, relational, and convergent approaches simultaneously
#### Constraints
- The contexts were designed for rising 9th graders; in the Perspective Plane context, transformational reasoning was not applicable

### Target Learners
- rising 9th grade students, including English Learners

### Learning Goals
- connected components, graph equivalence, mean as balance point, modular arithmetic, coordinate geometry in perspective
