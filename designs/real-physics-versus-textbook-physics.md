# Real physics versus textbook physics as a framing for instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The lecture frames a distinction between the physics students learn in classes—manipulating equations for carefully tuned "word problems"—and real physics, which "involves intuition, approximation, and quantitativ e reasoning to understand what matters in a physical situation." In real physics one must identify dominant effects, find scalings, and interrogate rather than merely use equations; formal techniques come only after the fundamental properties are understood. The bucket problem is used to demonstrate this contrast throughout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must confront ill-posed problems where no one specifies the equations or the numbers to use
#### Constraints
- The lecture states most physical questions do not admit a simple solveable equation; simple solveable problems are the exception, not the rule

### Target Learners
- Physics students with prior secondary-school textbook physics instruction

### Learning Goals
- Quantitative reasoning about what matters in real physical situations
