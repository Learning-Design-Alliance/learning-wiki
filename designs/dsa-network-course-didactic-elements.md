# Summer-course design combining pupil presentations, formalizing lectures, hands-on modules, problem sheets, programming, Rotation and Dokumentation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course interleaved several didactic elements: pupil presentations introducing phenomena phenomenologically, lectures that formalized the mathematics, small participatory modules, six problem sheets mixing pen-and-paper and programming tasks, and the DSA-structured Rotation (peer teaching to other courses) and Dokumentation (a collaborative written report). The article states: "In the course we used different didactic elements. Some of them, as the Rotation and the Dokumentation, were framed by the DSA structure, others, as pupil presentations and programming exercises, were chosen deliberately by us."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough course duration (approximately sixty hours over two weeks) to combine lectures, modules, exercises and peer-teaching events
- Instructor capacity to prepare individualized presentation topics and edit the Dokumentation
#### Constraints
- The initially planned one problem sheet per day proved too ambitious for the schedule

### Target Learners
- motivated and gifted pupils in the final two years of German high school

### Learning Goals
- network science concepts
- mathematical formalization of phenomena
- scientific communication and writing
