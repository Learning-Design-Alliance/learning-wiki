# Multi-week ED-based physics lab task with interwoven hands-on and computational scaffolds

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A five-week (weeks 06–10) engineering design problem embedded in a large-enrollment calculus-based introductory physics course, in which teams design a system to launch and safely deliver a ~50 kg payload to an island 150 m away. "Most of the lab sessions, to facilitate the integration of science in ED, were comprised of two interwoven parts": hands-on PASCO inquiry experiments and computational activities using PhET and VPython. Weekly audio-recorded discussions and a final written group report served as deliverables and data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teamwork within groups of 2–3 students
- GTA feedback and circulation during lab sessions
- Prior tutorials on ED for non-engineering majors
#### Constraints
- Scaffolds were designed assuming a launch mechanism, which the authors later realized may have unintentionally preempted student approaches

### Target Learners
- first-semester undergraduate physics students, about 25% women, mostly engineering majors

### Learning Goals
- applying physics principles (energy, momentum, angular momentum) to an authentic engineering design problem
- iterative design, modeling, and making assumptions and approximations
