# Three-stage engineering method for constructing simulated clinical scenarios

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article applies a case-construction engineering methodology, attributed to Pereira Júnior and Lima, in which "the planning and organization of the process is carried out in three stages": choosing the clinical case to be transformed into a simulated activity, assembling the order of structuring items for the station, and constructing the complete simulated station with instructions, checklists and decision flowcharts. The method turns a real clinical problem into a reproducible simulation station.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A defined clinical problem or case, which may come from curricular contents or from the real work of health professionals, is chosen before structuring begins
#### Constraints
- The article demonstrates the method only for one pediatric emergency sedation scenario

### Target Learners
- multidisciplinary and multiprofessional health teams in emergency services

### Learning Goals
- technical and non-technical skills for pediatric sedation and invasive procedures, including teamwork, communication and crisis management
