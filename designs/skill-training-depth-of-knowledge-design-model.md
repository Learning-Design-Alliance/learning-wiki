# Skill-training design model: introduce surface declarative knowledge, consolidate to conceptual understanding, then prepare transfer to applied settings

## Description
The article organizes its training design and analyses around models of learning-skill development, chiefly Hattie and Donoghue's (2016) framework and Efklides' (2011) MASRL model. These hold that trainings "should introduce surface-level declarative knowledge about a skill and consolidate that knowledge to a conceptual level of understanding," and should include "activities that promote transfer" so learners can apply skills in authentic settings. Metacognitive knowledge of skills accrues from metacognitive experiences. The studies test whether mastery at each depth predicts later course performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must provide exposure and opportunities to engage in SRL so learners develop metacognitive knowledge of skills
- Activities must assess declarative, conceptual, and applied knowledge of the skills
#### Constraints
- The article notes meta-analyses' moderator analyses "provide little information as to why such programs are effective"

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in high-enrollment STEM gateway courses

### Learning Goals
- Declarative, conceptual, and applied biology learning objectives
- Self-regulated learning skills including cognitive strategies and regulation of behavior and environment
