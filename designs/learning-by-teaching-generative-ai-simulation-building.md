# Learning-by-teaching generative AI: students prompt, validate, and refine an AI-built simulation to learn physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames its AI-engagement lab as an instance of learning-by-teaching, in which students act as teachers of the AI: "prompting genAI to produce effective simulations for modeling physics phenomena." A cornerstone is that students validate both the technical and scientific aspects of the AI's outputs and iteratively prompt the AI to produce increasingly accurate models, then use the resulting simulations to explore the physics content. The approach is positioned as extending earlier work showing simulations support learning about the physical world, now updated with generative AI capabilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must systematically validate both interface functionality and physical accuracy of the AI-generated simulation before using it to explore the content.
- Subsequent prompts beyond the initial provided prompt must be generated by the students themselves.
#### Constraints
- The article states the approach was tested for one physics concept (electric potential) with a homogeneous life-sciences population at a single institution, and generalization to other topics and contexts remains an empirical question.
- The underlying pedagogy is asserted as a design rationale; the study tested conceptual and affective outcomes, not modeling skills directly.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics for life sciences students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of electric potential and equipotential lines
- effective use of generative AI tools
- authentic STEM modeling and evaluation practices
