# Give early-university-access programmes a reliable framework of formalised organisational rules, a dedicated committee, and close timetabling collaboration with schools

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends giving such programmes a reliable framework: "all the procedures have been formalised in a set of organisational rules, and a committee has been set up to carry out the programme". University classes are scheduled during school hours, so active collaboration with school leadership teams ensures the schedule of Athena classes is designed "to have as little impact on high school hours as possible".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A committee set up to carry out the programme
- Formalised organisational rules covering all procedures
- Active collaboration with school leadership teams and timetabling offices
- Consent from the school principal, the subject teacher and parents or legal guardians of minors
#### Constraints
- School leadership can refuse to release a pupil from particular classes, typically laboratory sessions, which can force a change of Athena course
- After timetables are fixed in July, incompatibilities may require changing the pupil's assigned course

### Target Learners
- high-school pupils following a maturité or equivalent university-access curriculum

### Learning Goals
- smooth integration of university courses with secondary-school curricula
- successful completion of early university study
