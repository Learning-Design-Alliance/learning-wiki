# Personal-need customer pitches as the source of project problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The pattern begins with a graded in-class activity where every student pitches a need that is personal to them, in a 1-slide, 1-minute presentation. The article stresses that "this need is personal and cannot be something they project someone else might want," because a student cannot meaningfully assess usability of a solution for someone else's need. Suitable pitches come from the student's own lived experience (e.g., a frisbee player wanting a rotation-tracking frisbee), while projected needs for family members are deemed unsuitable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pitches must describe a need the student personally experiences
- Pitch format is a 1-slide, 1-minute presentation to the class
#### Constraints
- Pitching needs purely based on cost concerns relative to existing products is identified as a problem to be excluded in future pitch instructions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory prototyping courses

### Learning Goals
- framing a real problem from personal experience
- communicating a customer need
