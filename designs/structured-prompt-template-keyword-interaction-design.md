# Structured prompt-template and keyword-card interaction design for human-LLM co-creation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A novel interaction protocol for studying human-LLM foraging: six editable prompt templates divided into Exploration and Exploitation types, plus a shared pool of keyword cards contributed by course participants and categorized into concepts such as 'Actors', 'AI tools', 'Environments', and 'Subjects'. The templates "facilitated the exploration-exploitationbehavioral modes of IFT, standardized the interaction between participants, guided those without prior experience, and enabled systematic analysis of their foraging behavior". Participants crafted prompts by combining templates with guide or GPT-sourced keywords in 45-minute facilitated ChatGPT sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompt templates and keyword cards derived from participants' reported interests gathered before the course
- A researcher-mediated 45-minute session protocol with setup, introduction, execution, and conclusion phases
#### Constraints
- The structured design intentionally limits free-form prompting, so results do not reflect typical ChatGPT use
- The specific task complicates comparisons with previous web search studies that formally assess IFT principles

### Target Learners
- Doctoral-level educational scientists in an AI in Education course

### Learning Goals
- Pedagogical content creation and study-design scenario development with generative AI
