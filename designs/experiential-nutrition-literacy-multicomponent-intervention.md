# Multi-component experiential learning intervention pattern for children's nutrition literacy: classroom activities, home practice activities, family eating recording, and daily health education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention integrates four modules described in Table 1: experiential classroom activities (food label games, simulated shopping, dietary pagoda models, hands-on cooking), home-based nutritional practice activities with parents, family healthy eating recording, and daily nutrition health education. The article states that "The experiential learning-based intervention mainly includes experiential classroom activities, practical nutritional activities, family healthy eating recording and daily nutrition health education." According to the discussion, the multi-component design "systematically addresses all three dimensions of NL: functional (basic nutritional concepts in classroom lessons), interactive (food shopping and meal preparation), and critical (food labels interpreting and beverage sugar evaluation)."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery requires collaboration among nutrition experts who develop the curriculum and train teachers, teachers who execute activities, and researchers who orchestrate procedures; standardized protocols, slides, lesson plans, and trained teachers are specified.
#### Constraints
- The article states that reliance on family participation "introduces a potential risk to implementation fidelity and may contribute to variability in effectiveness across different socioeconomic or cultural contexts," and that the intervention did not explicitly distinguish ultra-processed from minimally processed foods.

### Target Learners
- School-aged children in grades 3–4 (aged 8–10 years) and their parents/primary caregivers

### Learning Goals
- Nutrition literacy across functional, interactive, and critical levels; food selection and preparation skills; healthier dietary habits
