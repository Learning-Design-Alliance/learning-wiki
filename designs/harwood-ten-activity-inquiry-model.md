## Description
The article adopts the Harwood model of inquiry, which it describes as representing "how science is conducted as a series of iterative processes, and which is applicable across disciplines". Harwood specifies ten activities the authors emphasize in their experiments: ask questions, define the problem, form the question, investigate the known, articulate an expectation, carry out the study, examine the results, reflect on the findings, communicate with others, and make observations. Tables 2 and 3 map each physics and chemistry lab task onto these ten activities to show the labs supply opportunities for meaningful scientific inquiry.
