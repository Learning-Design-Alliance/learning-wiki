# Jigsaw-structured interprofessional prescribing workshop with expert stations and home teams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The workshop used the "Jigsaw" learning technique, a cooperative learning technique where every student can be thought of as "a segment of the jigsaw puzzle". Students first form Home Teams (Jigsaw groups), then convene in six Expert stations covering the WHO Guide to Good Prescribing steps, then reassemble to teach each other and solve six case scenarios, with facilitators challenging presenters and giving prompt feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training of students on this learning technique was essential
- learning at each Expert station was facilitated by a pharmacologist, pharmacist or medical doctor
- two facilitators managed a Jigsaw group concurrently
#### Constraints
- tested in only one type of interprofessional activity (the prescribing skills workshop) at one university

### Target Learners
- final-year medical and pharmacy students working in mixed groups of approximately 2 medical to 1 pharmacy student

### Learning Goals
- prescribing skills and acceptance of interprofessional learning
