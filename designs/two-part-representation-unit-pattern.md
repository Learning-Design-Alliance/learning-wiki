# A two-part in-class unit on 'What is physics?' and 'Who does physics?' in a large engineering course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curricular pattern for large enrollment courses: a two-part unit (one 75-minute class period each plus readings, homework, and exam questions) on the role of people in science and on representation in STEM. Part one discusses the nature of science and who creates models; part two has students google "famous physicists", interpret APS participation data by race, gender, and LGBTQ identification, and discuss why representation matters via an anonymous notecard exchange.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supportive and inclusive classroom culture, which the authors call a necessary condition for successfully addressing the unit's goals
#### Constraints
- The authors first assess impact through students' social and emotional reactions; additional measures of the learning goals are left to future work

### Target Learners
- sophomore engineering majors in a 75-120 student modern physics course

### Learning Goals
- recognize and discuss the role of people in science
- identify the lack of diversity in STEM fields and discuss why representation matters
- consider who does science as part of science itself
