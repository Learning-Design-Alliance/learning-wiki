# Backwards design applied to IPLS syllabus construction, starting from biologically necessary topics rather than traditional physics coverage

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course's syllabus is derived by a backwards design approach: rather than covering traditional introductory physics topics and adding biology examples, the designer starts by identifying which ideas and topics are necessary to achieve stated learning goals for the target audience, then organizes modules accordingly. The article states: "this first-semester syllabus is the result of a backwards design process21 that started with a number of key ideas and topics – diffusion, fluid flow, mathematical modeling, and statistical mechanics – that it would be necessary to include to achieve the overarching goals listed above." The same approach is applied to the mathematical content of the course: "Beyond calculus, the mathematics that we employ is dictated by the topics that we seek to treat, i.e we also used backwards design here." Because this is likely students' final physics course, the authors reason there is no rationale to prepare for advanced physics classes nor any excuse to postpone compelling material.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear statement of overarching goals for the course before selecting content
- Identification of key ideas and topics necessary to achieve those goals
- A defined target audience whose disciplinary needs shape topic selection
#### Constraints
- Appropriate when the course will be students' final physics course, eliminating rationale for preparation toward advanced physics
- The authors caution that "there cannot and should not be a one-size-fits-all IPLS course, suitable for every situation"

### Target Learners
- Biological science and premedical undergraduates

### Learning Goals
- Apply physics to biological and physiological phenomena
- Develop quantitative and mathematical reasoning in biological contexts
