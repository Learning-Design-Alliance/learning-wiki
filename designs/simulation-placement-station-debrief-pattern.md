# Four-week simulation placement structured as five daily stations led in rotation by students, with daily practice-educator debriefs using Pendleton's rules

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The pattern organises a four-week simulation placement as an immersive practice environment. Students work in groups of four to five, covering five stations a day, each student leading a station with responsibilities including "taking patient history, leading the peer discussions on assessment and treatment planning, writing patient notes". Each station has a service user or scripted model, students spend about 60 minutes per station, and a 60-minute daily debrief with an HCPC-registered physiotherapist provides feedback modelled on Pendleton's rules encouraging self-reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A practice facilitator pre-selects patient models who read from scripts provided by the physiotherapy practice placement team
- A qualified, HCPC-registered practice educator observes student work and leads the daily debrief
#### Constraints
- Groups of four to five were felt by some participants to be larger than typical real-world physiotherapy staffing ratios for one patient
- Groups did not change periodically across the four weeks, which some participants felt limited learning from different cohort peers

### Target Learners
- Year 2 students on a 2-year MSc pre-registration physiotherapy programme in the UK

### Learning Goals
- Clinical assessment and treatment planning
- Teamwork and communication with peers and service users
- Professional behaviours including time management and reflection
