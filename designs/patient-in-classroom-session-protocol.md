# Patient-in-classroom session protocol for the Home Nursing Care course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A structured 90-minute session format in which patients with stable chronic conditions relevant to home nursing care join nursing classes. Each session included "a 15-minute structured patient narrative about living with their condition, a 30-minute moderated Q&A session facilitated by the instructor, a 30-minute small group discussion applying theoretical concepts to the patient case, and a 15-minute debriefing and synthesis." Twelve different patients participated across the 14-week course, each attending 2–3 sessions, and patients received a 2-hour orientation and compensation of approximately $20 per session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Patients screened for medical stability, communication ability, and willingness to participate
- Each patient received a 2-hour orientation session outlining educational objectives, session structure, and boundaries
- The same instructors taught both groups with identical contact time (21 h) and standardized training
#### Constraints
- Fidelity monitoring via monthly observation of 20% of randomly selected sessions using a 10-item checklist yielded a mean fidelity score of 92.4% (range: 85–100%)

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in semesters 5–8 of a Home Nursing Care practical course

### Learning Goals
- Connecting home nursing care theory to real patient needs and challenges
- Preparation for professional practice and clinical realities
