# Four interpretations of quantum mechanics employed to organize conceptual discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes epistemological discussion around four interpretations of quantum mechanics: i) the undulatory interpretation or Schrödinger wavefunction, ii) the complementarity principle or Copenhagen school, iii) hidden-variables or dualist-realist interpretation, and iv) the many worlds interpretation. Each is classified by ontological stance toward the photon (wavepacket, mathematical instrument, particle with guide-wave, or wavepacket without collapse) and by realist or positivist epistemology. These differing views are internally consistent yet all predict the same experimental result, making them suitable for enriching conceptual teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interpretations to be distinguished by ontological views (corpuscular, undulatory or dualist) and epistemological attitudes (realist or positivist)
#### Constraints
- The Copenhagen interpretation's original formulation has led to several variants, making it difficult to establish how exactly this interpretation is stated

### Target Learners
- physics teacher candidates
- upper secondary and introductory university students

### Learning Goals
- understanding wave-particle duality
- evaluating epistemological attitudes toward quantum theory
