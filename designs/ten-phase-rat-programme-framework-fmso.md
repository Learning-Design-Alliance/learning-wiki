# Ten-phase residents-as-teachers programme framework for a faculty of medicine

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a ten-phase RaT programme for the Faculty of Medicine of Sousse, inspired by the AMEE Guide's three underlying frameworks: Kellogg's Programme Logic Model for programme design, Dundee's three-circle model for educational content, and Kirkpatrick's pyramid for evaluation. The phases run from needs assessment and goal-setting through curriculum development, faculty development, resident training sessions, teaching opportunities, assessment and feedback, curriculum integration, evaluation and continuous improvement, acknowledgement and rewards, to long-term sustainability. The authors state the AMEE guide reported that "the potential benefits of RaT programmes resides in the enhancement of teaching abilities, which leads to greater self-efficacy and teacher identity."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Full integration into the medical school's broader curriculum
- Securing institutional and external funding for sustainability
- A leadership succession plan to maintain quality
#### Constraints
- The framework is proposed for the authors' institution and must be customised to the faculty's specific needs
- Patient care outcomes as the ultimate goal "requires currently further research"

### Target Learners
- Residents across specialties and the faculty members who mentor them

### Learning Goals
- Teaching ability, self-efficacy and teacher identity, capacity to evaluate and give feedback, interest in education as a career
