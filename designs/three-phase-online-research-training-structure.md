# Three-phase structure for a virtual research-training program: live interactive sessions, guided project selection, then mentored project work and presentation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organises its Astronomy from Archival Data project as a sequence of three phases. The authors write that "The project consisted of three phases", beginning with live weekend zoom sessions of lectures and interactive hands-on sessions on Virtual Observatory Tools, Data Archives and Science Cases, all recorded and posted online. The second phase let students complete watching videos and select projects and interact with mentors, and the third gave students time to work on projects of their choice and present their results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Live video-conferencing sessions with lectures and interactive hands-on sessions, recorded and posted online
- Reading material, quizzes, data sources and analysis techniques available on a website
- Mentors running sessions with student groups during project selection and project work
#### Constraints
- Scheduling had to avoid participants' own online sessions; the authors selected weekends, but overlapping classes and examinations later reduced attendance

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and postgraduate science students
- School teachers and interested students

### Learning Goals
- Accessing and analysing astronomy data from the internet
- Virtual Observatory tools and programming techniques
- Formulating and developing research projects, report writing and presenting in scientific journals
