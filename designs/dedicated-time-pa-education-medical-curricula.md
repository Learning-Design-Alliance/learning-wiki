# Physical activity teaching should be allocated dedicated, defined curriculum time in medical schools, backed by curriculum change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that PA education receive dedicated and defined time in undergraduate medical curricula, so that future doctors gain the knowledge, confidence and skills to promote PA and follow the many clinical guidelines supporting PA promotion. The authors argue this must be "backed by curriculum change, with an emphasis on PA promotion and delivery", and supported with behaviour change skills fundamental to preventive medicine and clinical communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated and defined time within the undergraduate curriculum, since sparse or integrated provision leaves PA teaching unquantifiable and omitted
#### Constraints
- Efforts to increase PA promotion would also need to focus on Board examinations, which ultimately determine undergraduate curriculum content; several schools only recognised their lack of PA education when surveyed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in UK medical schools

### Learning Goals
- knowledge of physical activity science and health benefits
- confidence and skills to promote physical activity and behaviour change to patients
- ability to follow disease-specific clinical guidelines supporting PA promotion
