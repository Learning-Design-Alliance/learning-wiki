# The ship metaphor framework for clinical skills education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article develops a metaphorical organising framework in which clinical skills education is pictured as "a ship's journey, where the student is the ship, navigating through stormy seas representing the challenges." Six metaphors structure the findings: the safety harness (simulation), the underwater life (bedside learning), the stormy seas (barriers), the ship (the student), the engine room tools (teaching modalities) and the guiding compass (faculty mentorship). Metaphors served as analytic tools condensing codes from faculty, student and patient groups into shared conceptual frames for triangulation. The framework underpins the authors' cross-cutting tensions of safety versus authenticity, standardisation versus variability, and mentorship versus service pressure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multi-stakeholder qualitative data so that metaphors can condense codes from diverse participant groups into shared frames
#### Constraints
- Developed and validated only at a single private medical college and teaching hospital in Pakistan, limiting transfer to other institutional or regional contexts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- house officers
- clinical faculty

### Learning Goals

- empathy and communication
- professional identity formation
