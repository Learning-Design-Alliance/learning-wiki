# Require formal training in both clinical neuroscience and interventional neuroradiology before physicians provide endovascular stroke treatment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The consensus principle is that safe endovascular ischemic stroke intervention requires formal training spanning both clinical neuroscience and interventional neuroradiology, because successful treatment demands rapidly integrating patient history, clinical examination, and neuroradiological studies into a treatment plan. The document states "there is a clear rationale for formal training in both clinical neuroscience and interventional neuroradiology," and that catheter skills from other circulations do not replace training in intracranial microcatheter navigation. It applies this principle by defining baseline qualifications and maintenance requirements for new practitioners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residency training in radiology, neurology or neurosurgery including documented training in diagnosis and management of acute stroke and interpretation of cerebral arteriography and neuroimaging under supervision of a board-certified specialist
#### Constraints
- The qualifications are stated for new practitioners not currently performing acute stroke intervention with mechanical thrombectomy; existing practitioners who acquired the listed skills are expected to meet only the maintenance requirements

### Target Learners
- physicians in radiology, neurology or neurosurgery seeking to perform endovascular stroke intervention

### Learning Goals
- safe intracranial microcatheter navigation and device placement
- patient selection and treatment planning for acute ischemic stroke
