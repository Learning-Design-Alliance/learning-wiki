# Panoramic radiograph interpretation assessment task with histopathologically confirmed lesions and per-case confidence ratings

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An assessment instrument in which students interpret 11 panoramic radiographs of "previously confirmed pathological bone lesions," each confirmed via biopsy or surgical excision, provide two preliminary diagnoses, and rate confidence for each case on a four-point scale (very sure to very unsure). Cases were projected on a large screen with adjusted lighting, randomly assigned across four sessions, with no time limit; a pilot with seven residents checked clarity and difficulty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lesions must have histopathological confirmation to serve as a diagnostic gold standard
- Pilot review of case clarity and difficulty before the main session
#### Constraints
- The two-diagnosis scoring method may slightly inflate accuracy rates, as the article states

### Target Learners
- fifth-year undergraduate dental students

### Learning Goals
- radiographic interpretation of pathological bone lesions
- differential diagnosis and confidence self-assessment
