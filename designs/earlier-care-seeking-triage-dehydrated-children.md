# Promoting earlier care-seeking and primary-level triage of dehydrated children with elevated creatinine may be the highest-yield intervention in this setting.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that mortality is driven by modifiable factors, the article suggests that children with partial volume depletion still respond to rehydration, whereas those presenting with severe dehydration or shock may arrive too late for recovery. It therefore recommends "Promoting earlier care-seeking and primary-level triage of dehydrated children with elevated creatinine" as potentially the highest-yield intervention, addressing the late-presentation pattern before clinical deterioration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Community awareness campaigns to reduce delayed presentation
- Primary-level triage capacity to identify dehydrated children with elevated creatinine
#### Constraints
- The protective association of mild-to-moderate dehydration (OR 0.13) likely reflects a critical intervention window rather than a causal protective effect of dehydration itself

### Target Learners
- Community members and primary-level healthcare providers in Rwanda

### Learning Goals
- Recognizing early signs of dehydration and AKI risk
- Seeking care before severe dehydration or shock develops
