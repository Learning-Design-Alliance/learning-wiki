# Five functions of the school psycho-clinical laboratory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article specifies a five-part functional charter for a school clinical laboratory serving exceptional children: clinical examination of exceptional children, establishment of reliable norms for normal children, acting as a clearing-house for placing exceptional children in appropriate special schools or institutions, training teachers in psycho-clinical testing methods, and supervising special-school curricula while offering courses on the psychology of mental deficiency. Its central aim is "the scientific investigation of abnormalities of development", with examination including graded serial tests, form-board and sensory-motor tests, standardized intellectual tests, and anthropometric measures, and percentile curves plotted for each child.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Full data from the other sources (medical, sociological, pedagogical) so the laboratory can perform its clearing-house function, and reliable norms for typical normal children against which to plot percentile curves
#### Constraints
- The laboratory cannot examine all pupils needing examination in a large school system, so testing must be partly delegated to trained special-room teachers; recommendations of the director are not subject to reversal except by the board or superintendent

### Target Learners
- exceptional children: pedagogically retarded, subnormal, supernormal and delinquent pupils

### Learning Goals
- diagnosis, classification and placement of exceptional children, and establishment of developmental norms
