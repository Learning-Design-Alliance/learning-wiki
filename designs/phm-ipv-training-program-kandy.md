# Four-day IPV training program for Sri Lankan public health midwives with local-language manual, field handbook, role-playing, and referral networking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A four-day training program designed by gender-based violence experts according to international guidelines, delivered by five trained community physicians to PHMs in 22 Medical Officer of Health areas. It used "role-playing and case reports" to build practical skills, materials prepared in Sinhala and matched to professional requirements, and a 52-page field handbook given to each PHM. Trainers stressed cultural sensitivity, privacy, confidentiality, and networking with local women's groups and social services for referral.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers with GBV expertise and postgraduate public health qualifications; a local-language training manual and field handbook; available referral resources such as Medical Officers of Health and local women's groups
#### Constraints
- The program did not require PHMs to document their IPV practices, which the authors say might compromise program effectiveness and make evaluation via document review difficult; the field handbook's excessive detail and 52-page length might make it less readable

### Target Learners
- Public health midwives (community health providers) in Sri Lanka

### Learning Goals
- Identifying IPV sufferers in the community
- Assisting and referring IPV sufferers
- Understanding IPV types, health effects, and the domestic violence law
