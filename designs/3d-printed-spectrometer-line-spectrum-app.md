# 3-D printed spectrometer with line spectrum app for measuring light wavelengths

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A 3-D printed spectrometer, modified from an open source public lab design, attaches to a smartphone camera to record sharp line spectra. The companion app displays intensity against position and, after calibration against fluorescent light using NIST reference values of 611.6 and 546.5 nm, can measure wavelengths of various light sources and identify Helium, Neon, Mercury and Hydrogen.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- camera calibration against fluorescent light using NIST reference values; a 1000 lines diffraction grating sheet attached to the spectrometer window
#### Constraints
- the app only contains the database of these 4 gases and any other gas may be wrongly identified as one of the 4 gases; some older phones have difficulty detecting the light sources and increasing slit width compromises the resolution of the line

### Target Learners
- Grades 11 and 12 physics students

### Learning Goals
- line spectra of gas lamps
- wavelength measurement
- diffraction
