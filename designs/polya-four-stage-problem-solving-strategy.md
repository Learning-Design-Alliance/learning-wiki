# Polya's four-stage problem solving strategy as an instructional approach for higher order thinking in mathematics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operationalizes problem solving strategy as Polya's (1973) four stages: "(1) understand the problem, (2) devise a plan, (3) carry out the plan, and (4) look back", with NCTM indicators such as identifying known and required elements, formulating mathematical models, applying strategies, and interpreting results against the original problem. In the study, partner teachers taught the experimental group with this strategy on system of linear equations material. The article treats it as the more effective alternative to the scientific approach for HOTS outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers implement the four Polya stages with their NCTM indicators in mathematics lessons
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness evidence comes from a quasi-experiment in 10th-grade linear equations in Medan, Indonesia, not from randomized assignment

### Target Learners
- 10th-grade high school mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- mathematical communication
- mathematical creativity
- problem solving
- mathematical reasoning
