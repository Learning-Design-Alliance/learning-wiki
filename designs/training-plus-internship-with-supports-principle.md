# Combine occupational training with an employer internship and layered supports to improve early career earnings for disadvantaged young adults

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The reviewed evidence indicates that a program model pairing full-time technical training with a subsequent employer internship, wrapped in advising, mentoring, stipends, and post-program placement services, can substantially raise short-term earnings for low-income young adults. The WWC judged this combination likely to increase short-term earnings, with an improvement index of +28 percentile points across two randomized trials. The model embeds work-based learning directly into occupational instruction and connects participants to employers in their target occupational fields.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Six months of full-time occupational and technical training aligned to employer needs
- A six-month internship placement in the student's selected occupational field
- Ongoing supports including advising, mentoring, and financial assistance throughout the program
#### Constraints
- The earnings advantage was demonstrated on short-term outcomes; medium-term earnings and employment showed no discernible effects, so the model's sustained impact beyond one year is not established by this evidence
- Implementation depends on employer payments for interns, foundation grants, and charitable donations, which financed nearly all operating costs

### Target Learners
- Low-income young adults ages 18 to 24 with a high school diploma or equivalent

### Learning Goals
- Occupational and technical skills for entry-level positions in information technology and financial services
- Professionalism and workplace behavior skills
- Career planning, job search, and placement
