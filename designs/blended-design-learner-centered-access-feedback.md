# Design blended learning environments around learner-centered access, self-evaluation, feedback and communication opportunities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's design was based on principles from the self-regulated and self-directed learning literature: materials grouped weekly and accessible at learners' convenience, varied material types for differing learning styles, end-of-material tests with visible scores for self-evaluation, and project-based and forum communication for active participation. The authors summarize that learners could "monitor, evaluate and receive feedback on their learning" in this learner-centered environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must be able to use technology effectively and develop a variety of materials to meet individual differences
- The instructor needs more time to give feedback as asynchronous communication opportunities increase
#### Constraints
- Designing a blended learning environment requires more time and effort than designing a traditional learning environment, and developing materials that encourage active participation is a major instructor problem

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- self-regulated learning
- self-directed learning
