# Provide overdose education and naloxone distribution universally to all clients in justice diversion addiction treatment, regardless of justice involvement or overdose history

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that overdose education and naloxone distribution (OEND) be built into justice diversion addiction treatment for every client rather than targeted to presumed high-risk subgroups. Because "OEND should be provided to all clients, regardless of pre-treatment overdose experience or justice involvement characteristics", screening-based targeting is unnecessary in this setting. The recommendation rests on the study's findings of high overdose exposure and low naloxone knowledge across all justice involvement classes and both genders.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A residential justice diversion addiction treatment setting serving people who use opioids, where clients can receive overdose education and naloxone
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single suburban Midwestern US facility; the authors note naloxone knowledge and overdose prevalence may not generalize outside this region because availability of harm reduction services varies geographically

### Target Learners
- Adults with opioid use in criminal justice diversion addiction treatment programs

### Learning Goals
- Recognizing opioid overdose and responding with naloxone
