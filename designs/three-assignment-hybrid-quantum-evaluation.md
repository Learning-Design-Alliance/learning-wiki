# Three-assignment evaluation scheme culminating in a group bin-packing optimization project on quantum hardware

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Student evaluation rests on three homework assignments: two individual assignments covering quantum computing basics and quantum-augmented K-means and SVM classification, and a group assignment covering all concepts required for developing hybrid quantum-classical applications. The group assignment targets bin-packing optimization on quantum hardware using QAOA or VQE, walking groups through preprocessing, QUBO transformation, Ising Hamiltonian transformation, encoding, solution, and analysis of the quantum solution against the classical one using metrics such as distance from optimum. Students may decline to implement a quantum algorithm in Assignment 2 if they justify the decision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of maximum three students
- Access to quantum backends and datasets provided by the lecturers
#### Constraints
- Assignment-based evaluation alone made it difficult to cover the topics of all lectures, especially the guest lectures

### Target Learners
- master's students in computer science

### Learning Goals
- designing hybrid quantum-classical applications
- combinatorial optimization with variational quantum algorithms
- encoding classical data into quantum data
