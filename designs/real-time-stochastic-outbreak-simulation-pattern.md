# Real-time stochastic outbreak simulation among course participants with realistic data reporting followed by dual-tradition analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The MMF pattern is a hands-on pedagogical design in which a stochastic outbreak is simulated among course participants themselves: infected individuals use random number generators to determine Poisson-distributed infectious contacts and Bernoulli-distributed symptomatic status, hand paper infection forms to peers, and report to simulated surveillance systems. Each outbreak generates two datasets: 'a realistic provider-based surveillance system that recorded symptomatic cases and time of symptom onset; and (2) an unrealistically accurate knowledge of the underlying transmission dynamics'. Participants then clean data, form research groups, and apply methods from both epidemiological traditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizers must set epidemiological parameters (initial infections, protective factors, R0, proportion symptomatic) suited to participant numbers and planned analyses
- A full roster of participants for tracking
- Brief training so participants can generate Poisson and Bernoulli random numbers
- Any software package that can generate Poisson and Bernoulli random variables
#### Constraints
- For course-credit use, the article recommends not initiating the outbreak until the course list is finalized and restricting access to the infection form so the epidemic stays confined to the closed population of course participants

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduates
- graduate students
- participants ranging from undergraduate students to professors

### Learning Goals
- understanding nonlinear stochastic epidemic dynamics
- study design
- bias and confounding
- survey design
- data cleaning and analysis
