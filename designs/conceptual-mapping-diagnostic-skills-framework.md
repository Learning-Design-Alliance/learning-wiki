# Conceptual mapping as a treatment for developing diagnostic and problem-solving skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Novak's conceptual mapping (based on Ausubel, Piaget, and Vygotsky) and on systems thinking, the article frames forcing in-depth analysis of technical problems through map construction as a way to develop diagnostic skill. The premise tested was that mapping the student's intentions during diagnosis would increase accuracy relative to expert process maps and make students 'more agile in their thinking regarding ill-defined problems.' Concept mapping is described as a graphical representation where nodes represent concepts and links represent relationships, integral to systems thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practice with ill-defined problems within a supportive and instructional environment, and knowledge of how to process information, form and test hypotheses, and make choices based on data
#### Constraints
- The experimental data did not support the conclusion that the treatment caused an increase in diagnostic performance; qualitative analysis found the majority of students treated the experience as a trivial academic exercise, seriously limiting their efforts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering and technology students (novice troubleshooters)

### Learning Goals
- diagnostic inference and root cause analysis
- systems thinking about technical problems
- metacognitive reasoning skills
