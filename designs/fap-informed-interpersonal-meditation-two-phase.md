# FAP-informed interpersonal meditation (FAP-IM): a two-phase intervention integrating intrapersonal mindfulness with interpersonal awareness

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
FAP-IM is a brief intervention that "integrates intrapersonal mindfulness meditation practices" with Benson's Relaxation Response in Phase 1 (body scan, breath attention, observing thoughts nonjudgmentally), followed by a Phase 2 in which participants "expand their awareness to include the others in the room" through an iterative back-and-forth between awareness of others and inner stimuli. Phase 2 draws on FAP concepts of awareness, courage, and behavioral cosmology, and ends with imaginal rehearsal of a small interpersonal risk and brief in-vivo small-group sharing. The article evaluates it in a pilot randomized trial against intrapersonal-only meditation and a control video.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A group room setting with other participants engaging in the same practice, a session interventionist, and baseline and post-course assessments
#### Constraints
- The intervention was brief (about one hour) and tested only in a pilot with non-treatment-seeking undergraduates, so findings may not generalize to other populations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in a lower level psychology class

### Learning Goals
- increase awareness of self and others
- reduce experiential avoidance
- increase connectedness and openness in interpersonal relationships
