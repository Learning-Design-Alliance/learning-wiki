# PIDD (SCID) infant high-fidelity simulation case with randomized student roles, standardized parent, and confederate support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A required, formative 15-minute simulation for groups of six second-year students in an SSH-accredited simulation center, featuring a high-fidelity infant mannequin presenting to an emergency department with fever, cough and inconsolability; a thorough parent interview divulges "recurrent infections (i.e., otitis media, pneumonia, rashes), chronic diarrhea, failure to thrive, and a maternal uncle's death in infancy". Three students actively manage the case (history, hypothesis-driven exam, initial management) while three observe, then roles swap for a second case; a nurse confederate and telephone consultation support struggling students. Faculty observe via one-way mirror and debrief with a standardized faculty guide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students with prior clinical skills preparation (exam, communication, EMT certification) to provide initial patient management
- Standardized parent actors trained by certified simulation faculty
- Faculty guides and pre-session faculty development meetings to standardize debriefing
#### Constraints
- Scheduled as an end-of-course formative exercise with no pre-work required
- Case deliberately leaves acute stabilization interventions outside students' taught knowledge

### Target Learners
- Second-year medical students in a basic science immunology/rheumatology course

### Learning Goals
- Recognizing primary immunodeficiency disease, linking immune system dysfunction to clinical manifestations, and initial patient management
