# Combine expository whole-group instruction with small-group active mentoring when teaching EBP to undergraduates

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The tested EBP program alternates instructional formats: early sessions used "expository methods with practice tasks to groups of 20–30 students", and later sessions used "active methods through mentoring to groups of 2–3 students" in which students carry out a review on a clinical question. This progression from guided instruction to mentored application is the program's core delivery design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions spread over 17 weeks with content on review question development, searching, study selection, data extraction, and synthesis
#### Constraints
- Delivered by only two instructors to fourth-year students, which the authors name as limits for curriculum-wide integration

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in their final year

### Learning Goals
- EBP knowledge and skills: formulating review questions, searching, appraising, extracting, and synthesizing evidence
