# STEM centers as informal learning spaces serving three communities of practice

## Description
The article's central framework designs campus STEM centers as specialized informal learning labs that "engage learners from three communities of practice: pre-college youth, undergraduates, and working professionals." The centers are positioned as "specialized labs for developing skills beyond the formal curriculum and closing the professional practices gap," with Tables 1 through 3 specifying learner background, audience purpose, proposed work, and learning purpose for each audience. The framework rests on an emancipatory, participatory theoretical stance aimed at increasing interest, confidence, and competency among underrepresented groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Higher education institutions need collaborative approaches to attract potential STEM candidates, with the education pipeline flowing into colleges and extending to the workforce
#### Constraints
- The formal college curriculum is described as having proven inadequate in closing the STEM workforce gap, motivating the informal design

### Target Learners
- pre-college youth ages 14-17 from fragile communities
- undergraduate female and minority students
- working professionals seeking STEM persistence

### Learning Goals
- STEM interest, confidence, and competency
- self-efficacy and STEM preparedness
- closing the professional practices gap
