# Constructivist-based flipped classroom design combining situational and collaborative learning for nursing skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article grounds its flipped classroom implementation in constructivism, which "emphasizes the important role of 'situation' and 'collaboration' in learning". Pre-class stages shift passive knowledge acquisition to videos, animations and presentations so learners construct meaning beforehand; classroom time is devoted to active, collaborative, problem-based activities such as case discussion, think-pair-share, and skills laboratory practice, with instructors acting as facilitators who correct misconceptions and give timely feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must engage with pre-class materials (videos, presentations, animations) before the lesson
- Classroom time must be organized for collaborative, active learning activities with instructor guidance
#### Constraints
- The article notes some prior studies found no statistically significant differences in certain skills

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- theoretical knowledge of vital signs, nutrition and drug administration
- psychomotor nursing skills (blood pressure measurement, blood glucose measurement, drug preparation, nasogastric tube insertion)
- persistence of knowledge and skills
