# Immersive flipped learning: merging flipped, collaborative, and immersive pedagogies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Immersive flipped learning is the article's organizing framework: students study pre-class materials ahead of time and engage in hands-on collaborative practice during synchronous online lessons hosted in a 2D Metaverse. The author describes it as "a novel effort to merge three pedagogies —flipped learning, collaborative learning, and immersive learning —to improve students' online participation and perceived learning outcomes." It addresses flipped-classroom challenges such as weak interaction in online settings, particularly in Japanese higher education where ICT integration has been slow.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A virtual environment supporting avatars, spatial audio/video, and breakout zones
- Pre-class materials shared ahead of synchronous sessions
- Collaborative tasks and digital tools for group work
#### Constraints
- Exploratory case study; no baseline group, which the author says affected generalizability
- Scalability and sustainability across populations and delivery formats not yet established

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in online or blended higher education
- second-language learners

### Learning Goals
- student online engagement
- perceived learning outcomes
- collaboration skills
