# Infrared-camera and interface-only radiative balance sequence for linking CO2 emissions to Earth's mean temperature

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A teaching sequence constructing the link between CO2 emissions and Earth's mean temperature for non-specialist lycée students, within the Model of Educational Reconstruction and an open-source structure. Its two central points, which the authors call « à notre connaissance inédits dans les approches existantes », are « l'interprétation d'expériences réalisées avec une caméra infrarouge d'une gamme spectrale permettant de voir l'émission et l'absorption du CO2 » and a radiative balance reasoned only at the interface between the Terre-atmosphère system and Espace.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An infrared camera whose spectral range allows CO2 emission and absorption to be seen; common school cameras (8–15 μm) do not overlap the CO2 absorption spectrum.
- A logical progression starting from infrared radiation detection, then solids, then gases, then radiative balances and temperature evolution.
#### Constraints
- Designed for non-specialist physics students with minimal conceptual prerequisites, which also makes it suitable for science outreach.
- Qualitative only: the article states that amplification phenomena lead to the need for a local description and quantification, deferred to a forthcoming article on climate-model construction.

### Target Learners
- Lycée general and vocational students, including non-specialists (Enseignement Scientifique)

### Learning Goals
- Construire le lien entre émissions de CO2 et élévation de la température moyenne de la Terre
