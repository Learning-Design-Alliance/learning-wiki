# A federated model for scaling chip-design education aligns common outcomes and shared infrastructure without enforcing identical curricula

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a federated model that aligns "common learning outcomes and practical design-to-silicon experience" without enforcing an identical curriculum across participating universities. Five European universities implement local Master's programs around a shared tapeout project and a common chip platform, following a "competence-centric rather than a one-size-fits-all approach" so course structures, regulations, and technical emphases remain local. The educational framework is organized as a matrix in which students progress "from basic to specialized chip-design skills" while theoretical and practical skill development complement each other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The universities define common base skills, especially the capability to develop a chip in practice, and suitable technical infrastructure must be in place.
#### Constraints
- The model does not enforce identical curricula; concrete course structures, regulations, and technical emphases remain local to each university.

### Target Learners
- Master's students in chip design and microelectronics

### Learning Goals
- taking a chip design from specification to tapeout and, where possible, testing it after manufacturing
