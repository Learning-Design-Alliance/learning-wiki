# National inter-university framework model for sustaining graduate education in a specialised discipline

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central organising model: a nationally coordinated master's programme jointly delivered by multiple universities and national research institutes, built on "a common curriculum, shared governance, coordinated mobility mechanisms, and access to research infrastructure distributed across multiple institutions". Unlike international mobility programmes, it is conceived "as a national strategy for preserving and strengthening graduate education in a highly specialised discipline through coordinated inter-institutional cooperation". Students enrol at one institution while the overall body is aggregated across partners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A formal partnership agreement defining responsibilities, review and amendment procedures, admission of new partners, and withdrawal procedures, with changes preserving the rights of enrolled students
#### Constraints
- The article notes limited regulatory provisions exist for nationally coordinated inter-university programmes, requiring careful interpretation of the existing framework

### Target Learners
- Master's students in physics preparing for research careers in high-energy physics

### Learning Goals
- Preparation for participation in large-scale international research collaborations such as CERN
