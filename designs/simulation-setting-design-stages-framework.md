# Simulation setting design framework: construction, application and assessment stages with briefing, intrassimulation and debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames health simulation design as "the structure adopted for its elaboration", in which settings are built through three stages — construction, application and assessment — and application comprises "briefing, intrassimulation, and debriefing". Briefing is the instruction moment of the case, intrassimulation implements the proposed simulation, and debriefing is when participants reflect on experiences, articulate theory and practice through critical thinking, and discuss aspects relevant to their practice. The study operationalized this framework in its CRA training and measured its perceived quality with the Simulation Design Scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well defined stages, clear objectives, prior planning, trained facilitators, and availability of material and technological resources
- Continuous assessment of settings, since all settings need constant adaptation even after testing
#### Constraints
- Many institutions lack tools to support the setting-construction stage, which may compromise effectiveness
- Excess of props can increase artificialism and result in participants blocking activities

### Target Learners
- Health professionals and students in continuing and professional education

### Learning Goals
- Clinical knowledge and team performance in cardiorespiratory resuscitation
