# Six-step workflow for fabricating patient-specific 3D-printed surgical phantoms from medical images

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a reusable production sequence for turning medical imaging into a training phantom: "The 3D printing process for phantom fabrication has multiple steps: (1) acquisition of high-quality computed tomography (CT) data on the anatomical structure to be modeled, (2) image processing to extract the region of interest from the anatomic structure, (3) 3D modeling from medical doctor needs, (4) quality assurance of the model to ensure its accuracy, (5) selection of the printing method and materials, and (6) printing of the phantom." Modeling and designing were the most time-consuming steps at 3–4 h, with total CT-to-STL time of approximately 5–7 h.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-quality thin-slice CT data (< 1 mm slice resolution), segmentation software, and clinician input on modeling needs
#### Constraints
- Modeling and designing were the most challenging steps owing to resolution, CT artifacts, and small infant anatomical structures

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees learning pediatric VATS

### Learning Goals
- fabrication of realistic patient-specific surgical training simulators
