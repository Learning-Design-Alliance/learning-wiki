# One-on-one 60-minute simulation-based mastery learning intervention for lumbar puncture and abdominal paracentesis using Peyton's four-step model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention was one-on-one, 60-minute SBML for each procedure delivered by one of three instructors, "employing Peyton's four-step model of procedure training as an educational framework". Training began with published procedure videos, then instructor demonstration with verbalised checklist steps, then participant practice on the simulator with direct, specific feedback. In accordance with mastery learning practices, participants practiced iteratively with targeted feedback on unachieved steps until they independently completed the entire procedure at the MPS (18/21 for LP, 21/25 for AP).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires trained instructors, procedural simulators usable for both training and assessment, published checklists with predetermined minimum passing scores (18 of 21 for LP, 21 of 25 for AP), and published procedure videos
#### Constraints
- The article reports a single episode of this training was insufficient to sustain skills for the majority of hospitalists over about 7 months in the setting of low procedural volumes

### Target Learners
- credentialed hospitalists who supervise trainees performing invasive bedside procedures

### Learning Goals
- technical competence in lumbar puncture and abdominal paracentesis to a minimum passing standard
