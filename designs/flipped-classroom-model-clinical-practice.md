# Flipped classroom model: pre-class preparation, in-class interaction, post-class consolidation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The flipped classroom is a blended learning approach that "reverse[s] traditional teaching by requiring students to prepare key concepts at home and engage in collaborative activities during class time". In the included studies, this typically meant students watched teaching videos or micro-lectures before class (often via WeChat), followed by in-class interactive Q&A, case discussion, and teaching rounds, with post-class summary and review. Teachers act as "observers and guides of activities" rather than lecturers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires students to prepare key concepts before class, typically via teaching videos or micro-lectures
#### Constraints
- Some students find pre-class preparation a burden and stressful; videos should be restricted to 20 minutes to be acceptable for most students

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate clinical medicine students in clinical practice

### Learning Goals
- Theoretical knowledge
- Practical clinical skills
