# Embodied metaphor: whole-body engagement with a metaphor supports learning of abstract science concepts beyond passive engagement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article frames embodied metaphors as a type of active learning especially well-suited to abstract neuroscience topics. While metaphors such as the "lock and key" metaphor for receptor activity are common, the article argues that "whole-body engagement in a learning environment supports and improves learning beyond what is observed through passive engagement with a metaphor" — e.g., acting out opening a lock with a key rather than passively hearing the metaphor. Students and foam projectiles take on roles such as soma, axon hillock, presynaptic axon terminal, post-synaptic cell, and metabotropic and ionotropic receptors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals
