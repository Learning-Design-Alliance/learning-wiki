# Four-week guided rubric internalization sequence: sample analysis, modeling, peer scoring, and justified judgment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The treatment embedded the rubric in a staged sequence across four weeks: clarifying rubric vocabulary, analyzing exemplary and defective samples with yes-no checklist questions, teacher modeling of scoring on imperfect models, then students analyzing, judging and scoring samples and peers' drafts while justifying their scores. This progression is presented as how students move from seeing criteria to internalizing them and applying them to their own writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidance from an expert teacher and repeated work with real writing samples for each rubric trait
#### Constraints
- The analysis sessions were conducted in students' mother tongue; the treatment covered two essay types over four weeks in one preparatory program

### Target Learners
- university EFL preparatory-program students aged 18-20

### Learning Goals
- internalizing criteria of good writing and applying them in compare-and-contrast and cause-and-effect essays
