# Use authentic, locally-sourced clinical materials to bridge textbook knowledge and clinical application

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central design choice was incorporating "real, de-identified prescriptions" sourced from the university's educational pharmacies, with a strict de-identification protocol removing all patient and physician identifiers. Students overwhelmingly perceived these authentic materials as effective for engagement (93.5%) and learning (90.3%), reporting they helped "bridge the gap between textbook knowledge and clinical application." The authors present this as enhancing ecological validity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A source of real, de-identified prescriptions and an approved de-identification protocol
- Faculty review and tiering of prescriptions by difficulty
#### Constraints
- Perceptions only; objective skill gains were not measured in this pilot

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students

### Learning Goals
- rational prescribing
- connecting pharmacotherapy knowledge to clinical practice
