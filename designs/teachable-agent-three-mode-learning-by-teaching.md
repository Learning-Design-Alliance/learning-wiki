# Learning-by-teaching with a teachable agent in three successive pedagogical modes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Magical Garden implements the learning-by-teaching pedagogy: the child instructs a digital tutee (the teachable agent) and learns by doing so. Each scenario is, in the article's words, "always repeated in three successive pedagogical modes": the child practices the task alone, then demonstrates it to the watching TA, then supervises the TA's attempt, accepting or correcting the TA's proposal with happy/unhappy smileys. The design yields two stated pedagogical gains: repeated, non-repetitive task practice, and the chance to evaluate someone else performing the task, which is more feasible for young children than reflecting on their own actions under cognitive load.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The child must be able to ascribe some knowledge to the TA that can differ from and be influenced by their own, and perceive the TA's behavior as goal-directed for meaningful instruction
#### Constraints
- Full benefit requires understanding that the TA has agency; the article questions whether preschoolers' less developed theory of mind supports this

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged 4–6

### Learning Goals
- early mathematics and number sense within the number range 1–9
