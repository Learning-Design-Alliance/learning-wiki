# Dual-hypothesis framework: playful interaction sustains attention while cognitive ergonomics reduce counter-arguing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's guiding psychological framework combines three research strands: attention/cognitive-effort research, Self-Determination Theory motivation, and entertainment-education work on counter-arguing. It converges on a dual hypothesis: "playful interaction enhances attentional engagement and motivational readiness", while "affective immersion and cognitive ergonomics reduce counter-arguing and facilitate access to abstract or nonlinear concepts". Games are treated not merely as motivational amplifiers but as environments structuring perception, action and reasoning through rules, feedback loops and goal hierarchies that lower the cognitive cost of problem access. The authors explicitly adopted this framework before designing their videogames.
