# LabRadar Doppler radar with Airsoft launcher apparatus for student drag-coefficient measurements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An affordable (~$600 US) Doppler radar device (LabRadar) paired with a spring-propelled plastic Airsoft launcher, a Kestrel 4500 weather meter, an electronic balance, and a dial caliper, used to measure drag coefficients of free-flying plastic spheres. The device triggers on Doppler detection and displays velocity at 3.05 m intervals from 0 to 15.24 m, and raw data files of "Doppler velocity vs. distance and time" can be downloaded per shot via USB for spreadsheet computation of Cd per shot.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A room of at least 12.2 m is needed because the sphere does not enter the radar beam until 3.05–6.10 m downrange, so near and far velocities can be measured over enough of a span to compute drag coefficients
#### Constraints
- An outdoor setting reduces radar reflections but an indoor setting better eliminates wind effects
- Firearms are poorly suited for classroom use due to safety considerations, motivating Airsoft projectiles

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory courses

### Learning Goals
- measuring drag coefficients
- connecting projectile-motion theory with experiment including air resistance
