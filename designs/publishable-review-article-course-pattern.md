# Course pattern: shared table of contents, student-owned sections, in-class defence presentations, and multi-round peer review culminating in publication

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A whole-course design in which students first find existing reviews, then collectively define a table of contents whose discussion the author calls the most valuable educational aspect for inducing active in-class participation. Each section is placed under the authority of one student, who elaborates its content and defends it in-class "by means of a presentation. It is a little bit like a research defence". Peer review then proceeds through in-class discussion, review by two randomly chosen students, and electronic commenting and editing, ending in journal submission.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A hot research topic with a genuine gap in the existing review literature
- Quick convergence on a TOC while valuing all students' inputs, since they will spend considerable time with the included topics
#### Constraints
- Designed for a modest enrollment in a single-instructor course setting; the author notes original laboratory research is difficult to conceive for large student enrollment

### Target Learners
- first-year master's students in physics

### Learning Goals
- learning about current or recent research
- academic writing
- presentation and discussion skills
