# Nine-month structured communication skills program for healthcare professionals (cognitive, modeling and role-playing components)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A postgraduate program taught in 2008 and 2009 with identical content, faculty, structure and sequence, targeting basic communication skills in its first three months. Classes met twice weekly for five hours and combined "theoretical presentations of the materials, role-modeling through video viewing and discussion, and role-playing in small groups of peers (six or seven students), with feedback by both peers and faculty". Role-playing represented "about 67 percent of the course load" and used scripts based on real faculty and student cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The same faculty, structure and sequence across applications; small peer groups grouped by professional background to maximize heterogeneity, each tutored by the same two faculty members throughout
#### Constraints
- The program's final three-month period has a lesser emphasis on practice, per the article

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals (physicians, nurses, clinical psychologists, physiotherapists, speech therapists) with a mean of approximately three years of practice

### Learning Goals
- Basic and advanced clinical communication skills, self-awareness, and communication in special situations
