# Video-annotation homework plus small-group seminar with physician tutor as an expansion of simulation-based communication training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern expands simulation-based communication skills training with a structured video-reflection cycle: after a first training session with simulated patients, each student labels four clips (two of their own, two of a predefined partner's) via an online annotation tool, marking one good and one improvable sequence with comments. Groups of four then select clips for discussion, and groups of eight meet a physician tutor for a two-hour seminar. The article states "Each student was asked to label four clips (two of their own, and two of their partner's) electronically via the online annotation tool". The annotations form the basis of the seminar discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to recorded encounter videos via a learning management system with an online annotation tool
- A predefined annotation partner different from the training-session partner
- A trained physician tutor for each group of eight students
#### Constraints
- Students were given the option to opt out of recording their encounters on legal advice, with alternative tasks available
- The evaluation measured perceptions only, not skill improvement or real-life transfer

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- communication skills in challenging consultations
- reflective practice on communication microbehaviours and holistic skills
