# Three-reason account of computer models as suitable physics learning environments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the conclusion the authors argue for computer models as physics learning environments on three grounds: "1) to visualize physics through multiple representations especially for invisible and very large scale concepts 2) ease of theory generation from real-life, “annoyance-free” and accurate simulations, 3) mathematical analysis & modeling to deepen inquiry." The account frames simulations as substitutes for impossible or dangerous real experiments, such as sending students into outer space, positioning students to role play as scientists interacting with inquiry-designed physics-equations-modeled phenomena.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-facilitated lessons by teachers, with computer models used as referents in classroom inquiry
#### Constraints
- Motivated by phenomena that are difficult, costly or dangerous to study in real life, such as the solar system scale

### Target Learners
- Secondary and junior college physics students

### Learning Goals
- Physics inquiry: visualizing, generating and testing theory, and mathematical modeling of gravitational phenomena
