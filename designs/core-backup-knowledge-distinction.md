# Core versus backup knowledge distinction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article divides the medical curriculum's total body of knowledge into two categories with different assessment formats. Core knowledge is what every graduate must reproduce without references and is assessed with closed-book questions, whereas backup knowledge is what a graduate should be able to "understand and apply properly with the use of reference sources, if necessary", assessed with open-book questions. In the PBLob curriculum each knowledge test has a closed and an open-book component, with the open-book part completed after closed-book answers are handed in.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The curriculum must define which knowledge is core and which is backup, and assess core knowledge with closed-book questions and backup knowledge with open-book questions
#### Constraints
- In the study, the content of core and backup knowledge was determined by one medical school; teachers of other schools might classify questions differently

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- long-term retention of functional medical knowledge
