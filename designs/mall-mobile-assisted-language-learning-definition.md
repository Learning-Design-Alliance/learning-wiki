# Mobile Assisted Language Learning (MALL) as learning that occurs any time and any place via mobile technology

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
MALL is a teaching method that uses mobile phone technology to stress learner centeredness and autonomy. The article defines mobile learning, citing O'Malley et al. (2003), as "any kind of learning that takes place when the learner is not in a fixed state" and, citing Geddes (2004), as learning "which takes place at any time and in any place," extending teaching beyond classroom walls. A key feature is that students feel responsible for their own learning. The study operationalises MALL through SMS delivery of vocabulary items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have access to mobile phone technology capable of receiving SMS messages
#### Constraints
- The study used mobile learning in a nonreciprocal way, with materials pushed to learners rather than exchanged

### Target Learners
- university freshmen learning English as a foreign language

### Learning Goals
- academic vocabulary learning and long-term retention
