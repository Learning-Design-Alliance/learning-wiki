# Three-phase situation awareness educational programme (web module, reflective discussion, SA-focused debriefing) added to in situ simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention was a two-hour, three-phase educational programme on situation awareness layered onto standard in situ simulation training with debriefing. Phase I was a one-hour web-based programme with video lectures, reflective and multiple-choice questions; phase II was a one-hour instructor-facilitated reflective discussion; phase III was a reflective debriefing after case 1 focused on SA. The article states "the intervention group were given a web-based educational programme (phase I), and two reflection sessions that focused on SA (phase II and phase III)". The control group received the same simulation cases and debriefing without the SA-focused components.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors delivering the intervention received a one-day educational session including SA and theories of didactics and experiential learning before session two
#### Constraints
- The programme was evaluated in interprofessional ICU teams in in situ simulation at two Swedish hospitals; effects on measured SA (SAGAT) were not detected

### Target Learners
- interprofessional intensive care teams (enrolled nurses, critical care nurses, physicians)

### Learning Goals
- situation awareness (perception, comprehension, projection)
- non-technical skills and team performance in critical care
