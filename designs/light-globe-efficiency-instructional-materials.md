# Light-globe energy efficiency problem-solving booklets and interactive explicit instruction slides

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The experiments used problem-solving booklets in which learners compute each globe's efficiency as a proportion of electrical energy taken in and decide which globe is the most "'energy saving'", using calculators. The design deliberately met productive failure criteria: questions avoided terms like 'power' and 'efficiency', drew on prior knowledge, and allowed multiple solution attempts. PowerPoint slides supported interactive explicit instruction addressing a common incorrect solution method (choosing the globe giving out most light energy).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must be intelligible to uninstructed learners, avoiding technical terminology, and represent an appropriate level of challenge.
#### Constraints
- Experiment 2's booklets added a conservation-of-energy step, raising the element count from eight to twelve interacting elements.

### Target Learners
- Year 5 primary school students (~10 years old) with no prior instruction in energy conservation

### Learning Goals
- Calculating energy efficiency and applying conservation of energy
