# Early-research-exposure course design built on digital-native conditions: social-media content hub, open-network access, computational tools, and virtualized labs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course design for first-year physics students deliberately leveraged students' status as digital natives through four design characteristics: a blog and social networks (Twitter, an open Facebook group) as the content and discussion center; incentivized use of online data sources during discussions and assessments; training in research-grade computational tools (Python, Tracker, LaTeX with collaborative writing in WriteLaTeX); and computer labs delivered via a free VirtualBox virtual machine running Xubuntu 14.04 on a 16 GB USB drive for equity and scalability. All course materials were licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Equity and scalability required not depending on students owning laptops, achieved by mounting the virtual lab in university computer rooms and using free, open software requiring no license purchases
#### Constraints
- Some online-accessible tools are more limited than their installed versions, as the article notes for TIC-based lab resources

### Target Learners
- first-year physics undergraduates (digital natives) at Universidad Industrial de Santander

### Learning Goals
- basic physics concepts and techniques
- computational data analysis and simulation skills
- motivation to continue in physics and research
