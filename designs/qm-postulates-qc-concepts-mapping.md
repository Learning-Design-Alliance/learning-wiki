# Correspondence mapping between quantum mechanics postulates and quantum computing concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course is organized around a mapping, illustrated in Table I, that "provides a straightforward mapping to facilitate understanding" between the postulates of quantum mechanics and quantum computing concepts: the wavefunction postulate corresponds to the qubit, the unitary-evolution postulate corresponds to quantum logic gates, and the measurement postulate corresponds to the readout process. This mapping underpins the course's strategy of teaching computing concepts first and showing afterwards how they are underpinned by quantum mechanical principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors pair each quantum mechanics postulate (state, evolution, measurement) with its quantum computing counterpart (qubit, gate, readout) when introducing the concepts.
#### Constraints
- The mapping covers the state, evolution, and measurement postulates as illustrated in Table I; other postulates are not mapped in the article.

### Target Learners
- high school students with limited computer science background

### Learning Goals
- understand how qubits, gates, and measurement are underpinned by quantum mechanical principles
- link theoretical concepts with practical uses
