# Metaverse-based clinical assessment platforms augmented by LLMs offer a promising complementary approach to conventional OSCEs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a framework in which metaverse environments with LLM-driven virtual patients serve as a complementary assessment tool rather than a replacement for OSCEs. It is grounded in expert consensus that conventional OSCEs face structural constraints including examiner variability, resource demands, and limited scenario diversity. The framework positions metaverse simulation as addressing standardization, scalability, and reproducibility gaps while retaining in-person formats for tactile and affective competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires integration of LLMs with predefined, expert-reviewed case data to ensure clinical accuracy and scenario consistency
- Requires high-performance computing equipment and stable internet connections
#### Constraints
- Cannot replicate procedures requiring tactile or haptic feedback such as palpation and percussion
- Emotional expressiveness of avatar patients is limited, reducing realism in empathy-focused encounters
- Study was exploratory and did not establish educational efficacy, validity, or feasibility in summative high-stakes contexts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- clinical competence assessment
- communication skills
- diagnostic reasoning
