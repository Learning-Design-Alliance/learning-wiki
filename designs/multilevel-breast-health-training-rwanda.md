# Multilevel breast health training intervention for community health workers, health center nurses, and hospital clinicians in rural Rwanda

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention trained three levels of Rwanda's health system simultaneously. Community health workers learned breast cancer symptoms and messaging that community members should visit their health center for any breast symptom. Nurses "were taught about signs and symptoms of benign breast disease and cancer and how to perform a CBE" and received "simple clinical algorithms for managing breast symptoms", plus ongoing mentorship from a hospital-based nurse-midwife. Hospital clinicians were trained in diagnostic breast ultrasound and ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy, and weekly breast clinics were launched.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must reach all nurses at intervention health centers and all community health workers serving their catchment sectors
- Ongoing mentorship and support from a hospital-based nurse-midwife trained in breast health
- Hospital-level diagnostic capacity, including clinicians trained in ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy and onsite pathology services
#### Constraints
- The authors state their experience "may not be generalizable to other districts in Rwanda" because Burera patients already had shorter delays before the intervention
- Programs must ensure access to timely, affordable, and high-quality treatment of all patients diagnosed with cancer

### Target Learners
- community health workers
- health center nurses
- hospital clinicians in a rural district health system

### Learning Goals
- recognize symptoms of benign and malignant breast disease
- perform clinical breast examination
- triage and refer patients with concerning breast findings
- perform diagnostic breast ultrasound and ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy
