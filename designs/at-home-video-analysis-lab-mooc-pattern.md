# At-home laboratory cycle combining smartphone video capture, open-source motion tracking, computational modeling, peer evaluation of video lab reports

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
YWYL implements a laboratory component in a MOOC without special equipment: every other week (five total labs) students capture video of real-world objects with smartphones, analyze motion with open-source tracking software, build VPython computational models to compare with their observations, and submit approximately 5-minute video lab reports. In the following week each student is randomly assigned five peers' reports to evaluate with an instructor-developed rubric. The labs are borrowed directly from the on-campus mechanics course and account for 65% of the course score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional videos introducing installation and use of the required software (motion tracking and VPython)
- TA-moderated online discussion forums as a proximal substitute for on-campus social interaction
#### Constraints
- Lacks a technical solution for deploying clicker questions effectively; students can simply skip embedded questions
- No common meeting environment, limiting Peer Instruction; no formal mechanism for the on-campus presentation sessions

### Target Learners
- MOOC students taking introductory calculus-based mechanics, including a special Georgia Tech physics section earning course credit

### Learning Goals
- Introductory mechanics content (Newton's laws, work and energy, rotational motion)
- Scientific practices such as making models, using computation, and comparing models and data
