# Acute simulation and life support course: a multi-station high-fidelity simulation for pre-foundation assistantship students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A half-day, four-station high-fidelity simulation course (Status Epilepticus, NSTEMI, IECOPD, Sepsis with AKI and hyperkalaemia) delivered during the pre-foundation assistantship at Chelsea and Westminster Hospital. Students in groups of five to six rotate through scenarios with pre-allocated roles, followed by a 15-minute debrief; scenarios end in cardiac arrest prompting ALS initiation. The course used "a pre-course handbook which included the presentation, investigation and management plan for 20 common acute conditions" derived from a local audit of admissions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty and simulated patients underwent pre-course training including documents to review and a 30-minute briefing
- Learning objectives aligned with the GMC's Outcomes for Graduates
#### Constraints
- CPR manikins with no real-time feedback lowered physical fidelity during ALS management, though psychological fidelity was rated high
- Students were not graded during the scenarios

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students on pre-foundation assistantship placements

### Learning Goals
- assess and manage an unwell patient using the ABCDE approach
- escalate and handover using SBAR
- initiate the Advanced Life Support algorithm
- recognise the effect of human factors on clinical ability
