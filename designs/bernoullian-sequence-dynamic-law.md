# The bernoullian sequence: introducing the dynamic law via motion as a succession of states of rest

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A teaching sequence for introductory physics in which the dynamic law is deduced rather than postulated. Students first learn the laws of rest (law of the lever, principle of virtual velocities), then define motion as "a succession of states of rest", illustrated by a mass-spring-mass oscillator representing an elastic collision. Applying the equilibrium law at each frozen instant shows the push or pull of the spring on the masses is F = ∆(mv)/∆t. The article presents it as an alternative to the newtonian and machian sequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work with the law of the lever and the principle of virtual velocities before the dynamic law is introduced
- A simulation or animation of the mass-spring-mass oscillator to freeze instants of the motion
#### Constraints
- The sequence only produces the law in one dimension
- It does not intend to exhaust all there is in the law of motion and is not self-sufficient; some conceptions may need separate work

### Target Learners
- students in introductory physics courses

### Learning Goals
- understanding the dynamic law (Newton's second law) and the meaning of its constitutive terms and functional form
