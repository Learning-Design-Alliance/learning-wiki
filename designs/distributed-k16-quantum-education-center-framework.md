# Distributed K-16 national center framework for quantum education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's organizing framework for scaling quantum education: a geographically and institutionally distributed national center or hub focused on K-12 through undergraduate (K-16) QISE education. "Structurally, the workshop attendees envisioned a center as both geographically and institutionally distributed," potentially with "regional clusters that link K-12, community colleges and universities, local and national industry, and the public." Clusters connect across common themes such as professional development and curricula, so national infrastructure scaffolded by local expertise can accelerate collaborative solutions adapted to different geographic and institutional needs. The framework defines quantum education as formal and informal learning opportunities from K-16 through graduate school, one aspect of broader workforce development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Investment at center scale, defined by the paper as greater than $5M per year, incorporating experts from multiple institutions
- Connections to graduate education, technical training, and employers even while focusing on K-16
#### Constraints
- The article states a center focused on K-16 should limit activities in other areas (e.g., master's programs, technical reskilling) to remain manageable and effective
- Findings are tailored to the US context, though the framework is offered as adaptable to other global contexts

### Target Learners
- K-12 students (ages 5-18)
- 2-year and 4-year undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- Quantum information science and engineering content knowledge
- Awareness of and pathways into quantum careers
