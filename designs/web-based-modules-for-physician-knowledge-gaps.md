# Use structured web-based training modules to address physician knowledge gaps in chronic disease management

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article concludes that "Web-based training can be effective in educating physicians on CKD-related issues", highlighting internet-based education's potential advantages of ease of access and use, interaction capability, and lower cost. The module paired a pre-test, scenario-based didactic content accessible only after the pre-test, and a post-test, producing significant knowledge gains. The authors frame this as a feasible approach given increasing scrutiny of resident work-hours and the need for flexibility in the timing of educational interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured module with pre-test, didactic section, and post-test; content developed from published literature and clinical guidelines and reviewed annually for accuracy
#### Constraints
- Studies assessing durability of knowledge from internet-based activities have shown mixed results; whether knowledge gained leads to improved care of CKD patients needs further study; the study was retrospective with no comparative control group

### Target Learners
- U.S. internal medicine residents and physicians-in-training

### Learning Goals
- Recognition and management of chronic kidney disease
