# Adaptive Optics Workbench activity: three engineering-challenge stations on a working AO system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The final AMSC inquiry is a series of three engineering challenge stations built around an adaptive optics system, whose stated objectives include to "Develop systems thinking by recognizing that a complex tool can be broken down into simpler components that work together as shown by block diagrams" and to troubleshoot, recognize trade-offs, and use feedback concepts. Station 1 has students measure focal-point deviation and invert measurements to deduce a hidden mirror's shape (an analogy to wavefront sensing); Station 2 asks students to improve an aberrated image via stopping down or phase-conjugate correction; Station 3 has students trace the optical path of a working AO workbench and produce a block diagram.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AO workstation with wavefront sensor, computer and deformable mirror
- Ray boxes, lenses and low-cost optical materials for the challenge stations
- Prior student practice drawing ray diagrams from earlier inquiries
#### Constraints
- Real-world AO systems are complex and use expensive components (∼10-30k); the course version is an introductory-level demonstrator

### Target Learners
- early-undergraduate STEM students preparing for technical internships

### Learning Goals
- systems thinking, troubleshooting, experimental design, and understanding of adaptive optics concepts such as wavefront sensing, feedback and phase conjugation
