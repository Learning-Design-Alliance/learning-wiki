# Self-directed learning operationalized as a community of practice with teacher coaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes SDL as organized "in a community of practice involving teachers and students" in which responsibility for learning outcomes is shared. Students identify their own learning needs and design learning activities within pre-defined competence outcomes, while the teacher acts as challenger, coach providing demand-oriented performance feedback, and monitor of learning progress. This contrasts with instruction-based learning where the program is structured week by week and the teacher leads monitoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-defined minimum competence outcomes and assessment criteria shared across conditions
- Teachers available to coach, challenge, and monitor student learning progress
#### Constraints
- The article notes SDL asks for high levels of reflective skills and metacognitive learning, and many students develop faulty mental models of how they learn

### Target Learners
- second-year bachelor physiotherapy students

### Learning Goals
- self-regulated learning
- physiotherapy knowledge and clinical performance
- physical therapy self-efficacy
