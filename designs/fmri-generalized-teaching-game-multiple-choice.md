# fMRI-adapted generalized teaching game with multiple-choice questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A scanner task adapting classic teaching games such as the rectangle game to fMRI constraints. Teachers study a question of four 6 × 6 pixel drawings, then provide up to three examples by revealing light blue squares of the correct answer on a canvas, see the example from the learner's perspective, and rate how likely learners are to answer correctly. Questions were "designed to be sufficiently varied to prevent teachers from learning a simple strategy that would generalize across target concepts". Behavioral data, task code, and analysis code were deposited on Open Science Framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be told their examples will be shown to future learners, and the question set must vary enough to prevent a cached rule such as touching opposite corners
#### Constraints
- Only positive examples contained within the target concept can be revealed, analogous to the constraint in past teaching games

### Target Learners
- adult teachers scanned with fMRI
- adult online learners tested on the provided examples

### Learning Goals
- teaching abstract multiple-choice concepts through selected examples
