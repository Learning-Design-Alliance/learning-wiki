# Embedding EDI reflection essays as graded weekly homework in a majors course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curricular pattern in which weekly homework includes short reflection essays on non-technical topics important to physicists, motivated by a first-day APS News activity and framed by a "Practicing Professionalism" course theme. Essays are graded pass/fail on engagement with pre-essay content, count as 5% of the course grade, and one problem was removed from each homework to manage workload. The article reports this fostered a broader physics identity among students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course framing that makes room for non-technical topics, such as the Practicing Professionalism theme
- Guiding questions and pre-essay readings to anchor each prompt
- Instructor time to read weekly essays and provide feedback
#### Constraints
- At Carleton this replaced one homework problem per week, a modest cost of time dedicated to practicing traditional content

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level physics majors in a ~30-person course

### Learning Goals
- examining what it means to be a physicist and do physics
- responding to EDI-related issues of interest to professional physicists
