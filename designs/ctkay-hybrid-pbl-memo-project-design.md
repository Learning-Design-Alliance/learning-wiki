# Hybrid PBL project design: authentic company problems delivered as four memos with driving questions, integrated into selected syllabus topics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors embedded a partial PBL project in the Financial Accounting Principles course using problems faced by a fast-growing halal food manufacturing company, delivered as four entry-document memos from the Finance Manager to the Assistant Accountant. They state the problems "helped to facilitate the self-directed learning of six of the ten topics found in the syllabus", with driving questions designed to help students identify what they needed to learn. Authenticity was ensured by interviewing the CEO and visiting the plant. The project contributed 30% of course marks, assessed with rubrics, written reports, presentations, logs and peer evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Driving questions designed before project activities to help students identify what they needed to learn
- Relevant supporting documents (invoices, bills, receipts, vouchers) attached to each memo
- Assessment rubrics, student logs and an instructor's manual as part of the documented project
#### Constraints
- The problems facilitated self-directed learning for only six of the ten syllabus topics; the rest remained lecture and discussion or problem solving

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate Accounting, Finance and Banking students

### Learning Goals
- recording transactions and preparing financial statements
- interpreting financial information for business decisions
- teamwork, communication and presentation skills
