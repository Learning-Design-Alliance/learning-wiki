# Combined PBL-MDT-flipped classroom intervention for an acute coronary syndrome clerkship

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A case-based teaching intervention for acute coronary syndrome that layers three methods: pre-class self-study of lecture videos and materials via an online platform, small-group problem-based learning, and simulated multidisciplinary team discussion. "The students were divided into groups of 5 to 6 members, with one student acting as the group leader. Each member took on the role of a doctor from different specialties" such as cardiology, critical care, respiratory medicine, ultrasound, and pharmacy, and the group simulated an MDT discussion of key ACS issues. The PBL component was "a hybrid model adapted from the classical McMaster model" with a two-session format of initial case discussion, independent research, and a follow-up resolution session. Two 120-minute sessions were conducted during a two-week clinical internship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have completed foundational courses such as Human Anatomy, Physiology, Pathophysiology, Pharmacology, and Diagnostics before the intervention
- A supervising teacher guides the clerkship discussions and evaluates group reports
#### Constraints
- The study reports only short-term (two-week) assessment; long-term retention was not measured
- The intervention bundles MDT, PBL, and flipped classroom simultaneously, so MDT's individual contribution could not be isolated

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate clinical medicine students aged 20-21 in clinical clerkships

### Learning Goals
- Theoretical knowledge of acute coronary syndrome
- Clinical skills and clinical reasoning
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and self-directed learning
