# Project-based empathy course pattern: interview a person from a different background and produce a reflective video artifact

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A semester-long course pattern in which student groups plan, conduct and record an interview with a person whose background differs from their own, then produce a video artifact including the interviewee's introduction, vignettes, and the group's reflection on the empathy they shared. The article states "A successful project-based learning assignment concludes with a tangible artifact", and videos were chosen because they "enable one to capture non-verbal signs the interviewee shows". Lectures on empathy concepts and communication skills ran alongside the project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of 5–6 students who contact the interviewee and gain permission themselves
- Interviewees readily approachable for students with no prior interviewing experience
- Weekly guidelines, formative feedback and instructor consultations as scaffolding
#### Constraints
- The course did not explicitly touch upon clinical empathy, targeting general empathic competencies in the pre-clinical phase instead

### Target Learners
- Year 2 pre-clinical medical students in a six-year program

### Learning Goals
- general empathic abilities
- empathic communication skills
- collaboration and self-directed teamwork
