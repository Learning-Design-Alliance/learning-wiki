# Flipped classroom as a blended, student-centered instructional model expanding active learning into class time

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines the flipped classroom as a model in which "students study instructional materials before class, typically online lectures, and apply what they learned in in-class activities". It is generally understood through blended learning, and its essential point is that "active learning activities are expanded", with in-class reciprocal actions distinguishing it from traditional teacher-centered lecture models. Instructors take on roles as curricular designers, instructors, and media developers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must engage with pre-class video or audio instructional materials, and in-class time must feature interactive student-centered activities, per the article's inclusion criteria for studies of the approach.
#### Constraints
- Implementation barriers the article names include instructor hesitancy to adopt new approaches and students' diverse learning needs; effectiveness depends in large part on whether students actually use the available pre-class time effectively.
