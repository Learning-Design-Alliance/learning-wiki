# Modularized PBL+AI framework for biomedical engineering education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A four-module instructional architecture that embeds generative AI inside problem-based learning for biomedical AI courses. The article states that "The design positions GenAI as a knowledge summarization module that accelerates baseline learning and supports code optimization, while preserving the role of PBL in fostering innovation and synthesis." The modules are Problem Formation (authentic biomedical briefs with curated deidentified datasets), Knowledge Inquiry Supported by AI, Problem-Solving (prototyping with team-instrumented debugging), and Presentation (reports, demos, peer review). AI is framed as a scaffold and baseline assembler of existing knowledge rather than a shortcut to solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Biomedical problem briefs anchored in real-world clinical or research scenarios with curated, deidentified datasets
- Institutionally approved AI tools that have undergone security and privacy review
- Instructor and TA facilitation with formative feedback at milestone stages
#### Constraints
- The article notes traditional PBL constraints historically limited activities to theoretical planning or preliminary prototypes; the framework addresses this with institutional compute infrastructure (PACE, industry partners) and standardized access policies

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate biomedical engineering students
- interdisciplinary teams balancing ML/AI experience, biomedical knowledge, and programming proficiency

### Learning Goals
- biomedical AI concepts and technical depth
- critical thinking and literature critique
- teamwork and scientific communication
- ethical, regulatory, and clinical awareness
