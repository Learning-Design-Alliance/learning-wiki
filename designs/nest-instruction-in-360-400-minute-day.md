# Nest a full 300-minute instructional program within a school day of 360-400 minutes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report argues that instructional time policy must build in realistic room for the human and operational demands of elementary classrooms rather than a maximally compressed schedule. Its observations showed schools operating "a highly compressed schedule" in which non-instructional demands spill into officially allotted instruction time. It concludes that a 300-minutes-a-day instructional program must be nested within a school day of 360-400 minutes, depending on lunch organization and whether recess is restored.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School day length must be extended beyond the current 330-minute closed campus schedule to absorb operational demands without eroding instruction.
#### Constraints
- The report notes Chicago's largely disadvantaged schools are asked to make achievement gains in less time than more advantaged surrounding systems; new charter schools chose 60-90 minutes longer days.

### Target Learners
- Chicago Public Schools elementary students

### Learning Goals
- Increasing delivered instructional time across the full academic program
