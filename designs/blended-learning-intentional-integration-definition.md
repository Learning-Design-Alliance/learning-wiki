# Blended learning as intentional integration of online and face-to-face learning opportunities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review adopts Garrison and Kanuka's definition of blended learning as "the effective and intentional integration of online and face-to-face learning opportunities". It uses this framing to organise technology-based teaching for health students' stroke clinical skills, noting blended approaches in clinical placements have involved videoconferencing, blogging, chat rooms, social media and web-based resources. The review proposes a blended-learning approach could support student-resourced rural stroke recovery services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Technology-based learning activities must be deliverable in less than 12 hours, synchronously or asynchronously, within a stroke-related clinical placement
- Design should align with teaching and learning theories and defined student learning outcomes
#### Constraints
- Challenges are well-documented, predominantly the availability of information technology skills and infrastructure, particularly in under-resourced and rural locations

### Target Learners
- Health students (medical, nursing, physiotherapy and allied health) on stroke-related clinical placements

### Learning Goals
- Clinical skills for stroke recovery, including assessment, treatment planning, and communication with people with aphasia
