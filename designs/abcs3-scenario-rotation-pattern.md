# Rotation-through-stations design: small groups cycle through all simulation scenarios in timed rounds

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The seminar's core structural pattern is a timed rotation: the 60 full participants were "divided into 10 groups (I-X) of six people", each group starting at a different scenario, and "after 10 cycles of rotation (and 10 repeats of each scenario by different groups), all groups (I to X) had attended all the scenarios (Sc1 to Sc10)". Each 30-minute scenario room held six participants and two instructors, with an electronic alarm signalling rotation. This lets a large cohort experience every scenario with hands-on roles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough parallel scenario rooms and instructor pairs to run all scenarios simultaneously
- Strict 30-minute time limits enforced by instructors and an alarm signal
#### Constraints
- Requires a full-category cohort size that divides evenly into groups matching the number of scenarios; observers could not interact and only watched via live-stream

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students working in small teams

### Learning Goals
- team-based diagnostic and therapeutic decision-making under time pressure
- hands-on procedural skills practice
