# Cardiac cycle dramatization: students role-play cells and components of a functioning heart

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An active-learning pattern in which each student physically embodies one component of the cardiac cycle — myocytes, sinoatrial node, atrioventricular node, valve leaflets, red blood cells, or neurotransmitters — with roles and receptors marked by signs, arranged in a figure '8' representing atrium and ventricle. Students act out depolarization, contraction, blood flow, valve closure as a clap simulating heart sounds, and staged scenarios of sympathetic or parasympathetic activity demonstrating inotropy and chronotropy. The article used it to teach cardiac cycle dynamics to first-year medical students, with emphasis on exercise responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor and an assistant facilitate the role-playing
- Students are assigned specific roles and receptors noted by signs on their chest or back
- The active learning process must be carefully planned and rehearsed in advance
#### Constraints
- The nature of a more engaging learning activity such as dramatization may not allow enough time to completely cover the same amount of content as a lecture

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- understanding of cardiac cycle dynamics including electrical activity, mechanical activity, neurotransmitters, receptor sites, heart sounds, and changes with exercise
