# Office-hours-experience lecture model: collaborative quantitative problem solving followed by Think-Pair-Share sequences whose answer choices model incorrect mathematical expressions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The reformed section recast lecture time as an "office hours experience" for the entire class at once: students first attempted a quantitative problem with neighbors while the instructor roamed and answered questions, then voted on a sequence of TPS questions whose "choices modelled incorrect mathematical expressions that highlighted fundamental errors and problem-solving difficulties". The sequence focused thinking on specific steps of the solution pathway and let one instructor engage over 200 students without collecting or grading additional work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems carefully chosen to elicit multiple and well-understood problem-solving and conceptual difficulties
- Sequentially crafted TPS questions targeting the particular mathematical or physics applications known to be problematic for students
#### Constraints
- Implemented by a single instructor with no teaching assistants present in lecture
- Developed for a large lecture hall with stadium-style seating and bolted-down desks; no Studio or SCALE-UP style rooms were available

### Target Learners
- students in large-enrollment introductory calculus-based physics courses, primarily physical sciences and engineering majors

### Learning Goals
- quantitative problem-solving
- conceptual understanding
- reasoning with multiple representations of the discipline
