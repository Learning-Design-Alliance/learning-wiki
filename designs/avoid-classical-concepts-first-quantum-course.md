# Avoid classical concepts (trajectories, wave–particle dualism, spin as literal spinning) in a first approach to quantum theory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that when quantum theory is first taught, "every reference to classical concepts should be avoided as f ar as possible", because teaching through classical models causes principal difficulties in understanding. It works by replacing classical scaffolding with the purely quantum phenomenon of spin and the term "quantum object", so pupils do not transfer Newtonian thinking before the quantum concepts are established. The article applies it by naming the three themes to avoid: position–momentum talk, particle–wave dualism, and images of spin as spinning around.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A first-course sequence that replaces classical scaffolding with a purely quantum phenomenon such as spin, introduced via experiments like Stern–Gerlach
- Substitution of the term "quantum object" for wave or particle until the quantum concepts are fixed
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a didactic argument, not an empirically tested result

### Target Learners
- gifted or interested pupils at the age from 16+ on in high school physics

### Learning Goals
- understanding the peculiarities and philosophical significance of quantum theory without transferring classical thinking
