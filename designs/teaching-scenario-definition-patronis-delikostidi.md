# Teaching scenario as a finite sequence of real or imaginary actions in a familiar context, used as interdisciplinary context and incentive for questioning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A teaching scenario is defined, in Patronis' and Delikostidi's terms, as "a finite sequence of real or imaginary actions and abstract (thought) situations" which may not aim at a rational achievement of an obvious purpose but follow a pattern or have a conceptual unit, taking place in a well-known social setting or imaginary space familiar to students. The article uses the teaching scenario as an interdisciplinary context and, by giving the element of dramatization, as an incentive for students' questioning and engagement. This contrasts with formalistic teaching in which mathematics is presented as self-sufficient and then 'validated' through applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A familiar context for students — a well-known social setting or a more or less known imaginary space
- A pattern or conceptual unit connecting the actions and situations
#### Constraints
- The article distinguishes this definition from the Greek School Network's usage tied to series of educational activities and objectified 'learning obstacles'

### Target Learners
- secondary education students of mathematics

### Learning Goals
- constructing mathematical models for motions and measurement in space-time
- reconsidering geometry as not an a priori system of knowledge
