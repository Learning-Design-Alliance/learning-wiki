# Five design principles: one artifact, dependency sequencing, progressive sim-hardware integration, scaffolded bounded choice, and paired collaboration with individual evidence

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article states five principles (P1-P5) describing the framework: coordinate disciplines through one artifact; sequence by technical dependencies with observable gates; integrate simulation and hardware progressively; combine scaffolded checkpoints with bounded choice; and pair collaboration with individual evidence. As the article puts it, "Progress from a worked example to a controlled modification and an independent decision." Teachers elicit cross-disciplinary explanations explicitly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Observable gates such as safety induction, power-on, standing, and walking
- Instructor-set limits on customization choices
- Rotation of operating and checking roles with separately recorded individual reasoning
#### Constraints
- Independent algorithm implementation is positioned as an extension, not a core requirement

### Target Learners
- Novice high-school learners in grades 9-12

### Learning Goals
- Explaining robot behavior across disciplines
- Distinguishing equipment faults, simulation errors, and learning failures
