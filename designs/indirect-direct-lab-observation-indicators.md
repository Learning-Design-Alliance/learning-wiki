# Observation-based assessment pattern combining indirect observation of group dynamics with direct interaction indicators

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Instructor's assessment rests on two broad categories of indicators, indirect and direct. Indirect actions include observing "the interactions among students" (whether any member leads or dominates, whether any member is left out) and the degree of involvement (everyone participating, taking turns, discussing the work, versus just writing results or sitting back). Direct actions concern the kind of questions students ask of the Instructor (trying to understand versus asking for a ready solution) and questions or challenges posed by the Instructor to the group or to an individual, plus technical and comprehension points. These are guidelines rather than rigid checkpoints, and the grade should be given in broad categories to mitigate the Instructor's burden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both indirect and direct actions performed by every Instructor during each lab session
- Grade given in broad categories to mitigate the burden on the Instructor
#### Constraints
- The listed actions are a non-exhaustive list; fluctuations must be expected due to student numbers, group behaviour and instrumentation problems

### Target Learners
- undergraduate laboratory students

### Learning Goals
- active involvement in experimental work
- collaboration
- questioning procedures and results
