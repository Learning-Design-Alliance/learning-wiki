# Use multi-disciplinary model rocket projects to integrate knowledge across the undergraduate engineering curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors frame the model rocket project as a multi-disciplinary activity integrating knowledge from "such courses as fluid mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics, chemistry, differential equations, and computer programming." Student teams predicted the time of flight, maximum altitude, and landing location, wrote numerical integration code, and measured input parameters experimentally; the authors argue such projects could be readily adopted at most engineering schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Off-the-shelf Class 1 model rockets and motors were used for safety, avoiding the need for a special FAA waiver
#### Constraints
- Wind modeling remained an unsolved problem and was the largest source of landing-location error

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- synthesizing knowledge across fluid mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics, chemistry, differential equations, and computer programming
- predicting and experimentally validating rocket flight outcomes
