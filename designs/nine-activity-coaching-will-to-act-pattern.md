# Nine-activity implementation pattern for the coaching method's will-to-act stage in art history seminars

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article enumerates nine activities implementing the final coaching stage in History of Visual Arts classes: seminar road maps, presentations on artists, the artist's portfolio, the student portfolio, games, reports, work with official museum websites, study of art reproductions, and analytical work filling in tables. The road map is described as "a sort of a cheat sheet because one sheet contains the period of art, the artists, and their key works", and each student assembles a personal "Portfolio of History of Visual Arts" combining road maps and artists' portfolios as an album for quick recall.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each student chooses one artist from the road-map list and prepares a presentation on that artist's listed works
- Students keep the road map at hand at all times
- Reproductions of paintings as a primary study source, since studying original paintings is impracticable
#### Constraints
- Original paintings are rarely accessible, so reproductions and electronic presentations are used; the article notes presentations "fail to convey the same richness of color, all details that high-quality reproductions can convey"

### Target Learners
- higher education students in art, design, and teacher-training programs

### Learning Goals
- recall of artists and their key works by historical period
- analytical understanding of artists' contributions to world visual arts
