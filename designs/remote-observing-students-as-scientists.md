# Remote observing sessions giving students the experience of working astronomers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Research-project groups observed with the Lick Observatory 1-m telescope remotely from the CfAO conference room, communicating with an astronomer at the observatory who assisted. The authors used this to reinforce an identity theme: "The remote observing capabilities gave students the identical experience of any astronomer, a point which we emphasized to reinforce the students-as-scientists theme." Sessions ran in the evening and had to be scheduled after students learned about observing but early enough to supply project data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Remote access to a research telescope, an on-site astronomer to assist, and scheduling that follows introductory observing instruction and precedes project data analysis.
#### Constraints
- Time-sensitive observing (e.g., the Variable Stars project) constrained the schedule; observing could not be arbitrarily late in the four weeks.

### Target Learners
- high-school students undertaking small-group research projects

### Learning Goals
- authentic astronomical data collection
- students-as-scientists identity
