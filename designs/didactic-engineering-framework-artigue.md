# Didactic engineering: a four-phase framework for designing and evaluating teaching sequences

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts didactic engineering (Artigue, 1998) as its design and evaluation framework, defined as "un conjunto de secuencias de clase concebidas, organizadas y articuladas en el tiempo de forma coherente por un profesor-ingeniero para efectuar un proyecto de aprendizaje de un contenido dado para un grupo concreto de alumnos". Its experimental process has four phases: preliminary analyses (epistemological, cognitive, didactic and pedagogical dimensions), a priori conception and analysis of didactic situations, experimentation, and a posteriori analysis and evaluation. The framework measures the impact of a didactic action by confronting the a priori analysis, which embeds local hypotheses, with the a posteriori analysis based on data from the actual enactment. The author assumed both teacher and researcher roles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The researcher-teacher conducts preliminary analyses of the knowledge, students' prior conceptions, and the teaching system before designing situations
#### Constraints
- Validation is qualitative, based on transcription analysis of student exchanges, with written questionnaire responses as the only quantitative results

### Target Learners
- Secondary school science students (here, ninth-grade 15-year-olds)

### Learning Goals
- Designing and evaluating teaching sequences aimed at conceptual change, here conservation of motion
