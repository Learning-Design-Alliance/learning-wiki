# Rotating student responsibility for weekly PBL tasks on an asynchronous discussion board with tutor audiovisual feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern operationalises the PBL responsibility principle online: each week a different group of 2–4 students took responsibility for the weekly task, posted a first answer within 3–4 days of the discussion board opening, after which other students joined the discussion, and those responsible summarised solutions for the tutor by a deadline. The tutor then "reviewed the summary document and recorded audiovisual feedback which was then delivered to the students via Blackboard (ELeUM)", with illustrations or additional reading added where needed. The pattern replaced classroom PBL tutorials for part-time students in full-time employment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A discussion board facility (Polaris on Blackboard/EleUM) accessible to all students
- A tutor who reviews summaries and records audiovisual feedback by a predetermined deadline
- A blended learning coordinator or equivalent to monitor the board and remind students of tasks and deadlines
#### Constraints
- The authors chose not to call the module blended learning because nearly all declarative and procedural learning took place online; face-to-face meetings were largely social, administrative or preparatory
- Feedback timeliness was rated lower by online students (mean 3.0) because it was asynchronous

### Target Learners
- Part-time postgraduate public health students in full-time employment

### Learning Goals
- Statistics: multilevel analysis of longitudinal data and exploratory factor analysis
