# Communication competencies should be integrated longitudinally and transversally across the medical curriculum rather than confined to specific courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study argues that communication competencies require "meaningful integration of communication competencies within the curriculum, fostering their longitudinal development and contextualization across areas." The authors found that while communication outcomes appeared in every syllabus analyzed, there was "limited incorporation of systematic, longitudinal development." They position communication as a transversal competency and a defining element of professional identity that should be deliberately cultivated across all curricular areas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricular planning that maps communication competencies across courses and academic years
#### Constraints
- The study analyzed only the formal curriculum through documentary analysis; perspectives of students and educators will be addressed in later stages of the project

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Communication competencies including patient-centered communication, teamwork, documentation, and emotionally sensitive communication
