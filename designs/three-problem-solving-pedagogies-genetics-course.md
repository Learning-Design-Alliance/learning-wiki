# Three cognitive-psychology-based problem-solving pedagogies for biology: prediction, faded worked examples, and tell-then-practice case studies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes problem-solving instruction into three pedagogies with distinct timing and theoretical grounding. Prediction activities use guided inquiry, including contrasting cases, before instruction, drawing on constructivism and desirable difficulties. Faded worked examples provide step-by-step expert solutions with progressively removed support after instruction, grounded in cognitive load theory. Tell-then-practice case studies apply recently learned concepts to real-world scenarios, grounded in constructivism and motivation through case framing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must be embedded in a course structure giving students repeated practice and feedback, as in the authors' flipped, high-structure design
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that worked examples are less potent as students gain expertise and are suited to near transfer rather than entirely novel problems
- Occasionally the schedule required direct instruction of concepts prior to prediction exercises, deviating from the ideal sequence

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate biology majors and pre-health students in a large-enrollment introductory course

### Learning Goals
- disciplinary problem solving in genetics
- conceptual understanding of genetics principles
