# Auditability principle and the seven-part anatomy of an agentic workflow turn

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines auditability as the discipline's defining principle: "a workflow that cannot be inspected, replayed or evaluated after the fact is craft, while engineering is accountable and reproducible." Its operational artefact is the audit trail, a frozen record linking intent to specification, context, agent trajectory and verification, which agentic work produces as a byproduct of normal operation. Each workflow turn has seven constituents running as a single act rather than sequential phases: intent, specification, context, plan, execution, verification and the audit trail, each with a corresponding curriculum module.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The practitioner must preserve, name and make navigable the material the workflow generates, so someone other than the original engineer can reconstruct what was decided, why, and what happened
#### Constraints
- The article notes classical software engineering struggled to produce audit trails routinely because engineers wrote them by hand after the fact; the principle's low cost depends on agent-generated records
