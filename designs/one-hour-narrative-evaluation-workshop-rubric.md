# 1-hour faculty development workshop and six-item utility grading rubric

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An interactive 1-hour workshop delivered at standing faculty development sessions in four Department of Medicine divisions, covering the value of narrative comments, actionable feedback from Radical Candor, and the framework, with step-by-step analysis of useful and not useful examples and practice on mock learner videos. Assessment used a grading rubric adapted from a published rubric and iteratively optimized with a CCC focus group: six items on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = not useful, 4 = extremely useful) for a total of 24. The interclass correlation for interrater reliability of the total score was .83. Appendices include slides, the framework, videos, surveys, rubric, and facilitator guide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two trained CCC-member graders for scoring narrative evaluations
- Delivery in person or virtually at faculty development sessions
#### Constraints
- Assessed at a single large academic institution in one specialty; the authors state results "may not be generalizable across other institutions or specialties" and the rubric "may require further reliability and validity testing before application in other settings."

### Target Learners
- Faculty who regularly supervise internal medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- Describe, apply, and formulate useful narrative evaluations for CCC use
