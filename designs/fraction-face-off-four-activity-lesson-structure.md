# Four-activity lesson structure combining explicit instruction, fluency games, and individual practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each Fraction Face-Off! lesson follows a fixed four-activity structure: "a warm-up word problem, group work during which the teacher demonstrates fraction concepts using explicit instruction, a speed game to build fluency, and a worksheet activity that students complete individually to check their understanding of the fraction concepts." The program pairs frequent fluency practice using speed tests or flashcards with conceptual practice requiring students to explain their reasoning about fractions to the group, plus ongoing assessment through daily worksheets and a cumulative review.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Up to a week of training for teachers before delivering content, with 1-hour follow-up meetings every other week
#### Constraints
- Lessons varied in length between 30 and 35 minutes in the study, and in years 2 through 5 only 25 minutes of each session were the same across the two intervention conditions

### Target Learners
- fourth-grade students who need assistance solving fraction problems

### Learning Goals
- fraction magnitude understanding
- fluency with fraction concepts
- explaining reasoning about fractions
