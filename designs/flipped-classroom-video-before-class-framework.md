# Flipped classroom as reversal of lecture and homework elements with pre-recorded videos before face-to-face active-learning sessions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article adopts the educause conceptual framework of the flipped classroom as "a pedagogical strategy in which the typical lecture and homework elements of a course are reversed": students watch pre-recorded lectures before class and perform active learning activities such as exercises, projects, or discussions in class. The review restricts the definition to implementations with instructor-provided pre-class videos and a face-to-face meeting, excluding text-only pre-class reading and fully online courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both pre-class activities (instructor-recorded lectures, screencasts, or similar videos) and in-class activities must be present
- A face-to-face class meeting must be included
#### Constraints
- The definition excludes the sole use of pre-class reading materials and comparisons of online courses without face-to-face meetings

### Target Learners
- health professions students, including medical students, residents, doctors, nurses, and learners in other health care disciplines

### Learning Goals
- improved student learning on objective course assessments compared with traditional instruction
