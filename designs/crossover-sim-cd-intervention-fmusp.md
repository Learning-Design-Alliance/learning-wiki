# Crossover simulation/case-discussion intervention with pre-, post- and retention MCQ testing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An instructional design in which two pediatric emergency themes (anaphylaxis in a teenager, SVT in an infant) were crossed over across two student groups, so each group experienced one high-fidelity simulation and one case-based discussion on the same scenarios. The design was "based on Chiniara's framework for instructional design in simulation and Cook's levels of instructional design", with identical scenario content, images and ancillary exam results across methods, and knowledge measured by piloted MCQs at pre-test, post-test and 4–6-month retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Same scenario, case history, physical exam findings, images and ancillary exam results presented in both methods
- Questions developed by pediatric emergency physicians blinded to the scenarios and piloted for validity and reliability
#### Constraints
- Groups were pre-assigned by convenience and not randomized; participation was voluntary with 33.7% loss to follow-up at retention

### Target Learners
- sixth-year medical students in a pediatrics rotation

### Learning Goals
- management of rare, high-acuity pediatric emergencies (anaphylaxis, SVT)
