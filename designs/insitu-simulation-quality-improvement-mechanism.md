# Use in situ simulation in real clinical environments as a mechanism for identifying latent safety threats and prompting quality improvement initiatives

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In situ simulation runs simulated clinical scenarios in the actual work environment using the site's own equipment, drugs, and procedures, making it possible to proactively identify latent safety threats before they cause patient harm. The article concludes that "In situ simulation is an effective mechanism both for identifying threats to patient safety and to prompt initiatives for improvement, supporting the use of in situ simulation in the quality improvement cycle in healthcare." The national NetworkZ program embedded this principle by encouraging hospitals to nominate a course instructor liaison to bring identified issues to local quality groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured debrief after each scenario with generic prompts about opportunities for improvement
- Postcourse report template for recording identified threats, protective factors, and countermeasures
- Trained local instructors (in this program, a 2-day training program supplemented by online modules)
#### Constraints
- Data from postcourse reports may underreport the true rate of LSTs, as some threats may not have been identified or recorded
- Delays or failure to address threats identified during simulation is common

### Target Learners
- Multidisciplinary hospital teams in operating theaters and other high-acuity clinical settings

### Learning Goals
- Identifying latent safety threats in the local work system and initiating corrective countermeasures
