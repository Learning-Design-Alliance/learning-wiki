# Registered holoimage overlay of patient anatomy onto physical task trainers for blind insertion procedural training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A session-level instructional design in which rendered holoimages of key cervical and thoracic structures are manually scaled and registered onto physical task trainers using surface anatomy landmarks such as the sternal notch, laryngeal prominence, and rib spaces. This provides learners "the equivalent of a 'visible human'" conveying locations, shapes, sizes, and juxtapositions of internal structures, and "permitted the stable overlay of task-relevant anatomy ... for training with enhanced visual cues and haptic feedback." It was applied to central venous catheter and tube thoracostomy insertion training on a CVL task trainer and a SimMan 3G manikin.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires AR/MR headset devices, pre-rendered patient holoimages, and physical task trainers compatible with landmark-based registration.
#### Constraints
- Registration is manual; spatial alignment between holoimage and task trainer was intermittently lost and required adjustments.

### Target Learners
- emergency medicine fellows, residents, advanced practice providers, and medical students

### Learning Goals
- blind insertion invasive procedural skills (central venous line, tube thoracostomy)
