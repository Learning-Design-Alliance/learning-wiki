# ENT emergency-safe boot camp pattern: a 4-hour blended, simulation-rich programme targeting three critical scenarios

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A standardised intensive simulation-rich training pattern in which novice surgical trainees are up-skilled in three named life-threatening scenarios before starting posts. The article describes a "competence-based educational model" "based on adult learning theories, with a focus on skills transfer in a simulated learning environment", delivered over 4 hours with "interactive didactic teaching as well as practical skills stations with simulation and virtual reality airway models", preceded by pre-reading and taught by senior ENT experts with protocol-based management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consultation with surgical faculty and the national training programme to identify challenging areas and commonly encountered life-threatening emergencies
- Clinical management based on a Cochrane literature review of best practice and protocol and procedure based
- Faculty of senior ENT experts, surgical trainees and simulation centre staff
- Prior provision of literature on the covered scenarios before the boot camp
#### Constraints
- Benefit is short-lived without subsequent deliberate practice and further distributive learning
- Making simulation equipment available for constant practice is limited by resources available

### Target Learners
- novice surgical trainees / core surgical trainees

### Learning Goals
- initial management of post-thyroidectomy haematoma, severe epistaxis and post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage
