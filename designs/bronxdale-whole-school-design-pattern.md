# Whole-school design pattern combining community vision, inquiry instruction with social-emotional supports, restorative discipline, and a professional community of practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report identifies a constellation of four mutually reinforcing school-design factors that it argues produced Bronxdale's outcomes: "a compelling vision of students and school as a caring, safe, collaborative community," "the integration of inquiry instruction with social-emotional supports," "educative discipline that puts restorative practices into action," and a faculty committed to "teaching the way that students learn best" while "continuously learning together in a community of practice." The pattern treats culture, pedagogy, discipline, and adult learning as one integrated design rather than isolated programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structures such as small class size and advisory to build close relationships; distributed leadership; intensive ongoing professional development; and multi-tiered academic, health, and social supports.
#### Constraints
- The report notes the school's evolution took about 7 years and that its first year, before these practices were in place, was "in disarray" with fights and an unsafe climate.

### Target Learners
- Diverse high school learners, including students from low-income households and students with disabilities in inclusion settings

### Learning Goals
- Self-reliant, independent learning; curiosity; creative problem-solving; agency; academic and emotional self-regulation
