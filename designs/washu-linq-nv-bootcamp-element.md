# WashU LinQ week-long quantum bootcamp built around a nitrogen-vacancy diamond microscope

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The WashU NRT replaced a standalone course with "low-disruption week-long bootcamps, forming multi-disciplinary teams to conduct multiple hands-on labs, coupled with lunch-hour lectures, coupled with discussions on specific quantum "keywords" as a framing device." The bootcamp used a quantum diamond (nitrogen-vacancy center) microscope as the common object of study, deliberately recruiting students from earth sciences, chemistry, and biology alongside physicists and engineers, and asked the full group to brainstorm use cases in their own fields. The NV center platform has emerged independently at multiple institutions as a preferred common-language teaching instrument.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared instrument simple enough to grasp quickly, near enough to quantum advantage to make it concrete, and relevant across fields
#### Constraints
- Even faculty from different disciplines could not agree on definitions of core terms such as coherence, entanglement, relaxation, and polarization

### Target Learners
- graduate students from earth sciences, chemistry, biology, physics, and engineering

### Learning Goals
- common quantum vocabulary and cross-disciplinary collaboration
