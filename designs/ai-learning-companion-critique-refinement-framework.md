# AI-as-learning-companion: leveraging LLM fallibility through a critique-refinement cycle to develop higher-order thinking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper's central framework reframes generative AI errors and hallucinations as a pedagogical resource rather than a limitation. By treating AI as a "learning companion" whose imperfect outputs prompt analysis, evaluation, and reflection, instructors structure an "iterative critique-refinement cycle" in which "AI's imperfect outputs serve as catalysts for evaluation, positioning critique as the central mechanism driving higher-order cognition". The cycle of generation, evaluation, and revision is aligned with Bloom's taxonomy, shifting emphasis from knowledge and application toward analysis, evaluation, and creation, and fostering metacognitive engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must extend beyond domain knowledge to include the ability to evaluate, contextualize, and revise AI-generated outputs
- Structured opportunities for evaluation and revision with imperfect responses
#### Constraints
- Over-reliance on AI can impede the development of core cognitive skills, so use must be scaffolded

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer science students

### Learning Goals
- higher-order thinking (analysis, evaluation, creation)
- metacognitive skills including self-monitoring and error detection
- critical evaluation of AI-generated solutions
