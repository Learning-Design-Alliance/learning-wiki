# Two-day forest physics project exercise with low-cost equipment and student-chosen projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A project-based undergraduate physics class held over two consecutive days in a forest near the classroom building: “in two days, students design and carry out physics experiments from scratch in the forest, analyze their data, and present their results in an exhibit they build together.” Students work in pairs with limited time and low-tech equipment, generate their own project ideas, and the teachers deliberately accept that projects often do not provide clear results, since the focus is on the process more than on precision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A natural setting a few minutes from the classroom building; low-tech equipment such as smartphones, ropes, rulers, and measuring tapes; about 5 hours of project time; teachers help synthesize ideas and check feasibility.
#### Constraints
- Going outside introduces some unpredictability compared with a classroom where all technical aspects can be controlled; the weather is an uncontrollable factor.
- Projects often do not provide clear results compared with prepared labs — this is deliberate, to show that a classroom experiment is a carefully contrived situation.
- Some student projects may have only a remote connection to environment, sustainability and climate.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students (second and third year, dual physics and geology curriculum)

### Learning Goals
- understanding that physics can describe and investigate the real world
- soft skills: group work, sharing and transmission of knowledge, creativity
- taking pleasure in doing physics and seeing the role physics can play in environmental issues
