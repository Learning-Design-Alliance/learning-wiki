# District strategy pattern: compensation increases, high-retention pathways, recruitment capacity, and added student-support staff

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report identifies a four-part pattern of district responses to shortages: "increasing teacher compensation, improving recruitment and hiring capacity, developing high-retention pathways into teaching within the district, and adding new staff to improve teaching and learning conditions." Districts used state and federal recovery funds for wage increases, stipends, and bonuses (especially for hard-to-fill positions and substitutes), Grow Your Own initiatives and teacher residencies, streamlined hiring such as open contracts and 24-hour offer turnaround, and new counselor, psychologist, social worker, and instructional coach positions intended to ease teacher workloads and support retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State or federal recovery funding to finance compensation increases, pathway development, and additional staffing
#### Constraints
- The report notes a rural district offered a $15,000 signing bonus and $3,000 moving stipend for high school mathematics and music positions but had not received any applicants, showing limits of compensation alone for hard-to-staff rural locales

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in California districts facing teacher shortages

### Learning Goals
- Stable staffing with fully prepared teachers; smaller class sizes; learning recovery and social-emotional support
