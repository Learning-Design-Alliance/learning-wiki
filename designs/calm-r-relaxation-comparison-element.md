# CALM-R: a relaxation-only active comparison condition controlling for nurse time and attention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
CALM-R (Child Anxiety Learning Modules – Relaxation only) is the active comparison condition used in the pilot RCT. It was developed to rectify the ethical dilemma of withholding intervention from children in distress, to control for nurse time and attention, and to increase nurse recruitment and subject retention. The article states "The key components of CALM-R included psychoeducation, deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, guided imagery, and relapse prevention." Relaxation was selected because it is a core CBT component, has been shown to reduce anxiety, and is already taught by professionals including school nurses. Like CALM, it comprised six modules delivered over 8 weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery by nurses randomized and trained in the condition, with the same six-module, 8-week brief-meeting structure as CALM
#### Constraints
- Contains relaxation skills only, without exposure, cognitive restructuring, or problem-solving components of full CBT

### Target Learners
- elementary school students ages 5–12 with elevated anxiety symptoms and/or anxiety disorders

### Learning Goals
- reducing anxiety symptoms and related impairment through relaxation-based coping skills
