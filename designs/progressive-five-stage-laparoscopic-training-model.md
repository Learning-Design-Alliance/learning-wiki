# Progressive five-stage laparoscopic training model moving from knowledge acquisition through box trainer, VR simulation, and ex vivo organ practice to animal-based surgery

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training design is a three-month progressive sequence of five stages: knowledge acquisition, box trainer training, virtual reality simulation, ex vivo organ training, and animal-based surgical training. The authors state the program "combines multiple teaching methods, including foundational knowledge acquisition, box trainer training, virtual reality simulation, ex vivo organ training, and animal-based surgical training," covering content "from basic skills to complex surgical procedures." Box trainers develop basic techniques, VR familiarizes trainees with workflows and emergency scenarios, and ex vivo and animal-based components bridge simulation with real-world clinical practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multidisciplinary team of experts designs the curriculum, standardized training protocols, and assessment criteria; equipment includes simulation boxes, laparoscopic instruments, a VR laparoscopic training system, ex vivo organs, and live animals
#### Constraints
- Adoption of ex vivo organ and animal-based training is hindered by high costs and ethical issues; many centers lack advanced simulation and animal-based training equipment

### Target Learners
- Clinical Professional Master's Degree Residents (CPMDRs) in surgical residency training in China

### Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic skills: hand-eye coordination, spatial awareness, suturing, procedural fluency, and operative decision-making
