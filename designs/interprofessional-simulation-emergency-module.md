# Simulation-enhanced interprofessional emergency training module with prehospital–handover–ED case structure and GAS-model debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A one-day, simulation-enhanced interprofessional training module for mixed teams of medical students, emergency nursing trainees, and student paramedics, developed on Kern's six-step approach. Teams rotate through simulated emergency cases in which "Each case consists of a prehospital section (for paramedics and medical students), a handover (everyone), and an ED section (medical students and emergency nurses)", followed by structured three-step GAS-model debriefing by an interprofessional instructor team including a communication expert. Modules open with an introduction to roles, professional self-concepts, misconceptions, and communication barriers, and feature a "Topic of the Day".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interprofessional instructor team of at least two professions plus one communication expert
- High-fidelity simulators or simulated patients
- Protected time for participants, with cases containing challenges for interprofessional collaboration
#### Constraints
- Longitudinal integration was not possible for medical students, whose modules were not compulsory, so different medical students attended each module

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students
- emergency nursing trainees
- student paramedics

### Learning Goals
- interprofessional teamwork and communication
- basic emergency care as an ad hoc interprofessional team
- mutual understanding of professional roles
