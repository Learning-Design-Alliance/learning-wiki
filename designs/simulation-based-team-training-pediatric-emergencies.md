# Simulation-based small-group team training as the standard educational approach for pediatric emergency preparedness

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames simulation-based training as the standard educational approach for improving team behavior in pediatric emergency care, allowing shared expertise and perspectives based on group knowledge and skills. It states that "Recent data highlight the value of simulation-based training for teams in acquiring technical and non-technical skills" and that small-group teaching provides a controlled environment supporting "repetitive practice with close supervision". On-site simulation embedded in hospital routine can increase learning opportunities for local staff.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small-group teaching with a controlled environment supporting repetitive practice with close supervision
#### Constraints
- This type of training is expensive in terms of equipment and human resources; many described approaches lack evaluation of short- and long-term outcomes

### Target Learners
- interprofessional teams of physicians and nurses in pediatric hospitals

### Learning Goals
- technical and non-technical emergency care skills, teamwork and communication in pediatric emergencies
