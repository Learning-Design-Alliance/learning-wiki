# Redesigned electricity-and-magnetism lab course with modeling, data-analysis, and model-testing activities on conventional apparatus

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A stand-alone weekly 2-hour lab course attached to an introductory electricity-and-magnetism lecture course, serving about one third of enrolled students. The redesign kept "the same physical apparatus as the previous version of the course" but changed pre-lab and in-lab activities toward "analyzing and interpreting measurements and data, connecting measurement and data with models, and evaluating and refining models based on data". First-half pre-labs introduced data analysis concepts and tools; second-half activities focused on modeling and testing models, e.g. exploring why a capacitor discharges exponentially and discovering off-axis and fringing effects in a current-loop experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly 2-hour lab sessions with graduate TAs, activities designed by a physics education researcher, and TA training meetings
- Apparatus evaluated for measurable model limitations and precision needed for students to observe them
#### Constraints
- Approximately one third of lecture-course students enrolled in the optional lab, due to major requirements

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students at a large, elite university

### Learning Goals
- analyzing and interpreting measurements and data
- connecting measurement and data with models
- evaluating and refining models based on data
