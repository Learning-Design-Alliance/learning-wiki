# Weighed-down cork and salt-stratified jug apparatus with Tracker/Fiji video analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A low-cost instructional apparatus: a wine-bottle cork cut in halves with a 10 mm steel marble embedded (soaked in vinyl glue, glued with ethyl cyanoacrylate, sliced until slightly denser than water), placed in 500 ml of tap water with coarse kitchen salt poured unstirred at the bottom. The article notes the choice of "coarse rather than ﬁne salt to avoid its partial dissolution while settling", giving better-defined initial conditions. Rise and oscillations are tracked with the open-source Tracker application after conversion to AVI video with Fiji, calibrated against a measured water height of 6.8 cm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A cork of density slightly larger than water; coarse kitchen salt (amounts spanning 10 to 46 g/l); a camera (a smartphone suffices) and video-tracking software
#### Constraints
- Cork density varies slightly between experiments due to moisture content; water temperature is uncontrolled
- The cork's finite size perturbs salt dissolution and process times

### Target Learners
- students from elementary school to undergraduate level

### Learning Goals
- observing levitation in a stratified fluid
- measuring Brunt-Väisälä oscillations and internal gravity waves
