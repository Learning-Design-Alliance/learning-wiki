# Video-based feedback methodology combining problem-based interviewing and agenda-led outcome-based analysis in small peer groups with real consultations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a VF methodology grounded in Lesser's problem-based interviewing (PBI) model and agenda-led outcome-based analysis (ALOBA) feedback, in which "Each medical resident presented a pre-recorded interview in a real-life setting to a group of peers, subsequently receiving feedback from colleagues and two facilitators". Sessions ran about 90 minutes over roughly 10 weeks within a curricular formative assessment. Facilitators coordinated the session, helped residents verbalize their self-image, and supported microanalysis of micro-behaviors including exact words and non-verbal communication, while the group assisted in finding alternatives to less effective behaviors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who coordinates the session, helps residents perceive their self-image and perform self-assessment, and links feedback to communication theories
- Pre-recorded consultations with real patients from a real-life primary care setting, taped close to the session date
#### Constraints
- The article was unable to causally link the intervention with its effects
- The study lacked standardization of raters and SPs and had variation in local supervisors

### Target Learners
- First-year medical residents in primary care residency programs

### Learning Goals
- Clinical communication skills
- Patient-centered consultation behaviors
- Self-assessment and reflective practice
