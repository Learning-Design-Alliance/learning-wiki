# Kolb-based integrated blended-learning course design (TraceX intervention)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The TraceX intervention operationalizes Kolb's experiential learning cycle by integrating preclinical and clinical content across "all 4 stages of Kolb's learning cycle": structured online modules (functional and topographic anatomy videos, physiotherapist-led examination videos, professionalism modules), a 90-minute flipped-classroom seminar, and a peer-assisted practice session with trained peer tutors giving feedback. The control course, by contrast, reflected "conventional, lecture-based learning with limited interaction and a more fragmented learning process." The design rationale is that meaningful learning requires progression through all cycle stages, which fragmented Flexnerian curricula do not provide.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Common learning objectives developed first and applied to both groups
- Interdisciplinary development involving GPs, didactics experts, an orthopedic surgeon, students, a physiotherapist, and a medical psychologist
- Structured feedback opportunities such as trained peer tutors facilitating practice
#### Constraints
- Peer sessions and exams were held via videoconferencing due to COVID-19 rather than face-to-face as intended
- The intervention bundled format with tailored material and feedback, limiting isolation of the blended-format effect

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- functional shoulder anatomy
- structured clinical examination skills
- professional conduct
