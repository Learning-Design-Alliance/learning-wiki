# Student-built instrument development project combining coding, testing, and materials-based construction skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A laboratory instructional pattern in which students themselves build, program, and test a working scientific instrument — here an automatic liposome extruder — from affordable, typically available parts. The article states that "Using this approach, students can acquire different skillsets including coding, testing, and advanced use of building materials based on their properties." The project is designed to fit the limited hours of a typical student researcher, can be expanded once completed, and can be adapted to student background in mechanical engineering, software engineering, chemistry, or other fields, enabling interdisciplinary cooperation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two syringe pumps and a computer, plus an Avanti Mini-Extruder, gastight syringes, membranes, and filter supports
- Basic knowledge of LabVIEW, which the article notes is provided in entry level textbooks
#### Constraints
- The flow rate-based extrusion system cannot be adjusted to mimic manual extrusion by adjusting the flow rate throughout the process

### Target Learners
- undergraduate to graduate students in STEM laboratory courses and research laboratories

### Learning Goals
- instrument development and construction
- coding and software development
- scientific testing and problem solving
- self-assembly and fluid dynamics concepts
