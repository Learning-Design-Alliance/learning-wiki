# Integrated laboratory classes as a complement to PBL that bridges basic sciences and clinical application

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article frames ILCs as a learning modality that addresses gaps left by discipline-based practical classes in an integrated curriculum. Whereas "PBL and lectures usually focus on the big picture, laboratory classes facilitate learning about details and hands-on experiences." ILCs were designed to be student-centered, fit the theme of the week, reflect integration, and provide meaningful learning by having students apply and integrate knowledge from biochemistry, pathology and microbiology to clinical scenarios, in contrast to teacher-centered transmission of factual knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment with curriculum outcomes and the theme of the week
- Student-centered design with tasks, small group work, and feedback
#### Constraints
- The framework was developed in response to a specific hybrid PBL curriculum at King Saud University and applies to preclinical laboratory classes

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- deep understanding of concepts
- application of knowledge
- integration across basic and clinical sciences
