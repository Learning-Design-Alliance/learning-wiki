# Six key practices of a community school serving immigrant, multilingual communities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report distills UCLA Community School's approach into six key practices: knowing and building on a community's history, assets, and culture; developing and articulating desired core competencies; nurturing shared leadership and collective agency; creating space for collaborative inquiry, professional autonomy, and teacher development; using data and stories to elevate the school's vision and track progress; and affirming everyone's role. The report says these practices "contribute to its success" and offers them as takeaways for practitioners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A cross-sector partnership structure with a primary university partner and community school coordination to sustain the practices
#### Constraints
- Practices are documented at one TK-12 school in central Los Angeles serving a large immigrant population; the report presents them as an example, not a tested recipe

### Target Learners
- TK-12 students in immigrant, low-income, multilingual communities

### Learning Goals
- College readiness
- biliteracy and multicultural competence
- civic agency
