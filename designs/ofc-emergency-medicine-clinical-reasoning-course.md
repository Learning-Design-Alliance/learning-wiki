# An online flipped classroom clinical reasoning course for emergency medicine clerkship, designed via a modified ADDIE model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (4 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An 8-week clinical reasoning course for emergency medicine clerkship students, developed using "a modified version of the ADDIE instructional design model, specifically adapted by Eltahir for virtual learning environments". It comprised asynchronous pre-class content (short videos under 30 minutes via WhatsApp and an LMS, with mandatory formative MCQ tests), synchronous 60-minute small-group case-based Zoom discussions using a think-aloud format, and bedside application sessions. Evaluation used Kirkpatrick levels 1 and 2 (satisfaction and OSCE-based learning outcomes).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants required a smartphone with WhatsApp to engage with asynchronous electronic content
- Mandatory formative tests via the LMS after each video module to ensure engagement before synchronous sessions
#### Constraints
- Excluded students with prior formal exposure to flipped classroom models or clinical reasoning workshops
- Excluded students missing more than 20% of online pre-class activities or more than one bedside think-aloud session

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students during emergency medicine clerkship

### Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and diagnostic logic in high-acuity emergency presentations
- History taking and differential diagnosis prioritization
