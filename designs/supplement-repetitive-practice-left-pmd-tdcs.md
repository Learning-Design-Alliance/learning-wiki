# Supplement repetitive practice of multiple motor skills with anodal tDCS at left PMd to gain sleep-related offline consolidation otherwise seen only after interleaved practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is that neuromodulation of targets beyond M1 during practice can improve outcomes of a low-retention practice schedule. The authors state that "neuromodulation of targets beyond M1, via application of anodal tDCS during practice, can facilitate not just the acquisition and retention of a single novel motor skill but also multiple skill memories developed simultaneously." Applying anodal tDCS at left PMd throughout repetitive practice produced an overnight offline gain matching interleaved practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 2 mA anodal stimulation via a 25 cm2 anode placed 2.5 cm anterior to M1 (FC3/FC4) with supraorbital reference, applied for the entire 20-min practice period
#### Constraints
- The overnight gain emerged only for left-PMd stimulation and only following overnight sleep; no early 6-h consolidation was induced, and blinding was not formally evaluated

### Target Learners
- adult learners practicing novel motor sequences (right-handed undergraduates in the study)

### Learning Goals
- retention and sleep-related consolidation of novel motor sequence skills
