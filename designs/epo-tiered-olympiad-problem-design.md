# EPO olympiad problem design: tiered tasks, alternative solution paths, and a take-home experimental set-up

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The EPO10 problem is structured as tasks of increasing difficulty grouped by age category (S, M, L, XL), with the full experimental set-up given to every participant. The article states "Different tasks with increasing difficulty are described in the sections corresponding to each age category" and that "The age-category serves for evaluation purpose mainly." Students who cannot perform the experiment may predict the result without measurements, and mathematical analysis is recognized as an alternative solution. The set-up is given as a gift so every participant can repeat the experiment at home.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A low-cost experimental set-up (wooden panel, double switch, two electrolytic capacitors, AA batteries, multimeter) that can be given to every participant
- Tasks graded by age category for evaluation purposes
#### Constraints
- The number of cables is limited in the given set
- Participants are asked to keep the humanitarian text as short as possible

### Target Learners
- secondary school and university physics olympiad participants across age categories

### Learning Goals
- experimental measurement skills with multimeters
- graphical data analysis of exponential decay
- connecting school physics to patented technology
