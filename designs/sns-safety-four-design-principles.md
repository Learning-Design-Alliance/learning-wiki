# Four context-sensitive design principles for educational materials about SNS risks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Through reflection on all eight studies, the authors revised their initial design principles into four contextually sensitive principles: individual reflection is critical; simulated environments are sufficient; involving parents is beneficial; and a short-term intervention is sufficient. Table 5 maps each revised principle to the initial principle it reinterprets, e.g. collaborative learning becomes "Individual reflection is critical" and authentic setting becomes "Simulated environments are sufficient".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials should build in time for individual reflection before peer discussion
- A homework task completed in collaboration with parents is proposed to involve all parents
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge the principles are context-specific, which jeopardizes external validity; generalizability to other behaviors and cultures requires future research

### Target Learners
- secondary education students aged 12 to 19

### Learning Goals
- awareness of contact risks on SNSs
- safer attitudes and behavior regarding privacy and cyberbullying
