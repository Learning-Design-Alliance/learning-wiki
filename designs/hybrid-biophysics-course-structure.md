# A hybrid interdisciplinary course structure balancing case studies, applications, and practitioner presentations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course was divided into three components: case studies applying physical approaches to biological problems (e.g., how high can a tree grow?), an applications approach introducing foundational physics first followed by biological uses (biophotonics, nuclear physics, MRI), and guest presentations from industry and university researchers. As the authors state, "a hybrid course that balanced case study approaches, applications approaches and presentations from biophysicists". Guest lectures comprised about 1/8 of lecture hours. The pattern suits second-year undergraduates from diverse science majors integrating physics with life sciences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Co-teaching by faculty from both disciplines: the article states the course was co-taught by biology and physics and astronomy faculty to ensure comprehensive coverage from both perspectives.
#### Constraints
- Enrollments were small (averaging 18 students, range 10–24), unlike typical second-year courses of well over 100.

### Target Learners
- Second-year undergraduate science students, including biophysics majors and life-science non-majors

### Learning Goals
- Integrating physics and mathematics with the life sciences
- Analytical, open-ended problem solving at the biology–physics interface
