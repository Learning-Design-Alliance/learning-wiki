# Four-level digital divide framework (motivational, material, skills, usage access) guiding technology support

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article applies digital divide theory (van Dijk; van Deursen and van Dijk) as an organizing framework of four access levels that human technology support can address. As printed: "Motivational access describes potential users' assessment of the value and ease of use of technology. Material access concerns people's technology ownership and its reliability (eg, internet access quality). Skills access refers to people's abilities to effectively interact with technology (ie, 'digital literacy/competence')." Usage access "encompasses how people use and benefit from technologies." The study structured its support services and analysis around these levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Technology support services designed to target each of the four access levels simultaneously
#### Constraints
- Applied to older and chronically ill hemodialysis patients in a US multicenter trial; mentoring was in English, so language access benefits were not shown

### Target Learners
- hemodialysis patients
- older adults with limited technology experience

### Learning Goals
- using patient-facing digital health technologies
- participating in telehealth-based peer mentoring
