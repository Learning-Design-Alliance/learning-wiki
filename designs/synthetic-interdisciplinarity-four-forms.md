# Synthetic interdisciplinarity as the organizing approach for the data science course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article adopts a taxonomy of four forms of interdisciplinarity — informed disciplinarity, transdisciplinarity, synthetic interdisciplinarity, and conceptual interdisciplinarity — and selects synthetic interdisciplinarity for its course, in which "theories and concepts are combined from other disciplines" while the originating disciplines maintain their identity by "retaining distinct content, boundaries, theories and methods". The choice reflects the distinctive nature of statistics, computer science, and the multiple substantive domain areas the course covered.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires scholars from at least two disciplines coherently combining their perspectives to achieve a common pedagogic goal, the article's definition of interdisciplinary courses.
#### Constraints
- The originating disciplines effectively maintain their identity by retaining distinct content, boundaries, theories and methods, rather than amalgamating theories and methods into one as in transdisciplinarity.

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate and graduate students from the colleges of business, liberal arts, and engineering and computer science

### Learning Goals
- Data science skills spanning computational tools, mathematical and statistical foundations, and domain-grounded interpretation and reporting
