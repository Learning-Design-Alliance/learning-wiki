# Low Dose High Frequency (LDHF) capacity-building approach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
LDHF is a capacity-building approach defined in the article as one that promotes maximal retention of clinical knowledge, skills, and attitudes through short, targeted in-service simulation-based learning activities, spaced over time, and reinforced with structured, ongoing practice sessions on the job. This study operationalized it as simulation-based high-frequency recurring HBB workshops with intermittent on-site supervision, adapted for community midwives who could not commute frequently for practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Short, targeted in-service simulation-based learning activities spaced over time
- Structured ongoing on-the-job practice sessions or supervised support between sessions
#### Constraints
- The article notes applying LDHF in remote settings with community midwives was challenging and highly resource-intensive, motivating the centralized recurring-session adaptation

### Target Learners
- In-service health workers such as community midwives in low-resource settings

### Learning Goals
- Retention of clinical knowledge, skills, and attitudes for newborn resuscitation
