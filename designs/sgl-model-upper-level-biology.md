# A small-group learning (SGL) model for upper-level university science courses built on permanent diverse groups, frequent collaborative data-analysis activities, and accountability in evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes an SGL model in which permanent, diverse groups of five to six students, formed transparently by subject major, sit and work together throughout the term. Lectures are interspersed with formative group activities at roughly 15-minute intervals in which groups "interpret figures, ... create models based on pertinent data, and ... design experiments." Accountability is built in by requiring individual quizzes before group quizzes and peer evaluation worth 10% of the final grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Permanent instructor-formed, diverse groups (here of five to six students based on subject major) maintained throughout the term
- Group activities and quizzes designed so that cooperation facilitates learning, using varied question formats and complex questions with simple answers to enable consensus
- Accountability mechanisms: individual completion of quizzes before group submission and peer evaluation included in final marks
#### Constraints
- The article did not examine the impact of group composition, activity design, or accountability individually, so their separate effects are untested
- Early implementation (Initial SGL, 2007) produced quiz-related anxiety, addressed by moving quizzes to the start of class and covering previous-day material

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate science students in an elective neurobiology course

### Learning Goals
- analysis and evaluation of scientific data
- development of scientific models
- conceptual understanding of course material
- scientific communication and collaboration
