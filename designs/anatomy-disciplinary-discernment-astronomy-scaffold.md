# Anatomy of disciplinary discernment as a scaffold for visualization-based astronomy instruction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Eriksson and colleagues' hierarchical framework, in which disciplinary discernment is "noticing something, reflecting on it, and constructing meaning from a disciplinary perspective," arranged from recognizing or naming objects in an astronomical representation, to assigning disciplinary meaning, to combining disciplinary elements into holistic understanding, to analyzing and critiquing the representation. The article uses this framework to structure its planetarium lecture as a purposefully constructed scaffold: simplest tasks involve identifying and naming planetary moons, while more sophisticated tasks combine principles (independent orbits, gravitational capture, accretion, impact ejection) into holistic understanding of scenarios for moon formation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor-guided tour through astronomical visualizations so learners can cross into increasingly advanced levels of discernment
#### Constraints
- The framework is applied here only to undergraduate moon concepts in introductory astronomy; the article does not claim it was tested as a variable

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory astronomy students

### Learning Goals
- disciplinary discernment about planetary moons, including their identities, motions, and formation scenarios
