# EPIC framework: exposure–persuasion–identification–commitment model of student buy-in to active learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The EPIC framework describes a staged buy-in process through which students move from being exposed to active learning, to being persuaded of its value, to identifying with it personally, to committing to engage in such practices in the future. As the article states, "The exposure–persuasion–identification–commitment (EPIC) framework aims to describe inevitable variations in the level of student commitment to active learning within a course." In this study, student commitment was operationalized as self-reported responses to 16 active-learning practices scored across the four EPIC subscales, and the framework structures the predictor–outcome analyses of trust and growth mindset.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be exposed to active-learning practices and self-report their persuasion, identification, and commitment responses to those practices.
#### Constraints
- The framework was applied within a single course with one instructor; the article calls its findings exploratory and preliminary.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science majors in large active-learning courses

### Learning Goals
- commitment to and engagement in active-learning practices
