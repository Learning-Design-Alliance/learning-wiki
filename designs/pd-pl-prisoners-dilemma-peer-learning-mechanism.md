# PD PL: a game-theoretical peer-learning mechanism mapping prisoner's dilemma onto paired writing and sheet exchange

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
PD PL is a mechanism that applies the prisoner's dilemma to peer learning: paired students briefly write about a taught concept, swap and read sheets, complete self and pair assessments, and take pre/post tests; scores computed from both sheets are announced on Edmodo. The article states "the total payoﬀ they gained will be taken into account for their mid-term score", motivating effort. Repeated sessions allow renegotiation or partner change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students are trained beforehand about the mechanism and scoring method
- An educational network (Edmodo) is used to announce scores after each execution
#### Constraints
- Students do not know in which sessions the mechanism is run
- Writing on the sheet does not require more than 5 to 10 minutes

### Target Learners
- university computer engineering students

### Learning Goals
- course concept understanding in programming, discrete mathematics, and compiler courses
