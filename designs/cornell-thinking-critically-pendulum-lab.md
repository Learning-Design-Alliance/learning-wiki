# Cornell Thinking Critically in Physics pendulum lab (model-testing design)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Cornell pendulum lab is a two-session activity within a stand-alone lab course, instructed by graduate TAs with a maximum of 20 students per section. The lab instructions discuss the pendulum period equation derived from a model, and in-lab guidance sets students on a path to produce data indicating a deviation from this model, creating an empirical-theoretical discrepancy that gives experimental practices purpose. Students are assessed on process and quality of work rather than on arriving at the correct conclusion. The design includes partner agreements, Jupyter/CoLab notebooks, a contrasting-cases invention activity introducing the t' statistic, and choices between task options such as reducing uncertainty or pushing on assumptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-lab instructions that constrain the field of inquiry while remaining open-ended enough to create decision-making opportunities for students.
- Instructor feedback and associated lecture discussion for students to reflect on their process.
#### Constraints
- The activity takes two lab sessions at Cornell rather than three; the specificity of instructions is scaled back over the unit so students take up more responsibility.

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students at a large, private, selective research university

### Learning Goals
- developing a more sophisticated understanding of what it means to know things and construct knowledge in physics
- cultivating student agency and experimental design, uncertainty, and data visualization skills
