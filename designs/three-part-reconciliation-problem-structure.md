# Three-part homework problems that elicit conceptual reasoning, calculation, and reconciliation of disagreements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the mathematical sensemaking course, homework problems were structured in three parts: first elicit intuitive conceptual reasoning, then require the formal calculation, then ask students to reconcile any disagreement between the two. The article reports that "Part (c) asks students to resolve potential disagreements in parts (a) and (b)," aiming to bring the two forms of reasoning into alignment. Through repeated practice, "seeking such resolutions becomes the normal problem-solving activity in the class," with scaffolding fading over the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Homework solutions must present a resolution after students have tried to reach one on their own; the pattern is modeled in class before students attempt it
#### Constraints
- Novice students may have difficulties generating the correct physics resolution in part (c) on their own

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- aligning conceptual intuitions with calculations
- seeking and valuing coherence between reasoning modes
