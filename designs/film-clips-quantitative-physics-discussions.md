# Use short clips from popular Hollywood films as the basis for quantitative physics discussions and calculations to teach physical science to non-science majors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course augments traditional lectures, PowerPoint slides and live demonstrations with short (5 to 8 minutes) film clips, often from the same films that perpetuate students' incorrect science ideas. Clips "set the scene" for discussions and calculations on topics from momentum and energy to time travel and black holes, and the instructor's analysis of each clip is posted on the course web site for review. Films are assigned as homework viewing, about one per week, before the related topics are discussed in class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Films must be viewed at home as homework prior to class discussion of the related topics
- multimedia-equipped classrooms and an instructor analysis of each clip posted to the course web site
#### Constraints
- Only short clips are used in class; the approach was implemented in large-enrollment general education sections for non-science majors

### Target Learners
- non-science majors in introductory general education physical science at the college level

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking about science presented in films
- distinguishing physical laws from pseudoscience
- understanding how science works and how theories are verified
