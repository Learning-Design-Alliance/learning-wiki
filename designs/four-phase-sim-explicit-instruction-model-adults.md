# Four-phase modified SIM explicit instruction model for adult learners

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a modified Strategic Instruction Model (SIM) instructional method adapted for adult-to-adult interaction in four phases: Phase 1 addresses learning readiness, goal setting and commitment; Phase 2 reviews and builds fundamental skills needed for the target strategy; Phase 3 is when "students are expected to master the target strategy"; Phase 4 is a generalization process applying strategies to different text types in academic, employment, and other settings. Within Phases 2 and 3, instructors guide learners through a four-step acquisition process of describe, model, scaffolded practice and feedback, and progress or mastery checks, with scaffolding incrementally removed and progress graphed to keep learners motivated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors trained in the scripted strategy and instructional method
- Audio or video recording of sessions for fidelity review
#### Constraints
- The method was designed for explicit, intensive delivery, which the authors found elusive in adult education settings with typical attendance patterns

### Target Learners
- Adult basic education learners with low literacy or limited educational attainment

### Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension and reading-related subskills
- Acquisition and generalization of learning strategies
