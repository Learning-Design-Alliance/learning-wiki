# Two-phase without/with-entanglement runs contrasted by a cross-correlation symbol analysis that makes entanglement operationally detectable

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A lesson-level design that runs two sequential measurement sets — particles not entangled, then entangled at the source — and has the audience compare cross-correlation symbols assigned to joint outcomes. "By construction of the demonstration, the correlation analysis of the experiment with entanglement gives the same symbol (triangle) for all experimental runs," whereas without entanglement both correlation symbols can occur, so a uniform symbol column operationally signals entanglement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- "Each experimental run is then repeated at least five times" in each set
- Volunteers A and B are blindfolded and "the Instructor also asks the audience beforehand to remain silent during the experimental run."
#### Constraints
- If after five repetitions the without-entanglement table matches the expected entanglement pattern, instructors "carry out further repetitions of the experiment without entanglement, until the corresponding data table has at least one row with the same color (spin projection) for Alice and Bob."

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade Chilean high-school students
- high-school students in other contexts

### Learning Goals
- distinguishing entangled from non-entangled particle pairs via correlation analysis
- understanding the role of statistics in Bell tests
