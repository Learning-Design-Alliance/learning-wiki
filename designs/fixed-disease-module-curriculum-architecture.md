# Fixed disease-module curriculum architecture combining contouring, structured plan review, MDT debriefing and narrative feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The curriculum was designed backward from core clinical tasks rather than lecture topics, organized into three fixed disease modules (locally advanced NPC without induction chemotherapy, middle/lower thoracic esophageal cancer, FIGO 2018 IB3–IIIC1 cervical cancer). Each module combined "one contouring task, one structured plan-review task, one multidisciplinary team (MDT)-style case presentation, and one narrative-feedback encounter", delivered over about 2 weeks with 6–8 hours of supervised faculty contact. The authors regard this architecture as making contouring and plan review explicit and assessable within routine training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty delivery by attending radiation oncologists with physicist and therapist participation in MDT-style debriefing and plan-review teaching
- Preparatory packages (contouring atlas, planning note, pitfalls summary) before each module
#### Constraints
- The fixed-module approach is described as most useful as a blueprint for sampling different dimensions of competence rather than producing uniform gains across all disease sites within a limited rotation

### Target Learners
- Radiation oncology postgraduate trainees

### Learning Goals
- Target delineation (contouring)
- Structured treatment-plan review
- Multidisciplinary clinical reasoning
