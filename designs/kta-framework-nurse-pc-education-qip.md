# Knowledge-to-Action framework as the guiding structure for a nurse education quality improvement project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The project was guided by the action cycle of the Knowledge-to-Action framework, which the article describes as "involves identifying the problem, adapting knowledge to the local context, assessing barriers to knowledge use, selecting, tailoring, and implementing interventions, evaluating outcomes, and monitoring and sustaining knowledge use". The QIP operationalized this through stakeholder engagement, a literature search, semi-structured interviews, tailored hybrid sessions, and pre/post evaluation. Sessions were also designed based on Knowles's Adult Learning Theory principles, addressing gaps identified by learners to encourage self-directed learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stakeholder engagement across project stages to adapt knowledge to the local context
#### Constraints
- Ongoing monitoring and evaluation of knowledge use were not possible because of the short duration of the QIP (eight weeks)

### Target Learners
- Nurses caring for heart failure patients in tertiary cardiac care units

### Learning Goals
- Palliative care concepts
- Symptom management
- ACP/GOC patient-family communication
