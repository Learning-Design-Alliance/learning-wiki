# Posterior probability lesson with natural frequencies, optional diagrams, and embedded questions or elaborative interrogations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A paper-and-pencil statistics lesson adapted from Heuer (1999) with materials from Sedlmeier and Gigerenzer's (2001) experiments, presenting "two examples of scenarios in which base rate and specificity probabilities were presented, and then the posterior probability was calculated using natural frequencies." Diagram conditions varied whether diagram information was also in the text; questioning conditions placed one question after approximately every paragraph. The lesson was evaluated in a randomized experiment with 245 undergraduates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All lessons began with a reminder that participants would answer questions based on the lesson; control participants were instructed to read the lesson twice.
#### Constraints
- Posttest and pretest problems were presented as text only, with no visuals.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory psychology

### Learning Goals
- calculating posterior probability from base rate and specificity information
