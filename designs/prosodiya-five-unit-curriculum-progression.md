# Five-unit curriculum progressing from syllable stress awareness to automation of spelling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The training's pedagogical structure is a five-unit progression: "The training consists of five curriculum units" covering syllable stress awareness, syllable structure, orthographic markers, spelling, and consolidation & automation. Each part starts with easy exercises and difficulty continuously increases and adapts individually across units, chapters, subchapters, and levels, so children first attend to stress patterns, then learn vowel-length marking rules, then consolidate and automate their knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Difficulty must adapt individually and content must be unlocked progressively to keep children on track
#### Constraints
- The acquisition phase covered the first two-thirds of the 66-level schedule, with the last third devoted to training and automation

### Target Learners
- German primary school children in grades 2–4 with poor spelling skills

### Learning Goals
- Orthographic knowledge of vowel length marking combined with syllable stress awareness
