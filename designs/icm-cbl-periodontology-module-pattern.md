# ICM periodontology module pattern: self-study preparation followed by small-group case discussion of digitized patient cases with lecturers as mediators

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level design combining the inverted classroom with case-based learning: students first acquire declarative knowledge via lectures, slides and self-study on an e-learning platform, then attend two four-hour units in which each student classifies a real digitized patient case (staging and grading), develops a patient-specific treatment concept, and presents and discusses it in small groups. The article states "the inverse principle was synergistically combined with “case-based learning” (CBL) for didactic reasons in this periodontology module", and that "The periodontology module comprised two teaching units of four hours each". Lecturers acted "rather as mediators than as instructors".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Correct classifications of the patient cases must be determined beforehand by experienced periodontists
- Self-study materials must not be too complex for students to understand on their own
#### Constraints
- Designed as a pandemic substitute for patient-free dental education; the authors state it is not an alternative to patient-based learning

### Target Learners
- final-year (10th-semester) dental students

### Learning Goals
- diagnostic confidence in classifying periodontal patients (staging and grading)
- development of patient-specific treatment concepts
- clinical decision-making
