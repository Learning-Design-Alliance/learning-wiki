# BLS training design combining Peyton's four-step approach, small-group practice and key-message emphasis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's BLS training combines Peyton's four-step approach, pair practice with scenario solving, a 1:6 instructor-to-student ratio, and emphasis on key messages. The authors state: "The most important strengths of our BLS training are the optimal instructor-to-student ratio, the application of Peyton’s four-step method and the unified theme of the practice." They cite evidence that a group size of six enables correction of more than 80% of errors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors hold ERC certificates or are trained following ERC educational guidelines; manikins (Ambu Man C Torso) are used for training, examination and assessment
#### Constraints
- Almost all students had prior BLS training, so the course functioned as a repeated session

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- basic life support and cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills
