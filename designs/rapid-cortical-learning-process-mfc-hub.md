# Rapid cortical learning process: the MFC as a hub supporting knowledge construction through a projection from the teacher's TPJ

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that within the limited time period of real classroom teaching, a rapid cortical learning process supports students' knowledge construction independently of hippocampal activity. In this account, "the MFC serves as a hub of a rapid cortical learning process, supporting knowledge construction through a projection from the teacher's TPJ." Blended teaching recruits this process: prior recalling begins construction in MFC with global attributes prioritized, and later lecturing facilitates a shift to local-attribute processing, whereas lecturing alone keeps representation of the original structure in TPJ.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Combination of low-structured student recalling and high-structured teacher lecturing within the classroom session
- Wearable fNIRS measurement of prefrontal cortex and right temporoparietal junction in both teachers and students
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one university graduate course with 21 students with valid data and four novice teachers
- fNIRS covered only the bilateral prefrontal cortex and right TPJ, so other candidate regions were not measured

### Target Learners
- University graduate students in real classroom teaching

### Learning Goals
- Construction and reorganization of knowledge structure taught in a lecture-based course
