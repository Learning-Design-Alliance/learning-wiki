# Hidden Time framework: undetected measurement delay as a bridge from kinematic data to relativity concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a 'Hidden Time' t*, defined as "a time not detected by the instrumentation", which accounts for the initial Y-offset as initial velocity times t* (or as an initial height imprecision). Three times are distinguished in the incline experiment: the effective motion time tx, the chronometer-measured time tm, and t*. This framing lets students connect experimental relations H = H(tm) with the theoretical model H = H(tx) and prepares the derivation of time-dilation-like relations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first appreciate the importance of precise time measurement from their own conventional-clock free-fall attempt before the Hidden Time discussion
#### Constraints
- The quantum-mechanical uncertainty discussion that could follow the offset is postponed to centre attention on time

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate Electrical Engineering students in introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- distinguish measured time from effective motion time
- understand proper time and simultaneity via information flow
