# Hierarchical sequential simulation course structure moving from precourse self-study to scenario practice and assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course organises training as a hierarchy of sequential modules that build from foundational knowledge toward applied practice: mandatory precourse reading and self-assessment, a seminar on simulation principles and human factors, introduction to the manikin and simulated circuit, a hands-on troubleshooting workshop, scenario training in pairs with observation via live video feed and expert-led debriefing, case-based discussion applying acquired skills to more complex cases, and a concluding formal assessment. The article states this structure was chosen "in order to integrate both the clinical and nonclinical training components required for successful ECMO delivery".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Precourse material completion was a mandatory requirement before the practical component.
#### Constraints
- Scenarios were drafted in consultation with medical education experts to deliver specific learning outcomes relevant to ECMO emergency situations.

### Target Learners
- intensive care clinicians learning a complex technical procedure

### Learning Goals
- technical circuit management
- nontechnical crisis skills such as communication and leadership
