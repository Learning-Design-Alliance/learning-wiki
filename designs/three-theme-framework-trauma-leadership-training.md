# A three-theme framework of perceived best practices for training trauma resuscitation leaders: formal vs informal curriculum, novice vs senior learner techniques, and interprofessional training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes its qualitative findings into three major themes of perceived best practices: "formal vs informal curriculum; training techniques for novice vs more senior learner; and interprofessional training." The framework maps seven training methods (reflection, feedback, hands-on learning, role modeling, simulation, group reflection, didactic) onto informal versus formal curriculum and onto learner level, as summarized in the article's Table 1. It was derived from modified grounded theory analysis of 25 interviews with attendings, residents, fellows, and nurses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs should include both formal and informal components, adapt methods to learner experience, and incorporate multidisciplinary and interprofessional opportunities
#### Constraints
- Derived from perceptions at a single large urban Level 1 trauma center; the study measured perceptions, not measured changes in learning, behavior, or clinical outcomes

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents
- Trauma fellows
- Attending physicians
- Emergency department nurses

### Learning Goals
- Trauma resuscitation team leadership
- Communication and collaboration in resuscitation teams
