# Australian university's streamlined alpha-launch process for piloting and scaling educational technology

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A multi-step institutional process for acquiring educational technology, developed by an Academic Director at an Australian university. It combines a prioritized list of technology initiatives informed by external research (Horizon and Gartner reports), a market scan, and a time-boxed pilot: "An alpha launch runs for 6 weeks, or half a semester, with the idea that if it yields positive results, the technology can be in place campus-wide by the beginning of the following semester." During the launch the Office of Learning and Teaching tracks five metrics including student evaluations, grades, engagement, faculty evaluation, and longer-term student resilience. The term alpha launch signals no guarantee of continued licensing, countering pilots that inevitably led to long-term use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leadership by a designated faculty member (the Academic Director) to spearhead the process
- Regular stakeholder feedback mechanisms, including a showcase every 6 weeks and a technology list re-prioritized every 6 months
#### Constraints
- The article states the alpha-launch study design is not sufficiently rigorous to allow causal inferences
- Recommendations about scaling up may be made before some important results (end-of-course grades, longer-term resilience metrics) are available

### Target Learners
- Higher education students at a university implementing educational technology campus-wide

### Learning Goals
- Improving pedagogy and student outcomes through vetted educational technology adoption
