# Full-day school workshop sequence of three 90-minute sessions combining learning activities with preference elicitation for systems modelling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The workshop design consists of three 90-minute sessions during school hours, using interactive activities and games to discuss the basics of the green energy transition, potential conflicts related to renewable installations, and climate justice. Questionnaires then collect student perspectives on key power system aspects aligned with the planning model. The workshops are described as facilitating "collective learning" and providing "context for informed energy choices," acting as a building block for mutual understanding between young people, local stakeholders, and national planners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery during school hours as full-day compulsory workshops
- Interactive activities and games designed to foster meaningful participant engagement
- Questionnaires aligned with the energy system planning model
- A follow-up feedback session to discuss how preferences influence system design
#### Constraints
- Tailored to the national Norwegian context and the highRES model's characteristics; the authors note a one-size-fits-all approach may not lead to effective policy implementation at local levels

### Target Learners
- Students aged 15-16 from diverse backgrounds in lower-secondary school

### Learning Goals
- Understanding renewable electricity technologies and their conflicts
- Developing informed, expressed perspectives on energy transition choices
