# General Rule vs. Case-by-Case analogical instruction structures

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A framework distinguishing two ways of structuring analogical instruction: a Case-by-Case approach in which students "use it to answer specific questions in the target physical domain," and a General Rule approach in which students use the analogy "to create a general rule that could apply across all situations in the target physical domain." The account holds that seeking a general rule pushes students toward the deep structure of the analogy, while analyzing cases in isolation fosters idiosyncratic, surface-feature-based rules. The article grounds this in cognitive-psychology work on analogical mapping and generalization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An analogy between a known domain and the target domain, plus a task structure that directs students either to specific cued applications or to generating a general rule
#### Constraints
- The article states the advantage may depend on the particular target physics principles and the kinds of questions used to assess learning; for some concepts, applying rules to situations may matter more than stating a rule

### Target Learners
- 1st or 2nd year undergraduate engineering majors in introductory calculus-based physics

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the relation between electric field and electric potential (direction and magnitude of the electric field from equipotential lines)
