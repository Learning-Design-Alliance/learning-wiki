# Arrange depression lectures or courses in nursing curricula to build both knowledge and favorable attitudes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because nurses who had attended depression lectures in school held more favorable attitudes toward depression (B = 1.854, p = .010 in the regression), the article recommends embedding depression content in nursing education. The authors state this finding "emphasizes that nurse faculty members at schools should arrange lectures or courses related to depression for nursing students, not only to increase their knowledge of depression but also to cultivate positive attitudes toward depression." Around two thirds of surveyed nurses (66.4%) had not attended any depression classes or courses in school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Depression lectures or courses must be arranged within nursing school curricula by nurse faculty members
#### Constraints
- The school-lecture association is cross-sectional and correlational; the sample was drawn from 3 Taipei-area hospitals and cannot be generalized to all nurses in Taiwan

### Target Learners
- Nursing students
- Registered nurses in acute-care hospitals

### Learning Goals
- Late-life depression knowledge and favorable attitudes toward depression
