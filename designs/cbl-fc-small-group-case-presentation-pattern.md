# CBL/FC nephrology bedside teaching pattern: pre-class materials, small-group case presentation, decision-role analysis, and teacher summary

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's CBL/FC intervention is a lesson-level design in which students receive topic-related cases, PowerPoint materials and handouts one week before class, prepare a case presentation in "small groups comprised of 4 or 5 persons", then in class "placed themselves in the role of the decision-maker" to work through diagnosis, differential diagnosis, further examination and therapeutic regimen, with teachers answering questions and summarizing pitfalls and pearls afterwards. After-school thinking questions and exam preparation close the cycle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers prepare topic-related materials including related cases, PowerPoint materials, and handouts, delivered one week before class
- Students preview materials and prepare slides for a case presentation
- Teachers are experienced clinical teachers who were trained before teaching
#### Constraints
- The study did not include CBL-only or FC-only groups, limiting conclusions about which component yields the best efficacy
- The study was limited to the Department of Nephrology, and approaches differ across departments, especially between surgery and internal medicine
- Students trained for only one month, which did not allow a crossover study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate clinical medicine students (clerks and interns) on nephrology rotation

### Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning and problem-solving
- medical history taking and physical examination
- medical record documentation
- consideration for patients' welfare
