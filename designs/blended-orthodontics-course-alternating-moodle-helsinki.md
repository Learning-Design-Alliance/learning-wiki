# Blended undergraduate orthodontics course with alternating remote and in-class lectures plus Moodle self-guided materials

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A mandatory third-year orthodontics course at the University of Helsinki delivered 19 theoretical lectures to 51 students, with nine live online lectures on Zoom, one video-recorded lecture, and the remainder in-class, using the same teaching staff for both formats. The Moodle platform supplied "additional reading material, videos, and interactive HTML5 educational game contents" for 11 sessions with activity-completion tracking, and learning objectives followed Bloom's taxonomy. The summative examination used 38 newly written single-best-answer multiple-choice questions completed in-class under Safe Examination Browser.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Handout material summarizing the main points was provided for all lectures; missed mandatory lectures required a written assignment verifying the learning objectives.
#### Constraints
- Lecture delivery modes were allocated in an alternating pattern rather than randomized, which the authors identify as a source of possible allocation bias.

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate dental students

### Learning Goals
- orthodontic theory: craniofacial growth, occlusion development, malocclusion etiology and classification, diagnosis, cephalometrics, and management of developing dentition
