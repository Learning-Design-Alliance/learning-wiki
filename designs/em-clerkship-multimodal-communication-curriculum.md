# Multimodal communication curriculum: asynchronous video plus direct CAT patient feedback delivered face-to-face

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's curriculum has two parts: "1) an asynchronous video on communication skills; and 2) delivery of direct patient feedback from the CAT questionnaire to the student." The approximately 13-minute video uses narrated slides and structured interviews with high-rated EM faculty and social work staff. Clerkship directors then delivered the patient's CAT scores and free-response comments to the student in a face-to-face meeting discussing ways to improve. It was designed using Kern's curricular development principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students watch the video and receive their CAT scores and patient comments from clerkship directors in a face-to-face meeting; trained staff administer the CAT to eligible discharged patients who can identify the student by photo
#### Constraints
- Only discharged, lower-acuity ED patients without interpreter needs were surveyed; the multimodal design prevented discerning which component drove the outcome

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students in an emergency medicine clerkship

### Learning Goals
- patient communication and interpersonal skills
