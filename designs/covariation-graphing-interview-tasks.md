# Three animation-based graphing tasks adapted from the Hobson and Moore mathematics study

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The replication used three think-aloud interview tasks with short animations (approximately 5 seconds, playable at will): Going Around Tacoma (distance from Tacoma vs. total distance traveled), Ferris Wheel (cart height vs. total distance traveled), and Square Track (distance from the wall vs. total distance traveled). Tasks were "minimally adapted from the mathematics study," with language changes made solely for participant understanding and validated by preliminary interviews. Participants received written work sheets and a computer with the animations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Animations functionally identical to those in the original RUME study; small language changes validated by preliminary interviews
#### Constraints
- Participants were not explicitly told the speeds were constant; the Tacoma and Square Track tasks involve only linear relationships and could not probe direct analysis of changing rate of change

### Target Learners
- physics PhD students as interviewed population

### Learning Goals
- eliciting covariational reasoning about relationships between two distances in motion contexts
