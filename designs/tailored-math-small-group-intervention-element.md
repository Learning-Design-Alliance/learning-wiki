# Tailored small-group mathematics intervention with screening, dialog-based teaching and an adaptive learning path

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 12-week small-group mathematics instruction intervention for the 20% lowest achievers, inspired by Math Recovery but with an adaptive learning path and assessment by the intervention teachers themselves. "Common to all of them was that they began with an investigative screening-test and -conversation, mapping pupils' understanding of mathematics, deficiencies (math holes) and their emotional perception of mathematics." Teaching was dialog-based, in 25–35 minute sessions four times per week, using specially developed materials, with teachers working to fill math holes and improve vocabulary, motivation and desire for learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers participated in a four-day preparation course; coached arms added a three-day coaching course for the school mathematics coordinator
#### Constraints
- Only around 50% of the intended 48 sessions were actually held, particularly in grade 8 due to cancellations

### Target Learners
- Low-achieving pupils in mathematics, the 20% lowest scorers in grades 2 and 8 in Danish public schools

### Learning Goals
- Mathematics competencies across curriculum areas, including filling individual math holes, mathematical vocabulary, and motivation for learning
