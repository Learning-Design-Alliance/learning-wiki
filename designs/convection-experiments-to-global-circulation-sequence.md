# Three-part sequence from small-scale convection experiments to a global-circulation worksheet

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This resource is organised as a three-part instructional sequence: two hands-on experiments establish that heated air rises and that a continuous heat source sustains a convection stream, after which a worksheet asks students to apply the same mechanism at planetary scale. The article states that "This resource consists of three parts that illustrate the power of the Sun driving a global air circulation system". The sequence moves from local, observable phenomena to the large-scale Intertropical Convergence Zone and Hadley cell.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials per group including a lamp of at least 100 W, fireproof surfaces, and either a cardboard tube or the provided construction template for the tower
#### Constraints
- The flying-flames activity involves an open flame and "is only suitable for students that are responsible enough to handle a flame", with teacher supervision or demonstration advised

### Target Learners
- Students aged 12-16 in middle or secondary school

### Learning Goals
- Explain that warm air rises above cooler air
- Explain that a continuous heat source produces a continuous convection stream
- Transfer the convection mechanism to Earth-scale circulation and climate zones
