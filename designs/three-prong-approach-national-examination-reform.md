# Reform national examinations using a three-pronged approach of monitoring exams, coordinated stimulating teaching and learning tasks, and portfolios of performance tasks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a 'three prong approach' to national examination reform: monitoring examinations addressing political, practical, security, fairness and accessibility issues; coordinating 'playful and stimulating teaching and learning tasks' through well-managed inter-agency implementation and public engagement; and a 'portfolio of performance tasks, as an agricultural educational instrument' promoting creativity and problem solving through artifacts of mastery learning and lifelong learning. This synthesizes the Malawi high-stakes testing challenges and England's lack of managerialism with the agricultural model of education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Solid inter-agencies management and coordination across bodies such as the Singapore Examinations and Assessment Board and the Ministry of Education, plus public engagement and stakeholder time to prototype in stages
#### Constraints
- Warnings against a hasty national roll-out approach; better coordination and more time for stakeholders to prototype in stages

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals
