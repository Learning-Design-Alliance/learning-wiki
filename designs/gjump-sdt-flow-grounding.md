# G-Jump framework grounded in Self-Determination and Flow theories with self-referenced progress over peer rivalry

## Description
The article grounds the G-Jump framework theoretically in Self-Determination and Flow theories. It holds that gamified assessment fosters intrinsic motivation by satisfying needs for autonomy through self-paced progression and competence via immediate feedback, while maintaining an optimal balance between task challenge and student skill level to prevent cognitive overload and facilitate flow. Design choices also align with Nicol and Macfarlane-Dick's model of formative feedback as a motivational mechanism supporting self-regulation.
