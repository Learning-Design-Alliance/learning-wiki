# Four-facet problem solving competency model grounded in rule acquisition and rule application

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's competency model divides problem solving skill into four facets: "analyzing givens and constraints", "planning a solution pathway", "using tools and resources effectively and efficiently", and "monitoring and evaluating progress". The first facet maps to rule acquisition and the remaining three to rule application, following recent research distinguishing knowledge acquisition from knowledge application. The model was developed from a literature review and implemented in Bayesian networks, one per game level, to produce overall and facet-level estimates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-game indicators must be identified for each facet to supply evidence to the Bayes nets
#### Constraints
- The model was developed and calibrated for middle-school students playing Use Your Brainz; the article notes the need to verify results with a larger sample

### Target Learners
- middle school students (7th grade)

### Learning Goals
- problem solving skill, overall and on four facets
