# Designing buggy projects for peers puts students in control of bugs and frames failure as a productive social experience

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Debugging by Design (DbD) is a constructionist activity in which student groups create, exchange, and solve open-ended, personally relevant buggy e-textiles projects. Students decide on bugs to include (at least 5 in code, at most 1 in the circuit diagram), write project statements, and prepare circuit and aesthetic diagrams for peers to build and solve. The approach "aims to put students in control over bugs, framing failure as a productive, social experience rather than a negative, discouraging one." It extends constructionist design-for-others approaches by shifting from designing functional artifacts to designing buggy ones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work within an open-ended physical computing unit (e-textiles) where facing failure and unanticipated challenges is expected, with teacher approval of designs and exchange of project plans between groups.
#### Constraints
- Explored in one specific context—designing e-textiles in an introductory computing course during virtual pandemic schooling.

### Target Learners
- secondary students in introductory computing courses, including historically marginalized populations

### Learning Goals
- debugging practices
- programming growth mindset
- comfort with failure
