# A three-part model for integrating computation into a standard lecture course without changing course structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a model for computational instruction that non-specialist faculty can adopt in standard physics courses. It relies on: (1) developing computational learning goals that reflect basic computing understanding, (2) creating instructional materials that leverage modern technologies, and (3) assessing how students use computation in an open-ended format. The authors state the model works "within the constraints of faculty who do not specialize in computation teaching standard physics courses by placing a strong emphasis on an adaptable curriculum."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An adaptable curriculum and instructional materials that do not require lecture time for computational instruction, leveraging the flipped classroom instead
#### Constraints
- Faculty lacking expertise with the chosen computational environment may find drawing from the limited resources challenging; resources do not yet exist for other courses such as electromagnetism or quantum mechanics

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level physics, engineering physics, and astrophysics majors in a middle-division classical mechanics course

### Learning Goals
- basic fluency with computational modeling, including mapping physical situations to computational tasks and numerically solving differential equations
