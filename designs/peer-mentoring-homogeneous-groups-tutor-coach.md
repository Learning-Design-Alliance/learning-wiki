# Blended peer-mentoring program structure: academically homogeneous peer groups of five to eight students with mentors acting as tutors and coaches

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Albion program assigns students to peer groups led by advanced undergraduates selected via application and interview for mastery of material, appropriate personality, and willingness to help. Students are grouped "according to mathematics background and enrollment in mathematics or physics courses, so that groups are as academically homogeneous as possible". Mentors run two-to-three hours of weekly meetings, tutor physics and mathematics, coach students on class expectations, normalize the struggle of transitioning to college work, and report meeting summaries and concerns to faculty via a shared Google document, maintaining two-way communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors must have completed at least the first two years of physics and calculus courses and be selected through an application and interview process
- Mentors need access to student assignments and practice materials, including solutions when available
- Two-way communication between mentors and faculty and staff, including reporting concerns about struggling students
#### Constraints
- Implemented at an undergraduate-only institution with no graduate students; mentors are paid undergraduates
- Mentor food expenses for group activities are reimbursed by the department

### Target Learners
- first-year physics and engineering (DDPE) students, including those below calculus placement

### Learning Goals
- mastering introductory physics and mathematics coursework
- normalizing the struggle of learning hard material
- accepting identity as a physics or engineering student
