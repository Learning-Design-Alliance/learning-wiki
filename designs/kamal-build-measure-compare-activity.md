# Build–find–measure–compare: a kamal activity sequence from construction to checking latitude against modern maps

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A 90-minute activity sequence in which students first build a kamal from plywood or stiff cardboard with a knotted cord, then learn to find Polaris via the Big Dipper, measure Polaris's elevation with the cord, and read their latitude from the knot–angle table. The sequence closes with students checking their result 'with a local map that provides coordinates or on-line services like Google Maps or Google Earth' and discussing why the values differ.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Finding Polaris is a prerequisite for the success of this activity
- a spot outside with a good view to the northern sky and the horizon
#### Constraints
- The activity can only run once the North Star is visible, so 'the summer time may not the best season to carry out this activity'

### Target Learners
- students aged 14–16 in middle or secondary school

### Learning Goals
- building and using a historical navigational instrument
- determining latitude on Earth from the elevation of Polaris
- finding Polaris to determine cardinal directions at night

- Pole Height Equals Observer Latitude [+M]
- Kamal Latitude Error Course Deviation [~M]
