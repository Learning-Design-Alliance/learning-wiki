# Five-phase design-thinking process bridging students as residents and as students in CCS urban development projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The educational design used a design-thinking process comprising five phases: "discovery, definition, ideation, experimentation and model construction, and testing and presentation." In discovery, professionals present the problem and students learn the urban-developer perspective through the What Is a Place? module, interview residents, and photograph good, bad, and developable places. Definition develops problem scoping and empathy with local community members; ideation generates many ideas; model construction uses LEGO, miniature and outdoor models, and digital tools such as Minecraft; and presentation elicits feedback from urban developers and architects. The process ran over two weeks per class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local urban developers who set authentic challenges connected to students' local environments and participate in feedback
- Teachers and researchers introducing the needs of both students and other citizen groups during discovery and evaluation
#### Constraints
- Interventions lasted one to two weeks per class; in the second iteration some classes were reduced to one week due to COVID-19 lockdown

### Target Learners
- Students aged 11–15 in grades 5–9 in vulnerable residential areas of Copenhagen

### Learning Goals
- Strengthening design-thinking skills and the ability to take part in solving complex, authentic problems derived from local expertise
