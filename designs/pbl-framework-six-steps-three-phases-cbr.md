# A six-step PBL framework with three learning phases for teaching CBR

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's framework, built from a thematic synthesis of 14 articles on PBL, organises PBL as six steps: knowing the problem scenario, identifying facts, identifying knowledge gaps, developing resources, gaining insights into applying knowledge, and reflecting on knowledge gain. A tutor guides students through three major phases of learning: problem analysis, self-directed learning, and reporting. Knowledge acquisition comprises a discursive, theoretical, conceptual level and an active, practical, experiential level, and the framework "emphasize on learning" rather than problem solving alone, in contrast to the Maastricht seven-step model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor to guide students through the problem analysis, self-directed learning, and reporting phases, with consultation arrangements available
#### Constraints
- The framework was developed from a synthesis of PBL literature from 2008 to 2017 and tested only in CBR-related higher education courses

### Target Learners
- University students in health, rehabilitation, education, or social work degree programmes

### Learning Goals
- CBR competence, problem-solving, critical thinking, self-directed learning
