# Cartoon-based lesson routine: read, interpret, debate, and draw cartoons about a local issue

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The experimental treatment followed a recurring lesson pattern: students worked in subgroups of 5-6 to read cartoons, explain what they understood, express views on the presented issue, consider why it was presented that way, voice agreement or disagreement with the artist, and relate the issue to their local area. Students then "were asked to draw their own cartoons about water issue", with an award for the best cartoon. This combines interpretation, argumentation and creative production around a controversial topic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Before implementation, the experimental group was taught two lessons about cartoons reading and drawing so they could understand and use cartoons during the lessons
#### Constraints
- Applied in a three-week unit on water in the Grade 4 social studies textbook in one Omani school

### Target Learners
- Grade 4 students (age 10)

### Learning Goals
- Awareness of water issues
- Critical thinking about a controversial issue
- Expressing and debating views
