# Composting as an interdisciplinary Natural Sciences teaching context organized around the three methodologies of science: observation, experimentation and mathematical analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes composting as a curricular bridge between environmental education and the Natural Sciences (Biology, Physics, Chemistry), worked through what it calls "as três metodologias da ciência: a observação, a experimentação e as análises matemáticas". Scientific discussion is driven by guiding questions (biodegradation, aerobic and thermophilic processes, microorganism classification), temperature monitoring supplies data for graphs and rates, and chemical equations (e.g., glucose respiration) support reasoning about conservation of matter and energy transformation. The framework is presented as covering a large share of the Natural Sciences curriculum while developing scientific literacy and student protagonism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A school composter with monitored parameters (temperature, aeration, moisture, C:N) so that observation, experimentation and mathematical analysis can all be exercised
#### Constraints
- The approach is developed for windrow (common) composting without worms; vermicomposting is presented as an alternative with different management needs

### Target Learners
- students from the sixth year of fundamental education (ensino fundamental)
- high school Biology students

### Learning Goals
- scientific concepts in Biology, Physics and Chemistry
- scientific literacy
- student protagonism
- collaborative school projects
