# Secret objectives: advertising hidden inconsistencies between experimental reality and provided information to provoke inquiry

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A secret objective is defined as 'any inconsistency between the experimental reality and the information provided to students prior to starting work on an experiment'. Students are explicitly challenged to identify these hidden discrepancies and then given freedom to explore and understand the experiment, which the authors present as a way of encouraging genuine inquiry elements in introductory laboratory courses while guaranteeing experimental success through the script.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- At least one secret objective that students might reasonably be expected to find must exist
- Data should be fitted approximately by the idealised theory within some range, with clear, testable sources of discrepancy
- Several laboratory sessions devoted to each experiment, with time identified as the crucial factor in successful implementation
#### Constraints
- Assessment must be aligned to recognise and reward inquiry behaviours rather than task completion or record keeping

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics laboratory students

### Learning Goals
- genuine inquiry behaviours
- critical analysis of experimental data
- research skills
