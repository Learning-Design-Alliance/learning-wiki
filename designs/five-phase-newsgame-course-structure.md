# Five-phase course structure for teaching game design through serious game projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's courses were structured in five phases: introduction via concrete example games, discovery of serious games through student web searches, a class on design methods and tools, group design and execution of a serious game project with the teacher acting as a mentor, and presentation and evaluation of executed projects. The design-and-execution phase "represented the majority of the course in terms of hours."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need access to game creation tools suited to their programming expertise, and example games to introduce the serious game concept.
#### Constraints
- Observed with engineering students in fourth- and fifth-year master's programs at two French establishments.

### Target Learners
- engineering students in computer science, civil engineering, chemical engineering, civil aviation, and meteorology

### Learning Goals
- video game design
- understanding of serious games and current events
