# Digital learning unit introducing qubit concepts with either the Bloch sphere or the Quantum Bead representation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A digitally delivered (iPad, SoSci Survey) learning unit for upper secondary students introducing quantum computing concepts — quantum state, superposition, quantum measurement, phase gate, and amplitude — with one of two three-dimensional visual qubit representations. The Bloch sphere "represents the state of a single qubit as a vector on the surface of a unit sphere", while Quantum Beads "visualize quantum states using a red-green color gradient mapped onto a sphere". Materials were identical in content, structure and language, differing only in the representation, followed by five application-oriented multiple-choice tasks with recorded processing times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks could only be solved once essential information had been extracted from the assigned representation; a colour perception screening (Ishihara test) was used to rule out difficulties interpreting the colour-coded Quantum Bead.
#### Constraints
- Materials were in German and made available only on request; the phase gate task did not differentiate between groups and was excluded from analysis.

### Target Learners
- Upper secondary school students (class levels 11–13, ages 15–18) with low prior knowledge of quantum physics

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of quantum state, superposition, quantum measurement, phase, and amplitude in the context of quantum computing
