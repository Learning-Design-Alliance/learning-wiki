# Inquiry as teaching science as science is done, with facilitator-guided student investigation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article defines inquiry as "teaching science as science is done": students learn scientific concepts by figuring them out rather than being given answers from a textbook or lecture. Inquiry activities are not completely open-ended; teachers monitor progress and guide students toward desired conclusions through facilitation, which the authors say helps students internalize content as activities they did and conclusions they figured out. Activities are backward-designed: goals and student needs are considered first and elements prioritized accordingly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators who monitor student progress and guide students toward desired conclusions
#### Constraints
- A student embarking on a self-designed investigation will likely hit dead ends or be confused by side issues, so support is needed

### Target Learners
- introductory college astronomy students, including students historically underrepresented in the sciences

### Learning Goals
- learning content intertwined with research skills such as interpreting evidence, reasoning, and asking questions
