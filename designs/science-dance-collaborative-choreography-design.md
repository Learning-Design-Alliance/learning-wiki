# Interdisciplinary science-and-dance course design with partial shared sessions culminating in collaborative choreography performances

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Two disciplinary courses (Polymer Science and Dance Choreography) each taught as usual for approximately 80% of sessions, with the remaining 20% spent in shared sessions covering nine 50-minute meetings. Students first did basic choreography exercises and were introduced to science concepts judged optimal for choreography, then worked in small interdisciplinary groups of 5-6 to propose and develop choreography based on scientific concepts, ending with performances to an audience followed by discussion. The instructors explicitly sought "a proof of principle not aesthetic fluid motion".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors from both disciplines co-plan and co-deliver; a fraction of each course's grades is based on the shared project
#### Constraints
- The article states the design was very ambitious: students spent only approximately 10-15 hours together over the semester because required disciplinary content could not be dropped

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science majors (seniors in chemistry, engineering, physics)
- undergraduate dance majors (freshmen and sophomores)

### Learning Goals
- translating between conceptual and physical representations of scientific phenomena
- collaborative group problem solving across disciplines
