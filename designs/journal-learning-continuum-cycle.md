# The journal learning continuum: topic selection, research, reflection, writing, coded feedback, self-correction and peer sharing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes journal writing as "a learning continuum in which students have several opportunities to learn throughout the continuum chain." The cycle starts with selecting a journal scenario or topic with instructions and criteria; students research the topic, review relevant vocabulary, grammar or culture, reflect, and express an opinion in writing; teachers give feedback via correction codes; students find and correct their own errors, verify the corrections, and often share what they learned with fellow students. Assignments progressed from lower-order thinking tasks early in the course to higher-order thinking and problem-solving tasks in semester three.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments need their own instructions and objectives, with criteria and measurable objectives set for journal writing
- Students set a time limit for journal writing (40-60 minutes)
- Journals are collected daily and feedback is given in the form of correction codes
#### Constraints
- The pattern was implemented within a fifty-week intensive government language course and the authors note the study is based on generalized knowledge that may or may not work for other similar classes

### Target Learners
- adult foreign-language learners in an intensive MSA course, aged 29-35, with university education

### Learning Goals
- listening and reading comprehension in Modern Standard Arabic
- reflective and critical thinking in the target language
