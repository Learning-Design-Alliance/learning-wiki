# Arduino open-project student-lab teaching unit

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A project-based lab for third-year fundamental physics students at Paris-Sud, organized as a two-day training period on Arduino boards and sensors followed by a five-day project period in which pairs conceive, build, test, and measure a phenomenon of their own choosing. The main pedagogical objective is "a realistic introduction to experimental physics", focusing on how to perform a scientific study. Students brainstorm topics collectively with no pre-made topic list, teachers validate feasibility and mentor, and assessment is a 15-minute oral exam and a written report.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A large set of diverse low-cost materials and sensors must be available so students can choose their own topics
- Teachers validate project feasibility and check for special needs
- Students must produce measurements, explain what they did and why, and discuss the physics
#### Constraints
- The Arduino boards' specifications present strong limitations compared to specialized data acquisition cards in terms of digitalization and sampling rate
- Students are told to expect delays or changes to their original project

### Target Learners
- third-year university fundamental physics students

### Learning Goals
- experimental physics skills from experiment conception to data analysis and interpretation
- autonomy in leading a research project
- team work
