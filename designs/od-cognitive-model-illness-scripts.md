# Proposed cognitive model of how clinical reasoning ability is scored during an Oral Debrief

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a cognitive model (Fig. 5) mapping the three think-aloud themes onto the scoring process: candidates' and examiners' own cognitive processes underpin the OD, candidates translate thinking into verbalised responses, and examiners steer discussion through questioning based on their own reasoning. In doing so "the OD elicited comparisons between the candidate's and examiner's illness scripts". The model organises the identified scoring factors, from markers of higher CR ability to markers of poor reasoning, with particular attention to patient safety implications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The OD must elicit verbalised reasoning via semi-structured examiner prompts so candidates' thinking can be compared against the examiner's own illness scripts
#### Constraints
- The model derives from think-aloud data with sixteen examiners in a small-scale pilot and is offered as a proposed model rather than a validated one

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students
