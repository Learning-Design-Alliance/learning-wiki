# Bimanual tracking task (BTT) four-week motor learning paradigm

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The bimanual tracking task is a laboratory motor-learning paradigm in which participants track a visual target moving along a target line by rotating two dials with the left and right index fingers, controlling the vertical and horizontal movements of a cursor. Performance is scored per trial as a percentage from 0–100% reflecting accuracy in speed, movement direction and distance from the target, with on-screen score feedback after each trial. In this study, "participants were stratiﬁed by sex and pseudo-randomly assigned to either a complex or a simple training group", training four sessions per week of 30 min for four weeks, with an individually adapting staircase-like level paradigm for the complex group and a standardized 18-task progress test administered six times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The task can be adapted to participants' improving skill levels over time to secure continuing motivation and challenge; the complex group requires an individually adapting staircase-like level paradigm
#### Constraints
- The article notes the task was chosen as a novel task not part of the daily motor repertoire, and that GM changes with motor learning had not yet been examined for the BTT specifically

### Target Learners
- young healthy adult volunteers (mean age 24.7 years)

### Learning Goals
- bimanual coordination and visuomotor integration
