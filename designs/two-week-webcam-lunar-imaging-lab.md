# Two-week lunar imaging lab for non-majors: Week 1 telescopic observation, Week 2 image analysis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-part laboratory for an 'Astro 101' survey course for non-majors in which students make real sky observations of the Moon in Week 1 and perform data analysis in Week 2. Students capture telescope images with an adapted webcam, 'produce a mosaic of the Moon's face' using digital tools, and measure crater sizes on their own images. It was designed to run 'without need for a campus observatory,' making it replicable at many institutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small telescope, a USB camera adapted from a webcam, and a computer; groups of four students sign up for 50-minute observing shifts and flexible scheduling around clear nights.
#### Constraints
- Originally built around 35 mm film and transitioned to webcams as film photography left the typical student's experience base; the author notes a future without webcams and is exploring smartphones.

### Target Learners
- non-major undergraduate 'Astro 101' students

### Learning Goals
- understanding that observational astronomy involves quantitative data acquisition with instrumentation
- making measurements from one's own images
- value of prior planning and good note-taking
