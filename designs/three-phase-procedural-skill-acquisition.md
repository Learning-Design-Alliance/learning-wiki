# Three-phase framework for acquiring procedural proficiency: simulated deliberate practice, patient-focused simulation, then supervised integration into patient care

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Kneebone's principles of transformational change and learning, the article proposes that procedural skills "should probably be learned in three distinct phases". Phase one is learner-centred practice of the procedural aspect with a well-defined goal, deliberate practice, direct observation and feedback to reach mastery, best done in a simulation laboratory. Phase two uses patient-focused simulation to practise the whole scope of the skill, including informed consent, collaboration and communication, in a safe environment where mistakes may benefit deeper learning. Phase three integrates the procedure into direct patient care with supervision, feedback and assessment, for which DOPS is suggested.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A knowledgeable educator must design practice conditions using validated learning principles
- Simulation laboratory settings for deliberate and directed self-guided practice
- Supervision, feedback and assessment during the final integration phase
#### Constraints
- Assessment of the second phase is logistically more complex and may require more time if included in a traditional OSCE
- OSCEs and IPPIs are expensive and labour intensive

### Target Learners
- Internal Medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- procedural skills proficiency including technical, communication and consent components
