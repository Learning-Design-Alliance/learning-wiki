# Classroom game apparatus: ball-hit measurements, compass sticks, and biased dice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The instructional element is a physical game kit: scientists prepare and measure qubit-students by hitting them with a ball, place compass sticks on the floor to mark the measurement axis and its positive side, and use biased dice (standard, tetrahedral, multi-dice, or apps) to reproduce the stochastic measurement outcomes. The article notes dice construction "can also be considered as an activity itself to work with probabilities". For measurements in x and z only, a standard die suffices, with odd numbers indicating outcome +1 and even numbers outcome −1.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Biased dice or apps matching the probabilities for each measurement-direction separation; a painted Bloch plane; compass sticks; a ball.
#### Constraints
- For the CHSH game, dice must give p0 = cos²(3π/8) ≈ 0.15 and p1 ≈ 0.85 for π/4-separated directions.
