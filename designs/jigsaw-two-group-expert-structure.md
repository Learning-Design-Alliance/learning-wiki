# Jigsaw technique as a two-group cooperative learning structure with expert teaching roles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes jigsaw as a cooperative learning structure in which "students are members of two different groups, the 'home group' and the 'jigsaw group'." Students first learn an assigned portion of the material as experts in jigsaw groups with peers from other home groups, then return to home groups to teach their portion. In this study the six physics laboratory experiments defined the six expert portions, and the structure was credited with making each student responsible for teaching material to the group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each member must be assigned a distinct portion of the material to learn and later teach
- Members should be informed about their responsibilities, per the authors' conclusions
#### Constraints
- The authors state it is not recommended for very crowded classes due to difficulty in controlling the class

### Target Learners
- science-education teacher candidates in university physics laboratory courses

### Learning Goals
- recognition and use of physics laboratory materials
- laboratory experiment skills
- understanding of experiment subtopics
