# Guided inquiry learning model via Liewelyin's six-stage inquiry cycle

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements guided inquiry as a six-stage inquiry cycle from Liewelyin (2002): "(1) Inquisition - starting from the statement to be investigated, (2) Acquisition - enabling a brainstorming procedure, (3) Supposition - identifying the statement to be tested, (4) Implementation - designing and carrying out  a plan, (5) Summation - collecting evidence and drawing conclusions, and (6) Exhibition - sharing and communicating results." It was delivered over 12 meetings to fifth-grade science classes, with the teacher acting as facilitator rather than transmitting knowledge directly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students lack prior inquiry experience, so the guided format with teacher-posed questions and structured stages is used
- Assessment during inquiry is continuous, using observation sheets, portfolio, and report product assessment
#### Constraints
- The article reports the model favored reflective-style students; impulsive students showed no significant advantage over conventional teaching

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade elementary school students

### Learning Goals
- science learning achievement
- scientific process skills such as predicting, experimenting, concluding, and communicating
