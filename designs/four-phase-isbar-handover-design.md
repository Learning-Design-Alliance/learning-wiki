# Four-phase progressive ISBAR handover teaching design: didactic instruction, role-play, scenario simulation and OSCE

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a four-phase intervention design for teaching ISBAR-structured handover in an obstetric simulation and practicum course. The phases progress from ISBAR-structured handover didactic instruction and expert modelling, through role-play with antenatal assessment and handover practice, to scenario simulation with standardized patients and video-assisted debriefing, and culminating in OSCE assessment. Within each scenario, students rotated through sender and receiver roles so that every student actively performed the ISBAR handover. The authors offer this sequence as a potentially useful educational strategy rather than a validated model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Eight parallel practice groups each facilitated by one designated faculty facilitator, all registered nurses with a master's degree or above who completed an 8-h standardization workshop
#### Constraints
- The authors state the four-stage sequence is offered as a potentially useful educational strategy rather than a validated model, and its effectiveness warrants further evaluation across different settings and populations before broader adoption

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- structured clinical handover communication
- clinical reasoning in obstetric contexts
