# Medical faculties implementing faculty-wide basic ultrasound training can choose the tutor-training format that best fits their local conditions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because tutee scanning-skill gains were significant and comparable whether tutors were trained by course, internship, or both, the authors conclude that faculties "can probably train their student-tutors in a way that optimally suits their specific conditions". Each format has stated trade-offs: the course guarantees full curriculum coverage and scales well but limits practice time; the internship offers abundant practice cheaply but risks incomplete topic coverage; the combination carries both advantages and both disadvantages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The curriculum should comprise only simple ultrasound skills that can be mastered in a very short timespan, since most students lack extensive supervised laboratory practice
#### Constraints
- The finding applies to very simple ultrasound skills; for more complex skills such as echocardiography, tutors trained even three weeks did not match faculty teaching success, as the authors note from prior work

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in faculty-wide mandatory ultrasound courses

### Learning Goals
- Basic ultrasound scanning skills
