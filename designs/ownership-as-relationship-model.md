# Ownership-as-relationship model of student project ownership

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The ownership-as-relationship model treats ownership as a dynamic relationship between students and their projects, not a static trait. In the article's own terms, "ownership was modeled as a relationship between students and projects that consists of three types of student-project interactions and evolves across three sequential project phases." The interactions are students' contributions to, emotional responses to, and new knowledge about the project; the phases are choice of topic and team, execution of the research, and creation of deliverables. This paper refines the third phase, renaming synthesis as deliverables, and elaborates the new-understanding interaction across all three phases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must work in groups of 2–4 on multi-week projects lasting 4–7 weeks in upper-level physics lab courses for the model's phases to be instantiated as described
#### Constraints
- The article states that, due to the qualitative design, it cannot make probabilistically generalizable claims and aims instead for theoretical generalizability; phase-less atypical interactions were excluded from the analysis

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in upper-division physics lab courses

### Learning Goals
- developing new knowledge about a project's domain, apparatus, management, and the nature of science
