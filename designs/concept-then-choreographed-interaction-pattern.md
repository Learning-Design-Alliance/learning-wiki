# Two-part workshop pattern: learn concepts through individual movement, then choreograph interactions in teams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The workshop follows a two-part design: first students learn about particles and connect each particle to a dance move proposed by themselves or the dancer, assimilating names together with body movements; second, teams produce a choreography of a particle interaction such as scattering, annihilation and pair production, deciding the particles, choreography and music themselves. The article states "a requirement is that at least one of the moves developed in the ﬁrst part should be retained", linking the parts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- At least one of the moves developed in the first part should be retained in the team choreography
- Students are given complete responsibility both for the development of the choreography and the choice of music
#### Constraints
- Team sizes are tied to the interaction choreographed: three students for scattering, five for annihilation and pair production, seven for an atom

### Target Learners
- Key Stage 3 school students in dance or science settings

### Learning Goals
- Understanding particle identities and interactions such as scattering, pair-production and annihilation
- Expressing scientific ideas through dance
