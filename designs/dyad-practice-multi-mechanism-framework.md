# Multi-mechanism theoretical framework for the effectiveness of dyad practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames dyad practice's effectiveness through three complementary accounts: peer-assisted learning theory emphasising "inter‐subjective cognitive co‐construction, shared knowledge and peer feedback"; motor-learning and neuroscience accounts attributing benefits to peer observation, grounded in mirror-neurons that are "activated when an individual observes and performs the same action", allowing rehearsal without hands-on practice; and cognitive load theory, under which dyad practice "may divide the cognitive load imposed on learners by shared information processing". The article uses this framework to motivate the trial and interprets its efficiency findings as confirming existing mechanisms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The article notes dyad practice may reduce learning effectiveness if the task is characterised by low cognitive load
#### Constraints
- The study did not attempt to explore the relative contributions of observation, feedback, shared knowledge or cognitive co-construction on the observed performances

### Target Learners
- medical students in final clinical rotations with minimal ultrasound experience

### Learning Goals
- complex clinical skills combining technical and diagnostic performance (transvaginal ultrasound)
