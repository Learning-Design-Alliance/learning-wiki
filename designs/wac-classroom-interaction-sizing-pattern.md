# Distributed workshop pattern sized to preserve live classroom interaction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The WAC pattern replicates the social dimension of classroom learning at a distance: students can turn to a neighbor, ask questions aloud, and hold two-way dialog with the instructor. The authors argue "Instant messaging and chat channel formats cannot duplicate this kind of interactivity", so enrollment is capped around 400 students and about 25 satellite sites, the level where student questions begin to overwhelm available time. Beyond that ceiling, interaction is greatly muted and a webinar or MOOC model would suffice instead.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Site TAs (no domain expertise needed) to keep minor technical issues from becoming overwhelming at scale, plus chat back channels for very technical issues
#### Constraints
- For less technical material the ceiling could be raised; if constant interaction and hands-on support are not critical, a webinar or MOOC model could suffice

### Target Learners
- Busy and productive students, faculty, and scientists who cannot travel to a centralized event

### Learning Goals
- Highly technical material requiring live questions, two-way dialog, and hands-on support
