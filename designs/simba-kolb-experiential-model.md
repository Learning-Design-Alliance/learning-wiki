# SIMBA model: simulation via instant messaging grounded in Kolb's experiential learning theory and Kern's six-step curriculum framework

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
SIMBA (Simulation via Instant Messaging for Bedside Application) is a simulation-based learning model that "leverages simulation gaming and Kolb's experiential learning theory to enhance the educational experience", using widely available digital platforms (WhatsApp and Zoom) for flexible, cost-effective, remote delivery of realistic clinical scenarios. In this study the model was adapted through "Kern et al's six-step curriculum development framework", a systematic approach for creating high-quality healthcare training curricula, in which stakeholder and participant interviews informed an LED-specific intervention. The model is presented as scalable and accessible for learners balancing clinical duties with limited protected teaching time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Anonymised, consultant-validated case transcripts; trained moderators; structured pre-, during- and post-session survey and feedback mechanisms
#### Constraints
- Unlike resource-intensive, in-person simulation models, SIMBA is explicitly positioned as a remote, instant-messaging format; the article notes future iterations could incorporate AI-driven feedback, adaptive learning pathways and augmented reality features

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals in postgraduate medical settings, here LEDs including IMGs

### Learning Goals
- Improving knowledge, confidence and skills in managing acute medical scenarios
