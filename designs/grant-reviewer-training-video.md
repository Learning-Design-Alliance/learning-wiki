# Eleven-minute grant reviewer training video covering five fundamental issues of rating scale use

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training artifact is an "11 minute professional quality training program video" emphasizing five issues: agencies depend on reviewers for accurate information; reviewer scores influence funding decisions; the meaning of each value on the NIH 9-point scale and weakness definitions; assigning evaluation scores matching the criteria; and why reviewers must carefully read and understand the criteria. It contrasts academic grading norms with grant scale values, e.g. a mid-range NIH score represents a "good" proposal, includes practice items with correct answers, and is designed for administration just prior to reviewing to minimize recall issues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administered just prior to reviewing proposals to minimize recall issues, as the article states
#### Constraints
- The training addresses only rating scale usage and review criteria knowledge; the article states it does not address all potential sources of noise that may affect scoring

### Target Learners
- Novice and experienced grant peer reviewers (Public Health professors in this study)

### Learning Goals
- Knowledge of NIH rating scale values and weakness definitions
- Accurate assignment of rating scale scores
- Careful attention to grant review criteria
