# Two-compartment module design balancing realism with feasibility: reserve anatomically detailed layers for critical tasks and simpler layers for repetitive practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the TURB training module, the bladder is built as two compartments with different fidelity levels. The lower compartment carries the trigone, two ureteric orifices, and four tumors in a three-layer wall (mucosa, muscle, serosa) and is "primarily dedicated to essential training and structured examination"; the upper compartment carries four more tumors in a two-layer wall for repetitive practice at lower production cost. Per the article, "The structural difference between the two parts (three-layered lower vs. two-layered upper) balances realism with feasibility, reserving anatomically detailed sections for critical tasks."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Replaceable, rapidly exchanged modules so each training session starts with consistent tissue-like properties
- Identification of which tasks are critical (examination-oriented) versus repetitive so fidelity can be allocated accordingly
#### Constraints
- The two-layer upper compartment provides reduced anatomical fidelity, so it is reserved for repetitive practice rather than structured examination

### Target Learners
- Urology residents practicing TURB resection
- Two trainees can share one bladder module per session

### Learning Goals
- Tumor resection with loop movement and depth control
- Tumor base resection and muscle sampling
- Repetitive resection practice across varied tumor sizes and positions
