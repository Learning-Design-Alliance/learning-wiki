# Ancestral Digital Self agent and reproducible stimuli workflow

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Ancestral Digital Self is defined as "an AI-generated pedagogical agent presented in prerecorded videos that mirrors the learner's facial features and vocal timbre, representing a historically situated version of the self". The team built a reproducible stimuli workflow with an archaeology team: generative AI created Wubeiling daily-life backgrounds, facial transfer and voice cloning produced the digital-self narrator, and a standardized female Lingnan-featured character served as the non-self agent, composited into subtitled videos in two counterbalanced A/B content sets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners provide a frontal photograph and a 10–15 s fixed-script voice recording for facial transfer and voice cloning.
#### Constraints
- The study evaluated fixed prerecorded narration videos rather than a real-time interactive system; content is history-specific (Wubeiling culture).

### Target Learners
- Adult learners (aged 19–29) from undergraduate, master's, and other educational backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- History learning (Wubeiling culture knowledge) and affective engagement with historical narratives
