# Robot-as-alien-creature inquiry exercise structure: demonstration, model-and-experiment assignment, group hands-on testing, closure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A lesson-level pattern that emulates an ethological research situation: the teacher presents a robot whose controller is unknown, demonstrates its light-following behavior, then asks students to 'develop a model of the internal works of the robot and to produce a hypothesis veriﬁable through experimentation'. Students work in small groups of 3-4 that they form themselves, run their proposed experiments on the robots, and the activity closes with either teacher explanation or student reports. The authors 'did not correct the models', only suggested simplifications to ease verification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough robots available so students can perform their experiments, or a teacher-selected subset of experiments tried on one robot
- Teacher visits groups to discuss whether experiments can decide whether a model should be discarded
- If demonstration of light tracking is desired, calibration prior to demonstration
#### Constraints
- The closure is left to the teacher's criteria; in the reported run time constraints led to teacher explanation of the controller rather than student reports

### Target Learners
- 3rd year bachelor mechatronics students
- students in courses from general introduction to science to hardware oriented lectures

### Learning Goals
- scientific thinking and reasoning
- hypothesis formation
- experimental verification of models
