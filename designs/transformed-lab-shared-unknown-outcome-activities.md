# Transformed introductory lab course design: shared unknown-outcome activities with group work, prediction, uncertainty use, and electronic lab notebooks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The transformed course redesigned every lab activity and lecture around faculty-derived learning goals. In the transformed lab sessions, "all students did the same activity in a given week, mostly working in pairs or groups of three," with activities prompting students to check with partners or other groups and sometimes to combine data with their whole lab section. Students generally did not know the numerical result of a measurement beforehand; instead they made preliminary measurements and used them to make predictions or measure unknown quantities, with explicit use of measurement uncertainty concepts, and created electronic lab notebooks during lab instead of writing reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities guided by a manual with a pre-lab assignment completed before class, and occasional supplemental lecture sessions
#### Constraints
- The course remained traditional in several ways: students still completed pre-lab assignments, attended weekly guided lab sessions, and attended occasional lectures

### Target Learners
- second-semester undergraduate physics students in a large-enrollment stand-alone lab course

### Learning Goals
- expert-like epistemology of experimental physics
- understanding of measurement uncertainty
- scientific communication
- favorable views about experimental physics
