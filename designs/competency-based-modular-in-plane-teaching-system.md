# Competency-based modular teaching system for the long-axis in-plane technique

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces a competency-based modular teaching system structured in three steps: training minimum required component skills (needle and probe handling, for both hands), integrating those skills with coordinated two-hand motion, and advanced training on simulated vessels varying in depth, size, and direction. Trainees "could repeatedly practice each component skill until it is mastered, and then integrate them as a complete skill set". Evaluation follows integration, and trainees who fail enter feedback-loop training. "Progression is based on acquiring competency in each component and not on time pent practicing the skill", distinguishing it from time-based training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Component skills must be mastered and pass evaluation before progression; trainees who fail enter feedback-loop training to acquire the necessary skill
#### Constraints
- Real-time feedback from an instructor cannot be obtained when the system is used for self-learning; difficulty maintaining motivation is an inherent drawback of self-learning systems, per the authors

### Target Learners
- senior physicians
- residents
- nurses novices to the in-plane technique

### Learning Goals
- performing the long-axis in-plane approach for ultrasound-guided vascular access with needle tip visualization and puncture accuracy
