# Conference-style writing as an extension of project-based learning that completes the pedagogical arc through authentic disciplinary communication

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The article argues that a conference-style paper functions as more than an added assignment: it acts as an extension of PBL by requiring students to synthesize research, structure an argument, and present technical work in a disciplinary genre. The authors state that "the conference-style paper helps complete the pedagogical arc of project-based learning by turning project work into authentic disciplinary communication." A project lets students investigate, model, and analyze; the paper requires organizing that work into a research narrative, demanding problem identification, literature engagement, method justification, and interpretation. In quantum education specifically, extended writing may surface gaps in understanding hidden behind symbolic manipulation and force conceptual integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PBL must already be established in the course; the paper is positioned as a culminating extension, not a standalone writing task
- Scaffolding and support structures distributed across the semester
#### Constraints
- Best suited to advanced courses, especially graduate students who are aspiring researchers; the literature does not identify the conference-style paper as a standard PBL endpoint, so it is one possible way among several to realize authenticity and public communication

### Target Learners
- graduate engineering students
- advanced undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- disciplinary participation
- scientific communication
- conceptual integration of quantum content
