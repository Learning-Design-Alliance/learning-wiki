# Three types of digital technology integration in schools with school-wide personalized learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article systematizes its three case analyses into a taxonomy of how schools integrate digital tools into daily practices under a school-wide personalized learning policy. As the authors state, "A systematization of our analyses resulted in three different types in terms of how schools integrate digital tools into their daily practices": selective use according to individual teacher preference, selective use according to individual student preference, and structural use in accordance with a school-wide strategy. The taxonomy is derived from qualitative content analysis of interviews, observations, and documents in three contrasting Swiss lower-secondary schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Derived from multiple-case analysis of schools that have implemented personalized learning school-wide, with data triangulated across student questionnaire, interviews, observations, and curriculum documents
#### Constraints
- Based on three cases selected as the schools with the most frequent digital technology use among 31 Swiss lower-secondary schools; the authors present the types as indications for future research and practice rather than a validated typology

### Target Learners
- lower-secondary students (8th grade) in schools with personalized learning concepts

### Learning Goals
- implementation of personalized learning through digital technology use
