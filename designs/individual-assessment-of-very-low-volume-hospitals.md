# Assess Very Low-Volume hospitals individually for core training hospital designation rather than relying solely on the inpatient volume threshold

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that hospitals below Japan's 3,000-inpatient regulatory threshold should be evaluated on their educational environment rather than excluded by volume alone, since "residents at Very Low-Volume Hospitals achieve GM-ITE® scores and mental health outcomes comparable to those at larger hospitals." Accreditation decisions should additionally consider supervision quality, rotation availability, and support systems that the study did not fully assess.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An individual assessment recognizing an educational environment where residents can acquire basic clinical skills
#### Constraints
- The study did not assess key accreditation factors such as supervision quality, rotation availability, or resident support systems, so results are evidence on inpatient volume rather than sufficient grounds for accreditation decisions

### Target Learners
- PGY-1 and PGY-2 postgraduate medical residents in Japan

### Learning Goals
- acquisition of basic clinical knowledge and skills
- resident well-being and burnout prevention
