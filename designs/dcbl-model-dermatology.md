# Dynamic case-based learning (DCBL) model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The DCBL model embeds the temporal dimension of disease progression into case-based teaching for dermatology practical education. Cases are arranged chronologically so that "Educators continuously monitor patient conditions to update the clinical database, while students use lesion images and disease records to engage in dynamic observation and analysis", refining diagnoses and treatment plans in real time. It is grounded in Social Constructivist theory and Situated Learning Theory, positioning students to co-construct understanding through interaction and authentic context-rich experiences, moving from rote learning toward diagnostic reasoning and adaptive clinical problem-solving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Typical dynamic cases with chronological clinical images, auxiliary examination results, and treatment plans at each time point of disease progression
- Educators who continuously monitor patient conditions and update the clinical database
#### Constraints
- The article reports the model had not yet been implemented in actual teaching at the time of the study; effectiveness evidence is limited to perceptions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students studying dermatology

### Learning Goals
- dynamic understanding of skin lesion progression
- diagnostic and therapeutic clinical reasoning
- self-directed learning
