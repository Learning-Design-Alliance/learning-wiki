# Labor-based contract grading (LBCG): an assessment framework where labor determines credit and student-designed contracts govern grade increments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
LBCG is an assessment framework in which "the time and effort (“labor”) a student spends on an assignment determines the credit they receive", while the contract component requires students to design projects with clearly defined goals, deliverables, and proficiencies. Students start from a baseline grade (here a B) that is decremented by incomplete core assignments and incremented only by completed extra assignments, making the final grade calculable at any time. The author positions LBCG as aligned with UDL's multiple means of expression, overlapping with specification-based and competency-based grading but differing in that students design the specifications and the rubric is a binary complete/incomplete.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small class sizes, since providing formative feedback and managing extra assignments is time- and labor-intensive
- Significant in-class discussion and small-group work to provide frequent informal formative assessment
#### Constraints
- Implemented in an upper-level course for motivated physics/STEM majors; the author notes applying it to introductory courses could be more difficult and might require significant adjustments

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics majors with widely varied preparation

### Learning Goals
- programming and computational methods in physics
- mathematical methods of physics
