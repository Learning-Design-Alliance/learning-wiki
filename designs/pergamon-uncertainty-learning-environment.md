# Pergamon multimedia learning environment with temple reconstructions and uncertainty expressions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A computer-based, linear learning environment about the ancient city Pergamon used as the study material. Participants worked through "a computer-based, linear learning environment about the ancient city Pergamon and two of its most important buildings—the temple of Athena and the temple of Demeter", including pictures of reconstructions with audio explanations in which lexical hedges expressed uncertainty values of twelve architectural elements per temple, each with a scientific justification. Four picture conditions varied the uncertainty visualization: none, stop light colors, geometric contrast, or both combined.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Audio explanations with the reconstructions had to be played in full length and could not be paused; navigation backwards was not possible
#### Constraints
- Uncertainty values and their verbal scientific justifications were fictional, created for experimental reasons

### Target Learners
- Adult participants from a broad variety of disciplines without archeology, history, or architecture background

### Learning Goals
- Memory for architectural elements, uncertainty values, and their scientific justifications

### Claims

- [Uncertainty values of more certain elements and their verbal scientific justifications were remembered better than those of uncertain elements](../claims/certain-uncertainty-values-remembered-better.md) [+W]
- [The appearance of architectural elements marked as certain is remembered better than those marked as uncertain in multimedia learning with uncertainty expressions](../claims/certain-elements-remembered-better-than-uncertain.md) [+W]
