# E-learning course 'How to effectively teach older people to use new technologies' and its 192-response open-ended dataset

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study's data source is an e-learning course titled "How to effectively teach older people to use new technologies", promoted through Universities of the Third Age networks and online platforms. Participants answered an open-ended Microsoft Forms prompt asking for three examples of effective teaching methods, forms, or tools that generate enthusiasm among older adults; the coding covered "a total of 192 responses - three responses each from 64 people aged 20 to 52 (7 men and 57 women)". Responses were exported to MAXQDA Pro and analysed with inductive sentence-by-sentence coding.

#### Constraints
- Respondents are prospective or current trainers, not older learners themselves; the authors note older adults' own perceptions were not collected

### Target Learners
- prospective and current trainers of older adults, caregivers, and andragogy/geragogy professionals

### Learning Goals
