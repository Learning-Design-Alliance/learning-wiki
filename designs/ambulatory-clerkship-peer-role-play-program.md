# Educational program combining ambulatory clerkship with peer role-play using real outpatient narratives

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An educational program combining an ambulatory clerkship, where each student interviews a real outpatient for 30 minutes, with a peer role-play exercise at a clinical simulation center. Actual outpatient narratives "were used as peer role-play scenarios in which the students acted as outpatients." Sessions include five to seven students and a supervising teacher; the patient does not participate and role-plays are videoed for group discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An attending doctor identifies a patient willing to be interviewed by a student before each encounter
- A supervising teacher and facilitator run the role-play and structured group debriefing
#### Constraints
- The patient does not participate in the role-play
- The program focused on communication skills and clinical reasoning, and the authors judged it insufficient for developing understanding of patients' narratives

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students rotating through the Department of General Medicine clerkship

### Learning Goals
- medical interview training, communication skills, clinical reasoning, and understanding patients' narratives
