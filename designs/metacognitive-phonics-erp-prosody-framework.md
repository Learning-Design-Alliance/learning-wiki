# Meta-cognitive phonics with prosodic ERP reinforcement as a mechanism for older L2 learners to overcome Critical Period Hypothesis barriers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's explanatory framework holds that older L2 learners can overcome Critical Period Hypothesis barriers by relying on cognitive reasoning rather than rote learning: meta-cognitive phonics techniques teach phonics patterns rather than sight words, so students can recognise or invent new lexis intuitively, while prosodic exercises using rhymes, songs or dictation create Event Related Potential events that, citing Nickels et al. (2013), open neurological circuits for efficient learning. The authors define prosody as "a series of exercises, which use the natural prosody of rhymes, songs or dictation to reinforce phonic patterns". The trial was designed to test these inter-disciplinary neurological findings in a TESOL classroom.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must concentrate on pattern recognition rather than memorised sight words
- Prosodic ERP-inducing material must accompany the patterns
#### Constraints
- The authors state the theoretical base comes from neurological research (Hensch, Nickels et al., Goswami) that still needed empirical testing in a TESOL classroom, which this small trial only partially provides

### Target Learners
- adolescent and young-adult L2 English learners beyond primary age

### Learning Goals
- L2 decoding skills
- L2 orthography and pronunciation
- lexical awareness
