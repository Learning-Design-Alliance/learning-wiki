# In situ hybrid fall simulation pattern: standardized patient actor, brief prebrief, 7–10 minute scenario, and three-phase debrief

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This lesson-level pattern embeds a scripted resident-fall scenario, enacted by a trained nurse actor as a standardized patient, directly on a living unit. Each activity combines a prebrief to establish psychological safety, a 7–10 minute simulation observed by a lead nurse, and a debrief with reaction, description, and analysis phases. The article describes it as "a lower-cost hybrid approach, using a trained actor representing a standardized patient—enacting a scripted scenario—and available technology" suited to resource-constrained nursing homes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A lead nurse trained in simulation design and facilitation to observe and debrief
- Nurse actors prepared with scenario objectives and a script, with training sessions before the first simulation
#### Constraints
- High-fidelity manikin simulation may be less feasible in resource-constrained nursing home settings
- Participation was expected of all staff within the vicinity; some participants found being observed intimidating

### Target Learners
- Frontline nursing home staff: RNs, registered practical nurses, and nurse aides/personal support workers

### Learning Goals
- Fall prevention best practices
- Postfall assessment and postfall huddle procedures
