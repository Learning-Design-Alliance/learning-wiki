# Brief text-based AI literacy intervention covering LLM working mechanism, limitations, and usage strategies

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A short educational text, created for the intervention group and grounded in Walter's (2024) conceptualization of AI literacy, that added three sections to a general ChatGPT introduction: how LLMs predict the next token, their hallucinations and biases, and prompting/usage strategies. The article states its "content was based on Walter's (2024) conceptualization of AI literacy, given its emphasis on the limitations and risks of AI" and included screenshots of ChatGPT errors, excluding ethics. It was delivered online via LimeSurvey before a reliance task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivered as readable text before the reliance task; the study excluded participants who read it in less than 30 seconds, implying meaningful reading time is needed.
#### Constraints
- The actual change in AI literacy was not directly measured; no pre-post measure was included, so the article cannot determine how much students' knowledge changed.

### Target Learners
- 12th-grade high school students

### Learning Goals
- Understanding how LLMs work, their limitations and risks, and effective usage strategies for ChatGPT
