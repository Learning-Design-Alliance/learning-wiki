# A learner-centered pharmaceutical care course combining dialogic expository teaching, role play, simulated patient, case studies, and virtual patient

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This 16-week mandatory course for fifth-year pharmacy students pairs pharmaceutical care content with a deliberately varied set of active learning strategies: dialogic classroom expository lessons in which knowledge is constructed through exchange, questions and discussion; role play with students alternating patient and pharmacist roles; a trained postgraduate simulated patient presenting a standardized scenario; case-study discussions; and virtual patient clinical cases running from patient assessment through care plan documentation. An expert focus group first identified the competencies needed for pharmaceutical care practice and developed activities to foster them. The course was piloted in 2013 and revised from student feedback before the 2014 study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an expert focus group to identify the competencies necessary for the practice of pharmaceutical care and to develop specific learning activities and teaching methods that would foster these competencies
#### Constraints
- the article notes the course's little contact with real patients, so a set of active learning techniques were used that can decrease the gap between training and actual patient experience

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate pharmacy students

### Learning Goals
- pharmaceutical care competencies: assessing patients' drug-related needs, identifying and solving drug-related problems, care plan development, communication and counseling
