# PHYS 170: a backwards-designed IPLS course re-imagining the introductory physics syllabus around biologically and medically relevant modules

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
PHYS 170 is Yale's calculus-based first-semester introductory physics for the life sciences course, offered since 2010 to roughly 140 students per year through two 75-minute class sessions, 50-minute discussion sections, and optional study halls. The syllabus is organized into 18 modules arranged as a concept map, with modules 5-10 and 17 deviating significantly from tradition: probability, random walks, diffusion, low Reynolds number fluid flow, mathematical modeling, statistical mechanics applied to molecular-biological processes, and gene circuits. The course was built by a "backwards design process21 that started with a number of key ideas and topics – diffusion, fluid flow, mathematical modeling, and statistical mechanics – that it would be necessary to include to achieve the overarching goals listed above." The stated goals include enabling deeper scientific understanding of biological systems, demonstrating applications of physics to life sciences via authentic examples, seeding enduring appreciation of quantitative approaches, and developing curricular materials other instructors can use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students have previously taken a first course in calculus
- Students possess chemical and biological sophistication from prior introductory chemistry and biology sequences
- Emphasis on Wolfram Alpha to facilitate sophisticated mathematical manipulations without requiring Mathematica knowledge
- Biologically-authentic examples embedded even in traditionally-covered modules
#### Constraints
- The laboratory component remains a separate half-credit class following a more traditional syllabus
- Multi-dimensional integrals are largely avoided, removing a source of significant discomfort
- Eigenvalue/eigenvector demands are limited to differentiating an exponential function and solving two simultaneous equations in two variables

### Target Learners
- Biological science majors and premedical undergraduates at an R1 research university
- Students from groups underrepresented in STEM disciplines

### Learning Goals
- Apply physical and mathematical principles to biological systems including the human body
- Use quantitative reasoning and modeling to understand living systems
- Develop appreciation of the power of physical and mathematical approaches in biology and medicine
