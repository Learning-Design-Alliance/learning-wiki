# Successful out-of-school science programs for girls share role modeling, hands-on interactive learning, and collaborative group projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Across the reviewed programs (GIST/VISTA, WIEP, Stevens OWP, FLEDGE-ling, PREP), the article identifies recurring design elements of successful interventions for girls in science. It states the most important aspects are: "Role model female scientists and exposure to a variety of career scientists", "Interactive learning, especially hands-on labs and activities with presenters", and "Group projects/activities to foster collaborative learning and teamwork". The pattern operates at the program level, structuring multi-week out-of-school experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to female scientists, engineers, or advanced students willing to serve as presenters and role models
- Laboratory and activity facilities supporting hands-on work
#### Constraints
- The article grounds this pattern in a cross-section of described programs rather than a controlled comparison of design features

### Target Learners
- High school and middle school girls

### Learning Goals
- Interest and confidence in science
- Awareness of science careers
- Collaborative and teamwork skills
