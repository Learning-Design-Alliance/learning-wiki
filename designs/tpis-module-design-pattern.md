# Teaching Physics in School module structure: selection, training, weekly school placement and reflective assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A year-3 optional 10-credit module whose main learning component is "a weekly 3 hour per week school placement in a local secondary school physics classroom". It differs from lecture-based options through an application and interview process, a 3-hour pre-placement training workshop, a named school teacher mentor, eight weekly reflection sheets, a 15-minute peer presentation, a 2000-word data-gathering assignment, and mentor formative comments. Placements include lesson observation, support roles, demonstrations and teaching short lesson segments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supportive school placements within reasonable travelling distance
- Mandatory DBS security check for working with under-18s
- School mentors provided with a handbook and kept to a low administrative burden
- Schools asked to provide physics lessons rather than general science as a priority
#### Constraints
- Places are limited by the number of school placements available
- Students are voluntary and supportive, not staff or trainee teachers, and must not hold exclusive responsibility for pupil safety, wellbeing or learning
- Sometimes general science classes are used as a compromise

### Target Learners
- year 3 undergraduate physics students at a selective research-intensive university

### Learning Goals
- communication and professional skills
- understanding of the realities of secondary physics teaching
- supporting school pupils' physics learning
