# 7E learning cycle model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The 7E learning cycle is a constructivist, inquiry-based instructional model with seven phases: elicit, engage, explore, explain, elaborate, evaluate, and extend. The article describes it as helping "make for an  effective learning process through seven steps," beginning by examining learners' prior knowledge before new content and ending with explicit transfer of learning. In the study it was operationalized as a Teachers' Instructional Guide used to teach ionic theory, oxidation-reduction, mole concept, and electrolysis over four weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be trained to follow the seven steps within a reasonable timeframe
- Assessment of prior knowledge (eliciting) must not be omitted
#### Constraints
- The article reports it is more complex, somewhat 'alien,' and requires more class-time and teacher expertise than case-based learning

### Target Learners
- senior secondary school chemistry students

### Learning Goals
- chemistry conceptual achievement
- transfer of learning
- positive attitude to chemistry
