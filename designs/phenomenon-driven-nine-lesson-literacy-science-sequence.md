# Nine-lesson phenomenon-driven sequence: introduce phenomenon, observe, question, experiment, share data, read, revise model, extend

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide sketches a nine-lesson sequence (approximately 45 minutes each) driven by a single phenomenon: why some playground surfaces are hotter than others. Students read an engaging narrative, record initial ideas in journals, make playground observations, narrow a testable question, design and conduct experiments, share and argue over data, read with a need to know, revise a class consensus model, and design engineering solutions. The guide notes "the entire set of lessons are driven by the single phenomenon."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A puzzling everyday phenomenon that can be explained if students master the focal science concepts
#### Constraints
- Lessons 1-3 are appropriate for grades K-6; Lessons 4-9 are most appropriate for grades 3-6
- The sequence takes place over nine lessons of approximately 45 minutes each, daily or across weeks

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in grades K-6

### Learning Goals
- Explaining a scientific phenomenon (Sun's effect on Earth's surface)
- Authentic reading, writing, and scientific discourse
