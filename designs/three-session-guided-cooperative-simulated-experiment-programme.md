# Three-session simulated-experiment programme combining cooperative and guided learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern that organizes a computational practice into three scheduled sessions: a theoretical lecture with a worked example, a spreadsheet session introducing the open-source tool, and a simulation-plus-validation session. The article states it presents "a teaching plan organized in three sessions that combine cooperative with guided learning methodologies", with students in small groups of 2-3 per computer terminal guided step by step through exercises. Planning is deliberately not constrained to the classroom, since free open-source codes let students install tools on their own desks and continue work off campus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free and open-source codes so students can install and run the tools on their own desktops, ensuring "the accessibility and repeatability of sessions"
- A meditated session structure that sequences prior knowledge, new knowledge, and the skills and attitudes to be developed
#### Constraints
- The OpenFOAM code's UNIX-based structure means that part of the lessons "has to be carefully step-by-step oriented" for learner comprehension

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- understanding non-steady-state diffusion and Fick's second law
- validation procedures for computational codes
