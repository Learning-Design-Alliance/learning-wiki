# The epistemologized algebra-based physics course: a reform shifting student epistemologies from equations and factoids to coherent, principle-based reasoning.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Between 2000 and 2005, the physicist and UMd-PERG colleagues reformed the algebra-based physics class to make it more appropriate for life-science students, a project that "epistemologized best-practices research-based instructional methods". The reform "emphasized shifting student epistemologies", moving learners "from pieces to coherence, from equations to concepts, from externally-transferred knowledge to internally-generated reasoning". The article reports the course "showed strong gains on both standardized conceptual and attitudinal measures" but did not significantly modify content for life-science needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research-based instructional methods as a starting point
- Emphasis on shifting student epistemologies toward coherence and internally-generated reasoning
#### Constraints
- The course did not significantly modify content to accommodate the needs of life science students, in part because the course served other populations as well

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life-science students and pre-meds in algebra-based introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- view physics knowledge as coherent and principle-based rather than as independent factoids and equations to memorize
