# Indoor gamma spectroscopy experiment with aluminum attenuation coefficient derivation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 4-hour preparatory laboratory activity in which students assemble a 4” × 4” NaI(Tl) detector with PMT and digital MCA, calibrate the gamma spectrum using the 40K and 208Tl photopeaks, and derive the linear attenuation coefficient of aluminum with a collimated 137Cs source and 16 aluminum layers. The experimental μ of aluminum (0.184 cm−1) is compared with the NIST reference μmass of 0.0747 cm2/g and with μ for air, so students "master, through a joint and interdisciplinary approach, the concepts of the highly penetrating nature of the gamma radiation".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 4” × 4” NaI(Tl) detector with MCA, a collimated 137Cs point-like source, aluminum layers, and the GammaTOUCH Android app for acquisition and calibration
#### Constraints
- Differences from the NIST reference value arise from factors the article names, such as the stainless steel crystal housing or the imperfect collimating effect of the hole in the lead shielding box

### Target Learners
- pre-collegiate students in groups of 5–8

### Learning Goals
- interpreting gamma-ray spectra
- determining linear attenuation coefficients
- understanding the high penetration capacity of gamma photons
