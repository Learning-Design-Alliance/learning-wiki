# Four design characteristics for virtual assembly training systems: realistic environment, appropriate difficulty, prudent guidance, natural interaction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates four key design aspects derived from constructivist theory for its VR/desktop assembly systems. These include a "Realistic Environment" with high-fidelity rendering that engrosses users, an "Appropriate Level of Difficulty" (a bench vise chosen for "its simple structure and appropriate assembly difficulty"), "Prudent Guidance" via floating and spline-based arrows that minimize cognitive load, and "Natural Interaction" mimicking physical object manipulation within the user's reachable range.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The design must minimize cognitive load; students new to virtual training need ample guidance, user-friendly interfaces, and logical interactions
- Difficulty must be moderate: too high instigates anxiety and lack of confidence, too low breeds tedium
#### Constraints
- Difficulty calibration was for a simple bench vise task; complex scenarios were not considered

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in engineering drawing courses

### Learning Goals
- structural cognition of mechanical assemblies
- assembly-disassembly skills
