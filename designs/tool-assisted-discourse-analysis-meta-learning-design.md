# Course design model: learning collaborative learning through tool-assisted discourse analysis to develop group-level meta cognition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a course design whose goal is to learn collaborative learning for first-year undergraduates, aiming to transform students' beliefs from a passive, individual model to an active, collaborative model supported by Knowledge Building and Constructive Interaction. Over a 15-week course with two five-week knowledge-building projects, students reflect after each project; the experimental design adds analysis of their own discourse with KBDeX. The authors claim this develops the group level of meta cognition, which they argue group activity experience alone cannot achieve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge-building project activities followed by a reflection workshop
- Tool-assisted discourse analysis during reflection for the meta-learning effect
#### Constraints
- The study did not evaluate knowledge creation outcomes; the authors state the limitation is that evaluation of knowledge creation outcomes was not involved

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- transformation of learning beliefs toward active collaborative knowledge creation
- meta-learning skills
