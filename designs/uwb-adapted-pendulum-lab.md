# UWB adaptation of the Thinking Critically pendulum lab (community and consequential-activity design)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The UWB pendulum lab is a three-session activity, instructed by professors with a maximum of 24 students per section, adapting the Cornell curriculum. Its design "emphasizes meaningful, intentional use of experimental practices and the importance of collaboration and community feedback." Students generate and revisit team agreements, measure a single class pendulum released from 10° and 20° with no instructions on how, build histograms of shared measurements, brainstorm and then implement their own uncertainty-reduction methods, and apply the t' statistic. The final experimental prompt is neutral with respect to outcome, and the simple harmonic model is introduced only after lab notes are turned in.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared data table and communal artifacts to spark inter-group discussion about results and conclusions.
- Instructor engagement during lab meetings that reinforces that students' use of tools should have purpose and meaning.
#### Constraints
- There is an agentive tradeoff: constructing a histogram is not the students' choice, though the task involves choice and deliberation such as selecting an appropriate bin size.

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students at a small public university campus on the quarter system

### Learning Goals
- engaging in intentional, purposeful activity and perceiving their work as consequential for producing knowledge about physical phenomena
