# Multi-view approach: lens imaging as a superposition of elemental images from different viewpoints on the lens

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes reinterpreting Kepler's point-to-point account of lens imaging: each point on the lens produces a whole camera-obscura-style image, and images from different points represent different views. The author writes that "each point on the lens produces a whole image" and adopts the term elemental images from Integral Imaging. The composite screen image is sharp only where these elemental images coincide, giving a holistic account that matches students' preconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pinhole array covering the lens to uncover the elemental images
- A translucent projection screen whose distance can be varied
#### Constraints
- Concepts of the students' holistic approach may vary among individuals, as the article notes for Fig. 1(b)

### Target Learners
- 12th-grade high school physics students

### Learning Goals
- Understanding how a lens forms images of extended objects

### Claims
- Pinhole Makes All Object Distances Sharp [+M]
- Elemental Images Sharp Only Where They Coincide [+M]
- Elemental Images Grow And Overlap As Screen Recedes [+M]
