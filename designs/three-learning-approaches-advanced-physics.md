# Three learning approaches for advanced physics: empirical, conceptual and theoretical, adapted from Piaget's reasoning levels

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose that "We identified three main learning approaches: empirical, conceptual and theoretical", adapted from Piaget's concrete operational, transitional, and formal operational reasoning levels. In reflection and refraction, the empirical approach uses demonstrations and Snell's law calculations, the conceptual approach uses Fermat's principle of least time, and the theoretical approach introduces Maxwell's equations as the overarching framework with the highest abstraction. Students use a combination of the approaches to master a topic. The framework is implemented as iOLM's three sublevels, ordered by abstraction (theoretical, conceptual, empirical).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An initial test (level 0) to determine each student's prior knowledge, since prior knowledge is assumed to influence learning rate
#### Constraints
- It is "not always possible to draw a clear distinction between each sublevel based on one of these three learning approaches, since some levels are less prone to a clear distinction"; the article had too little data to analyze the effectiveness of each approach independently

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students beyond freshmen, preselected on prior problem-solving ability

### Learning Goals
- mastering electromagnetic reflection and refraction through problem-solving success and topic explanation
