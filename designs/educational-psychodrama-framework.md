# Educational psychodrama as a constructionist, action-based teaching-learning model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Educational psychodrama, originating in Jacob Levy Moreno's group psychodrama, is defined as "a teaching-learning process based on the integration of personal interrelationships, action, the use of games and active teaching techniques". It shares with constructionist paradigms the notion of students as active agents in their learning, emphasizes the emotional and experiential component of learning, and considers three stages in knowledge assimilation: memory, play and dramatization. Techniques include role-playing, role reversal, roda viva, mirror, psychodramatic images and soliloquy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The use of these techniques must be defined and structured in the specific teaching context of higher education
#### Constraints
- Its application in higher education should be limited to the learning process and the pedagogical content, focusing on knowledge acquisition and professional training rather than personal events of students and teachers

### Target Learners
- university students
- university teachers

### Learning Goals
- active and meaningful learning
- professional skills
- creativity in teaching-learning processes
