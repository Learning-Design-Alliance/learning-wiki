# Spot the difference teaching method (SDTM): a game-based observational learning model for clinical skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
SDTM adapts the childhood "spot the difference" game — "a prototypical change blindness task" — to clinical skills training. Teachers predesign difference points (DPs), error-prone points inserted into flawed videos matched to standard videos; trainees watch, mark deficiencies on scoring sheets, then perform and verbally correct the skill, integrating observation learning, PBL, guided learning and peer-assisted teaching. The rationale is that the game doubly activates the right posterior parietal cortex, implicated in imitation, attention and long-term memory encoding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reference standards, either flawless videos or a set of training criteria, are essential elements for SDTM
- Difference points must be discussed and decided by the teaching group before class
#### Constraints
- PBL has situation dependencies, while SDTM does not; SDTM questions are teacher-designed with constant correct answers
- The training process of comparing former and later operation videos differs from the original game

### Target Learners
- first-year surgical residents

### Learning Goals
- basic clinical skills such as history-taking, cardiopulmonary resuscitation, dressing change, disinfection and draping, gowning and gloving
