# Pitch-level and intonation-contour framework for English If-clauses (Discourse Intonation Model perspective)

## Description
The article presents a four-level pitch framework (Low /1/, Normal /2/, High /3/, Extra High /4/ pitch phonemes) in which levels combine into intonation contours. It states that "these pitch levels do not bear any meanings alone. Instead, they come together and form patterns through which phrases, clauses, and sentences are made." Within this framework, If-conditional sentences take the /2 3 2→/ - /2 3 1↓/ pattern when the If-clause precedes the main clause and two words carry primary stress, and the /2 3 1↓/ pattern when only one word is stressed. The instruction is contextualized in Brazil's Discourse Intonation Model, which connects intonation to discourse function.
