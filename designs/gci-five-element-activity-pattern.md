# Five-element GCI activity pattern: human reasoning first, strategic AI consultation, critical evaluation, artifact creation, metacognitive reflection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A repeatable lesson-level sequence the authors operationalize across two 50-minute class sessions. Activities begin with individual thinking and pair/group collaboration using thinking routines, after which "only after human reasoning establishes foundation do groups consult AI for specific purposes (surfacing additional perspectives, critiquing preliminary analyses, generating alternative approaches)". Groups then interrogate AI outputs against their collective reasoning, produce tangible artifacts such as concept maps and layered diagrams, and close with explicit reflection on what humans contribute that AI cannot. The pattern holds AI as an enhancer rather than a replacement of collective human intelligence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI consultation must occur after human question generation and initial analysis, and explicit metacognitive framing must be provided throughout.
#### Constraints
- The authors describe it as one possible implementation designed for their context and constraints; the reported evidence is two sessions without control groups.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in group work

### Learning Goals
- collective inquiry and sense-making
- critical AI literacy
- formative visibility of thinking
