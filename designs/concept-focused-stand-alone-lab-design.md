# Stand-alone lab course design optimized for reinforcing lecture-course concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The studied lab courses were one-credit pass-fail courses with nine two-hour experiments, closely coordinated in timing and content with the lecture course, with goals explicitly focused on improving understanding of key lecture concepts. Design features include pre-lab activities with prediction questions, standard simple apparatus, "regular, explicit prompts for students to compare their observations to predictions, or to look for discrepancies or surprises in their data and to explain their causes", minimal error analysis, worksheet write-ups, and Logger Pro for efficient data collection. TAs received a teaching seminar, weekly meetings, and used Socratic questioning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TA preparation via a Teaching of Physics Seminar, weekly meetings with instructor and lab coordinator, and lecture attendance
- Pre-lab activities preparing equipment, technical skills, and physics concepts
- Detailed lab manuals with instructions and hints to avoid complications
#### Constraints
- Experiments kept simple without complex equipment; minimal or no error analysis; write-ups limited to worksheets

### Target Learners
- first- and second-term calculus-based introductory physics students, mostly engineering majors

### Learning Goals
- understanding of key concepts from mechanics and electricity and magnetism lecture courses
