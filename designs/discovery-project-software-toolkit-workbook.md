# Free and low-cost astronomy software toolkit plus printed student workbook supporting the discovery workflow

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The pilot used a chain of mostly free tools: Stellarium for basic concepts such as co-ordinate systems and magnitudes, Astrometrica for asteroid detection, Maxim DL and MuniWin for image analysis and light curves, the VSX and Catalina Sky Survey databases for checking prior discoveries, and WinEffect for detailed light curve analysis. A printed Young Astronomers Student Workbook, adapted from the Russian version, let students record progress and contained "short exercises and practical computational instructions". Photometric data reduction was completed by the astronomers before sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with school IT departments to ensure software was correctly installed; a projector was helpful but not necessarily a requirement
#### Constraints
- Maxim DL is only free for a trial period of 30 days; software sometimes failed, on which occasions workbook exercises were a good alternative

### Target Learners
- Key Stage 3 pupils in after-school astronomy clubs

### Learning Goals
- Astronomical data analysis
- variable star detection
- co-ordinate systems and magnitudes
