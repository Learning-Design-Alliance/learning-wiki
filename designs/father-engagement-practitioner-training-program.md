# Father engagement practitioner training program (face-to-face and online formats)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A brief training program developed by psychologists and child and family practitioners to build practitioner competencies for engaging fathers in parenting interventions, delivered in two formats. The face-to-face format was 4.5 hours and "included a combination of didactic presentations, use of video vignettes ... active skills training, and group activities and discussion on five topics", covering barriers to father engagement, positive engagement strategies, managing conflict, and planning father-inclusive practice. The online format covered the same content via audio-visual materials and workbook activities in about 2 hours, without rehearsal and feedback. Both formats were rated on average 4.1/5 for helpfulness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators follow a facilitator's manual; the article reports on average 93.8% adherence to manual content across workshops
#### Constraints
- The online format did not include active skills training in the form of rehearsal and feedback on roleplay of skills

### Target Learners
- Practitioners delivering parenting interventions across professional disciplines, including psychologists, social workers, counsellors, nurses and support staff

### Learning Goals
- Improving practitioner competencies in engaging fathers
- Increasing organizational father-inclusive practices and rates of father engagement
