# Require advanced high-school mathematics (Math III and Math C) for entrance to science-education departments and make undergraduate mathematics compulsory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because undergraduate-level physics requires advanced mathematics but most Japanese science-education departments require only Math I, A, II and B, the authors recommend requiring Math III and Math C in the Second-stage entrance exam, making differential and integral calculus and linear algebra compulsory undergraduate subjects, and recommending the Suken certification so students can recognize their achievement and be motivated to study advanced mathematics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Entrance examinations and curricula that include Math III, Math C, calculus and linear algebra; Suken 1st or pre-1st grade exams whose coverage is at most undergraduate-level calculus and linear algebra
#### Constraints
- Currently most departments do not require Math III or Math C, and giving university credits for passing Suken is not prevalent in Japan

### Target Learners
- prospective science teachers in university departments of science education

### Learning Goals
- advanced mathematics proficiency as a basis for learning physics
