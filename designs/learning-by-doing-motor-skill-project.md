# Learning by Doing: a personally meaningful direct-experience project for acquiring motor skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An active learning methodology in which each student "chose a motor skill of their preference (e.g. dance styles, musical instruments, sports, circus skills, etc.) to acquire and practice for 10 weeks so that students experimented with ML strategies within their own experience." Students defined a performance goal, chose performance measures, practiced 3–8 h per week, kept a log, joined biweekly discussions, wrote a paper with recommendations for learning the skill, and demonstrated their skill level. The article reports this gave students firsthand experience putting ML theory into context and let them experience what patients experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 10-week window with regular practice time (3–8 h per week) and biweekly structured reflection forms
- A teacher role of offering timely feedback and opportunities for students to create personal, new knowledge rather than transmitting knowledge
#### Constraints
- The article states the differential effects of the experiential approach compared to an exclusively lecture-based version of the course must be tested

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physical therapy students in early semesters without clinical placements

### Learning Goals
- applying motor learning theory to personally meaningful skill acquisition
- developing affective skills by experiencing learning as a patient would
