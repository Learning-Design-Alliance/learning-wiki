# Introduction to Modeling in Biology: a graduate course teaching empiricists to read theory papers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A semester-long University of Arizona course for ecology and evolution Ph.D. students with little mathematical training, taught since 2014 to enrollments the authors report as "in the range of 6-16 students". Its two course-level objectives are to extract insights from theory papers while skipping unfollowable math, and to understand the diverse roles theory plays in population biology. Students read historically important modeling papers, classify each model, and complete a take-home final exam on a new theory paper.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student commitment to investing time in reading papers before class, supported by graded quizzes and reading guides
#### Constraints
- Low enrollment is recommended when teaching a student population with low math preparation, because extreme math phobia requires individualized instructor attention
- Take-home assessments are vulnerable to completion by chatbots supplied with course-specific material, which may require modified assessment procedures

### Target Learners
- empirically focused ecology and evolution Ph.D. students with little prior mathematical training
- theoretically oriented graduate students seeking the big picture of modeling's role
- advanced undergraduates intending doctoral study

### Learning Goals
- extracting biological insights from theory papers
- understanding the roles of models in generating and testing hypotheses
- critical thinking about what constitutes scientific evidence
