# Modular first-semester design combining experimental and computational/discussion modules

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The first semester of the BioLEd curriculum is organized as discrete modules so instructors at any institution can adopt portions of the curriculum. It consists of "seven experimental and five computational/discussion-based modules" covering literature analysis (C.R.E.A.T.E.-method adaptation), pipetting, buffers, LDH kinetics, bioinformatics, recombinant expression and purification, quantitation, and molecular docking. Modularity was a major design aim, affording partial implementation at predominantly undergraduate institutions or PhD-granting research universities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors can choose to incorporate discrete elements into preexisting courses rather than implementing the whole year-long sequence
#### Constraints
- Modules involving LDH kinetics (Modules 4 and 5) are the ones not done with students' assigned POI

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biochemistry students at any college or university
