# When research-tested interactive learning modalities are provided, a textbook requirement may be unnecessary for achieving Astro 101 conceptual learning goals

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that a course built around research-validated interactive learning strategies — Think-Pair-Share questions, Lecture-Tutorials, and Ranking Tasks — can achieve its conceptual learning goals without a required textbook. As the abstract states, "if research-tested alternative learning modalities are provided, students' ability to learn the content of an Astronomy 101 course can be independent of a textbook requirement". The principle rests on the exam and concept-inventory comparisons in this single-instructor course. It is offered as a question for instructor decision-making, not a universal prescription.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research-tested interactive learning strategies (Lecture-Tutorials, Ranking Tasks, Think-Pair-Share) must be implemented with quality, which the article notes is critical to success
- Assessment must closely match the instructional material and course learning goals
#### Constraints
- Applies to courses whose learning goals do not pertain directly to textbook use, e.g. not courses where memorizing facts presented only in the textbook is a goal
- If reading a textbook is a central course goal in its own right, the author says instructors should absolutely require it
- New instructors may find a textbook helpful for organizing the course, and forgoing one may be better left until proficient with the alternative strategies

### Target Learners
- college nonmajors in introductory astronomy (Astro 101)

### Learning Goals
- understanding the nature of science through astrophysics
- solving high-level conceptual problems in astronomy
- developing intrinsic interest in astronomy
