# Multisensory planetarium prototype representing stars with tactile bars, LEDs, sounds and vibrations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The multisensory planetarium is a 1.5-meter Plexiglas hemisphere hosting roughly a hundred brass elements representing stars up to the fourth magnitude, each with a 3mm LED on top and a small vibrating motor in its terminal part. A first prototype is a Plexiglas tablet showing the Cassiopeia constellation with five brass elements, neoprene damping, a speaker, and an Arduino board that reads touch and activates LEDs, motors and sounds. The authors state that "Arduino is an open-source electronics platform based on easy-to-use hardware and software."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Touch input on the tactile elements must be readable by the Arduino board, which processes inputs via code loaded onto the board and activates the corresponding LED, motor or sound outputs.
#### Constraints
- The planetarium represents the celestial vault visible on June 10, 2023 from Castellaro Lagusello, since the sky changes with geographical location and observation time.

### Target Learners
- sighted, deaf and blind or visually impaired (BVI) users of astronomy outreach activities

### Learning Goals
- understanding apparent and physical features of stars in the night sky, including position, magnitude and distance
