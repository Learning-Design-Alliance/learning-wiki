# Psychotherapy apprenticeship intervention: weekly observation or co-treatment with rotating licensed therapists plus post-session reflective conversation and log entries

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention supplements training-as-usual with weekly attendance at real treatment sessions at outpatient mental health and substance use clinics. Per the protocol, "students will attend weekly treatment sessions with licensed therapists at the mental health and substance use clinics over a period of 10 weeks, each week with a different therapist and patient." Students observe passively or act as co-therapists, with participation left to each therapist, student, and patient. Each session is followed by an unstructured 15–20 minute reflective conversation with the therapist and a written reflective log entry. The intervention was refined based on an unpublished pilot trial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Therapist employees at participating clinics willing to include students in sessions and to obtain verbal patient consent for a student co-therapist
- A set weekday and time at each clinic for scheduling apprenticeship sessions
- Students sign a confidentiality agreement prior to the intervention
#### Constraints
- The intervention is shorter in duration and less intensive than full-time apprenticeships in other fields, by design meant to supplement, not replace, other educational methods
- There is no control over what clients students meet or what happens in sessions; exposure to challenging interactions is random
- Rotation between therapists means students are less able to form a secure relationship to one role model or follow the full course of treatment with one client

### Target Learners
- First-year Master's clinical psychology students

### Learning Goals
- Development of therapist interpersonal skills and procedural knowledge
- Self-efficacy for performing psychotherapy
- Habituation to the emotional challenges of working with distressed patients
