# Four design criteria for effective e-learning modules: authenticity, interactivity, feedback and integration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article enumerates four key criteria drawn from its own previous successful modules: "Authenticity - case based to optimise real-world relevance"; "Interactivity - responds to users input"; "Feedback - facilitates remediation of misconceptions"; and "Integration - provision of an overarching conceptual framework that takes into account prior learning and curriculum objectives". The leukaemia module was designed against these criteria, and the authors attribute its measured learning impact to interactivity, dialogic feedback and curriculum integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit learning objectives, curriculum integration, and interactive tasks with immediate feedback built into the module
#### Constraints
- The authors' evidence for these criteria comes from their own trials in pathology topics (glomerulonephritis, lymphoma, leukaemia) with senior medical students

### Target Learners
- Medical students, particularly senior students studying conceptually difficult topics

### Learning Goals
- Understanding conceptually difficult biomedical topics within an authentic clinical context
