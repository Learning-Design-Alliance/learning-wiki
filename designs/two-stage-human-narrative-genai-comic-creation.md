# Two-stage data comic creation pattern separating human-led narrative design from human–AI co-creation of visual elements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a two-stage production process for building data comics with GenAI assistance. In Stage 1, a researcher extracts key insights and classifies them into Bach et al.'s content relation categories, mapping them onto panel layouts, with reviewers validating choices. In Stage 2, image elements are generated with DALL-E 3 via ChatGPT-4, integrated into pre-defined panel layouts, and assembled with storytelling elements in Canva. The authors state the process separated "human-led narrative design from the human–AI co-creation of visual elements".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researcher expertise in data science, visual analytics, and visualisation or dashboard design to extract insights and review outputs
- Established data comic design patterns (Bach et al.) to structure content relation and layout decisions
- GenAI text-to-image tooling (DALL-E 3 via ChatGPT-4) and an assembly tool (Canva)
#### Constraints
- Comics were created by researchers, not students or teachers, so the process is described for expert-led production
- The article does not report effects of the process itself, only of the resulting comics

### Target Learners
- university students from diverse disciplines

### Learning Goals
- interpreting and reasoning with visual data
- comprehending data-driven insights
