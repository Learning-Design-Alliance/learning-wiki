# One-day high-fidelity interprofessional/uniprofessional simulation course with scenario-based participation and facilitated debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SaIL Centre intervention is a one-day, intermediate-fidelity simulation course of six scenarios (five acute illness and one communication scenario) for 12 participants per course, with learners taking turns in scenarios while peers observe via live audiovisual feed. Each scenario is followed by a facilitated debrief "focusing primarily on non-technical skills, lasting approximately 45 min", delivered using the 'debrief diamond' tool. Between August 2010 and May 2012, 21 IP and 53 UP courses were taught.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators trained in debriefing (minimum a two-day debriefing essentials course) and with a minimum of debriefing experience
#### Constraints
- IP cohorts require doctors and nurses/midwives in approximately a 1:1 ratio; the study ran in a live educational setting where course allocation was driven by demand and availability

### Target Learners
- Early-postgraduate nurses and midwives
- FY1/FY2 doctors

### Learning Goals
- Self-efficacy in managing emergency situations
- Communication
- Teamwork
- Leadership and situational management
