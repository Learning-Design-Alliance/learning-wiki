# Sandwich feedback model: positive-corrective-positive feedback sequence following DOPS assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes sandwich feedback as "a sequence of positive-corrective-positive statements", first represented by LeBaron and Jernick in 2000, in which "one dose of critical/corrective feedback sandwiches between two doses of positive/reinforcement feedback such that the first and last statements are positive". In this study it was operationalized as Feedback-Based Learning: after a DOPS observation, the instructor gave five to ten minutes of structured private feedback starting with strengths, then corrective points, then strengths again with an action plan, documented in the student's portfolio and repeated one week later. The article notes the model "needs low levels of feedback-giving expertise by the trainer and low self-assessment and reflection skills by the trainee".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured observation of performance (here the DOPS checklist) to anchor the feedback
- A private five-to-ten-minute feedback meeting with the instructor
- Documentation of the feedback summary in the student's portfolio and a repeated evaluation stage to compare progress
#### Constraints
- The article states the model requires low levels of feedback-giving expertise by the trainer and low self-assessment and reflection skills by the trainee

### Target Learners
- final-semester undergraduate operating room technology students in apprenticeship courses

### Learning Goals
- perioperative clinical competence
- technical-surgical procedural skills
- professional behavior in the operating room
