# I-ScaN: a longitudinal faculty POCUS training program with four elements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
I-ScaN (Integrated Sonography Course at NYU) is a faculty development program for hospitalists consisting of "1. Introductory course including didactics, image interpretation and hands-on instruction; 2. Supervised practice with patients; 3. Competency assessment; 4. Skill maintenance and quality assurance." It begins with roughly one month of self-study, followed by a two-day course, then a longitudinal phase with ultra-portable devices, daily scanning, anonymized clip uploading to a secure site with expert online feedback, weekly 1:3 bedside hands-on sessions, and monthly case conferences. Successful completion required ≥80% on the final knowledge test, ≥80% on the hands-on test, and an image portfolio.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to ultrasound devices on the teaching services
- Expert review of uploaded clips
- Local hands-on teachers at approximately a 1:3 teacher-to-participant ratio
#### Constraints
- The article notes wide variance in faculty participation in program elements and practice patterns despite encouragement

### Target Learners
- Hospitalist faculty at academic medical centers

### Learning Goals
- Point-of-care ultrasound image acquisition and interpretation
- Clinical integration and supervision of POCUS
