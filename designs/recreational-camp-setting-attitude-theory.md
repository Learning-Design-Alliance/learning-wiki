# Recreational camp settings as effective environments for developing positive attitudes toward inclusion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that summer sport and leisure activity camps, emphasizing less-competitive sports, social interaction, and fun rather than academics or winning, provide a unique experience where children can learn new skills, build friendships, and experience personal growth. In this setting, children get the chance to participate in activities and games with peers on a daily basis, which foster inclusion and develop close relationships. The authors hypothesize that the camp's emphasis on having fun daily and being part of a team rather than competition and winning explains why sport-specific attitudes improved here, contrasting with prior school physical education findings.
