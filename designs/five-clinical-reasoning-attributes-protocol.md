# Five clinical reasoning attributes (perception, information processing, analysis, deliberation, metacognition) as the organizing framework for debriefing questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes its debriefing protocol around clinical reasoning attributes extracted from the literature. More than half of reviewed studies emphasized seven attributes, but "The attributes of heuristics and intuition, however, were excluded in this protocol" because prior research indicates those are used by expert-level nurses. The remaining "5 clinical reasoning attributes, i.e. perception, information processing, analysis, deliberation, and metacognition were included", each matched to core questions at the corresponding debriefing stage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Core questions must be matched to the learning outcome of each debriefing stage
#### Constraints
- Heuristics and intuition were excluded as attributes appropriate to expert-level nurses rather than undergraduates

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning: perceiving patient problems, processing information, analyzing, deliberating, and metacognitive reflection
