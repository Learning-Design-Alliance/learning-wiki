# Providing new mathematical resources (quaternions) in advance to defuse simultaneous physical-mathematical concept change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper proposes a didactic approach in which students meet the mathematics of quaternions before confronting unfamiliar quantum-mechanical phenomena such as spin. The authors ask "whether it would me more beneficial for an effective learning process to defuse this change of concepts represented simultaneously in a physical and mathe-matical manner by providing the students with new mathematical resources in advance." Because the quaternion is, physically, "nothing new" but a convenient description of rotation, students can take a meta-conceptual standpoint when discussing relativity, being familiar with multiple mathematical approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first learn the quaternion formalism linked to a known physical phenomenon, rotation, before encountering the new theory
#### Constraints
- The paper presents this as a proposal; it reports no empirical test of the approach's effect on learning

### Target Learners
- university-level physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding special relativity
- understanding spin and Pauli matrices
- meta-conceptual understanding of theory choice in physics
