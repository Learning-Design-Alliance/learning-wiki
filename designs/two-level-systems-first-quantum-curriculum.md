# Begin introductory quantum mechanics with two-level systems to immerse students in inherently quantum phenomena and reduce mathematical demands

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article argues that developing introductory quantum mechanics with two-level systems (two-level atoms, spin 1/2 particles, interferometers, qubits) has multiple advantages: it "immediately immerses students in inherently quantum mechanical aspects of physics" by focusing on experiments with no classical explanation, allows direct discussion of interpretive aspects and quantum information theory, and requires only basic linear algebra such as manipulating 2x2 matrices instead of solving integrals and differential equations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The needed prerequisite mathematics is introduced in an ad hoc way, including complex numbers, matrix multiplication, eigenvalue problems for two-dimensional systems and Dirac notation
#### Constraints
- The resources focus on the introductory level and thus the needed prerequisite mathematics is limited

### Target Learners
- students in their first year of a physics degree
- self-study learners with high-school level mathematical understanding

### Learning Goals
- quantum mechanical concepts such as complementarity, incompatible observables and single photon interference
- interpretive aspects including EPR paradox and entanglement
