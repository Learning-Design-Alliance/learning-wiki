# Complementary educational advantages framework: PBL strengthens problem awareness and clinical reasoning while virtual simulation enhances intuitiveness, interactivity, and operability

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that PBL and virtual simulation offer complementary educational advantages when combined. PBL "provides a problem-oriented framework that encourages students to identify learning needs, actively seek relevant information, analyze clinical scenarios, and generate potential solutions", while virtual simulation "provides a visual, interactive, and operational environment in which students can apply their judgments and rehearse procedural strategies in a safe and controllable setting". The article states that virtual simulation "appears to enhance the intuitiveness, interactivity, and operability of teaching, whereas PBL may strengthen problem awareness, self-directed learning, and collaborative engagement".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires both approaches to be integrated rather than used separately, as the article states a single teaching method may be insufficient to meet the dual learning needs of trauma orthopedic education
#### Constraints
- The article notes this interpretation is the authors' pedagogical perspective and that the two-arm design could not distinguish the independent effects of PBL alone, virtual simulation alone, or their potential synergistic interaction
