# NE290: Nuclear History, Politics, and Futures — a remote special-topics course for nuclear engineering students

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
NE290 is a special-topics course at UC Berkeley spanning over a century of nuclear history in four units (20th-Century Physics, Physics in WWII, the Early Cold War, and the Late Cold War and Modern Era), designed to address nontechnical ABET student outcomes and seven Bloom's-based learning objectives. Grading combined "Weekly Reading Responses 30%", "Class Participation 20%", and "Term Paper 50%". The course was taught fully remotely via Zoom in Spring 2021, with guest lecturers recruited through videoconferencing and all course materials released on GitHub.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designed to address and exceed the nontechnical ABET student outcomes O1-O3 (ethics, teamwork, and acquiring new knowledge)
#### Constraints
- First offered during the COVID-19 pandemic, so the entirety of the course was conducted remotely via Zoom

### Target Learners
- graduate and undergraduate nuclear engineering students

### Learning Goals
- recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations
- sequence the historical basis for nuclear armament
- synthesize solutions to current nuclear policy problems from historical precedents
