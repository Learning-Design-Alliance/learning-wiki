# Research-platform-based lab session on phase modulation and Fourier analysis with progressive experimental stages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A half-day (3-hour) lab session, run 2012–2016 with about forty students, staged in three steps of increasing complexity: spectral analysis of a continuous wave, spectral analysis of phase-modulated light in RF and optical domains, and modification of spectral properties via fiber and shapers. It deliberately avoids an exclusively numerical approach, letting students "manipulate on state-of-the-art equipments of a genuine research platform" to directly monitor consequences of temporal phase modulation. The design targets known student difficulties with phase-modulated signals and corrects misconceptions about optical versus electrical spectra.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a research platform with high-end lightwave characterization equipment (estimated acquisition budget of approximately 500 k€)
- An 8-hour lecture beforehand detailing temporal, spectral and spatial properties of light
- A detailed advance handout with technical data sheets of all devices
- Permanent presence of a teacher or researcher during the session
#### Constraints
- Equipment cost makes cooperation with a research platform the only way to make these instruments available to master students
- All optical elements are connectorized so no alignment is needed; polarization-maintaining elements ensure stability

### Target Learners
- second-year Master's students in Physics, Lasers and Materials

### Learning Goals
- understanding joint temporal and spectral (Fourier) analysis of signals
- distinguishing optical from electrical (RF) spectra
- understanding temporal phase modulation and its spectral signatures
