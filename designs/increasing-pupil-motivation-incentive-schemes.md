# Increasing Pupil Motivation loss-framed incentive schemes (financial and event)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Two incentive schemes for Year 11 pupils in deprived English secondary schools, designed by the University of Bristol project team. The financial scheme gave pupils £80 per half-term, deducted at fixed rates for missing thresholds in attendance, behaviour, classwork and homework; the event scheme used tickets lost in the same ratio, with pupils attending a pupil-chosen event if enough tickets were retained. Both were "Framed to pupils as money was 'theirs to lose'", incorporating loss aversion, and were paired with feedback letters detailing performance each half-term.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Effort must be monitored by schools across the four domains of attendance, behaviour, classwork and homework
- Feedback letters to pupils detailing performance and how it determined rewards
#### Constraints
- The design does not allow the effects of incentives and feedback to be estimated separately
- The design does not allow a direct test of whether loss-framed rewards are more effective
- The trial tested one particular level of incentives; effects of higher or lower levels would need further evidence

### Target Learners
- Year 11 pupils (aged 15-16) in relatively deprived secondary schools

### Learning Goals
- GCSE attainment in Maths, English and Science
- Pupil effort in attendance, behaviour, classwork and homework
