# Integrate reversed screen orientation into novice ultrasound-guided vascular access training to support early skill acquisition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend exploring reversed screen techniques—flipping the ultrasound image in the transverse plane via the device's 'reverse' function—as a training aid for novices learning USGVA. They write that "The study supports the integration of reversed screen techniques in US training to improve skill acquisition and reduce complication rates in vascular access procedures." The recommendation targets the early learning phase, not routine clinical use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be novices without prior US experience; the reversed image is produced by activating the 'reverse' function on the ultrasound device in the transverse (short-axis) plane.
- Theoretical instruction on both orientations, as delivered in the study's 30-minute WFUMB-based sessions, precedes hands-on practice.
#### Constraints
- The article states the reversed orientation "was not intended for use in routine clinical settings" and results "are limited to short-term procedural outcomes and cannot be generalized to clinical practice or long-term skill acquisition."

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students (intern doctors) with no prior ultrasound experience

### Learning Goals
- ultrasound-guided vascular access procedural skill acquisition
- spatial perception and hand-eye coordination during needle insertion
