# Conceptual Problem Solving (CPS) framework: principle, justification, plan

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
CPS is a framework for solving physics problems, not a curriculum, adapted from college strategy-writing work for high school students. It contains three parts: "Principle (the principle or concept applicable to the problem), justification (explanation of why the concept or principle is appropriate), and plan (numbered steps that provide the “recipe” for solving the problem". Students format solutions in two columns, with plan steps on the left and equations on the right, supported initially by worksheet templates and scaffolding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolding such as headings or blank worksheet templates prompting each part when first introduced
- Teachers grade or require use of the approach on homework or exams to overcome student resistance to writing
#### Constraints
- Not a curriculum; designed to be adapted to existing course materials
- Writing principle-justification-plan before solving is encouraged but not required

### Target Learners
- high school physics students in non-AP introductory mechanics courses

### Learning Goals
- selecting appropriate physics principles for problems
- justifying principle applicability
- planning quantitative solutions
