# Adaptive AI scaffolding within a constructivist, inquiry-based learning design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article grounds NMC2025 in constructivist and inquiry-based learning models, positioning the learner as an active participant while AI and automation provide scaffolding that minimizes cognitive load. The authors write that "NMC2025 provides scaffolding in multiple adaptive layers" and that the AI assistant "modulates the difficulty by tailoring its feedback to the student's inputs", diagnosing knowledge gaps (e.g., a too-low cutoff energy) or offering subtler optimization tips for stronger inputs. Inquiry cycles run from broad question through hypothesis generation, experiment design, observation, and reflection, with the AI accommodating student-initiated deviations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must actively input, justify, and revise simulation parameters; the AI asks the student to make choices rather than proceeding autonomously.
#### Constraints
- The system does not explicitly maintain a long-term user model: it "does not store past student performance or misconceptions yet", performing only implicit step-wise student modeling.

### Target Learners
- advanced STEM learners, including graduate students

### Learning Goals
- scientific decision-making
- inquiry skills
- self-directed learning and verification of AI information
