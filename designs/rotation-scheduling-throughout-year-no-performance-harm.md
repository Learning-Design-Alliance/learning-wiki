# Distribute student rotations across the year according to training-center capacity without expecting harm to final OSCE performance

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From the finding that clerkship timing showed no consistent effect on final OSCE performance, the authors recommend: "dividing students to complete different rotations throughout the year, according to the capacity of the training centers, staff availability, and applied curriculum, should not affect their performance on final exams". They attribute this to students' good long-term clinical capacities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A final examination common to all rotation groups and rotation slots filled across the academic year
#### Constraints
- The one significant pooled pairwise difference (third vs second rotation, P = 0.029) was not present in individual years; middle-of-year rotations were interrupted by many holidays

### Target Learners
- 6th-year clinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- Retention and later application of clinical skills learned during rotations
