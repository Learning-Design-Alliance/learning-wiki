# Eight-laboratory 5E-informed image-to-evidence sequence for introductory Solar System astronomy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reusable course-level design in which students begin with a galaxy-size investigation establishing pixel scale, angular size, physical size, proportional reasoning, and measurement uncertainty, then apply the same image-to-evidence approach to Solar System objects. Each investigation follows a 5E-informed structure and produces a focused student product, such as a measurement table, graph, comparison chart, or claim-evidence-reasoning statement. The article presents it as a sequence of eight modules (galaxy scale, Galilean moons, comparative planetology, planetary apparent size, two lunar labs, and two solar labs) usable with live observations or prepared image sets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A typical one lab meeting uses 5-10 minutes for an Engage prompt, 15-25 minutes for live image acquisition or archived-image inspection, 15-20 minutes for measurement, and 5-10 minutes for explanation and evaluation
- Teachers should supply target lists, lunar phase windows, galaxy distances, solar safety instructions, and backup images before class
#### Constraints
- Designed to fit within an hour meeting time
- Activities may be implemented with live observations or prepared image sets when weather, scheduling, or local observing conditions prevent data collection

### Target Learners
- high-school astronomy students
- dual-enrollment Earth and space science students
- community college introductory solar system astronomy students
- introductory university students

### Learning Goals
- pixel scale and angular/physical size reasoning
- orbital motion inference
- observation versus inference distinction
- relative surface history from crater density
- solar rotation estimation
