# Real-World Problem-Solving (RPS) introductory physics course format

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The RPS course is a highly interactive introductory calculus-based physics format designed based on the theory of deliberate practice, contrasting with didactic lecture. Class time is mostly spent on students' "usage of scaffolded worksheets while solving real-world physics problems" in groups with feedback from learning assistants, punctuated by peer-instruction clicker questions. Assessments differ from lecture: open-resource quizzes, a two-stage individual-then-group quiz format (fall), or quiz reflections returning 50% of missed points (spring), with homework mixing conceptual and real-world scaffolded problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolded worksheets and a problem-solving template for real-world problems
- Learning assistants providing feedback during group activities
- Trained teaching assistants structuring recitation review and practice
#### Constraints
- The fall 2021 iteration was in a theater-style lecture hall while spring 2022 was in a SCALE-UP classroom, so classroom setting varied between iterations
- Lab activities were identical cookbook activities across all sections and beyond the RPS instructor's control

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in introductory calculus-based physics

### Learning Goals
- real-world problem-solving skills in introductory physics
- physics conceptual understanding and problem solving
