# 90-minute lesson 'Mit der Rakete zur ISS' for grades 7/8 on momentum, thrust and single-stage rocket velocity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A structured 90-minute classroom activity for grades 7/8 in which students "machen sich mit dem Begriﬀ des Impulses vertraut", learn why rockets must be large, solve an equation by iteration in a spreadsheet, and "berechnen die Endgeschwindigkeit einer einstuﬁgen Rakete". It requires prior mechanics knowledge, worksheets, calculators and computers with a spreadsheet such as MS Excel, and includes a thematic introduction, calculation tasks on thrust and propellant, and the iterative final-velocity exercise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior knowledge of the fundamentals of mechanics: the fundamental equation of mechanics, the concept of momentum, and the concept of force
- Calculators and laptops/PCs with a spreadsheet such as MS Excel; prior first experience with spreadsheets is described as helpful
#### Constraints
- The material covers only an introduction to the basic concepts of rocket propulsion and a first calculation of final velocity under simplified assumptions; external forces are not considered, and multi-stage rockets and gravity are deferred to a follow-up activity

### Target Learners
- Grades 7/8 secondary school students

### Learning Goals
- Momentum (Impuls)
- thrust and recoil principle
- iterative equation solving with a spreadsheet
- final velocity of a single-stage rocket

### Claims
- Iterative Small Steps Converges Rocket Equation [+M]
- Step Count Differences Shrink Iteration [+M]
