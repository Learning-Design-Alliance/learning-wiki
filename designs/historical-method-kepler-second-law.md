# Historical method (genetic approach) for teaching Kepler's 2nd law via conceptual conflict between the equant and equal areas

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article develops a teaching framework that partially recreates Kepler's original line of thought in Astronomia Nova, grounding the 2nd law in the conceptual conflict between the equant model (equal angles at equal times) and Kepler's law (equal areas at equal times). It draws on cognitive recapitulation, which the article states as "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny", and on the correspondence principle, so students understand why the equant worked and why it had to be discarded. The method combines this narrative with quantitative formulation through Kepler's equation, M = E – e sin E, and an active-learning computational exercise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need familiarity with coordinate systems on the celestial sphere and the equant model, which the article notes many students and instructors have little familiarity with
- Two 75-minute class sessions as part of a module on Kepler's laws
#### Constraints
- Tested only with upper-division undergraduate and graduate students; effectiveness at lower-division or general-education courses is unconstrained per the article
- Best used in graduate curricula because Kepler's equation is transcendental and computational techniques are needed

### Target Learners
- junior and senior physics/astronomy undergraduates
- first-year astronomy PhD students

### Learning Goals
- understand Kepler's 2nd law quantitatively via Kepler's equation
- understand the interplay between theory and observations and the importance of observational accuracy
