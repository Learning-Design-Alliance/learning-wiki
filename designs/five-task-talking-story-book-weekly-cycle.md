# Weekly five-task talking-story-book cycle integrating listening, retelling, comprehension checking, phonics and story completion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a weekly instructional cycle built around one talking story book, in which learners listen once or twice, then complete sequenced tasks across the week: "Task One Memory-Based Understanding", narrative retelling, true-false questions, phonics word completion, and a written conclusion or completion of 3-5 lines. Each task was assigned to a specific day and was designed to assess a component of reading comprehension such as auditory memory, sequencing and phonics discrimination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each participant needs a well-illustrated talking story book with audio played at a child-like pace and tone
- Tasks are spread across the week, one talking story book per week
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only with Saudi sixth-grade EFL learners over four weeks using Lady Bird Level 3 stories of 1300 to 1600 words

### Target Learners
- young EFL learners in elementary school

### Learning Goals
- reading comprehension
- retelling
- spelling
- phonics
- auditory memory
