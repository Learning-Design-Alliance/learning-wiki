# Interest-led application projects as a framework for engaging students in QISE through connections to their existing interests

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's organizing framework holds that connecting quantum information science and engineering to students' existing interests—particularly medical and biological applications—provides an entry point for students who would not otherwise consider QISE. It rests on four stated functions: letting students "lead with their interests, thereby getting them more deeply engaged in the material," providing a framework for covering a difficult topic, attracting students who considered QISE outside their interest area, and enabling exploration of ethical issues for an ethically aware workforce. The framework was enacted through the Pathways Program and the Ideas course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects must allow students to choose or connect to applications aligned with their own interests
- Opportunities for discussion and access to relevant reading material to deepen ethical understanding
#### Constraints
- Neither program drew students for whom medicine was a primary interest, so the framework alone does not solve recruitment
- A student continuing in QISE needs significant mathematics, computer science, or physics/engineering beyond such a project

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate students
- graduate students

### Learning Goals
- engagement with quantum information science and engineering
- career interest exploration
- ethical awareness of quantum technologies
