# Structured workflow integrating donor-specific pathologies and XR technologies into a case-based learning framework for gross anatomy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article validates a repeatable workflow in which donor MRI scans are reviewed by a radiologist and anatomist, uploaded to a web-based DICOM viewer, and paired with clinical, radiological, and pathological reports, anatomical correlations, 3D photogrammetric reconstructions, and segmented models (Figs. 1–3). The conclusion states: "This study validates a structured workflow for integrating donor-specific pathologies and XR technologies into a CBL framework." Unlike idealized digital models, it uses authentic anatomical variations derived directly from body donors to mirror real-world clinical complexity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Permanent body donations with consent for medical imaging and three-dimensional reconstruction for educational purposes
- Collaborative radiologist-anatomist review of images before upload
- Defined learning objectives and case workflow per module
#### Constraints
- Only two abdominal cases with relatively rare conditions were included
- Restricted access to digital anatomical materials with usage disclaimers prohibiting unauthorized downloading or distribution

### Target Learners
- First-year medical students in gross anatomy laboratories

### Learning Goals
- Understanding anatomical variability relative to typical anatomy
- Connecting physical 3D structures with 2D radiologic counterparts
