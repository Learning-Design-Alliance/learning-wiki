# A two-cycle design-based research telementoring program for pediatric POCUS in Uganda using a portable ultrasound device with telementoring software

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention comprised a two-week in-person POCUS course followed by recurrent, synchronous telementoring sessions using a portable ultrasound device (Philips Lumify™) with telementoring software via tablet (REACTS™). The tablet camera provided a vantage point for the mentor to assess hand and probe handling, and a pointer feature (green dot) allowed the mentor to overlay a pointer on the live ultrasound feed to support instruction and feedback. Twenty telementoring sessions took place (11 in cycle 1 and 9 in cycle 2), with cycle 2 adaptations including fortnightly scheduling, expanded practice beyond tuberculosis, and simplified standard operating procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A two-week in-person course was considered a necessary basis for continued remote training via telementoring, and a standard operating procedure described setting up the connection and acquiring, saving and reporting POCUS images.
#### Constraints
- Due to workload and time constraints, participation narrowed from twenty-four clinicians trained in person to five key users, of whom only two continued into the second cycle.

### Target Learners
- Pediatricians, clinical officers, medical officers and nurses in resource-limited settings

### Learning Goals
- Pediatric point-of-care ultrasound competency development (lung and mediastinal POCUS)
