# A 12-video sequential, gated-release computer-based video training program with a homemade practice board for self-directed surgical skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The artifact is a curriculum of 12 stepwise tutorial videos averaging 8 minutes (range 3–15 min) teaching knot tying and suturing "introduced in discrete, incremental steps". Videos pair didactics on instruments, suture, and needles with demonstrations, best-practice tips, and common mistakes. Videos were released two at a time every two weeks, and students had to "submit practice material before access to the next set of videos was provided". The course was run as a semester-long preclinical elective with an internal online platform and ungraded practice submissions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need a homemade practice board, provided sutures and ligatures, and purchased forceps and needle driver
#### Constraints
- Practice samples were not graded
- Implementation to date was limited to one school's preclinical elective

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- basic knot-tying skills
- basic suturing skills
- familiarity with surgical instruments and sutures
