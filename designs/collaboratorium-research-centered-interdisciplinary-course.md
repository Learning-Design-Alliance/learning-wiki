# The collaboratorium: a research-centered interdisciplinary course model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The collaboratorium is the instructional model the authors propose for adaptive interdisciplinary courses. The article defines it: "We define a collaboratorium as a research-centered course spanning multiple disciplines." Students are part of an interdisciplinary collaborative team carrying out a research project (a capstone) in what the authors term problem-based learning, and the model "facilitates forming heterogeneous small groups of students during and after classes, on site and online" to support dialogical learning. Instructors drawn from multiple disciplines function as a collective student resource. ALICE operates within this interdisciplinary medium; each student need not master all concepts, only enough to ensure the collaboratorium succeeds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Heterogeneous small groups formed early around student interests, with instructors processing students' disciplinary background information when projects are selected
#### Constraints
- The article notes a collaboratorium could exist without ALICE; ALICE is the interdisciplinary medium in which it operates, not a prerequisite

### Target Learners
- graduate and senior undergraduate students from diverse disciplinary backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- interdisciplinary research problem-solving and collaborative competency development
