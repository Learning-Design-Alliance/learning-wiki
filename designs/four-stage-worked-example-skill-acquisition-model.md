# Four-stage model of cognitive skill acquisition through the worked-example approach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes the worked-example approach as a procedure that "usually involves four stages" of cognitive skill acquisition. In Stage 1 learners receive instructional explanations communicating basic knowledge of principles and concepts; in Stage 2 they study worked examples with self-explanation prompts; in Stage 3 they turn to initial problem-solving, which deepens understanding and can serve self-assessment; in Stage 4 they practice extensively to enhance problem-solving speed and accuracy. The first three stages correspond to the early and intermediate phases of skill acquisition, focused on understanding the rationale of solutions, while Stage 4 corresponds to the late phase focused on efficiency. The present study targets the transition out of Stage 3, where accurate self-assessment decides whether learners restudy examples or proceed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners navigating the sequence in a self-regulated manner must accurately self-assess their level of understanding after initial problem-solving to decide whether to proceed to Stage 4.
#### Constraints
- The article states it is generally unclear how many problems learners need to solve at what times to effectively consolidate the acquired schemata in Stage 4.

### Target Learners
- learners acquiring cognitive skills through example-based learning, such as university students learning mathematical urn model problems

### Learning Goals
- understanding the rationale of solution procedures
- enhancing speed and accuracy of problem-solving
