# Progressively graded simulation practice with spaced repetition for surgical skill acquisition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The rat tail program structures practice as short, recurring sessions sequenced from easier to harder material: training "started with larger vessels at the proximal end of the rat tail and moved toward the finer vessels at the distal end". The twice-weekly schedule over one month was chosen, the authors state, to encourage "spaced repetition—a strategy proven to improve memory retention and skill acquisition". Repetition and categorization of essential skills (coordination, visual adaptation, positioning, dissection, suturing) are emphasized throughout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation model offering vessels of graded diameter so difficulty can increase progressively
- Supervised practice sessions schedulable at regular intervals alongside clinical duties
#### Constraints
- The article states the schedule suited its clinical setting (two classes per week for a month, 16 hours total); it did not experimentally compare scheduling variants

### Target Learners
- novice microsurgeons
- surgical trainees

### Learning Goals
- microsurgical technique proficiency
- skill retention and mastery through repetitive graded practice
