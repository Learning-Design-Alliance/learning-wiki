# Clinical reasoning as cognitive and meta-cognitive processes guiding eCREST's design and measures

## Description
The article adopts a framework, drawn from Young et al's mapping of clinical reasoning theories, in which good clinical reasoning requires recognizing relevant features and testing hypotheses, monitoring one's own processes for errors and biases, and a sufficient knowledge base. The authors state: "we focus on clinical reasoning theories related to cognitive and meta-cognitive processes and display how these relate to eCREST in Table 1." This framework maps case-based learning and prompted reflection onto eCREST features and yields the study's measures: data gathering, diagnostic flexibility, diagnostic accuracy, and reflections.
