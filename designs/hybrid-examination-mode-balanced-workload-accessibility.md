# Prefer a hybrid examination mode combining physical examiner presence with remote candidates to balance workload, efficiency and accessibility

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes the hybrid format — physical presence of examiner pairs with remote online participation of candidates — as a way of conducting examinations that reduces perceived examiner workload while preserving integrity. It reports that "the hybrid examination mode... was associated with a reduction in perceived workload" and argues the approach "could offer a more balanced and efficient way of conducting examinations while maintaining their integrity and effectiveness", with potential cost savings and improved accessibility for candidates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examination infrastructure supporting both a physical plenary/examiner venue and remote candidate participation, with an examination office organising interactions between examiners and candidates
#### Constraints
- The workload reduction was statistically significant but relatively small, less than 30%; the article states this potential rather than a demonstrated advantage for grading quality

### Target Learners
- postgraduate medical examination candidates and examiner pairs in high-stakes oral assessment

### Learning Goals
- standardised evaluation of professional knowledge with reduced examiner burden and improved candidate accessibility
