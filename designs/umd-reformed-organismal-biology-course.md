# Reformed University of Maryland Organismal Biology (Org Bio) course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
An introductory biology course at the University of Maryland reformed to focus on basic principles in biology, chemistry, and physics rather than a "forced march through the phyla." Its organizational framework includes "the relevance of universal physical and chemical laws and importance of diverse structure-function relationships." Pedagogically, instructors made lectures more interactive with clicker questions and class discussions, and dedicated a third of class periods to small group activities including concept-mapping, discussions, and enactments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors present equations as referents for biological phenomena and revisit them in discussions of form, function, and evolutionary affordances and constraints.
#### Constraints
- The course is a single institutional setting; the authors state their data cannot be generalized beyond this one course.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life-science majors in introductory organismal biology

### Learning Goals
- applying fundamental physical and chemical principles to biological processes
- principle-based understanding of organismal diversity, structure, and function
