# Jigsaw laboratory cycle: heterogeneous home groups plus expert groups alternating around shared experiments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's Jigsaw implementation organises a 15-week laboratory course into five-person heterogeneous Jigsaw groups formed by academic achievement, and topic-based expert groups of the same size. Each member studies one sub-topic, performs its related experiments, and teaches it back in the home group before all groups run experiments by turns. The article reports that "five Jigsaw groups with five people w ere formed" and "five Expert Groups consisting of f ive members were set up", with 2 hours for first Jigsaw activities, 4 for expert groups, 4 for second Jigsaw activities and 10 for experiments per field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The main content chosen for group work must be divided into sub-categories for the equal responsibility of group members
- Cooperative tasks must be challenging enough to require joint effort and include sufficient tasks for each member to contribute
#### Constraints
- When students believe their individual efforts are not related to group performance, social evasion and fool effect may occur
- Some student teachers' not doing their job properly affected other student teachers' learning badly

### Target Learners
- third-grade prospective science teachers in tertiary education

### Learning Goals
- scientific process skills
- physics, chemistry and biology laboratory content
- cooperative social skills
