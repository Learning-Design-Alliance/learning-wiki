# Mini-CEX-guided four-stage virtual simulation training framework (one platform, four modules, three levels, three projects)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (3 causal, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 3 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes an organizing framework in which the Mini-CEX competency-based assessment model guides a four-stage clinical skills pathway: theoretical instruction, faculty demonstration, virtual simulation practice, and bedside clinical training. Its construction is summarized as "one platform, four modules, three levels, and three projects", with the four modules spanning medical theoretical knowledge, clinical skills demonstration, virtual simulation experimental operation, and bedside clinical practice. The framework aims to provide a closed-loop learning feedback mechanism integrating teaching resources, processes, and evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated personnel responsible for daily operation and maintenance of the platform to ensure system stability and continuity of teaching activities
#### Constraints
- Virtual environments' haptic feedback and resistance simulation still differ from real clinical settings

### Target Learners
- final-year clinical medicine interns

### Learning Goals
- clinical skills competence
- clinical reasoning
- procedural competence
