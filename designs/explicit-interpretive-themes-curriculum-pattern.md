# Transformed modern physics curriculum making interpretive themes central: make realist expectations explicit, provide evidence against them, and attend to student attitudes across topics.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (7 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course-level design pattern for introductory modern physics in which questions of classical and quantum reality become a central theme and students' own beliefs become an explicit topic of discussion. The transformations had three stated objectives: "(a) make realist e x p e c t a t i o n s e x p l i c i t"; (b) provide evidence against those expectations; and (c) attend to student attitudes on interpretive themes across a broad selection of topics. Standard topics were augmented with interpretive discussion and new topics such as delayed-choice experiments were added, building on an existing interactive-engagement curriculum with clicker questions, peer instruction, and simulations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Builds on a base curriculum already using interactive engagement techniques (in-class concept questions, peer instruction, computer simulations)
- Students need language and working definitions of multiple quantum interpretations (e.g., Realist, Copenhagen, Matter-Wave) established before interpretive arguments are extended across topics
#### Constraints
- The authors note explicit instruction is typically not meaningful for students beyond the specific contexts in which interpretive questions arise

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based modern physics students, engineering and physics majors

### Learning Goals
- recognize the difference between experimental uncertainty of classical physics and fundamental uncertainty of quantum mechanics
- articulate personal stances on interpretive questions such as the reality of the wave function and localized electrons
