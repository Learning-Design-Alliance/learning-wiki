# Five conceptual ideas—superposition, entanglement, complementarity, tagging, and measurement—as the core of quantum teacher training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes quantum teacher training around five carefully defined conceptual ideas. Superposition is when "a state vector is composed of more than one component" in a relevant basis; entanglement is a non-factorizable superposition over more than one degree of freedom; complementarity concerns whether which-way information exists; tagging is "the reversible creation of an entangled state that provides which-way information"; and measurement is an irreversible process used for detection or state preparation. The author argues these must be mastered before formal development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers master these concepts before moving into more formal developments of quantum mechanics
#### Constraints
- Superposition is not mathematically well-defined independent of the basis chosen for the system being studied

### Target Learners
- Pre-service and in-service K-12 and introductory physics teachers who are quantum illiterate

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics sufficient to teach quantum ideas to their own students
