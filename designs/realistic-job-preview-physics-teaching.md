# Realistic job preview approach to introducing physics undergraduates to teaching careers

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A recruitment framework in which candidates are shown both positive and negative aspects of an occupation so they can judge whether it fits their preferences, aiming at long-term job satisfaction and lower turnover. The article situates the Teaching Physics in School module as a realistic job preview: "it aims to show job candidates both positive and negative aspects of a role in an attempt to ensure they have realistic expectations". The module deliberately confirmed stressful and hard aspects of teaching rather than showcasing only positives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Placements must expose students to authentic, realistic aspects of the job, including its difficulties, not an overly optimistic presentation
#### Constraints
- The article notes a temptation to showcase only exciting positive parts of teaching because of recruitment shortage, which the approach resists

### Target Learners
- year 3 undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- informed career decision-making about secondary physics teaching
- development of communication and professional skills
