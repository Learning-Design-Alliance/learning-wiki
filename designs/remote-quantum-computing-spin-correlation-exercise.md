# Remote quantum-computing exercises as a route to conceptual understanding of spin correlations and entanglement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a learning design in which students first derive spin correlation functions analytically, then build quantum circuits that create the states and rotate measurement bases, and finally estimate expectation values from repeated runs on real quantum processors. The authors state that "This exercise is ideal for remote learning and generates data with real quantum mechanical systems that are impractical to investigate in the local laboratory." The experiments "illuminate general features of quantum mechanical systems that are otherwise difﬁcult to access."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the free IBM Quantum Experience platform for designing and testing real quantum circuits remotely
- Students must calculate multipartite spin correlation functions and design and analyze quantum circuits
#### Constraints
- The IBM qubit is not a spin-1/2 particle; the same mathematics applies only because both are two-state systems
- Measurements on the platform are limited to the computational basis, so arbitrary-basis measurement requires rotation operators

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- calculation of multipartite spin correlation functions
- design and analysis of quantum circuits
- remote measurement with real quantum processors
- conceptual understanding of total spin, spin components, and entanglement
