# Spaced education: content spaced out and repeated over time as questions with detailed explanations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines spaced education as "an approach to distance learning in which content is spaced out and repeated over time in the form of multiple choice questions and detailed explanations". Spacing and the testing effects inherent in the approach have been found, citing prior randomized trials, to increase retention of clinical knowledge and skills. The Senegal pilot operationalized this with 20 questions answered up to 4 per day, repeated until answered correctly twice, then retired.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Material must be delivered over time; the authors caution that substantially shortening the course may not yield the same knowledge improvements
#### Constraints
- Optimal course duration and question count may vary by content and learner preferences; 55% of pilot participants found the 8-week duration too long

### Target Learners
- health workers in low-resource settings

### Learning Goals
- retention of clinical knowledge through repeated testing
