# Web-based computer-tailored nutrition education intervention with basic and plus versions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A Web-based computer-tailored (CT) nutrition education intervention for adults delivered via a login website, targeting fruit, vegetable, high-energy snack and saturated fat intake. "Both versions consisted of four modules (i.e. fruit, vegetables, high-energy snacks and fat), each containing of three sessions that could be worked through during six consecutive weeks." The basic version targets knowledge, awareness, attitude, self-efficacy, goal setting and action- and coping planning; the plus version adds environmental-level feedback on home food availability and storage and on supermarket availability and prices. Completion took about 160 minutes, with e-mail reminders between sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants need Internet access and sufficient Dutch reading and writing proficiency; the information was written at grade level 4-6 for comprehensibility by lower educated people
- The plus version requires supermarket-specific availability and price information, collected by observing supermarkets or from websites and flyers
#### Constraints
- Exposure to the second and third (follow-up) sessions was very low, which the authors say is often observed in online interventions
- The environmental-level feedback was not extensive enough to achieve sustained changes, per the authors

### Target Learners
- Dutch adults aged 20-65 from the general population, including low/moderate-educated adults

### Learning Goals
- Increase fruit and vegetable intake; decrease high-energy snack and saturated fat intake; develop self-regulation skills (goal setting, action and coping planning)
