# Audacity-based echo-delay measurement activity on NASA Apollo recordings

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An open research activity in which students analyze NASA-recorded Apollo radio conversations to measure the time of flight of Earth-Moon radio signals. Using Audacity, "the time windows of single syllables and of their echoes can be clearly identiﬁed and isolated", allowing precise delay measurement. The activity ran with 10 groups of two or three students aged 14-19, first with chronometers and then with audio software, and the mp3 files were taken from NASA's web site.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded NASA Apollo communications available from NASA's web site
- A word in the recordings with a clearly audible echo (e.g. the word "over" in a Houston call)
#### Constraints
- Only delays in the astronaut's replies carry the flight time when the tape was recorded at Houston
- At intermediate (non-24h-separated) times radio signals were probably following different paths

### Target Learners
- high-school students, age range 14-19

### Learning Goals
- time-of-flight measurement and the constancy of the speed of light
- estimation of measurement uncertainty
- the ellipticity of Kepler's orbits
