# Deliver intelligence-theory interventions as curriculum-embedded sessions during the transition to university

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends delivering short interventions promoting an incremental view of intelligence to first-year students as part of normal first-year class activities, because transition is a time when students are unclear what success requires. The intervention "formed part of the usual classes and drew on psychological research to make it appear to be a 'normal' seminar activity", and the article cites What Works? (2012) that curriculum embedding enhances efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A one-hour presentation and activity that can be delivered within existing first-year seminar sessions
#### Constraints
- A one-hour intervention is unlikely to change perceptions and behaviours across an entire academic year without repetition or top-up

### Target Learners
- first-year university students

### Learning Goals
- adaptive beliefs about intelligence
- learning goal orientation
- positive learning behaviours during transition
