# Course-integrated series of online hackathon events aligned to course content aspects, supported by lecture, feedback, and team management plan interventions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern integrates a series of online educational hackathon events at strategic points throughout a course rather than as a one-off event at the end. Each hackathon covers one aspect of the course content — in this case "asset-related concepts, risk-related concepts, risk-treatment related concepts" — with tasks building on prior events' analyses of real-world use-cases. Three interventions support the events: video lectures and live streams before each event, online feedback sessions and written feedback on submitted write-ups, and a team management plan document for task assignment and deadlines. Hackathon outputs such as the cumulative security analysis report can count toward the course grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hackathon tasks must be crafted with the course curriculum and lecture paths in mind
- Teams of three or four members select a use-case; prepared use-case information should reflect real-world software systems
#### Constraints
- Findings cannot be generalized beyond the specific course since another study on a different course and with different use-cases might yield different results
- Sample selection for analysis may include bias

### Target Learners
- university students in an online cybersecurity course

### Learning Goals
- applying theoretical security concepts (asset, risk, and risk treatment analysis) to real-world software systems
- teamwork and collaboration in online settings
