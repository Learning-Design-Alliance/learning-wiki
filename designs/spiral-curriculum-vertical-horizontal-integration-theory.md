# Spiral curriculum theory: vertical and horizontal integration that revisits clinical themes at increasing depth

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes the spiral curriculum as an integrated design "designed to be repetitive yet progressive" that "uses both vertical and horizontal integration". Vertical integration means students learn basic and clinical sciences in parallel throughout training (exemplified by problem-based learning), while horizontal integration combines disciplines such as anatomy, physiology, and biochemistry into interdisciplinary courses, commonly organized as body-system blocks. The spiral approach adds repeated, deepening revisiting of clinical themes, which the authors, citing Brauer et al., call "the most ideal form of integration" for enhancing trainees' knowledge base.
