# Progressive sentence disclosure method combining word and sentence learning via sequential subject-verb-object revelation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A presentation method grounded in Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning and the Segmenting Principle that decomposes sentences into subject, verb, and object components presented sequentially. Previously shown words remain visible while new ones are highlighted using transparency cues following the Signaling Principle. Once all components are introduced, the full sentence is displayed. The article describes it as "sequentially revealing sentence components with synchronized multimodal cues (text, images, audio)" to reduce extraneous load and support multitasked learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presentation must sequentially reveal subject, verb, and object components while retaining prior context and highlighting new elements
- Multimodal cues (text, images, audio) must be synchronized and time-matched across conditions
#### Constraints
- Sentence segmentation and image selection were done manually in this study
- Validated with L2 languages whose grammatical structures are similar to L1

### Target Learners
- Novice second-language learners (A1 fluency)

### Learning Goals
- Second-language vocabulary and sentence-structure recall
