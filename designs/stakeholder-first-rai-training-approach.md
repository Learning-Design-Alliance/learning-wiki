# Stakeholder-first approach to RAI education operating at two levels of engagement: organizational and practitioner

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's organizing framework holds that responsible AI learning requires engaging tensions between differing stakeholder perspectives, and that this is achieved through two levels of engagement. The first engages organizational partners and managerial representatives, "leveraging their active participation and institutional buy-in to shape the practitioner experience"; the second engages practitioners responsible for technical and non-technical deliverables through direct instruction and active learning with case studies. Organizational representatives drive adoption by elevating responsibility within the organization and signaling its importance to internal and external stakeholders.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leadership-level support within the organizational partner to embed RAI principles into culture
- Iterative co-development of training materials with input from partner staff
#### Constraints
- The collaboration faced lack of access to proprietary information and organizational restructuring that shifted project ownership and complicated collective buy-in

### Target Learners
- organizational representatives and managers
- technical and non-technical practitioners

### Learning Goals
- integrating RAI principles into design, development, and deployment of AI systems
- inter-role collaboration across design, legal, and technical teams
