# Run the first PbL group meeting as a simple written internet chat and keep the second meeting on site — a feasible low-fidelity hybrid PbL format

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends keeping the established PbL process intact and changing only the communication channel: the first of two group meetings takes place as a written online chat, the second on site, with the case supplied as a PDF. The authors argue that such a "low fidelity" approach "could be a hybrid approach to digital teaching even in the post-pandemic era", and note it aligns with Barrows' notion of distributed PBL by combining synchronous and asynchronous electronic communication without interfering with the PbL process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students and tutors communicate via a basic chat without threaded replies or file exchange; the PbL case must be readable separately, e.g. as a PDF on the learning platform
- Students must already be familiar with the PbL format so the changed channel does not alter the process
#### Constraints
- The internet forum offered between the two meetings was not used at all
- PbL process itself, tutor behaviour and learning goals derived from discussions were not analysed in the supporting study

### Target Learners
- third-year medical students in a pharmacology competence-area course

### Learning Goals
- collaborative learning in problem-based small groups on pharmacological aspects of metabolic syndrome
