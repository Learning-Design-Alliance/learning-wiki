# Use a non-compensatory ranked model to short-list applicants for medical school selection interviews

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends ranking applicants by their lowest rank across selection tools rather than their mean rank when deciding who is invited to interview, because success in medicine requires high achievement on both academic and aptitude measures. The authors conclude that "a non-compensatory model is preferable for selecting applicants for medical student selection interview", since it raised thresholds without disadvantaging low-SES applicants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each applicant must be rankable on every selection tool used (here UMAT scores and ATAR marks), so that the lowest rank, with ties broken by highest rank, can determine the final order.
#### Constraints
- The article recommends that similar analyses be performed with data from other jurisdictions to assess generalisability of the findings.
- The analysis focused on thresholds rather than means, since means sit well above the threshold range where failure risk is greatest.

### Target Learners
- Applicants to undergraduate medical programs being short-listed for selection interviews

### Learning Goals
- Improving the quality of the applicant pool invited for interview while maintaining socioeconomic diversity
