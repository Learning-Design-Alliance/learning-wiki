# Four-stage historical-controversy sequence: predict, equate the two falls, confront the incongruity, construct the conservation principle

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a mechanics sequence around a historical controversy staged by Galileo. Stage 1 has students predict where stones dropped from a tower and from a ship's mast fall and debate their answers; stage 2 makes them admit the equivalence of the two falls, yielding the incongruous conclusion that the Earth is immobile; stage 3 exploits the resulting cognitive conflict; stage 4 has students construct the conservation principle and compare it with Salviati's text. The article states the sequence "pretende que los estudiantes aprendan el principio de conservación del movimiento en el contexto de la emergencia del principio de inercia". It rests on two hypotheses: students identify with Simplicio and accept the equivalence of the falls.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students know the Earth rotates on its axis, providing the certainty that creates the conflict
- A historical fragment (Galileo's Dialogue) staging opposed positions students can identify with
#### Constraints
- The choice of Earth's rotation (rather than revolution) has inconveniences the authors discuss, since some students judged the two motions incomparable
- Without experimentation, some students reject the constructed explanation

### Target Learners
- 15-year-old ninth-grade students in heterogeneous classes

### Learning Goals
- Principle of conservation of motion
- Emergence of the principle of inertia
