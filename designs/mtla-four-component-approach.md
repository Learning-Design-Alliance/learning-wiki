# Metacognitive teaching-learning approach (MTLA) with four components: think-aloud, metacognitive scaffolding, discussions, and metacognitive journal writing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
MTLA is a teaching approach that emphasizes promoting students' awareness of their abilities by accustoming them to pose metacognitive questions covering understanding of concepts and problems, connecting new knowledge with previous knowledge, using relevant strategies, and reflecting on process and outcome. In this research it comprised think-aloud modeling by the teacher, metacognitive scaffolding through questioning (linked to Vygotsky's ZPD), pair, group and class discussion grounded in social constructivism, and metacognitive journal writing requiring students to clarify and reflect on their thought. It was implemented over one full semester against conventional teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teachers to model thinking aloud and to pose scaffolding questions that bridge what students can do alone and with assistance
- Requires social interaction structures (pair, group, class discussion) and journal-writing tasks that prompt metacognitive reflection
#### Constraints
- In this study it was applied to tenth-grade mathematics content (exponents and logarithms, quadratic functions, quadratic equations and inequalities, linear equation systems) in an Indonesian senior high school

### Target Learners
- tenth-grade senior high school mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- mathematical logical thinking ability
- self-regulated learning
