# Group poster presentation day as a large-class assessment pattern for community-service learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson/module-level design in which student groups of four design and co-present a poster about their community-service project to faculty assessors and peers at a scheduled poster day. At UKZN, "Sixty -three groups of four students each co-presented their work at the 'Making a Difference Poster Day', with an average of four groups being assessed per hour by two faculty assessors and their peers using a standardized marking rubric." Each group had 15 minutes (10-min presentation, 5-min questions), and the poster contributed 25% of the module mark.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of four students with a shared project experience to present
- A standardized marking rubric and paired faculty assessors
- Scheduled presentation slots (15 min per group) within a poster day
#### Constraints
- Assessed a group product, so the same mark went to all group members, which only 60% of staff considered fair to individual contributors

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students in large classes

### Learning Goals
- communicating community-service experiences
- analysing and synthesizing project learning
- teamwork and collaboration
