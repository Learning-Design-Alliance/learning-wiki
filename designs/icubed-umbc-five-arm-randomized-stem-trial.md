# iCubed@UMBC five-arm randomized control trial of STEM student success interventions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
iCubed@UMBC is a five-year NSF-funded investigation (Project Number 1038170, awarded 2010) into cost-effective, scalable strategies to increase STEM degree completion at UMBC. Eligible consenting freshmen are "randomly assigned into five separate groups": high-status faculty mentoring, professional staff mentoring, active-learning discussion sections in CASTLE, study-group assistance, and a control group offered a $50 bookstore gift card. Participation spans fall and spring semesters across three cohorts (2011-2012, 2012-2013, 2013-2014 academic years).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have math placement scores eligible for college algebra or higher and may not be participants in UMBC scholarship programs
- Consenting students sign consent forms before random assignment
#### Constraints
- Programs as currently administered can impact a limited number of students due to cost
- It is unclear which individual facets of UMBC's existing programs matter most for retention and graduation

### Target Learners
- Entering freshmen at a public four-year university intending to major in STEM disciplines

### Learning Goals
- STEM retention in the major
