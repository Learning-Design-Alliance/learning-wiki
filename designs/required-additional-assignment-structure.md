# Fortnightly assignments structured with Required (basic tier) and Additional (advanced tier) sections

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the authors' courses, each fortnightly assignment — the major basic-tier task, worth 50% in total — is split into a basic tier labelled "Required" and an advanced tier labelled "Additional". The Required section states that completing all required exercises correctly earns a grade of 5, with three of four questions earning a grade of 4; the Additional section states that attempts there "can earn additional marks, but will not count towards the grade of 1–5 for the exercises". Because assignments are the first marked task, they are used to communicate the assessment scheme to students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructions in both sections must clearly state how grades and additional marks are determined
- Introductory materials and the course profile also describe the assessment scheme
#### Constraints
- Advanced tier questions are more difficult and more open-ended than basic tier questions

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- Demonstrating fundamental conceptual understanding (basic tier) and higher-level, open-ended problem solving (advanced tier)
