# Simulation-based EOL communication workshop with professional actors and facilitated video-based debriefing at the Israel Center for Medical Simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A workshop developed with a steering committee of physicians, psychologists, nurses, social workers and ethicists at the Israel Centre for Medical Simulation (MSR). It used "simulation with actors on EOL scenarios followed by facilitated video-based group discussion", built on six scenarios covering eliciting EOL preferences, handling conflicts, requests to do "everything", and feeding-tube decisions. Professional actors were trained to respond to trainee behavior and give personal feedback. Since 2011 it reached 1324 providers from 32 medical centers in 100 workshops, later extended to nursing homes, dialysis, ICU, neurology, neurosurgery, family medicine and emergency rooms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained professional actors able to respond flexibly to trainee behavior and provide personal feedback
- Facilitated video-based group debriefing after each simulation
#### Constraints
- The artificial setting barely reproduces the nuances of sensitive EOL communications with patients and families, per the authors' limitations section

### Target Learners
- staff physicians
- department chiefs
- head nurses
- residents

### Learning Goals
- end-of-life communication skills
- eliciting patient preferences
- handling conflicts and dilemmas
- teamwork
