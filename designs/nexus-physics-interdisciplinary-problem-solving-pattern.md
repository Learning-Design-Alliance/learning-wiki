# Interdisciplinary introductory physics course structured around group problem-solving tasks connecting physics, biology, and chemistry

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The NEXUS/Physics course ran as a typical introductory physics course structurally (three 50-minute lectures, one 2-hour lab, one 50-minute recitation weekly), but its "lectures and discussion sections involved extensive group problem-solving tasks that were designed to build connections between chemistry, biology, and physics." Lecture offered student-centered group activities such as open-ended white-boarding, clicker questions with peer discussion, and weekly quizzes, with an instructor who invited alternative explanations. Recitation tasks were designed as starting places for students to explore the realm of applicability of physics to biology and chemistry rather than following a pre-determined path.

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals
- building coherence between physics and biology; developing competence and identification in physics
