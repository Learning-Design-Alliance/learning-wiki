# Inductive learning through qualitative pattern analysis as the paradigm suited to information-age physics education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper's organizing framework holds that the traditional deductive physics paradigm—rigorous derivations after mathematical preparation—must be augmented because navigating today's information abundance depends on "pattern recognition and prompt qualitative analysis." It argues "Inductive learning (using examples and intuition-based) is most suitable for the development of such skills," grounding this in brain functionality based on pattern recognition and neural-network training. Qualitative methods are approximate yet rigorous, fast and intuitive, enabling recognition, classification and prediction, and bring learning closer to active research practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple examples ('patterns') analyzed by qualitative methods, with special training and professional guidance for back-of-envelope techniques
- Qualified instructors to guide students through the material
#### Constraints
- The authors state the inclusion of qualitative methods is not a complete substitute for a traditional, well-organized QM class where mathematical derivations follow conceptual content

### Target Learners
- college and graduate students in science and engineering
- physics instructors

### Learning Goals
- pattern recognition and prompt qualitative analysis
- qualitative understanding, simplified models, and order-of-magnitude estimates
