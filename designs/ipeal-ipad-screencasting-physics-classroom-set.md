# iPad-supported screencasting and digital lab reports in high-school physics (iPEAL classroom set)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The iPad Enhanced Active Learning (iPEAL) project supplied five high-school physics classes with a shared set of 38 iPads, one per student during class. Students "created screencasts of their textbook problem solutions," recording think-aloud audio with stylus work on the iPad screen, and these screencasts "were later made available to other students." Students also produced digital lab reports embedding videos, such as the car-and-coffee-cup inertia video. The tools supplemented traditional physics assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom set of iPads with screencast applications and styluses, shared across class periods
#### Constraints
- Devices were shared with four other students throughout the day; the school initially lacked funds for AppleCare, cases, and styluses

### Target Learners
- high school physics students in regular and Advanced Placement courses

### Learning Goals
- physics problem solving with recorded explanations
- data collection and analysis in inquiry labs
