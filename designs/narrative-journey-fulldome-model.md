# Narrative journey model of fulldome presentation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A presentation model for fulldome theaters in which "audiences are taken on virtual tours with few cuts or edits, making the visual experience nearly seamless." Point-of-view camera shots make viewers feel they are moving through space rather than observing passively, and camera motions are slowed because fast movement causes motion sickness, while cuts must be spaced so viewers can adjust to each new scene. The continuous journey across multiple scales lets viewers reflect on size relationships and link processes at different scales. The study's virtual flights through planetary moon systems were designed in this format, with one exception (camera cuts to show orbital resonance).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive real-time planetarium software capable of seamless, continuous trans-scalar flightpaths between celestial bodies
- A live presenter or navigator controlling the visualization in real time
#### Constraints
- Camera motions must be slowed and cuts limited to avoid motion sickness and disorientation
- The study's resonance demonstration was the only exception to the continuous journey model, requiring camera cuts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory astronomy students

### Learning Goals
- understanding the number, appearance, orbital motions, and origins of moons in the Solar System
