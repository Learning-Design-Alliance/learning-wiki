# Three-tool gated quiz system (AMES, CHAGS, QUICK) for large introductory physics courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes an integrated program of three internet-based "teaching-while-quizzing" tools administered in sequence: AMES math quizzes first, then CHAGS homework grading and QUICK chapter quizzes, with "a student must get a perfect score on all questions in order to move on" to the next chapter's work. The gating structure forces remediation early and keeps students current. It was designed so instructors can adopt it "with modest effort" in lecture courses of typically 1,500 students per semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Internet delivery system such as WebCT, problem pools, and test-bank items for quiz generation
- students must complete AMES, CHAGS and QUICK to pass the course
#### Constraints
- described for large, traditional, calculus-based lecture courses; the authors note they have not been able to widely implement Interactive Engagement methods

### Target Learners
- first-year engineering students in large university introductory calculus-based physics courses

### Learning Goals
- problem-solving skills including translating physical problems into symbolic variables and equations
- prerequisite mathematics facility
- keeping up with course content
