# The Grade Game: an in-class single-play Prisoner's Dilemma with exam points at stake

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study, `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The Grade Game is a single play simultaneous move game with a dominant strategy, modeled after the classic Prisoner's Dilemma, played in Eco101 after a Game Theory module covering dominant strategies and the Nash Equilibrium. Students are randomly paired and circle MORE or LESS points on the next exam: "If one student chooses MORE while his partner chooses LESS, he will receive 2 extra points and the partner will receive -10 points." Outcomes are designated as points that can be won or lost on the next in-class exam to make the game realistic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class play so the instructor can enforce the restriction of communication between students and give each student equal time to participate
#### Constraints
- Bonus or penalty points were not actually awarded on the subsequent exam; this was announced at the end of the class period

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in a Principles of Economics course

### Learning Goals
- understanding the Prisoner's Dilemma, dominant strategies, and the Nash Equilibrium as applied to oligopolistic market structure
