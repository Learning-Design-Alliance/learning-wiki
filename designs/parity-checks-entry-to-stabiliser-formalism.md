# Parity checks as an intuitive entry point into the stabiliser formalism of quantum error correction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article positions parity checking — a concept from classical error correction — as the pedagogical core of its game. "Simple families of codes such as stabiliser codes -to which the popular surface code [7], [13] belong- relies on, among others, the concept of parity checks." Because this concept is central to many codes, both classical and quantum, and "because of how intuitive it can be constitutes a fair candidate for early learning," the game models it through marble-colour comparisons between pairs of Messengers, with the quantum version extending the same mechanism to colour and shape (bit flips and phase flips).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The reader/educator is assumed to already be familiar with the aspects of QEC being modelled in the game
#### Constraints
- The article is not meant as an introduction or presentation of QEC to the reader
- Some quantum-specific barriers — the no cloning theorem and wave function collapse upon measurement — make classical error correction primitives impossible, and quantum errors come in two kinds, bit flips and phase flips, versus only bit flips classically

### Target Learners
- children aged 7 and above in early K12

### Learning Goals
- understand parity checks as used in the stabiliser formalism
