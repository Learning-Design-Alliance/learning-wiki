# Writing-to-read lesson sequence with speed-practice and simulated e-mail activities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The program's instructional pattern moves from sound-level to text-level production: lessons "begin by having students say and write individual sounds and the letters that represent those sounds, and then progress to having students write words, sentences, and stories." Two supplementary activities extend the pattern: the Power Fountain activity for practicing "speed and accuracy in writing and typing" after attaining a certain level, and the E-mail Tower, "a simulated e-mail program," for practicing newly acquired skills by writing short messages. Teachers or parents can print individual or group progress reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The software plus corresponding activity book lessons; a teacher or parent able to print progress reports; computer access at school or home
#### Constraints
- Speed and accuracy practice via the Power Fountain activity is available only after students attain a certain level; the E-mail Tower is described as optional

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers aged six to nine
- Struggling readers and writers

### Learning Goals
- Phoneme-letter association
- Sounding out, typing, and writing words, sentences, and stories
