# TupperSat Satellite Subsystems laboratory module

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A 10-credit second-term module in UCD's 12-month MSc in Space Science & Technology in which student teams complete a full satellite development cycle in a 12-week term: they design, build, launch by weather balloon, operate and recover a payload on a standardised platform. Teams receive a starter kit (Raspberry Pi, GPS, sensors, LoRa transceiver, power bank), a EUR 100 budget for additional materials, and must pass Preliminary Design, Flight Readiness and Flight Acceptance Reviews before launch. Instructors act as customer and launch authority, and the module "adopts a tailored version of project phases, from Phase A (Feasibility) to Phase E (Operations)".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams must build within a household plastic storage container enclosure, under 1 kg mass, using the specified balloon attachment
- Teams must telemeter location, altitude, internal and external temperature and air pressure via the instructor-operated ground segment
- Assessment combines group deliverables (25%), an individual final report (15%) and continuous assessment (60%)
#### Constraints
- Requires substantial staffing: two staff spending a combined about 4-6 full days per week on the module
- Annual consumable costs run to EUR 2000-2500, about half spent on the launch campaign

### Target Learners
- postgraduate MSc students from science, engineering or mathematics backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- systems engineering
- project management
- embedded programming in Python
- teamwork and documentation
