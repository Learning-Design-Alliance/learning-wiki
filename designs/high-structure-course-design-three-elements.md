# High-structure course design: preclass preparation, in-class active learning, and postclass self-testing bracketing intensive active learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course design in which students "learn basic content on their own prior to class, with a quiz based on assigned readings or videos," apply content to higher-order problems through peer-based active-learning exercises during class with instructor feedback, and test understanding after class with a low-stakes quiz or practice exam. The article describes it as bracketing "intensive active learning during face-to-face interactions in the classroom with individual preparation prior to class and self-testing after," distinguishing it from flipped models that omit the postclass self-testing element. The first author implemented it iteratively in BIO 110 at EMU.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students prepare basic content before class via quiz-incentivized readings or videos
- In-class time is devoted to higher-order active-learning exercises with peers and instructor feedback
- A low-stakes postclass quiz or practice exam provides self-testing before high-stakes exams
#### Constraints
- The article reports the design as published failed to help EMU students when preclass preparation relied on textbook reading, succeeding only after substitution with instructor-authored video lectures

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science majors in large-enrollment introductory biology at an open-enrollment regional university

### Learning Goals
- introductory biology content: biological molecules, cell structure and function, energetics, cell division, genetics, evolution, and ecology
