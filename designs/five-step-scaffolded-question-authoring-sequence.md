# Five-step scaffolded sequence of question answering, evaluation, and solo-then-cooperative authoring

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a semester of PeerWise-supported activities so that answering and evaluating existing questions precedes solo authoring, which precedes cooperative authoring with peer feedback. The exercises "leverage analogical comparison, self-explanation, and confronting misconceptions," and generative prompts ask what would happen if a step were inhibited or activated and invite analogies. Each step was two weeks apart, with questions assigned to randomly distributed learning outcomes covering exam content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online question bank platform (PeerWise) with rating and comment functions
- Instructor-led tutorial on Bloom's taxonomy and reflective questioning
- Learning outcomes allocated across the cohort
#### Constraints
- Steps 3 (solo) and 4 (cooperative) targeted different content domains, confounding their comparison

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate biochemistry students in large classes

### Learning Goals
- deep conceptual understanding of molecular biology and metabolism beyond rote recall
