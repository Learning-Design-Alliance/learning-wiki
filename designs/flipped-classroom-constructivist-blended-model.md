# Flipped classroom as constructivist blended learning that moves lower-order Bloom's levels outside class

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes the flipped classroom as "a specific type of blended learning" rooted in constructivist learning theory, in which students acquire foundational knowledge through instructional videos before class and class time is devoted to "problem-solving, deep conceptual learning, group discussions, and the clinical application of existing knowledge." Lower levels of Bloom's taxonomy are addressed outside class while in-class time advances higher-order skills. In the trial this design was implemented over four two-hour sessions with Telegram-delivered materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-class materials available online before each session
- Students arrive prepared to engage in discussion and group work
#### Constraints
- The article notes pre-class preparation time (60–80 minutes per session in its logs) is a key practical concern where student workloads are high

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- health professions students

### Learning Goals
- foundational content knowledge
- higher-order cognitive skills such as application and analysis
