# SMART® four-strategy volunteer tutoring pattern: reading to, reading with, re-reading, and questioning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The SMART® program structures minimal-training volunteer tutoring around four reading strategies: "reading to the child, reading with the child, re-reading with the child, and asking the child questions about what has been read." A volunteer handbook describes these strategies, and volunteers rely on their own judgment for any other needs. The pattern intentionally places minimal demands on volunteer tutors and classroom teachers, with training focusing as much on tutoring logistics as on reading instruction techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A volunteer handbook describing the four reading strategies
- 1–2 hours of volunteer training, preferably before the school year begins
#### Constraints
- The program intentionally requires minimal training, so volunteers rely on their own judgment beyond the four strategies

### Target Learners
- First- and second-grade students at risk of reading failure

### Learning Goals
- Reading engagement, fluency, and comprehension through one-on-one reading interaction
