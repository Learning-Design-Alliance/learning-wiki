# Three-method projectile launcher measurement activity comparing stopwatch, maximum-height, and high-speed-camera techniques

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A lab activity in which students measure the launch speed of a ball from a spring-loaded projectile launcher (Pasco 6801) three ways: stopwatch time-of-flight, maximum height against a mounted two-meter stick read by eye, and camera timing at 250 frames per second. Each student records ten heights and ten times and computes averages and launch speeds. The article describes it as suitable for "an introductory college level (or advanced high school) course".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A spring-loaded projectile launcher, stopwatch, two-meter stick mounted next to the launcher, and optionally a high-speed camera are needed; students stand on a lab table so their eyes are at the appropriate level for height readings.
#### Constraints
- The camera setup records one launch per lab group of three students; the authors note similar results could be obtained with "a cheaper camera with a lower frame rate, and averaging several trials".

### Target Learners
- Introductory college algebra-based physics students
- Advanced high school physics students

### Learning Goals
- Distinguishing random from systematic uncertainty
- Comparing measurement techniques
- Applying constant-acceleration kinematics
