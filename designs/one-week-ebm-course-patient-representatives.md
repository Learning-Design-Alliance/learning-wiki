# One-week generic EBM course for patient and consumer representatives built on four core EBM steps and controversial medical topics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A five-day (or 2 × 3-day) generic EBM course for medical laypersons, adapted from Oxford EBM courses and prior courses for non-medical health professionals. Teaching followed "cognitive learning and teaching principles" with small-group work, a 60-page advance preparation handbook, and five additional teaching sessions on statistics, media, risk communication, drug approval, and review boards. Controversial topics such as hormone therapy, colorectal cancer screening and a homeopathy meta-analysis were used to demonstrate EBM principles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants without previous EBM training willing to develop skills to critically appraise scientific literature
- A handbook of about 60 pages with publications, vocabularies, glossaries and work sheets sent two weeks in advance for advance preparation
#### Constraints
- The curriculum needs updating as EBM develops
- Offering the programme to other patients or consumers would need further simplification and adaptation

### Target Learners
- Patient and consumer representatives who are medical laypersons, including self-help group representatives, professional counsellors and patient advocates

### Learning Goals
- Basic EBM competencies: question formulation, literature search, critical appraisal, calculation of risk measures, and communication of study results to patients
