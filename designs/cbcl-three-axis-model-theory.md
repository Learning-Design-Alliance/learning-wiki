# CBCL as a three-axis pedagogical model combining team-based, problem-based, and case-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Case-Based Collaborative Learning is described as combining "principles of team-based learning, problem-based learning, and case-based learning through structured preparation and collaborative discussion of clinical cases." The article adopts the model described by Besche et al., which organizes the experience into "three interconnected axes: instructional design, facilitation, and professional transformation," with success depending on coherence between prior preparation, case complexity, teacher mediation, and post-class consolidation. CBCL is presented as reorganizing the roles of student, teacher, and clinical case rather than being merely a technique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coherence between prior preparation, appropriate case complexity, teacher mediation, and post-class consolidation
- Adequately prepared facilitators and calibrated case complexity
#### Constraints
- Much of the CBCL literature is limited by single-center experiences, heterogeneous implementation models, and predominance of subjective outcomes

### Target Learners
- Medical undergraduates
- Residents in radiology and other specialties

### Learning Goals
- Vertical integration of basic and clinical knowledge
- Engagement and collaborative reasoning
- Self-regulated learning
