# Standardized optics workstations, lab-skill activities, and YouTube screencasts supporting Mathematica data analysis and LabVIEW

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The transformed course includes standardized optics workstations 'that can be adapted to any of the optics experiments conducted in the lab,' each furnished with a DC power supply, oscilloscope, and waveform generator, with all new equipment 'general purpose, flexible-use, and low-cost lab grade.' A suite of short lab-skill activities covering 'uncertainty, computer-aided data analysis, and LabVIEW' runs in lecture time before each lab section. A series of screencasts on YouTube demonstrates common activities like importing data into Mathematica, providing on-demand support for students and easing faculty adoption.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Equipment chosen for ubiquitousness and adaptability to a variety of experimental investigations rather than uniqueness
- Reuse and adaptation of existing lab equipment as much as possible
#### Constraints
- The lecture time was also used for students to give oral presentations to classmates and instructors

### Target Learners
- senior-level undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- technical lab skills
- computer-aided data analysis
- measurement automation with LabVIEW
