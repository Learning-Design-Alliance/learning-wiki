# Scaffold and govern ultrasound training continuously across medical school and internship, starting early with gradually increasing complexity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends organizing ultrasound education as a scaffolded, governed continuum rather than isolated courses: "Starting early – possibly in medical school – with a scaffolded educational plan continuously offering support while gradually increasing complexity fosters skill development". Training governance and scaffolding continuous learning are presented as responses to interns' independently pursued training and prevalent barriers. The recommendation rests on the survey's findings that structured components and tests associated with perceived competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires structured educational components including practical training on patients, required scan numbers, and competence tests, which the article found associated with perceived competence.
- Requires continuous exposure to ultrasound, since the article states scanning competence develops over time and requires practice.
#### Constraints
- Traditional lectures cannot stand alone, and restricting teaching to short practical sessions is insufficient according to the article.
- Implementation has previously been challenged by lack of qualified faculty teachers and ultrasound equipment, though the article says the importance of these factors may be transient.
- Filled curricula in medical school and internship pose dilemmas for prioritization.

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Medical interns in their postgraduate internship year

### Learning Goals
- Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) scanning competence
