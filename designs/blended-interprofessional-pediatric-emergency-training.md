# Blended interprofessional pediatric emergency training combining in-house guidelines, profession-specific virtual patients, and simulation-based team training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A training course for medical and nursing staff at a tertiary pediatric center built on a blended learning approach based on in-house guidelines, profession-specific VPs as individual preparation, and simulation-based team training. Eight virtual patients were developed in physician and nurse versions, each guiding participants through an emergency encounter with questions, feedback, pictures, video clips and interactive graphics, implemented in ILIAS and mandatory before an 8-hour interprofessional hands-on training day in groups of two physicians and two nurses with debriefing by physician–nurse tutor tandems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Work-up of the preparatory virtual patients was mandatory for participation in the hands-on team training
- Tutor tandems comprised a physician with PICU experience and a PICU nurse to ensure interprofessional feedback
#### Constraints
- The manikins used could not change skin color, show cyanosis or a convulsion, and could not give input to pulse oximetry or blood pressure measurement

### Target Learners
- postgraduate medical staff
- nursing staff in a tertiary pediatric hospital

### Learning Goals
- pediatric emergency knowledge and skills
- interprofessional teamwork and communication
