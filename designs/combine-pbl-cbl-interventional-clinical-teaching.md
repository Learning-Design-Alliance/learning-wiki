# Combine problem-based learning with case-based learning when designing clinical practical teaching for interventional procedures such as TACE

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends the combined PBL+CBL teaching mode for TACE clinical education, concluding it "significantly improves the teaching effect and significantly improves the theoretical and surgical operations, meeting the requirements of clinical education." PBL supplies student-centered, question-guided group work while CBL anchors learning in real clinical cases that students investigate before teacher-led analysis. The authors suggest the combination allows more student-teacher interaction and helps forge a preliminary mind of clinical logic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teaching must combine question-guided problem-based group work with real clinical cases that students inquire into before the teacher systematically explains, as the article describes both methods.
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is 7 small Chinese studies mixing RCTs and observational designs with subjective outcome measures; the authors state researchers outside China should interpret results with caution and that further high-quality studies are needed.

### Target Learners
- medical students
- surgical trainees in interventional radiology

### Learning Goals
- theoretical knowledge of TACE
- practical operational skills
- clinical thinking capacity
