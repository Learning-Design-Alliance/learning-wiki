# Collaborative expert-role design combining experiments, simulations and theory around a research game

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A didactic design implemented in Class 3, inspired by profession simulation games and collaborative learning, in which the teacher assigns students to three expertise teams: experimental physicists doing analogue experiments, IT professionals performing virtual experiments in the game, and theoretical experts working on the mathematics. "Students worked in their group for three class periods. They were then mixed with students from the two other groups to share observations and to produce a poster or film with their conclusions."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A didactic design developed in collaboration with the class teacher
- the game introduced in previous lessons by the teacher
#### Constraints
- The design was implemented in one Class 3 intervention over four school periods; its effect on perceived learning was not tested as a controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- third-year Danish high school students aged 17-20 in a technical high school

### Learning Goals
- strengthening students' perceived learning of quantum physics
- participating in authentic professional quantum physics practice
