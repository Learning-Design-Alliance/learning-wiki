# Four principal components of a successful bridge between courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A WPI faculty group evaluating the bridge model identified four principal components of a successful bridge: "contextual learning", an outside expert guest lecturer, pinpointing possible confusions in style, terminology, and notation, and communication between the instructors of the two courses. The article explains that contextual learning gives students an identity by grouping them by major, the outside lecturer lends credibility, and notation explanation removes stumbling blocks. The components were speculative but backed by anecdotal student comments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires coordination between instructors of the two linked courses so overlapping material and notation differences can be identified and explained.
#### Constraints
- The article states the successful components were speculative, largely backed up by anecdotal comments of the students rather than quantified assessment.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in introductory physics service courses

### Learning Goals
- seeing connections between a physics course and the student's engineering major
- deeper understanding of overlapping course material
