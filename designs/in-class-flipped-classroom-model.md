# In-class flipped classroom model for nursing practice teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The in-class flipped classroom is a localized variant of the flipped classroom in which, unlike the traditional flipped classroom approach, the in-class flipped model relocates self-directed pre-class learning activities from outside to within the classroom setting. The article grounds it in Community of Inquiry theory (teaching, social, and cognitive presence) and situated learning theory, and implemented it in pediatric nursing skills lab courses to alleviate workload, time constraints, and lack of familiarity associated with pre-class activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must possess "extensive professional expertise and contingency ability" to guide students' self-directed problem-solving.
#### Constraints
- The approach was implemented within pediatric nursing skills courses; when applied in other courses, appropriate adjustments may be needed, such as modifications to the time allocation of the instructional procedures.
- The study was conducted at a nursing school in China, which may limit generalizability to other contexts.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in skills lab courses

### Learning Goals
- practical nursing skills and clinical competence
- self-directed learning and higher-order cognitive skills
