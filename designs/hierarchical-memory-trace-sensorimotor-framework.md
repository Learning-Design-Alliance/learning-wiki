# Hierarchical memory-trace framework for sensorimotor training design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that bodily experiences be organized by the proximity of their memory traces to the learning content: specific (pencil-and-paper), intermediate (arm), and general (body) levels. It assumes "memory traces generated by bodily experiences more distal to the content of the activity" need to be "nested within memory traces of more proximal levels" to create synergies in learning. Training conditions are nested: arm training combines specific and intermediate traces, arm-body training all three levels. The framework draws on embodied cognition, the modality-like "multimodality effect", the variability effect, and the human movement effect from cognitive load theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Higher-level bodily movement training must be combined with lower-level training (e.g., handwriting exercises in all conditions) so that distal memory traces are nested within proximal ones
#### Constraints
- A pilot study without handwriting exercises in the arm and arm-body conditions showed null or negative effects of bodily engagement, which the authors attributed to the lack of specific sensorimotor experiences

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten students (around 5 years old) acquiring pre-reading skills

### Learning Goals
- Letter knowledge (letter recognition, letter-name knowledge)
- Letter script skills (dictation, stroke direction, legibility)
