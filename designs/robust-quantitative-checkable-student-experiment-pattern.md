# Student physics experiments designed around robust data extraction, quantitative results, and checking against fundamental physics principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design pattern for undergraduate laboratory experiments: the setup is easy to assemble and safe, data acquisition is robust and insensitive to the environment, results are quantitative, and the measured quantity can be checked against fundamental physics principles (here, gravitational acceleration). The authors state that for ordinary laboratory situations "we designed the experiments with the data extraction that is ro bust and clear cut", picking the highest spectral peaks even though reflections create non-ideal spectra with multiple peaks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experiment that can be easily set up and safely conducted in an ordinary laboratory
- Data acquisition that is robust and not sensitive to the environment
- Analysis not necessitating higher mathematics, with inexpensive readily available components
#### Constraints
- Reflections in ordinary student laboratories inevitably cause non-ideal spectra and multiple peaks; approaching objects with higher peak frequencies tend to be easier to measure because interference affects the highest-frequency peak less substantially

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students majoring in any field, including non-science majors

### Learning Goals
- quantitative measurement of a fundamental physics phenomenon (the Doppler effect)
- connecting measurement results to fundamental physics principles
