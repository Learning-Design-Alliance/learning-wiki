# Three calculation-concept crossover assessment items

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Three assessment items operationalize the crossover framework: a qualitative judgment question (does mechanical energy increase, decrease, or stay the same during an explosion, embedded in quantitative parts), isomorphic calculation questions about a block on a ramp held by string, pulley-block, or static friction, and a cued symbolic evaluation question asking students to debunk a proposed angular-speed expression without calculating. The article states these items show "how the increased flexibility provided by introducing calculation-concept crossover in one's 'problem-solving toolbox' can lead to more efficient, insightful, and accurate problem solving."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Items must be scored for approach type (calculation vs conceptual) as well as correctness; incorrect approaches of either kind are still classifiable
#### Constraints
- The cued symbolic evaluation item tests skill rather than proclivity plus skill, because spontaneous checking on classroom tasks may depend on time constraints and student expectations

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students in large lecture sections

### Learning Goals
- calculation-concept crossover approaches
- mathematical sensemaking
