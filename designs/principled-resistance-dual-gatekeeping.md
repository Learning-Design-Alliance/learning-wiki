# Principled resistance: theory-grounded, dual-layer gatekeeping of AI-generated educational content

## Description
The article conceptualizes "principled resistance" as "deliberate, theory-grounded pushback against AI outputs that fail pedagogical standards", taking three concrete forms: rejecting (requesting regeneration), revising (manual editing), and overriding (vetoing automated flags). Rooted in Mayer's CTML, the framework holds that educators and algorithms catch different kinds of problems—humans detect nuanced pedagogical mismatches while algorithms verify structural integrity—so building both checkpoints into one pipeline creates overlapping coverage neither could achieve alone. It reframes pedagogical friction as a site of professional accountability rather than inefficiency.
