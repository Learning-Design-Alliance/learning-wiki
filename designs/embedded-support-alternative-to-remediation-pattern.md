# Embedded instructional support within a college-level course as an alternative to remediation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course-design pattern in which underprepared students are enrolled directly in a college-level course that embeds added instructional time and support rather than being routed into remedial courses. In this study, General Chemistry-IS covered "typical content taught in a freshman-level chemistry course" while requiring weekly 1.25-hour TA-led sessions with small groups of 12 students (maximum), focused on problem-solving practice. The pattern aligns with reform recommendations to integrate underprepared students into college-level content courses while providing additional support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly instructional sessions led by trained TAs, adding 1.25 hours to the weekly course time
- TAs receive substantial training prior to the semester (a 15-hour initial training period in this study)
#### Constraints
- The study design did not permit analysis of which aspects of the treatment were effective
- Applicability demonstrated at a single institution with students underprepared in math

### Target Learners
- College students identified as academically underprepared in mathematics

### Learning Goals
- Succeed in a college-level general chemistry course without prior remediation
