# Four coordinated disciplinary tracks (ME, Build, EE, CS+AI) with dependency-gated progression

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The curriculum organizes content into four coordinated tracks connecting disciplinary ideas to practical decisions: Mechanical Engineering connects geometry, balance, and motion to predictions; Build turns assembly into practice in interpreting instructions and diagnosing faults; Electrical Engineering develops safe working practices before power-on; and CS+AI uses simulation, learning, and physical comparison. "On-robot control requires the electrical power-on gate, and walking requires a standing test," while simulation can begin independently for early conceptual progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Safety checks that constrain hardware work
- Concepts introduced immediately before their application and revisited in debriefs
#### Constraints
- The progression figure is explicitly not a synchronized timetable

### Target Learners
- High-school students working in pairs on a shared robot

### Learning Goals
- Connecting geometry, balance, motion, and reward-based learning to robot behavior
