# SOM-PBL: an integrated instructional model combining symptom-oriented mind mapping with problem-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
SOM-PBL is a dual-pedagogical framework in which PBL anchors authentic clinical scenarios while symptom-oriented mind maps deconstruct complexity into actionable subtasks. The article describes "a self-reinforcing learning cycle—problem-driven contextualization (PBL), knowledge structuring (Mind Mapping), and practice-based validation". It is grounded in cognitive schema theory, emphasizing structured knowledge architectures in clinical reasoning, and was evaluated in ICU clerkships against traditional lecture-based teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-designed structured mind-map templates with central symptoms, collaborative PBL sessions, scaffolded authentic case sequences, and formative feedback on submitted maps
#### Constraints
- The intervention has a cognitive rather than psychomotor focus, so it did not improve procedural skills in the trial
- Perceived additional cognitive load was reported by about one-third of students

### Target Learners
- Fifth-year undergraduate medical students in ICU clerkships

### Learning Goals
- Knowledge integration across disciplines
- Clinical reasoning and diagnostic rigor
- Time-sensitive decision-making in critical care
