# Bernoulli's optical solution to the Brachistochrone as a framing device for teaching the PLA

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents Johann Bernoulli's 1696 solution to the Brachistochrone problem as a pedagogically powerful framing device. Bernoulli's solution was to imagine the Brachistochrone as a path of light traveling through an optically homogeneous medium, exploiting that light naturally takes the path of least time. Since light follows Snell's Law when changing mediums, whatever path light takes must satisfy Snell's Law at each point. This reframes a mechanics problem as an optimization problem for light, connecting the Brachistochrone to the Principle of Least Action and giving students an analogy between light and a ball rolling down a cycloid.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must understand Snell's Law and the behavior of light changing mediums for the analogy to be meaningful.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the Brachistochrone simulation is naturally challenging to engineer due to the many degrees of freedom afforded to the user.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in Optics or Advanced Mechanics courses

### Learning Goals
- understand the Principle of Least Action as applied to the Brachistochrone problem
- recognize the analogy between light paths and least-time curves
