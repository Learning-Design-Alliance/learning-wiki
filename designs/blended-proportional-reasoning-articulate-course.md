# Blended video-based proportional reasoning math course built in Articulate 360 with segmented videos, worked examples, and standardized UX interface

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The blended lesson was developed using Articulate 360, an e-learning authoring suite. An hour-long video lesson was "segmented into ten parts instead of one continuous stream" to reduce fatigue, cognitive load, and mind-wandering, with embedded practice problems using worked examples and free-response reflection questions. The interface followed standardized usability heuristics, with consistent vectors, typographical emphasis, standardized information placements, a course roadmap, and accessibility features such as time triggers, audio narrations, and reminders. Video control was limited to pausing, with no playback or restart of lesson segments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The course requires individual laptops with headphones for students and a standardized interface adhering to usability heuristics for UX design
#### Constraints
- Students could only pause videos and were not able to playback or restart video lesson segments, a design choice made for experimental control and classroom time management

### Target Learners
- fifth and sixth grade students in Southern California school districts

### Learning Goals
- proportional reasoning
- flexible use of multiple solution strategies
