# Activity 2: combating future forest fires using K-means clustering and Apache Zeppelin

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A problem-based learning activity adapted from a forest fire prevention case study, in which students derive a resource allocation solution for wildfire response in Oregon using a frame-model-program-decide mode. "The K-means clustering algorithm is selected to partition the locations of historical forest fires into clusters", and cluster centroids assist in allocating firefighting infrastructure to regions most prone to burning. Students work with US forest fire data from 2000 to 2016 in the Apache Zeppelin web-based data science notebook.

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- Assumes driving factors of past wildfire trends are not changed or reduced when predicting the future

### Target Learners
- high school geography students

### Learning Goals
- predicting wildfire occurrence from historical data
- making data-informed fire management and infrastructure investment decisions
