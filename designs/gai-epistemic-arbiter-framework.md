# GAI as an epistemic arbiter mediating the transition from social conflict to collaborative reasoning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that GAI in collaborative problem-solving functions not merely as an information provider but as an "epistemic arbiter" that supplies facts and visualizations students use to resolve disputes constructively. Grounded in Distributed Cognition Theory, which "conceptualizes cognition as distributed across people, tools, and social interaction," and the ATC21S social/cognitive CPS dimensions, the framework holds that GAI reshapes the socio-cognitive structure of collaboration by lowering the participation threshold and mediating movement from conflict to solution-oriented reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- GAI outputs must be available to groups as external evidence (facts, visualizations) during moments of disagreement
#### Constraints
- Students rarely questioned or critically evaluated AI-generated outputs, indicating a risk of cognitive reliance without pedagogical scaffolds

### Target Learners
- elementary school students (fifth grade)

### Learning Goals
- collaborative problem-solving skills, including conflict management and collaborative reasoning
