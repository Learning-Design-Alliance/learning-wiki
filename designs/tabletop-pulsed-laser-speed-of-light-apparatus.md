# Inexpensive tabletop pulsed-laser and oscilloscope apparatus for direct speed-of-light measurement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A small tabletop setup in which a pulsed laser (1.0 MHz quartz-generated modulation, 650 nm, 30 µW average output) is reflected off a mirror onto a photodiode detector, and a 200 MHz oscilloscope measures the time of flight τ = 2L/c. The article states that "The total space required by the experimental equipment is about 1.5 m by 0.5 m" and that full circuit diagrams for the laser and detector are provided so other institutions can build it from inexpensive components.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Inexpensive components for building the light source and detector, with circuit diagrams explicitly described
- A 200 MHz-class oscilloscope, which the article notes are "now quite standard and are affordable"
#### Constraints
- Accuracy is limited by time-interval measurement error (~0.2 ns); a slower 100 MHz oscilloscope increases the error at the same distance

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students, particularly non-science and non-engineering majors
- possibly high school students

### Learning Goals
- directly experiencing the finiteness of the speed of light
- measurement uncertainty in a physical experiment
