# Convert existing manualised group programmes into serious game formats to broaden reach while preserving behaviour change technique fidelity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates that a theory-based group parenting programme can be translated into an interactive serious game, showing that "programmes struggling to reach the desired community can be transformed transparently and preserve the underlying knowledge base". Conversion involved selecting exercises convertible into first-person role play, mapped against the original intervention map, with user feedback on characters and message framing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exercises must be well received in the group programme, convertible into first-person role play, and mapped against key elements of the original intervention map
#### Constraints
- Higher attrition in the game condition suggests information dosing and in-game text volume must be addressed; the study was a small self-selected one-sitting feasibility pilot

### Target Learners
- Parents of children aged 5–14

### Learning Goals
- Improve parents' sex and relationships communication with their children
