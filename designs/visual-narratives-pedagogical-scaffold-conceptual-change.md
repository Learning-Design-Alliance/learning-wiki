# Visual narratives as a pedagogical scaffold that creates favorable conditions for conceptual change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames educational comics within a multimodal learning account in which combining text and images supports the construction of more robust mental representations, particularly for abstract or counterintuitive phenomena. Comics integrate "narrative sequencing, graphical representation, and contextualization", situating physics concepts in contexts closer to students' experiences. The authors conclude that visual narratives act as "a pedagogical scaffold that creates more favorable conditions for conceptual change", rather than as a direct mechanism of conceptual improvement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment between the comic-based instructional design and the targeted conceptual domains, which the article identifies as necessary for domain-specific conceptual gains.
#### Constraints
- The article states visual narratives are not a direct mechanism for conceptual improvement in themselves; their primary contribution lies in enhancing students' disposition toward learning.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate Chemistry and Pharmacy and Biochemistry students in non-physics university programs

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of mechanics
- Favorable motivation and affective disposition toward physics
