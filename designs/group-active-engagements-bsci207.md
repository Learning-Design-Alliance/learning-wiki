# Group Active Engagements (GAEs): permanent small groups in the principle-based organismal biology class build and apply conceptual, physical, and mathematical models of principles.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Group Active Engagements are the active-learning structure adopted in BSCI 207 after the physicist-biologist dialogue; "Each GAE is designed to help the student groups discuss and then organize their knowledge into a conceptual, physical, and/or mathematical (i.e., an equation) model of a particular principle". Students then apply the model to biological phenomena in class and in out-of-class problem-solving sessions. The H-P equation GAE, for example, has groups argue about which organism has the more "powerful" pump (giraffe vs. acacia), reconciling pressure, flow rate, and resistance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students are assigned to permanent small groups within the large classroom
- A significant number of class periods are devoted to GAEs, with follow-up group homework problem-solving sessions
#### Constraints
- GAEs do not typically use an idealized model first, but use simplified descriptions of real organisms to maintain an obvious connection between physics principles and biological phenomena
- Little effort is devoted to fitting the equation into the broader conceptual foundations of physics

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology majors in an introductory organismal biology course

### Learning Goals
- use general principles (e.g., gradient-driven flows, the Hagen-Poiseuille equation) to organize knowledge and explain biological phenomena
