# Weekly lecture-plus-assignment cycle with vendor talks and hypothesis-first experimentation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course is organized into weekly meetings, each pairing an assignment with a relevant lecture or vendor talk, so that practical tasks build progressively on instructed content. Before conducting experiments, students are directed to develop hypotheses about expected results, which the authors state can 'help them improve analytical skills and get deeper into quantum principles'. Vendor talks from IQM, AQT, and planqc expose students to superconducting, trapped-ion, and neutral-atom technologies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to real quantum systems and vendor collaboration for technology-specific talks
#### Constraints
- Students found lectures on ion-trapped and neutral atom quantum technologies challenging to follow compared with superconducting technology

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in quantum computing

### Learning Goals
- connecting quantum theory to real-system experimentation
- formulating and testing experimental hypotheses
