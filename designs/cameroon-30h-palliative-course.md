# A 30-hour classroom-based palliative care course underpinned by experiential learning theory, delivered by local facilitators with affordable resources in Cameroon

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course was a 30-hour classroom-based course "underpinned by the experiential learning theory", developed via a modified systematic review, review of international core curricula, and consultations with Cameroonian and UK palliative care educators. It was inserted into the regular nurse training program of one University, delivered in five days spaced over one month, and taught by 3 nurse educators, 2 specialist palliative care nurses and a chaplain. Content covered four modules: palliative care principles, communication and breaking bad news, pain and symptom management, and psychosocial, spiritual, ethical and bereavement issues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivered by facilitators selected for their academic and professional backgrounds and prior teaching experience on the topics they facilitated
- Uses local and affordable resources to aid future sustainability
#### Constraints
- Designed for a resource-poor context where palliative care drugs are not on the national drug list and no palliative care policy framework exists

### Target Learners
- Second and third year preregistration nursing students in Cameroon

### Learning Goals
- Palliative care competencies: communication, pain and symptom management, psychosocial and spiritual care, end-of-life and bereavement care
