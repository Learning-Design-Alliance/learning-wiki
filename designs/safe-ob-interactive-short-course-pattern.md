# Low faculty-to-participant ratio with scenario teaching, skills sessions, and small-group discussion instead of lectures

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The SAFE-OB course embodies a short-course design pattern in which interaction, not lecturing, carries the learning: "Lectures are kept to a minimum. The course is designed for 32 participants and 6–8 faculty instructors to keep the faculty/participant ratio low to optimize learning through interaction." Teaching uses "scenario teaching, skills sessions, and small-group discussion," and the authors note the "SAFE-OB style of teaching allows time for discussion," which they argue overcomes barriers such as skepticism and misunderstanding and lets participants share experiences for contextual relevance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient faculty instructors (6–8 for 32 participants) to sustain interaction
- Skills sessions with simulation-based assessment tools
- Discussion time within the course timetable
#### Constraints
- Delivered as a 3-day refresher for already-trained providers, not initial training
- Adaptations required where equipment (e.g., bougies) or medications were unavailable

### Target Learners
- Practicing anesthesia providers in low-resource settings

### Learning Goals
- Retention and application of emergency obstetric anesthesia knowledge and skills
