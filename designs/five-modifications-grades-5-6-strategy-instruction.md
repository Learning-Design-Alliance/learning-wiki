# Five modifications adapting a grades 7–12 strategy intervention for fifth and sixth graders

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article enumerates five targeted modifications: increased explicitness of instruction (expanding one routine lesson into three lessons on modeling, paraphrasing, and visualizing), expanded emphasis on metacognition with additional sequenced prompts, a new prerequisite lesson on concepts of the four operations, extension of initial instructional sessions from 3 to 6 days, and added visual supports (operations decision tree, paraphrasing and visualizing frameworks). The authors state: "we extended the initial instructional sessions from 3 to 6 days, expanded the focus on paraphrasing and visualizing strategies, increased the explicitness of the meta-cognitive components, taught and then reinforced concepts of the four arithmetic operations, and added visual supports."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Modifications were based on previous research, developmental characteristics of fifth and sixth graders, and a pilot study identifying deficits in concepts of operations
#### Constraints
- The weekly practice session script was not altered from the original intervention

### Target Learners
- Fifth and sixth grade students, including those with LD

### Learning Goals
- Math problem solving, including paraphrasing, visualizing, and solution planning
