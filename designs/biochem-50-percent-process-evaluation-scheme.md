# Split assessment scheme: 50% process evaluation with eight weighted components plus 50% final examination

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An assessment redesign for the reformed biochemistry course in which, per the article, "The degree of achievement of the course objectives is assessed via the process evaluation (worth 50% of the total grade) and the final examination (worth 50% of the total grade)". The process evaluation comprises stage tests (24%), chapter tests (18%), topic reports (16%), speech competitions (14%), study summaries (12%), note organizations (8%), case designs (6%), and topic discussions (2%), aiming to assess participation, applied knowledge, summarization, presentation and research skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor capacity to administer and grade eight distinct formative components across the semester
- Apps for topic discussions and a question repository for final-exam item selection
#### Constraints
- Reported in the context of this single-institution course; the authors note the single-institution sample limits generalizability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in biochemistry and molecular biology

### Learning Goals
- continuous formative feedback on knowledge application, participation, summarization, presentation and research skills
