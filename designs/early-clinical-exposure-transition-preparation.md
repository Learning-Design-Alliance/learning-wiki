# Early clinical experiences immerse preclinical students in clinical reality to ease the preclinical-to-clerkship transition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operates on the rationale, drawn from Dornan et al., that early clinical experiences (ECE) prepare preclinical students for clinical rotations by "immersing them in the reality of the clinical environment, by facilitating application of knowledge to clinical practice, and by improving students' motivation for learning". In this study the mechanism is instantiated as real-patient practice in PHC centers, whose authentic context is assumed more challenging and preparatory than extended manikin-based skills laboratory training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinical setting with real patient contact and adequate supervision is needed during the preclinical phase.
#### Constraints
- Assessment of effectiveness in this study was based only on student opinions, not objective performance measures.

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students

### Learning Goals
- application of trained clinical skills to real patients
- motivation for learning
- preparedness for clinical rotations
