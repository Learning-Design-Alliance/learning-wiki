# Deliver brief manualized interventions through typically trained school mental health providers to overcome the research-to-practice gap

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that evidence-based interventions can reach real-world students when designed for school delivery: brief (16 sessions under 20 minutes), manualized, implemented during the school day with only two parent meetings, and delivered by "typically trained school mental health providers" without ongoing supervision. In this trial, providers received only the manuals and two 1-hour review meetings, yet implemented both interventions with fidelity and produced significant improvements. The authors conclude this service delivery model "may facilitate overcoming the oft cited research-to-practice gap."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- manualized intervention feasible with school resources
- providers review the manual before implementation
- fidelity monitoring to guard against diffusion across conditions
#### Constraints
- the design carries a risk of diffusion across conditions because each provider delivered both interventions
