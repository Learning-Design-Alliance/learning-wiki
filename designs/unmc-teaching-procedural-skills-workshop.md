# 90-minute teach-the-teacher workshop on procedural skills teaching (University of Nebraska Medical Center clinician educator track)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 90-minute interactive workshop for medical residents and fellows (postgraduate years 2–6) from 11 disciplines, developed as part of a clinician educator track. It integrated "Gagne's model of instructional design with evidence-based teaching practices" (Peyton's four-step model and deliberate practice), used small-group role-play applying Gagne's model to create a handwashing curriculum, and required few resources—each partner group received hand lotion costing under $10—and no simulation mannequins. Evaluation used paired pre/post surveys analyzed by paired t test, framed by the Kirkpatrick model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A lecture room accommodating approximately 20 learners with tables for small-group interaction
- A participant packet with a role-play exercise and theory reference sheet
- Two faculty facilitators with procedural teaching experience
#### Constraints
- The "wide, not deep" generalizable approach may challenge learners with highly specialized procedural practices such as advanced endoscopy or invasive surgeries
- Integrating multiple theories may confuse participants without background knowledge of educational theories

### Target Learners
- medical residents and fellows from diverse specialties

### Learning Goals
- apply Gagne's instructional design model to procedural skills teaching
- apply Peyton's four-step model
- define and apply deliberate practice
