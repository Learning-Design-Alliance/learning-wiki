# MEU's four learning instruments: toys and models, mathematical games, role-plays, and history of discovery

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The program classifies its activity designs by dominant learning instrument. "We divide them into four categories: a) the use of toys and models, b) mathematical games, c) role-plays with songs and poems, and d) the history of discovery." Each approach is said to provide complementary tactile, auditory, social and story-based experiences. Examples include Lycra fabric spacetime models, an exponential dice game on a logarithmic number line, the Discovery of Zero role-play, and the history of pi and curved-space measurement at Wallal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities are placed first in each lesson to secure engagement before formal explanation
#### Constraints
- The article notes that excessive unstructured play can lose the connection between toy and concept, and drama can take precedence over conceptual understanding unless the teacher draws out meaning

### Target Learners
- Students from Year 3 to Year 9

### Learning Goals
- Making advanced mathematical concepts less abstract and emotionally positive to learn
