# Three-phase short-term research program structure: skill-building workshops, team research projects, final symposium

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The SIREN proof-of-concept structure moved students through three phases in three weeks: Weeks 1–2 delivered hands-on technical and essential-skills workshops, Weeks 2–3 shifted to team-based investigations using cosmic detectors, and Week 3 ended with a final symposium. As the article summarizes, "Students progressed from skill-building workshops to collaborative research projects under the guidance of advisors and peer mentors, culminating in team presentations at a final symposium."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Six interdisciplinary teams of four students, each guided by one advisor and one peer mentor
#### Constraints
- Some participants recommended mid-program check-ins or additional time for project development within the fast-paced timeline

### Target Learners
- community college undergraduate students in STEM disciplines

### Learning Goals
- technical and research skills
- collaboration and peer learning
- communication and scientific dissemination
