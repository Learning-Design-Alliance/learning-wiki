# Research-validated grading worksheets with contrasting elaborated and brief student solutions for intro physics and QM problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 5 studies (3 qualitative, 1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
A pair of research-validated grading worksheets and contrasting student solutions used in the TA professional development course: "The grading worksheets were previously developed and validated by three of the authors in collaboration with two graduate student researchers in physics education for use with TAs/instructors". The worksheets ask TAs to grade the solutions out of 10 points in a quiz context, list solution features, and justify the weight assigned to each feature; all solutions have the correct final answer, but only the elaborated ones explicate the problem solving process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student solutions created based upon common student responses to the problems and iterated between researchers and other physics instructors
#### Constraints
- The contrasting solutions were designed for the specific intro physics and QM problems used in the study

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate teaching assistants

### Learning Goals
- grading criteria for student problem solutions
- reflection on problem solving practices that educational literature suggests promote desired problem-solving practices

### Claims

- [Tas Grade Elaborated Higher Qm Significant Intro Not](../claims/tas-grade-elaborated-higher-qm-significant-intro-not.md) [+M]
- [Two-thirds of the TAs stated they would grade QM and intro physics solutions using different criteria](../claims/two-thirds-tas-stated-different-criteria-intro-qm.md) [+W]
- [Physics TAs more often graded the minimally reasoned solution SSE higher than the elaborated solution SSD, and average grades did not change significantly from semester start to end in one group measured before and after one semester of teaching experience and TA professional development](../claims/ta-grading-practices-unchanged-after-pd.md) [+W]
- [TAs' grading approaches indicate a subtractive scheme, weighing domain knowledge and correctness in the detailed solution but rarely docking the brief solution for missing justifications](../claims/tas-subtractive-grading-scheme.md) [+W]
- [The preference for the minimal-reasoning solution over the detailed-reasoning solution softened but persisted in the homework context](../claims/tas-homework-grading-gap-softened.md) [+W]
- [Physics TAs using a CA-inspired rubric scored an elaborated solution similarly to their pre-rubric score and did not match expert rubric scores, particularly for a brief solution](../claims/tas-rubric-scores-differ-from-expert-scores.md) [+W]
- [In a quiz context, TAs graded a minimal-reasoning solution (SSE) significantly higher than a detailed-reasoning solution with canceling mistakes (SSD)](../claims/tas-quiz-grading-favors-minimal-reasoning-solution.md) [+W]
- [TAs who judged the intro physics problems easy often inferred correct understanding in the brief intro solution even when the solution showed no evidence of it](../claims/tas-inferred-understanding-brief-intro-solution-without-evidence.md) [+W]
