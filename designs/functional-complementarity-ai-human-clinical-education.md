# Functional complementarity model of AI and human roles in clinical skills education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that AI and human educators need not perform identical instructional work: "AI agents may be especially useful for repetitive practice, consistent role-play, checklist monitoring, Socratic prompting, and the generation of preliminary formative feedback," while faculty and human standardized patients contribute contextual interpretation, nuanced emotional response, individualized remediation, and judgments about learner readiness. On this account, partial substitution is acceptable only for specific, bounded tasks, with the boundary set by error consequences, task uncertainty, relational sensitivity, and availability of human review.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- meaningful human review of consequential judgments
- AI systems that scaffold rather than substitute student reasoning
#### Constraints
- the system should not autonomously determine whether a learner is clinically competent, make progression or credentialing decisions, or replace human-led remediation in emotionally or professionally complex cases

### Target Learners
- medical students in early clinical training

### Learning Goals
- simulated clinical interviewing
- independent clinical reasoning
- empathic communication
