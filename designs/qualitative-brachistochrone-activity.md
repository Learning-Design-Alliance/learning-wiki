# Qualitative brachistochrone activity with demonstration model and graduate facilitation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A downloadable question set on the classic brachistochrone problem, introduced with a handmade model demonstrating the faster cycloid path and the tautochrone property. The article states the activity "requires a qualitative analysis of the famous brachistochrone problem and helps students review approximately half of a typical introductory mechanics course." Four groups worked it, with seven graduate students facilitating the introductory sections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A handmade brachistochrone model with straight, descending and ascending paths for the opening demonstration
- "Fifty minutes is needed to work through this"
#### Constraints
- A quantitative solution isn't feasible in introductory classes, so the activity targets qualitative understanding
- The online activity has a 5th question not discussed here since our students didn't get to it given time constraints

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based mechanics students (science/engineering and physics/astronomy/honors)
- fifth-semester physics/astronomy majors
- graduate students facilitating sections

### Learning Goals
- qualitative understanding of the brachistochrone and tautochrone
- kinematics graphing and Newton's Laws
- energy conservation and motion along curved trajectories
