# Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics (MDA) framework adapted to educational gamification

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article uses the Mechanics-Dynamics-Aesthetics framework (Hunicke et al., 2004) as its organising gamification model, adapted to the education field. It defines mechanics as "the system's set of constituent elements, the relation linking between them, and the way in which a system can routinely function", dynamics as how the player-student interacts with the mechanics, and aesthetics as the perceptions and sensations-emotions experienced by students while playing. The authors chose MDA over the Points-Badges-Leaderboards system because PBL "only fits one player type: 'achievers'", whereas MDA was used "to achieve an inclusive gamified atmosphere for all student-player types".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Design must attend to dynamic and aesthetic spheres in class, and not only to gamification mechanics such as the PBL system, per the authors' discussion.
#### Constraints
- The authors argue the PBL gamification system only fits one player type (achievers), which motivated the use of MDA instead.

### Target Learners
- Primary school students (Year 6 and Year 7) in Physical Education classes

### Learning Goals
- Motivation, dispositional flow, basic psychological needs, rhythmic motor skill, and commitment to learning in PE
