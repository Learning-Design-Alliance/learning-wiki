# Community cultural wealth, freedom dreaming, and desire-based frameworks positioning youths’ and parents’ reimagined realities as precursors for social change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The article draws on community cultural wealth ([Yosso, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/1361332052000341006)), freedom dreaming (Kelley, 2002), desire-based research (Tuck, 2009), and the integrative model of minority child development. These frameworks challenge deficit views and position hopes and dreams as “a precursor for social change.” The study focuses on aspirational capital, which “involves youths’ and families’ ability to maintain hopes and dreams in the face of structural inequality.” Participants’ magic-wand re-imaginations elicit this aspirational capital to identify school-level intervention targets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research must center youths’ and parents’ voices, positioning them as experts in naming their reality and desired changes.
#### Constraints
- The frameworks were applied to Latinx youth and parents in six high schools with high Latinx and economically disadvantaged enrollment in one Central Texas district.

### Target Learners
- Latinx high school students and their parents

### Learning Goals
- School belonging and engagement
- Socio-emotional well-being
- Healthy youth adjustment despite cultural stressors
