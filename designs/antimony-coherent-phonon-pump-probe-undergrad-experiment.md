# Undergraduate pump-probe experiment measuring coherent a1g phonons in antimony via transient reflectivity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The experiment is an undergraduate physical chemistry lab in which students measure the transient reflectivity of a single-crystal antimony sample with an 800 nm femtosecond Ti:Sapphire laser. Per the article, students "measure the pump-probe reflectivity of a single-crystal antimony sample to determine the period of coherent phonon oscillations initiated by an ultrafast pulse excitation, which is analyzed by fitting to a sine wave." The setup fits on a 2 foot by 4 foot optical table and uses LabVIEW control, a delay stage, and lock-in detection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mode-locked femtosecond oscillator (the Coherent Vitara-S) is the most costly item required; pump pulses above approximately 1 nJ at the sample and pulses under approximately 100 fs are required for adequate transient signals.
- Students first complete a one-hour prelab lecture and prelab questions before attempting the lab.
#### Constraints
- The procedure is designed so students never align the class 4 laser beam or view it with the safety enclosure opened; student alignment would require close supervision due to potential retinal damage and skin burns.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physical chemistry laboratory students

### Learning Goals
- concepts in solid-state chemistry including phonons, coherence and quantum superposition
- concepts of ultrafast spectroscopy including pump-probe technique, autocorrelation and lock-in amplification
