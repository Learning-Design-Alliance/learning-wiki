# The LDHF/m-mentoring training approach: onsite, simulation-based, competency-focused low-dose high-frequency learning plus mobile mentoring

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The LDHF/m-mentoring approach is a training model in which, per the article, "This approach primarily involves simulation education, onsite, more interactive, and team-oriented training". Its stated principles include competency-focused learning that concentrates on what providers "need to know", simulation- and case-based learning, appropriately spaced brief learning periods, team-focused facility-based training, ongoing practice and quality improvement, and peer coaching. Mobile mentoring reinforces gains through weekly SMS reminder messages and quiz questions and structured monthly mentoring calls from a master mentor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation aids such as anatomical models (MamaNatalie/NeoNatalie) for repeated deliberate practice
- Peer Practice Coordinators to lead practice sessions and log practice
- Mobile phone access so mentors can deliver reminder messages, quizzes, and monthly mentoring calls
#### Constraints
- Barriers reported qualitatively included different work schedules preventing group practice sessions and unavailability of equipment hindering translation into practice
- The article notes operational challenges in ensuring mentors always had airtime for mobile mentoring

### Target Learners
- Doctors, nurses and community health extension workers providing maternal and newborn day of birth care in low-resource facilities

### Learning Goals
- BEmONC clinical skills competency including newborn resuscitation, active management of third stage of labour, PE/E and PPH management, and retention of those skills over 12 months
