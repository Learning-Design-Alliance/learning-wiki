# Trauma-informed procedural skills curriculum: 20-minute didactic, 30-minute case-based discussion, and 15-minute splinting simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A multi-modal educational intervention for senior medical students consisting of "a 20-minute interactive didactic on trauma-informed care principles", followed by "a 30-minute case-based discussion" on TIC modifications to common ED procedures such as laceration repair and incision and drainage, and "a 15-minute simulation scenario" performing splinting on a standardized patient who discloses childhood trauma. It was evaluated in a multi-site randomized controlled pilot and is released as appendices including a facilitator guide, surveys, and checklist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training and expertise, plus trained volunteers or standardized patients, are required to deliver the intervention
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only at two tertiary academic medical centers with dedicated medical student educators, which the authors say may limit generalizability

### Target Learners
- Senior medical students on an emergency medicine clerkship

### Learning Goals
- Applying trauma-informed care principles during emergency department procedures
- Comfort and skill in minimizing patient distress and re-traumatization during procedures
