# One-week symmetry module for the introductory physics curriculum with supporting website

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The module is a proposed curriculum insertion of a week's length that introduces symmetry as invariance under transformations, the symmetries of space and time, Noether's theorem, and the resulting conservation laws, extending to gauge symmetry and relativity. It aims to show beginning students that physics is as lively and contemporary as molecular biology and as beautiful as the arts. Expanded materials are maintained at the website www.emmynoether.com, which the authors continually update as their educational experiment proceeds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The authors note that as they proceed to the deepest foundations of matter, energy, space and time, they must be more descriptive for beginning students.
#### Constraints
- The article calls itself a very incomplete survey of the role of symmetry in physics; much material cannot be presented in the brief article and is deferred to the website.

### Target Learners
- high school students
- early college students in calculus-based introductory physics

### Learning Goals
- understanding symmetry and invariance
- connecting conservation laws to symmetry principles
- seeing physics as contemporary and beautiful
