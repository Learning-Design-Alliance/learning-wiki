# Digital-camera parallax classroom activity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An activity in which a digital camera is placed at two locations A and B, representing two positions of Earth half a year apart, and two photos of a model star are used to measure the star's pixel shift p; combined with a calibration factor and the shift distance b, the distance d follows from the imaging relation d = (λf)·b/p. The article notes this is "Closer to real astronomical parallax measurements both in concept and in practice" than the theodolite activity, and that it "requires no more than the ability to read oﬀ image pixels" in a program such as Gimp.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital camera, a calibration procedure using an object of known length at a known distance to determine (λf), a distant reference object for aligning the two images, and image-processing software to read pixel coordinates
#### Constraints
- The pinhole-camera approximation should be tested for the specific camera–lens combination before the activity; the target should be placed in the approximate viewing direction of the cameras, not off to the side

### Target Learners
- students grade 10 and older

### Learning Goals
- understanding how astronomical parallax measurements work, including image alignment against distant reference objects and calibration

- Pinhole Model Calibration Accuracy [+M]
