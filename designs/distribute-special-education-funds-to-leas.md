# Distribute state special education funds to LEAs rather than exclusively to SELPAs to promote coordination and inclusive practices

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report recommends transitioning over time from distributing state special education funds exclusively to SELPAs to distributing them to LEAs, which could then contract with regional entities for regionalized services. Because most students with disabilities spend most of the day in general education, the authors argue "Such coordination could be optimized by distribution of AB 602 funds directly to LEAs," aligning funding with LCFF accountability for all students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- LEA authority to make decisions about how best to use funds for coordination purposes
- Maintenance of effort and excess cost fiscal requirements under IDEA must continue to be met
#### Constraints
- The need for some regionalized services persists, especially for students in high-cost categories and small LEAs
- The funding system alone cannot bring forth necessary changes, though it can communicate state intent

### Target Learners
- Students with disabilities in California K-12 schools, including those in general education classrooms 80 percent or more of the day

### Learning Goals
- Access to and progress in the general education curriculum; coordinated services across general and special education
