# Explicit, structured small-group intervention combining vocabulary, story reading, main idea identification, and text structure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article pilots a theory-based intervention in which, over 10 weeks, a research assistant offered three 30-minute sessions per week to small groups of three to four students, totalling 15 hours. Instruction was "structured (it followed a detailed protocol) and explicit (a clear and coherent terminology was used)", with students' participation continuously required. Most sessions combined tier-2 vocabulary teaching, story reading, main idea identification, and text structure review, with three separate sessions on anaphoric relations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A detailed protocol and clear, coherent terminology; the same research assistant delivered sessions to reduce adaptation difficulties for students with ASD
#### Constraints
- Conclusions on efficacy cannot be drawn because of the small sample size; the anaphoric-relations component showed no effect, possibly due to only three sessions

### Target Learners
- students with high-functioning autism spectrum disorder, age approximately 9 years, in regular or special classrooms

### Learning Goals
- reading comprehension
- vocabulary
- main idea identification
- anaphoric relations
- text structure
