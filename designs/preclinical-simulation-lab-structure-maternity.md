# Three-week preclinical simulation lab structured as briefing, recorded practice, and 20-minute debriefing every 40 minutes of practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's simulation lab ran over three weeks (one day per week per section of 8-10 students, 8 am to 4 pm) before clinical placement, week by week covering antenatal, labor and delivery, and postpartum skills. Each session begins with a patient report and a work-allocation meeting, and "The student's practice was recorded and used for debriefing and evaluation. Debriefing was done every 40 minutes of practice for 20 minutes." This mirrors the briefing-participation-debriefing structure the article attributes to Cant & Cooper (2009) and the Jeffries framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors received about 2 weeks of specialist simulation training, mandatory for all course instructors, before conducting sessions
#### Constraints
- Sessions were limited to the first 3 weeks of a 16-week semester prior to clinical placement

### Target Learners
- undergraduate female nursing students in a maternity course

### Learning Goals
- basic maternity skills: history taking, physical examination, antepartum, labor and delivery, and postpartum care
