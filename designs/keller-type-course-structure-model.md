# Keller-type course model: content divided into Nu ordered units with Na assessment opportunities and Nc units required for approval

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines a Keller-type course as one whose content is divided into Nu units 'organized in a definite numerical order (1, ..., Nu)', with students required to 'show mastery in each unit before going to the next one', a total of Na assessment opportunities, and a minimal number Nc of units to be completed for approval. In typical Keller courses Na is much greater than Nu, whereas in traditional courses Na is approximately equal to Nu. This structural definition underlies the mathematical model of the paper.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must demonstrate mastery of each unit before advancing to the next unit
#### Constraints
- The model assumes no dropping out of the course and that in each opportunity a student tries to pass in just one unit of content

### Target Learners
- higher education students in Keller-type or mastery-learning courses

### Learning Goals
- mastery of sequentially ordered units of course content
