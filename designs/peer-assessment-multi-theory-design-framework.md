# Multi-theory framework underpinning the peer assessment program design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The PA program's design was built on a mix of theoretical constructs related to learning and professional behavior change. Table 2 maps six theories to operationalizations: social constructivist learning theory (authentic cases, simulated role-play, pre-defined roles and a performance-based format), self-regulated learning theory (self-assessment and improvement planning), situated learning theory (delivery within communities of practice), social cognitive learning theory (mastery and vicarious experiences by performing and observing), stages of change theory (peers tailoring feedback), and theory of planned behaviour (introducing peers to the assessor perspective to build a mutually accepted quality standard).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The program requires structured tasks with pre-defined participant roles, a performance-based role-play format, and a trained coach to facilitate feedback and group safety.
#### Constraints
- The framework was operationalized for guideline implementation in professional physical therapy practice; the article notes effective peer assessment practices are context-specific and culture dependent.

### Target Learners
- Dutch primary care physical therapists (postgraduate professionals)

### Learning Goals
- Guideline-consistent clinical performance including knowledge, communication, and hands-on clinical skills for low back pain management
