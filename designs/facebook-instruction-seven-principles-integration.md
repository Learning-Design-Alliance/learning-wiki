# Integrate Facebook into instruction following the seven Chickering and Gamson principles to foster engagement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article maps its Facebook instructional practices onto the seven principles for improving undergraduate education (Chickering & Gamson, 1987), arguing that "this study provides support for the 7 principles of good practices of improving engagement in undergraduate education (Chickering and Gamson, 1987) along with the Facebook platform". Facebook features extend communication, support collaborative projects, enable prompt feedback, and maximize time on task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should demonstrate a positive perspective toward Facebook and provide clear instructions and guidelines of Facebook participation, as the article states
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that positive results may not be solely attributable to the technology, and that instructors' skills and personal characteristics may be the major cause for increasing student grades and learning engagement
