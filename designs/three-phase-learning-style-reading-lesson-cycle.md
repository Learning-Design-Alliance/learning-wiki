# Three-phase EFL reading lesson cycle with warm-up and evaluation, delivered through learning-style varied activities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's instructional pattern organizes each weekly reading lesson into a short warm-up followed by pre-reading, while-reading, and post-reading phases, ending with an evaluation on a similar text. Pre-reading activities activate background knowledge; while-reading activities guide strategic reading; post-reading activities extend content through other skills. The article states "reading lessons were conducted through activities divided into three phases after a short warm-up period". Both experimental and control groups followed this structure; only the activity design differed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Texts selected at appropriate difficulty, with suitable syntactic structures, appropriate length, age and interest appropriateness, and a reasonable amount of new information
- Teaching delivered across repeated weekly sessions (three one-hour sessions weekly in the study)
#### Constraints
- The study ran over a limited 8-week, 24-class-hour period, which the author lists as a limitation on generalization

### Target Learners
- freshmen university students at low English proficiency (A1/A2 self-reported)

### Learning Goals
- English reading comprehension
- English self-efficacy across the four language skills
