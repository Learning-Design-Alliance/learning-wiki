# Four-phase group-based simulation assessment with teams of four demonstrating non-technical skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A summative, 100% weighted module assessment in which first-year student paramedic teams managed "a clinical emergency with two patients" designed for their competence level. Students worked in "teams of four, demonstrating NTS (teamwork, decision-making, leadership, communication and reflection) and professional standards", with individual contributions assessed. The design ran through four phases: pre-briefing and role allocation, group management of the simulation, group reflection-on-action, and debriefing with the TALK tool.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Service users were employed to act in the role of patients during the simulation assessments, ensuring realistic interactions
- Students used university tablets and a Microsoft Form for the reflection-on-action phase, submitting it within the 60-min period
#### Constraints
- Some students could hide in group simulation; this was mitigated by encouraging stronger team members to involve quieter members, with such contribution recognised within the marking

### Target Learners
- first-year (level 4) student paramedics in UK higher education

### Learning Goals
- non-technical skills including teamwork, decision-making, leadership, communication and reflection
- safe and effective care delivery in simulated prehospital environments
