# New Medical Science biochemistry course reform pattern: online–offline blended learning plus flipped classroom with student 'little teachers' plus humanistic-ethical case integration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A course-level instructional pattern for biochemistry and molecular biology tailored to China's New Medical Science initiative, combining three strategies the authors describe as serving "knowledge application + competency development + value shaping". Pre-class online modules let students master foundations independently; in-class sessions apply knowledge to clinical scenarios; students act as "little teachers" presenting key concepts while teachers coach and target gaps; small-group case analyses link scientific content to ethical reasoning (e.g., COVID-19 resource allocation dilemmas, Nobel laureate research journeys).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-class online modules (e.g., MOOCs) students can complete independently before in-class application sessions
- Instructors willing to shift from lecture delivery to coaching, observing presentations to identify knowledge gaps
- Case materials linking biochemical content to ethical and clinical scenarios
#### Constraints
- The authors note learning effectiveness depends greatly on students' active learning attitude and self-study abilities; less motivated or less confident students need more teacher assistance

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students (stomatology and clinical medicine)

### Learning Goals
- biochemistry and molecular biology knowledge and application
- critical thinking
- ethical reasoning
- communication and interdisciplinary clinical translation
