# Sequenced AR tiling activities progressing from basic rectangles to composite shapes with increasing unit-size variety

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention used four weekly AR activities, each about 60 minutes, sequenced from measuring a rectangle with two unit sizes, to L-shapes with two and then three unit sizes, to a large rectangle with four unit sizes. The design rests on the premise that "Understanding the same area can be tiled with different shapes and measured using various unit squares is essential for developing area measurement competency", so the authors "intentionally instructed students to use different unit sizes when measuring the area of shapes". Later activities pushed students toward efficient strategies such as covering more area with fewer, larger units.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an instructor present as a social scaffolder to explain each activity and guide reasoning
#### Constraints
- activities were implemented over four weekly sessions in a summer program setting

### Target Learners
- elementary students with weak area measurement reasoning

### Learning Goals
- decomposition and recomposition of composite shapes
- multiplicative reasoning with rows and columns
