# Pauli-matrix spin formalism as introductory mathematical apparatus for school quantum theory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This element is the 2×2 Pauli-matrix apparatus (σx, σy, σz acting on two-dimensional Hilbert space) presented as the mathematical core of a school-level quantum theory course. The article supplies a table mapping each mathematical term — vector as physical state, operator as physical quantity, eigenvalues as possible measurement results (+1, −1), eigenstates as states with a fixed value, vector addition as superposition, and squared development coefficients as probabilities — to its physical interpretation. It works because "the mathematics of spin is quite simple" while still conveying the essential structures of quantum theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students capable of computing eigenvalues and eigenstates from the matrices
- A supporting interpretation table connecting mathematical terms to physical meanings
#### Constraints
- Position and momentum operators and their eigenstates are described as mathematically far more difficult to handle than the 2×2 spin matrices

### Target Learners
- high-school pupils with modest mathematical background

### Learning Goals
- understanding the essential mathematical structures of quantum theory: state, operator, eigenvalue, eigenstate, superposition and probability
