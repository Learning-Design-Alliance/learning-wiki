# Computer-based cognitive representation approach: dual externalization of problem solving and knowledge construction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The approach externalizes complex cognition in learning clinical reasoning by combining two representations: "argument mapping to externalize the problem-solving process and concept mapping to reveal the knowledge constructed from the problems". Learners articulate critical information as data nodes, generate hypotheses, and reason with justification links in an argument map, while representing conceptual knowledge as concepts and relationships (causal, hierarchical, cross-link) in a concept map, with cross-links between the two maps. It is informed by cognitive apprenticeship theory, which emphasizes that complex tasks should be situated in authentic contexts and thinking processes made visible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners interact with a simulated problem context to obtain case information before representing it
- Scaffolding and coaching support (a learning flowchart and case-specific personalized guidance) to facilitate the learning process
#### Constraints
- The article notes concern about a limited number of cases in such applications due to programming costs

### Target Learners
- Year 3 to Year 5 medical students learning clinical reasoning

### Learning Goals
- Clinical problem solving and diagnostic reasoning
- Knowledge construction and systemic understanding of the problem domain
