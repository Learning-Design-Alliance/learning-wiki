# Four e-assessment types designed and implemented in the Non satis scire project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Non satis scire project, a Dutch national Research & Development project funded by the SURF foundation, involved teacher educators and master students from all 13 Dutch research universities in collaboratively designing, implementing and evaluating formative and summative e-assessments. As the article states, "Four e-assessment types have been addressed: 1) knowledge tests on learning and instruction, 2) providing feedback on students’ plans for research on teaching practice, 3) providing feedback on students’ web-based video clips of teaching practice and 4) digital self-assessments of student-teachers’ reflection." The types were carried out in 27 interventions across the 13 programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation within post-graduate teacher education programs combining 50% institutional courses and 50% school teaching
#### Constraints
- Some interventions were not fully developed at evaluation time, and in some programs the infrastructure did not fully support the interventions (absence of a web-video server or no large computer rooms)

### Target Learners
- post-graduate teacher education master students in the Netherlands

### Learning Goals
- assessment of student-teachers' competence as new teachers against the Dutch Teacher Standard competencies
