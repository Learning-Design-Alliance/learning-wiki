# The Problem Project: a four-part student-created-and-solved physics problem assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Problem Project asked students in introductory Physics 5B (fluids, waves, optics) to "create a problem that would be appropriate as a homework or exam problem in the class". Submissions had four sections: stating the problem, solving it, explaining why it was interesting, and explaining its pedagogical value. It was posted in the second-to-last week of a ten-week term, completed by 245 students, worth 10% of course credit, and graded on sophistication, solution accuracy, independence, and the pedagogical-value description at roughly five minutes per submission.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment rubric judging sophistication, solution accuracy, independence from previously-assigned problems, and the student's description of pedagogical value
#### Constraints
- Grading was not blind (the grader could see student names)
- Only 10% of course credit was allocated, so persistence at higher stakes is untested

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students (largely engineering and physics majors)

### Learning Goals
- demonstrating conceptual understanding of fluids, waves, and optics; engaging in expert-like problem construction and metacognitive reflection
