# Research-process-tracing workshop pattern: set a question, analyze quantitatively, build a hypothesis, verify with a simple model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A three-section workshop design in which beginners trace the fundamental research process rather than merely observing a phenomenon. Students analyze Brownian-particle movies in Tracker, run a dice-game random-walk experiment to test the collision hypothesis, then connect the result to protein dynamics in cells. The authors state students learned "by tracing the fundamental research process: setting a problem, doing quantitative analysis, building a hypothesis, and verifying the hypothesis using a simple model." They judge this preferable to the typical situation where students only observe random motion and are told it reflects thermal fluctuation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Motivated participants recruited voluntarily; teaching assistants supporting subgroups via online chat; parents assisting with software installation and data upload
#### Constraints
- Requires a personal computer for every participant; weak internet connections caused schedule delays in lectures and practice

### Target Learners
- motivated elementary school students in grades 4-6

### Learning Goals
- understanding the mechanism of Brownian motion
- experiencing the scientific research process
- connecting microscopic motion to protein dynamics in cells
