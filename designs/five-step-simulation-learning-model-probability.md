# Five-step simulation-based learning model for teaching probability (problem, prediction, model, test, evaluation)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article builds its learning environment on Cobb's 3R logic (randomize-repeat-reject), extended by combining it with a simulation-based learning approach into "a 5 -step simulation -based learning model was created". The stages are: Problem (introducing real-life problems), Prediction (taking estimations for the solution), Model (creating a simulation consisting of reasonable random processes), Test (producing data from the created model), and Evaluation (querying the distribution of the data and reaching a conclusion to generalization). The model structured the four-week TinkerPlots activities in the experimental group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities require dynamic statistical software such as TinkerPlots so learners can build simulation models and change the number of trials
#### Constraints
- Activities in the paper are about a finite sample space and discrete probability calculations; the study does not include all activities relating to probability theory

### Target Learners
- prospective mathematics teachers in statistics/probability courses

### Learning Goals
- inference and prediction skills in experimental probability
- understanding of randomness and variability
