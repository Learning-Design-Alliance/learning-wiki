# Semi-free hands-on experiments, small-group cooperative work, and self-chosen inquiry projects as the instructional core of a frontier-physics outreach school

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The program's instructional pattern combines three student-focused techniques: experiments "designed to increase the inclusion of the students to the inquiry process by introducing semi-free hands-on activities instead of fully-guided cookbook-type experiments"; cooperative learning in small groups of five; and inquiry-based teaching via self-chosen projects. Lectures were enriched with visualization and daily-life examples, and thought-provoking questions were prioritized to foster independent thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experimental setups designed to keep required technical and theoretical information at a minimum level for high school students
- An experienced instructor team including active researchers
- Basic-level lectures to accommodate heterogeneous scientific backgrounds
#### Constraints
- Six copies of each setup were prepared due to budget constraints, with students split into groups of five
- Projects were bounded by basic guidelines for safety and originality

### Target Learners
- 11th and 12th grade high school students with varied scientific backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- Fundamentals of experimental high energy physics
- Inquiry process and problem-solving skills
- Communication and critical thinking skills
