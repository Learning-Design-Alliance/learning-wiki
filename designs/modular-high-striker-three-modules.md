# Modular fair-game prototype: three separately designed modules plus a track for teaching electromagnetic induction and energy conservation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The project reproduces the traditional 'High Striker' fair game as a design-and-build activity whose "main goal is to design and build an electromagnetic propulsion system actuated by a mechanical interaction that allows a sphere to move along a track". Implementation is required to be modular so each component can be designed, built, and implemented separately for better follow-up and feedback, yielding three modules plus the track. Modules map onto specific physics content: electromagnetic induction for the shooting module and energy conservation for the track.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The implementation must be modular so each component can be designed, built, and implemented separately for follow-up and feedback
#### Constraints
- The sphere cannot be metallic because it would be attracted by the induced electromagnet in the shooting module

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in an introductory electromagnetism physics course

### Learning Goals
- electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law, Lenz's law, induced electromotive force)
- energy conservation and collisions
