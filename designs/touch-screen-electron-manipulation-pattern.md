# Touch-screen manipulation of electrons and bonds as an interactive multimedia design for abstract reaction mechanisms

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The pattern transfers control of learning to students through touch-screen interaction with multimedia content: students slide their fingers on the screen to move electrons or atoms, form and break bonds, predict mechanistic steps, and check their answers. The article states that "By transferring the control of learning to students with its touch-screen feature, students are able to form and break apart a chemical bond by sliding their fingers on the screen", enabling analysis of electron movement and bond formation and breaking. Activities were designed around the revised Bloom's taxonomy levels from Remember to Create.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive multimedia content with digital imagery, sound, and animation coordinated with student interactivity
- Practice activities that apply the principles of nucleophile, electrophile, and free radicals
#### Constraints
- Applied to fundamental organic reaction mechanism concepts; the article reports field trials with other difficult concepts as future work

### Target Learners
- Net-generation university students accustomed to mobile devices

### Learning Goals
- Visualizing and predicting the mechanistic steps of organic reaction mechanisms and developing conceptual thinking
