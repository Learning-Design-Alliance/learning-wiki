# Hybrid Quantum-Classical Systems course pattern: lectures paired with hands-on exercises on real or simulated quantum hardware

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a master's-level course as a combination of frontal lectures and practical exercises so that computer science students learn quantum computing from an information systems and architecture perspective rather than as an isolated standalone machine. Students "learn how to decompose applications and implement computational tasks on a hybrid quantum-classical computational continuum", applying techniques they already know from computer science. Exercises run on real or simulated quantum hardware reached through a cloud frontend, and evaluation is assignment-based, culminating in a group project on hybrid application design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need bachelor's-level mathematics and computer science knowledge; no prior knowledge of (quantum) physics is assumed
- Access to quantum programming frameworks and to real or simulated quantum backends via cloud providers
#### Constraints
- The measurement-based programming model is not part of the exam material
- Assignments alone made it difficult to cover the topics of all lectures, especially guest lectures

### Target Learners
- master's students in computer science and related subjects

### Learning Goals
- explaining how quantum computation differs from classical models
- decomposing hybrid applications across a quantum-classical continuum
- developing and implementing hybrid quantum algorithms
