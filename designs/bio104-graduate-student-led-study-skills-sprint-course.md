# BIO 104: an 8-week, pass/fail, graduate student–led metacognition-based study skills sprint course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
BIO 104 is a low-cost supplemental course designed and taught by graduate TAs alongside introductory biology, requiring no faculty labor. It adapts the study cycle ([Cook et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3109/0142159x.2012.714886)), teaching students to "study early and often, in short but intensive bouts," to make an outline after every lecture, and to construct concept maps from memory for self-assessment. TAs collected and graded one outline and one concept map weekly on 10-point content and organization scales with feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires graduate TAs or advanced undergraduate facilitators willing to teach the course and grade weekly assignments
- Requires students to voluntarily enroll after the first lecture exam
#### Constraints
- Enrollment is voluntary and self-selected, so results represent a self-selected group rather than a randomly selected cohort
- Instructor-led, pre-exam study sessions were not part of the course

### Target Learners
- First-year introductory biology students scoring ≤ 77% on the first lecture exam

### Learning Goals
- Effective study habits and metacognitive self-assessment
- Deep learning of introductory biology content
