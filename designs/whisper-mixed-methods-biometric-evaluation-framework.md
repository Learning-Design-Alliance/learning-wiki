# Mixed-methods evaluation framework combining biometric, behavioral, and qualitative measures for multisensory learning tools

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an evaluation design for measuring the impact of personalized audiovisual environments on cognitive focus, emotional state, and experiential satisfaction. It combines eye-tracking (dwell time, fixations), facial expression recognition, posture coding, pre/post surveys including the Big Five Inventory, retention quizzes, and semi-structured interviews, with triangulation across methods. The authors state that "The evaluation follows a mixed-methods approach combining biometric data, behavioral observation, and qualitative feedback to form a multi-dimensional understanding of system impact."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A controlled research room with a front-facing camera for FER, an eye-tracking sensor, and audio recording equipment
- A researcher present to oversee sessions without interfering
#### Constraints
- The framework is currently theoretical: pilot results and real user data are not yet available, and future work will involve user testing with diverse populations including neurodivergent learners

### Target Learners
- self-directed learners participating in controlled evaluation sessions of study environments

### Learning Goals
- measuring cognitive focus, emotional state, and learning outcomes (memory retention) during self-directed study
