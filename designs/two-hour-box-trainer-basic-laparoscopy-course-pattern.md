# Two-hour structured basic-laparoscopy pattern: tasks of increasing difficulty, five repeatable attempts each, learner-to-tutor ratio 2:1, crossover transfer task

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern structures a brief psychomotor-skills course for surgical novices: after a theoretical and technical introduction, learners perform "tasks of increasing difficulty and complexity" on standard box trainers, with each task consisting of five rapidly repeatable attempts to capture the full learning curve. The design deliberately avoids tasks shared with other surgical techniques to minimize prior-experience bias, and ends with a crossover task on the non-familiar viewing system. The authors designed it "considering a learner-to-tutor ratio of 2:1 and data suggesting the plateauing of learning curves at approximately 10 repetitions".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A learner-to-tutor ratio of 2:1 with tutors trained in standardized laparoscopy courses
- Standard box trainers with repeatable tasks of increasing difficulty
- Approximately two hours of training time, given learning-curve plateauing at approximately 10 repetitions
#### Constraints
- Tasks were picked to be feasible for novices and to reflect vision and depth perception, limiting transferability into clinical practice
- The two-hour format cannot train manually overly challenging tasks

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Junior surgical residents (PGY-1/PGY-2) without prior laparoscopic experience

### Learning Goals
- Basic laparoscopic psychomotor skills such as grasping, transfer and positioning under endoscopic vision
