# Nine TILT-ed supplemental decision-making assignments for graduate QM

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A set of nine weekly supplemental assignments developed for a first-semester graduate QM course, requiring few or no calculations and instead targeting specific problem-solving decisions from a 29-decision framework, such as "what are the important features of the problem (#4)" and "whether the result matches expec- tations (#20)". Assignments followed a TILT-ed design listing SLOs and example decisions, were graded with a coarse rubric and detailed feedback, and offered up to a 5% course-grade bonus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content knowledge in the discipline, since discipline-specific decision-making requires it
- Timely detailed feedback and opportunity to incorporate it, per the deliberate-practice framework
#### Constraints
- Assignments were optional supplements with minimal point incentives
- Problems were typically not situated in authentic contexts
- Total time was estimated at 11 hours over the semester, low relative to the course

### Target Learners
- first-year graduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- discipline-specific problem-solving decision-making in quantum mechanics
