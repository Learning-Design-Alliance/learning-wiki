# Razalas' Method of Grouping

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An inverse grouping method used at the seatwork stage of Direct Instruction, reversing Salazar's Method: "the first stage is an individual competition and the next stages, a group member is added based on the student choice until the group has three members". Groups of three defend solutions for 5 bonus points each; the member who presented is eliminated to work individually, groups shrink to two then one, and membership must change each round. Accumulated points are ranked and added to the final examination score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Used at the seatwork stage within Direct Instruction, with the teacher presenting problems one at a time
- Bonus point incentives (5 points per successful defense) added to final examination scores
- Group membership must not be the same in the next groupings
#### Constraints
- Studied only in Integral Calculus at one Bahrain university with two small intact classes (9 and 21 students)
- Female students in one section were excluded due to cultural orientation

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate computer science and engineering students in Integral Calculus

### Learning Goals
- Problem-solving in Integral Calculus (areas under curves, area between curves, volumes of solid revolution)
