# UTB introductory physics course taught predominantly through commercial video games

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An introductory undergraduate physics course offered at the University of Texas at Brownsville from Dec 2010 to Jan 2011, taught predominantly through commercial video games playable on the PS3 console. The authors state: "we put commercial games to the test by usin g them as the main medium for teaching an entire physics course." Games used include Little Big Planet, Shaun White Skateboarding, Guardian of light, and Uncharted 2, each chosen for specific pedagogical affordances such as collaborative play or physics fidelity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games must run on accessible consoles (the course used the Playstation-3) and be selected for physics simulation fidelity and controllability
#### Constraints
- The course was introductory undergraduate level and taught at a single institution (UTB) during Dec 2010 to Jan 2011

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- kinematics and dynamics in Newtonian mechanics
