# Structured competency-based US-PIVC simulation course (asynchronous theory module plus supervised hands-on session with individual equipment)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course comprised "six sessions over three full days" at a clinical skills teaching unit, each capped at 21 participants, with each student having "individual access to the equipment" (Butterfly iQ3 handheld probe, iPad, US phantom, cannulas, gel, wipes). It combined a 60-minute asynchronous online theory module (teaching handbook, step-by-step guide, 16-minute demonstration video, five formative SBA questions) with a 120-minute synchronous session containing a 45-minute theory course, 45 minutes of closely supervised practice and a 30-minute 1–1 objective assessment. Competence was assessed with the validated P‐UGVA Rating Scale at a pass threshold of 29/40.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual access to US equipment for each student
- A teaching fellow leading the US session, facilitated by academic foundation doctors, clinical skills educators and university academics
- The validated P‐UGVA Rating Scale for the 1–1 objective assessment
#### Constraints
- Each session had a maximum capacity of 21 participants
- Delivered at a single institution's clinical skills teaching unit for final-year medical students

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students preparing for foundation training

### Learning Goals
- Performing ultrasound-guided peripheral intravenous cannulation competently and confidently
