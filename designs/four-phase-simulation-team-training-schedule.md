# Four-phase simulation training schedule combining role introduction, demonstration, video exemplars and scenario practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After defining team roles, the authors designed a simulation schedule so that "simulation training occurred in four phases": Phase 1 introduced the new roles and team theory, Phase 2 demonstrated the function of the new team, Phase 3 presented videos of USA trauma teams in action, and Phase 4 had the teams simulate different scenarios. Training was held daily for 4 hours per day for all ED staff involved in trauma care, with Phase 4 progressing from role assignment through coordination, role reassignment in complex cases, to instructor training and debriefing assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All ED staff involved in trauma care must be trained in their assigned roles and in the concepts of team communication
#### Constraints
- The training program was implemented as a quality improvement program, not for the purpose of the present study

### Target Learners
- Emergency department nurses and physicians involved in trauma care

### Learning Goals
- Team communication, role assignment and reassignment, prioritization of life-saving treatments, and timing of decision making in trauma resuscitation
