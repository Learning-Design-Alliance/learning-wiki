# UCLA Migrant Student Leadership Institute as a third space

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The MSLI is presented as an illustrative example of a third space—an approach or zone of development that transforms 'conflict and difference into rich zones of collaboration and learning.' It reorganized educational practices so that college-bound students of color from migrant farmworker backgrounds could see themselves as learners and social change agents, drawing on rigorous texts, mini-lectures, discussions, writing and reading conferences, field trips, theater, and college preparation workshops, and connecting students' community histories to present-day realities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizers must work outside pre-existing conventions of what a learning space and its tools are
#### Constraints
- The third space emerges from the collision of official and unofficial scripts; redirecting students back to the official curriculum thwarts its transformative potential

### Target Learners
- college-bound students of color from migrant farmworker backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- critical inquiry, seeing oneself as a learner and social change agent, and revising constructions of self, community, and schooling
