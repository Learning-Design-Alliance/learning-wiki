## Description
An undergraduate-level course at the Maryland Institute College of Art, started in 2014 in the Animation Department, combining "introductory astrophysics, taught by a scientist, and studio components". NASA-affiliated scientists present research on topics such as black holes, supernovae, dark matter and gravitational waves; students pick a topic and work in small groups with a scientist as mentor, and animations are screened at the NASA Goddard Visitor Center and freely available at astroanimation.org. Animations must be accurate to the science but may be whimsical, abstract, poetic and inspirational rather than directly explanatory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accurate representation of the science in the animations
- Proximity to dedicated scientists, described as crucial for the class success
- A high level of passion and commitment to face challenges of the collaboration
#### Constraints
- Remains difficult to schedule such integrated classes in academic settings
- A small drop in course registration occurred during Covid confinement when physical visits to NASA were not possible

### Target Learners
- undergraduate animation students

### Learning Goals
- introductory astrophysics concepts
- applying animation practice to science communication and collaboration
