# Human Structure: a 6.5-week condensed, integrated medical anatomy course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Human Structure is the University of Oklahoma College of Medicine's condensed anatomy course, reduced from a 16-week, 130-hour course to "a six and one-half week, 105-hour course" within an integrated systems-based pre-clinical curriculum. It combines 29 hours of traditional lectures, 12 hours of clinical correlation lectures, cadaveric dissection with rotating dissection pairs and peer teaching, 42 student case-based presentations, daily ARS quizzes, weekly laboratory practical quizzes, online weekend quizzes, and three summative examinations, plus embryology, histology, and radiology content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinician course director and practicing clinicians presenting cases and radiologic images
- Faculty-produced independent study materials and a commercially available dissector
- Anatomy reviews reinforced in subsequent systems-based courses
#### Constraints
- The first 2010 iteration, which excluded limbs and used required readings introducing new material, was not well received by students

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- foundational gross anatomy, embryology, histology, and radiology competency
- clinical application of anatomical knowledge
- professionalism development
