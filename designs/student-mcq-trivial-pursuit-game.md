# Hybrid Trivial-Pursuit/Millionaire game played on a repository of instructor-reviewed student-generated MCQs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A classroom knowledge game in which teams answer multiple-choice questions drawn from a repository of student-authored, instructor-reviewed MCQs on a Trivial Pursuit board. "The game was played on a Trivial Pursuit board, each color of the spaces in the wheel-like track corresponding to a diﬀerent course chapter." Each MCQ had one correct answer and three distractors, Millionaire-style; teams of two or three competed, with a five-correct-answers-in-a-row limit and a pseudorandom question draw.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must first review student-generated MCQs, discard unsuitable ones and amend potentially suitable ones, then make the repository and answer key available to students before playing
#### Constraints
- With limited human resources the method can only be applied to small groups of students, because it requires comprehensive revision of a large number of questions; the class game was limited to two hours, with a wedge-count tie-breaking rule

### Target Learners
- 2nd-year Telematics Engineering undergraduates in higher education

### Learning Goals
- revising course content across all chapters in preparation for the exam MCQ test
- stimulating social interaction in the classroom and enhancing motivation
