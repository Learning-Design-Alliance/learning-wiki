# PechaKucha as a concise 20×20 visual presentation format whose effects are explained by reduced cognitive load

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes PechaKucha as "a structured presentation format in which 20 slides are displayed for 20 s each, resulting in a concise, time-bound delivery", emphasizing visual storytelling over text-heavy slides. It invokes Cognitive Load Theory as the explanatory account: "The concise, visual format of PechaKucha likely reduced cognitive load, enabling students to process procedural steps in DB&C exercises more efficiently." The fast-paced, structured delivery is proposed to maintain attention and engagement during psychomotor skill instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty review and pilot applications of the presentation; the article states the final version was reviewed by Nursing Fundamentals faculty and piloted for clarity and feasibility.
#### Constraints
- The article notes some prior studies found no significant knowledge improvements and that the rapid pace may hinder grasping highly detailed, comprehensive content, so content suitability must be evaluated before implementation.

### Target Learners
- second-year nursing students
- undergraduate health sciences students

### Learning Goals
- psychomotor skill acquisition such as deep breathing and coughing exercises
- engagement and satisfaction with instruction
