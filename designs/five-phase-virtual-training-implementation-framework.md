# Five-phase implementation framework for virtual surgical training in low-resource settings

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review proposes a structured five-phase implementation framework (Fig. 2) to translate policy into action for institutions adopting virtual surgical training. It begins with a readiness and needs assessment, followed by resource and technology planning, design and integration aligning virtual training with existing curricula and validated assessments such as OSATS and the Global Rating Scale, implementation through pilot programs with routine monitoring and iterative feedback, and finally credentialing and scale-up aligned with national certification bodies. The authors state it offers "step-by-step, practical guidance that practicing surgeons and institutional leaders can use to translate policy into action".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Readiness and needs assessment identifying local training gaps, infrastructure capacity, and faculty engagement
- Cost-effective, context-appropriate tools and platforms
- Validated assessment methods such as OSATS and the Global Rating Scale
#### Constraints
- Aimed at low-resource settings; the review notes implementation is difficult without reliable electricity, stable internet, and suitable physical spaces for simulation labs

### Target Learners
- Practicing surgeons, institutional leaders, and surgical trainees in low- and middle-income countries

### Learning Goals
- Safe transition from simulation-based training to patient care and sustainable surgical workforce development
