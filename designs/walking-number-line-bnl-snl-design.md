# Walking number line design: body-scale number line plus desk-scale number line with figurine, using a four-step orientation-and-movement procedure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The design consists of a body-scale number line (a 20' × 4' paper roll displaying a number line from −5 to +5, with step-spaced marks and addition/subtraction signs at the ends) and a small number line (an 8.5″ × 11″ sheet with a scaled-down line and a puppy figurine). Students follow four steps: "start by standing on the first number in the problem"; face the addition or subtraction direction; "walk forwards if the second number (the addend or subtrahend) is positive and backwards if the second number is negative"; and walk as many steps as the absolute value of that second number. The operation is enacted as a shift in orientation and the second number's polarity as direction of movement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Signs marking addition and subtraction at the two ends of the body-scale line
- A figurine on the desk-scale line for re-enacting the walking solution
#### Constraints
- The material boundaries at −5 and +5 led some students to reject solutions outside that range, per the limitations section

### Target Learners
- Grade 7 students in general education inclusive math classes, pre-identified as struggling with integer operations

### Learning Goals
- fluency with adding and subtracting positive and negative integers
