# Bunsen burner flame-test activity for identifying chemical elements by flame colour

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A laboratory activity in which students heat dry samples of chemical compounds in the hottest zone of the Bunsen burner flame using a platinum wire (previously cleaned in hydrochloric acid until the flame became colourless) and observe the resulting flame colours. The article states: "The experiment performed with students consisted in observing the different colors of the Bunsen burner flame produced by different chemical elements." Figure 9 shows colours for chlorides of Sodium, Calcium, Potassium, Barium, Lithium, Strontium and Copper.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Bunsen burner
- platinum or nickel-chrome wire
- hydrochloric acid for cleaning the wire
- dry samples converted to volatile chlorides
#### Constraints
- Observation by portable spectroscope is mentioned as an enhancement but stated to be beyond the scope of the paper

### Target Learners
- secondary-school students

### Learning Goals
- quick identification of chemical elements by flame colour
- electronic transitions between atomic energy levels explaining emitted light
