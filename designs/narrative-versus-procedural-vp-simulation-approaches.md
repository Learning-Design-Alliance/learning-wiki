# Narrative versus procedural simulation approaches to virtual patient design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article distinguishes two approaches to VP simulation. The procedural or problem-solving approach suits clinical reasoning and diagnosis, where learners "collect data and make diagnoses and treatment decisions based on their anamneses". The narrative approach (situational simulation) is "generally found in a personal storyline that progresses over time around a logic of cause and effect and that involves a decision-making process that yields different outcomes", with the patient's own solution-finding driving the script. The clinical team chose the narrative approach to mesh with a collaborative nurse-patient relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The narrative approach requires that the patient's identification of solutions drives the direction of the script, consistent with a strengths-based, facilitator role for the nurse.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the divergence arose because a generic procedural template reflected an approach appropriate for teaching clinical reasoning and diagnosis, not relational skills.

### Target Learners
- nurses in continuing professional development
- health care professionals

### Learning Goals
- relational and communication skills consistent with motivational interviewing
