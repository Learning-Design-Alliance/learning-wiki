# Computational making project structure combining physical artifact construction with computational modeling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article defines a making project in its computational physics course with a recurring structural pattern: 'A making project in our Computational Physics course includes the following elements:' an open-ended prompt, construction of a physical artifact from provided and found materials, quantitatively characterizing phenomena (e.g. video capture and tracking software), producing a computational model of the constructed artifact, and critique activities and iteration comparing simulated and experimental data. The pattern operationalizes the metamodel by allowing students to move between seeing physical phenomena and modeling them computationally, with the making environment positioned as producing shared public artifacts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate time must be provided to explore materials, produce first iterations or drafts, and revise the artifacts
- Open-ended prompts such as 'make an oscillator' that invite exploration rather than focusing on a particular scenario
#### Constraints
- The described implementation took place over 4-5 class periods in a semester-long course with 18-30 students working in teams of 3-4

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in physics, math and CS

### Learning Goals
- computational modeling
- connecting physical phenomena to models
- scientific practices
