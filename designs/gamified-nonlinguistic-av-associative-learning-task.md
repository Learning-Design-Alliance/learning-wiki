# Gamified non-linguistic audio-visual associative learning task for children

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A gamified task, adapted from an adult paradigm, in which children learn associations between pairs of unfamiliar environmental sounds and novel visual symbols (Bamum-alphabet bears) across 6 blocks of 12 trials (72 total). The article states "The task was presented as a game to engage children, with a storyline involving three bears building a balloon for their journey around the world." Stimuli were "deliberately selected to be non-linguistic, difficult to label verbally" to minimize phonological and familiarity confounds, and pairings were scaffolded across blocks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two familiarization phases (passive auditory and 1-back visual) precede the learning task; children start at the a priori guessing rate of 1/3
- Bear locations change across trials to prevent spatial learning; feedback follows each response
#### Constraints
- The asymmetry between auditory (passive) and visual (active 1-back) familiarization may have led to stronger encoding of visual inputs; individual differences in familiarity or affective associations with the sounds may have introduced variability

### Target Learners
- Italian-speaking children aged 8-12, including children with developmental dyslexia

### Learning Goals
- Audio-visual associative learning through repeated exposure
- Adaptation to novel associations and updating of the mapping state space
