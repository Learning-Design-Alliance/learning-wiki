# An introductory CRE designed around the five PBL design features: centrality, constructive investigation, driving question, authenticity, and autonomy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course "Scientific thinking and medical research" was designed based on "the five unique design features of PBL, namely centrality, constructive investigation, driving question, authenticity, and autonomy." The project of conducting research and producing a final research report was central to the curriculum; students engaged in constructive investigation from defining a driving question to final reports, chose real-world research questions, and led planning, conducting, and presenting while professors facilitated. The first five weeks delivered research-method lectures before group research began.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A driving question chosen by students that serves to organize and drive research activities and ends in a final research report addressing it
- Professor facilitators assigned to each group, oriented to the course concept, facilitation, and evaluation

- The article notes PBL had not been widely implemented in the medical education field at the time of writing
