# Six-family guided LLM-use protocol operationalizing scaffolding, help-seeking, self-regulation, and AI-literacy principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The guided-use protocol is a student-facing training and rule set delivered before Topic 2 with weekly reminders, framing the model as "a fallible tutor rather than an answer source" and emphasizing "explanation-seeking, stepwise help , verification, and ethical use." It defines six observable rule families: R1 process-over-answer, R2 concept tutoring, R3 stepwise-hint seeking, R4 active practice and retrieval, R5 verification and calibrated trust, and R6 ethical/no-help use. The authors describe it as translating established learning-science principles into transcript-codeable LLM-use behaviors rather than a generic prompting template.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit slide-based orientation with examples of productive and unproductive prompts before LLM use begins, plus weekly reminders
#### Constraints
- The same LLM platform and model were used in both LLM-access conditions; guidance differed through training and rules rather than a separate model or hard-coded guided mode

### Target Learners
- second-semester Electrical Engineering and Computer Engineering undergraduates

### Learning Goals
- probabilistic reasoning including conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, random variables, and distributions
