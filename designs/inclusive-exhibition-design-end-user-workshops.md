# Inclusive exhibition design through early end-user involvement and multi-level content

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The exhibition development process targets the broadest possible audience by accounting for visitor differences (age, educational background, nationality, socio-economic status, disabilities) during content development. Key messages are featured in multiple exhibits with ranging levels of complexity — for example Proton Football requires no prior knowledge while Proton Puzzle requires preknowledge about elementary particles. "An important step towards more accessible exhibition content isincluding different end-user groups in the (early) content development stages such as by hosting a series of collaborative ideation and prototyping workshops", which produced tactile elements, subtitles on video exhibits, and infrastructural improvements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- including different end-user groups in the (early) content development stages via collaborative ideation and prototyping workshops
- offering key topics at multiple levels of complexity across paired exhibits
#### Constraints
- The representation of abstract concepts traditionally relies on 2D visualisation, which the authors note leads to a lack of valuable experiences for the blind and visually impaired

### Target Learners
- visitors with disabilities (blind, visual and hearing impairments, mobility difficulties)
- visitors with varying scientific educational backgrounds
- international visitors

### Learning Goals
- accessible engagement with abstract physics concepts
- early engagement with science and technology
