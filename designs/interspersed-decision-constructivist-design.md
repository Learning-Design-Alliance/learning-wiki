# Constructivist pedagogical design: decision points interspersed throughout the inquiry task

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's pedagogical framework organizes the experiment as inquiry-based learning in which "opportunities for decision making are interspersed throughout the task" rather than confined to hypothesis formation (Fig. 2). Because what is learned "depends on the students’ choice, and not solely on the instructor or on the physics", the design is presented as constructivism in action. When students identify that their judgments cause bias, the authors propose conceptual conflict between positivist beliefs and constructivist experience that can change views of scientific reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must make genuine judgments during measurement and analysis, some without uniquely correct answers, so decision–result relationships can be analyzed
#### Constraints
- The measurement portion is primarily expository because of time and safety constraints

### Target Learners
- final year undergraduates, masters students, or first year doctoral students in chemistry, (bio)physics, nanotechnology, materials science, or quantum information

### Learning Goals
- evaluation: judge the quality of measurements
- analysis: identify bias arising from one's own judgments
