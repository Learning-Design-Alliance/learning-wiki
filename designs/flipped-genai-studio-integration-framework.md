# Integrated framework assigning complementary roles to flipped preparation, exemplar calibration, GenAI exploration, and studio refinement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a pedagogical framework that connects flipped preparation, exemplar reference, GenAI-assisted visual exploration, and studio-based refinement in a practice-based design course. Rather than treating flipped learning and GenAI as separate innovations, the design "assigned them complementary roles: preparing, calibrating, exploring, making, and refining". Pre-class videos and readiness checks support preparation, the gallery provides feasible examples, the GenAI module supports early visual exploration, and instructor feedback with hands-on studio practice supports feasibility judgement and material realization. The authors state the framework helps explain why outcome differences appeared most clearly in creativity, design skills, and problem solving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-class activities must be structured and connected to in-class work rather than optional content delivery
- GenAI-supported visual prototyping must be connected to clear criteria, reflective comparison, and subsequent physical making
#### Constraints
- GenAI outputs cannot reliably represent executable knitting instructions or physically feasible textile outcomes
- Empirical support is from one small historical-cohort course implementation

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate design students

### Learning Goals
- knit yarn design combining visual ideation, material experimentation, and technical development
