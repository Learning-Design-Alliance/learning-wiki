# Prefer symbolic reasoning over numerical computation because numbers hide general trends

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The book deliberately minimizes numerical examples, working "almost exclusively with symbols" because "numerical values tend to hide general trends." The author warns readers the text is "Math Without Numbers" so its scarcity of numbers does not take them aback. The principle is that symbolic manipulation exposes structure and generality that plugging in values obscures, and readers are pushed to "reason beyond plugging in numbers whenever they appear."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Readers need comfort with symbolic manipulation, which the book builds through derivations rather than calculator exercises.
#### Constraints
- The author frames this as a deliberate stylistic choice of this text, not a claim about all mathematics instruction.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- generalizing mathematical relationships
- reading equations as statements about structure
