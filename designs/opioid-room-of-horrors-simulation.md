# Opioid Room of Horrors: an error-driven simulation with four errors and four hazards for safe opioid administration training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The opioid Room of Horrors is a 30-minute paired simulation in which healthcare professionals prepare and administer an opioid to a fictitious geriatric patient in a realistic room with a mannequin, mock electronic medical record, medicine cabinet and nurse trolley. The room hides four errors and four hazards covering steps of the opioid medication-use process, which participants must detect or avoid; assessors then debrief with feedback and a refresh of the 5R rule and the double-check process. It was built on real-life medication administration error reports from the hospital's geriatric department.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A scenario, errors and hazards based on recurrent, well-documented local medication administration error reports, approved by an expert group
- A simulated clinical environment including a mannequin with wristband and subcutaneous access, a mock EMR, fictitious opioid drugs and medical devices
- Two educators (a clinical pharmacist and a nurse) to assess and debrief each session
#### Constraints
- Designed for a specific institutional context (geriatric department at Lausanne University Hospital), with transferability to other institutions and medications proposed but not yet evaluated

### Target Learners
- Nurses
- Healthcare assistants
- Physicians in a geriatric department

### Learning Goals
- Applying the five rights (5R) rule during drug administration
- Performing an independent double-check
- Detecting medication administration errors and avoiding hazards in the opioid medication-use process
