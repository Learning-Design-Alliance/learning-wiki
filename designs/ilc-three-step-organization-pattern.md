# Integrated laboratory classes organized through three steps: identification, preparation, and implementation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a reusable lesson-level design for integrated laboratory classes in a PBL curriculum. "The creation of such laboratory classes was achieved through three key steps: Identification, preparation, and implementation." Block committees identify classes that fit the week's theme; working groups of 3–4 academics plus a curriculum developer meet 4–5 times to define outcomes, tasks, and procedures; implementation runs each 2-hour class three times with about 90 students split into two groups of 45 who rotate between case-scenario and laboratory-procedure stations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A working group comprising academics from biochemistry, microbiology, pathology and medical education disciplines, meeting approximately 4–5 weeks before the class
- Case scenarios, tasks, laboratory procedures, and hands-on materials prepared in advance, with facilitators briefed at a final meeting
#### Constraints
- Not all practical classes can be integrated due to the nature of their contents and educational objectives; such classes were left as discipline-based classes

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students in years 1–2 of a hybrid PBL program

### Learning Goals
- integrating knowledge across biochemistry, pathology and microbiology
- applying knowledge to clinical case scenarios
- laboratory procedure skills
