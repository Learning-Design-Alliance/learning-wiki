# Air-resistance tutorial problem with greater-than-terminal-velocity throw and explicit coordinate system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A tutorial question in an air-resistance unit asks students to find an equation for the velocity of a ball "thrown vertically downward at greater than terminal velocity" with drag proportional to v, taking +y downward. The article states it deliberately included "two uncommon elements" — the greater-than-terminal-velocity condition, which many students do not expect, and the explicit coordinate-system definition — in order "to raise a specific issue about signs with students." The problem surfaced incorrect sign placements in the ma term for a majority of the class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires students to use Newton's Second Law to set up a scalar equation before solving the differential equation for velocity.
#### Constraints
- Used in a sophomore-level mechanics course employing the Intermediate Mechanics Tutorials; the article reports it within small-group tutorial activities.

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- Setting up scalar equations from vector statements of Newton's Second Law with correct signs
