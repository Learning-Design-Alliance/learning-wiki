# Six-phase workshop training framework culminating in a finished outreach product

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The programme deployed a repeating six-phase structure in each of its seven two-hour workshops: "Homework T asks, (b) Theory, (c) Brainstorm, (d) After -Session T asks, (f) Peer Feedback, and (g) Expert F eedback". Homework prepares students before the session; theory and brainstorming happen in-session; after-session tasks produce the product; and iterative peer then expert feedback over 2-3 weeks refines it before products are uploaded to the network website or YouTube channel. This framework is the article's core mechanism for combining skill development with portfolio creation across a PhD timeline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each workshop pairs theoretical background with an activity students perform, observe, or experience
- Feedback is iterative: an initial round of peer feedback followed by expert feedback from learning scientists, science communicators, education researchers, and quantum physicists
#### Constraints
- Implementation and testing of products was left to students' own interests and opportunities in their outreach settings

### Target Learners
- early-stage researchers (PhD students)

### Learning Goals
- science outreach and communication skills
- creating implementable outreach products
