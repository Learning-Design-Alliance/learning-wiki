# A historically informed sequence reconstructing pre-Newtonian theories of light and colour, from Empedocles through Descartes, precedes the experimental work

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This lesson-sequence pattern devotes the first part of a historical science project to reconstructing the mindset of a seventeenth-century scholar before studying the target scientist's own work. The article states the first part "was intended as a means of acquiring the same mindset as any modern science scholar of the western seventeenth century, whose training in 'philosophical' reasoning started from ancient Greek tradition". Eleven meetings covered Empedocles, Aristotle, Euclid, Lucretius, Ptolemy, Avicenna, Alhazen, Scholasticism, Renaissance optics and Kepler, mechanism, and Descartes, using simple observations, original-text readings, and a recapitulation meeting on modificationism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher able to source and guide readings of original historical texts
- Simple apparatus for basic observations and experiences
- Weekly meetings over several months
#### Constraints
- The sequence was limited to stops related to light and colours, although incursions into mathematics, philosophy, medicine and physiology proved instrumental

### Target Learners
- outstanding high-school students with a philosophy and history curriculum background

### Learning Goals
- historical philosophical reasoning about light and colour
- appreciation of how scientific ideas developed
