# Structured 14-day video-based self-training workflow with recorded practice, peer or expert scoring, and standardized feedback checklists

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A standardized training procedure in which residents receive a teaching video, practice a skill in a multi-camera-equipped training room during a self-scheduled hour, submit their recorded performance, and receive scores plus feedback on a "standardized marking table (aligned with China's official residency training guidelines)". The 14-day cycle allocated Days 1-3 to video preparation, Days 4-10 to self-practice, and Days 11-14 to "scoring, feedback, and data collection". It was evaluated in the RCT comparing peer versus expert feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multi-channel video capture system (three fixed cameras and one forehead-mounted wearable camera) with network storage
- Standardized marking tables and detailed scoring instructions for all feedback providers to ensure consistency and reliability
- Dedicated training room with all models, instruments, materials and instructions, reset before each session
#### Constraints
- Participants were not blinded to the feedback source
- Only three skills with pre-assessment pass rates between 30% and 70% were used, to avoid excessive simplicity or difficulty

### Target Learners
- first-year surgical residents in a standardized residency training program

### Learning Goals
- fundamental clinical skill acquisition (wound dressing change with suture removal, indwelling urinary catheterization, simple interrupted suture)
