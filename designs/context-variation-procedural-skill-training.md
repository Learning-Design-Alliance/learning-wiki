# Context variation as a training strategy for skill transfer in invasive procedural learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Context variation is the training principle that exposure to varied practice examples and conditions enhances skill transfer and retention. In this study it was operationalized as a training group that "alternated between six different contexts at 10‐min intervals", mixing needle-guided, partially guided, and freehand techniques with varying visual constraints. The article tested whether this would produce superior skill acquisition and transfer compared with single-technique training, and found no advantage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires deliberately designed variation of practice contexts (e.g., alternating techniques, adjusting ultrasound views, varying placental positions) within training sessions.
#### Constraints
- In this trial, context variation showed no significant advantage over needle-guided or freehand training for CVS skill transfer among novices.

### Target Learners
- Ultrasound novices (medical students) learning invasive procedures

### Learning Goals
- Skill acquisition and transfer of ultrasound-guided procedural skills
