# FC-TBL literature seminar course design: pre-class preparation, in-class team application, and post-class reflection across thematic research units

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course is a core compulsory module (two credits, 40 contact hours) organized into ten thematic units aligned with nursing research types. Each four-hour unit follows an FC-TBL sequence: students complete assigned preparatory materials individually before class, then "worked in stable teams on structured application tasks" in class, producing shared products such as article critiques, concept maps, or evidence syntheses. After class, students write individual reflective summaries and revise presentations based on feedback, forming what the authors call "a closed-loop instructional process of knowledge acquisition, ability internalization, and cognitive development."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have completed core graduate-level coursework in research methodology and evidence-based practice before enrollment, providing a foundation in research literacy.
- Stable teams of 13–15 members each with elected leaders and assigned roles (literature retrieval, methodological analysis, concept mapping, presentation delivery, slide integration, feedback recording) to support coordinated collaboration.
- Each group must select one paper from a Chinese core nursing journal and one from a high-impact SCI journal for comparative analysis and presentation.
#### Constraints
- Roles are used as an organizational scaffold rather than isolated task ownership to reduce the risk of fragmented work.
- The intervention emphasized team-based inquiry and did not require each student to develop a complete individual research proposal.

### Target Learners
- second-year master's nursing students (graduate nursing postgraduates)

### Learning Goals
- research competence (problem identification, literature retrieval, research design, research practice, data processing, thesis writing)
- research self-efficacy
- critical appraisal of research literature
- academic communication
- team collaboration
