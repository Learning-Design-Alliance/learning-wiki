# Integrate combined 2-D and interactive 3-D digital food models into dietetic training, with tailored support for students who struggle across formats

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a pedagogical framework in which standardized 2-D food images and interactive, zoomable 3-D food models are embedded in an online platform and used for repeated portion size estimation practice inside a formal dietetic practicum. The authors report that "82% of students supported the educational integration of digital food models into the formal NP course" and that repeated training raised quantification accuracy to 42.9%. They conclude that "a tailored training program may be needed to master digital food viewing skills using 2-D and 3-D models."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an online platform that supports both 2-D image display and rotatable, resizable 3-D models (the article used Sketchfab and LimeSurvey)
- 3-D food models constructed via photogrammetry from standardized 360° videos and 2-D images with fiducial markers as size reference
- Training in operating the 3-D zoom/rotate functions to prevent portion distortion
#### Constraints
- Constructing high-quality 3-D food models with Agisoft Metashape is time-consuming and labor-intensive (2–8 h per model in this study)
- Photogrammetry struggles with translucent, white, or textureless foods and shiny or transparent containers
- 3-D models did not improve vegetable estimation accuracy
- Pilot evidence from a single cohort of 65 second-year students with no sample size calculation

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dietetic students

### Learning Goals
- food identification from digital images
- food portion size quantification
- estimated total calories of meal sets
