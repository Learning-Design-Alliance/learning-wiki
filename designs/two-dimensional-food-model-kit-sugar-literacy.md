# Kit of life-sized two-dimensional food and beverage models with sugar-teaspoon models and nutrition fact labels

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The intervention's core teaching aid is a kit of life-sized two-dimensional food and beverage models with nutrition information including added sugar on the back, drawn from NutriKitUSA and Sip Smart BC materials. Each team of 3 or 4 students received "a kit with more than 150 life-sized food and beverage models, 100 models of sugar teaspoons, and colored copies of seven nutrition fact labels". The two-dimensional format was chosen for feasibility in schools because of lower cost, weight and size than three-dimensional models, though the authors note it "may be less durable".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires space for teams to work with the models, in a room with large tables or on a gym floor
#### Constraints
- Two-dimensional models may be less durable than three-dimensional models

### Target Learners
- Older adolescents aged 14 to 19 in secondary school Physical and Health Education classes

### Learning Goals
- Knowledge of added sugar content in foods and beverages
- Knowledge of food guide servings and sugar guidelines
- Nutrition facts label reading skills
