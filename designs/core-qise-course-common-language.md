# Create a dedicated core QISE course, designed for non-physicists and grounded in linear algebra, to give students from diverse backgrounds a common language

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central curricular principle is that "Creating one core course, or a short series of courses, dedicated to QISE principles and applications is crucial when providing students with diverse educational backgrounds a common language." The symposium participants agreed it should not be a traditional physics quantum mechanics course: "It will likely be heavily based on a linear algebra approach to quantum mechanics," with prerequisites open to any technical undergraduate major. It also serves as a foundation for advanced QISE study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course prerequisites must be set so students with undergraduate degrees in any technical field can enroll
- The course must be designed for non-physicists rather than mirroring a physics department quantum mechanics course
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as expert consensus from the symposium, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- post-secondary students entering QISE Master's programs from physics, engineering, computer science, chemistry, and math backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- shared foundational understanding of quantum information science principles and applications
