# Timescale-based course organization: structuring a modern physics course around a logarithmic timescale axis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A course-design pattern in which lecture material is chosen and organized along the logarithmic scale of experimentally accessible sub-second timescales, down to about 10^-27 s. The author argues that existing timescale compilations "often contain just compilations of numbers and curious facts, mostly unrelated to each other" and that a timescale-based course can instead build "a coherent educational story line". The pattern introduces modern physics from an unconventional perspective, with each time interval contributing new protagonists and analysis techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must select and organize material according to guidelines such as systematically covering time intervals and emphasizing timescale-driven connections between physics areas
#### Constraints
- The author notes there is no single textbook providing all necessary material, so the instructor must invest significant time and effort into learning a vast range of topics

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science-major students
- high-school students (popular science variant)
- physics graduate students and researchers (advanced variant)

### Learning Goals
- developing timescale intuition
- introducing modern physics across domains
