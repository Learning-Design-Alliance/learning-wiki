# Text-based recalibration paradigm for probing letter-speech sound mappings

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An audio-visual perceptual-learning paradigm in which "aba"/"ada" text is combined with an individually determined ambiguous /a?a/ speech sound during exposure blocks, shifting later auditory-only perception of the ambiguous sound toward the text. In this study children completed four runs with 24 exposure blocks and post-test trials on /a?a/ and its two flanking sounds, inside fMRI across three yearly sessions. The paradigm was "speci ﬁcally designed to investigate reading-induced changes in the left posterior STC" and avoided the working-memory demands of congruency paradigms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual pre-test to identify each child's most ambiguous sound (response proportion closest to 0.5)
- MR-compatible audio presentation in silent gaps between volume acquisitions
#### Constraints
- Behavioural recalibration did not reach significance in the MRI environment in this age range, likely due to scanner noise or altered visual attentional focus
- The simple two-word text stimuli may not optimally engage the VWFA

### Target Learners
- Children aged 8–11 learning to read in an orthographically transparent language (Dutch)

### Learning Goals
- Short-term perceptual learning of letter-speech sound associations; probing automatisation of audio-visual letter-speech integration
