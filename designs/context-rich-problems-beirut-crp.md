# Context-Rich Problems as a cooperative active-learning strategy for physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Context-Rich Problems (CRPs) are problems "designed to be solved by more than one person and are presented as a story regarding a real situation", which the article describes as "a good active learning strategy that allows to promote qualitative and quantitative reasoning, the prediction of results and their veriﬁcation, as well as the integration of knowledge". Unlike traditional problems, obtaining the necessary data can be part of the resolution. The article instantiates this framework with three CRPs based on the Beirut explosion for university classical mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems are presented as a story regarding a real situation and are designed to be solved collaboratively by more than one person.
#### Constraints
- Solving a CRP as proposed requires classroom time: between 45 and 60 minutes if data is delivered by the teacher, or about 90 minutes if data acquisition is part of the resolution.

### Target Learners
- higher education / university physics students

### Learning Goals
- qualitative and quantitative reasoning
- prediction and verification of results
- integration of knowledge
- collaborative skills
