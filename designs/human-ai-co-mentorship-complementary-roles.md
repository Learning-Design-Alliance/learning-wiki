# Human-AI co-mentorship model with complementary tactical and strategic roles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a co-mentorship model in which AI tools and human mentors play fundamentally different, complementary roles in project-based learning. AI provides "just-in-time tactical support: generating code scaffolding, suggesting hyperparameter ranges, and offering quick explanations of unfamiliar concepts," while human mentors frame problems, validate architectural decisions, and supply domain intuition and a complete project vision. The article states "The most effective co-mentoring configuration was rathercomplementary: AI handled routine implementation tasks, freeing human mentors to focus on higher-level reasoning, debugging and conceptual explanation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human mentors with domain expertise must remain available for strategic decisions, validation of AI outputs, and conceptual explanation.
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single cohort of high-school and early-undergraduate students at US-style institutions over one summer; the authors caution against extrapolating to other contexts.
- Findings are specific to the versions of LLM tools available during the summer of 2025.

### Target Learners
- high-school students
- early-undergraduate students
- novices with limited domain expertise

### Learning Goals
- research and programming skills
- conceptual understanding of machine learning and finance
- critical evaluation of AI outputs
