# Five-stage concept checking-based consciousness-raising task sequence for grammar lessons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's instructional model divides each grammar lesson into five stages adapted from Harding and Taylor (2005) and Cunningham and Moor (2005): "(1) lead-in, (2) providing input, (3) consciousness-raising tasks, (4) checking and summarizing, and (5) practice." Learners first encounter the target form in contextualized input, then discover rules through pair and group tasks, receive teacher checking and feedback, and finally practice independently without teacher interference. The direct explicit group followed the same sequence except that step 3 replaced tasks with teacher presentation and explanation of the rules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contextualized input (reading or listening) containing examples of the target structure
- Pair and group work on rule-searched tasks
- Teacher checking, feedback, and rule summary after the task stage
#### Constraints
- The treatment phase lasted eight weeks with 90-minute sessions in this study; both groups received the same amount of time, contextualized input, and practice

### Target Learners
- EFL high school students at pre-intermediate level

### Learning Goals
- Discovery of the form and meaning of target grammatical structures
- Retention and productive use of English tenses
