# Four-step Physics problem-based learning cycle used for classroom assessment in both conditions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a four-step Physics PBL sequence used in both the inverted and traditional classrooms: students are presented with a real-life problem, understand and solve it in groups using an adaptation of the seven Maastricht steps, present and discuss their findings in a 10 to 15 minutes presentation, and finally reflect with facilitators on what they gained. It served as the end-of-module assessment format for both groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator to introduce and explain the problem scenario and guide group work
- Group organisation of students for collaborative problem solving
#### Constraints
- Used as the assessment format at the end of every module in this pre-university physics course

### Target Learners
- pre-university physics students

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking skills
- problem-solving skills
- application of mechanics concepts to real-life problems
