# Three student-centered PBL variants: project-based, problem-based, and practice-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report describes three distinct active-learning methods that share the acronym PBL and are student-centered: project-based learning organizes learning around complex group projects with the teacher acting like a research mentor; problem-based learning presents ill-structured real-world problems where process is valued over the final solution; and practice-based learning combines course content with opportunities to practice, now standard in healthcare education. Each is illustrated with medical physics examples such as linac room design projects, radionuclide-selection problems, and QA competency assessment under a qualified medical physicist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher still determines learning goals, establishes academic rigor, and sets timelines and rubrics for evaluation
#### Constraints
- Scaling methods for large classes is challenging; evaluating each student's contribution objectively in collaborative work is difficult; students do not produce traditional assessment results

### Target Learners
- medical physics graduate students and residents

### Learning Goals
- problem solving, practical clinical skills, teamwork, and professional practice development
