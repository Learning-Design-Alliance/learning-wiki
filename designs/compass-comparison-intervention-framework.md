# COMPASS: a theory-driven intervention addressing dimensional and temporal comparison processes and their associated beliefs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
COMPASS (comparison processes in students' self-concept formation) is an intervention framework that enhances subject-specific academic self-concepts by educating students about dimensional comparisons (comparisons of one's own performance across subjects) and temporal comparisons (comparisons with one's own past performance) and the beliefs moderating them. The article states that "COMPASS represents a coherent, theory-driven application of comparison theories that jointly addresses dimensional and temporal comparison processes and their associated beliefs." Its modules work with psychoeducation, exercises, and discussions, delivered in roughly 120 minutes with lectures, class discussions, group exercises, and mnemonic posters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery in regular school lessons by trained intervention leaders with group sizes limited to a maximum of 20 students to ensure active discussion engagement
#### Constraints
- The social comparison module was excluded because it did not change the targeted belief and led to an unintended decrease in students' German self-concept, and the cohort-comparison idea is not applicable in nonacademic track schools

### Target Learners
- Students in Grades 9 to 11 at German secondary schools of the academic track

### Learning Goals
- Subject-specific academic self-concepts in math or English
- Belief in the positive interdependence of mathematical and verbal abilities
- Belief in the changeability of abilities
