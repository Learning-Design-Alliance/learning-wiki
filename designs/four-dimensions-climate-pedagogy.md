# Four-dimension framework for effective climate pedagogy in a science classroom

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that an effective climate pedagogy operates along four dimensions: a scientific-technological dimension (basic science, impacts, evidence, projections), a transdisciplinary justice dimension (societal and ethical implications, climate justice), an epistemological dimension, and a psychosocial-action dimension. The epistemological dimension "enables the student to undergo an epistemic shift" so students see the crisis as a symptom of a social-scientific paradigm. The framework is offered as one among many possible, explicitly rejecting one-size-fits-all claims, and is intended for adaptation to any general science course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Embedding in a transformational learning approach with a collaborative, trust-based classroom culture
#### Constraints
- Classroom-level rather than program-level; the author notes a truly transdisciplinary approach would have a different starting point

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate general physics students, including non-science majors; adaptable to high school and college science courses

### Learning Goals
- Understanding climate science and its impacts; climate justice; epistemic shift regarding social-scientific paradigms; personal agency and engagement with social-environmental problems
