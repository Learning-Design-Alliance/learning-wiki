# Multiple-representational tasks combined with collaborative group work, whole-class discussion, and self-reflection for restructuring teachers' conceptions of matter

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern in which learners classify physical models (paper-clip arrangements), draw particulate representations of solids, liquids, gases, elements, compounds and mixtures, view dynamic animations, build play-dough models, and share and discuss representations. The article states that "multiple representational tasks, combined with collaborative group work, discussion, and self-reflection, provided preservice teachers an opportunity for generating more scientific representations of matter." The instruction lasted three class periods in two phases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must span the macroscopic, submicroscopic, and symbolic representational levels
- Whole-class discussion to reach consensus views about representations and definitions
#### Constraints
- The article notes the instruction gave no special attention to distinguishing homogeneous from heterogeneous mixtures, which it links to weaker gains on physical composition

### Target Learners
- preservice chemistry teachers
- third-year students in a five-year teacher education program

### Learning Goals
- understanding the structure of matter at three physical states
- classifying elements, compounds, pure substances, and mixtures at different representational levels
