# Conceptual-change instructional design combining constructivist disequilibrium, andragogy, and cognitive flexibility principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention design rests on a conceptual change model in which inaccurate schemas must be directly confronted to prompt the cognitive effort necessary for accommodation: students are provided "an appropriate conceptual pathway" from naive understandings to scientific ones while their erroneous schemas are perturbed through activities designed to create disequilibrium. This is combined with andragogy principles (drawing on prior experience via familiar scenarios like bathtubs and bank accounts, real-life relevance, problem solving) and cognitive flexibility theory's five principles, including avoiding oversimplification, using multiple representations, teaching through cases, interconnecting cases, and having learners construct knowledge. The four Thinkspace tasks instantiate these principles in a snow-cone business scenario.

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- The authors report these design principles alone were likely necessary but not sufficient, since most students still relied on the misconception after the intervention

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physical geology students
- adult learners in large lecture courses

### Learning Goals
- conceptual change about stock-flow systems and conservation of mass
- generalizing budget concepts across contexts
