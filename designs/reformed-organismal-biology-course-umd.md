# Reformed Organismal Biology course at the University of Maryland combining content reform with group active engagement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reformed version of the third course in Maryland's introductory biology sequence, focusing on "highlighting the broad principles that yields a coherent unified picture of the structure, diversity, and function of organisms." One section restructured the class into two-thirds conventional lecture and one-third group active engagement (GAE) periods, plus clicker questions, homework, and reading assignments, with exams composed primarily of short written responses. The reform was funded through the university and an NSF-CCLI grant.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time and institutional support, including NSF-CCLI funding, to develop and evaluate the reformed content and pedagogy
#### Constraints
- The course retains much of the subject material of standard introductory textbooks while emphasizing universal physical and chemical principles and common genomic heritage

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology majors, pre-med, and pre-allied health students in a large introductory course

### Learning Goals
- principles-based understanding of the structure, diversity, and function of organisms
- student engagement and scientific reasoning
