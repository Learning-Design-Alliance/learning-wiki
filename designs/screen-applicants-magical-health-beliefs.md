# Screen prospective chiropractic students for magical health beliefs to prevent a mismatch between student and institution

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose that the validated ten-item Magical Health Beliefs questionnaire be used at admission, because magical thinking predicted both conservative chiropractic beliefs and willingness to treat beyond the evidence base in their survey. They state that "the questionnaire 'Magical Health Beliefs' could be a useful instrument to screen future chiropractic students to prevent a mismatch between student and institution". Institutions would use the score to include or exclude applicants according to their own outlook.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a validated magical-health-beliefs questionnaire administered to applicants before enrolment
#### Constraints
- Magical thinking may be an intrinsic personality trait, so the authors note it is unlikely that mere education and information will change it more than marginally; findings come from one Australian program and generalisability to national and international students would need validation

### Target Learners
- prospective and enrolled chiropractic students

### Learning Goals
- alignment of student health-belief profile with an institution's evidence-based (MSK) or conservative educational outlook
