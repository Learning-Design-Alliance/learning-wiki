# CU's iterative assessment development process guided by consensus learning goals and free-response format

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
CU's four upper-division assessments were developed through an iterative design cycle: faculty working groups draft questions targeting consensus course-scale learning goals, followed by expert review, think-aloud student interviews with 5-15 volunteers, and classroom testing with item revision. The consensus learning goals, which "emphasized students' ability to synthesize, generate, and justify their responses", motivated the free-response format and distinguish CU's approach from other assessments that focus on content coverage via textbook reviews and faculty surveys.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consensus learning goals articulated with faculty who taught the course
- Expert review and student think-aloud interviews before classroom testing
- Iterative refinement based on expert reviews, student interviews, and classroom performance
#### Constraints
- Small upper-division class sizes limit statistical power for validation
- Level-appropriate questions must avoid excessive calculation time

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of core upper-division physics content
- reasoning and problem-solving skills such as justifying a solution method
