# Contrasting examples as a generative-processing instructional method whose preconditions for critical-thinking transfer are questioned

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames contrasting examples—comparing fictitious students' correct and erroneous solutions side by side—as a method expected to induce generative processing, structural alignment, and focus on underlying principles, drawing on cognitive load theory and the temporal contiguity effect. It reasoned that "As contrasting can help learners to focus on key information and thereby induces generative processes directly relevant for learning". The study's null results led the authors to state that the findings "raise questions about the preconditions of contrasting examples effects".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners compare correct and erroneous solutions presented side by side and are prompted to indicate the erroneous solution and step, following explicit CT instruction
#### Constraints
- In this classroom study with elaborate instructions preceding practice, contrasting examples showed no advantage over correct examples, erroneous examples, or practice problems on learning or transfer

### Target Learners
- first-year higher professional education students (Public Administration and Safety and Security Management)

### Learning Goals
- avoiding biased reasoning (syllogistic, conjunction, confirmation-bias, and base-rate tasks)
- transfer of critical-thinking skills to novel task types
