# Surgical cognitive simulation (SCS): a distinct multi-sensory cognitive training process beyond mental practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
SCS is a training programme the authors designed for application in operating theatres, comprising "evidence-based performance-enhancing strategies, including preperformance routines, goal setting, thought management and focused, mental practice". The authors argue SCS is not merely a relabelling of mental practice or mental imagery, because those terms are imprecise and limit the process to visual information, whereas SCS engages multiple sensory modalities and broader cognitive functions such as situational awareness and anticipation. Trainees received SCS training from a surgical performance coach plus self-study material and were advised to perform cognitive simulation for approximately 20 min the day before procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial training with a facilitator (a surgical performance coach) plus reading material for self-study
- Regular application by the trainee in real operating theatre work, supported in this study by monthly feedback on application
#### Constraints
- The article states an earlier study found application of SCS dropped significantly after 4 weeks without reinforcement

### Target Learners
- General surgical trainees in postgraduate years 3–5

### Learning Goals
- Technical surgical skill acquisition and operative performance
- Preoperative preparedness, intraoperative focus and stress management
