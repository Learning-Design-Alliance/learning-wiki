# Nano AI as the GAI collaboration tool in the Interstellar Navigation project-based science curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the six-week quasi-experiment, the experimental group used Nano AI as its GAI tool during collaboration, while the control group relied on printed materials. The curriculum, "Interstellar Navigation: Reaching for the Sky," was "developed based on Project Based Learning principles and comprised six sequential modules," with students working in 40 small groups of five to six members on six tasks across weekly 80-minute sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups need shared access to the GAI tool during collaborative sessions; both conditions were taught by the same instructor
#### Constraints
- The only condition difference was GAI access versus printed materials; the tool was used within one project-based elementary science curriculum

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade students in primary school

### Learning Goals
- collaborative problem-solving within elementary science project tasks

### Claims

- [Interviews revealed six themes: enhanced efficiency, improved quality, facilitated reflection, increased interaction, improved collaboration, and concerns about over-reliance](../claims/gai-dst-interview-six-themes.md) [+W]
- [GAI-supported fifth-grade groups show more social CPS behaviors (participation, conflict management) but fewer overt cognitive behaviors (task planning, solution reasoning) than control groups](../claims/gai-shifts-cps-toward-social-behaviors.md) [+W]
- [Students most frequently used GAI for knowledge acquisition (60.440% of logs), followed by visualization requests (24.725%) and analytical support (8.242%)](../claims/gai-log-themes-knowledge-acquisition-dominant.md) [+W]
