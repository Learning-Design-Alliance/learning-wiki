# Co-teach interdisciplinary courses with contributions from physicists attuned to biology and life scientists attuned to physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that fulfilling an interdisciplinary approach "requires obligatory contributions from both physicists and biologists", realized by co-teaching from the biology and physics and astronomy departments. The authors state that a crucial requirement for mounting such a course is "requiring an almost obligatory contribution by both physicists attuned to biology and life scientists attuned to physics". Based on their success, they conclude that typical first- and second-year Life Sciences and Physics courses could be co-taught in a more interdisciplinary way, extendable to other science majors such as chemistry.
