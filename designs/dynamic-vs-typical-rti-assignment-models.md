# Dynamic RTI versus Typical RTI: two models for timing assignment to multi-tier reading intervention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article contrasts two assignment models for multi-tier reading intervention. Dynamic RTI "immediately refers grade 1 students with the weakest skills to the most intensive intervention supports (Tier 2 or Tier 3)", whereas Typical RTI "starts all students in Tier 1 and after 8 weeks, decides whether students who did not respond to general instruction in Tier 1 should move to Tier 2". The experiment randomly assigned matched pairs of students within classrooms to one of the two models, holding everything else constant except the timing of supplemental intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial screening in September using teacher reports and multiple screeners with local-norm cut points (below the 40th percentile at the school level)
- Periodic progress monitoring allowing movement across tiers every 8 weeks
#### Constraints
- Studied in seven schools in a mid-size southeastern city during their first year of RTI implementation, with less than 3% Limited English Proficient students

### Target Learners
- First-grade struggling readers

### Learning Goals
- Word reading, decoding, and reading comprehension growth in first grade
