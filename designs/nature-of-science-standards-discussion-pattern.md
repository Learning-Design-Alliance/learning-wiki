# Two-part guided discussion pattern pairing standards analysis with application to a contested science-standards controversy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level discussion pattern in which students first analyze an official definition of science (summarize it, identify key words, write a grade-level definition, critique it) and then apply it to a locally relevant controversy, here Intelligent Design, ending with a group consensus policy statement. The activity "consists of two parts," with Part 2 built around two questions: whether ID is science and whether ID viewpoints should be taught in Kansas science classrooms. Groups of 3-4 discuss and write their reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Background instruction on the nature and process of science (2-3 days defining terms such as theory, fact, hypothesis, model, law) precedes the activity
- Students read a partial copy of the science standards, including the definition of science, outside of class before the activity
#### Constraints
- The author states the activity is not intended to be a debate and instructs students not to attack or belittle fellow students' ideas, referencing the standards' "Teaching with Tolerance and Respect" section to keep the environment safe for honesty

### Target Learners
- elementary education majors in an undergraduate physical science course for non-science majors

### Learning Goals
- understanding the nature and process of science
- familiarity with state science education standards
- evaluating the scientific status of Intelligent Design
