# Rapid-cycle deliberate practice as a framework for high-acuity, low-occurrence procedural training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rapid-cycle deliberate practice is an educational framework using repeated practice cycles with continuous instructor input, in which learners are stopped, redirected, and instructed to restart the procedure whenever a predefined step is not completed to mastery. This optimizes time on challenging steps while minimizing repetition of correctly performed ones. The article applied it with 1:1 supervision on a manikin and bronchoscopy simulator to teach awake fiberoptic intubation, a novel application in emergency medicine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 1:1 instructor supervision during practice sessions
- A procedural checklist defining mastery-level steps for restarting the procedure
#### Constraints
- Requires simulation equipment and instructor time; the article notes the curriculum needs study among a broader range of instructors to ensure reliability

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents and attending physicians

### Learning Goals
- Awake fiberoptic intubation procedural skill
