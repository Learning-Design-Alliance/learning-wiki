# Generative Collective Intelligence (GCI) framework: AI as a cognitive bridge within collective human reasoning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper's central theoretical proposal adapts Kehler, Page, Pentland, Reeves, and Brown's Generative Collective Intelligence framework for engineering classrooms. The framework "rejects the view of intelligence as an isolated, algorithmic property and instead understands it as fundamentally social, relational, and collectively constructed", positioning AI in dual roles as an interactive computational agent and as a technology that organizes collective knowledge. In the authors' implementation, AI is consulted only after human reasoning establishes a foundation, serving as "a cognitive bridge rather than a replacement tutor". Students experience AI as one voice among many, interrogated through collective judgment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human reasoning must establish the collective foundation before AI is consulted; AI consultation is tied to specific purposes such as surfacing perspectives or critiquing preliminary analyses.
#### Constraints
- The authors state this represents "one possible implementation among many, designed specifically for our context and constraints".

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- collective problem-solving
- critical evaluation of AI outputs
- metacognitive awareness of when AI adds value
