# Blended I.N.F.O. and S.T.O.P. facilitation model for interdisciplinary clinical debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study assembled a locally appropriate blended approach to clinical debriefing facilitation that combined two published debriefing models: the psychological safety focus of "I.N.F.O." ("Immediate, Not for personal assessment, Fast facilitated feedback, and Opportunity to ask questions") and the structure of "S.T.O.P." ("Summarise the case; Things that went well; Opportunities for improvement; Points of action"). A debriefing cognitive aid with suggested scripting supported facilitators, and the model was used to train 13 ED specialists, 1 social worker, 5 senior nurses and 18 ED registrars in 40 min of faculty training. Debriefers standardised the opening of CDs but were not instructed on what to discuss.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development in the blended approach (40 min training) plus a debriefing cognitive aid with suggested scripting
- Debriefings of clinical events with at least 3 persons, occurring within 1 h of the event in an acute care setting
#### Constraints
- The authors state there is no universally applicable clinical debriefing method; all methods have pros and cons and should be applied with consideration of local historical, clinical and cultural context

### Target Learners
- Emergency department healthcare providers and students, including physicians, nurses, social workers and registrars

### Learning Goals
- Team-based learning after clinical events
- Identification of improvement opportunities and latent patient safety threats
