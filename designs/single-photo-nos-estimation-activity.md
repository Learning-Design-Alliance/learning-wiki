# Single-photograph estimation activity as a structured introduction to Nature of Science in upper-secondary STEM

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A classroom-ready activity in which students start from one authentic, serendipitous observation — a sunrise photograph of Mont Blanc's shadow on a cloud layer — and move through hypothesis formulation, geometric and mathematical modelling, approximation, uncertainty analysis, and comparison with external data. The article states it "combines geometric reasoning, basic trigonometry, and order-of-magnitude estimation", and that the discrepancy with the accepted radius "provides a concrete illustration of modelling assumptions and observational limitations". It requires no more than upper-secondary technical level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A photograph showing a mountain shadow projected on a cloud layer, with known summit elevations, observer location, distance, and camera focal length for pixel-to-angle conversion
#### Constraints
- The result is an upper bound, biased towards overestimate by refraction, topography, and the sensitivity of the radius to the angle of light

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary students

### Learning Goals
- nature of science concepts
- geometric reasoning and trigonometry
- order-of-magnitude estimation
- uncertainty analysis

### Claims
- Sunrise Photo Earth Radius Upper Bound 4 2 Rt [+M]
- Refraction Corrected Radius Bound 1 7 Rt [+M]
