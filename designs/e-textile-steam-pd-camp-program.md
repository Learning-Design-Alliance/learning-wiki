# Five-day e-textile-supported STEAM teacher PD camp with seven structured projects and one open-ended project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A five-day professional development program at a Turkish university, run as two camps for middle school science teachers, focused on "designing and developing STEAM projects using e-textile technology, with an emphasis on integrating art, design, and technology." It encompassed "seven structured e-textile projects" (badges, wristbands, necklaces, color-changing star clusters, light-sensing lighthouses, heat-sensing thermometers, color-sensing scarves) plus one open-ended project, progressing from serial and parallel circuits to sensors and Arduino programming, with hands-on projects integrating problem-based scenarios and engineering design stages adapted from Hynes et al. (2011). Five facilitators supported groups of two or three teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Purposively selected teachers with little or no prior hands-on e-textile STEAM experience
- Supply of e-textile materials, tools, and facilitator support throughout the program
#### Constraints
- Evaluated with self-report post-surveys and a small, voluntary follow-up sample (5 completed surveys)

### Target Learners
- in-service middle school science teachers from rural and socioeconomically disadvantaged public schools

### Learning Goals
- electricity and circuitry concepts
- STEAM project design
- Arduino programming basics
- interdisciplinary integration of art, design, and technology
