# The bridging model for connecting service courses to students' majors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The bridging model is a relatively simple alternative to fully integrated curricula for helping students appreciate connections between courses in different departments. Rather than coordinating entire curricula across departments, it isolates majors from one course into a dedicated recitation section where discussion, projects, homework, and examples emphasize connections to the other discipline, and explains differences in approach and notation for overlapping material. It was applied to a physics course on oscillations and waves linked to an EE course, and the article states the model can be applied to a variety of courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires overlapping material between the two courses and instructor communication across departments; recruiting of majors into the bridge section via flyers, announcements, and email.
#### Constraints
- The article contrasts it with integrated curricula, which it notes take more time and may be deemed cost-ineffective by administration; the bridging evaluation relied on small samples too small to reach statistical significance.

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level engineering students in introductory physics service courses

### Learning Goals
- appreciating connections between courses in different departments
- improved performance, attitude, and motivation toward the service course
