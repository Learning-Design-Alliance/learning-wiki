# Video modeling pedagogy with dynamic particle models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Video modeling, a pedagogical approach by Douglas Brown, couples computer model building with traditional video analysis: students build dynamic particle models driven by data from video analysis and test their mental models "by juxtaposing their mental models against the analysis of real life videos". In this article the approach lets students refute impetus-style misconceptions of projectile motion by iteratively improving models until they match the real video, producing what the authors call Eureka experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The article states that only after the determination of the variables through video analysis can the pedagogical advantage of video modeling be tapped, so model values must come from prior video analysis.
#### Constraints
- The misconceptions addressed are those the article discusses: a non-zero x-direction force, a positive y-direction force, and significant air resistance at low velocity.

### Target Learners
- GCE Advanced Level and Ordinary Level physics students in Singapore junior colleges and high schools

### Learning Goals
- testing and refining personal theories of motion against real-world evidence
- overcoming projectile motion misconceptions
