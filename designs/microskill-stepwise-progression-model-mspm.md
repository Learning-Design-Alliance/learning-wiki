# Microskill stepwise progression model (MSPM) for undergraduate tooth extraction training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured teaching framework that deconstructs tooth extraction into nine sequential stages of increasing procedural complexity. From Stage 2 to Stage 9, students must complete five targeted observations and five successful supervised hands-on performances at the current stage before progressing. The model is informed by deliberate practice, mastery-oriented learning, workplace-based assessment, and entrustment-based progression principles. Failed attempts are not counted towards the progression threshold.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Progression requires five targeted observations and five successful supervised hands-on performances at the current stage before advancing to the next stage.
- Each clinical attempt is directly observed by the supervising instructor and recorded as either 'Pass' or 'Fail' according to predefined criteria.
#### Constraints
- The numerical threshold was used as a pragmatic minimum exposure benchmark for this pilot study rather than as a literature-validated competency cut-off.
- The model was developed and evaluated only for tooth extraction, and both the nine-stage framework and modified DOPS tool were procedure-specific.

### Target Learners
- Fifth-year undergraduate dental students

### Learning Goals
- Procedural competence in tooth extraction
- Safe progression through increasing case complexity
