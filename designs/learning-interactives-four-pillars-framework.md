# Four-pillar conceptual framework for tailoring generative UI to learning: curriculum alignment, agency and motivation, guidance and scaffolding, formative feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article develops a conceptual framework of four pillars for tailoring generative interfaces to learning: "curriculum alignment; agency and motivation; guidance and scaffolding; formative feedback." Each pillar is mapped to a concrete UX/UI choice in Table 1: articulated teacher-approved learning objectives, progressive difficulty challenges balancing achievement and self-efficacy, AI-generated scaffolding such as multi-tiered hints and real-time context-aware feedback that prompts critical thinking rather than revealing answers, and introspective intros and solvers with worked solutions. The framework grounds the generation pipeline's pedagogical guardrails.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each pillar must be implemented via a corresponding UX/UI choice in the generated interactive, as detailed in Table 1
#### Constraints
- Off-the-shelf Gen-UI models lack optimization for pedagogical precision, so the pillars must be imposed as guardrails rather than assumed

### Target Learners
- K-12 and undergraduate STEM students across primary, middle school, high school and undergraduate levels

### Learning Goals
- STEM conceptual understanding through active inquiry, hypothesis testing, and variable manipulation in simulations and mini-games
