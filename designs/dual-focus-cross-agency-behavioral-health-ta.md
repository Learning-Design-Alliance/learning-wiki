# Any statewide behavioral health TA model should combine cross-agency collaboration with a dual focus on student outcomes and sustainable service infrastructure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Research for this project suggests design features for capacity-building TA in school-based behavioral health. First, collaboration across county agencies (COEs, county behavioral health, SELPAs) is critical so services are coordinated and funding maximized; the existing AB 2083 cross-agency county-level MOU structure could be leveraged. Second, the brief recommends "the need for capacity-building TA on both of these areas of focus": improving student behavioral health outcomes (e.g., finding meaningful outcome measures) and building infrastructure for sustainable access (e.g., breaking down funding silos, increasing Medi-Cal reimbursements, developing data-sharing MOUs). Third, access should be expanded without making TA a requirement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration between county agencies regardless of which agency leads TA at the county level
#### Constraints
- An analogous statewide measure for student behavioral health does not currently exist, so Tier 2 eligibility would have to rely on proxies such as LEA demographics, Dashboard data, or CHKS data

### Target Learners
- Local educational agencies and community-based partners statewide

### Learning Goals
- Improving student behavioral health outcomes and building sustainable infrastructure for integrated systems of care
