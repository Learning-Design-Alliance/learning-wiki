# Face-name TMR experimental protocol: two music-associated classes, nap cueing, and interference learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A laboratory procedure in which participants learned 80 face-name pairs (40 per class, ostensibly Japanese History and Latin-American History classes), each class with a distinctive instrumental music track. Learning combined face viewing with spoken and written names, recall training with feedback, and visualization practice. Presleep tests assessed recognition (230 faces: old, rotated, new) and name recall; a mean 59-minute nap followed with manual TMR cueing during N3; interference learning of 20 new pairs preceded postsleep tests.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EEG recording to detect N3 sleep and pause cueing on transitions to other stages or wake
- Spoken-name cue intensity manually controlled at 45–50 dB peak, the highest volume possible without causing arousal
#### Constraints
- Due to a technical error, the study could not test whether TMR effects differed for spoken-name-plus-music cues versus music-only cues within the cued class

### Target Learners
- adult participants (18–31, mean age 23.38) recruited from a university population and surrounding community

### Learning Goals
- face recognition and name recall for newly learned people
