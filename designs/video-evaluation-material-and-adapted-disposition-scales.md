# Four fictitious interview videos and adapted French critical thinking disposition scales for adolescents

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study material comprises four 45–55-second fictitious interview videos on meat consumption reduction, in which the interviewee's position and expertise (expert vs layperson) varied, with expertise signaled by a banner and interview location. Students rated source and information trustworthiness on nine-point scales, then completed adapted French questionnaires measuring self-efficacy, epistemic justification, intellectual humility, and reasoning style. As the authors note, "The four videos had been produced for an earlier research project on students' evaluation of conflicting videos".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Item comprehension was checked with a pretest among 11-12 year-old students (N = 14) and teacher review, since scales were translated and adapted for a young audience.
#### Constraints
- The authors state their work "does not allow us to claim French validations of the questionnaires and their adaptation to another population"; the video material was purpose-built for research and may be at odds with videos adolescents actually encounter online.

### Target Learners
- Middle school students aged 11–16
- High school students aged 15–19

### Learning Goals
- Critical evaluation of sources and information in online videos
