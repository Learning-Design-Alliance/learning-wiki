# Pre-clerkship simulation-based venipuncture training program with hybrid simulators, Peyton's Four-Step Approach, and mastery-learning feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-hour skills-laboratory training session for small groups of 3-4 students per hybrid simulator, combining a part-task trainer for technical venipuncture skills with standardized patients for communication role-play. Instruction followed "Rodney Peyton's 'Four-Step Approach'"; communication practice drew on the ALOBA scheme. Students practiced as many times as desired and "iteratively received direct feedback and targeted practice on the steps that were not achieved until they could independently complete the entire procedure", per mastery learning practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hybrid simulators pairing a part-task trainer with standardized patients
- Instructors providing direct, specific feedback on technical performance and communication
- Small groups of 3-4 students per simulator
#### Constraints
- Opportunities for redundant training and deliberate practice were virtually nonexistent in the resource-limited local context
- Skills taught in the pre-clinical phase were not formally assessed and not revisited in following years

### Target Learners
- Pre-clerkship (second-year) medical students

### Learning Goals
- Technical venipuncture competency
- Doctor-patient communication during procedures
- Self-confidence in performing procedures
