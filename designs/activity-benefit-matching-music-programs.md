# Matching musical activities to targeted wellbeing benefits: group, instrumental, product-creation, fun, challenging, and non-musical activities each serve distinct goals

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents an example list of how activities within a music group can be appropriated for particular goals: group activities facilitate social connection and communication; instrumental activities such as improvisation offer an accessible, non-confronting way to engage with difficult feelings; creating a musical product promotes ownership, pride and self-efficacy; fun activities increase engagement; challenging activities help at-risk groups face difficult issues; and non-musical activities let uncomfortable students participate and be recognised as contributing members.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A specific need or program goal must be identified before selecting activities; musical products should focus on expression rather than musical skill
#### Constraints
- The article states the list "is not intended as an exhaustive account of activities or benefits"

### Target Learners
- bereaved students
- at-risk secondary students

### Learning Goals
- social connection
- communication
- self-efficacy
- engagement with grief and loss
