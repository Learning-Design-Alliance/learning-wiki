# Provide an additional supportive year of study (a gateway course) so students from educationally and socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds can enter medicine and demonstrate academic potential beyond their secondary-school attainment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Gateway courses are six-year widening participation routes into UK medicine that add an extra year of study before or within the first two years. Per the article, "The additional year supports the transition into HE and provides a variety of skills and knowledge designed to help students succeed in their future studies", often with clinical or community placements and a highly supportive environment, using contextual admissions with reduced A-level requirements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Entry via course-specific eligibility criteria identifying educational and/or social disadvantage
- Supportive environment with skills and knowledge modules designed to help students succeed in future studies
#### Constraints
- The context of disadvantage does not disappear on entry; the article reports greater attrition and year repetition for gateway students
- Attrition is higher in the 2nd and 3rd academic years of the gateway course

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students from educationally and socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- Access to medical school and successful progression to graduation as a doctor
