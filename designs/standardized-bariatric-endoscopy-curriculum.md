# Establish a standardized endoscopic training curriculum specifically designed for metabolic and bariatric surgeons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends coordinated efforts by educators, institutions, and professional societies to standardize endoscopic education for bariatric surgeons, because its survey revealed "significant gaps in structured endoscopic training" alongside low procedural volumes and confidence. It notes that no standardized endoscopic training curriculum has yet been formally recommended by professional societies dedicated to metabolic and bariatric surgery, and that existing certifications such as FES do not cover EBTs or MBS.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordinated efforts by educators, institutions, and professional societies
- Curriculum content covering diagnostic endoscopy, complication management, and EBTs, which the article says are essential components of contemporary MBS
#### Constraints
- The article notes existing FES certification's core curriculum does not include didactic content related to EBTs or MBS
- Global heterogeneity and limited-resource settings make establishing training pathways challenging

### Target Learners
- Early-career metabolic and bariatric surgeons (aged ≤45 years, Young-IFSO members)

### Learning Goals
- Diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopy skills
- Endoscopic management of MBS-related postoperative complications
- Endoscopic bariatric therapies (IGB, ESG, TORe)
