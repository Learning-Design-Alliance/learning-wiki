# Highly structured scripted tutoring lesson pattern: sequenced phonics routines, leveled book reading with prediction routines, immediate corrective feedback, and motivational games and points

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Each 25–45-minute session followed a fixed sequence: 15–20 minutes of scripted phonics chart routines, 10–20 minutes of book reading at the student's instructional level, and 5–15 minutes of supplemental games, with a 100-point chart for effort. The article states "The lessons were very structured, which means that they followed a systematic phonics scope and sequence, implemented routine book reading procedures (e.g., making predictions, summarizing), and included explicit instruction with corrective feedback." All materials were provided so tutors made no curricular decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors follow scripted directions and a predetermined lesson sequence rather than selecting activities or materials themselves
- Texts are matched to each student's instructional level (approximately 90% accuracy) and placement is set by placement tests administered by professionals
#### Constraints
- The study did not compare different amounts of tutoring or levels of supervision, so the contribution of structure alone is not isolated

### Target Learners
- Struggling elementary readers in Grades K–6 at risk for reading failure from low-income families

### Learning Goals
- Word identification and decoding (phonics and word attack)
- Oral reading fluency
- Passage comprehension
