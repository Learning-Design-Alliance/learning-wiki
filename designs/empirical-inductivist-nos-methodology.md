# Empirical-inductivist methodology for NOS teaching through repeated observation of a non-directly-observable phenomenon

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An approach, adopted from Santiago's work, in which students partially mirror the procedures of scientific research by relying on repeated observation and investigation of a phenomenon. The article deliberately chose electromagnetic induction because the phenomenon cannot be directly observed, creating an opportunity for students to construct knowledge through their own actions while developing core investigative skills. It underpins the activity's problem question about how a scientist knows whether an experiment confirms a theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A physical phenomenon whose effects are not visually evident, so that observation and inference must be distinguished
#### Constraints
- The article warns the approach must push students beyond naïve inductivism — the assumption that observation is inherently objective and always leads to unquestionable conclusions

### Target Learners
- Basic education and undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the role of observation and inference in scientific understanding
- Critically examining the theory-experiment relationship
