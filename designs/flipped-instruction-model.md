# Flipped instruction moves lecture content outside class and uses class time for active, learner-centered activities.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The flipped or inverted classroom is defined as "the practice of moving the lecture portion of the course outside of the classroom and engaging in active learning activities during class time." Course material is disseminated before class, often through short video lectures, freeing class time for collaborative problem-solving and student-centered exercises. The model assumes good teaching limits passive knowledge transfer and promotes inquiry, collaboration, and critical thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students prepare before class by engaging with disseminated materials
- Instructors provide access to lecture content outside class, often via recorded videos or online resources
#### Constraints
- May increase instructor workload and preparation time
- Students may be initially slow to adapt to new expectations

### Target Learners
- Higher education students across disciplines

### Learning Goals
- Active learning, collaboration, critical thinking, and student engagement
