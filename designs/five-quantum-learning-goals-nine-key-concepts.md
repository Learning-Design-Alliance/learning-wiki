# Five learning goals of increasing complexity mapped to nine national quantum information science key concepts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents five conceptual learning goals for teachers—analyzing superposition in spin and light experiments, distinguishing events and tagging from measurement, explaining entanglement's violation of local realism, explaining interaction-free experiments, and describing real measurements of single quanta. These goals are "anchored in these nine concepts" developed at an NSF workshop, with explicit mappings such as goal (1) mapping to concepts (ii), (iii), and (iv), and they lean toward the quantum sensing side of quantum information science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Goals are organized around understanding experiments and the formal ideas needed to explain those experiments within a quantum-mechanical framework
#### Constraints
- The goals do not focus on all nine NSF concepts, nor are they formulated strictly in terms of those concepts

### Target Learners
- Teachers preparing to introduce quantum ideas in primary and secondary education

### Learning Goals
- Describe superposition, distinguish tagging from measurement, explain entanglement tests, explain interaction-free experiments, and describe real measurements of single quanta
