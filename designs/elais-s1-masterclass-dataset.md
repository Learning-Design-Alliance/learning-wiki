# Real research dataset from the ELAIS-S1 sky region observed by VLT/ESO, Spitzer and XMM-Newton

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The masterclass uses data from a real research project, partly carried out within a PhD thesis, covering the ELAIS-S1 sky region observed in visible wavelengths by VLT/ESO, in infrared by the NASA satellite Spitzer, and in X-rays by ESA's XMM-Newton. Four sources are chosen: a starbust galaxy, a passive galaxy, and two AGNs. Simplified procedures built on 'professional astrophysical data analys is softwares, such as MIDAS of the European Southern Observatory or, in recent ye ars, Python codes' let students work directly on the sources, measuring flux, distance, redshift, luminosity and number of stars.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simplified procedures based on professional astrophysical data analysis softwares so students can work directly on the sources
#### Constraints
- The flux of the first source was previously measured by the researchers; participants discover it by clicking on the image of the source

### Target Learners
- high school students aged 16-18 in university outreach activities

### Learning Goals
- characterizing astrophysical sources by measuring flux, distance, redshift, luminosity and mass
- identifying an active galactic nucleus powered by an accreting supermassive black hole
