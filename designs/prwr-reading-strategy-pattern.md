# PRWR: a four-phase preview-read-write-recite pattern embedded in a three-phase teaching framework with scaffolding questions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
PRWR organizes academic reading into four phases — preview, read, write, and recite — delivered through a pre, whilst, and post teaching framework in which "several questions or questioning were deployed" in each phase to scaffold comprehension. Preview activates content and formal schemata via title, headings, and visuals; reading focuses on micro skills such as context clues, main ideas, and factual information with underlining and marking; writing produces a summary of key points; reciting restates the summary using recall words. The article presents detailed syntax tables of activities and guide-questions for each phase.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher-deployed questions in each phase to scaffold comprehension of academic texts
- Guide-questions targeting micro reading skills (context clues, denotation and connotation, topic, main idea and supporting details, factual information)
#### Constraints
- Studied at the tertiary level with non-English department students; the article notes limited prior PRWR research at this level

### Target Learners
- non-English department university students (EFL sophomores)

### Learning Goals
- academic content-area reading comprehension in terms of word attack skills and text attack skills
