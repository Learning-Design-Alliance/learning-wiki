# Randomization of inputs as a method for stochastic physics exercises

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents a method for incorporating a stochastic point of view into physics exercises of mathematics education. The core of the method is the randomization of some inputs, while the system model used does not differ from what would be used in the deterministic approach. Exercises from projectile motion and statics demonstrate the method, with outputs treated as random variables whose probability distributions, expected values, variances, and relative standard deviations are determined.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students and teachers familiar with elementary probability theory and mechanics
#### Constraints
- In most exercises the distribution of random inputs is a uniform distribution for simplicity

### Target Learners
- high school students in specialized mathematics classes
- university students who took a course on basic probability theory

### Learning Goals
- understanding basic concepts of stochastic mechanics
- recognizing the random nature of everyday phenomena

### Claims

- [Randomized projectile motion exercises yield non-uniform output distributions even with uniform inputs](../claims/uniform-inputs-nonuniform-outputs-projectile.md) [+W]
