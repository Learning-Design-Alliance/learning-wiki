# PBLCS: problem-based learning in a cooperative situation as a student-centered learning model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
PBLCS combines problem-based learning with cooperative learning as a student-centered model in which "the teacher gives the problem to the students" and "teachers guide students to solve the problem, either as individual or as a team". It is grounded by the authors in constructivism (Piaget, Vygotsky, including ZPD), Dewey's democratic class theory, and humanistic theory. The article applies it with a dedicated PBLCS module to mathematics teaching on differential functions, expecting gains in motivation, independence, self-confidence and social relationships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who poses problems and guides rather than providing final answers
- Cooperative teamwork among students on real problems
- An appropriate PBLCS module
#### Constraints
- The article applies it in senior high school mathematics, specifically the differential function topic

### Target Learners
- senior high school (class XI IPA) students

### Learning Goals
- mathematics conceptual mastery (differential functions)
- interpersonal and intrapersonal intelligence development
