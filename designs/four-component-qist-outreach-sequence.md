# Four-component quantum information outreach sequence: lecture, formalism practice, simulator practice, algorithm game

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The outreach program is structured as four sequenced components: "Overview of QM and QIST (lecture)", "Practice with formalism from 'Q is for Quantum' (hands-on activity)", "Practice with IBM Q experience (hands-on activity)", and "Solution of a game based on a quantum algorithm (hands-on activity)". The lecture builds conceptual background, the QI4Q formalism gives students a calculational tool, IBM Q sessions let them test predictions on simulators and hardware, and the game introduces quantum algorithms. The authors report the most comprehensive version was employed over a two-day event in the CTech2 camp.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students create IBM Q Experience accounts before coming to the program, partly because of IBM age restrictions on users
#### Constraints
- The approach is not intended to replace proper, linear-algebra-based treatments of QIST

### Target Learners
- high-school students
- early undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- understanding superposition, entanglement and measurement
- understanding what a quantum algorithm is
