# Pencil-and-paper electronics practicum: a hands-on, low-cost experimental pattern for teaching basic physical electronics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A lesson/lab pattern in which students draw graphite conductive traces on standard office printer paper with soft B-type pencils and characterize them with a handheld multimeter. The authors state the approach "does not require expensive tools or materials, and it can be easily adapted to suit a wide range of student skill levels," and is used to teach basic circuit theory, sensors, and signal processing through three experiments: sheet-resistance measurement, a strain gauge, and a temperature sensor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Everyday stationery items: standard office printer paper, at least two pencils with very different B grades (e.g. 2B and 6B), ruler, scissors, adhesive tape, and a handheld multimeter
- Optional items the article lists: a printer for guiding-line outlines, magnets and double-sided tape for stable loading, wire with copper and Kapton tape for contacts
#### Constraints
- The article notes stochastic sample-to-sample deviations arise because the trace is a network of interconnected graphite flakes, so results vary across samples drawn with the same pencil

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- basic principles of electronics and sensors
- electrical transport concepts
- data analysis and linear regression fitting
