# Argument visualization: tree diagrams exposing the logical structure of argumentative texts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Argument visualizations are tree diagrams that illustrate the logical relations in argumentative text. "Claims, the sentences contained in white boxes, are the fundamental units of argument visualizations", grouped beneath horizontal green brackets labeled "because" (reasons, which aim to raise confidence in a conclusion) or red brackets labeled "however" (objections). Their "hierarchical layout is intended to re ﬂect the hierarchical structure of real arguments", with color and grouping cues binding elements that share a common function; dashed borders mark premises that remain implicit in the text. In the seminar, students produced such visualizations of real scholarly texts as the medium for analysis, discussion, feedback, and revision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training uses real academic texts rather than highly simplified arguments
- Timely, detailed, individualized instructor feedback on students' visualizations
- Students actively generate their own representations rather than receiving explanations of the readings
#### Constraints
- The present findings do not disentangle the contribution of visualization itself from the intensive, interactive course and its explicit focus on argument analysis

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students, primarily first-year students at a selective university

### Learning Goals
- analytical reasoning
- parsing argumentative texts into logical components
- constructing and evaluating written arguments
