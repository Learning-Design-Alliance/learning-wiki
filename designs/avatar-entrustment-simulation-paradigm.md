# Avatar-based ad hoc entrustment simulation with 36 clinical scenarios and FACSGen-animated expressions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A cost-effective online simulation paradigm for studying entrustment decisions: participants read 36 narrative clinical entrustment scenarios in randomized order and viewed two 5-second avatar videos per scenario, deciding dichotomously whether to entrust the task and rating trustworthiness on a 4-point Likert scale (0 to 3). "The trustees were represented by avatars. After each scenario, the participants viewed two 5-second videos of these avatars." Facial expressions (Duchenne smile, neutral) were standardized using FACSGen action units; 18 female and 18 male avatars were each shown once per expression.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized facial expressions animated via Facial Action Coding System action units for clarity and reproducibility.
#### Constraints
- The authors note avatar realism was limited by FACS compatibility restrictions, which limited reproducibility and standardization of expressions; only avatars' heads were designed to focus on facial expressions.

### Target Learners
- medical students (second and fourth semester)

### Learning Goals
- ad hoc entrustment decision-making within the entrustable professional activities framework
