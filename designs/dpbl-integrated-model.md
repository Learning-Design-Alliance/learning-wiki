# Direct Problem-Based Learning (DPBL) integrated model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
DPBL is a conceptual framework combining direct instruction with problem-based learning activities, designed to improve students' knowledge, creative thinking, and teamwork skills. The article describes it as "a combination of the Direct Instruction and Problem-Based Learning activities" and structures it in three phases covering introduction and identification, application and solution, and evaluation and conclusion. It was modified from the UTM PBL model and applied to a third-year Computer Networks subject.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers take the role of presenting theory and objectives, presenting the problem, and monitoring group activities while students work in small groups
#### Constraints
- The article considers the approach appropriate for the high levels of education

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate Computer Networks students

### Learning Goals
- knowledge
- creative thinking skills
