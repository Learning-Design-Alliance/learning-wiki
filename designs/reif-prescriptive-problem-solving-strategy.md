# Reif's prescriptive problem-solving strategy as a standard for expert-like problem solving

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article adopts Reif's prescriptive problem-solving method as the standard against which example-solution features are measured. It is a prescriptive instructional model explicating an expert-like approach, comprising "three major components" of problem analysis, plan-explicit solution construction, and answer checking. The study's feature clusters C1-C3 map onto these stages, and example solution III was designed to reflect this systematic decision-making process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors explicitly model the strategy, require students to follow it, and coach them while doing so, per the cognitive apprenticeship approach the article describes
#### Constraints
- The article notes prescriptive strategies reflect only some aspects of actual expert problem solving and leave out aspects such as back-and-forth moves where the solver reconsiders their approach

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Learning Goals
- developing an expert-like problem-solving approach
