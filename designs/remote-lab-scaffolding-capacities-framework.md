# Scaffolding capacities required for an educational-oriented remote laboratory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines a set of pedagogical capabilities that remote laboratory environments must provide to effectively support practical learning: a common view of the experiment, shared control over remote experiments, synchronous communication tools, social/artifact awareness tools, learning analytics for tutoring, and a user-friendly authoring tool. The authors motivate this with the claim that "the scaffolding around the lab may be at least as important as the lab itself" and with a survey where students pointed to help and support and engagement. They use the framework to evaluate six existing remote lab projects, concluding they "do not focus on pedagogical concerns".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The remote lab environment should give learning actors access to a common view of the experiments and shared control, plus synchronous communication, awareness, learning analytics and authoring capacities
#### Constraints
- Existing projects studied (iLab, LiLa, LabShare, WebLab-Deusto, Lab2Go, Go-Lab) partially or fully lack most of these capacities, and none takes experiment instructional design into account

### Target Learners
- distant learners in STEM higher education

### Learning Goals
- inquiry skills and practical disciplinary skills through remote laboratory work
