# Task-based learning in alternating practice and study blocks integrating theory into workplace-based clinical training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A curriculum pattern in which students alternate four-week practice blocks, focused on learning in actual patient cases, with four-week study blocks providing systematic knowledge in seminars and lectures. "This educational format, rooted in the so-called „Task-based Learning", supports the acquisition of clinical knowledge and funds the student's self-responsibility." Starting from a patient's complaint, students elaborate the underlying disease, differential diagnostics and therapy, deepening basic-science knowledge, with the last week of each study block free for individual study and closed by an interdisciplinary examination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured clinical practice training with bedside teaching, study guides, case documentation and assessment support, in contrast to unstructured clerkships
#### Constraints
- Within the ten four-week practice blocks, the students complete the internships that are required by the ÄAppO

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in years 3–5

### Learning Goals
- clinical expertise
- integration of theory into practice
- self-responsible learning
