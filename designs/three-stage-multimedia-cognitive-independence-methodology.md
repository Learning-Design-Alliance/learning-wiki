# Three-stage methodology for developing cognitive independence of future Informatics teachers through multimedia tools

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a staged methodology in which the role of multimedia expands across a theoretical stage (semesters 3-4), an integrating stage (semesters 5-6), and a practical stage (semesters 7-8). At the initial stage multimedia is "a means of visual presentation of educational material", at the integrating stage students learn to create multimedia tools, and at the practical stage they "carry out creative multimedia projects, both group and individual ones". The methodology aims to deepen multimedia knowledge, shape positive attitudes, and develop skills to create and apply multimedia products in teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Purposeful, consistent work across all three stages of university study, with updated course topics that merge into existing curricula without changing total hours
#### Constraints
- Developed and tested specifically for future Informatics teachers within a credit-based education system at one university

### Target Learners
- Future Informatics teachers (undergraduate students)

### Learning Goals
- Cognitive independence across informative, operational, motivational, emotional-volitional, and reflexive components
