# Naturalistic in-school sleep intervention pattern: weekly randomized cross-over naps after regular teacher-led lessons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern embeds a nap intervention in ordinary schooling: curricular content is taught by the regular class teacher, students are "randomly sorted into sleep and non-sleep sets of equal size" each week, and each student experiences all conditions. Retention is measured days later with teacher-normed multiple-choice tests. The design bypasses experimenter-novelty confounds and between-group differences that limited prior school nap studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A scheduled slot after a morning class, a quiet dark room with mats and eye masks, and curricular content divided into equivalent parts verified on an independent sample
#### Constraints
- The authors did not monitor rest/activity (actigraphy or EEG) and did not assess sleep inertia at awakening because the school schedule ended at 12:00

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade students in a public school

### Learning Goals
- retention of declarative curricular content in Science and History
