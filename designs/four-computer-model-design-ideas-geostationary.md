# Four computer model design ideas for effective physics simulations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates four design ideas the authors believe raised the effectiveness and usefulness of the simulation for students' active learning: "1) simple and realistic 3D view and associated learning to real world, 2) comparative visualization of a permanent geostationary satellite 3) examples of non-geostationary orbits of different 3-1) rotation sense, 3-2) periods and 3-3) plane and 4) intentionally incorrect physics model [16] for conceptual discourse." These ideas shape what students can see and compare in the model, from realistic Earth textures to deliberately wrong orbits that challenge reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The design ideas were implemented in the EJS model using Java 3D, texture maps, and checkbox-controlled reference objects.
#### Constraints
- The ideas were developed and trialed in the context of junior college geostationary orbit lessons; the article does not claim generalizability beyond that setting.

### Target Learners
- junior college physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding geostationary orbit conditions and reasoning about incorrect physics
