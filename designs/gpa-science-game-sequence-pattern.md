# Game-based physical activity plans integrating science outcomes from simple to complex across a five-week unit

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lesson-level design in which the outcomes of a science unit are mapped onto a sequence of game-based physical activities, integrated "from simple to complex" across five weeks. Twelve games (traditional, curriculum-based, and researcher-designed or adapted) each carried one or more science outcomes, and separate lesson plans were prepared for each timing strategy, yielding 36 plans in total. Safety outcome 4 was embedded in every plan because of the risk inherent in physical play.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games whose rules are easily comprehended by students and that include an emphasis of competition, per the pilot study feedback
- A suitable physical setting such as a school garden with game materials
#### Constraints
- Generalizability stated to hold only if the physical conditions (school garden with hoop, lines, materials) are met
- Students resisted returning indoors after outdoor play, so concept discussion was built into the outdoor lesson

### Target Learners
- elementary school third-grade students (about nine years old)

### Learning Goals
- science concepts of the unit Let's Learn about Force (motion, push and pull forces)
