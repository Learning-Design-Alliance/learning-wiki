# Riccioli and his Almagestum Novum as a teaching resource on how science works

## Description
Giovanni Battista Riccioli (1598-1671) and his 1651 Almagestum Novum serve here as instructional material about the nature of science. The Almagestum Novum contains Riccioli's moon map establishing modern lunar nomenclature, his measurement of the acceleration due to gravity ("9.6 m/s2"), and his 77 arguments against heliocentrism, most of which he himself dismissed as without merit. It is available online, and an English summary of the arguments exists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the historical sources: the Almagestum Novum is available online, and an English summary of the 77 arguments is available on the physics arXiv
#### Constraints
- Riccioli's anti-Copernican conclusion was wrong; the Coriolis effect and the Airy Disk were not understood until the early 19th century, more than 150 years after the Almagestum Novum

### Target Learners
- physics students
- introductory astronomy students

### Learning Goals
- understanding the nature of science
- understanding the Copernican Revolution and the evaluation of evidence
