# Project-based course structure pairing real science/engineering problems with algorithm implementation on shared HPC infrastructure

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course is organized around programming projects drawn from a textbook, where students identify and formulate engineering problems via mathematical modeling, solve them with numerical methods, and design computer programs. A "uniform computing environment for all students" on the Arkansas High Performance Computing Center removes installation burdens and lets students "easily switch between different compilers" and use third-party packages. Performance assessments are based on 60% assignments and 40% final project, with final projects chosen to match student research interests and presented visually, verbally, and in writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a shared high-performance computing environment with educational allocations; a project textbook with interdependent chapters sequenced by prerequisites
#### Constraints
- Content and pace adjusted to what typical undergraduate students can handle; Chapter 26 only briefly introduced at semester's end

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate students in science, engineering, and mathematics

### Learning Goals
- formulating problems as mathematical models
- implementing numerical algorithms in C
- scientific programming and presentation skills
