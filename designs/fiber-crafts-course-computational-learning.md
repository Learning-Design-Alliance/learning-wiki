# Fiber crafts course (weaving and fabric manipulation) as a context for computational learning with middle school youth

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 6-session fiber crafts course, facilitated twice at a K-8 charter school, offered weaving with three loom types and fabric manipulation with a laser-cut matrix template as contexts for performing computational concepts. "The participants of the course were 16 middle school students between 11 and 14 years old," self-selected via an interest survey. The crafts were chosen because they are matrix-based, connected historically to computing, and require routine movements that perform algorithms such as loops and conditional pairings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-warped looms and prepared materials (laser-cut grid template, threads, needles) so sessions could focus on production
- Facilitators familiar with the crafts and with the study's purpose regarding computing-crafting connections
#### Constraints
- Facilitators did not ask students to recognize the crafting tools and materials as co-actors
- Soft-robotic actuator animation sessions were excluded from this analysis

### Target Learners
- seventh- and eighth-grade students in a maker-centered school program

### Learning Goals
- performing computational concepts (loops, variables, conditionals) through physical crafting
