# Anyspeed motion airtrack lab pattern: spring-launched gliders with pre/post-collision gate pairs and traveling clock

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article specifies an instructional laboratory configuration: a spring compressed by distance x0 transfers kinetic energy K0 to a glider, which crosses gate-pair A (measured by lab clock tA and traveling clock τA), then collides with and sticks to a second glider before crossing gate-pair B (tB, τB). Using kinetic energy as the independent variable is chosen for consistency with trends toward introducing conservation of energy early and with AAAS benchmarks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A compressable spring, two gliders, a moving clock and two gate pairs with timers (pre and post collision)
#### Constraints
- Conditions chosen so glider motion is essentially frictionless; AAAS benchmarks treat energy as a major exception to the principle that students should understand ideas before being given labels for them

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students; explorers of any age

### Learning Goals
- discover patterns in motion data
- test models such as Newton's laws and relativistic metric relations
