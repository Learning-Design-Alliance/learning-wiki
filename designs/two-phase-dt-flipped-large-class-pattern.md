# Two-phase design-thinking flipped classroom pattern for large classes: teachers design via empathy-define-ideate-prototype-test, then students redesign through the same five steps

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern embeds Stanford design thinking into a large-class flipped classroom in two phases. In the design phase teachers act as designers of the instructional plan, using empathy maps with students to identify pain points such as "insufficient interaction, limitations on the development of students' higher-order thinking and emotional abilities, and lack of personalized guidance." In the implementation phase students become designers whose product is a presentation of a knowledge point, advancing through Empathy, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test over a seven-day preparation period with in-class presentations, peer commentary, and voting.
