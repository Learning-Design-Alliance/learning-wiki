# Nine-task at-home laparoscopic curriculum with embedded asynchronous feedback and end-of-curriculum assessment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curriculum of nine sequenced tasks (peg transfer, running the bowel, pattern cutting, needle loading, and suturing variants) derived from the FLS and Lapp curricula and developed following Kern's six-step approach. Residents practiced at home, submitted task videos every two weeks, and received "asynchronous feedback to residents about task performance using the rubrics and additional free-form written comments," followed by an in-person end-of-curriculum assessment and debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty members with laparoscopic experience to provide feedback and facilitate the assessment
- A skills-center staff member to assemble and distribute materials and track task completion timelines
- At-home equipment including box trainers and task materials, distributed to each resident
#### Constraints
- Implemented at a single large academic medical center; most likely to succeed at similar programs with laparoscopic surgeons to drive implementation and feedback
- Programs must determine whether schedules and duty hours permit at-home practice
- Rubrics are newly created with limited validity evidence; developed primarily for formative feedback

### Target Learners
- First- to third-year residents in general surgery, obstetrics/gynecology, and urology

### Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic instrument coordination with dominant and nondominant hands
- Retraction and tension with the nondominant hand
- Intracorporeal and extracorporeal laparoscopic suturing and knot tying
