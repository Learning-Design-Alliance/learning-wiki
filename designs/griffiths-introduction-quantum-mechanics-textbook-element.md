# Griffiths' Introduction to Quantum Mechanics as the shared main textbook, with supplementary materials including interactive java-applet software

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
All five interviewed faculty members used Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (Griffiths, 2nd edition, Pearson Prentice Hall) as the main course textbook; some added supplementary materials such as Townsend's A Modern Approach to Quantum Mechanics, volume 3 of the Feynman Lectures, or Phyla Quantum Physics, an interactive book with java applets of visual quantum mechanics concepts. Faculty judged the textbook well written and engaging but lacking instruction, examples, and explanations of concepts, and assessments across the courses were very similar, typically two midterms and one final.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Used as the main textbook for junior/senior-level quantum mechanics or modern physics courses
#### Constraints
- Faculty reported it lacks instruction, examples, and explanations of the concepts, and is very peculiar, deep, and hard compared to other QM textbooks

### Target Learners
- junior and senior undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual and problem-solving understanding of quantum mechanics
