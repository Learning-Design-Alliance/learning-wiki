# 20-hour online self-instructional basic life support course built with ADDIE on the Moodle platform

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article developed and administered an online self-instructional basic life support course for undergraduate nursing students. It used "a 20 h workload using the ADDIE (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) instructional design model", with educational objectives and teaching strategies based on Bloom's Taxonomy and Andragogy, and was "implemented using the Moodle platform". Students received logins to a virtual environment with a virtual class and theoretical testing, and were supervised by nurses specialising in Distance Education and Emergency. The course was favourably viewed by participants and specialists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning and organization with defined educational objectives, content relevant to the participant profile, and distribution of the workload, as the article states for online courses
- Access to the Moodle virtual environment and logins for students; supervision by nurses with Distance Education and Emergency experience
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a quasi-experimental design with a convenience sample at a single public university in São Paulo, Brazil
- A university strike and shutdown may have overloaded students and made participation difficult, per the authors' stated limitation

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students (1st–4th year) at a public university

### Learning Goals
- Theoretical knowledge of basic life support
- Practical BLS and CPR skills including AED use in a simulated cardiac arrest scenario
