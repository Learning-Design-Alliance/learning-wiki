# Weekly case-based ethics education combining active learning, a structured decision-making model, and corrective feedback, delivered in either an ethical or an accounting context

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A semester-long instructional pattern in which each week the instructor introduces a case, students spend approximately 15 minutes discussing it, complete a seven-step decision-making model adapted from Langenderfer and Rockness (1989), and conclude with classroom discussion of recommendations. The article states the process "incorporates active learning, a structured decision-making model, and corrective feedback." The same case fact patterns were used in both contexts; the ethical context added the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct and labeled cases "Ethics Case," emphasizing Steps 2, 3, 5, and 7, while the accounting context emphasized Steps 1, 4, 6, and 7.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A new case introduced and discussed each week for 12 weeks, with student discussion time and completion of the seven-step decision-making model
- Corrective feedback through concluding classroom discussion in which students present their recommendations
#### Constraints
- The article notes a pretest was foregone to avoid sensitizing participants, and students were not randomly assigned to conditions across semesters

### Target Learners
- undergraduate accounting majors in the first Intermediate Accounting course

### Learning Goals
- ethical sensitivity to subtle ethical issues in accounting practice
- moral reasoning in professional accounting judgment
