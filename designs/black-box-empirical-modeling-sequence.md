# Black-box data-driven modeling sequence: construct, test, then interpret an empirical model before theory is introduced

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This lesson-level pattern postpones the thermodynamic description while students construct an empirical model from their own data. Students treat the system as a black box, infer relationships among heating time, power, water mass, and temperature rise, and rescale until the data collapse; only after the model is constructed and tested is theory introduced. The article states that "This black -box framing should not be interpreted as unguided discovery" — students receive structured guidance on apparatus, data recording, condition selection, and analysis, with instructor-mediated discussion supporting interpretation. The sequence supports three successive tasks: empirical model construction, thermodynamic interpretation, and separation of component contributions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured experimental and analytical guidance outlining the sequence of measurements, graphing, and rescaling
- Instructor-mediated questioning responsive to students' evolving interpretations
- Additional experimental conditions not used in model construction for a predictive test
#### Constraints
- The article notes the approach was implemented with water masses from 170 to 240 g and heater powers from 9 to 25 W, limited by immersion requirements
- No controlled assessment of student learning outcomes was provided

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in an introductory physics laboratory course, working in groups of two or three

### Learning Goals
- constructing and evaluating empirical models from experimental data
- identifying relevant variables and judging the domain of validity of relationships
- connecting empirical models to thermodynamic theory
