# Network structure: clusters of at least three like-minded schools aided by an external partner, restructuring time, size, and isolation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Chicago Annenberg Challenge's core design pattern required schools to apply not individually but in networks—clusters of at least three like-minded schools aided by an external partner—to enhance collaboration and community support. Grantees were asked to focus on how they might "alter the basic elements of time, size, and isolation": reorganizing the school day, creating smaller personalized environments, and connecting educators to the communities they serve. External partners were intended to provide community resources, develop the pool of assisting organizations, and build adult leadership at schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- At least three schools per cluster; an external partner for each network; a rich invitational application process
#### Constraints
- Funds were stipulated not to go to the CPS central administration; the pattern was grounded in the 1988 decentralization context

### Target Learners
- students and educators in Chicago Public Schools network schools

### Learning Goals
- school restructuring to improve students' opportunities to learn through collaboration and community support
