# Three-phase clinical simulation framework: preparation, participation and debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review describes clinical simulation in health as an educational strategy that exposes participants to hypothetical scenarios mimicking clinical practice, structured in three phases. In its words, "its phases, called preparation, participation and debriefing, must be conceptualized and understood". Preparation is divided into pre-simulation (educational materials and skills training) and pre-briefing/briefing (facilitator-participant interaction with clear scenarios, objectives and learning roles); participation involves carrying out the proposed scenario; debriefing is a discussion/reflection process during or after the scenario. The review applies this framework to caregiver training for hospital discharge, arguing that simulation "planned and executed in three stages enables the teaching and learning process of complex subjects".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preparation phase must be conceptualized and understood, including delivery of educational materials and skills training before the scenario
- Debriefing must occur during or after the scenario as a discussion/reflection process
#### Constraints
- The article notes financial, structural and organizational challenges, including caregivers' literacy level and their emotional and affective states, which influence learning

### Target Learners
- Informal family caregivers of patients with chronic conditions being discharged from hospital

### Learning Goals
- Technical care skills (e.g., tracheostomy, oxygen therapy, drainage management) and non-technical skills such as confidence, decision-making and managing anxiety for home care
