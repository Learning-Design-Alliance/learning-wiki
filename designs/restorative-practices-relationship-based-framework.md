# Restorative practices as a relationship-based, nonpunitive approach to school discipline

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Restorative practices (RPs) are a school discipline approach centered on the belief that individuals are connected through a web of relationships, and that when harm occurs those relationships are damaged and need repair. The article describes RPs as "a nonpunitive approach to dealing with conflict" and "a means of building community", grounded in accountability based on collective, fair processes and relationships rather than top-down compulsory control. School implementations include weekly community-building circles, restorative conferences, and problem-solving circles using structured questions such as "What happened?" and "What do you think you need to do to make things right?". The theory holds that relationship-building (prevention) and problem-solving (intervention) support student social emotional learning, prosocial engagement, and conflict resolution, which in turn should reduce suspensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation requires community-building structures such as weekly circles in small groups, restorative conferences when conflicts arise, and staff trained in restorative mindsets
#### Constraints
- The article notes no one single definition of RPs exists in the literature, and the experimental evidence base on RP effects on suspensions was extremely limited before this trial

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in urban public schools

### Learning Goals
- Reducing exclusionary discipline and suspension disparities
- Building student-teacher relationships and social emotional learning
- Conflict resolution and prosocial community engagement
