# Build preventive and therapeutic programs against academic stress, promoting resilience and positive emotions, early in medical education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that because psychosocial variables associate with poor performance and stress effects appear cumulative across the academic cycle, educators should "construct and apply early in medical education preventive and therapeutic programs against academic stress and promoting resilience and positive emotions". These can be embedded in the curriculum or delivered by counselors and tutors, and the authors judge them more cost-efficient than treating existing burnout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery through the medical curriculum itself or with the help of counselors and tutors, as the article states
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional study with a limited, self-selected, female-skewed sample; the authors call for multicenter prospective studies
