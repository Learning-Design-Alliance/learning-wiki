# Tiered assessment as differentiated assessment with optional advanced tasks (challenge by choice)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Tiered assessment is a differentiated assessment strategy in which all students attempt a lowest tier of assessment tasks and higher tiers are optional. The article states it is "a form of differentiated assessment [1] where students can attempt a series of assessment tasks, usually of increasing difficulty", with the lowest tier sized so that successful completion is a passing score and higher tiers allow demonstration of higher-level learning objectives. The core mechanism, "challenge by choice", makes advanced tasks optional to avoid inflating struggling students' workload and anxiety while keeping higher performers challenged. The authors apply this framework in upper-level undergraduate physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The lowest tier should allow students to demonstrate sufficient foundational knowledge, with successful completion of this tier a passing score
- Advanced tasks should allow higher-performing students to be challenged at an appropriate level
- The aggregation method must track achievement on the different tiers and communicate progress to students
#### Constraints
- Attention to date has largely been in K–12 education, especially special education; relatively little concerns higher education physics

### Target Learners
- Second and third year undergraduate physics (electromagnetics) students with a diverse range of student ability

### Learning Goals
- Demonstrating foundational knowledge and application of fundamental concepts at pass level
- Demonstrating higher-level achievement such as originality and insight through advanced tasks
