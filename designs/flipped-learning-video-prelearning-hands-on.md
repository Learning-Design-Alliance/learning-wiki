# Flipped learning as video pre-learning followed by in-class hands-on training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article defines flipped learning as "a pedagogical approach in which the instructor-led lecture is done prior to class, while homework or assigned problems are done in the scheduled class". In this study it was operationalized as watching a 15-minute Japanese Red Cross Society video one week before a 30-minute hands-on session with discussion and question answering, with no lecture provided. Learners can view content "as many times as necessary to master the knowledge at any time and at their own pace", enabling student-centered in-class practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have access to the internet and an existing instructional video, or instructors must produce one
- In-class time is used for discussion, question answering, and hands-on practice rather than lecture
#### Constraints
- In this study it was unclear how much or how deeply participants learned from the video since no log-in system was used

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students
- potentially laypersons learning bystander CPR

### Learning Goals
- bystander CPR psychomotor skills and key elements of resuscitation
