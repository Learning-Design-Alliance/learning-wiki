# Universe Sandbox VR Moon-phase activity with six degrees of freedom, a closer Moon, and a buddy-system deployment

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An immersive Moon-phases-and-eclipses activity built on the free VR package Universe Sandbox 2, offering "six degrees of freedom ('6-dof'), meaning 3 axes of rotation plus 3 directions of translation" so students can change viewpoint and fly to different locations. A deliberately non-authentic "closer Moon" was created so students could see phases and shadows and create an eclipse by grabbing the Moon with HTC Vive hand controllers. Deployment used a buddy system where paired students alternated wearing the headset, reading instructions, recording results, and monitoring the headset view on a large 2D display, in the campus library BuildIT MakerSpace, with a pre-lab introductory video added from earlier feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Headset and hand-controller equipment with space in the campus library BuildIT MakerSpace so students had access most of the time
- A buddy-system pairing in which the non-wearing partner reads instructions, records results, and monitors the headset view on a large 2D display
- A pre-lab introductory video describing the headset and hand controllers
#### Constraints
- Keeping the Sun-Earth-Moon system at accurate relative scale proved awkward educationally because the angular sizes of the Sun and Moon are too small compared to the full field of view; a closer Moon was used instead

### Target Learners
- General education introductory astronomy (Astro 101) lecture and lab students

### Learning Goals
- Understanding Moon phases and eclipses from different vantage points, including seeing the phases of the Earth from the Moon
