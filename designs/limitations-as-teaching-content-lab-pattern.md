# Laboratory exercise pattern that uses a measurement method's practical limitations as explicit teaching content on the divergence of practice from theory.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a laboratory exercise design in which the known accuracy problems of the three ammeter and three voltmeter methods are not hidden from students but become the lesson itself. The authors state that the methods' "downsides and limitations can be used as a part of the teaching process in educating students on the divergence of the practical from the theoretical". The pattern spans pre-experiment preparation, the experiment itself, and post-experiment simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The exercise's measuring methods must have stated, analyzable accuracy limitations that students can explore.
#### Constraints
- The limitation topics are tied to this specific power-factor laboratory exercise in a Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering course at Zagreb University of Applied Sciences.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in professional study of electrical engineering

### Learning Goals
- understanding the difference between theoretical results and practical application of measurements
