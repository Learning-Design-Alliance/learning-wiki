# Preserve traditional blackboard lectures by streaming them at high quality to online students while keeping bidirectional communication and recorded lectures as backup

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is to preserve the quality of traditional blackboard lectures during pandemic restrictions rather than replace them with a new pedagogy: lectures are held live for up to 50% of students on site and transmitted via low-bandwidth full HD screen sharing to students at home. Bidirectional communication is an integral part, allowing online students to communicate directly with the professor and with on-site students via an airlink or speakerphone. Most lectures were also recorded and made available via the Moodle–Kaltura platform; the article states "the possibility of recording lectures may lead to an added value to the learning experience" by letting synchronous attendees review particular parts when studying autonomously.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- high-quality view of the blackboard and professor combined with high audio quality as the two most important pillars
- bidirectional communication so online students can ask questions and participate in open discussions
#### Constraints
- the concept deliberately does not present a novel pedagogical concept of how to teach mathematics
- stringent requirements on hardware and software and on professors' technological skills are the stated shortcomings

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students following lectures online or on site

### Learning Goals
- preserving the quality of traditional blackboard teaching of mathematics
- enabling online students an experience as close to a real classroom experience as possible
