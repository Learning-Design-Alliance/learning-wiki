# Behavioural architecture: an AI-in-education design that adapts to how a student learns, not only to what they learn next

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper proposes behavioural architecture as an alternative to AI bans and content-only tutors. In it, "the system adapts to how a student learns, not only to what they learn next", addressing personality, motivation and emotion surfaced through conversation rather than inferred from hidden behavioural traces. The student co-authors the record the system keeps, and the designer role — what the system treats as true about the student — shifts from the AI vendor alone to a distribution among educator, student and system.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A baseline pathway set by the instructor is needed as a reference for adaptation; without it, there is nothing to adapt from.
- The system's understanding of the student is built once, early in the student's education, and refined continuously over months, not within a single course window.
#### Constraints
- The system must not draw psychological conclusions from learning patterns; meaningful consent to psychological labelling is unavailable inside an institution that holds power over the student's academic future.
- The architecture has not been empirically tested; the paper states the research base needed to determine what makes it pedagogically safe or harmful does not yet exist.

### Target Learners
- Students in formal education using AI-mediated learning systems across courses and years

### Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement and learning outcomes shaped by personality, motivation and emotional state, alongside content mastery
