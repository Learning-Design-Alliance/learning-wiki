# Physics by Inquiry with computer simulation as an active-learning referent for making sense of Newtonian mechanics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's explanatory framework is that students' commonsense beliefs about motion with forces persist because they lack first-person experience and "real-life referents". It argues "computer simulation could be an appropriate substitute for active learning referents, provided simulations are carefully developed, used in appropriate context, aided with challenging inquiry activities and facilitated by teachers who believe in the effectiveness of the tool". Under this account, the constant-velocity computer model coupled with the PbI worksheet allowed students to 'make sense' of and experience the 1st Law.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Carefully developed simulations, use in appropriate context, challenging inquiry activities, and facilitation by teachers who believe in the tool's effectiveness.
#### Constraints
- In this study the account explains the 1st Law gain; the same design did not yield a significant 3rd Law advantage, which the authors attribute to visualization design rather than the framework as a whole.

### Target Learners
- Junior college physics students with commonsense (mis)conceptions about motion with forces

### Learning Goals
- Conceptual change on Newton's 1st and 3rd Laws
