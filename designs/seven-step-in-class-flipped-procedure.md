# Seven-step in-class flipped classroom procedure for nursing skills lab courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A lesson-level instructional pattern designed according to Merrill's first principle of instruction, whose principles are "problem-centered" and comprise a cycle of activation, demonstration, application, and integration. In the current study, the instructional procedures of the in-class flipped classroom comprised seven steps during a 160-minute skills lab course: distributing videos and materials via Rain Classroom, group self-directed learning, targeted live demonstrations, guided re-practice, scenario simulation with peer and instructor evaluation and guided reflection, and post-class review materials and experiment reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students grouped within each experimental class into small teams of 3 to 4 students, each with 1 leader, and the Rain Classroom platform for distributing materials and addressing questions online.
#### Constraints
- Described and evaluated within a 160-minute pediatric nursing skills lab course; time allocation may need modification in other courses.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- pediatric nursing clinical skills
- transition from mechanical imitation to clinical competence
