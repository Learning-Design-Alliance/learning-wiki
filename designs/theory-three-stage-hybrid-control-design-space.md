# A three-stage design space for hybrid control in dynamic pairing: before, during, and after pairing up.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes the dynamic pairing process into a design space of control distribution across three stages: "Stage 1—Before Pairing Up, Stage 2—During Pairing Up(pairing, collaboration, and unpairing), andStage 3—After Pairing Up." Across these stages, the authors identified seven hybrid-control design challenges formulated as How Might We questions. This theory frames how agency and control can be distributed between teachers and students at each moment of pairing, from preparation and timing to transitions and future pairing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom context where students transition between individual tutoring and collaborative peer tutoring.
#### Constraints
- The design space was derived from teacher and student participants in algebra equation-solving contexts.

### Target Learners
- K-12 mathematics students
- secondary mathematics teachers

### Learning Goals
- classroom orchestration of collaborative learning
- student agency in pairing decisions
