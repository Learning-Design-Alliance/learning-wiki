# Leadership as enacted behaviors and a developmental process shaping team non-technical skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study frames leadership in two complementary ways: as "a set of behaviors enacted by healthcare leaders" and as "a developmental process in which competencies are built over time through continuous learning." It draws on team-centric, transformational, and task- versus person-focused leadership theory, and applies this framing to explain how leadership fosters non-technical skills such as communication, teamwork, and role clarity in hierarchical, resource-constrained LMIC healthcare settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leadership must be understood and enacted at both formal and informal levels within healthcare teams
#### Constraints
- The framework was developed and interpreted within Balkan LMIC healthcare contexts characterized by hierarchical structures and limited resources

### Target Learners
- healthcare leaders (physicians and nurses) in LMIC university hospital clinics

### Learning Goals
- developing non-technical skills including communication, teamwork, role clarity, and leadership competencies
