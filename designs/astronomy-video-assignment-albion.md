# Astronomy video assignment with rubric, peer and instructor grading, and class viewing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A course activity in which pairs of students create a 5:30 ± 30-second video on an astronomy topic of their choice, combining photographs, video, animation, sound, music, and text — what the author calls "digital storytelling." Videos require a title, at least three credible references (not Wikipedia as primary source), and posting online; grading averages classmates' evaluations (33%) with the instructor's (67%) and counts 10% of the course grade, using criteria of content, science, delivery, scientific accuracy, and adherence to requirements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A title and a reference list with at least three credible sources, also submitted as a hard or electronic copy
- Videos posted on youtube.com, the college public drive, or a shared Google drive and tested on classroom equipment
- Attendance at all class viewing days and participation in evaluating classmates' videos
#### Constraints
- Videos over 6 minutes are turned off and not considered in viewing or grading
- A 10% grade deduction applies for not adhering to requirements, including non-participation in evaluation or absence from viewing

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-science majors in an introductory Astronomy survey course

### Learning Goals
- in-depth investigation of a self-chosen astronomy topic
- scientific accuracy and synthesis in a creative product
- constructive peer critique and collaboration
