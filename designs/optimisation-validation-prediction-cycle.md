# Three-stage theoretical modelling cycle: optimisation, validation and prediction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A reusable activity sequence in which students move through the three stages of theoretical modelling with a simple formula and real mass data: 'Using the worksheets, students can visually optimise the model and explain observed trends in nuclear data', validate the fit against experiment, and predict the limits of nuclear stability. The article frames this as giving an operational, and thus deeper, understanding of theoretical modelling in physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simple spreadsheet software and real atomic mass data: 'The activity requires the use of simple spreadsheet software'.
#### Constraints
- Discrepancies up to the LD model's typical accuracy of 0.1 MeV per particle are acceptable in the optimisation and validation procedure.
- Extrapolation 'is generally less reliable than interpolation'.

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- A-level students

### Learning Goals
- understand theoretical modelling, its validation and predictive power
- develop simple computational skills for numerical prediction
