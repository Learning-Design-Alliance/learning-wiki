# Teach healthcare responses to trafficking as survivor empowerment through trauma-informed care, not forceful removal

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The simulation is designed around a stance the article makes explicit: "One principal point for this simulation was to reinforce that healthcare's role is not immediate forceful removal of trafficked persons against their will, but rather a focus on survivor empowerment and meeting their stated needs through a trauma-informed approach." Students practice disclosing confidentiality, building trust, and developing an action plan for the patient's immediate needs, using non-judgmental, open-ended questioning per the HEEADSSS framework. Trauma-informed care acknowledges that trauma is prevalent and avoids re-traumatizing patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must practice complex communication skills in a clinical setting before high-risk patient encounters
- SP feedback focused on communication skills and trust-building rather than case details
#### Constraints
- Learners are not expected to confirm that the patient has been trafficked; the scenario aims to arouse suspicion for abuse in the form of trafficking

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Learning Goals
- trauma-informed communication
- building patient trust
- developing action plans that meet patients' stated needs
