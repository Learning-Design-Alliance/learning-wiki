# Parallel study of light and microwaves through matched laboratory experiments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A unit-level laboratory design in which the same experiments (inverse square law, reflection and refraction, double-slit interference, polarization, interferometry) are performed for both light and microwave radiation. The author reports that "the abstract invisible properties of microwaves are visualized via observing visible properties of light", while microwave observations in turn help visualize the mechanism of light polarization. The pattern uses identical apparatus designs so students see the similarity of measurements across the electromagnetic spectrum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A light source or He-Ne laser for optics and microwave transmitter and receiver equipment (e.g., PASCO sets) for the microwave experiments
#### Constraints
- In microwave experiments the phenomena are invisible and must be observed through meter readings of radiation intensity rather than direct viewing

### Target Learners
- engineering majors, especially electrical engineering and telecommunication students in general physics laboratory courses

### Learning Goals
- understanding that light and electromagnetic radiation have the same properties and follow the same laws
- visualizing abstract electromagnetic wave concepts
