# Four web-based digital escape rooms aligned to medical curriculum committees and clerkships

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes four web-based escape rooms developed for undergraduate medical students: two for third-year committee courses and two for fourth-year clerkships, playable individually on PCs, laptops, and tablets. Development followed an iterative "design-apply-review-check-revise" cycle between the design team and a technology firm. The rooms embed case-based puzzles, hidden clues, hints, a countdown timer, and an experimental scoring system with point deductions for hint use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Development required an iterative cycle between the design team and a technology firm, completed after 20 meetings
#### Constraints
- Piloted with fourth- and fifth-year students rather than the intended third- and fourth-year audience, to avoid exposing the main-implementation cohort

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- applying committee and clerkship learning outcomes (e.g., infectious diseases, neoplasia-hematology-gastroenterology, pediatrics, endocrine system) to solve case-based puzzles
