# Speech-memory paradigm: a short-term auditory-cognitive training with natural continuous speech and varying memory load, in audio-only and immersive stereoscopic VR modalities

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The training element is a speech-memory task in which participants hear natural continuous sentences with varying memory loads (one vs two sentences, 60 items per session) and answer a verbal comprehension question after each item. As the article describes it, "The AU and IM approach used the same training task and consisted of a speech-memory task, whereby participants were presented with natural continuous sentences with varying memory loads and were required to answer a comprehension question after each sentence." The immersive version presents the speaker via a stereoscopic three-dimensional 180-degree video in an HP Reverb G2 head-mounted display; sessions lasted about 40-45 minutes, and comprehension questions after high-load items always referred to the first heard sentence, with mock items every tenth trial to prevent listening strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Natural continuous speech material presented at a normalized 70 dB level with comprehension questions to enforce processing
- Randomized item order and single presentation of each item to reduce repetition-based learning effects
#### Constraints
- The immersive environment predominantly increased redundant sensory information given the very favorable listening situation, per the authors' interpretation; perceived immersion was not systematically assessed

### Target Learners
- healthy older adults (61-80) with mild to moderate hearing loss and varying cognitive capacities

### Learning Goals
- speech comprehension of natural continuous speech
- selective attention and working memory engagement during listening
