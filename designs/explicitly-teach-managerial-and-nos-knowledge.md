# Explicitly teach and value managerial knowledge and knowledge about the nature of science throughout multi-week lab projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For instructors who want to foster project ownership, the article argues that teaching domain concepts and equipment may not suffice: "It could be beneficial to also ensure that managerial knowledge and knowledge about the nature of science are explicitly taught and valued throughout the project." Managerial choices at the start of a project, the authors note citing Jordan's realization, have implications for all subsequent phases. The principle directs instructor attention beyond concepts and apparatus toward project management and the nature of experimental physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must run multi-week, student-driven experimental projects so that choice, execution, and deliverables phases exist across which the knowledge can be taught and valued
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a recommendation for instructors who aspire to foster a sense of ownership among students working on experimental projects

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students working on multi-week lab projects

### Learning Goals
- fostering students' sense of project ownership and their managerial and nature-of-science knowledge
