# Beginner-friendly workshop series preceding a hackathon as a gateway to quantum computing learning and outreach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The event paired a six-day series of introductory Spanish- and English-language workshops with a five-day hackathon, designed so that, as the authors state, "this event was organized with beginner friendliness as the key priority". Workshops introduced applications such as optimization, chemistry, algorithms, machine learning and games, and the hackathon let teams of 2-5 build a first quantum computing project with mentor support in Spanish.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Workshop materials had to be adapted for the audience: "the original materials have to be adapted to Spanish and adapted to an introductory level"
- Mentors selected from programs such as the Qiskit advocate program who could speak or write in Spanish to provide one-on-one support to teams
#### Constraints
- The authors note that "the amount of overhead in supporting more time zones can make organization logistically more complex, and so a balance is needed" when assembling an organizing team across more countries

### Target Learners
- Spanish-speaking beginners in quantum computing in Latin America
- students and non-students of all education levels new to quantum concepts

### Learning Goals
- introduce quantum computing concepts and applications at a beginner level
- support creation of a first quantum computing project
- build confidence to continue learning quantum science
