# Proficiency-based progression (PBP) training methodology

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
PBP is described as "an innovative surgical training paradigm that uses objective metrics to ensure trainees reach a predefined proficiency benchmark based on expert performance". Implementation requires metric-based assessment with defined proficiency benchmarks, deliberate practice to the benchmark, and trained raters who score videos until they demonstrate inter-rater reliability (IRR) of >0.8 consistently before assessing trainees. In this article the methodology underpins the four compared training conditions and the cost analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Objective performance metrics developed and validated for the task
- Raters trained to an inter-rater reliability of >0.8, consistently (more than two times consecutively), before scoring
- A predefined proficiency benchmark based on expert performance
#### Constraints
- Metric development and validation entail a fixed upfront cost (€132 377 in this study), which is only amortised at larger trainee cohorts

### Target Learners
- Medical trainees and surgical residents learning robotic surgical skills

### Learning Goals
- Reaching expert-referenced proficiency on robotic suturing and knot-tying tasks
