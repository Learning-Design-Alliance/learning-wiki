# Four-activity flipped-classroom learning design for CPR in nursing education (MCQ, RQI skills training, TBL, simulation)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A longitudinal learning design in NTNU’s bachelor nursing program combining "two pre-class activities and two in-class activities": multiple-choice questions based on ERC guidelines, mannequin-based skills training with Resuscitation Quality Improvement providing real-time computer-generated feedback, facilitated team-based learning with case-based discussions, and low- to high-fidelity simulation in teams. Pre-class activities are non-mandatory and flexible; in-class activities are mandatory and scheduled. The design evolves across three study years, from basic CPR skills toward communication, teamwork and advanced techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured design that helps students identify learning objectives and understand how to achieve them; teacher facilitation and justification of why the design was chosen
#### Constraints
- Pre-class activities are non-mandatory, and studies report challenges during pre-class activities including increased study-effort, unfamiliarity with the format, and lack of motivation among students

### Target Learners
- Bachelor nursing students across three years of study

### Learning Goals
- CPR theoretical knowledge, practical skills, assessment, decision-making, teamwork and competence in stressful cardiac-arrest situations
