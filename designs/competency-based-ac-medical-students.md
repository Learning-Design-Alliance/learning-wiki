# Competency-based assessment center for first-year medical students (interview, group discussion, role play with self-report questionnaire)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A one-day assessment center developed to measure four core competencies of the medical curriculum — social-ethical, communicative, self, and teaching — selected from a demand analysis because the ideal number of AC dimensions is "considered to be 2–4". It combined a self-report questionnaire used as the basis for a 30-min structured interview, a 45-min group discussion, and a 15-min role play, with behaviors rated on 5-point scales by trained post-doctoral faculty raters. Each student was observed by a different rater per element and received personal feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Raters require a half-day training prior to the assessment day; a demand analysis must define the competencies and specific observable behaviors and attitudes for each element
#### Constraints
- The implementation of such an AC requires high personal and financial efforts; not all raters took part in all elements, so inter-rater reliability could not be computed; participation was voluntary

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- social-ethical competency
- communicative competency
- self-competency
- teaching competency
