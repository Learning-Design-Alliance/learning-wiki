# Progressive fading of argumentation scaffolds across a semester sequence

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study implemented a four-semester progression of argumentation scaffolds in weekly recitations: no instruction (Fall 2022), CER definitions with students listing their own CER before writing full arguments (Spring 2023), and, in Fall 2023 and Spring 2024, first labeling provided statements as claim, evidence, or reasoning, then listing their own CER, and finally constructing their own arguments. This increasing structure was associated with essay topics concentrating around physics-principle themes rather than procedural or surface features.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recitation worksheets with identical written scaffolds for all students; weekly GTA training with standardized slides introducing scientific argumentation
#### Constraints
- Scaffolding occurred exclusively in recitation, with no formal argumentation instruction from lecture or lab, which the authors say restricted student exposure and made instruction quality partly reliant on GTA discretion

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large-enrollment introductory calculus-based physics

### Learning Goals
- shifting written arguments from procedural reasoning toward conceptually grounded argumentation
