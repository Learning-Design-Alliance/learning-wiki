# A disability-education clerkship pattern combining clinical experience, ADA-focused didactics, first-hand accounts from persons with disabilities, and reflective assignments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The revised mandatory 2-week PM&R clerkship combines four components: clinical experiences within PM&R; 8 hours of didactic lectures including "virtual encounters with persons with disabilities"; mandatory readings and prerecorded lectures on rehabilitation topics; and a musculoskeletal exam practical. The 2022–2023 revision added ADA content, material on the disability paradox and ableism, more interactive first-hand accounts from persons living with a disability, and a short written assignment asking students to recall a hurdle in caring for a person with disability and develop a recommendation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to PM&R clinical experiences and persons with disabilities willing to give first-hand or virtual accounts; the article notes only 2.5% of U.S. medical schools have a mandatory PM&R clerkship
#### Constraints
- Generalizability is limited by the single-institution sample with a robust PM&R department and the scarcity of PM&R residency training programs (105 programs for 192 medical schools)

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students

### Learning Goals
- knowledge of physician responsibilities under the ADA
- comfort in caring for persons with disabilities
- awareness of ableism and the disability paradox
