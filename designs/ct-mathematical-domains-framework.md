# Computational thinking as a universally applicable problem-solving approach connected to mathematical domains

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames computational thinking following Wing (2006) as "an approach used to solve problems, design systems, and understand human behavior founded on computer science concepts", applicable across school subjects rather than tied narrowly to programming. It connects CT to three mathematical domains — number knowledge and arithmetic, algebra, and geometry — drawing on Papert's turtle geometry, Lakoff and Núñez's arithmetic metaphors, Kaput's algebra strands, and links between CT and spatial reasoning in geometry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Digital CT activities such as robots and block programming integrated into mathematics lessons
#### Constraints
- The connection between CT and arithmetic remains unclear, as some studies found CT does not necessarily improve number understanding

### Target Learners
- Primary school students in grades 2–3

### Learning Goals
- Number knowledge and arithmetic
- Algebraic thinking
- Geometry and spatial reasoning
