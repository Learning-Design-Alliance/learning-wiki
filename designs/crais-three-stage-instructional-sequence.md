# Concrete–representational–abstract instructional strategy (CRAIS): a three-stage sequence from concrete materials through drawings to symbols

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
CRAIS is an instructional strategy in which each mathematics concept is taught in three sequenced stages: modelling with concrete materials, then semi-concrete drawings representing concrete objects, then symbolic notation using "only numbers, notation, and mathematical symbols". Each part builds on the previous instruction to promote learning, retention, and conceptual knowledge (Access Center, 2004). The article applies CRAIS to circles, volume of solids, and angles of elevation and depression, involving students in cutting, modelling, and drawing before symbolic representation. Its root is traced to Dale's Cone of Experience, where learners retain more by what they do.
