# Co-develop and co-teach interdisciplinary QIS courses with teams spanning multiple disciplines

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes interdisciplinary co-instructional teams as a way to cover the breadth of QIS: each instructor teaches the material most relevant to their own expertise, students see material from multiple angles, and instructors with limited QIS background solidify it through colleagues. David developed his course "as part of a team of four" including a physicist, an electrical engineer, and a math-oriented former EE/CS faculty member; Carl co-developed his course with a fellow physicist and a computer science theorist, and intentionally recruits co-instructors so there is not "a CS version of the class, and a physics version of the class."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to colleagues with complementary disciplinary expertise (physics, CS, engineering, math) willing to co-develop and co-teach the course
#### Constraints
- The article presents this from the perspective of a small number of interviewees; it is an instructor-reported practice, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and hybrid undergraduate/graduate students in interdisciplinary QIS courses

### Learning Goals
- Understanding quantum mechanics formalism and quantum computing topics from multiple disciplinary perspectives
