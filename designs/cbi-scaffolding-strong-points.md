# CBI's observed supports for learners with disabilities: timely feedback, individualized scaffolding, group scaffolding, and psychological freedom

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on observation during the intervention, the article lists strong points of CBI for children with learning disabilities: "Timely feedback or correction" of mistakes with mathematic symbols, individualized scaffolding possible in small groups, group scaffolding for perceptual and motoric problems done "without consuming too much time", and more frequent self-confidence, motivation, and courage to ask or answer questions. These are the authors' observed mechanisms, not separately tested outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small cluster size so the teacher can provide timely correction and individual responses
#### Constraints
- Corrections of this kind are described as hardly possible in FII because many students might need the same scaffolding

### Target Learners
- students with learning disabilities in inclusive primary schools

### Learning Goals
- mathematics learning, cognitive engagement, learning confidence
