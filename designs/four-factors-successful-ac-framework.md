# Four-factor framework for successful astronomy preprint journal clubs (commitment, environment, content, objective)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that successful Astro-ph/Astro-Coffee journal clubs rest on four interrelated factors identified by in vivo coding and theming of interviews with 9 implementers: "commitment (how the organizer and attendees participate in AC), environment (how conducive and comfortable AC is conducted), content (the discussed topics in AC and how they are presented), and objective (the main goal/s of conducting AC)". The objective acts as a tether connecting the other three factors: when the objective changes, the implementation of the rest changes too. The framework is grounded in communities-of-practice and social constructivist accounts of journal clubs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A committed organizer and committed attendees who actively participate
- Clear objectives established and reviewed with all participants before starting
#### Constraints
- Factors were derived from interviews with 9 implementers, counting only themes raised by at least 3 respondents
- Assessed in one university's AC with small sample sizes (10 enrollees, 8 volunteers, 2 audiences)

### Target Learners
- graduate astronomy students
- undergraduates in graduate courses
- postdocs and faculty

### Learning Goals
- keeping up with the latest astronomy literature
- reading and critically evaluating preprints
- learning research fields beyond one's own
