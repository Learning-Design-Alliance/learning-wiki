# Theory of Didactical Situations as an intermediate framework for designing kinesthetic physics lessons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article uses Brousseau's Theory of Didactical Situations (TDS), a design and analytical framework developed for mathematics but "proven usable for a wide variety of subjects, including physics", to structure teaching with the pulling model. TDS revolves around adidactical situations in which students engage directly with a novel problem and refine concepts via feedback from a material and social milieu; it incorporates abstract elements from grand theories of learning — here conceptual metaphor and image schemas — into classroom-realizable designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An adidactical situation needs a task engaging students autonomously with the knowledge to be learned, specified conditions under which the task is solved, and an expected progression towards the knowledge; the didactical environment must enable students to evaluate their own thinking.
#### Constraints
- The adidactical situation must be embedded in a didactical structure, and premature institutionalization can occur if students turn to the teacher too early.

### Target Learners
- secondary and tertiary mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of force and acceleration consistent with Newtonian mechanics
