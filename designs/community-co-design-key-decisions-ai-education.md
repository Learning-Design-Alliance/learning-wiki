# Co-designing community AI education through four key decisions: balanced topics, conceptual grounding, local relevance, and dialogical learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This pattern describes the recurring design decisions the research team and community partners made when co-creating the AI education session. Balanced topics covered both positive and negative sides of AI; conceptual grounding used "educational materials suitable for a general audience without prior AI expertise"; local relevance drew on community examples such as deepfakes and local landscapes; and dialogical learning treated members as co-producers of AI understanding rather than recipients of expert knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Community partner involvement in feedback and revision rounds on session materials
- Content accessible at an 8th-grade reading level with technical terms avoided
#### Constraints
- The pattern was developed for adults outside formal educational systems in a community setting rather than academic classrooms

### Target Learners
- Adults outside formal educational systems in a local community

### Learning Goals
- Building a comprehensive mental map of AI
- Understanding core AI concepts such as data, training, and bias
