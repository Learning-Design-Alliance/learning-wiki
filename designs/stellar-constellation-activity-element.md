# The Stellar Constellation Activity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A 45-minute outreach activity at the Nottingham Trent University observatory in which "two groups of 6 pupils role playing two groups of people from a different cultural background" ('Explorers' and 'Natives') develop stellar constellations and stories in a planetarium showing a night sky, using artefacts such as a sextant, candles and flashlights and themed handouts. Groups present and discuss their constellations, and the activity ends with the Greek Orion story and the Plough's use by escaping slaves. It is assessed with a pre- and post-activity questionnaire modelled on the Astronomy Diagnostic Tool.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supervisor for each group is always present to facilitate the activities and stimulate discussion
- Simplified star charts and role-themed handouts are provided to aid constellation drawing and story writing
#### Constraints
- The activity failed to demonstrate the three-dimensional structure of constellations and created an unintended America-distance association

### Target Learners
- secondary school pupils from age 9 upward
- PGCE primary science students

### Learning Goals
- global citizenship concepts of values, perceptions and cultural understanding
- correcting common astronomical misconceptions about constellations and the Pole Star
