# Eye Movement Modeling Examples (EMME) as an instructional tool for multimedia learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
EMME are recordings of a model's eye movements performed during a task, overlaid onto the task material so that learners can see where, how long, and in what order the model looks. In this study the EMME were short videos in which "A white spotlight on the otherwise grey shaded page represented a gaze fixation", with spotlight size varying with fixation duration, and no verbal instruction. They were shown on the first four pages of an illustrated text on mitosis to demonstrate effective multimedia processing strategies such as inspecting the picture before reading and looking at corresponding picture elements during text-reading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be informed in an introductory text that the abstract gaze cue (spotlight) represents human eye movements and that spotlight size illustrates fixation time.
#### Constraints
- In this study EMME-guidance was presented only on the first four pages of the learning material, and posttest items referred only to Part 2 to avoid direct influence of the external guidance.

### Target Learners
- University students learning biology (mitosis) from illustrated texts

### Learning Goals
- Applying effective multimedia processing strategies (selecting, organizing and integrating text and picture information)
- Understanding the biological processes of mitosis
