# A school workshop-informed participatory modelling framework that integrates stakeholder preferences across the entire energy system modelling process

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The framework couples educational workshops with a national electricity system model so that participant preferences enter scenario design, optimization, results discussion, and feedback. Full-day school workshops with interactive activities and games discuss the green energy transition, conflicts of building more RETs, and climate justice, and questionnaires capture perspectives on power-system aspects; preference coefficients derived from responses are integrated into the modified model. The workflow, illustrated in Fig. 1, develops "more socially acceptable energy transition pathways" and enabled "a meaningful and fact-based dialogue between students on possible future electricity system designs." It addresses the gap that participatory ESM approaches often fail to support collective learning and overlook young people.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A spatiotemporally detailed energy system model that can be modified to encode stakeholder preferences as constraints
- Questionnaires aligned with the model's power-system dimensions to capture participant perspectives
- An inter- and multidisciplinary team (the study involved psychologists, political scientists, sociologists, biologists, RET scientists, and energy system modellers)
- A follow-up feedback session to let participants reflect on the outcomes of their choices
#### Constraints
- Applied to young people in Norway; the authors suggest combining the national focus with local considerations through regional case studies since youth in coastal regions may not share the sampled preferences
- Potential bias arises from interpreting and analyzing the workshop results
- Scenarios can be tailored to local socio-political context; the framework is considered most applicable to global north contexts

### Target Learners
- Young people aged 15-16 in lower-secondary school
- Underrepresented community groups in energy planning

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the basics of the green energy transformation and renewable electricity technologies
- Understanding trade-offs, conflicts, and climate justice in energy system planning
- Forming and expressing informed opinions about the energy transformation process
