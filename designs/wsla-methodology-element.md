# Work Station Learning Activities (WSLA): five-step workstation-based active learning methodology for integrated basic science teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
WSLA is a flexible and scalable teaching tool for progressively integrating basic science subjects into the biomedical curriculum, described as teaching "a particular topic (e.g. pH regulation) across a series of different workstations run in a clinical scenario." Its five steps are: pre-session content on virtual campus, a pre-test, presentation of a clinical scenario with workstations and team-based worksheets, a general review with assessment, and a post-test. In this study three 2-hour WSLA sessions integrated Biochemistry/Physiology and Anatomy/Physiology content for first-year medical students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-session content provision and a pre-test to ensure student readiness before the hands-on session
- Team-based worksheet activities at each workstation solved as a group
#### Constraints
- Aligned with the correlation level of Harden's Integration Ladder, i.e., discipline-based instruction with integrated teaching alongside, not a fully integrated curriculum

### Target Learners
- first-year medical (health science) undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- integrating basic science concepts with clinical application
- teamwork and collaborative reasoning in clinical scenarios
