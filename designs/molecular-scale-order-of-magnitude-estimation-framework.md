# Order-of-magnitude estimation framework linking molecular scales to macroscopic material properties

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a framework in which a few microscopic scales — atomic mass, chemical bond length, intermolecular spacing, and bond or intermolecular energy — plus temperature in energy units, generate order-of-magnitude estimates of macroscopic material properties via dimensional analysis. The author argues that "a large fraction of these parameters can usually be estimated to within an order of magnitude using elementary physical ideas which are accessible to freshmen in introductory physics courses". Because most materials share similar building blocks, many properties appear universal up to O(1) constants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need the microscopic scales (bond length, intermolecular spacing, bond energy) either derived or stated as facts early in the course
#### Constraints
- Electrical properties of materials are excluded because they depend strongly on charge carriers and vary strongly between materials
- Gases are excluded since their universal behavior is already well explained in statistical mechanics

### Target Learners
- freshmen in introductory physics courses
- advanced high school students

### Learning Goals
- connecting microscopic molecular physics to everyday material properties
- estimating orders of magnitude of material properties
