# IP-STAR: an instructional model integrating RCDP methodology within the TeamSTEPPS framework for interprofessional simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
IP-STAR combines Rapid Cycle Deliberate Practice, in which learners "repetitiously perform a simulation scenario, segmented into progressively complex stages, with frequent and succinct micro-debriefs by faculty", with the TeamSTEPPS framework whose four core components — communication, leadership, situational monitoring and mutual support — are presented as teachable-learnable skills. The model was operationalized as a two-hour in-situ pediatric cardiac arrest simulation for mixed MBBS and nursing teams, with hard stops for objective-critical actions and soft stops for team-dynamics variability. The authors position this combination as previously largely unexplored for undergraduate interprofessional healthcare education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty trained in RCDP and TeamSTEPPS principles to deliver micro-debriefs and score TPOT; a validated scenario script (s-CVI 0.93) segmented into rounds with predetermined learning objectives
#### Constraints
- Evidence for RCDP for non-technical team skills was described as relatively sparse before this study
- The study was a pilot in an institute with no formal IPE curriculum

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical (MBBS) and BSc Nursing students

### Learning Goals
- pediatric cardiac arrest management knowledge and skills
- teamwork competencies including communication, leadership, situational monitoring and mutual support
