# Region-organized interdisciplinary course series with a majority-weight original-research final project

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a course design pattern in which cultural astronomy content is divided into broad regions such as Africa, North America, and the Pacific, with the geographical focus rotating each year. Courses are structured to accommodate students from all parts of the university while carrying enough mathematics and science to serve as a general science requirement. The majority of the grade lies with a final project comprising a presentation and a written document of original research, for which the whole course is designed to prepare students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- enough mathematics and science to serve as a general science requirement
- a final project that had to be original research
#### Constraints
- the structure of the university in the USA feeds demand for such classes; this may not hold in countries without general education requirements, as the author notes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate majors and non-majors meeting a science requirement

### Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- formulating hypotheses
- testing hypotheses
- understanding cultural astronomy data
