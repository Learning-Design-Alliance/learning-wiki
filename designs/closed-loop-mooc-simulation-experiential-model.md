# Closed-loop blended model aligning MOOC theoretical preparation with virtual simulation deliberate practice, framed by Kolb's experiential learning theory

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a teaching model combining "'online MOOC pre–study and virtual simulation experiments' with 'offline classroom guidance and practical hands–on exercises'" in three stages: pre-class preparation, in-class instruction, and post-class review. The authors describe a "closed–loop model of 'theory → simulation → reflection'" aligned with Kolb's experiential learning cycle, and say the model operationalizes Ericsson's deliberate practice through structured, repetitive, feedback-rich virtual simulation. Unique design features include AI-powered real-time feedback, structured debriefing from simulation analytics, and progressive difficulty scaling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a MOOC platform with recorded micro-lecture videos and a virtual simulation experiment platform
- Instructor-led in-class sessions with assessment, demonstration, group practice, and self/peer assessment
#### Constraints
- The authors note MOOCs are predominantly applied to theoretical instruction with few successful hands-on implementations, which the simulation component addresses

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate clinical medicine students

### Learning Goals
- basic surgical clinical skills such as asepsis, instrument identification, suturing, and wound debridement
