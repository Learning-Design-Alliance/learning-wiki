# A scaffolded three-course computational physics sequence tailored to learner level, from beginner simulation to advanced research problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes integrating computation into the physics curriculum through three consecutive courses, each tailored to the learners' level: an entry-level course for students with no programming experience, an intermediate course for sophomore and junior STEM students, and an upper-level course on advanced numerical algorithms. The author frames the design explicitly: "Scaﬀolded learning, an instructional method that provides successive support steps, helps students develop skills progressively and achieve higher understanding levels." Supports are gradually removed as students advance, and the sequence spans projects "from simulating a simple bouncing ball to modeling black hole and neutron star collisions."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses must be offered in succession and tailored to each learner level, with supports gradually removed to encourage independent learning
#### Constraints
- The author notes that these classes suffer from low enrollment because they are elective

### Target Learners
- high school seniors and college freshmen with no programming experience
- sophomore and junior STEM students
- upper undergraduate and entry-level graduate physics and mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- progressively build computational skills in physics
- apply computational thinking to real-world scientific problems
