# Hybrid introductory-programming instruction: CFC, analogies, and templates mapped onto the phases of the programming process

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes combining three teaching approaches across the programming process: comment-first coding (CFC) to transfer step-by-step solutions from natural language into code, analogies to construct new concepts from familiar ones, and expert templates as reusable units of programming knowledge. As the article states, "different approaches (CFC, analogies and templates) were introduced at different phases of the programming process": CFC and analogies for learning language features and general concepts, templates for program design, and analogies with templates for problem solving. The package functions as scaffolding that brings students to a higher level of understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must plan where and when each approach is used across lessons; the article says a proper planning is needed to adopt these approaches to the lessons
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is impossible to apply the approaches at every stage of the programming lessons
- In the study, students worked with templates chosen by the instructor, who only guided them in finding other templates

### Target Learners
- second-year novice programmers in computer and instructional technologies departments

### Learning Goals
- basic programming concepts
- language features
- program design
- problem solving in introductory programming
