# Four-type negative feedback manipulation contrasted with a no-feedback comparison group

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study operationalizes negative feedback as four distinct types assigned between groups. Upon an error, "Group A subjects were given explicit metalinguistic information about the generalization we hoped they would learn"; "Group B subjects were told that their response was wrong"; "Group C subjects were corrected when they erred, giving them a model of the response desired along with implicit negative evidence that their response was incorrect"; and "Group D subjects, having made an error, were asked if they were sure about their response." A comparison group received no feedback. This taxonomy separates explicit metalinguistic, explicit rejection, model-plus-implicit-negative-evidence, and elicit-confirmation feedback types.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must first be trained on the target structure so that errors can be elicited and feedback delivered upon error
#### Constraints
- Feedback was delivered in response to errors on a single trained structure, the English dative alternation, with adult Spanish-speaking ESL learners

### Target Learners
- Adult Spanish-speaking learners of English as a second language

### Learning Goals
- Learning abstract constraints on the English dative alternation
