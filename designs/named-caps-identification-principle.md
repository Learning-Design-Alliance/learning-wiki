# Use named caps to facilitate team member identification in critical care and operating room teams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Wearing caps displaying each team member's name and profession is a simple strategy to facilitate identification where badges are concealed by sterile gowns and protective equipment. The article reports that "Wearing named caps represents a simple and safe strategy to facilitate identification, particularly in operating rooms," supporting directed communication and role recognition. The randomized simulation study found higher TEAM scores with named caps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Caps display the participant's name and profession; useful where conventional identification badges are concealed
#### Constraints
- Effects demonstrated only in a simulated environment with newly formed, inexperienced teams; validation in real clinical settings is needed before conclusions about patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- anesthesiology and intensive care residents
- nurses and nursing assistants in critical care teams

### Learning Goals
- teamwork and non-technical skills
- directed communication and role recognition
