# Arbitrary multi-sensory representation of non-visible scientific data as a Universal Design for Learning approach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advances arbitrary (non-figurative), redundant and multi-modal sensory representation as an approach for engaging learners with non-visible scientific data. Because such representations do not imitate reality, they "naturally suggest the presence of a code of interpretation" and avoid the misconception that "the user or learner can mistake the (unknown) visual nature of the object with its visual representation". In a Universal Design for Learning perspective, "arbitrary multi-sensory representations can be very effective to create an effective common ground for inclusion among people with diverse abilities and learning styles". The approach guided the design and testing of the haptic, acoustic and multi-sensory representations and the resulting exhibit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Representations must be clearly arbitrary rather than figurative, and the code must be declared: the article argues highly figurative representations should be always accompanied by "a declaration of their disconnection from reality" and possibly the explication of the code.
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is impossible to design environments, experiences and activities that can be used in a meaningful way by all ("barrier-free utopia"), so no representation is universal by default.

### Target Learners
- sighted and blind or visually impaired adult learners in outreach, museum and educational contexts

### Learning Goals
- understanding non-visible astronomical data and processes
- recognizing the arbitrary, coded nature of scientific representations
