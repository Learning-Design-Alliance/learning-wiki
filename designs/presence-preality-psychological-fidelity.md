# Presence and PREality as two distinct psychological constructs underlying cineVR's psychological fidelity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article distinguishes two psychological concepts from the virtual reality literature. "Presence" is defined as "a person's subjective sensation of being in a (usually virtual) scene, enabling them to interact with and feel connected to the world outside their physical bodies via technology." "PREality" is "designed to prepare the viewer for an anticipated experience by placing them in the actual environment where they intend to work," creating a sense of déjà vu. The article states that 360-degree videos do not need to be location-specific to create presence but do need to be location-specific to create PREality, and that the described production methods use both to establish a high level of psychological fidelity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Creating PREality requires filming in the actual environment where the learner intends to work, i.e., location-specific video
#### Constraints
- Learners at other institutions will retain presence but will not obtain PREality from videos filmed at a different institution because of differing physical environments

### Target Learners
- medical trainees
- trauma care providers

### Learning Goals
- familiarity with the trauma bay environment before real-life exposure
- empathy with team-member perspectives
