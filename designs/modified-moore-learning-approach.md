# Modified Moore learning approach for advanced mathematics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A learning approach that motivates students to learn mathematical proving actively: students 'were motivated to think independently , start with a simple problem for improving a solution accompanied with its supporting reason and to communicate their ideas writtenly or orally'. Written communication occurs on a board or in article form; oral communication involves presenting and defending work in front of class with little lecturer guidance. The article implemented it in an advanced abstract algebra course to train self-regulated problem solving, composing relevant reasons, and convincing peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation to no more than 24 students, or grouping students of varied prior mathematics ability when there are more
- Students compose proofs themselves rather than copying lecturer's proofs passively
- Adequate time, namely one semester, for advanced courses
#### Constraints
- In this study it was conducted during a half semester, which the authors say was too short for advanced proving tasks
- Low prior-ability students needed revised materials with more explanation and exercises

### Target Learners
- undergraduate mathematics students in advanced abstract algebra

### Learning Goals
- mathematical understanding ability
- mathematical proving ability
- active learning habits and positive learning disposition
