# Cubical room geometry lets learners project field information onto three surfaces at once

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The CUBE uses a cubical rather than spherical visualization room, which the authors report supports exploring the three-dimensional structure of the fields: "some users place the charge near the corners of the CUBE to see how the field is projected onto three surfaces at once to get a better sense of the three dimensional structure of the electromagnetic fields and Poynting vector." The cube's flat walls display contour heat maps that can be read from multiple viewing directions simultaneously.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A room geometry whose walls display the field projections; the authors note a spherical room would not permit this three-surface corner view
#### Constraints
- The authors note a spherical geometry "has other benefits that recommend it," so the cubical choice is a trade-off rather than a universally superior design

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- develop three-dimensional intuition for electromagnetic field structure
