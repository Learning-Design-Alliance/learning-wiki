# M-TBL: modified team-based learning delivered in a digitally powered flipped classroom without peer evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
M-TBL is "TBL without the peer evaluation step, which is the final step in the original (US) version of TBL". Peer evaluation was removed because Japanese nursing students were reported to be reluctant to engage in it. In the acute-care course, students viewed 30–45 min teacher-made lecture videos on demand one to two weeks before class, took an individual readiness assurance test of about five questions worth 30% of the grade, then worked in randomly allocated groups of five on three paper-based simulated cases, developing a nursing plan and presenting it; three teachers scored the group work with unified rubrics for 20% of the grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must view the on-demand lecture videos before class, since IRATs related to the videos ensured students could only participate in the class if they viewed the video
- Groups of five, based on reports that groups of five to seven are optimal
#### Constraints
- Designed and tested with Japanese undergraduate nursing students in one mandatory acute-care nursing course at a single university

### Target Learners
- Third-year undergraduate nursing students

### Learning Goals
- Team approach for inter-professional collaboration
- Critical-thinking disposition
- Self-directed learning and mastery of practical nursing skills
