# Semester-long scaffolded build-then-use microscope project as the unifying vehicle for an optics course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A course-level pattern in which the semester is organized around designing, building, and then measuring with a single instrument, broken into scaffolded modules (condenser, imaging path, calibration, resolution, advanced project) each concluding with a poster presentation. The authors ground it in the claims that hands-on, kinesthetic activities produce learning gains and that long-term project-based learning stimulates interest. Scaffolding level is varied by venue: extensive in short graduate modules, absent in the advanced laboratory course where students use published articles as guides.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups of three to four students per experimental system with instructor and TA support
- Scaffolded worksheets and module structure for novice audiences
#### Constraints
- In venues without scaffolding, students must engage the literature themselves for experimental design

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors
- cross-listed life science and engineering undergraduates
- graduate students

### Learning Goals
- optical design and construction
- measurement technique and experimental design
- scientific communication via posters and manuscripts
