# Synergistic complementarity of PBL, MDT, and flipped classroom methods

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's explanatory account for why combining the three methods should outperform any one alone: each method compensates for the others' limitations. "For example, PBL alone may lack the structured knowledge acquisition provided by FC, while FC may not fully simulate the collaborative decision-making required in clinical practice", and "MDT discussions without a strong foundation in problem-solving or self-directed learning may fall short of fostering independent clinical reasoning". The authors further propose that among the three components "MDT likely played the most central role in promoting clinical reasoning and problem-solving skills", with PBL and flipped classroom enhancing active learning and engagement. This is the authors' interpretation rather than a tested decomposition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Methods should be combined so each addresses gaps in the others: PBL for critical thinking and problem-solving, flipped classroom for self-directed learning, MDT for interdisciplinary realism
#### Constraints
- The study could not isolate the individual contribution of MDT; the component attribution is speculative, as the authors themselves acknowledge

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clinical clerkships

### Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning
- Interdisciplinary understanding of complex conditions
- Self-directed learning
