# Modified BOPPPS model: experiential anchoring of the Bridge-in phase guided by experiential learning and situated cognition theories

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's modified BOPPPS model redefines the Bridge-in phase from a "theoretical bridge" to an "experiential anchor": ward visits (concrete clinical observation) and thoracoscopy simulation (embodied operational practice) serve as dual cognitive scaffolds. It embeds Kolb's learning cycle — "concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active application" — within the BOPPPS framework, and invokes situated cognition theory on the grounds that "knowledge acquired in context is more readily transferable to clinical practice". The model aims to reshape students' cognitive schema from a linear theory-to-practice model to a three-dimensional "disease-anatomy-operation" framework, addressing decontextualized knowledge acquisition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ward visits and thoracoscopy simulation must be integrated into the Bridge-in phase while all other BOPPPS components and the total 300-minute teaching time remain unchanged
- Instructor standardization (same senior instructor, 2-hour standardized training, an operational manual, and third-party compliance monitoring at ≥ 95%) is needed for consistent implementation
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in thoracic surgery teaching for lung tumour-related content at a single center with a small sample and no long-term follow-up
- Group allocation was by intact class, non-randomized, so unmeasured confounding cannot be fully excluded

### Target Learners
- fifth-year clinical medicine undergraduate students

### Learning Goals
- clinical imaging analysis ability
- comprehensive clinical application ability
- clinical reasoning bridging classroom learning and bedside practice
