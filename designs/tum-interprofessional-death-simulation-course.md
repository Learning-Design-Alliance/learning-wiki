# Interprofessional death simulation course with unexpected death via advance directive and extended debriefing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An interprofessional, simulation-based course ("Training Interprofessional Teams for Daily Clinical Practice and Emergencies") at the TUM Medical Training Center consisting of "five sessions with a duration of 3 to 3.5 h per session" over two and a half weeks. Its fourth session confronted participants with an unexpected death: after about five minutes of unsuccessful resuscitation a trainer delivered the patient's advance directive, and participants stopped resuscitation. A 15-minute prebriefing omitted the death possibility, the simulation used a high-fidelity manikin, and a two-hour debriefing followed Lederman's three-stage model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Three to five trainers with medical and nursing backgrounds and experience in debriefing supervising each session
- An ethics-approved design with psychosocial support offered and written informed consent for the study
- Deliberate avoidance of mentioning the advance directive beforehand so participants do not suspect the scenario outcome
#### Constraints
- Nursing trainee participation was compulsory while medical students participated voluntarily, which the article notes as a difference in recruitment
- Participants received no education on advance directives prior to the simulation and their prior AD knowledge was not recorded

### Target Learners
- Medical students from seventh to ninth semester
- Third-year nursing trainees

### Learning Goals
- Teamwork and communication skills in clinical and emergency situations
- Dealing with termination of resuscitation and patient death
- Self-care and reflection after experiencing a patient's death
