# The Swarthmore IPLS design framework: authentic biological contexts, expansive framing, cognitive apprenticeship, and preparation for future learning as a transfer goal.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes an introductory physics course for life sciences built around authentic biological contexts that are integral to each unit rather than optional add-ons, with transfer to later biomedical work as a core goal. Two named pedagogical strategies undergird the course, which the authors describe as "expansive framing" and "cognitive apprenticeship": content is explicitly framed as connected to students' other coursework and futures, and the instructor demonstrates problem-solving with attention to decision-making steps and simplifying assumptions. The course goal is grounded in the preparation for future learning concept of transfer, seeking to make students able to recognize biomedical situations where physical ideas add understanding.
