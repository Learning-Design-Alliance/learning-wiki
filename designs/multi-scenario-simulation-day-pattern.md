# Multi-scenario simulation day pairing lecture with role-based team clinical cases.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern organizes a single-day learning event around multiple distinct clinical scenarios, each with defined tasks and learning objectives tied to basic science concepts. Students "assumed different roles within a healthcare team," worked in small groups (average 5 students per day), and rotated through scenarios including one with a live standardized patient, preceded by a traditional lecture and followed by structured debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple scenario stations with manikins or standardized patients
- Small team sizes to allow active role participation
- Scenario-specific learning objectives and assessment checklists
#### Constraints
- Participants disliked unstructured waiting time between simulations on higher-attendance days; the authors suggest multiple instructors working simultaneously for larger groups

### Target Learners
- pre-health undergraduate students across all college years and majors

### Learning Goals
- application of basic science to clinical emergencies
- teamwork and interprofessional role awareness
- clinical skills such as intubation and CPR
