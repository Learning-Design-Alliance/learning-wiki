# Teach quantum mechanics axiomatically, using representation-independent Dirac notation and postponing historical visualizations, applications and interpretations.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's central design principle: present the postulates systematically as "the shortest and most eﬃcie nt path to familiarizing with the basics and universal use of quantum mechanics", keep formulas in representation-independent Dirac form, and defer visualizations and explanations to specialized courses because they "produce the puzzles and make the theory ”myste rious”" before becoming useful.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Proper student preparation before starting: courses in the classical-mechanics Hamilton formalism, basic statistical physics, theory of probability and a basic course in linear algebra, so the Hilbert and rigged Hilbert space can be smoothly introduced.
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges the full mathematical foundations are demanding and time-consuming for starters, so "some balance is required for the introductory course", and notes neither the outcome nor unique solutions can be expected of finding that balance.

### Target Learners
- graduate and undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding the basic postulates and universal formalism of non-relativistic quantum theory
