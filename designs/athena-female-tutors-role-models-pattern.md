# Stereotype-free early university study with female tutors as role models and targeted support for young women

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The programme's design combines a "stress-free environment" for discovering and exploring university mathematics and physics, free from self-beliefs and gender stereotypes, with targeted support for young women: "In the case of female students, special emphasis is placed on recruiting female tutors", who "act as role models, known personally to the pupils" and are "a living demonstration of how to overcome gender stereotypes". Tutor mentoring is intended to help students develop a more positive and resilient self-concept when facing difficult course content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recruitment of female tutors for female students
- Tutors drawn from undergraduates, postgraduates, doctoral students or young researchers who provide targeted support
#### Constraints
- The article reports participants' gender ratio remains below the maturité-level overall ratio (GR = 1.4)

### Target Learners
- pupils in the final or penultimate year of Secondary II (high school)
- young women considering careers in physics or mathematics

### Learning Goals
- developing a positive, resilient self-concept when faced with difficult course content
- considering scientific careers, especially in physics and mathematics
