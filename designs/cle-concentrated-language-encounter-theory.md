# Concentrated Language Encounter (CLE) approach: staged lesson sequences producing target texts through scaffolding, routines, and teacher-student negotiation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The CLE approach, developed by Gray at Traeger Park and defined as "a sequence of lessons that focused upon the production of a specific text," teaches language through texts chosen for their social function, with the teacher pre-analyzing the language resources needed. Lessons are staged as "routines" in which the same text is revisited over time with expanding scope, grounded in Vygotskyan scaffolding where teachers let children do as much as they can and gradually hand over control. Two lesson-sequence types are distinguished: Type A encounters produce written texts from shared experiences, and Type B encounters derive from experiences with texts read to, with, or by students. The article stresses CLE is experiential but differs from child-centered whole-language approaches in the strength of the adult role and the acceptance of imitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers analyze and define the language contextual resources necessary for producing the target text prior to teaching
- Lesson sequences must be staged as routines with repeated revisiting of the same text over time
- Both teachers and children take an active part in negotiating the children's learning
#### Constraints
- The article states it is misleading to presume an affinity with child-centered language experience approaches such as whole language, despite the experiential character of CLE

### Target Learners
- beginner and less experienced readers
- disadvantaged and minority-language students
- advanced primary and secondary learners

### Learning Goals
- literacy in Thai and English
- control of a range of text types and genres
- spoken, written, and nonverbal communication
