# Transversal curvature-centered didactic proposal linking differential calculus with Newtonian mechanics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an inclusive, applied treatment of the curvature differential concept in which "se presenta una manera incluyente y aplicada del concepto de diferencial de curvatura, específicamente con la intervención de problemas de la mecánica de Newton". Its double purpose is to give the derivative a versatile modeling sense and to approach classic initial-physics topics from a proposal centered on curvature. It draws on García's Complex Systems Theory notion of interdiscipline as process, linking geometric and differential concepts of curvature with applied physics cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination between calculus and physics instructors so topics can be treated simultaneously or nearly simultaneously
- Students must have operational command of the derivative plus conceptual and geometric understanding beyond a first course level
#### Constraints
- The curvature definition constructed is valid in the Euclidean plane with Cartesian coordinate systems; generalization to more dimensions or other coordinate systems is not treated

### Target Learners
- first-year university students in science and engineering programs

### Learning Goals
- understanding the derivative as a modeling operator
- connecting geometric curvature to centripetal acceleration and force
- reaching higher Bloom-taxonomy levels (analyze, evaluate, create)

### Claims
- Centripetal Acceleration Linear In Curvature [+M]
- Parabolic Centripetal Force Maximum [+M]
