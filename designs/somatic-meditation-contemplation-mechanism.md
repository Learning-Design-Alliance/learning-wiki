# Contemplative pedagogy: somatic meditation followed by contemplation as a mechanism for connecting formal theory to lived experience

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that a two-part contemplative practice — a guided somatic meditation in which students "direct their attention into and throughout their bodies, gradually expanding their awareness into the surrounding space," followed by contemplation of electromagnetic phenomena — connects formal physical principles to personal experience. The authors report the meditation contributed by reducing distractions to achieve "a state of mental openness" and by structuring the progression of students' observations along the meditation's trajectory from body to space to universe.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students submit a written reflection after completing the practice, graded on completion only
#### Constraints
- Effects on somatic, affective, and cognitive states would not persist following a single meditation session, per the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students, including prospective physics majors and engineering sciences majors

### Learning Goals
- awareness of connections between formal physical principles and personal experience
- intrinsic motivation to understand electromagnetic theory
