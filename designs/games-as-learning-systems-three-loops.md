# Games as learning systems: three interwoven motivational, exploratory, and identity dynamics

## Description
A theoretical model developed by Beatty identifying the key elements and dynamics that enable good video games to function as effective learning environments. At its heart are "three interwoven dynamics": an intrinsic motivation loop built on increasingly difficult challenges and feelings of achievement, an exploratory learning loop of goal-directed trial-and-error experimentation, and an identity growth loop in which the player experiences new ways of seeing and being in the world. The paper used it to derive the six structural course features.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supporting structural features such as "copious and timely positive feedback", minimal-consequence failure with immediate informative feedback, and story details connecting the player to in-game identity.
#### Constraints
- The model is described as "rather complex" and is presented here as background; the course redesign was a pilot test of features the model suggests.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students in computational physics courses

### Learning Goals
- MATLAB programming, numerical methods, and identity as a computational physicist
