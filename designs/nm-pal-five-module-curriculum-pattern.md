# The integrated NM-PAL curriculum pattern: five synergistic modules combining narrative cases with small-group peer mentoring

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The NM-PAL model integrated senior medical students (recruited from the top 30% of their class, given 10 hours of training, and supervised via weekly debriefing) as peer mentors within a reciprocal learning community. The curriculum consisted of "five synergistic modules: in-depth NM-case reading, case discussions, medical terminology acquisition, doctor-patient role-playing, and academic presentations," totalling 30 instruction hours, delivered in small classes of 6–8 students versus lecture-based classes of 30–40.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior peer mentors recruited based on academic performance, prior teaching experience, and communication skills
- A standardized teaching protocol and weekly faculty debriefing sessions with mentors
#### Constraints
- Both groups completed the same total formal contact hours and curriculum objectives
- Benefits are sensitive to the perceived expertise of the peer mentors

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Contextualized clinical competence and early professional identity formation
