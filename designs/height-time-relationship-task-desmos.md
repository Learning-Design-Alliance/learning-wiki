# Height-Time Relationship Task, a three-screen Desmos digital task on projectile motion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Height-Time Relationship Task is a three-screen interactive task within the 'How Accurate Is Your Aim?' sequence, developed in Desmos Classroom, in which students launch a ball, select a line segment representing its changing height, trace a moving point constrained by height and time segments, and interpret the resulting height-time graph. Its design 'utilized the affordances of technology to synchronize the animation of the physical phenomenon with its graphical representation', for example lengthening the height segment as the ball rose. The study analyzed students' activity on the third, graph-interpretation screen.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires technology such as laptops or tablets running the Desmos/Amplify Classroom activity; the participating school was chosen for its familiarity with using technology in classroom instruction.
#### Constraints
- The reported analysis focused only on the students' activity on the third screen, so it does not capture how their reasoning may have been influenced by earlier screens of the same task.

### Target Learners
- ninth-grade middle school students

### Learning Goals
- interpreting graphs as representations of covarying quantities
- understanding height-time relationships in projectile motion
