# Probability lab-tutorial activity sequence (coins, dice, ball toss video, oscillating cart)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A set of laboratory-tutorial materials in the Intuitive Quantum Physics course that introduces probability with "discrete, non-physics systems (such as coins, and dice)" and then moves to macroscopic physical systems: a ball toss analyzed by counting video frames and an oscillating air glider binned into five regions. Students build probability histograms, compare predictions to experimental outcomes, and respond to a fictitious student dialogue embedding common misunderstandings. Most material is introduced in a single 3-hour lab-tutorial period combining individual, small group, and large group activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rudimentary knowledge of mechanics is needed for one activity, but otherwise the material requires no additional preparation
- Video clips (BallToss.mov, OscillatingCart.mov) and frame-counting capability
#### Constraints
- The authors state the data are meant to illustrate issues when teaching probability and not to make claims about effectiveness of instruction

### Target Learners
- non-science majors in a general education physics course, typically math phobic

### Learning Goals
- probability of outcomes summing to 1
- relating time spent in a region of space to probability of being found there
- distinguishing predicted distributions from actual ensemble outcomes
