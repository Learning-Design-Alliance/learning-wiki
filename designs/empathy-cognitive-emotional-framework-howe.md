# Empathy as encompassing both cognitive and emotional processes (Howe's framework) as the conceptual basis for teaching empathy

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course adopted a conceptual framework in which empathy "encompasses both cognitive and emotional processes" suggested by Howe, treating empathy as a complex concept not clearly defined in medical education research. This dual-process view drove both the instructional design (communication-skills practice alongside perspective-taking experiences) and the choice of the IRI, which measures four dimensions including perspective-taking and empathic concern. The article contrasts this with the Jefferson Scale, which regards empathy as primarily a cognitive process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction and measurement aligned with the dual cognitive-emotional definition of empathy
#### Constraints
- The course did not address clinical empathy, which takes place in the patient–doctor relationship context and is incorporated later in the curriculum

### Target Learners
- entry-level medical students

### Learning Goals
- foundational empathic competencies preceding clinical empathy
