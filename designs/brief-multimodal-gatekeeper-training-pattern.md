# Brief multi-modal gatekeeper training: film screening plus QPR role-play skills practice plus expert question-and-answer panel

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-hour community event pattern combining three components in fixed order: "The film screening occurred first, followed immediately by an experiential educational session, and finally an interactive question-and-answer session." The experiential session is QPR-based: a 15-minute lecture on questioning, persuading, and referring, then dyadic role play with a scripted scenario and skill feedback form, then a moderated panel of four bicultural, bilingual experts answering written audience questions. The article evaluates this pattern in a controlled study with Japanese-American community members.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A film appropriate to the population — the authors state subsequent work "is needed to identify a list of criteria to guide film selection"
- Bicultural and bilingual experts for the question-and-answer panel
#### Constraints
- The authors note film selection is "an admittedly subjective choice for which we cannot provide empirical guidance" and that portrayal of suicide in film can influence viewers negatively, including imitative suicidal behavior

### Target Learners
- lay community members in an at-risk ethnic minority population
- Japanese Americans, expatriates, and related stakeholders

### Learning Goals
- gatekeeper behaviors for suicide prevention (questioning, persuading, referring)
- self-efficacy for assisting someone at risk for suicide
