# Testing Brownian-motion hypotheses through the Langevin framework, from Brownian motion to inertial confined dynamics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes stochastic-trajectory analysis around Langevin dynamics and its limiting regimes: the overdamped Brownian regime, where "inertia is negligible compared to friction" and the random force is Gaussian and uncorrelated, and an inertial confined regime modeled by adding a harmonic restoring force to the Langevin equation. The experiment is deliberately designed so that "students will be prompted to test each hypothesis against their findings", moving from ideal Brownian motion to inertial confined dynamics through data analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students at this level are expected to have a solid foundation in mechanics and thermodynamics, along with some basic concepts of statistics.
#### Constraints
- The harmonic potential is not a very good representation of the real confinement potential in the large time scale; higher terms in the potential approximation should be included.
- Due to limitations imposed by temporal and spatial sampling, experimental PSD results cannot be fitted directly by the model expression.

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding Langevin dynamics and Brownian motion
- acquiring statistical methods applicable where stochastic processes emerge
- conceptualizing out-of-equilibrium phenomena through experimentation
