# Integrated PBL: a two-stage teaching model combining clinical cases as the clue with PBL teaching as the core, layered on lecture-based classroom teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The integrated PBL model organizes undergraduate medical teaching into two stages: a basic-medicine stage (6th semester) focusing on linking organ structure, function and pathology to symptoms and signs, and a clinical-medicine stage (8th semester) focusing on case analysis and clinical skills. It integrates "basic and clinical medicine courses by using clinical cases as the clue and PBL teaching as the core", delivered in 48 credit hours per stage within small study groups of 8 or 9 students, alongside regular LBC courses. Students preview materials, discuss cases in groups led by an appointed group leader, set learning goals, self-study, report back, and receive instructor summaries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers with master degrees or above, teacher qualification certificates, and intermediate or higher professional titles, trained on PBL teaching in advance
- Course materials, cases and handouts delivered to students at least a week before class
#### Constraints
- The study applied the model in only 1 center and 1 major (clinical medicine) over 2 semesters
- Teacher shortages in China limit implementing PBL for all undergraduates

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate clinical medicine students in their 6th and 8th semesters

### Learning Goals
- Integrating basic and clinical medicine knowledge
- Case analysis and logical reasoning
- Independent learning and problem-solving
- Teamwork spirit and humanistic care literacy
