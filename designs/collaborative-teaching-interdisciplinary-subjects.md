# Teach interdisciplinary practical subjects through collaboration between the subject teacher and teachers of other school subjects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends delivering interdisciplinary subjects like PVE through multi-teacher collaboration rather than a single specialist, because collaboration pairs the PVE teacher with teachers whose specialties match curriculum units (science for agricultural and health units, arts for painting and engineering drawing, math for financial units). It found this "better improved students’ achievement and attitudes towards PVE in comparison to teaching only by the PVE teacher", and also yielded better skills training, subject integration, progress recording for guidance, and time utilization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Joint planning before each unit; both teachers attending the lessons; commitment of teachers and school administration; training of the teacher team
#### Constraints
- Lengthy planning time, timetable conflicts, team-member compatibility issues, possible student reluctance toward team-implied techniques, and difficulty in schools with teacher shortages

### Target Learners
- basic-education students studying interdisciplinary practical subjects

### Learning Goals
- practical skills mastery
- achievement in interdisciplinary content
- positive attitudes towards the subject
