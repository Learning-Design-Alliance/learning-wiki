# Scientific induction as a learning-environment framework that maps onto the four principles of productive disciplinary engagement

## Description
The article frames scientific induction as a classroom process in which students observe physical phenomena, collect evidence, postulate and test claims about mechanisms, and communicate evidence-based claims to peers for evaluation. The authors see "almost a one-to-one correspondence" between this process and Engle and Conant's four measures of productive disciplinary engagement, in which "students to be authors and producers of knowledge (…) rather than mere consumers". They use this framework to explain why inductive physics environments can include ELLs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning environments must problematize content, make students authors of evidence-based claims, hold students accountable, and provide intellectual and material resources
#### Constraints
- The article notes a big assumption regarding the level of language dexterity required for students to participate; if linguistic complexity is too high, spaces will most likely exclude emerging bilingual students

### Target Learners
- first-grade emerging bilingual students (beginner/intermediate ELLs)

### Learning Goals
- evidence-based scientific reasoning
- co-development of science concepts and English language skills
