# Three-stage student-protagonist laboratory methodology replacing the traditional script-based protocol

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an active teaching-learning methodology for physics laboratory classes in which "the student has the autonomy to propose scripts and equipment, instead of following a practice roadmap already defined". The method is split into three parts: (i) theoretical exposition, (ii) proposal seminar with theme choice and experimental design, and (iii) final seminar for exposition of the experiment carried out. Only the theoretical exposition is the teacher's responsibility; the other stages "depend on the protagonism of the students (evidently, with teacher guidance)".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A theoretical exposition stage gives students the technical-theoretical knowledge needed for the later project stages, and the teacher acts as tutor or mediator with students having autonomy
#### Constraints
- The article notes the analysis extends to other disciplines only with the reservations that the teacher attests to the safety of the experiments and that activities can be fully carried out by students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in experimental physics laboratory classes

### Learning Goals
- professional skills such as innovation, creativity, proactivity, protagonism, critical sense and scientific thinking
- relating theoretical concepts to practice through self-designed experiments
