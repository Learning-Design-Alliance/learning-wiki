# Twelve habits of mind for students in a question-driven physics classroom

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The ASKIT/A2L project identifies twelve good habits of mind — natural tendencies or willingnesses applied to mental processes — that students should develop to cope with a question-driven, interactive physics classroom. The report breaks "the desirable tendencies of students into six basic and six advanced “habits of mind”", from seeking alternative representations and comparing and contrasting to generating multiple solutions and meta-communicating. Teachers target these habits when designing and choosing items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must encourage the habits during activities and experiences, and students need opportunities to commit to answers, explain reasoning, and learn from mistakes.
#### Constraints
- The report states the advanced habits of mind are "more easily applied to later stages of learning".

### Target Learners
- high school physics students
- college physics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding
- critical thinking
- problem solving
