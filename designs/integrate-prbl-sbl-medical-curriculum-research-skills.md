# Integrate research-oriented project-based learning and simulation-based case analysis into the undergraduate medical curriculum to strengthen academic performance and originality

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding research-oriented PrBL and simulation technologies such as the Anatomage table into medical training, arguing these active formats support research competence, science communication, and academic integrity. Its exploratory results back this: "The Anatomage table aids in a better understanding of medical problems and helps in formulating research questions," and PrBL groups outperformed comparison groups on rubric scores and Turnitin originality. The authors present this as initial insight for refining competency-based curricula, not as established causal practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful integration into medical training with standardized prior academic exposure
- Faculty mentorship and common assessment rubrics across comparison groups
#### Constraints
- No direct comparison or pedagogical superiority claim between PrBL and SBL is warranted, as the cohorts differed in academic level, curricular exposure, and instructional context
- Causal attribution is limited by the non-randomised cross-sectional design and possible novelty (Hawthorne) effects

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Research competence and scientific inquiry
- Science communication and academic writing
- Clinical reasoning through case analysis
