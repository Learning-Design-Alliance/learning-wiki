# Mentoring partnership framework grounded in action research principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames student/TA mentoring partnerships through action research, whose definitions "all revolve around the three key concepts of demo- cratic participation, social justice, and community empow erment." A mentoring partnership is one where "a leader or mentor, acts as the catalyst for the partnership," focusing on relationship-building and asking questions to enact change, with partner equality crucial so mentees are not the only beneficiaries. The study applies this framework in a studio physics course, viewing data through a social constructivist lens where knowledge is built through group interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mentor who focuses on relationship-building and eliciting the mentee's needs within the partnership
- Neutralization of the power differential between mentors and mentees for social justice
#### Constraints
- The mentoring partnership is inherently unbalanced, with one individual in an experienced role, and the learning partner may resist efforts to overturn this system

### Target Learners
- college STEM students
- graduate teaching assistants

### Learning Goals
- student empowerment and voice
- rapport between students and mentors
- reduction of classroom inequity
