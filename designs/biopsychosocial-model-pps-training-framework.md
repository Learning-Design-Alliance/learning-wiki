# Use the biopsychosocial model as the theoretical framework for PPS training programmes and holistic consultations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends educators utilise the biopsychosocial model, "endorsed by several of the included studies", as a theoretical framework for developing training that addresses the multifaceted presentation of PPS. It supports moving away from the biomedical model, breaking mind–body dualism, and structuring consultations around biological, psychological and social domains so patients take agency in their own care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be clearly intended to develop psychologically informed practice working holistically at the interface of somatic and psychological care
#### Constraints
- The review reports a gap in existing programmes around explanations of psychosocial and physiological interrelating factors, and that only one included study used a tool facilitating a structured consultation

### Target Learners
- healthcare practitioners in primary and secondary care
- medical educators designing PPS curricula

### Learning Goals
- holistic assessment, treatment and management of PPS
- making the link between symptoms and psychosocial stressors
- patient self-management and agency
