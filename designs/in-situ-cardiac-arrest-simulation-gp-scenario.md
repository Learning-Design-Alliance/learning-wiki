# In situ cardiac arrest simulation scenario with actors, high-fidelity mannequin swap, and filmed debrief for whole primary care teams

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A research-team-designed in situ simulation of a cardiac arrest occurring in a GP waiting room. An actor collapses and is swapped for a high-fidelity mannequin behind a dividing screen so staff can use chest compressions and their own defibrillator; a second actor plays a distressed relative and a third an annoyed patient. The scenario ran for approximately 20 minutes followed by a roughly 45-minute debrief using 'the diamond' debriefing method, with filming tagged for the debrief. All consenting staff had an active role with no observers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actors and rehearsals to elaborate the clinical outline and pre-empt participant questions
- The centre's own emergency equipment and space appropriate for the simulation
- A separate private room for the postsimulation debrief and signage to shield the public from distress
#### Constraints
- Space constraints in each centre provided an additional challenge, though viewed as beneficial
- Filming increased participant stress levels for some

### Target Learners
- Clinical and non-clinical primary care staff (GPs, nurses, healthcare assistants, managers, receptionists, administration)

### Learning Goals
- Managing acute medical emergencies as a team
- Interprofessional teamwork and communication
- Familiarity with on-site emergency equipment
