# High-fidelity immersive simulation with think-aloud verbal probing as a research and teaching tool for cognitive errors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents high-fidelity clinical simulation, using the SimMan simulator with actors as team members, combined with a modified think-aloud technique of verbal probes and a Likert-scale questionnaire, as a step beyond case notes and screen-based tools for studying cognitive errors. It argues that "clinical problems, recreated in a high-fidelity simulated environment, with engineered complexity, uncertainty and temporal demands, is likely to reflect the real life challenges more closely", because information arrives sequentially as in real clinical medicine. Debriefing used advocacy inquiry, pairing observations of actions with inquiry into participants' thought processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-fidelity scenarios with actors, trained assessors with expertise in critical care, and a structured cognitive-error questionnaire with adequate internal consistency
#### Constraints
- Retrospective design with no independent corroboration of assessor observations; verbal probing "can potentially introduce bias" as probes may be perceived as cues to reconsider

### Target Learners
- doctors in their first post-graduate year (PGY1 interns)

### Learning Goals
- recognition and management of cognitive errors in diagnosis synthesis and treatment decisions
