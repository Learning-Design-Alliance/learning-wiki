# Schema-based instruction (SBI) for proportional reasoning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study, `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
SBI is an instructional approach rooted in schema theory and cognitive models of mathematical problem solving, aimed at improving learning of ratios and proportional relationships. Its components are "activation of the mathematical structure of problems," representation of information using diagrams, development of procedural flexibility through selection of appropriate solution strategies, and problem solving and metacognitive strategy instruction. It integrates features such as explicit and systematic instruction, scaffolding with guided questions, corrective feedback, and frequent cumulative review, and was delivered here as two replacement units on Ratio/Proportion and Percent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher professional development covering implementation of SBI components, including sorting problems by type, using schematic diagrams, generating ballpark estimates, and selecting appropriate solution methods
- Teaching materials such as visual diagrams, problem solving checklists, teacher guide, and student workbook and homework books
#### Constraints
- In this study the intervention ran six weeks of daily instruction, below the 12-week criterion recommended in best evidence syntheses, and percent effects were not sustained at delayed posttest

### Target Learners
- Seventh-grade students with mathematics difficulties in problem solving (below the 35th percentile on a problem-solving measure)

### Learning Goals
- Proportional problem solving across ratio, proportion, and percent topics
