# Bloch-cube measurement game: X, Y, and Z questions with dice-rolling to enact the Born rule

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A reusable lesson-level pattern in which students simulate quantum measurement with a Bloch cube: they roll the cube to set a random initial state, then answer one of three questions — "X Question: |+x⟩ state or |−x⟩ state?" and similarly for Y and Z. If the question matches the face-up state the state is unchanged; otherwise the cube is rolled and the state changes to the positive or negative face of the asked basis. Students calculate the full probability table |⟨□|□⟩|² (Table III) from the superposition definitions, with matching outcomes 100% and non-matching outcomes 50%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must know the superposition definitions of |±x⟩ and |±y⟩ in the Z basis and the Born rule probability formula; a Bloch cube or a coin per student.
#### Constraints
- Probabilities are restricted to the six cube states; the article notes an alternative "would be to flip a coin".

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate students
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- understand probabilistic measurement outcomes
- apply the Born rule
- distinguish distinct from non-distinct states
