# Modified predict-observe-explain (POE) instructional design for scenario-based tutor lessons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The lessons follow a sequence in which tutors see a scenario, predict the best approach, explain their rationale, observe the research-recommended approach during an instruction phase, and complete a common posttest using both MCQs and open responses. The article states "This instructional design is considered a modified predict-observe-explain (POE) approach and is theoretically related to Gibbs' Reflective Cycle", a cyclical model structuring learning by doing. The three learning-by-doing conditions (MCQ only, open response only, or both) are embedded in the prediction phase of this design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lessons must present a scenario prompting prediction of the best approach, followed by an explanation of rationale, an instruction phase showing the research-recommended approach, and a common posttest
#### Constraints
- The design was implemented in six scenario-based advocacy lessons for training paraprofessional tutors, with Flesch-Kincaid readability spanning grades 6-9

### Target Learners
- undergraduate paraprofessional tutors supporting middle-school students

### Learning Goals
- advocacy skills including addressing microaggressions, unconscious assumptions, cultural competence, implicit bias, opportunity gaps, and helping students manage inequity
