# Study Group Meeting (SGM) program: a voluntary peer-led team-learning workshop supplementing introductory biology lectures

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SGM program is a PLTL-model supplement to two team-taught introductory biology courses at Swarthmore College, fully implemented in 2005–2006. Peer science associates selected from students successful in the course host 2-hour evening workshops twice weekly plus pre-quiz sessions, roughly 30 per semester, where students work in groups of three to seven on faculty-generated challenge questions. The article states that "Defining features of PLTL include peer leaders trained in tutorial methods; challenging questions suitable for collaborative problem solving; and designated meeting times outside lecture." Attendance is strictly voluntary and answers are not collected or graded.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer facilitators trained in pedagogical principles and practices through a weekly seminar course
- Faculty-generated challenge questions addressing higher levels of Bloom's taxonomy
- Designated meeting space and times outside lecture
#### Constraints
- Participation is strictly voluntary
- Faculty generally do not attend SGMs
- Challenge question answers are not collected or graded

### Target Learners
- First-year introductory biology students, including URM students and students with weaker biology backgrounds

### Learning Goals
- Comprehension of introductory biology lecture material
- Ability to articulate solutions to scientific problems
- Persistence as biology and STEM majors or minors
