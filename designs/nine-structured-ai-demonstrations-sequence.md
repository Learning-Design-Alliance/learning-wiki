# Sequence of nine structured instructor-led AI demonstrations for modeling strategic LLM delegation and evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A deployable classroom intervention: nine short, instructor-led AI demonstrations delivered at roughly weekly cadence, progressing from baseline tool literacy through pedagogical framing (metacognition, Socratic tutor system prompts), strategic delegation of low-risk programming tasks for a truss design project, and LLM benchmarking. As the article states, "Each demonstration occupied approximately 10–15 minutes at the end of a regularly scheduled class meeting and recurred at roughly weekly cadence." Demonstration workflows and the Socratic tutor system prompt are publicly documented in appendices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivered by the regular course instructor within existing lecture time without displacing core content
- Complete workflows and system prompt are documented and reproducible from the published appendices
#### Constraints
- Demonstrations evolved dynamically by instructor judgment rather than a rigid script, so components driving outcomes cannot be isolated
- Some timing was misaligned with student readiness and end-of-semester fatigue affected later sessions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in a core problem-solving course

### Learning Goals
- strategic LLM delegation
- model evaluation and benchmarking
- metacognitive awareness of AI's role in learning
