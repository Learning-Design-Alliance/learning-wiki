# Two high-fidelity simulated emergency scenarios used to evoke acute stress in role-playing residents

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study used two scripted emergency scenarios on a high-fidelity computer-based mannequin simulator with remotely controlled vital signs, set up like a real emergency room. One involved a fifty-five-year-old patient with right ventricular myocardial infarction progressing to cardiogenic shock and third-degree atrioventricular block; the other a twenty-six-year-old with cocaine intoxication developing unstable ventricular tachycardia. Scenarios were elaborated by three experienced facilitators and tested for two years prior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized physiologic responses programmed and activated by a physician facilitator from a control room
- All medications and equipment available in the simulated room
#### Constraints
- Scenarios were finalized 30 minutes after start regardless of clinical management

### Target Learners
- second-year internal medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- management of emergency clinical deterioration
- team performance under acute stress
