# Qubit-first learning path: a two-state axiomatic curriculum using light polarization as the physical realization of qubits

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The curriculum introduces quantum mechanics in its axiomatic structure restricted to two-dimensional Hilbert spaces, so that state vectors, superposition, measurement, unitary evolution and entanglement can be taught with elementary linear algebra. The article maintains "a continuous interplay between mathematical formalism and physical implementation", in which "Light polarization provides the experimental implementation of a qubit, polarization states correspond to vectors in a two-dimensional complex space, wave plates implement unitary transformations, while polarizers realize projective measurements." The progression runs from classical states and the CHSH card game through the two-state formalism and polarization to entanglement and Bell-inequality violation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Only elementary mathematical tools already familiar to upper-secondary students: vectors, matrices, basis transformations, complex numbers and probability
#### Constraints
- The article states the traditional historical approach requires extensive classical physics background, which this pathway avoids; the implementations described were extracurricular with more time and laboratory resources than the standard curriculum

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary school students
- in-service secondary-school physics teachers

### Learning Goals
- conceptual foundations of quantum mechanics: states, superposition, measurement, entanglement
- connection between mathematical formalism and experimental practice
