# Use trained senior students as examiners in formative OSCEs to overcome faculty examiner shortages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical faculties use students in higher semesters as examiners for formative practical examinations, because implementation of such structured formative assessments "often fails at the faculties due to the availability of examiners from the teaching staff". With precisely defined tutor training courses and checklists, student examiners produced assessments matching those of medical expert supervisors, allowing a large cohort to be assessed at low cost.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Precisely defined student tutor training courses and checklists
- Basic didactic training for the student examiners
- Supervisory second assessments for quality assurance
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single formative OSCE at one faculty; the article states it is not possible to generalize to other institutions or general conditions

### Target Learners
- Pre-clinical medical students in large cohorts

### Learning Goals
- Formative assessment of practical clinical and communication skills
