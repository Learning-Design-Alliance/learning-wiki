# Optimisation–validation–prediction–extrapolation sequence for teaching scientific modelling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Binding Blocks spreadsheet activities organise student engagement with theoretical modelling into sequential stages. As the article summarises: "In a first stage, the model is optimized, in the sense that the coefficients are determined to match a given subset of data. In a second stage, the model is validated... Finally, the third stage involves a prediction." Part 2 adds extrapolation: using the model beyond its initial domain, from nuclei to neutron stars, so students experience how models bridge systems 19 orders of magnitude apart in size and how predictions can validate or invalidate a model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simple mathematical model with visualisable spreadsheet output so students can fit, compare and predict without cumbersome derivations
#### Constraints
- The extrapolation activity may be counter-intuitive at first, though the authors state it remains entirely within the domain of scientific exploration

### Target Learners
- secondary school A-level students

### Learning Goals
- key stages of scientific modelling
- understanding of optimisation, validation, prediction and extrapolation
- role of modelling in science, its advantages and potential limitations
