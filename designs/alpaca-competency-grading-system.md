# ALPaCA: Assessment of Learning Proficiency and Competency Achievement grading system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
ALPaCA is a competency-based grading model for calculus-based introductory physics in which a student's grade is determined from achievement of competency in six broadly defined content areas, plus homework and class participation. Grades per content area are converted from accumulated points across cumulative exams, and the final course grade is the geometric mean of the eight individual grades, which the authors note incentivizes "students to continually improve their competency in all course content." The initial version used three section exams and a comprehensive final with individual and group portions; later iterations use weekly single-content-area quizzes. The article evaluates its use in PHSX 211 at the University of Kansas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must demonstrate proficiency with content on more than one assessment
- Students who fail an initial assessment have sufficient subsequent opportunities to demonstrate proficiency
#### Constraints
- The initial implementation is described as cumbersome and somewhat complicated
- The point thresholds and broadly defined content areas may not appropriately assess proficiency, per the authors' limitations section
- Comparisons with pre-ALPaCA performance are problematic due to differing grading methodologies (curved pre-ALPaCA grades, fewer assessment opportunities)

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in calculus-based introductory physics, mostly physical sciences and engineering majors

### Learning Goals
- proficiency with course content across six physics content areas, including retention of knowledge and skills
