# Structuring PLCs with clear purpose and collaborative-work frameworks (High-Leverage Team Actions)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because PLCs without clarity of purpose may drift toward undirected planning or devolve into coffee chats, MiC districts organized PLCs more intentionally over time. Tim Kanold's High-Leverage Team Actions (HLTAs), 10 activities for collaborative work based on NCTM's Principles to Actions, encouraged "a focus on mathematics (with activities organized around a unit of study) and a process for iterative improvement of a PLC's capacity." Dinuba paired HLTAs with coach support, PLC-leader meetings, principal sessions, process maps, and reflection rubrics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structures that guide clarity of purpose for teachers' collaborative time
- Support such as coach guidance, leader capacity-building, and monitoring (e.g., PLC team logs, reflection data)
#### Constraints
- Research cited in the report notes teachers do not always find PLCs helpful learning structures, and fostering collaboration is difficult to do well and not a guaranteed path to improved outcomes

### Target Learners
- K–8 mathematics teachers in PLC teams
- PLC leaders
- Site administrators

### Learning Goals
- Improving collaborative teacher learning around mathematics instruction, high-cognitive-demand tasks, and formative assessment
