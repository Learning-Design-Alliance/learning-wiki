# Three improv-theater principles—active listening, "yes-and," and the "button"—form a framework for structuring unplanned instructor-student interactions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that spontaneous instructor-student interactions share characteristics with improvisational theater and can be guided by three improv principles. It states these principles "embody widely-studied theories of physics education yet provide succinct, actionable reminders of how to put these theories into practice." The framework assigns each principle a role: opening the conversation in a shared direction, progressing toward comprehension, and underscoring a general principle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor must be willing to treat unplanned interactions as requiring thinking on one's feet rather than following a script
#### Constraints
- Principles are framed for unplanned interaction spaces such as office hours, recitations, and in-lecture questions, not for pre-planned course design

### Target Learners
- physics students in introductory university courses

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of physics
- productive instructor-student dialogue
