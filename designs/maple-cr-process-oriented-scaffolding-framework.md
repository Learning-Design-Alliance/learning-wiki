# MAPLE-CR process-oriented scaffolding framework: cognitive, social-interactional, and regulatory scaffolding for AI-supported PBL

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a process-oriented view of scaffolding for AI-supported PBL, defining scaffolding as "structured support embedded in the learning workflow to help learners perform reasoning, interaction, and reflection activities that would otherwise require sustained tutor facilitation." MAPLE-CR instantiates three complementary scaffolding forms: cognitive scaffolding (prompts for hypothesis generation, evidence justification, revision, and synthesis), social-interactional scaffolding (simulated tutor and peer roles sustaining dialogue and externalization), and regulatory scaffolding (workflow structuring, objective-coverage monitoring, phase transitions, and feedback loops). The framework treats scalability as preserving the cognitive, dialogic, and feedback-regulatory functions that make PBL educationally meaningful rather than merely automating human labor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an LLM-supported multiagent environment with tutor, peer, evaluator, and advisor roles, a shared data and knowledge layer, and case-linked pretests and posttests aligned with learning objectives.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the study did not isolate specific scaffolding effects, and simulated group-process behaviors varied across sessions, so AI is unlikely to fully reproduce the nuance of human facilitation.

### Target Learners
- First-year postgraduate medical interns (resident interns)

### Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning
- Diagnostic hypothesis generation and justification
- Collaborative discussion and reflection skills
