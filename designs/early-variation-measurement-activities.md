# Build early statistical thinking on measurement activities that let students observe, represent and contrast variation in both hand-drawn and software formats

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends an early instructional focus on variation and distribution through meaningful measurement activities. Students measure, observe the spread of values, create representations of their own choosing by hand and in software, and contrast two scenarios that embody different sources of variation. The authors conclude that findings "highlight the importance of an early focus on variation and distribution, with meaningful activities that motivate students to conduct and observe measurements, together with creating both hand-drawn and software representations to relate their experiences."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must be aligned with the school curriculum so they form a core component rather than add-on learning experiences, as the authors state for their design
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence comes from year 4 classes in one middle socio-economic school within a 3-year design study; teachers needed preparatory meetings providing professional development on the core statistical ideas

### Target Learners
- elementary school students, specifically year 4 (fourth grade)

### Learning Goals
- appreciation of variation in data and measurement
- representing and describing variability
- using variability to make comparisons
- informal inference and prediction
