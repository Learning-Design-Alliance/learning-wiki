# Aviation type-rating model as a framework for multi-platform robotic surgical training and credentialing

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article draws an analogy between robotic surgical training and aviation credentialling across different aircraft, described as type rating. In aviation, type rating is "an approved and standardised course of training which upon successful completion; the pilot is qualified to fly a particular type of aircraft", involving simulator exercises, recurrent training, line checks and maintenance of recency to avoid skills fade. Manufacturers design aircraft for similarity to allow cross-licensing, and the article suggests robotic platforms may similarly be cross-credentialed when risk metrics show they are not dissimilar enough to cause harm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardised training courses per platform type
- Recurrent training and recency requirements to prevent skills fade
- Assessment of platform similarity based on operation feel, console similarities and critical risk management
#### Constraints
- Robotic surgical training standards have not yet reached the example set in the aviation industry
- The decision-making behind cross-licensing similar aircraft is complex and only analogous

### Target Learners
- early robotic surgeons training across multiple robotic operating platforms

### Learning Goals
- safe and proficient console operation across multiple robotic platforms
