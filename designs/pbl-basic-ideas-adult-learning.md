# Problem-based learning built on adult learning principles as a foundation for patient education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames PBL as an adult-learning-principles-based educational model for patient education. It states that "The basic ideas of PBL are to: have an investigative approach to learning, take responsibility for the learning, use real-life situations, reflect on one's own learning". In COR-PRIM, PBL is used as the foundation for the learning process, with patients' beliefs about self-care identified and incorporated as the starting point for education, on the premise that learning based on patients' beliefs is necessary for effective patient education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors trained in the central characteristics of PBL, e.g. learner-centred, self-directed, real life situations and problem-solving
#### Constraints
- The article notes most education programs for patients with cardiac problems have not been based on such adult learning principles

### Target Learners
- adult patients with chronic disease, here coronary heart disease

### Learning Goals
- self-management and self-care behaviour change
