# Blended learning design for evidence-based medicine: pre-class materials and subgroup debriefing plus instructor summary

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's blended learning intervention combines 'internet plus' resources with a flipped classroom: one week before class students receive "a learning material including short videos, handouts, and knowledge maps" plus advance questions; students self-form into subgroups and create debriefing slides of learning outcomes. In class, subgroups debrief, the instructor comments and scores reports on "content integrity, response to questions, production of slides, and fluency of presentation," then gives a detailed summary targeting common problems and distributes multimedia resources for review. ## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must invest time in transition to the learning pattern and pre-class preparation of resources such as videos and handouts
- Classroom equipment including projectors, screen, and appropriate lighting
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness was affected by the time, energy, and experience invested by the teachers

### Target Learners
- first-year medical postgraduates

### Learning Goals
- mastering evidence-based medicine basic theory (question-finding, evidence grading, evidence retrieval)
