# Flipped model of instruction as pre-class electronic delivery of lecture content with in-class application

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The flipped classroom moves direct instruction outside class, usually via electronic means such as videos, and transfers application assignments into classroom time. The article describes it as attempting to move the lecture outside of class and transferring "the application assignments, formerly homework, into the classroom". In this study, learners watched instructor-made reading videos before class, then spent class time reflecting on, discussing, and practicing what they had learned under teacher supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must watch the instructional videos at home before class
- Technology access for viewing lecture material at home
#### Constraints
- Dependent on student participation; there is no way to guarantee students will watch the lectures at home
- Inequities in students' home lives can be a barrier where students lack technology access

### Target Learners
- Intermediate-level Iranian EFL learners aged 19-25 at a private language institute

### Learning Goals
- EFL reading comprehension
- Learner autonomy and engagement in language learning
