# Project-Based Learning as a constructivist, student-centred vocabulary teaching method

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes PBL as "a teaching/learning approach which organizes students' learning activities around projects", introduced into second and foreign language education to reflect student-centred teaching principles and commonly associated with constructivist theory. It shifts the focus from teacher telling to learning by doing, uses real-life activities, and provides comprehensible input and output opportunities. In the study it was operationalised as group projects (4-5 learners) applying each week's six new lexical items and reporting them orally and in writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects require learners to investigate complex issues relevant to the lesson topic
- Group work with opportunities to negotiate and communicate with peers
#### Constraints
- The article notes that PBL class design, project duration and group size are very context-specific

### Target Learners
- EFL learners aged 16-18 at elementary level in a private language institute

### Learning Goals
- Recall and retention of new English vocabulary
- Collaborative and active learning skills
