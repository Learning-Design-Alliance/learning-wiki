# Platform-based active avoidance (PBA) paradigm

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The PBA paradigm is an operant defensive-learning task in which an auditory tone CS predicts a footshock US and mice avoid shock by moving onto a central platform safe zone within the first 5 s of CS onset. The authors developed it because it "eliminates appetitive reinforcement", letting animals learn "to avoid shock by moving onto a permanently available platform without any competing reward demands", thereby isolating threat-driven avoidance and defensive response selection from food-motivation confounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A permanent safe zone (8 × 8 cm platform) continuously available in the operant chamber; an auditory CS (12 kHz, 50 dB) coterminating with a footshock US; trial-by-trial scoring of platform entry within the first 5 s of CS onset by an experimenter blind to shock intensity and group.
#### Constraints
- Continuous platform availability produces stay behaviour (conditioning stay rate: 46%; retention test stay rate: 15%), so passive occupancy must be separated from cue-evoked entries when interpreting avoidance learning.

### Target Learners
- mice (CalcaCre/+ and C57BL/6J, approximately 3-6 months old)

### Learning Goals
- cue-evoked active avoidance (stimulus-response associative learning)
- defensive response selection between passive and active strategies
