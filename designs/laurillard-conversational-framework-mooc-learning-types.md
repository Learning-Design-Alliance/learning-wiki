# Laurillard's conversational framework and its six learning types as an organizing lens for MOOC activity design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article uses Laurillard's conversational framework, adopted by the FutureLearn platform, to classify MOOC media activities by the learning experience they elicit: acquisition, discussion, inquiry, practice, collaboration, and production. The framework posits four phases (discursive, interactive, adaptive, reflective) in which learning proceeds through dialogue between teacher and learners and among learners. The authors mapped each iterative digital literacy intervention to a learning type goal (e.g., the poll task as "Practice: students could apply their understanding of the concepts surrounding resource evaluation") and argue that "a range of activities designed to elicit different learning styles [36] was key in the development of the online resource evaluation skills" for their diverse learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must be sequenced so learners develop understanding, apply it in a scaffolded environment, and progress to more complex individual or small-group tasks
