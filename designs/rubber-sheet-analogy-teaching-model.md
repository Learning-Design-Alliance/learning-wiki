# The rubber sheet analogy as a teaching model for space-time, with known weaknesses to be addressed explicitly

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The rubber sheet analogy (RSA) compares the fabric of space-time to a stretched rubber sheet: a small weight produces a warp, and a thrown marble falls toward the weight or turns around it. The article treats the RSA as a powerful teaching model for the key GR idea that "mass tells spacetime how to curve, and spacetime tells mass how to move", while dedicating its second educational card to the model's weaknesses so the teacher is fully aware of the model being used.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit teacher awareness of the analogy's limits, addressed in the second educational card; appropriate selection of marbles and weights for the phenomena to be shown
#### Constraints
- The analogy hides that space-time is four-dimensional, that it has a temporal dimension, that curvature around massive objects is symmetric in all dimensions, and that there is no friction in the Universe like there is on the sheet

### Target Learners
- High school students

### Learning Goals
- Qualitative comprehension of space-time curvature, gravity, and GR phenomena without resorting to equations
