# A postulation-minimizing formalism for teaching senior undergraduate electromagnetism

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a course-level theoretical framework in which electromagnetic theory is derived rather than postulated. It starts from the static case and is 'strictly based on Helmholtz theorem of uniqueness of vector fields, on primary experimental observations upon the sources of electric and magnetic fields, on Lenz's law and on principles of superposition and reciprocity of interactions'. From this base, all laws and rules arise without further postulation, with electricity and magnetism treated in parallel, and Maxwell's equations built up step-by-step. The approach is positioned as a mean term between conventional axiom-based textbooks and highly synthetic axiomatic works.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need the vector algebra and vector calculus background, particularly the theorem of Helmholtz, reviewed at the start of the course
- Assumes, critically, the validity of Newtonian mechanics and Galilean transformations
#### Constraints
- Presented only for electromagnetic systems in a vacuum (microscopic theory); macroscopic theory is deferred to a later part
- Helmholtz theorem use is almost entirely restricted to the stationary case

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- unitary understanding of electromagnetic theory
- preparation for Maxwell's equations
- conceptual understanding of field sources
