# Archimedes submarine engineering design unit

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A project-based unit in which grade 8 students act as mechanical engineers answering a public contract to build a stable submarine that moves up, down, and forward while carrying a load, using fluid transfers and predominantly recycled or reclaimed materials. Students "design, build, and test their own prototypes" across design, blueprint approval, iterative build-test cycles, and final scored trials, and write a report linking theory to their device.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pneumatics, hydraulics, and density are taught before the project
- A water-filled testing tank, plus syringes (60 mL), clear plastic tubing, tubing connectors, straws, glue, and balloons available for prototypes
- A rubric and report directions; the report accounts for half of the overall project points
#### Constraints
- Stability while carrying the load is the most challenging aspect of the project
- Student frustration may arise by about the tenth test
- Students tended to reflect negatively on the report requirement

### Target Learners
- grade 8 science students (adaptable to any grade level where the Archimedes principle is covered)

### Learning Goals
- Archimedes principle and density
- fluid properties and the behavior of materials immersed in water
- engineering design and iterative testing
