# Three binary-system types distinguishable by gravitational wave signal shape

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes the activity's science content around three binary systems whose gravitational wave signals differ in shape: compact star binaries, coalescing binaries, and extreme mass ratio binaries. It states that "astrophysical systems can be distinguished by their gravitational wave signals," and the activity asks participants to match templates for each type to noisy data. This taxonomy forms the basis of the matching task and the post-activity discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Theoretical calculations that model the expected gravitational wave signal from astrophysical systems, called templates
#### Constraints
- At the level of this icebreaker, participants only become familiar with the idea that systems are distinguishable by their signals; detailed source information such as masses and distance is left to a follow-on classroom extension

### Target Learners
- students and adults in classes, workshops and meetings

### Learning Goals
- understanding that astrophysical systems can be distinguished by their gravitational wave signals
- introducing gravitational wave astronomy content
