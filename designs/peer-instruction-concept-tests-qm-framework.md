# Peer instruction with concept tests as a formative assessment framework for quantum mechanics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Peer instruction, popularized by Mazur, structures lecture time around multiple-choice concept tests tied to core concepts: students first answer individually, then "discuss with a partner the answers to the concept test questions and then they are polled" by clickers, cards, or hands before instructor-led discussion guided by the answer distribution. The authors adapt this framework to QM, embedding research-based concept tests at 10-15 minute intervals in lectures and exploiting verbal, graphical, and mathematical representations in sequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Concept test questions prepared in advance with distractors based on common difficulties
- Instructor discussion of correct answers after polling, adapted to the distribution of student answers
#### Constraints
- Some questions require basic knowledge of calculus and linear algebra; complicated mathematical manipulations are not involved in any concept tests

### Target Learners
- junior-senior level undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics, including bound and scattering states
