# Physics-department DS/ML courses should differentiate themselves by using physics examples and datasets and leaving foundational AI pedagogy to computer science courses

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A design principle for courses developed in physics departments at the DS/ML intersection: they must distinguish themselves from other courses and be valued by their students by leaning into HEP strengths rather than duplicating computer science offerings. The authors advise leaving foundational AI pedagogy to CS courses and using physics data wherever possible, e.g. "Classiﬁcation of jets and galaxies over cats and dogs", so the course remains recognizably a physics course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The course must remain a physics course taught in the Physics Department, using as many physics examples and datasets to support instruction as possible.
#### Constraints
- Foundational AI pedagogy is left to the CS courses; the authors locate HEP strengths in the analysis and interpretation of large scientific data sets and physics-inspired AI.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate physics students in physics-department DS/ML courses

### Learning Goals
- machine learning and data science applied to physics data analysis
