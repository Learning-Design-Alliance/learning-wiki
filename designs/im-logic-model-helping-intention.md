# Intervention mapping with a logic model of change linking knowledge, empathetic skills, and self-efficacy to helping intention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Intervention mapping (IM) is described as "a systematic method for developing, intervening, and implementing health promotion programs by integrating theories, empirical evidence, and practical input", rooted in the socioecological model. In this project, the logic model of change defined helping intention toward hypothetical friends with cancer as the short-term primary outcome, with knowledge, empathetic coping skills, and self-efficacy as intrapersonal mediators, and three long-term performance objectives (reduced public bias, improved responses to disclosure, support aligned with survivors' aspirations). Change objectives were mapped to personal determinants in a matrix, and theoretical change methods were drawn from stigma and discrimination, protection motivation, social cognitive, and learning theories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multidisciplinary planning team reaching consensus on the logic model of the problem and of change
- Empirical evidence, literature reviews, and user needs data to populate each IM step
#### Constraints
- The logic model served as a theoretical framework rather than a definitive causal pathway
- Quality of life of cancer survivors was the ultimate goal but was not directly measured in the model
- No specific performance objective or change objectives were established for environmental impact

### Target Learners
- Adult public with no history of cancer diagnosis

### Learning Goals
- Increase helping intention toward friends with cancer
- Increase accurate knowledge of cancer and cancer survivors
- Acquire empathetic coping strategies
