# Use Leidenfrost-levitated liquid nitrogen droplets instead of rolling ball bearings in rotating saddle demonstrations to eliminate friction-induced misconceptions

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends replacing rolling spheres with LN2 droplets, which levitate via the Leidenfrost effect on a vapor cushion, "eliminating rolling dynamics and greatly reducing friction". This makes the motion closely approximate frictionless point-particle dynamics, produces a sharply defined stability threshold, and, in the authors' words, "effectively eliminating common misconceptions caused by frictional and rolling dynamics". The authors recommend this improved demonstration for clearly illustrating dynamic stability principles in undergraduate physics instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to liquid nitrogen and a rotating saddle apparatus; condensation must be cleared between trials to maintain the Leidenfrost effect
#### Constraints
- The comparison is based on the authors' own setup with saddles of β = 1.06 and β = 2.67; threshold frequencies are specific to that geometry

### Target Learners

### Learning Goals

- avoiding misconceptions from frictional dynamics
