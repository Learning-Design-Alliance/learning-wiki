# Multiuser immersive VR pediatric sepsis resuscitation simulation scenario (Unity, Oculus Rift)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A VR simulation scenario coded in Unity and accessed via an Oculus Rift headset connected to a VR-capable laptop, allowing two participants (a nurse and a physician) to move freely and interact within a virtual high-acuity room. The virtual patient was an 8-year-old male with pneumonia and sepsis, with dynamic changes in mental status, perfusion, and respiratory status. The room included a vital sign monitor, intravenous pole with fluids, oxygen delivery device, stethoscope, thermometer, and syringes. First-person audiovisual data from both users were recorded for analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 5-minute orientation to VR navigation and equipment functionality from a trained facilitator before the scenario begins
#### Constraints
- Nonverbal communication was limited: only participants' virtual hands and glasses were visible, so eye contact and body language could not be assessed

### Target Learners
- pediatric critical care and emergency medicine attendings and fellows
- pediatric ICU and emergency department nurses

### Learning Goals
- recognition and initial management of pediatric sepsis and developing shock
