# Complementary-affordances account: AI dialogue as supported monologue versus human dialogue as interactional-competence practice

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's explanatory framework holds that AI and human dialogue "create distinct but complementary conditions for L2 practice." AI dialogue "structurally approximates supported monologue": sustained output with fewer, longer turns, slower responses, less floor competition, and more fluent within-turn production, shifting learners "from reactive turn management to more deliberate, content-focused production." Human dialogue instead exercises interactional competence through rapid turn exchange, floor competition, and negotiation sequences. The framework predicts AI's pedagogical contribution is specifically structural (syntactic priming), while human partners scaffold turn competition, clarification requests, repair, and collaborative floor management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- The AI condition reflects one system configuration (model, voice, prompt, VAD setting, and task interface) with university German learners performing one task type
- Uptake measures cannot fully separate priming from parallel activation driven by shared task context

### Target Learners
- second- and third-year university L2 German learners

### Learning Goals
- L2 spoken interactional fluency
- lexical and morphosyntactic uptake
- interactional competence
