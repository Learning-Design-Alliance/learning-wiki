# Five key features of successful graduate-education programs: active voluntary participation, tiered engagement, practical experience, reflection, and community

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' analysis of the PFP program and the Teaching and Learning Physics course identifies five features contributing to the programs' success and durability. As printed: "First, students are active participants who help construct the program, not merely observers, and participation is generally voluntary. Second, the programs provide tiered levels of participation... Third, participants can engage in direct, practical experiences in teaching and in education research. Fourth, the programs provide a framework for reflection... Finally, the programs build and support a community of physicists with shared commitments to education." The pattern operates at the program level, structuring graduate students' engagement with teaching and education research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A program organizer and sustained graduate student interest in the issues addressed by the program
- Recognition of the program as legitimate within physics, and a flexible format reflecting participants' and organizer's interests
#### Constraints
- Except for modest funding, official administrative support has not been essential and has lagged behind bottom-up support, per the authors

### Target Learners
- physics graduate students at research universities

### Learning Goals
- preparation for roles as educators
- understanding of physics education research
- professional and career development
