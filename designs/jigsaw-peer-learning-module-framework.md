# Jigsaw peer learning as a social-constructivist, student-centred alternative to teacher-led tutorials

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames the peer learning module (PLM) as a student-centric strategy grounded in a social constructivism model in which students "actively construct knowledge by interacting with peers". Its instantiation, the jigsaw technique, gives each member a particular piece of learning material to master and then teach to peers, promoting accountability, collaboration and communication. The article positions PLM against conventional tutorials within a shift from educator-centred to learner-centred medical education under CBME requirements for interactive sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups with each member responsible for mastering and explaining an assigned subtopic
- A faculty member acting as facilitator rather than lecturer
#### Constraints
- Requires adequate time; students reported less time for discussion than tutorials
- Depends on every member explaining effectively; a failed explanation leaves peers with knowledge gaps

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- physiology conceptual knowledge
- communication and teamwork skills
- active participation and engagement
