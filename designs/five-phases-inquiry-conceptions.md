# Five phases of teachers' conceptions of inquiry, from ambiguous talk to coherent and consistent implementation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a five-phase model describing how participating teachers' conceptions of inquiry evolved: ambiguous talk, realization of unclear ideas, collective development of a shared definition, coherent and consistent dialogue, and coherent implementation in practice. The authors state "the Streamline teachers began the study talking ambiguously about inquiry (Phase I), but through our common activities and talk came to realize that their own ideas on the topic were unclear (Phase II)." Conference presentations were treated as evidence of Phase IV.
