# Undergraduate anesthesiology internship model with supervised and independent clinical shifts

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A clinical education model in which final-term anesthesiology students who have reached appropriate competence complete some clinical courses with an instructor present and some independently in hospital wards, working under the direct supervision of an anesthesiologist and an anesthesia expert. In this implementation each student spent 51 hours per internship unit, with 60% of hours in the morning shift and 40% in evening plus night shifts in general and gynecological operating rooms. The article evaluates it via SWOT-based qualitative content analysis, finding both significant strengths and correctable weaknesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Final-term students who have reached appropriate levels of competence
- Direct supervision of an anesthesiologist and an anesthesia expert
- Strong basic-course teaching so students acquire sufficient skills and knowledge before the internship
#### Constraints
- Implemented in a Type 3 university without anesthesia residents or graduate students, which the authors state limits generalizability
- More difficult to apply in non-teaching hospitals

### Target Learners
- Final-year undergraduate anesthesiology students

### Learning Goals
- Clinical competence and skills in operating room and anesthesia practice
- Independence, self-confidence, and clinical decision-making
- Professional identity and patient-centered care
