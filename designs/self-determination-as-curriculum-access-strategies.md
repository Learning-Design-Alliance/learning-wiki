# Self-determination skills function as access strategies that supplement students' means of reaching the general curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames self-determination not merely as a personal competency but as a functional mechanism of curricular access: teaching these skills supplies students with additional means of engaging the general curriculum. As the authors put it, "Promoting self-determination provides students with disabilities with additional strategies to access the general curriculum." In this account, problem-solving and study planning operate as strategies students deploy to participate in standards-based academic work. The framework was substantiated in an intervention with middle school students with intellectual disabilities in which both skill groups outperformed an alternating control condition.
