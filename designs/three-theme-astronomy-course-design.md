# Course built on three overarching themes: light as astronomers' only data, iterative scientific investigation, and students' capacity for scientific thinking

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The COSMOS astronomy course was organized around three stated themes that drove content, activities, and motivation. The first theme fixed the content (telescopes, instrumentation, properties of light, the Universe); the second shaped activities so students practiced scientific processes; the third aimed at motivation. As the authors state, "Three overarching themes of the course were: (i) all astrono mers have to study is light; (ii) scientiﬁc investigation is an iterative, mul ti-step process; and (iii) the stu- dents are capable of scientiﬁc thinking and inquiry."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities and lectures must be selected so that each reinforces one of the three themes, with content flowing from the light theme and process practice from the investigation theme.
#### Constraints
- Designed for a four-week residential enrichment program for high-school students; the authors note students were least familiar with inquiry-style learning, which had to be accounted for in design and scheduling.

### Target Learners
- high-school students in a residential summer science program

### Learning Goals
- astronomy content knowledge
- scientific investigation skills
- motivation toward science careers
