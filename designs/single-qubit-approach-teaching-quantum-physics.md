# Teaching quantum mechanics through the single qubit as the simplest quantum system

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes organizing a high-school introduction to quantum mechanics around the single quantum bit. States, operations and measurements on the qubit are systematically compared with the classical bit, so that superposition, stochastic measurement behavior, state change under measurement, and the uncertainty relation all arise from one minimal system. The authors state: "We present an approach for teaching quantum physics at high school level based on the simplest quantum system - the single quantum bit (qubit)." Applications such as quantum cryptography and the no-cloning theorem follow from the same framework, and extending to two or more qubits leads to entanglement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-school mathematics: two-dimensional vectors, matrix multiplication and scalar products
- Bloch sphere visualizations usable directly in class
- Discussion of explicit physical realizations alongside the abstract representation
#### Constraints
- The mathematical formulation of full quantum mechanics is complex and needs suited simplifications for high school students
- Entanglement and multi-qubit systems are deferred to a separate publication

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Learning Goals
- superposition principle
- stochastic behavior under measurements
- state change due to measurement
- Heisenberg uncertainty relation
