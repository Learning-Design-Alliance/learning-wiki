# Four-phase flipped classroom cycle for a nursing course: online self-directed pre-class learning, face-to-face team-based learning, simulation, and post-class assignments

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern structures each course topic as a recurring four-phase cycle. The first phase is "an online self-directed learning pre-class phase in which students reviewed assigned reading materials via the online e-campus platform"; the second is a face-to-face TBL activity beginning with an individual quiz, followed by group discussion and an appeal process; the third is a face-to-face simulation exercise based on an actual clinical case; the last phase involves completing post-class assignments, reflection reports, and evaluations on the platform. In the study, this cycle was applied to five adult-health nursing topics over 36 course hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors upload pre-class reading materials, case descriptions, assignments, and evaluation forms on an online platform two weeks before the course begins, and faculty training sessions are held to ensure consistency of content and materials.
#### Constraints
- The pattern was implemented in a classroom-based two-year RN-to-BSN program at a single private university with two campuses; students were not monitored for whether they actually reviewed the online materials.

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in a two-year RN-to-BSN bridge program

### Learning Goals
- Adult-health nursing competencies including pathophysiology, physical assessment, nursing care, and psychosocial issues for leading causes of death
