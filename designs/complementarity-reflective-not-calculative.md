# Complementarity belongs in physics instruction as a topic for reflection on the meaning of the science, not as a calculation tool

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The dissertation opens with Abraham Pais's reflection on why complementarity is treated unevenly in teaching: "Why do some textbooks not mention complementarity? Because it will not help in quantum mechanical calculations or in setting up experiments." Yet, as the quote continues, "Bohr’s considerations are extremely relevant, however, to the scientist who occasionally likes to reflect on the meaning of what she or he is doing." The dissertation adopts this framing: complementarity is not a tool for problem solving but a perspective through which students come to interpret what the science of quantum physics means. Its pedagogical materials therefore target conceptual reflection on wave-particle duality rather than computational technique.
