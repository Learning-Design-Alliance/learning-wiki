# Structured 2-day simulation curriculum combining standardized preparation, demonstration, supervised practice, and feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The training pattern used in this study: all residents first complete a baseline anatomy test and receive standardized preparatory materials (a step-by-step procedural manual and a training video comparing real and simulated wedge resections), then a structured 2-day program in which "Each session included a brief anatomy review, technical demonstrations by senior surgeons, and approximately 30 minutes of supervised, hands-on practice per trainee." Both groups trained in small teams with real-time faculty feedback and peer observation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized preparatory materials to ensure consistent foundational knowledge
- Small-team training with a trainee-to-instructor ratio of 5:1 and real-time faculty feedback
#### Constraints
- The FLS comparison curriculum focused on core laparoscopic skills and did not replicate full procedural steps

### Target Learners
- surgical residents

### Learning Goals
- thoracoscopic wedge resection technique
- anatomical recognition
- technical skill acquisition
