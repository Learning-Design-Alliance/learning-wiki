# Simulation-based education as a scaffolded learning environment bridging classroom and clinical settings across three stages of placement preparedness

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes its findings as a framework in which SBE scaffolds learning of essential clinical skills before practice placement across three critical stages of learning: priming for clinical environments, enhancing feedback literacy, and tackling complexity of clinical practice. The simulated learning environment "links classroom and clinical settings, providing learners with opportunities to practice the skills essential for their professional roles," underpinned by experiential learning. The authors contend SBE offers "the perhaps ideal scaffolded learning environment to gain these skills without patient risk."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires alignment of pre-placement SBE content with the escalating complexity students encounter on placement
#### Constraints
- The authors state SBE is not a substitute for exposure to the in-person clinical environment
- Optimal timing of SBE will vary depending on the local curriculum and learners' needs

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physical therapist students preparing for clinical placement

### Learning Goals
- Readiness for clinical placement
- Clinical skills including documentation, safety, manual handling, and subjective assessment
- Feedback literacy
