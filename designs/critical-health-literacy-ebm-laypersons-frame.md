# Critical health literacy as the organizing frame: most material elements target critical appraisal of health information

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames its material's content through Nutbeam's (2000) construct of critical health literacy, one of three dimensions of health literacy, defined as "a more advanced cognitive skill to critically analyze and use information to better control life events and situations." Most of the 18 selected elements concern step three of EBM, critically appraising evidence, and thus relate to critical health literacy. The authors argue that acquiring it lets laypersons recognize misinformation, integrate information with their own health, and participate in health policy and guideline discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Laypersons must be able to critically assess health information and research evidence and use them appropriately
#### Constraints
- The material teaches basic components of EBM and epidemiology and does not address issues around which opinions may differ, such as vaccinations

### Target Learners
- Health-conscious laypersons and patient advocates

### Learning Goals
- Critically analyzing health information and research evidence for self-care and civic participation
