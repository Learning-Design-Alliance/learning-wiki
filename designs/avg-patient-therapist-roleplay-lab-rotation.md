# AVG laboratory rotation in which students role-play both patient and therapist across diagnosis-based stations

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A reusable lab design in which students rotate through small-group stations, each built around a patient case with a specific functional level and AVG system. "Students acted as the patient to appreciate game operation, physical requirements, and level of difficulty. They also approached each station as the therapist to practice guarding skills, cueing, monitoring, modifying and considering exercise prescription". Stations were followed by a faculty-facilitated full-class debrief with guiding questions, embedded across two consecutive semesters of a PBL curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty facilitators at each station
- Patient cases already familiar from PBL tutorials
- AVG systems and games matched to patient functional levels (stability, mobility, mobility plus)
#### Constraints
- Described and evaluated in a single DPT program at one university in the Northeast United States

### Target Learners
- doctoral physical therapy students

### Learning Goals
- confidence using active video gaming as a physical therapy intervention
- guarding, cueing, and exercise prescription skills
