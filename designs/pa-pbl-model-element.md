# PA-PBL: peer-tutor-facilitated problem-based learning with clinical history-taking simulation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
PA-PBL is a blended model in which problem-based learning sessions are facilitated by trained same-grade peer tutors and added to standard lectures. Groups of "8-12 students were formed, with a peer tutor in the same grade who had underwent preparatory training prior to the activity's initiation." Each round runs four phases: preparation with department-approved cases, about 90 minutes of problem exploration in which students simulate clinical consultations and ask the peer tutor questions as they would a patient, one week of self-directed learning, and about 60 minutes of knowledge sharing with whiteboard presentations reviewed by researchers. In the trial it supplemented lectures in the Infection and Immunity module across four rounds in one academic year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematically trained peer tutors selected via nomination and self-recommendation who remain with the same group throughout
- Cases adapted and approved by the department before use
- Recorded discussions reviewed by researchers to ensure quality
#### Constraints
- Implementing PA-PBL incurs higher costs than lecture-based learning due to peer tutor training and small-group coordination resources

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate clinical medicine students

### Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- knowledge application and mastery
- teamwork and communication skills
