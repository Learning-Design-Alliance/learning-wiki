# Co-design with school practitioners as the guiding framework for developing school-based PAL programs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article positions co-design as the underlying theory guiding its methodology: an "iterative, exploratory, and cyclical process" in which school practitioners (teachers, physical education teachers, pedagogical advisors) collaborate with an interdisciplinary research team in kinesiology, neuropsychology and digital art. The process was operationalized through the Double-Diamond model, "an open innovation framework consisting of four phases: Discover, Define, Develop (co-design), and Deliver (implementation)", and was reported to produce tailored, curriculum-aligned solutions and a sense of pride that supports implementation and sustainability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration between school practitioners and an interdisciplinary research team from the start of design
- Alignment with the school's curriculum and mission
- Iterative meetings and a collaborative tool guiding prototype iterations
#### Constraints
- The article notes co-design workload was heavy for teachers (described as "a huge workload") and the process occurred during the pandemic context

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students (fourth grade, ages 9-11)

### Learning Goals
- Integrating physical activity with academic learning in mathematics and French
