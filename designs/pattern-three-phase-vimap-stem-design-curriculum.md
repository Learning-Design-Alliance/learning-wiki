# Three-phase ViMAP-Tangible STEM design curriculum

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The curriculum unfolds in three phases: individual agent-based programming of open and closed geometric shapes in ViMAP; using those shapes as computational models of continuous change over time, using events from The Lorax; and a dyadic STEM design challenge to build a hybrid physical-computational drawing machine. The article states the activities "were designed to integrate multiple domain-specific learning goals" spanning science, math, engineering and technology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the ViMAP visual programming environment with NetLogo visualization
- Ultrasonic sensors and building materials for constructing physical control structures
- Teacher modeling of design practices within a cognitive apprenticeship approach
#### Constraints
- Phases 1 and 2 are individual; only Phase 3 is collaborative in dyads
- In the reported setting, classes met once a week on six consecutive Saturday mornings

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in grades 3 and 4

### Learning Goals
- Shape drawing as computational models of change over time
- User-centered design practices
- Multiplicative, rate and coordinate reasoning
