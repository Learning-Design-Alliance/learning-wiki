# Public medical schools can use MCAT-based admissions and exam support to steer students toward workforce-needed specialties

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that public state medical schools charged with meeting state workforce needs use the predictive link between MCAT and USMLE scores when selecting or supporting students. It states that "if an institution needs to develop, for example, dermatologists, for a state that is lacking in such, the institution may be well-served to select students with high MCAT scores, or to provide extra USMLE study support." Conversely, it notes a focus on standardized exam scores may be unwarranted when a state needs more family physicians.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The institution must be a public state school with a stated workforce need in a specific specialty, and MCAT scores must be available at admission
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that if a state is in need of more family physicians, a focus on standardized exam scores over other priorities may be unwarranted

### Target Learners
- medical students at a public state medical school

### Learning Goals
- residency specialty choice aligned with state physician workforce needs
- preparation for USMLE licensing examinations
