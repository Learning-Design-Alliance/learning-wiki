# Project-based learning as a student-oriented, cooperative instructional approach originating from Dewey

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames project-based learning as an instructional approach originating from Dewey (1938) in which "students work in small groups on academic tasks" integrating multiple disciplines, producing a collective outcome over an extended time period. Projects require "a driving question or problem that directs the activities that lead to a final product" and deal with real-life problems. The article distinguishes PBL from traditional approaches as student-oriented: students define problems, collect and evaluate information, and create a product, developing autonomy and learning strategies. It uses this framework to design the magazine and wall newspaper projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects need an extended time period ranging from a few lessons to a whole year
- students must work in teams of two or more members
- projects require a driving question or problem and a final product
#### Constraints
- The article notes communicative activities can create anxiety, which socio-affective strategies within team projects are meant to address
