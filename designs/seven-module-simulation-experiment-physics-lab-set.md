# A set of 7 laboratory modules combining computer simulations with experiments for calculus-based physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a complete set of laboratory classes for a 1-year calculus-based physics course, composed of "7 modules designed to bring together experiments and computer simulations". Topics include motion with drag, pendulum with arbitrary amplitude, the Magnus effect, centripetal force, a ball rolling on shaped tracks, Doppler shift with Fourier transformation, and equipotential lines. The set was taught for 2 years at Princeton International School of Mathematics and Science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Wolfram Mathematica and a physics laboratory with equipment students can use and extend
#### Constraints
- Designed for a limited lab schedule of 40 minutes of class time once a week plus out-of-class work, 3 weeks per module
- Author notes the sequence "is not set in stone" and instructors should adjust it for their students

### Target Learners
- advanced high school students in AP physics
- college students in calculus-based physics

### Learning Goals
- performing computer simulations of physical phenomena
- designing and conducting experiments
- comparing simulation predictions with experimental data
