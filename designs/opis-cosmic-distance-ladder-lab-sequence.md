# OPIS! lab sequence organized around the cosmic distance ladder, in which students reproduce great astronomical discoveries with self-collected data

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
OPIS! is a series of eight interconnected labs for non-majors and potential majors in which students use the same research instrumentation as professionals to collect their own images and spectra and reproduce discoveries of the past 400 years. It "is built around the cosmic distance ladder, which serves as an organizing principle in most introductory astronomy courses/sequences", reinforcing classroom experiences. Labs progress from geocentric vs. heliocentric models of Venus, to asteroid parallax, RR Lyrae standard candles, the Great Debate, and 21-cm Galaxy rotation curves, with a multi-messenger capstone in development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An introductory lab in which students learn to use Skynet and the Afterglow image-analysis application precedes the data-collection labs.
#### Constraints
- More is done with archival data for phenomena that take longer than a semester to collect (e.g., Cepheid stars, Type Ia supernovae).

### Target Learners
- Undergraduates in small to very large introductory survey astronomy courses
- Non-majors and potential majors alike

### Learning Goals
- Understanding the evolution of our knowledge of Earth's place in the universe
- Measuring cosmic distances
- Gaining technical and research skills
