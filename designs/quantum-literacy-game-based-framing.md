# Quantum literacy as an educational challenge addressed through game-based visual learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article frames quantum literacy as "developed to formalize this need as an educational challenge", given that quantum computing brings both substantial benefits and risks such as threats to cybersecurity. It situates Quantum Odyssey within game-based learning, defined as "the exposure of students to games that have an educational objective to be achieved through the game play", and gamification, arguing the game's rapid and continuous feedback lets students "fail without fear when learning". The framing draws on visualization research holding that visualizations help students develop scientific concepts and manage uncertainty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visual models must strongly scaffold students' own mental models to support conceptual shifts from classical Newtonian realism to quantum methodological holism
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is "still a shortage of data and studies on STEM games and gamification" that this pilot works to counteract

### Target Learners
- non-specialists in quantum mechanics
- school students and undergraduates

### Learning Goals
- developing quantum literacy
- understanding counter-intuitive quantum computational thinking
