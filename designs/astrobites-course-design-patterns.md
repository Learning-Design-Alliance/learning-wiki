# Two documented course designs using Astrobites: student blogging modeled on the site, and journal-club paper selection with Astrobites as context source

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Two prior university courses illustrate reusable designs for incorporating Astrobites. In a Caltech inquiry-based introductory astronomy course, students maintained their own blogs to record problem-solving techniques and produce original content such as interviews with astronomers and research-topic summaries, with Astrobites presented as a model blog. In a Swarthmore journal-club-style discussion course for advanced undergraduates, students selected and summarized papers on a rotating basis, using Astrobites "as a resource for selecting interesting papers and as a source for background information and context."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In the journal-club design, students must be responsible for selecting and summarizing research papers for the week's seminar on a rotating basis
- The blog-based design requires students to record problem-solving techniques and produce original content including interviews and research summaries
#### Constraints
- These designs were reported as anecdotes from a small educator/student survey, described by the authors as illustrating only a subset of the possibilities

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate astronomy students
- advanced undergraduate astronomy students

### Learning Goals
- engagement with modern research topics
- selecting and summarizing research papers
- increasing student engagement outside the classroom
