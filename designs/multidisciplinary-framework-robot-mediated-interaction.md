# Multidisciplinary conceptual framework of child development, intercultural communication, and culturally sustaining pedagogy for robot-mediated interaction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article synthesizes child development, intercultural communication theory, and culturally sustaining pedagogy into a framework for designing robot-mediated collaborative interaction. CSP is described as an approach that "respects, maintains, and builds on children's diverse languages, cultures, and identities," and intercultural communication theory highlights newcomers' gradual adaptation through prolonged exposure in nonjudgmental, supportive climates. The framework guided both the design of loosely structured fantasy play, personalized invitations, familiar contexts, and bilingual communication, and the interpretation of the qualitative data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small-group settings of two children with one robot, which the authors describe as the most effective way of using technology with children
#### Constraints
- Natural dialogue was not possible because natural language processing and automatic speech recognition for children is still developing

### Target Learners
- culturally and linguistically diverse kindergarten children

### Learning Goals
- positive collaborative peer interaction
- cultural and social integration
