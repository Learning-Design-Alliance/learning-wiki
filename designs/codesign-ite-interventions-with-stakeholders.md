# Co-design initial teacher education interventions with stakeholders to improve acceptance, adoption, and sustainability

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a participatory co-design approach for initial teacher education interventions, relying on stakeholder knowledge, which is common in the health education field. The authors state that "Intervention programs in initial teacher education co-designed with stakeholders have proven to be more efficient than informing an intervention based solely on the dissemination of information" and that co-design including stakeholder ideas is more likely to be accepted, adopted, and sustained. In this study, initial design principles from the systematic review and stakeholder interviews are used with the unit coordinator to co-design the final-year unit, keeping the same rationale, outcomes, structure, and assessment type.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Co-design must involve the unit coordinator and lecturer, informed by design principles derived from a systematic review and semistructured stakeholder interviews
#### Constraints
- The co-designed unit must retain the same rationale, outcomes, structure, and assessment type as the existing unit

### Target Learners
- final-year HPE preservice teachers

### Learning Goals
- health literacy and eHL capabilities
- health literacy teaching practices
