# Graduation coaching logic model: proactive identification, individualized student plans, and data-driven monitoring within a constructivist socio-cultural framework

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The graduation coaching logic model is a case-management-based intervention adapted from health care and social services, in which a coach "developing/monitoring/modifying an educational development plan" meets weekly with at-risk students, arranges tutoring, liaises between school and home, and monitors academic progress at least weekly. Grounded in constructivist and socio-cultural theory, coaches help students "co-construct viewpoints from which they can understand how their personal world and their educational world intersect." The model was implemented in a rural high school with data-driven classification dashboards, real-time gradebook access, and an Activity Reporting System supporting formative and summative evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated graduation coach (half-time in this study) with access to electronic gradebook and confidential student records
- Cooperation from students and parental involvement and support
- Referral programs and support services for individual students
#### Constraints
- Coaching is a resource and staff intensive process
- Coaching may not be the right support service for all students; students not responding within a reasonable period may be removed from the caseload
- Graduation coaching may not be enough to help students overcome academic problems because of the complexity of personal and behavioral problems

### Target Learners
- at-risk 9th-grade high school students in rural settings

### Learning Goals
- improve core-course academic performance
- promote student retention and progression to graduation
