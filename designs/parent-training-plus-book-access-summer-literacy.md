# Combining book access with trained parent involvement is a promising approach to improving literacy skills over the summer

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that providing access to appropriate books and maximizing the role of parents could serve as an effective approach to improving literacy skills, based on the RCT findings of gains in comprehension, fluency, and receptive vocabulary. Parents were trained in paired reading plus comprehension, fluency, and vocabulary strategies, and children received weekly interest- and level-matched books. The authors recommend school boards implement this type of intervention for children in Grades 3 to 6 after replication in larger-scale studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents attend a training session and receive ongoing support (e.g., periodic telephone calls) to implement paired reading and complementary strategies
#### Constraints
- Effects not yet replicated in larger-scale studies; the comprehensive design does not allow attribution to specific components

### Target Learners
- Grade 3 and Grade 5 children with low to average reading and vocabulary skills

### Learning Goals
- reading comprehension
- oral reading fluency
- receptive vocabulary
