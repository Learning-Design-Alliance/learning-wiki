# Forsyth’s theory-based conceptual model of maternal HIV/AIDS and child behaviour outcomes

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The conceptual model underpinning the NIH-funded Kgolo Mmogo study explains variation in children's psychosocial adjustment in terms of risk and protective factors. "The theory-based conceptual model, as designed by Forsyth (2005), regards maternal HIV/ AIDS and unsafe social environments as risk factors for negative child behavior outcomes." The broader study's aims were to assess the effectiveness of a theory-based support intervention for mothers and children and to identify maternal, medical and psychological factors and child-related mediating variables contributing to adaptive functioning. The model draws on components of interventions previously used in African communities and frames the support group intervention as a way to enhance resilience opportunities and adaptive behaviour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions built on the model address both maternal (coping, parenting) and child (coping, social skills) levels, consistent with the model's multi-factor account of adjustment.
#### Constraints
- The article reports the model as designed for young children (6–10 years) of HIV-infected mothers in South Africa.

### Target Learners
- Children aged 6–10 years affected by maternal HIV/AIDS
- HIV-positive mothers

### Learning Goals
- Adaptive functioning and resilience in children facing parental chronic illness
