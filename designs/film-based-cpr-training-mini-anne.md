# Film-based CPR training method with individual Mini Anne manikins and a 31-minute Swedish instruction film

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A standardised whole-class CPR training method advocated by the Swedish Resuscitation Council, in which students each use an individual training manikin (Mini Anne) and follow "instructions in Swedish on a 31 min film" to practise identifying cardiac arrest, raising the alarm, compressions, ventilations and the recovery position, per ERC guidelines 2010. The teacher acts as a facilitator who introduces the lesson, starts and pauses the film, gives advice and answers questions. Skills were measured with the modified Cardiff test (12 items, total score 12–48 points) on a Resusci Anne manikin.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires individual training manikins for all students, the instruction film, and a teacher trained (here 5 hours) to act as facilitator
#### Constraints
- Training was delivered in Swedish only; the instructors acted only as facilitators, and the article notes an individual instructor's enthusiasm and/or characteristics may affect students' learning

### Target Learners
- 13-year-old seventh-grade students in compulsory school

### Learning Goals
- Basic life support skills: identifying cardiac arrest, calling emergency services, chest compressions, ventilations, recovery position
