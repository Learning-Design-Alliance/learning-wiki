# University of Oslo computational physics curriculum: computation integrated from the first semester through project-based courses and computational essays

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The physics curriculum at the University of Oslo integrates computation throughout the degree. The article states that "The computational elements of the curriculum are structured as follows: at the beginning of their bachelor's studies, students are taught the basics of calculus, mechanics, programming, and computational modeling." First-semester courses give a Python baseline; later courses add computational essays, an astronomy course with a large optional project, a C++ computational physics course with five large projects, and a project-based machine learning course where students implement models from scratch. Group work on projects adds collaboration challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Later courses assume a degree of competence with computation established by the first-semester programming and computational modeling courses.
#### Constraints
- The study population is specific to this Norwegian program; most students enter with some high-school programming experience since 2020.

### Target Learners
- physics bachelor's and master's students

### Learning Goals
- programming for scientific applications
- numerical methods such as Runge-Kutta and Markov chain Monte Carlo
- collaborative computational project work
