# Avoid adding cautionary 'be careful with your back' messaging to cognitive interventions for chronic low back pain

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that cognitive interventions for LBP should not mix reassurance about spinal robustness with cautionary advice. It states that "By adding even a little caution at the end of such a cognitive session, the patient's likelihood of acquiring better coping strategies may be reduced", and that in a non-injury model one is devoid of such caution. The principle follows from the authors' interpretation that fear avoidance is a natural consequence of the traditional medical model and that double messages undermine the intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A consistent non-injury message across all clinicians and materials involved in the intervention.
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge that clinicians reared in the traditional medical model face a real chance of giving double messages.

### Target Learners
- adults with chronic low back pain, particularly those with fear-avoidance beliefs

### Learning Goals
- acquiring better coping strategies
- reducing inappropriate pain-focusing and cautious movement
