# Four-step blueprinting process for planning a representative virtual patient collection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a replicable planning process for VP collections, run by an interdisciplinary partnership across five European countries. Criteria are first defined and categorized, then data sources for representativeness checks are identified, the blueprint is populated, and consensus is reached. As the authors state, partners "developed a blueprint for a collection of 200 VPs in four steps: (1) Defining the criteria (e.g., key symptoms, age, sex) and categorizing them into disease-, patient-, encounter- and learner-related, (2) Identifying data sources for assessing the representativeness of the collection, (3) Populating the blueprint, and (4) Refining and reaching consensus." The process was motivated by the lack of guidance for designing VP collections rather than individual VPs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An international, interdisciplinary partnership able to reach consensus, plus identified external data sources (WHO, EUROSTAT, national competency frameworks) against which representativeness is assessed
#### Constraints
- The article notes the approach was "challenging and time-consuming" and that no Scandinavian or Balkan country was represented in the consortium, so Northern or Southeastern European perspectives may be missing

### Target Learners
- medical students in European higher education

### Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning through deliberate practice with virtual patient cases
