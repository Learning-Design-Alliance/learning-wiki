# Socially-oriented persuasive strategies: social learning, upward social comparison, and competition.

## Description
The article defines "socially-oriented strategies" as "a class of persuasive strategies that provoke an intentional or unintentional change in an individual attitude or behaviour due to the influence of other people," drawing on social influence theory and Social Learning Theory. The three examined strategies are upward social comparison, in which people "compare themselves to similar others who are performing well"; social learning, where "the user learns through observation, imitation, and modelling the behaviour of others"; and competition, typically implemented as a leaderboard where users "can see their position relative to that of their peers." These strategies come from the Persuasive Systems Design Model's "social support" category of seven strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
