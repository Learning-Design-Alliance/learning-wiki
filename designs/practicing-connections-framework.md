# The practicing connections framework: instructional design organized around three connection types and three design principles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The practicing connections framework holds that deep understanding in complex domains develops when students repeatedly practice making connections among a domain's contexts and practices, its core concepts, and its key representations. The article states: "we propose three types of connections that should be the focus of instructional design: the world (including practices and contexts); core concepts, which organize the domain; and key representations used for communication and thinking within a domain." It pairs these with three pedagogical principles: making connections explicit, productive struggle, and deliberate practice over time. The framework was developed while designing an online introductory statistics textbook and is offered as a complement to skill-focused frameworks such as 4C/ID.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must be designed over long periods such as a semester-long course, since understanding in complex domains develops slowly
- A detailed analysis of what expert practice in the domain looks like must precede design decisions
#### Constraints
- The authors position the framework as a complement to frameworks such as 4C/ID, which they say apply especially well to complex skills; the framework targets domains requiring reasoning with abstract conceptual structure

### Target Learners
- Novice students in complex academic domains such as statistics, mathematics, and science

### Learning Goals
- Deep understanding and transferable knowledge in a complex domain
- Adaptive expertise: flexibly applying knowledge in novel contexts
