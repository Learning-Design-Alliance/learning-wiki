# Split-semester CURE design: traditional instruction first half, authentic research investigation second half

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course is organized as a two-phase pattern: students first build foundational knowledge through lectures, homework, and a midterm exam, then apply it to an unanswered research question in the laboratory. The article describes a CURE as "a technique of 'inquiry-based' course development, where undergraduate students must apply course contents to critically think about an unsolved scientific problem". Students valued the sequencing, with one writing that the lecture-based first half "really helped me build off of what I had learned so that I could apply it to second half of the semester".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a working research apparatus (a capacitively coupled argon plasma chamber) and diagnostics (Langmuir probe, optical emission spectrometer) that students can operate
- A genuine unanswered research question connected to the course content
#### Constraints
- The article notes the educational results came from a class of only six students and should not be generalized beyond that class

### Target Learners
- undergraduates from a wide range of scientific and engineering backgrounds at a small liberal arts college

### Learning Goals
- apply fundamental plasma physics to an authentic unsolved research problem
- develop laboratory, data-analysis, literature-reading, hypothesis-formulation, and science-communication skills
