# Flipped classroom, same-level peer-assisted triad practice model with performer, coach and monitor roles

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This element is the article's teaching model: students watch an expert demonstration video before class, then practice in self-directed triads where "each of the triads consisted of a “performer”, a “coach” and a “monitor"" — the performer practices hands-on, the coach gives just-in-time feedback, and the monitor assesses performance and feedback quality. Roles rotate through three 10-minute segments, tutors answer questions but give no hands-on demonstrations, and laminated handouts, videos and rubrics are available for reference. It was trialed with 205 penultimate-year medical students learning bag mask ventilation and intravenous cannulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be assigned and rotate through the performer, coach and monitor roles, with demonstration videos and assessment rubrics available for reference during practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes students were not immediately corrected after observing a flawed performance, and tutors reported some monitors gave feedback when not supposed to, so student compliance impacts execution

### Target Learners
- penultimate-year undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- clinical procedural skills such as bag mask ventilation and intravenous cannulation
