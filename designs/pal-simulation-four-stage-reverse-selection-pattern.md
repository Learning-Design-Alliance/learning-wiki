# PAL simulation curriculum pattern: four-stage course with team grouping, clinical observation, reverse-selection lesson plan development, and peer-facilitated simulation drills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a reusable one-month curriculum in which trainees are grouped into teams of about three based on the 'Resuscitation Triangle' and 'High-Performance Team Dynamics' frameworks, observe real ED cases, and develop confidential lesson plans. A "'reverse selection' strategy" has each group design cases outside its specialty interest to challenge the other group, ending in a full afternoon of high-fidelity drills with GAS-model debriefing, peer feedback, and a 5-minute presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires mentor guidance for lesson plan development and a high-fidelity simulation room with simulators and resuscitation equipment
#### Constraints
- Implemented with small groups of 2–3 trainees per group in a single-institution postgraduate emergency department rotation

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate year (PGY) physicians rotating through the emergency department

### Learning Goals
- Clinical confidence, reasoning ability, resuscitation familiarity, teamwork, leadership, and communication in critical care
