# Peer-assisted learning with social and cognitive congruence between tutors and tutees

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article frames its course within peer-assisted learning (PAL), defined as "the development of knowledge and skill through active help and support among status equals or matched companions". It holds that "tutors’ and participants’ cognitive and social congruence seem to raise learning efficacy": peer tutors of international background act as authentic role models, use more comprehensible language than lecturers, and foster a permissive learning atmosphere. The study applies this framework by staffing the tutorial with four international-background peer tutors who were former course participants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors are status equals or matched companions of the participants, ideally sharing the participants' international background.
#### Constraints
- The article states that, to its knowledge, no studies on the effectiveness of peer-guided courses for international students had been conducted prior to this one.

### Target Learners
- international medical students in their second year

### Learning Goals
- preparation for end-of-semester examinations
- confidence in examination-related abilities
- presenting medical content in German
