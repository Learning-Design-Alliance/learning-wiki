# NiCE Teacher Workshop

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Networks in Classroom Education (NiCE) workshop was a four-day professional development event (two full and two partial days, July 2017, at West Point) for 21 K-12 teachers with more than 300 years of cumulated teaching experience. Teachers were seated in five focus-area groups — K-2nd grade, 3-5th grade, 6-8th grade, High School, and Educational Coach — each "partnered with a network scientist". The schedule blended lectures, hands-on activities, discussion groups, and repeated "group speed rounds and feedback" sessions in which groups presented lesson-plan progress for feedback from scientists and peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers were asked to review three short online videos introducing network science before arriving
- Each teacher group required a partnered network scientist for support
- Teachers were asked to complete their projects for review within two weeks of departure
#### Constraints
- Participant selection was limited to schools and locations near the organisers, predominantly on the East and West coasts of the USA, to ensure adequate pre- and post-workshop support

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers across all grade levels and subjects including math, English, Spanish, science, social studies, history, and music
- Educational coaches

### Learning Goals
- Understanding network science concepts
- Applying network thinking to classroom lesson design
