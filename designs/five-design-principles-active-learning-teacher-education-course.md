# Five design principles for a technology-rich active-learning teacher education course

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The redesigned educational technology course followed five design principles embedding active-learning strategies across curriculum, pedagogy and assessment: flipped learning via short (under ten minutes) online videos; course staff as "critical friends" and co-explorers rather than experts; "cyclical tutorials linked to assessment" following a three-wave 'Sandpit–Synergy–Showcase' structure; group assessment with a shared mark in three of four tasks; and formal critical reflection. Together these scaffolded four target competencies: curation, collaboration, creation and critical reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors willing to act as co-explorers rather than digital-tool experts
- Assessment tasks aligned with the Sandpit–Synergy–Showcase tutorial cycle
#### Constraints
- The authors report the course evoked student resistance, anxiety and polarised evaluations, so the pattern does not guarantee satisfaction

### Target Learners
- First-year preservice teachers

### Learning Goals
- Twenty-first century skills: curation, collaboration, creation, critical reflection
