# Scaffolded open-ended computational final project assessed with a seven-dimension rubric

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the last six weeks of the course, students selected a physical system, developed a computational model, and wrote a report; most (90%) worked in groups of no more than three. The project was scaffolded through a project description, a status report, and instructor meetings with struggling groups. Reports and code were scored with a rubric of "seven dimensions," four mapping to course-scale learning goals (Physics, Model, Working Code, Outcomes) and three to scientific practices (Presentation, References, Synthesis), each scored 0 (poor) to 4 (excellent).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Six weeks of course time, instructor feedback on project descriptions and status reports, and a rubric released four weeks before the due date
#### Constraints
- The authors report the computational final project was not sustained in later iterations because it is a significant investment of research-focused faculty time given large enrollment; only three students (all working alone) failed to turn in a project

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level physics majors completing a middle-division mechanics course

### Learning Goals
- engaging in authentic scientific work: modeling a physical system, writing working code, reflecting on outcomes, and communicating findings
