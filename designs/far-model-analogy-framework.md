# The FAR model: a three-phase framework (Focus, Action, Reflection) for structuring analogy use in science teaching

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The FAR model is an instructional framework the article uses to structure analogical modeling in class. It comprises "three interconnected phases: Focus, Action, and Reflection": in Focus the teacher explicitly identifies relevant features of source and target domains; in Action students apply the analogy to explain, explore, or predict; in Reflection they critically evaluate the analogy's strengths, limits, and misconception potential. The article implemented it in the Rutherford black-box activity, aligning it with constructivist principles and meta-conceptual thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher analogy-competence and explicit identification of relevant source and target features during the Focus phase
#### Constraints
- The article notes analogies often fail when limits are not made explicit or prior knowledge is overlooked

### Target Learners
- first-year high school students (14-15 years old)

### Learning Goals
- understanding atomic structure and the nature of scientific models
