# Three co-existing recitation task types: smartphone experimental, programming, and standard recitation tasks

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course combined three task types on weekly exercise sheets: nine smartphone-based experimental tasks (19% of exam-prerequisite points), three Python programming tasks in pre-written Jupyter notebooks (6%), and roughly fifty standard textbook recitation tasks (75%). As printed, "nine smartphone-based experimental tasks (Exp) and three programming tasks (Pro) were integrated into weekly exercise sheets of the introductory physics course Experimental Physics I at RWTH Aachen University during the winter semester 23/24". Students worked self-directed in groups of three at home, except the first Exp-task done guided during recitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks were graded toward an exam prerequisite of at least half of 260 possible points
- Programming tasks were tailored to a concurrent programming course most students took; they were optional for others
#### Constraints
- Programming is not taught in the course itself
- Standard recitation tasks carry mostly high to very high exam relevance, while Exp- and Pro-tasks carry low exam relevance

### Target Learners
- first-year university physics students, over 300 in the course, mostly in their first semester

### Learning Goals
- hands-on experimentation with first-hand data collection
- applying physics formulas within provided code
- traditional problem-solving exercises
