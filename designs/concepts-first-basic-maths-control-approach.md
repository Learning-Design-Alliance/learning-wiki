# Concepts-first, basic-maths-only introductory control teaching approach

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The approach places a basic control systems course in the third semester, immediately after basic calculus and physics, so that fundamental control concepts can be studied "only with basic maths". It targets the diagnosed problem that students wield advanced calculus mechanically while missing the underlying concepts, and precedes the traditional two-step curriculum of frequency-domain and state-space control courses. Techniques are taught through time-domain analysis of first-order models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Taught just after basic calculus (limits, derivatives and integrals) and physics (dynamic equations and laws of motions), alongside intermediate calculus.
#### Constraints
- Many of the demonstrations and assessments in the guideline, such as closed-loop settling time and steady-state error computations, are "only valid for these kinds of models" - first-order systems.

### Target Learners
- early-semester undergraduate engineering students (third semester of the program)

### Learning Goals
- fundamental concepts of control theory
- intuitive understanding of feedback, modelling and on-off, P, PI and PID control
