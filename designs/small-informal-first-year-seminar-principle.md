# Offer emerging-field seminars early in the engineering program in a small, informal discussion-style setting to build belonging and retention

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing an emerging field like nanoengineering "at an early stage to the engineering undergraduate students" through informal discussion-style lectures with the class size "restricted to fifteen students". The small size "helped the group to become a tight-knit community of engineers" and helped students "get comfortable with the academic environment that is important for a first year student". The authors state the objective is "to engage students in scientific inquiry and engineering design process to retain them in engineering disciplines".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Class size restricted to fifteen students to keep communication informal
- Laboratory tours, documentaries, presentations, and peer review to make the course interactive
#### Constraints
- Reported for an elective first-year seminar with no prerequisite apart from freshman standing at one institution

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate engineering students

### Learning Goals
- early exposure to an emerging engineering field
- sense of belonging in the engineering community
- retention in engineering disciplines
