# Cycloid brachistochrone laboratory apparatus: grooved flexible track, billiard ball, digital timer with fork-type light barriers, string and ruler

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A low-cost apparatus for the introductory mechanics laboratory consisting of "a thin, grooved track made of a long flexible metal bar (or hard plastic) fixed by clamps", a small billiard ball, a digital timer connected to a fork-type light barrier, and string and ruler to measure arclengths. The track is fixed at point A by a screw while the other end through fixed point O is variable, letting students reshape the path between descents; light barriers at O and A trigger the timer electronically. Students record descent times and path lengths for eight paths to identify the minimum-time curve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The track must be at least 2 meters long both for a good demonstration and to detect significant time differences
- The starting point O at which the timer is triggered electronically must remain fixed across all paths
- An extra piece of track at A is necessary to provide proper flattening at the minimum of the inverted half cycloid
#### Constraints
- The experiment uses only half of the cycloid, 0 ≤ φ ≤ π; the authors note a longer track could cover the second half but symmetry makes this unnecessary

### Target Learners
- freshman physics students
- sophomore physics students
- non-physics major students in introductory mechanics

### Learning Goals
- understanding the brachistochrone (minimum time of descent under gravity)
- energy conservation relating speed and vertical position
- experimental error analysis comparing measured and theoretical arclengths
