# Two-week hybrid workshop combining online lessons with in-person detector assembly and a full research cycle

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The workshop runs for two weeks every Chilean winter in hybrid mode: online classes on particle physics, electronics and scientific methodology, two in-person days at the university where girls assemble detectors and take data in groups of about 5 led by female physics-student tutors, then a second online week of programming and data analysis. Girls "built their own experiment, take data with it, analyze it, come up with a scientific question, and then report on their research results", completing a scientific report in a format of their choice with question, introduction, analysis and summary sections, followed by constructive feedback in a safe space and a closing talk by a female scientist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Female tutors (undergraduate and graduate physics students) trained in the workshop contents and detectors
- About 90% attendance and submission of a research report were required for a diploma
- Access to a computer with internet connection during remote sessions
#### Constraints
- Requires funding for girls and their guardians to stay in Santiago for a few days
- Limited reliable particle-physics resources in Spanish required making lessons self-contained
- Legal, ethical and administrative efforts were needed because participants are minors

### Target Learners
- High-school girls aged 15–16 from all regions of Chile

### Learning Goals
- Critical thinking and transferable scientific skills
- Understanding what science is and how it is done
- Quantitative analysis of numerical data through mathematical reasoning
