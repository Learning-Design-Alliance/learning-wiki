# Online CURE design following the five CURE qualities: scientific practices, discovery, broader relevance, collaboration, and iteration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course was designed as a CURE following the qualities defined in Auchincloss et al. (2014): use of scientific practices, discovery, broader relevance and scientific impact, collaboration, and iteration. Students asked questions, formed hypotheses, gathered and analyzed real research data on brown dwarf companions from Keck/NIRC2 AO imaging, worked in small groups online, and presented and revised interpretations after peer challenge. The pattern shows how a CURE can be delivered fully online with synchronous workshops, breakout-room activities, and a virtual observing run.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An ongoing research project with no clear results or answers that students can genuinely contribute to
- Small class size to enable focused feedback and manageable group presentations
- Publicly available analysis software students can install on their own computers
#### Constraints
- Piloted with eleven students in a compressed 6.5-week Summer session at a single institution
- Grading was pass/fail to keep the environment low-pressure

### Target Learners
- second-year online undergraduate astronomy majors, including military-affiliated, full-time-worker, and transfer students

### Learning Goals
- observational astronomy research practices and techniques
- confidence and self-efficacy with research tools
- sense of belonging and science identity
