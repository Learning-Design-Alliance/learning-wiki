# Air-versus-CO2 balloon infrared-camera experiments isolating gas absorption and emission

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A pair of infrared-camera experiments with latex balloons, one filled with air and one with pure CO2, observed first cooler (in an ice bath) and then warmer than ambient temperature, so that emitted and absorbed radiation do not compensate. The article stresses that the comparison with the air balloon at the same temperature « permet de dissocier l'influence du CO2 de celle de la membrane du ballon », which can both reflect and absorb part of the infrared radiation, and that CO2 is as transparent as air in visible light.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An infrared camera whose sensitivity range overlaps the CO2 absorption spectrum; the experiments were run with a camera lent by a physics laboratory.
#### Constraints
- Not possible with the most common school infrared cameras, whose spectral range (8–15 μm) is chosen so gas presence does not affect building-temperature readings; gas-detecting infrared cameras cost of the order of 50 000 €.

### Target Learners
- Lycée students (non-specialists)

### Learning Goals
- Mettre en évidence l'émission et l'absorption du rayonnement infrarouge par le CO2
