# Research-validated cyclic development model for interactive learning tutorials

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The QuILT development model is a cyclic iterative process in which a preliminary tutorial version is built from a theoretical cognitive task analysis and research on student difficulties, then implemented and evaluated with individual students and faculty experts, its learning impact assessed, and refinements made based on that feedback. The article states the process "included the following stages", beginning with "Development of the preliminary version based on a theoretical cognitive task analysis [61] of the underlying knowledge and research on student difficulties with relevant concepts". Think-aloud interviews with 15 students informed each version, with modifications made after every individual interview.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analysis of student difficulties after lecture-based instruction to identify prior knowledge the tutorial builds on
- Individual think-aloud interviews and faculty expert review to refine successive versions
#### Constraints
- Earlier interviews led to larger changes while later ones were refinements; not all interviewed students were probed on the same issues due to time constraints

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics majors in a first-semester quantum mechanics course

### Learning Goals
- quantum mechanics concepts underlying quantum key distribution, including photon polarization states, measurement collapse, and protocol security
