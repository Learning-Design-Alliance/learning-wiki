# Storytelling-centric conceptual design: Resources, Tags, and Stories mapping to curation, organisation, and externalisation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The article proposes a conceptual design in which informal learners' experience is organised around three elements — Resources, Tags, and Stories — corresponding to three levels of learner engagement: curation, organisation, and externalisation. As the authors state, "Resources enable content collection (e.g., annotating YouTube segments), Tags organise materials into evolving learning paths, and Stories synthesise activity into personal narratives." This framing makes learning visible, evaluable, and shareable, and explicitly extends the COIL model and prior design suggestions. The authors present it as a transferable structure for embedding storytelling into self-regulation tool design in informal contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners engage in tagged curation of online resources so that stories can be generated from curated reflections; the article notes story quality depends on the curation process
#### Constraints
- The article states the approach was designed for and studied with informal programming learners who are regular social media users, and may not benefit those who are not social media users or prefer instructor-led approaches

### Target Learners
- informal programming learners, novices to experienced, who are regular social media users

### Learning Goals
- self-regulation of self-directed programming learning, including goal clarity, reflection, and continuity
