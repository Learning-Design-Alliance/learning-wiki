# Select IPLS content through stakeholder conversations so that only physics concepts with authentic biological applications are taught

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The reformers derived course goals and content from conversations with faculty stakeholders across Biology, Environmental Science, Radiologic Science, Psychology, and physics. Finding, as they put it, "a perhaps-surprising lack of sentiment for 'must-have' topics that no respectable physics course could leave out," they chose to teach only concepts with authentic biological applications (or foundational to them) and eliminated less relevant standard topics such as planetary motion and rolling motion, while including diffusion and nonlinear stress and strain. Topics retained from the traditional course were retaught to emphasize their role in understanding biological systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Conversations with faculty from the client life-science departments to define goals for students
#### Constraints
- Given the time limitations of a single two-semester sequence, physics-specific laboratory skills, error analysis, and laboratory report writing receive lower emphasis

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life-science majors, many aspiring to medical or other health-science careers

### Learning Goals
- applying logic and simple models of natural processes
- drawing inferences from simple measurements
- using physics concepts in biological contexts
