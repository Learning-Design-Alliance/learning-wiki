# SCIP trial simulation quality assurance program for standardizing actor performances

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The quality assurance program built for the SCIP randomized trial combined a two-person QA team of clinical fellows who reviewed every simulation video and transcript, double data entry in REDCap with discrepancy resolution, an operations manual for consistent error coding, and three feedback modes for actors. As the article describes, each simulation was run by a study team member "who provided immediate feedback to the actor based on real-time observation of the simulation," supplemented by written QA error reports and three 60-minute group feedback sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actors were hired through auditions testing their ability to receive critical feedback and adapt performances, then trained through repeated video-recorded rehearsal simulations with group feedback sessions until the study team was confident in script adherence
#### Constraints
- The article notes many programs "will not be able to support a dedicated, 2-person QA team in addition to immediate feedback from the study or teaching team"

### Target Learners
- Practicing ICU physicians (intensivists) participating in simulated family meetings
- Standardized family member actors

### Learning Goals
- Consistent, high-fidelity actor performances that preserve valid measurement of physician communication behavior in simulation-based trials
