# A modified Modeling Cycle for data-acquisition system development structures lab activities in five stages

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article adapts Hestenes' Modeling Instruction into a lab-development framework in which students build and deploy models of physical measurement. Lab activities are structured in five named stages: "1. Context-Rich Concrete Preparation, 2. Sensor Data Collection, 3. Mathematical Model-Building, 4. Algorithmic Model-Building, 5. Model Deployment." Students work in small, meaningfully chosen groups and break for whiteboarding sessions in which groups describe what they have figured out. The authors argue this develops a constructivist pattern students can adapt to new sensors and new algorithm demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need an initial introduction to electronics (voltages, currents, soldering) and to basic programming concepts: variables, control structures, recursion, and IO
#### Constraints
- The approach was designed for University Physics lab sections where students purchase a personal lab kit

### Target Learners
- University Physics (introductory calculus-based physics) students

### Learning Goals
- Mathematical model-building mapping sensor readings to physical parameters
- Algorithmic/computational thinking
- Sensor calibration and data acquisition skills
