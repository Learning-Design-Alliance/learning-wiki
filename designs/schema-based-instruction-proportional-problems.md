# Schema-based instruction (SBI) for proportional problem solving

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study, `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
SBI is an instructional approach rooted in schema theory that incorporates cognitive models of mathematical problem solving and integrates features such as explicit and systematic instruction, scaffolding with guided questions, corrective feedback, and frequent cumulative review. In this study it comprised four components: "activation of the mathematical structure of problems", "representation of information in the problem using diagrams", "development of procedural flexibility through selection and use of appropriate solution strategy", and problem-solving and metacognitive strategy instruction. Teachers implemented two replacement units (Ratio/Proportion and Percent) with teacher guides, visual diagrams, problem-solving checklists, and student workbooks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers require professional development covering SBI key features, problem-type sorting, schematic diagrams, ballpark estimates, and multiple solution strategies, as delivered in the study's intensive 2-day (16 hr) training
#### Constraints
- The study did not address exactly what features of the SBI intervention were most effective, and it is not known whether certain components are more relevant for some students than others

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade students with mathematics problem-solving difficulties (below the 35th percentile)

### Learning Goals
- proportional problem solving involving ratio, proportion, and percent
