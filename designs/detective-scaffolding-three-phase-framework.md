# Detective scaffolding framework: a three-phase instructional sequence grounded in ICAP, productive failure, anchored instruction, and Evidence-Centred Design

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A three-phase instructional sequence (Hypothesis Activation → Evidence Structuring → Causal Integration) in which students investigate a realistic industrial defect scenario using staged in-class polls as designed evidence probes. The framework operationalises the ICAP hierarchy, with Phase 1 an Active-level task, Phase 2 Constructive, and Phase 3 ideally Interactive; it deliberately invites an initial misconception as a productive failure mechanism, and the detective narrative acts as an "anchor" that "legitises incorrect intermediate hypotheses as 'leads' rather than errors". Each poll is an ECD instrument targeting a specific reasoning capability, from hypothesis generation to causal synthesis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A realistic scenario dataset with a hidden root cause embedded among salient but non-causal variables
- Sequenced polls each targeting a specific reasoning capability with defined success criteria
- Feasibility within a single lecture slot is preserved by keeping the design minimal—three phases, four polls
#### Constraints
- The Q4 convergence reflects persisting teams rather than the full cohort, since participation declined from 52 to 9 responses
- Evidence to date is a single-site, single-module implementation; replication across institutions and disciplines is needed

### Target Learners
- Year 3 undergraduate engineering students
- pre-university Year 12 students with no engineering background

### Learning Goals
- evidence-based causal reasoning and root-cause identification
- temporal and structural data-pattern analysis
- formative visibility of reasoning progression
