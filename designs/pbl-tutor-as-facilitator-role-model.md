# The facilitator role model for PBL tutors

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article frames the PBL tutor's role as a facilitator rather than a frontal teacher: "PBL does not require the front-line facilitator, but the facilitator himself." Tutors stimulate student learning, promote teamwork, support self-responsible learning, and provide feedback. The role change was practised repeatedly in training because it was difficult for most tutors, especially for clinical experts teaching in their own field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors need opportunities to practise and receive feedback on facilitator behaviours during training
#### Constraints
- Clinical participants often fell into the role of frontal teaching in cases from their own field, which was noticed in external and self-observation

### Target Learners
- PBL tutors in medical curricula
- clinical and non-clinical teaching staff

### Learning Goals
- adopting the facilitator role in PBL groups
- stimulating student learning and teamwork
