# Multi-iteration thermal expansion activity cycling through model, apparatus, and procedure refinement

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A multi-week lab pattern in which students repeatedly design, test, and refine an experiment. Students first explore the apparatus at hands-on stations, then design a procedure, build an initial algebraic model neglecting wire mass and elasticity, and then run follow-up tests characterizing systematic effects such as "elastic stretching of a room temperature wire under lo ads of various mass." Results from one iteration inform refinements to the model and apparatus in the next, with instructors seeding questionable assumptions through anecdotal observations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An apparatus with systematic effects accessible to introductory-level concepts, and class time for small- and large-group consensus discussions
#### Constraints
- Findings from one group's test were apparatus-specific (e.g., the mirror to minimize parallax errors)

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- characterizing systematic effects and uncertainty
- iterative experimental design
