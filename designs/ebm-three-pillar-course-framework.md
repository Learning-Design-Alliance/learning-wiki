# Three-pillar evidence-based medicine course design: RCTs as centerpiece, Bayes' theorem, and science and society, with backward design from these aims

## Description
The article proposes a context-rich course framework in which probability and statistics are taught through medicine, using "the randomized controlled trial as the centerpiece of the course, because it exemplifies the most salient features of the scientific method". The other two pillars are "biomedical applications of Bayes’ theorem and science and society content". Backward design from these three overarching aims was used to select probability and statistics content, focusing on eliciting and countering documented probability misconceptions in their medical context. The framework doubles as a substitute for traditional biostatistics, bioethics, or science-and-society electives.
