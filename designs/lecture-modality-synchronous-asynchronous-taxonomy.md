# Lecture delivery modalities: synchronous versus asynchronous online formats with distinct pedagogical trade-offs

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The article organizes lecture delivery into modalities contrasting online with offline and synchronous with asynchronous delivery. Synchronous teaching "allows for easier two-way live communication," while asynchronous teaching "offers more flexibility due to on-demand availability"; the modalities "can be combined to mitigate disadvantages, e.g. by recording synchronous lectures." The authors used this framing to plan and switch formats across semesters of a large analytical chemistry course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lectures needed to be recorded in any case for subsequent viewing by students unable to attend synchronous meetings, per institutional regulation
#### Constraints
- With insufficient technical equipment, support, or unreliable internet connectivity, asynchronous lectures are preferred, per the literature the article cites

### Target Learners
- undergraduate chemistry and related majors in large-enrolment university courses

### Learning Goals
- maintaining a lecture hall-like learning experience for quantitative analytical chemistry content
