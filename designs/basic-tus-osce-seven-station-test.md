# Seven-station OSCE for basic thoracic ultrasound competences

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A performance assessment comprising two theoretical stations (15 video-based MCQs each) and five practical hands-on stations covering "knobology and image optimization, ultrasound-guided pleural aspiration, and assessments of pleural effusion, interstitial syndrome, and pneumothorax". Stations run 9 minutes with 1-minute rotation, allowing seven candidates per 70-minute round; assessment sheets score 0–15 points per station to a maximum of 105. It was designed to assess the "shows how" level of Miller's pyramid in a standardized environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained examiners experienced in thoracic ultrasound and pleural intervention
- Standardised written examiner instructions and assessment sheets per station
- Simulation equipment (US Mentor simulator, pleural aspiration phantom) and simulated patients
#### Constraints
- The complicated OSCE set-up requires simulated patients and many examiners drawn from clinical care
- Direct observation made complete blinding of examiners impossible in this study
- It assesses the "shows how" level and does not provide a complete competence profile

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Physicians learning basic thoracic ultrasound

### Learning Goals
- Basic thoracic ultrasound theoretical knowledge
- Practical scanning and procedural skills
- Integration of ultrasound findings into clinical context
