# Two-phase audit of intellectual disability content in Australian medical school curricula

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A two-phase audit conducted from 2013 to 2014 across Australian medical schools offering AMC-accredited degrees. Phase 1 involved Deans or their delegates in interviews or questionnaires about course structure; Phase 2 surveyed unit coordinators about how intellectual disability content was taught and by whom. Twelve of twenty schools (60% response rate) completed Phase 2, covering 42 compulsory and 8 elective intellectual disability units analysed descriptively. The audit provides a baseline for assessing future education enhancement strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation of Deans and unit coordinators via interview and self-report survey instruments
#### Constraints
- The article notes ascertainment bias may have influenced the results in that schools with intellectual disability content may have been more likely to take part
- Self-report data collection may have inflated reported teaching amounts, and inconsistent course structure definitions made direct comparisons across schools difficult
