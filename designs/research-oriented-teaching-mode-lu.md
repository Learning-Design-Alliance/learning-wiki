# Research-oriented teaching/learning: an open, interactive mode that delivers researchers' ideas through knowledge rather than transmitting information

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines research-oriented teaching/learning as a style of undergraduate instruction, best suited to research universities, in which knowledge and information serve as carriers for ideas about science and research. The authors write that it is "a sort of style: open, interactive" and that they "encourage discovery and creativity, deliver ideas from researchers." Research training is implanted into teaching to strengthen enthusiasm of discovery, broaden knowledge coverage, and deepen understanding of fundamental principles, while still fulfilling normal introductory-course requirements. The mode is grounded in the Boyer Report's principle that learning is "based on discovery guided by mentoring rather than on the transmission of information."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors with active research backgrounds who can deliver ideas from researchers, not only knowledge
- A research-university atmosphere with needs for research personnel, though the authors believe many elements are commonly applicable
#### Constraints
- The authors state they "can not tell about applicability in other universities" beyond research universities, expecting practice evidence from other school types

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in introductory physics courses at research universities

### Learning Goals
- Scientific thinking, critical thinking, creativity, research skills, and understanding of fundamental physics principles
