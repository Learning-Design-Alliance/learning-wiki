# Deliberate practice as an instructional approach for expert-level understanding

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article applies Ericsson et al.'s deliberate practice framework to course design: rather than one-shot activities, instruction allocates "multiple sessions for relevant instruction, increasingly complex activities, and frequent revision and feedback opportunities as a means to improve students' performance." In B300 Vascular Plants this meant five assignments progressing through the steps of phylogenetic tree construction, with content acquisition placed within a framework of skill development. The authors argue this better cultivates expert-level understanding than stand-alone exercises, because naïve theories and scientific knowledge tend to coexist after isolated instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple sessions of relevant instruction across a course rather than a single activity
- Activities that increase in complexity incrementally
- Frequent revision and feedback opportunities
#### Constraints
- The article notes increasing time on task alone has little effect on achievement; the structured, increasingly complex practice is the operative component

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate biology students in an upper-level plant systematics course

### Learning Goals
- Tree-thinking: interpreting relationships within phylogenetic trees, comparing among trees, and constructing trees
