# Hierarchical modular laboratory design tiering the same investigation for introductory and graduate learners

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The investigation is structured as a tiered laboratory in which a foundational "Short Lab" covers mass triangulation and spherical symmetry constraints while an advanced "Spacetime Deep Dive" adds the lensing Jacobian, orbital instabilities, and high-rigor statistics such as inverse-variance weighting and matched-pairs t-analysis. The author states "This investigation is intentionally designed with a hierarchical modularity to accommodate a spectrum of learners," and notes simpler evaluative methods can be substituted for introductory settings so the same materials scale from foundational exploration to metrology probe.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A smartphone running the freely available Black Hole Vision app, with a calibrated meter stick mounted at a fixed distance as the measurement target
#### Constraints
- The high-rigor statistical framework "transcends the typical statistics and data analysis methods available to undergraduate students" and is intended primarily for the graduate-level Spacetime Deep Dive

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory General Relativity courses
- graduate students in General Relativity or Relativistic Astrophysics

### Learning Goals
- quantitative black hole image analysis
- distinguishing physical metric signatures from instrumental noise
- precision measurement and data validation
