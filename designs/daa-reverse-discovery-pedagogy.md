# DAA activities as reverse discovery: a discovery-based pedagogy that starts with the expert artefact

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) activities are described as an inherently discovery-based pedagogy that starts with the artefact, an instance of a typically well-engineered solution. Through systemized disassembly and subsequent analysis of components, students engage in an iterative process of observation and follow-up probing to understand component functions and their interconnection. The article frames DAA as overcoming a flaw of typical discovery activities, which tend to be unable to constrain students' explorations, by starting with the expert version. In engineering education, DAA is positioned as nurturing engineering thinking through interactions with artefacts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an artefact that is an instance of a typically well-engineered solution for students to disassemble and analyze
#### Constraints
- Both studies by the authors used a camera, a tangible and predominantly mechanical device; the authors state new types of engineering artefacts, beyond those that are predominantly mechanical and/or tangible, need to be assessed

### Target Learners
- first-year engineering students

### Learning Goals
- understanding part-function relationships of engineered artefacts
- transfer to novel design and diagnosis problems
- motivation for engineering
