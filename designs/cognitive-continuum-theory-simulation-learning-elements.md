# Cognitive continuum theory with four simulation learning elements as a design framework for disaster triage training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article applies cognitive continuum theory, which posits that "cognition moves along a continuum from intuition to analytical thinking", with the suitable process depending on task characteristics: intuition supports fast responses when time or information is limited, while analytical processes suit detailed decision-making. Simulation design operationalizes this through four learning elements—analytical cognition in tasks, situational awareness, cognitive control, and self-awareness—integrated into the VR disaster triage training with pre-briefing, structured simulation, and reflective debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation tasks that mimic real situations with realistic and immersive tasks, enabling safe repetitive learning so learners can optimize cognitive judgments
#### Constraints
- Applied and evaluated here only in the disaster triage context with nursing students

### Target Learners
- Nursing students as future healthcare first responders

### Learning Goals
- Structured evidence-based triage decision-making
- Context-sensitive clinical judgment
- Self-regulation under pressure
