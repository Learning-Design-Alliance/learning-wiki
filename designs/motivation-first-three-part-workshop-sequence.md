# Motivation-first three-part workshop sequence: everyday analogy, quantum concepts via demos, then programming

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The workshop sequences instruction motivation-first: Part 1 grounds computational hardness in "a constructed analogy based on everyday life" (a morning-routine optimization calculator), Part 2 introduces quantum concepts through plush-toy demos and discussion, and Part 3 lets students "rediscover quantum physics concepts in practice" through programming. The authors describe the motivation-first method as "an obvious extension of context-based learning", which augments students' perceived relevance of the topic and motivates curiosity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An interactive, judgement-free learning atmosphere emphasizing participation over correctness
- A relatable everyday analogy students can reason through with simple logic and a problem graphic
#### Constraints
- Designed for a single 80-90 minute session; the abridged 50-minute version condenses the first two segments into a concise 20-minute introduction

### Target Learners
- high school STEM students in Grades 9-12

### Learning Goals
- building curiosity and perceived relevance of quantum computing before formal concepts are introduced
