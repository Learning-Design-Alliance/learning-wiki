# Design criteria for teacher-authored educational video games: simple mechanics, progressive interest, and balanced ludic-didactic integration

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates three criteria its educational video game was designed to meet: "the game should not have complex mechanics and any student, even those who do not usually play video games, should be able to quickly understand it and play it easily"; the game should let the student get more interested as it progresses; and learning objects should pop up at an appropriate frequency and size so the game mixes the ludic and didactic aspects. These criteria shaped the Flappy-Bird-based game used in the quasi-experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning objects popped up at an appropriate frequency and of an appropriate size
- Mechanics simple enough for students unfamiliar with video games
#### Constraints
- Criteria were applied to a single game in a software design lesson

### Target Learners
- computer science university students, including non-gamers
