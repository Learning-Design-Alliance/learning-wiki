# E&M electrostatics demonstration-based activity where students generate and test models

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A second-semester E&M lab activity in which "students generate models of electrostatics from a series of demonstrations (e.g. electroscopes, attraction of packing peanuts, simple Faraday cages), and then design experiments to test those models." The instructor shows each group a different demonstration without explanation of any aspect of the set-up. Students brainstorm models, design ways to test them, and extend their investigation by using their model to predict outcomes of other groups' demonstrations. The sequence is inspired by the Investigative Science Learning Environment framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Demonstrations and set-up are provided but can be fully manipulated
- Sufficient ambiguity to necessitate within-group and across-group collaboration
#### Constraints
- This activity prioritizes collaboration skills and ethical experimentation practices alongside model testing through controlling variables, different from the data acquisition goals in the mechanics example

### Target Learners
- students in second-semester introductory electricity and magnetism

### Learning Goals
- generate and test models of electrostatics
- design and extend experiments to test models
- collaborate across groups to refine testable ideas
