# Layered distractor design combining clinical complications with a family-member communication crisis

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The scenario embeds two sequential distractors: a clinical one (venous access malfunction causing ineffective sedation and psychomotor agitation) followed by a ventilatory one (decreased consciousness requiring support), interleaved with a simulated anxious family member who enters filming with a phone demanding information. The article's guidance says distractors "should be designed with the purpose of aiding in learning and bringing the scenario closer to real conditions, however, they should not excessively divert the participant's attention".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator scripted to trigger each complication at defined moments and scripted family-member behavior, so distractors unfold at controlled times
#### Constraints
- Distractors must not excessively divert participants from the proposed objectives, per the article's own design rule

### Target Learners
- multidisciplinary pediatric emergency teams practicing under realistic interpersonal pressure

### Learning Goals
- recognizing and resolving sedation complications while managing distressed family members and maintaining team communication
