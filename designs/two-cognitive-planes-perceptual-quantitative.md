# Two cognitive planes of representation: perceptual equivalence of naked-eye observation versus quantitative astronomical data

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article organizes its multisensory mappings on two distinct cognitive levels, both accessible to all users. The first plane offers a perceptual equivalent of what can be observed with the naked eye (position, apparent brightness). The second plane is "more quantitative and objective" and represents data that cannot be sensed directly, such as stellar distance retrieved from instrumental observations, using vibration and duration-coded light and sounds. The authors write that this choice "allows us to offer an equal perceptual fruition of what can be observed in the night sky without using instruments and telescopes, and on the other hand, a likewise equal perceptual representation of astronomical data, on two different cognitive levels, both accessible to all."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The quantitative plane requires astronomical data (e.g., stellar distances) retrieved from instrumental observations, since these values cannot be perceived with the senses.
#### Constraints
- The vibration mapping the distance is only included in the second (quantitative) representation, keeping the perceptual plane free of instrument-derived data.

### Target Learners
- sighted users
- BVI users in informal astronomy settings

### Learning Goals
- distinguishing perceptual appearance of the night sky from instrument-derived physical properties of stars
