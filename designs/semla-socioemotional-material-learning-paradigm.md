# SEMLA: a group-based socioemotional and material learning paradigm for preschool

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
SEMLA (socioemotional and material learning) is a group-based preschool pedagogy in which children explore an overarching problem through hands-on investigation with diverse materials and artefacts, emphasizing emotional engagement, face-to-face interaction, and verbally mediated reflection with teacher scaffolding. The article describes it as "an investigative learning strategy with emphasis on the STEAM subjects", implemented in small groups of six to eight children, targeting early math through measuring, estimation, and construction activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers guide small groups of six to eight children, supply creative materials, and scaffold individual children with questions, comments, and information during collaborative investigation
#### Constraints
- It had not yet been scientifically evaluated before this trial, and the trial found no measurable effects on the targeted skills

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged four to six

### Learning Goals
- language and communication
- executive functions
- socioemotional comprehension
- early math skills
