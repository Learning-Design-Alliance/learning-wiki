# Peyton's Four-Step Approach-based SP training program for delivering DRE skills

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A structured training session qualifying standardized patients to conduct abdominal examination skills training including pain-free DRE for medical students. "The training was designed to enable SPs to instruct medical students, to guide role-plays, to adhere to time management, to evaluate the quality of students’ skills performance and to give appropriate professional feedback to students." It followed Peyton's Four-Step Approach, encompassed 4 teaching units totalling 3 hours, used a preparatory written script, video demonstration, trainer demonstration on the SP, SP practice on a part-task trainer, role-play run-through, and a supervised dress rehearsal before field-testing with final-year students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SPs receive a detailed script to study in advance covering the program, their role, and anatomic and technical fundamentals of the DRE
- SPs are examined by an experienced physician in internal medicine prior to and during the training course
#### Constraints
- The authors state this is the only published model for training physical and intimate examination skills including step 3 of Peyton's Four-Step Approach; evaluation here is limited to first training experiences of four SPs

### Target Learners
- standardized patients preparing to teach intimate physical examination
- final-year medical students taught in the subsequent field-test sessions

### Learning Goals
- digital rectal examination technique
- patient education and communication about the examination
- giving professional feedback from the patient perspective
