# Design introductory lab experiments so students explicitly confront the passage from an idealized theoretical scheme to a real experimental asset

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's central design principle is that first-year laboratory work should make the idealization gap visible rather than hide it. Pendulum experiments are chosen because in these many variations, they emphasize "the difference between theory and practice in the passage from an idealized scheme to a real experimental asset, which becomes evident, for example, when the pendulum bob cannot be considered an idealized point mass." The course walks students through hypothesizing an alternative physical model and checking it experimentally.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Apparatus simple enough that systematic effects (thread rigidity, extended bob, initial conditions) are detectable at the precision of a few parts per thousand
#### Constraints
- Effects are only noticeable if the standard deviations of the measurements are smaller than 0.3-0.4 %

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- distinguishing theory from experimental practice
- understanding limits of mathematical approximation
