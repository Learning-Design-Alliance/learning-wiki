# Integrated robotics course framework organizing a complete research workflow around one shared robot artifact

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The framework organizes mechanical assembly, electrical setup, simulation, policy learning, system identification, and physical deployment around a shared robotic artifact. It combines "parallel disciplinary tracks, sequencing based on technical dependencies, progressive integration of simulation and hardware, layered performance checkpoints, and structures for balancing collaborative work with individual accountability." It was developed iteratively and refined through a five-expert formative review.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared robotic artifact connecting disciplinary work to observable behavior
- Staged tasks and bounded customization adapted for high-school learners
#### Constraints
- The article states the framework "does not establish that a humanoid course teaches introductory RL more effectively than simpler alternatives"
- Effectiveness remains untested

### Target Learners
- High-school students in grades 9-12

### Learning Goals
- Understanding how systems acquire behavior through reinforcement learning
- Integrated STEM reasoning across mechanical, electrical, and computational work
