# Three-stage cognitive-theory sequence for Web-based reading strategy training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes its 20-hour Web-based reading strategy program around Anderson's (1993) cognitive theory, in which "L2 learning proceeds through three stages: Cognitive, associative, and autonomous". In the cognitive stage the teacher explicitly explains each strategy and learners verbalize and apply it; in the associative stage learners complete tasks independently with teacher support for fluency; in the autonomous stage learners apply all strategies independently while reading hypertexts. The four strategies taught were note taking, skimming, inferring, and using context clues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who explicitly explains strategies and supports learners during the associative stage
- Web-based hypertext reading tasks for practice across the three stages
#### Constraints
- The article reports the sequence only for upper-intermediate learners, arguing lower levels may lack the grammar and vocabulary to employ the strategies effectively

### Target Learners
- upper-intermediate adolescent EFL learners (15-18 years old)

### Learning Goals
- L2 hypertext and general reading comprehension
- use of general reading strategies (skimming, inferring, context clues, note taking)
