# Teach introductory computation by emphasizing interpretation of code and modeling of interactions rather than writing programs from scratch

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that early-career introductory courses emphasize making physical meaning of programs over constructing them from scratch. The authors state that exposure to computational modeling with a strong emphasis on the interpretation of code and the modeling of interactions would serve the broadest population of students early in their careers, a decision shaped by their mostly engineering audience and the course's place at the beginning of students' careers. Computational learning goals therefore center on identifying and interpreting initial conditions and parameters, and constructing the integration loop within provided programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning goals that target interpretation of existing code and modification of provided models
#### Constraints
- Not appropriate if learning goals include that each student write their own program from scratch; the authors note their instructional model does not do that

### Target Learners
- first-year science and engineering students in introductory mechanics

### Learning Goals
- interpreting computational models
- modeling physical interactions
- understanding variables and control structures
