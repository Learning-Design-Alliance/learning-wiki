# Structured inquiry learning model

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The structured inquiry model is a constructivist-based instructional model that "promotes the involvement of learners actively and creatively in the search for, examine, formulate concepts and principles of geometry". Learning is student-centered: students formulate hypotheses, perform experiments related to the geometry material, and draw conclusions to answer the hypotheses under teacher guidance. The article applies it to abstract three-dimensional geometry content for high-school students whose concrete-operational stage makes abstract concepts hard to grasp.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers guide students to rediscover concepts (guided reinvention) rather than transfer knowledge intact
- Student attitudes such as curiosity, flexibility, critical reflection and honesty support the process
#### Constraints
- Success is judged by how actively students search and find, not only by mastery of the material, per Sanjaya (2009) as cited

### Target Learners
- high school grade 10 mathematics students

### Learning Goals
- understanding of geometry concepts
- intellectual thinking skills and problem solving
