# Introduction to process control course (third-semester course at UFSC's CAE program)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course sequences conceptual process notions (process, actuators and sensors, manipulated and controlled variables, disturbances, open- and closed-loop operation, operating ranges), first-order modelling and system characteristics, then the industrial control techniques: "on-off control, proportional control, proportional-integral (PI) control and proportional-integral-derivative (PID) control". The syllabus is "integrally centered in time-domain analyses, considering simple first-order models", with practical topics such as set-point weighting, anti-windup and noise filtering.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Placed in the third semester, just after basic calculus (limits, derivatives and integrals) and physics (dynamic equations and laws of motions).
#### Constraints
- Demonstrations and assessments such as closed-loop settling time and steady-state error computations are only valid for first-order models.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate Control and Automation Engineering students in their early semesters

### Learning Goals
- analysis and design of simple control systems
- connecting basic mathematics to realistic process control problems
