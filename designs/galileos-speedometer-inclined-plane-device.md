# Galileo's speedometer: an inclined plane with horizontal extension for measuring instantaneous speed

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An inclined plane with a horizontal extension, copied from Galileo's folio 163v (circa 1602): a sphere accelerates down the incline ab and then moves uniformly on the horizontal sector bc. Measuring the time T to cross distance D on the horizontal gives the instantaneous speed at the ramp's end as D/T, which is why the authors "dubbed the device of ﬁgure 2 'Galileo's speedometer'".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A smooth transition between the inclined and horizontal tracks: the authors bent a straight rail into a small curved path to avoid the sphere bouncing at the junction.
- A timing method, such as video analysis with Tracker, photogates, or bells hung over the sphere's path.
#### Constraints
- It measures the instantaneous speed at the end point of the incline, obtained from distance D and time T on the horizontal track.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- high-school students

### Learning Goals
- assign an operational meaning to instantaneous speed
- obtain the laws of fall from measurements
