# Taxonomy of the roles of models in the scientific method (Figure 1 scheme)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organizes the contributions of theory within Strong Inference into a scheme (Figure 1) distinguishing defining questions and hypotheses, generating empirically testable predictions, and using models to prove or disprove the plausibility of hypotheses. It argues that in population biology, unlike physics, "models are more often used to prove the plausibility of a hypothesis previously thought to be implausible, or to disprove the plausibility of a hypothesis previously thought to be reasonable, without deriving empirically testable predictions". The course uses this scheme as its central classification tool, and students are asked in quizzes and the final exam to classify each paper's model within it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction in Strong Inference and the nature of scientific evidence so students can situate each model within the scheme
#### Constraints
- Statistical models are explicitly declared outside the scope of the class
- Some papers, such as climate forecasting, fall outside the scheme entirely and are treated as forecasting rather than hypothesis testing

### Target Learners
- graduate students in ecology, evolution, epidemiology, and behavior

### Learning Goals
- understanding what constitutes empirical versus theoretical evidence
- classifying the role of a model in a scientific argument
