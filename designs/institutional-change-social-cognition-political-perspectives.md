# Social cognition and political perspectives on department-wide institutional change

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Reflecting on their department-wide change effort, the authors applied a framework for institutional change, focusing on "a social cognition perspective, which involves identifying and potentially changing the underlying beliefs of a group; and a political perspective, which includes building coalitions to support collective actions." Their surveys surfaced faculty and student beliefs about UREs; recommendations were designed to change some beliefs (e.g., student beliefs about the nature of UREs) while honoring others (e.g., faculty beliefs about mentoring); and pre-proposal discussions built a supportive coalition that produced an official departmental committee.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Change efforts should identify the underlying beliefs of faculty members and students, for example through surveys, before designing recommendations that align with or change those beliefs.
#### Constraints
- The article applies the framework within one physics department and notes that underlying beliefs about the nature of the mentor-student relationship remain to be unpacked in future work.

### Target Learners
- physics faculty and undergraduate majors in a department-wide change effort

### Learning Goals
- improving institutional access to undergraduate research experiences
