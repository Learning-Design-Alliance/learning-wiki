# Teach life skills through children's literature using a four-phase lesson structure: pre-reading, during-reading, post-reading, and evaluation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents "a detailed teaching strategy for teaching life skills through children literature" built on short stories and songs. Each lesson follows four phases: pre-reading activities that define and promote the targeted life skill, during-reading activities for skill acquisition and performance, post-reading activities fostering skill maintenance and generalization, and a formative evaluation phase involving parents. The program organized 16 short stories and 16 songs into four thematic units covering cognitive, personal, social, and linguistic life skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A criterion for selecting stories and songs suited to students' needs, attitudes, and the targeted life skills
- A teacher's guide and students' booklet organized by life skill themes
#### Constraints
- Implemented in English language clubs held two hours weekly over eight weeks; teachers reported procedural difficulties with space and large class numbers

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade primary EFL pupils

### Learning Goals
- cognitive, personal, social, and linguistic life skills
- language learning strategies
