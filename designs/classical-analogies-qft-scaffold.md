# Classical analogies as a scaffold mapping Newtonian mechanics onto quantum-field-theory concepts.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper's organizing framework treats two classical systems (a body in a fluid, a sliding block with friction) as analogs of modern-theory structures: the added mass S plays the role of the self energy Sq, the parameters ρ and µk play the role of coupling constants, the series expansion of M*(Λ) mirrors Feynman-diagram expansions, and the Λ-independence requirement hints at renormalization group equations. The author states the aim "to set up, within the Newtonian formalism, equations which will help undergraduate students to hav e a mathematical, rather than conceptual, view of non-trivial issues concerning modern theories."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should read chapter 2 (especially sec. 2.3) of Griffiths to substantiate the QFT discussion; advanced students may perform evaluations from Refs. [9, 10].
#### Constraints
- The analogies are qualitative; a quantitative QCD discussion is too difficult at this level, and quark masses are model dependent and not directly observable.

### Target Learners
- junior-level undergraduates in classical mechanics
- advanced undergraduates in particle physics or quantum field theory courses

### Learning Goals
- understand bare vs effective mass
- understand self energy and dressing by interactions
- understand perturbative vs non-perturbative regimes and asymptotic freedom

### Claims
- Sliding Block Mstar Not True Effective Mass [+M]
- Perturbative Accuracy Depends On Coupling [+M]
