# TED-talk video materials as pre-task authentic input

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The experimental group's main materials were "some supplementary video files extracted from TED conferences, to which students were exposed before doing the task". Videos were selected according to students' level and the content or spoken genre of each task, using criteria that they be relevant to students' background knowledge and culture, authentic representations of real-life spoken discourse including fillers, hesitation markers, fixed expressions, ellipsis and vague language, and amenable to analysis for raising awareness of spoken discourse features.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must be relevant to students' background knowledge and culture, authentic, and suitable for close analysis
#### Constraints
- Selected from TED talks according to the students' level and the genre tackled by each task

### Target Learners
- first-year university ESL students

### Learning Goals
- awareness of spoken discourse characteristics
- speaking sub-skills

### Claims
- [Students reported that analysing authentic TED talks was motivating and that self/peer evaluation of their video-recorded speaking tasks helped them overcome anxiety and track their progress](../claims/task-cycle-activities-reported-benefits.md) [+W]
