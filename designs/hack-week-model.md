# Hack weeks as a hybrid model combining pedagogy, peer learning and collaborative project work

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Hack weeks are week-long events that fill the gap between pedagogically focused workshops and project-focused hackathons. The authors state they are "week-long events that combine pedagogy (often focused on statistical and computational techniques) together with time for hacks and creative projects", aiming to encourage collaboration and learning among people at various career stages. All events share a common core of three components: tutorials in state-of-the-art methodology, peer-learning, and project work in a collaborative environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A participant group diverse across gender, discipline and career track to support cross-seniority knowledge transfer
- A flexible, reconfigurable workspace with room for lectures, interactive exchanges and small-team project work
#### Constraints
- The measure of success is field-specific, depending on how much the promoted concepts were already adopted within the community
- Long-term impact on collaboration networks and career outcomes requires follow-up over multiple years

### Target Learners
- Researchers across career stages in scientific disciplines (astronomy, neuroscience, geoscience)

### Learning Goals
- Modern data analysis workflows
- Open science and reproducibility practices
- Collaboration and interdisciplinary exchange
