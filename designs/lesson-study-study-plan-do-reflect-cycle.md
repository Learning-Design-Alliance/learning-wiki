# Lesson study as collaborative study-plan-do-reflect inquiry cycles that build knowledge, beliefs, norms, routines, and materials for continuing instructional improvement

## Description
The article models lesson study as collaborative inquiry cycles in which "teachers conduct collaborative 'study–plan–do–reflect' inquiry cycles designed to improve classroom instruction". Teams study curriculum content and long-term goals, plan a research lesson taught by one member while others collect data on student learning, then discuss the data in post-lesson reflection. A theoretical model (Figure 1) links Desimone's five features of effective professional learning — active learning, collective participation, content focus, duration, coherence — to intervening changes in teacher knowledge, beliefs, professional community, and instructional materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams plan, enact, and collect data on a research lesson, then hold a post-lesson discussion of the collected data

- educator teams conducting professional learning

### Learning Goals
