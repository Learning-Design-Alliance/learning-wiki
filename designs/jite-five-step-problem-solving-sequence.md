# JITE five-step learning sequence for complex multidisciplinary problems

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Under JITE, students working on a complex problem such as nanotechnology follow an enumerated five-step sequence: identifying the intersecting disciplines, acquiring the needed information and understanding from each, synthesizing the parts into an acceptable whole, assessing requirements for further development, and establishing the accomplishment's values in surrounding cultures. The article states that 'the hallmark of this approach is that students shall have to learn' these five capabilities in order. The sequence operationalizes JITE at the level of a whole project experience and links technical work to cultural valuation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A complex problem that intersects several disciplines
- Organization and communication skills must be taught even if some students possess them
#### Constraints
- Steps culminate in valuing the accomplishment relative to the cultures of surroundings, nation and the world, which requires humanities and social science input, not only science and mathematics

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students undertaking JITE projects

### Learning Goals
- Multidisciplinary problem identification, information gathering, synthesis, further-development assessment, and cultural valuation of outcomes
