# Theoretical chemistry course structured as a micro-to-macro teaching sequence across four modules

## Description
The article proposes a TC course that rebuilds theoretical-physics teaching content for chemists, organized from micro- to macro-system. It states that "the TC course gives the basic theory of dynamics, including classical and non-relativistic quantum theory", then statistical mechanics as a bridge between macroscopic and microscopic systems, then electronic structure and Hamiltonian construction, and finally classical and quantum molecular dynamics with kinetics. The course "needs at least 40 academic hours, including about 8 academic hours for experiences and examination", and is positioned as an advanced undergraduate or graduate course that trains critical and abstract thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have completed general physical chemistry and college physics, which "provide sufficient knowledge reserves for the present TC course".
- Requires mathematical and computational tools such as linear algebra, integral and differential equations, and programming.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the course "might be considered to be a certain degree of difficulty for chemists".

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate chemistry students
- graduate chemistry students

### Learning Goals
- chemical reaction theory (dynamics and kinetics)
- critical and abstract thinking with mathematical tools
