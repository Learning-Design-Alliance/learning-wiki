# Two-day outreach structure: day one of mastery-learning microcycles, day two of a decomposed hackathon challenge competition

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The event's grain structure pairs a first day of lectures each followed by a formative assessment with "a series of microcycles, in the sense of mastery-based learning", and a second day in which students tackle real-world problems. The competition was organized into three modules (quantum communication, quantum devices, quantum optimization), where "Each module consisted of one or more challenges, which in turn consisted of several objectives" stated as OITT sentences and graded pass/fail against specifications, with a breadth requirement of one challenge per module and optional advanced objectives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecture content must be a prerequisite for the hackathon challenges, and problems must be adequately decomposed into sizable chunks to be tractable after one day of lessons.
#### Constraints
- The authors found two days were not enough: there was more material presented than time given to digest it, and only one day was available for participants to get acquainted before team formation.

### Target Learners
- high school students new to quantum computing, including those from underrepresented groups

### Learning Goals
- understanding single-qubit operations, teleportation, and applying quantum computing to real-world problems
