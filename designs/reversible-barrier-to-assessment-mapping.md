# Reversible barrier-to-assessment mapping as an instructional design framework

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)

## Description
The article organizes instruction through what it calls an explicitly traceable mapping from documented learner difficulties to learning goals, activity responses, and assessment evidence. The mapping is reversible: an instructor can begin with an assessment response and trace it back through the learning goal and activity to the documented difficulty. The article states this reverse mapping provides a practical check on instructional alignment and a portable design strategy for adapting the approach to other quantum-mechanics topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Documented learner difficulties must be identified first, so that learning goals, simulator displays, activity prompts, and assessment evidence can each be derived from and traced back to them
#### Constraints
- The article states the mapping is defined by the relationship among learner difficulty, learning goal, activity, and assessment evidence rather than by the particular simulator, and adaptation to other topics requires replacing the targeted barriers and instructional responses

### Target Learners
- undergraduate quantum-mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- coordinating mathematical state representations with measurement outcomes in quantum mechanics
