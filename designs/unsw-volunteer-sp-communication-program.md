# University of New South Wales volunteer simulated patient program for preclinical communication skills training

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A volunteer SP program implemented in 2015 at the University of New South Wales, replacing a prior student roleplay program for preclinical communication skills training. Volunteer SPs were "drawn from the local community and given support to play realistic patient roles at our clinical skills centre using pre-written scenarios." Sessions ran across 5 organ-system courses and 2 discipline-specific courses, with the last session of each course dedicated to communication skills via RP or SP interactions. The transition was cost-neutral for the institution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteers from the local community supported to play realistic patient roles using pre-written scenarios
- Clinical skills sessions embedded across year 1 and 2 courses
#### Constraints
- SP programs using trained professional medical actors require significant administrative and financial investment; the volunteer model was adopted as a more sustainable alternative

### Target Learners
- Junior (preclinical, years 1-2) undergraduate medical students

### Learning Goals
- Generic communication skills
- Clinical communication (history-taking) skills
- Physical examination and procedural skills
