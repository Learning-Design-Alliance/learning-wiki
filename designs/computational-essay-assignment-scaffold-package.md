# Computational essay assignment with scaffold package (seed simulations, example essays, rubric, help sessions, presentations)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A computational essay is "a genre of scientific writing that combines live code, written prose, mathematics, and pictures or diagrams in order to make an argument, explain an idea, or tell a story", written in computational notebooks such as Jupyter. The UiO design pairs the assignment with scaffolds: a written project description, a 5-category 0-4 rubric with a 14/20 pass threshold, stripped-down seed simulations (storm cloud, lightning, cyclotron, railgun, magnetic bottle, and later neuron, polar molecules, dipole antenna), example essays in an online showroom, weekly drop-in help sessions, and mock research-group presentations of 8 or 13 minutes plus two minutes of questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need baseline programming and numerical-methods preparation; the design presupposes a level of computational literacy, and scaffolds (examples, prompts, help sessions) must be provided.
- A pass threshold of at least 70% (14/20) on the rubric was specified.
#### Constraints
- The authors note universities without computation integrated into physics courses may struggle to replicate the intervention, though scaffolds can be tuned by adding guidelines or adjusting seed complexity.

### Target Learners
- 3rd-semester undergraduate physics students

### Learning Goals
- defining and answering a self-chosen research question via simulation
- identifying and addressing model assumptions
- scientific communication of methods and results
