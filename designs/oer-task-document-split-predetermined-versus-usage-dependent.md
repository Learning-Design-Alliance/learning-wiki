# Framework-based task characterization distinguishes predetermined design aspects from usage-dependent aspects when sharing tasks as Open Educational Resources

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When publishing tasks as Open Educational Resources, the DigiPhysLab project splits the six design principles: aspects related to learning objectives, task conception, and design of materials "are predetermined by us in the students' versions of our task documents," while aspects of target group, implementation, and circumstances "are dependent on the actual usage" and appear only as suggestions in instructors' versions. This lets other instructors adapt tasks to local conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Separate student and instructor versions of task documents
#### Constraints
- Suggestions for implementation and circumstances are based on experiences from the three faculties and may not transfer to other local conditions

### Target Learners
- university physics students and instructors adopting shared tasks

### Learning Goals
- adaptable reuse of experimental tasks across institutions
