# Three-step project pattern: dimensional analysis, experiments, theoretical modeling

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The project follows the sequence typical of engineering and physics practice: students first perform dimensional analysis to rationally simplify the problem, then design experiments whose data reveal an empirical relationship among the dimensionless parameters, and finally build a theoretical model explaining the data. The article states "The activity follows the three steps typical in engineering or physics practice" and that students can follow every step with freedom in each, under teacher guidance. The pattern is integrated progressively alongside the theoretical content of a Fluid Mechanics course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in groups of three and submit a short report after approximately one month before solutions are discussed
- Instructor intervention is intentionally limited, focusing on conceptual guidance rather than explicit solutions
#### Constraints
- Implementation details may vary depending on the context and the preferences of the instructor

### Target Learners
- engineering or physics undergraduates

### Learning Goals
- dimensional analysis
- experimental techniques from design to data analysis
- theoretical modeling
