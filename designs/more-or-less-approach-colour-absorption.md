# A 'more or less' approach to light intensity and partial absorption as an alternative to 'all or nothing' reasoning in colour phenomena

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This account holds that interpreting everyday colour phenomena requires considering both the spectral composition of light and its intensity, i.e. partial absorption, rather than the dichotomic presence/absence logic of classical additive and subtractive mixing rules. The article builds on de Hosson and Kaminski (2007) to lead students "from an 'all or nothing' way of reasoning to another one in 'more or less' terms", making the visibility of a laser beam's impact on any coloured surface intelligible. The teaching pathway operationalises it in two phases: a presence/absence phase with coloured shadows, then a critical more-or-less phase with a laser pointer.
