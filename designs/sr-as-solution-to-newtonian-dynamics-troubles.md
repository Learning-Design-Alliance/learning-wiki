# Teaching special relativity as the solution to the troubles of Newtonian dynamics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes an approach in which special relativity is presented as the resolution of problems internal to Newtonian mechanics, exemplified by the infinities of uniformly accelerated motion, rather than as a set of new postulates imposed on students. Its main section derives the kinematic effects — time dilation, length contraction, Doppler effect, relativity of simultaneity, and Lorentz transformations — using "thought experiments with the exchange of ﬂashes of light of null duration between two inertial reference frames", complemented by simulations and discussion of experimental corroborations. The proposal addresses documented student difficulties with frames of reference, the speed of light, and reciprocity of relativistic effects.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must make a dedicated effort to tailor the material to their specific teaching environment, choosing topics based on students' prior knowledge and their own preferences
#### Constraints

- preliminary classroom testing is limited to two settings

### Target Learners
- students in elementary physics courses (intermediate between high school and university)
- upper years of Italian scientific Lyceum (last three years)

### Learning Goals
- understand the postulates and kinematic effects of special relativity
- overcome entrenched Newtonian/Galilean conceptions of time, frames of reference and speed limits
