# Three-phase tutoring structure for math word problems: understanding, calculation, and summarization

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies a three-phase structure organizing parent-child math word problem tutoring. The article states that "word problem solving was described as a process that embodies three recurrent phases, namely understanding, calculation, and summarization." Each phase establishes a temporary focus of attention and shared expectations about what counts as progress, regulating when reasoning advances and how instructional authority is distributed. Phase boundaries are actively enforced by parents rather than implicitly assumed, serving as an interactional script that guides instruction and regulates problem-solving behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents must actively enforce phase boundaries and authorize transitions between phases for the structure to regulate tutoring interaction
#### Constraints
- Derived from interviews with 11 Chinese parents and 2 teachers in the context of upper-elementary math word problem tutoring
- Aligns with classical problem solving models according to the article

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary students in grades 4-6 (ages 10-12)

### Learning Goals
- Mathematical word problem solving including interpretation, calculation, and reflection
