# Serious educational game for burn management covering eight content domains with mastery-based progression and instructor feedback

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A serious game developed by a multidisciplinary team of software engineers and academic faculty to teach burn management to medical students. Content spans domains such as "Basic Concepts of Burns", "Burn Assessment", "Burn Diagnosis", escharotomy and fasciotomy procedures, fluid resuscitation calculation, referral decision-making, communication and professionalism, and ethics. Students view instructional videos, answer time-limited questions, and receive automated correctness indicators, explanatory text, and instructor-recorded video explanations; incorrect answers require feedback review before advancing, and progression requires a minimum accuracy threshold of 60% per stage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the software platform with individual login credentials during the one-week burn rotation
- Multidisciplinary design collaboration and expert content review to establish content validity
#### Constraints
- Engagement and effectiveness were evaluated in a pilot phase and a single-center trial; effectiveness may be influenced by alignment between task complexity and learner expertise, including a potential reverse expertise effect among novice learners

### Target Learners
- Medical students beginning burn clinical rotations with no prior burn management experience

### Learning Goals
- Burn assessment and diagnosis
- Burn management procedures and fluid resuscitation
- Referral decision-making, communication, professionalism, and ethics in burn care
