# Half-day simulation course pattern: briefing–simulation–debriefing–conclusion with real-case scenarios, trainee-played patient, observer checklists and four learning-objective elements

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The programme's reusable course pattern runs scenarios "in a usual format (briefing–simulation–debriefing–conclusion)" with at least two simulation rounds per course. Scenarios reproduce the full clinical workflow from EMS prenotification through CT interpretation to thrombolysis, use real cases adapted for education, assign trainees to roles including the patient to build communication insight, and use four auditorium observers with checklists feeding a three-phase debriefing. Learning objectives cover logistics/workflow, decision-making, teamwork (crew resource management principles) and patient communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardly equipped simulation centre (hospital bed, patient monitor, artificial CT scanner, audio-visual equipment, control room)
- A neurologist experienced in thrombolytic treatment leading sessions with at least a simulation technician and a methodologist/lecturer
- Attendance by all healthcare professionals in the logistics pathway, with department heads encouraged to attend
#### Constraints
- Scenarios were adapted for educational purposes rather than being exact clinical reproductions
- The patient role is played by a trainee rather than a trained actor

### Target Learners
- Stroke-centre physicians, nurses, paramedics and radiologists involved in acute stroke logistics

### Learning Goals
- Shortening door-to-needle time
- Teamwork and closed-loop communication
- Thrombolysis decision-making
- Communication with patients
