# Oral and visual narratives as tools for high school students to make sense of the electric field

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study argues that "oral and visual narratives can become tools for high school students to make sense of concepts such as the electric field". A narrative is defined as a story comprising "characters (who), time (when), location (where), and a series of events (what happened)". Before formal instruction, students cast electric charges as characters and electric interactions as events in drawings and speech, and the author reads these narratives to access the electrostatic ideas students express.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students produce narratives in both visual and oral formats about charges as characters and interactions as events
#### Constraints
- Narratives need not be explanations; the study treats them as possible descriptions of the interaction between electric charges

### Target Learners
- high school students aged 15-16 prior to formal instruction on electrostatics

### Learning Goals
- making sense of electric charges, electric interactions, and the electric field
