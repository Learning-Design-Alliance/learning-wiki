# Tufts minimal-instruction pendulum lab (Galileo challenge and meaning-making session)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Tufts pendulum lab runs over three sessions of up to 14 students, instructed by graduate and undergraduate TAs, with no lab-specific lecture and no homework. The written materials are minimal, just half a page long for each of the first two sessions, presenting the empirical challenge but providing no instructions on how to accomplish it. The first task asks how precisely students can measure a pendulum period; the second frames the challenge against Galileo's claims, which typically leads students to expect to confirm them, setting up an empirical discrepancy. The third session is a whole-class meaning-making discussion, framed by a translated quote from al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham about submitting only to argument and demonstration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TAs who focus on listening and supporting the substance of student ideas, treating confusion as productive and discouraging black-box use of algorithms.
- A yearly teaching-team review of the instruction sheets so they can be revised responsively to student ideas.
#### Constraints
- With limited time, there is no instruction or guidance toward established statistical measures of variation or representational forms; the authors note that with more time they could follow students' inventions with explicit instruction.

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students at a small, private, selective research university

### Learning Goals
- learning how to learn, including assessing the quality of their knowledge and making decisions they see as reasonable
- shifting from doing school toward doing science
