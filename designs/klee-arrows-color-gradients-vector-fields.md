# Klee's arrows and color gradients as a mathematical bridge to vector fields

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's organizing framework: Paul Klee's pedagogical sketchbook devotes its sections 37 to 40 directly to the arrow, distinguishing the real and the symbolic arrow and characterizing it as a "synthesis of cause and effect", giving it spatial and temporal directedness. Klee's account of color gradients carrying a sense of direction matches the use of colors in topographic maps indicating the gradient direction of a scalar height field, making his art analyzable in terms of vector fields, gradient, curl, and divergence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need prior vector and differential calculus to access the mathematical side of the connection.
#### Constraints
- The authors state the level of abstraction of mathematics in Klee's art is comparably high; some participants chose to visualize fractals instead, suggesting the abstraction was probably too high for them, and suggest pairings like M.C. Escher and tilings could provide easier access.

### Target Learners
- gifted high-school students with calculus background

### Learning Goals
- understanding vector fields, gradient, curl, and divergence through art
