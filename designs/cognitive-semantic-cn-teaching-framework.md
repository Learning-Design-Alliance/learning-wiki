# Cognitive-semantic framework for teaching compound nouns: encyclopedic meaning, grammar-lexicon continuum, and symbolic nature of language

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The chapter draws on three tenets of cognitive linguistics to frame compound-noun instruction: word meaning is encyclopaedic, so students can draw on specialised domain knowledge to interpret CNs; grammar and lexicon form a continuum, so lexicalised CNs are learnt as terms; and grammar is symbolic in nature, so CN formation can be explained graphically via metonymy-style highlighting diagrams. The framework motivates the experimental condition of the case study and three research questions about implicit learning, repeated exposure, and word order.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must possess specialised domain knowledge (from internships or field study) that they can mobilise to interpret compounds in their discipline
#### Constraints
- The graphic representation fails to capture a small number of figurative CNs based on metaphor or metonymy

### Target Learners
- French lower-intermediate (A2-B1) university students of English for Specific Purposes in science and technology

### Learning Goals
- understanding and correctly producing English noun+noun compound constructions in specialised domains
