# Learning outcomes framed as demonstrations of application, analysis, evaluation, behaviour and attitude rather than knowledge reproduction

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines learning outcomes not as knowledge to reproduce but as descriptions of professional behaviour: 'learning outcomes describe behavior and attitude'. Drawing on Bloom's taxonomy, Fink, and Allan's distinction between subject-based and personal transferable outcomes, the authors write outcomes that students can only exhibit if they know, understand and can apply underlying details. Each LO is detailed in a rubric clarifying levels of performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Outcomes should be abstract, complementary, non-overlapping lemmas covering the whole field of expertise
#### Constraints
- Such LOs suit large units of 30–60 ECTS, not small curricular units

### Target Learners
- BSc ICT students

### Learning Goals
