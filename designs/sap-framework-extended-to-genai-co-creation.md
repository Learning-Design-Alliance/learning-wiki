# Students-as-partners framework extended to GenAI tool co-creation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The students-as-partners (SaP) framework positions students and staff in a "collaborative, reciprocal process through which all participants have the opportunity to contribute equally, although not necessarily in the same ways, to curricular or pedagogical conceptualization, decision-making, implementation, investigation, or analysis". The article applies SaP values of respect, reciprocity, and shared responsibility to co-creating custom GPTs, arguing that co-creation with GenAI can extend the SaP paradigm to include GenAI tools, with students as co-creators rather than passive users or risks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students and academics engage as equal partners in decision-making with shared responsibility, reciprocity, and mutual learning
#### Constraints
- Co-design does not make all expertise identical: students contribute learner expertise while academics verify disciplinary accuracy and pedagogical appropriateness

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science students

### Learning Goals
- digital literacies and critical understanding of AI
- partnership-driven learning and reflexivity
