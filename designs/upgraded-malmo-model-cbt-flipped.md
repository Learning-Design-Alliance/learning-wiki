# The upgraded Malmö model: case-based teaching with flipped classroom seminars replacing pure PBL in dental education

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The upgraded Malmö model retains the original model's pillars while replacing pure problem-based learning with case-based teaching, in which teachers design cases and provide direction, study materials and structure. The original model rested on activation of prior knowledge, encoding specificity by contextual learning and elaboration of knowledge, with pillars including "Active learning—actively engaging students through discussions, problem‐solving, case studies, roleplay and other methods." Cases are introduced gradually, and learning activities such as flipped classroom seminars, quizzes and peer-assessed assignments are added. The change responded to rising examination failure rates and students struggling with the responsibility PBL demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must design cases based on subject-matter expertise and provide thorough study instructions with clear learning goals and literature references
- Students need some degree of prior knowledge to address the cases, unlike PBL
#### Constraints
- Not all knowledge and understanding objectives are studied through cases; teacher-directed delivery of disciplinary knowledge is used for some objectives
- Group work is mandatory only during the first year, thereafter voluntary but highly encouraged

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students (also dental technicians and dental hygienists in the original model)

### Learning Goals
- clinical competence and professional approach
- self-directed continuing learning
- critical thinking
