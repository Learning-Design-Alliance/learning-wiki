# Sprint structure with soft and hard deadlines, burndown charts, and group progress meetings

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course is divided into three phases (imperative programming, object-oriented programming, project), each split into two-week sprints with one assignment. Each assignment has a soft deadline at sprint end and a hard deadline one sprint later; missing a hard deadline triggers a face-to-face planning meeting with no punishment or lost points. Students plot remaining achievements as burndown charts and meet a TA in groups of about 10 each sprint to compare progress. The authors note that experiments with other progress visualisations "most of which led to increased stress".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Discrete time units are needed to make progression visible given freely timed demonstrations
#### Constraints
- Judging from experiments with more/fewer/longer/shorter sprints, finding a balance between a sense of urgency and student stress is key

### Target Learners
- second-year university programming students

### Learning Goals
- planning and following up on learning
- self-regulated pacing through the course
