# GeoGebra-based RC capacitor discharge fitting activity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A laboratory activity in which the discharge curve of a capacitor in an RC circuit driven by a square wave generator is displayed on an analog oscilloscope, photographed with a smartphone, and imported into GeoGebra. Students sample the curve by clicking points, superimpose an analytically defined exponential with a slider-defined time constant, estimate uncertainty with upper and lower envelope curves, and compare their manual fit with the automatic exponential regression (RegExp). The article reports it was designed during data analysis in a physics lab didactics course for pre-service teachers and is intended to be tested with high-school students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-budget instruments: square wave generator, RC circuit with ceramic capacitor, analog oscilloscope, smartphone, and GeoGebra; at least a sample of 10 measures of potential as a function of time
#### Constraints
- The article states the analysis of electric circuits is not the most effective moment to introduce fitting, since students meet electricity late and exponentials are not the best entry curve

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students
- pre-service physics teachers

### Learning Goals
- estimating the RC time constant
- understanding fitting and uncertainty
- applying analytic geometry to a physical problem
