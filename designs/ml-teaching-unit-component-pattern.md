# A teaching unit combines a theoretical presentation, a fillable Jupyter notebook lecture with mini assignments, ungraded quizzes, varied reading material, and homework with coding and non-coding tasks.

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author's teaching units pair each machine learning theme, such as a new algorithm or a concept like cross-validation, with a Physics or Astronomy problem. Each unit combines "traditional coursework elements (for example, slides and review questions) with more hands-on content", including a fillable Jupyter notebook lecture with 10-15 minute mini assignments, ungraded multiple-choice quizzes usable as think/pair/share, mixed reading resources, and homework with coding and non-coding tasks such as writing pseudocode.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecture notebooks, slides, quizzes, reading materials, and assignments must be developed so the traditional and hands-on components talk to each other.
#### Constraints
- Tailored to introductory courses at the advanced undergraduate/early graduate level in grade-bearing classes; for informal settings such as summer schools the author skips assignments and makes each unit self-contained.

### Target Learners
- Advanced undergraduate and early graduate STEM students

### Learning Goals
- Foundational ML methods and discipline-wide concepts in a physics context
