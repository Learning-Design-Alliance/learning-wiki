# Open-inquiry light-decay laboratory pattern: model proposal, measurement with accessible devices, and validity-range discussion

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This lesson-level pattern asks students to study light decay curves for sources with different geometries, "proposing a model to explain the experimental results and discussing the limits of validity". It integrates theoretical analysis with hands-on experimentation using accessible resources: students analyze a theoretical model, design a simple setup with homemade materials, and compare measured curves against models to identify validity ranges. The activity was proposed during the COVID19 pandemic as a distance activity and maintained afterwards because of positive feedback from students and teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires light sources of different geometries and smartphone light sensors available to students at home or in the laboratory
#### Constraints
- All the results presented were obtained by a small group of students working at home, so the reported dataset is small

### Target Learners
- science and engineering students

### Learning Goals
- formulating models from experimental data
- studying validity ranges of physical laws
- fostering creativity and problem-solving skills
