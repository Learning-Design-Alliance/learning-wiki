# Constructivist and cooperative learning theories, informed by TPACK, as the theoretical basis for integrating flipped classroom and team-based learning

## Description
The authors ground their FC-TBL instructional model in constructivist and cooperative learning theories, further informed by the Technological Pedagogical and Content Knowledge (TPACK) framework. Constructivism "emphasizes that learners construct knowledge through active engagement and reflection, rather than passive reception." The TPACK lens highlights that "effective flipped learning depends on instructors' integrated understanding of content, pedagogy, and technology when designing pre-class materials and orchestrating in-class learning activities." The authors also invoke Vygotsky's zone of proximal development to explain how peer interaction and timely instructor guidance enable learners to reach higher cognitive levels, and Bandura's social cognitive theory to explain self-efficacy gains through mastery accomplishments, vicarious learning, and social persuasion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must assume multiple roles as facilitator, supporter, and evaluator—providing resources before class, guiding academic exchange with immediate feedback during class, and implementing individualized pedagogical support after class.
#### Constraints
- Evidence for the FC model has largely concentrated on undergraduate populations; its application in graduate-level literature seminar courses remains underexplored according to the authors.
