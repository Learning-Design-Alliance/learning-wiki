# Drag-equilibrium model of parachute terminal velocity

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A mechanistic model of vertical fall through an atmosphere: drag acts opposite to gravity and is "proportional zur Dichte des Mediums", to cross-sectional area, and to the square of flow velocity, scaled by the dimensionless drag coefficient cw. Setting acceleration to zero at constant terminal speed yields vE = √(2·m·g/(cw·ϱ·A)); unlike vacuum free fall, terminal velocity depends on the object's mass.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The drag coefficient cw must be known for the medium and object; the material notes it can be determined experimentally, for example in a wind tunnel.
#### Constraints
- The lesson applies the model under stated simplifying assumptions: vertical fall, neglected buoyancy, massless parachute, constant gravitational acceleration, homogeneous density, constant temperature, pure CO2 atmosphere, ideal gas.

### Target Learners
- students in grades 9-13 (Sekundarstufe)

### Learning Goals
- understanding forces and terminal velocity in fall with air resistance
- distinguishing drag-dominated fall from vacuum free fall
