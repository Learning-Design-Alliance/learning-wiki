# Laboratory function-learning task for classifying concept-building approaches

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A computer-paced laboratory task, unrelated to course content, used to index each student's concept-building approach. Students predict an output variable (amount of Beros released) from an input variable (amount of Zebon absorbed) across 200–260 training trials following an inverted-V function, then complete 36 no-feedback test trials with novel inputs. Extrapolation mean absolute error is compared against the simple exemplar model's MAE of 34.72 with 95% confidence intervals to classify learners as exemplar or abstraction learners. The task takes approximately 40 minutes and was administered online outside class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must reach a learning criterion (final training block MAE < 10) for classification; those with MAE ≥ 10 cannot be classified because "with incomplete learning, transfer patterns are not diagnostic of a particular learning approach"
#### Constraints
- Administered online, which the authors suspect contributed to low effort and failure to meet the learning criterion for many students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory biology students

### Learning Goals
- diagnosing individual differences in concept-building approach independent of course content
