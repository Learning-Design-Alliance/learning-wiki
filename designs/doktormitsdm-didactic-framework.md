# doktormitSDM didactic framework: in-situ ultrashort feedback, handling of control, and stimulated continuous self-observation

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The training rests on a didactic concept addressing feasibility, clinician barriers, and sustainable transfer. Feasibility is pursued by delivering the interactive component at the participant's ward: the authors "presumed that behaviour change was most likely to happen when the interactive part of the training was provided at the participant's ward (in situ) and shaped and structured as an ultrashort unit of 15 minutes." Behaviour change is addressed via attitude-related beliefs, subjective social norms and behaviour control, with feedback kept explicitly qualitative and non-judgemental to avoid devaluation. Control is handled as the key to learning potential: voluntary relinquishment of control through delivering one's own work samples, and re-transferal of control through transparency, is meant to model a good doctor–patient dialogue and induce self-observation even in the trainer's absence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be recognizable as both highly professional and precise in its detailed descriptions and explicitly qualitative
- Trainers must adapt to each trainee's SDM level and continuously evaluate the trainees' capacity and tolerance
#### Constraints
- Marginal discrepancies between training content or context and the doctors' reality might give reason to adopt a rejecting stance
- Feedback must be stopped before resistance rises

### Target Learners
- specialist doctors
- medical students (adapted online version being tested)

### Learning Goals
- shared decision-making communication
- self-observation and self-reflection on own consultation behaviour
