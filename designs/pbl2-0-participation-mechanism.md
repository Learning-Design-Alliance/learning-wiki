# Increased participation as the key mechanism of effective problem-based learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' explanatory account of why PBL works and why it had gone stale: "the key ingredient is increased participation," with engagement in dynamic, interactive problem solving while working through a case. In the pre-intervention format students over-prepared their own individual presentations and 'tuned out' during peers' presentations; PBL 2.0 restores interaction by requiring universal preparation of all objectives, so students must bolster their fund of knowledge before day-two discussions. The authors frame this as a return to the team-oriented problem-solving origins of PBL.

### Learning Goals
