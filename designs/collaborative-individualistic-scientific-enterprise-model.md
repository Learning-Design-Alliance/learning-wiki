# Collaborative-individualistic model of the scientific enterprise as a design rationale for assessment

## Description
The article defines four peer-involvement environments and argues authentic science is collaborative and individualistic rather than cooperative or competitive: "collaborative: peer interaction is freely available, encouraged, but not necessary", while cooperative groups have defined roles and shared rewards and competitive environments reward only top performers. Research is characterized as not a well-structured task because the outcome is unknown, and publication depends on objective merit rather than relative ranking. The Buddy Quiz is designed to emulate this environment, with each student submitting their own paper as first author.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual grades unrelated to the peer's performance
- Unconstrained rather than role-structured peer discussion
#### Constraints
- The authors do not claim the Buddy Quiz reproduces the societal and political dynamics of cooperative models of professional science, such as government-corporate research contracts
- Races to expensive fundamental discoveries, as in experimental particle physics, are an exception where cooperation governs

- secondary science students
