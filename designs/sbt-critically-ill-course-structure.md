# A 4-day simulation-based course combining short lectures, workshops, scenario-based simulation and structured debriefing prepares first-year doctors for managing critically ill patients

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The mandatory course runs 4 days across the first two months of clinical practice, combining short lectures, workshops and SBT scenarios of around 20 minutes followed by feedback or three-phase debriefing (description, analysis, application). Teams of five doctors role-play first-year doctor, senior doctor and nurses. The article reports that "The balance between theory and praxis and the time for reflection generates optimal outcome from the course".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors certified in SBT and in conducting feedback/debriefings
- A safe learning environment without risk of embarrassment in front of colleagues
- Scenario realism, with complexity limited to allow focus on relevant learning goals
#### Constraints
- Scenario difficulty needs to be balanced
- A low level of updated theoretical medical knowledge impairs focus on non-technical skills learning
- Realism depends on participant attitude; if one refuses to play, realism drops heavily

### Target Learners
- first-year doctors in their foundation year

### Learning Goals
- handling the critically ill patient
- non-technical skills such as communication, teamwork, leadership and situational awareness
- use of the ABCDE, SBAR and ACCEPT algorithms
