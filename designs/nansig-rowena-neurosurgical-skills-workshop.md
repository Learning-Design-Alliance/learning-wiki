# NANSIG SBNS-accredited neurosurgical skills workshop using the ROWENA simulator with a 2:1 student-to-teacher ratio

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An annual national UK workshop teaching neurosurgical skills solely to medical students and foundation trainees, launched in 2017 and SBNS-accredited. It uses the ROWENA (Realistic Operative Workstation for Educating Neurosurgical Apprentices) simulator, chosen "for its anatomical accuracy and tissue-handling capabilities resembling real/cadaveric tissue". Five workstations each held a simulator, 3-point head clamp and drill, with five registrar teachers at a 2:1 student-to-teacher ratio covering structured modules (positioning, burr holes, ventricular access, flaps). Delegates were selected by triple-blind marking of applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delegates selected via triple-blind marking of applications on commitment to neurosurgery
- Registrar teachers covering structured outcomes according to listed modules
- Risk assessment and safety contingency plans in partnership with sponsors
#### Constraints
- Small delegate cohort by design, due to the focus on low tutor to delegate ratios during teaching

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Foundation year trainees pursuing neurosurgery

### Learning Goals
- Fundamental neurosurgical skills such as burr-hole creation
- Confidence assisting in theatre
