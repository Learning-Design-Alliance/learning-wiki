# Dictogloss task sequence for form-focused vocabulary learning

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Dictogloss is a form of dictation in which students hear and reconstruct a whole text rather than doing so line by line (Wajnryb, 1990). In the study's FoF condition, the teacher first activated background knowledge with questions, students read a text, then "teachers read a short text containing new words twice and at a normal speed," students noted what they could, and in "small groups of three" reconstructed the text and compared and analyzed the different versions produced. The pattern integrates listening, note-taking, collaborative reconstruction, and form analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A short text containing the target new words; small groups for reconstruction; a comparison and analysis phase of the produced versions
#### Constraints
- In this study the technique was used with beginning-proficiency Iranian ESP learners and technical vocabulary from their field of study

### Target Learners
- University ESP students at beginning proficiency level

### Learning Goals
- Productive knowledge of new technical vocabulary
