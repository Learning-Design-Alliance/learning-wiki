# The non-injury model: an educational cognitive account of chronic low back pain built on the robustness of the spine

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The non-injury model, adapted from Indahl, is a cognitive/educational framework for managing chronic low back pain. It reframes the back as a strong structure and teaches that pain is not a sign of injury: the article describes it as "based on a 'non-injury model' intended to focus on the back as a strong structure, where pain was not to be taken as a sign of injury caused by any wrongdoing". It contrasts with the injury model, in which pain is taken as a sign of injury from increased loading, which the authors say has medicalised common LBP without clinical utility. In this trial the model was delivered as a brief educational programme aiming to instil confidence and reduce pain focus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery requires a clinician able to convey the message so that the patient internalises it; the article states a crucial part is "how effectively it is delivered, and the person's internalisation of the message received".
#### Constraints
- The authors state comparisons with interventions based on the traditional injury model are "not useful" because the interventions pose different challenges.

### Target Learners
- adults with chronic or recurrent low back pain

### Learning Goals
- reducing fear-avoidance beliefs about the back
- building confidence that normal and strenuous activity does not damage the spine
- encouraging natural rather than cautious, pain-focused movement
