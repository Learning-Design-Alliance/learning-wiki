# Iterative research-driven curriculum development cycle for addressing student difficulties with mathematics in physics

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes an iterative process in which research on student difficulties guides curriculum development, and continued research feeds back into further development. Preliminary insights come from classroom interaction and examination analysis, followed by "iterative development, administration, and analysis of pretests, examination questions, and videotaped individual demonstration interviews." In the tutorial format employed, "the curriculum is developed with awareness of student difficulties" and activities are designed so students are engaged and responsible for their own construction of ideas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Conducting research on student difficulties before and during curriculum development
- Weekly instructor training sessions and exam questions on tutorial material to sustain implementation
#### Constraints
- The difficulties documented occurred after instruction that included lectures addressing the problem and readings of a standard text

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics university students

### Learning Goals
- applying mathematical ideas learned in math courses to physics problem solving
