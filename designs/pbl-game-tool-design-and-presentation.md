# Project-based learning game technique: students design and present physical game tools embodying physics laws

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This pattern combines project-based learning with game techniques: student groups design physical game tools that embody target physics concepts, build them over several weeks, and present and explain the physics to classmates. In the study, participants "were divided into 7 groups and asked to design game tool projects appropriate for the assigned topics" covering Newton's Laws of Motion, producing projects such as a rocket car, swing, dominos, slide, bumper carts, carousel, billiard, bowling balls with Newton's cradle, golf, ball spinning, and arrow. In the fifth week students presented the game systems to their classmates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in groups on assigned physics topics
- A presentation phase in which students explain the physics of their game tools to classmates
- A multi-week classroom period for designing and building the game tools
#### Constraints
- Students had difficulties finding necessary materials, so the article recommends providing needed equipment and appropriate infrastructure before implementation
- Presentation was the most challenging phase for students due to anxiety

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade high school physics students

### Learning Goals
- Understanding Newton's Laws of Motion (inertia, action-reaction, friction, second law)
