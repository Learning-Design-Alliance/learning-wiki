# The diagnostic-review mechanism: AI issue-level diagnosis passing through student interpretation, revision, teacher review, and reflection

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a classroom sequence in which AI diagnostic feedback becomes formative only through downstream activity. "The classroom sequence was task submission, AI diagnosis, student interpretation, independent revision, structured teacher review, reassessment, and reﬂection." The framework integrates formative assessment, feedback literacy, self-regulated learning, and teacher-feedback research, ordered by how diagnostic information moves through interpretation, revision, reassessment, and reflection. Teacher review comprises "conﬁrmation, correction, supplementation, and re-explanation of AI feedback", connecting automated evidence with situated writing judgment and making review workload observable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Issue-level AI diagnosis that separates problem diagnosis from revision suggestions and does not rewrite the composition
- Structured student interpretation before revision (restating problems, explaining effects, identifying uncertainties, specifying a revision plan)
- Structured teacher review after revision, followed by reassessment and reflection
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from two intact classes taught by one instructor, leaving class selection and instructor effects in the observed group contrast
- Student time-on-task was not recorded, and the contrast may include additional interpretation, reflection, and revision time
- Cycle order coincided with task type, and no delayed post-test was administered

### Target Learners
- University EFL students in English writing courses

### Learning Goals
- L2 writing quality and revision skill
- Feedback literacy and self-assessment accuracy
- Self-regulated revision behavior
