# District homeless programs provide a coordinated web of six support categories: essential items, transportation, academic services, health supports, specialized unaccompanied-youth support, and family supports

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Across all five study districts, homeless program staff coordinated services provided by the district program, schools, and community-based organizations. The report enumerates the categories: "Essential items. District homeless programs in our study prioritized providing students and their families with basic necessities, including food, hygiene products, clothing, shoes, and school supplies." Transportation to and from school is required by the McKinney-Vento Act; academic services include tutoring, summer programs, and post–high school planning; health supports include physicals, screenings, counseling, and safety classes; unaccompanied youth receive shelter-embedded staff and prepaid cell phones; families receive linkages to child care, counseling, and emergency funding to prevent loss of housing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- McKinney-Vento-funded liaisons with dedicated time to provide and coordinate supports, plus partnerships with schools, shelters, food banks, medical providers, nonprofits, child welfare agencies, and housing authorities
#### Constraints
- Program staff consistently described transportation as a large cost for their programs, and emergency family funding was provided only when resources and need were known

### Target Learners
- k–12 students experiencing homelessness, including unaccompanied youth and their families

### Learning Goals
- educational engagement, attendance, and academic success for students in temporary housing
