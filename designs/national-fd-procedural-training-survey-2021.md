# National survey of internal medicine fellowship directors on procedural training expectations (REDCap, N = 424)

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A survey developed by the authors and administered via REDCap to 1,463 fellowship directors in the fifteen internal medicine subspecialties identified through the ACGME website; 424 responses were received (29% response rate). It asked directors to classify each of 19 procedures as requiring "only knowledge and understanding of, some experience but not competence in, or competence to perform," plus Likert attitude items and a free-text opinion question.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contact information sourced from the ACGME website; administered anonymously via REDCap with weekly reminder emails over six weeks
#### Constraints
- Competency was not defined in the survey because it is generally determined by individual programs

### Target Learners
- internal medicine fellowship directors
- internal medicine residents

### Learning Goals
- identifying subspecialty-specific procedural training expectations

### Claims

- [Internal medicine fellowship directors in 8 of 15 subspecialties prefer incoming fellows have competence in 1–10 (mean 5) of 19 listed procedures](../claims/fd-prefer-competence-1-10-of-19-procedures.md) [+W]
- [Fellowship directors in most subspecialties report fellows perform procedures, except geriatrics, hospice/palliative care, and infectious disease](../claims/fellows-perform-procedures-except-three-subspecialties.md) [+W]
- [Free-text concerns about the ABIM procedural requirement change cluster into themes including core competency skills, practical concerns, fellow readiness, culture shift, subspecialty-specific competency, and fellowship responsibility](../claims/fd-concern-themes-abim-change.md) [+W]
- [Most surveyed fellowship directors indicated performing procedures is safe and would improve patient care, patient ownership, and ability to obtain informed consent](../claims/fds-view-procedures-safe-and-valuable.md) [+W]
- [Preferred procedures for incoming fellows vary by subspecialty, e.g. cardiology directors prefer vascular access skills while hematology/oncology directors prefer only lumbar puncture](../claims/procedural-preferences-vary-by-subspecialty.md) [+W]
- [Changes in procedural competency requirements stem from patient safety concerns and work hour limitations, and residents have become less comfortable performing common bedside procedures](../claims/procedural-requirement-change-drivers-and-comfort-decline.md) [+W]
