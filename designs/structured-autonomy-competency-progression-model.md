# Structured autonomy model balancing surgical education with patient safety

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a model in which resident autonomy is implemented through a structured, competency-based progression framework rather than time-based seniority alone. Under this model, "autonomy should be granted based on demonstrated competency rather than seniority alone", with eligibility gates (exams, evaluations, coursework), preoperative attending review, on-call attending backup, and postoperative departmental feedback creating a supervised yet progressively independent learning environment. The model aims to balance comprehensive surgical training with patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Competency-based eligibility criteria preceding independent operating
- Attending availability on call during resident-led procedures
- Ongoing postoperative quality and safety review
#### Constraints
- Supported by retrospective single-center evidence; the authors note that the single-center nature with a well-established residency program may limit generalizability to other training environments

### Target Learners
- orthopedic surgery residents
- surgical training program directors

### Learning Goals
- operative independence in complex trauma procedures
- patient safety during trainee-led surgery
