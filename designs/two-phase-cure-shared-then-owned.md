# Two-phase course-based research experience: shared protocol first, then student-owned projects

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's research experience is designed in two phases within a regular course. In the first phase "all students in the course work together on the same research, and follow the same research protocol with the same data", which scales without one-on-one attention and eases grading. In the second phase students "choose their own research problem and use the same data and same tools to make discoveries", providing ownership, which the article calls an important element of undergraduate research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All activities must take place as part of a course and scale to a large number of students
- Discovery-enabling software tools usable without strong programming skills or prior computer science knowledge
#### Constraints
- First-year students' level of preparation cannot be assumed, so the research must rely on existing computational tools and existing databases

### Target Learners
- first-semester undergraduate computer science students

### Learning Goals
- research methodology: defining a research problem, designing and testing solutions, basic statistical inference, criticizing results, and describing work in a research paper
