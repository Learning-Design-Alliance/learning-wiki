# Quantum interactive learning tutorial (QuILT) on a quantum eraser using a Mach-Zehnder Interferometer with single photons

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A quantum interactive learning tutorial (QuILT) on a quantum eraser for students in upper-level quantum mechanics. It "uses a guided approach to learning" and "adapts existing visualization tools to help students build physical intuition about quantum phenomena." A computer simulation lets students "manipulate the MZI setup to predict and observe what happens at the detectors for different setups," and the tutorial addresses difficulties with single-photon interference, polarizers as measurement devices, and erasure of which-path information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Includes a warm-up with background information about the MZI setup and pre-/posttests to be given before and after the QuILT
- Makes use of a computer simulation in which students can manipulate the MZI setup to predict and observe what happens at the detectors
- Versions were iterated with three physics faculty members and administered to graduate and upper-level undergraduate students to check that questions were unambiguously interpreted
#### Constraints
- Students make simplifying assumptions about the MZI setup, e.g., all optical elements are ideal, polarizers are absorbing, and the beam-splitters are infinitesimally thin with no phase shift
- Designed for students in upper-level quantum mechanics

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate quantum mechanics students
- first-year graduate quantum mechanics students

### Learning Goals
- reason about which-path information and its erasure in a Mach-Zehnder Interferometer
- apply quantum principles such as wave-particle duality, interference of a single photon with itself, and collapse of a quantum state upon measurement
