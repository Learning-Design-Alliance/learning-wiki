# Organise the course as a four-part trajectory dominated by application practice with interleaved structure and procedure checkpoints

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The redesigned course is organised into four parts - Foundation, Understanding Python, Applying Python, Case study - that "roughly correspond to a 10%/10%/70%/10% time split". The application-heavy main part deliberately includes Structure and Procedure units "to prevent rote pattern copying", so conceptual checkpoints and procedural routines are interleaved with application work rather than separated into theory-only chapters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structure (S) content is kept concise and positioned as enabling checkpoints that support application work.
#### Constraints
- The contents must fit into 12 lectures plus approximately 4 hours of self-study per week over a 12-week term.

### Target Learners
- first-semester students in a non-computer-science bachelor programme with little or no prior programming experience

### Learning Goals
- developing, assessing, and extending programs (Application and Procedure knowledge)
- interleaving conceptual understanding with application practice
