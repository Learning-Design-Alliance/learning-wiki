# Three-level framework for institutional change: coordinating faculty, department, and administration levels

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article conceptualizes a university as a multi-leveled, interconnected system and proposes that change efforts target three levels: faculty, department, and administration. Each level is "a subsystem of the university (i.e., a collection of people, structures, and norms) that can be acted upon by a change process," with different change processes appropriate at different levels. Because the levels interact (e.g., administrative measures of teaching effectiveness influence faculty classroom practices), the framework holds that change efforts should target all levels rather than a single level in isolation. Departments are taken as the key unit of change because faculty are most influenced by their department's culture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Change efforts must account for the interrelations between levels, not act on one level in isolation
- Goals of the change effort must be made explicit to support alignment and evaluation of activities
#### Constraints
- The framework excludes other actors (e.g., students, postdocs, staff) because it focuses on actors most likely to influence department culture
- Boundaries between levels can be blurry, e.g., individuals serving dual roles as faculty and administrators

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students at research universities

### Learning Goals
- sustained adoption of research-based, student-centered teaching practices in STEM higher education
