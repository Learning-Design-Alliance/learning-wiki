# Mitigate the faculty workload of writing-intensive curricula with load credit, shared courses, and peer review

> **Design** · [All designs](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Writing-intensive curricula demand substantial instructor time for assignment design, writing instruction, and frequent feedback, so departments should commit resources deliberately. La Verne's department gave capstone-series instructors an extra unit of teaching load, shared the courses across faculty once established, listed the series in new-hire workload requirements, allocated departmental funds for postbaccalaureate teaching assistants, and used peer review to reduce first- and second-draft feedback burden. The article notes that "As long as courses are shared among the faculty, the workload decrease appears to balance the increase in effort required of course faculty."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departmental commitment and priority in program learning outcomes
- Peer review integrated into the curriculum
- Teaching assistants or equivalent support to facilitate peer-review sessions
#### Constraints
- Faculty resistance and time costs remain: cited studies report a writing-intensive course being equated to teaching two courses, and lab-level variation in this study reflected differing faculty investment

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate science students in writing-intensive programs

### Learning Goals
- Sustained writing instruction and feedback across a multisemester curriculum
