# Triage of every principle and pattern page (2026-10-02)

`triage.tsv` sorts the 837 principle and pattern pages into what they are, so the canonical
ones can be converted to the conditional-model format (`principle-pattern-authoring.md`) and
the rest folded into or linked from them. It is generated by
`scripts/triage_design_pages.py --table` from two model runs, and **writes no page**: every move
it implies is a decision listed below.

## How it was made

Each page was shown, with its title, description, opening text, inbound links, claims cited
and the number of sources that wrote it, beside its ten most similar pages of the same kind
(BM25 on title and description, then on the slug's own words), and classed as:

| Class | Meaning | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| canonical | the general, reusable page for its idea | convert it |
| duplicate | the same idea as a neighbour that should be the single page | merge, keep an alias |
| variant | a narrower version, one source's framing or one application | keep, link from the canonical page |
| design | (patterns) a design for one setting, course, population or product | never a pattern (settled rule); where designs live is open |
| misfiled | not a principle or pattern at all | move to the suggested kind |

Two runs, by models of different families: **a** GPT 5.6 Luna ($0.52) and **b** DeepSeek V4
Pro ($1.28). They agreed on the class of 64% of principles and 48% of patterns. The
disagreements are systematic, not noise: read in a sample, GPT draws the design and misfiled
lines where the wiki's settled rules do, and DeepSeek calls most single-setting designs
"variants" and most recommendation pages "canonical". So the `verdict` column is: agreement
where they agree; **design** where one says design and the other variant (both say it is not a
general page); otherwise GPT's verdict, with `review` set. Each row keeps both verdicts and both
reasons, so any row can be re-read.

The numbers beside the verdicts (`inbound`, `claims`, `sources`, `converted`) are facts about
the page; the verdicts are model judgements and have not been read by a person.

- **The core to convert is about 100 pages.** Canonical pages with 5 or more inbound links: 106,
  of which 15 are already converted; with 10 or more: 66 (12 converted). Canonical pages with
  fewer than 3 inbound links (108) are general but little used, and can wait.
- **Most review flags are on pages little rests on**: 305 rows are flagged, 38 of them with 10 or
  more inbound links and 104 with 3 or more. Those 104 are worth reading before any move.
- **Duplicates point both ways**: for 20 of the 46, the named target has fewer inbound links than
  the duplicate (`flipped-classroom` 92 against `flipped-learning`; `cognitively-guided-instruction-
  cgi-for-math` 73 against `cgi-for-math`). Which page survives is decided at merge time, by
  inbound links and content, not by the column.
- **Seven of the 24 converted pages are classed as variants or duplicates**: `principles/
  cognitive-load-theory` (of `cognitive-load-management`, 395 inbound links), `scaffolding-and-
  fading` (of `scaffolding-fading`), `self-regulated-learning` (of `self-regulation`),
  `cooperative-learning` (of `social-interdependence`), `direct-instruction` (of `explicit-
  instruction`), `peer-discussion` (of `peer-learning`) and `patterns/team-based-learning` (of
  `cooperative-learning`). Where a converted page is the better page, it should become the
  canonical one and the others fold into it.
- **Misfiled pages** would move to: method 35, strategy 34, theory 23, design 18, process 11,
  claim 10, element 7. A move across kinds changes a page's id namespace, which an alias cannot
  carry, so the few with inbound links need their links repointed.
