## Brief test (2026-10-05)

Same design as wave 4.
- **Briefs:** 30, written by GPT 5.6 Luna before any draft existed.
- **Answers:** Kimi K3, with the token limit raised to 16,000. No answer was cut off.
- **Grading:** Gemini 3.8 Flash and DeepSeek V4 Pro, scored answers and blind pairs in both orders.
- **Cost:** $3.33 for answers, $2.42 for grading, $0.61 for the first rework re-test, $0.41 for the second.

**What the numbers show:**
- **The largest gains in fit of any wave**: brief fit +0.53, situation fit +0.74, both up on complete and on sparse
  briefs.
- **Decision value rose more than in wave 4** (+0.81, against +0.38). The old pages here were thinner, and wave 4's
  setting checklist had the pages cover the settings the briefs used.
- **Accuracy rose by 0.90.**
- **The pairwise signal is mostly Gemini's**: DeepSeek picked the answer shown first in 42 of 60 pairs, Gemini in 29.
  All four judgements agree on 14 of 30 briefs. Median answer length was 626 words (OLD) and 665 (NEW).

**Where the new page lost or tied:**
- **Self-monitoring lost both briefs 1–3.**
  - *Complete brief* (pharmacy technicians): it asked for a pause in which learners name what they know, what they
    are unsure of and their next move. The new page's rule that "checks are tests, not feelings" led answers to
    replace that routine. Graders preferred the old answer, which used it.
  - *Sparse brief* (community-garden volunteers): graders preferred the old answer's lighter routine.
- **Feedback loops lost its sparse brief 1–3.** For volunteers on busy shifts, graders preferred the old answer's peer
  buddies and wall tally to the new answer's facilitator checks and paperwork.
- **Ties at 2–2**: modeling (both briefs), strengths-based approach (complete), validity and reliability (both briefs).

## Rework

Both losses had the same cause: **no row for scarce facilitator time.** The new designs assumed a facilitator with
time to check every learner; the old answers worked without one. Rows added, each an untested proposal:
- **Self-monitoring, "facilitator time is scarce"**: two fixed pauses with a three-line card, partners check each
  other against the posted criteria, a shared progress board, and the facilitator looks at one pair per pause.
- **Feedback loops, "little facilitator time or equipment"**: buddy pairs against a posted two-or-three-point
  checklist, a wall tally, and the facilitator rechecks a sample.
- **Self-monitoring, after the first re-test, "the objective names a self-check routine"**: use that routine word for
  word, and attach one quick check to its "what I know" line.

| Brief | Wave 5 | Rework 1 (two answers) | Rework 2 (two answers) |
|---|---|---|---|
| feedback loops, sparse | 1–3 | 2–2, **3–1** | |
| self-monitoring, complete | 1–3 | 1–3, 1–3 | **3–1**, 2–2 |
| self-monitoring, sparse | 1–3 | 1–3, 2–2 | 1–3, 1–3 |

The complete brief is won once the page says to keep the routine the brief names. **The sparse self-monitoring
brief (community-garden volunteers) still loses 1–3**: graders prefer the old answer's two timed pauses and central
progress board to the new answers' partner checks, which they read as more facilitator load. It is left open rather
than tuned further against one brief.

**A page can be right and still lose when it overrides the brief.** The self-monitoring page's caution about
self-judgement is sound: judgments of learning are often inaccurate. But when a brief names the routine it wants, the
page has to say how to run that routine well, not replace it. The new row does that.
