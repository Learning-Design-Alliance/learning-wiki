## What the wave found, open

**No claim tests the page's own relationship on 13 of the 15** (all but adaptive learning, whose two q4
syntheses compare tutoring systems with conventional instruction, and inquiry-based learning, whose two
syntheses rest on the same two entries). Each page says so.

**Wrong-claim links on the old pages**: `principles/adaptive-learning` linked "ITS outperform large-group
instruction" to the expertise-reversal claim, "formative assessment information improves instructional
decisions" to the feedback-levels claim, and "learning-styles matching does not improve outcomes" to the
intuitive-learners claim; `claims/manipulatives-require-connection-to-concept` links
`patterns/anchored-instruction` with the text "Anchored instruction improves learning", as if it were a claim.
The new pages cite the right claims; the old text stays in the deprecated blocks.

**Claim titles that overstate their entries**: `refutational-text-order-enhances-retention` (14-day advantage
only over inverted text), `mastery-improves-engagement-attrition` (attrition from one evaluation, time-on-task
from observation), `practice-presence-raises-cbi-posttest-achievement` ("with feedback"; the entry describes
none), `interdependence-type-no-achievement-effect-asynchronous` (title says adult reentry students, entry says
280 undergraduate business majors; also flagged in wave 1).

**Stale "no evidence" text beside entries**: `personalized-learning-effects-vary-with-fidelity`,
`rubrics-improve-student-work`, `considering-the-opposite-reduces-bias`, `adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes`,
`analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer`, `misconceptions-interfere-with-new-learning`.
