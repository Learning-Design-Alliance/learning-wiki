# Evidence kind and rigour: a pilot rubric

The `q` code ranks every study on one ladder built for causal questions: 4 is a pre-registered
RCT or a strong meta-analysis, 1 is a case study, a qualitative study or an argument. A careful
qualitative study therefore reads as "low quality", although it may be the best available evidence
for the question it answers (how learners experience something, why a design fails in context).
This pilot codes two things instead of one, on a small sample, before anything changes wiki-wide.

## Kind: what sort of evidence the study is

| kind | examples |
|---|---|
| `causal` | randomised or quasi-experiments, single-case designs: a manipulated condition and a comparison |
| `quant-synthesis` | meta-analysis, systematic review with quantitative pooling |
| `review` | narrative or scoping review, book-length synthesis without systematic search |
| `associational` | correlational, survey, longitudinal or observational with statistical controls |
| `qualitative` | interviews, focus groups, ethnography, case study, thematic or discourse analysis |
| `design` | design-based research, development or evaluation of an artefact, practitioner report of a design |
| `theoretical` | an argument, model or framework, with or without cited support |

## Rigour: how well it does what its kind can do, 1–3

Judged against the kind's own standard, never against another kind's.

- **causal** — 3: randomised, adequately powered, pre-registered or with low risk of bias (attrition,
  blinding, fidelity reported); 2: randomised with clear risks, or a well-controlled
  quasi-experiment; 1: weak comparison, tiny sample, or confounded assignment.
- **quant-synthesis** — 3: systematic search, bias and heterogeneity assessed, enough studies for
  the pooled estimate; 2: one of those missing; 1: few studies or an unsystematic search.
- **review** — 3: broad, balanced, engages contrary evidence; 2: selective but transparent; 1: advocacy.
- **associational** — 3: large or representative sample, validated measures, relevant controls;
  2: one of those weak; 1: convenience sample, single-item measures, no controls.
- **qualitative** — 3 (CASP-style): clear aims, method suited to them, sampling and data collection
  described, analysis systematic and shown (codes, themes, excerpts), reflexivity or credibility
  checks (member checking, triangulation, multiple coders), findings grounded in the data;
  2: most present; 1: thin description, no analysis shown.
- **design** — 3: iterative cycles with evidence gathered at each, outcomes measured; 2: one cycle
  with some evidence; 1: description of a design with no evaluation.
- **theoretical** — 3: coherent, explicit mechanisms, engages evidence and alternatives; 2: coherent
  but thin on evidence; 1: assertion.

## What the pilot asks

1. Do the two codes change what a reader would conclude, compared with `q` alone?
2. Can a model code them consistently (two independent runs)?
3. What would it cost across the wiki, and what would the extraction prompt, validator and banners need?
