## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kaplan-Rakowski 2026

Kaplan-Rakowski, R., Norton, S., & Wojdynski, T. (2026). The impact of high-immersion virtual reality on anatomy learning, enjoyment, presence, and cognitive load. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10676-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANCOVA in the same randomized experiment (N = 91) using eight adapted items from Leppink et al.'s cognitive load tool (alpha .82). The non-significant difference is reported with F (1,89) = .20, p = .65; equivalence was not tested.

> "the mean of the group using VR (M = 5.26, SD = 1.63) was slightly lower compared with the group using video (M = 5.30, SD = 1.39). However, that difference in mean values was not statistically significant (F (1,89) = .20, p = .65)."

### Kaplan-Rakowski 2026 (2)

Kaplan-Rakowski, R., Norton, S., & Wojdynski, T. (2026). The impact of high-immersion virtual reality on anatomy learning, enjoyment, presence, and cognitive load. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10676-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analyses in the same experiment examined the three load subscales separately; descriptively the VR group reported slightly higher intrinsic and germane load and marginally lower extraneous load, all non-significant.

> "Additional analyses examining intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load separately mirrored the insignificant differences observed between groups for the global cognitive load measure."
