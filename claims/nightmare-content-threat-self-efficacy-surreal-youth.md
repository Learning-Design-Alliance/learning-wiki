## Evidence

### Harb Gerlinde C. 2020

Harb Gerlinde C., Schultz Jon-Håkon. (2020). The nature of posttraumatic nightmares and school functioning in war-affected youth. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0242414. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242414

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Content coding of 58 nightmare scripts (91% of the 64 students) by two doctoral-level psychologists using the Harb et al. (2012) coding instrument. The study reports "the dreamer’s life under threat (90%)" and an average threat degree of 4.1 (SD = .85), with 69% dreaming of immediate or catastrophic threat.

> "Most nightmares featured the dreamer’s life under threat (90%), many were set in the midst of violence or war (46.6% and 15.5%, respectively) and/or included gruesome scenes of death (45%)"

### Harb Gerlinde C. 2020 (2)

Harb Gerlinde C., Schultz Jon-Håkon. (2020). The nature of posttraumatic nightmares and school functioning in war-affected youth. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0242414. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242414

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic coding of the 58 nightmare scripts, with one primary and an average of three secondary themes per nightmare (Table 3). Rated as primary or secondary, 59 to 78% of nightmares showed themes of lack of control, lack of self-efficacy, and fear of death.

> "The most common primary themes for nightmares were being under attack (22%) and experiencing a personal sense of lack of self-efficacy (21%), followed by a feeling of lack of control over external events (16%) and being chased (14%)."

### Harb Gerlinde C. 2020 (3)

Harb Gerlinde C., Schultz Jon-Håkon. (2020). The nature of posttraumatic nightmares and school functioning in war-affected youth. PLoS ONE 15(11): e0242414. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0242414

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Content coding of nightmare scripts (Table 1). The authors contrast this with the less surreal nightmare content of US combat veterans with posttraumatic nightmares, and note fear was the most frequent primary emotion (77% reporting anxiety, fear, or horror).

> "Approximately half (48%) of the nightmares contained surreal or unreal dream elements, such as skeletons, monsters, ghosts or talking animals."
