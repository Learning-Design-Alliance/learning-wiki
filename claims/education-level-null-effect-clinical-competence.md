# Educational level shows no significant effect on total clinical competence except a higher ensuring-quality score among master's-degree nurses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There were no significant differences between bachelor's and master's degree nurses in total clinical competence or most dimensions, except that the ensuring quality dimension was significantly greater among master's degree nurses (P<0.001). [→ Babaei Keivan 2023](#babaei-keivan-2023)
`q2 i?` Gender had no significant effect on total clinical competence, though female nurses scored significantly higher on the helping role, teaching-coaching, and diagnostic functions dimensions. [→ Babaei Keivan 2023 (2)](#babaei-keivan-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Babaei Keivan 2023

Babaei Keivan, Sadeghian Efat, Khodaveisi Masoud. (2023). The Predictors of Clinical Competence among Hospital Nurses: A Cross-Sectional Study in Hamadan, West Iran. Ethiopian Journal of Health Sciences. https://doi.org/10.4314/ejhs.v33i1.5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional comparison by educational level among 270 nurses; Table 1 shows ensuring quality means of 22.22±6.6 (bachelor's) versus 33.45±5 (master's), P<0.001, with total CC P=0.767. No effect size printed.

> "there were no significant differences between bachelor's and master's degree nurses respecting the mean scores of CC and its dimensions (P>0.05), except for the mean score of the ensuring quality dimension which was significantly greater among master's degree nurses (P<0.001)"

### Babaei Keivan 2023 (2)

Babaei Keivan, Sadeghian Efat, Khodaveisi Masoud. (2023). The Predictors of Clinical Competence among Hospital Nurses: A Cross-Sectional Study in Hamadan, West Iran. Ethiopian Journal of Health Sciences. https://doi.org/10.4314/ejhs.v33i1.5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional gender comparison among the 270 nurses (63% female); total CC showed no significant difference (P=0.099) while three dimensions were significant. No effect size printed.

> "The mean scores of the helping role, teaching-coaching, and diagnostic functions dimensions of CC among female participants were significantly more than male participants (P<0.05), while there were no significant differences between male and female participants respecting the mean scores of total CC and its other dimensions (P>0.05)."
