# Neighborhood deprivation is no longer a statistically significant predictor of self-reported grades once cognitive ability is included in the models, and models including cognition fit better

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When composite cognition is factored in, ADI is no longer a significant predictor of grades (OR 0.94, p = 0.12 at 2-year; OR 0.93, p = 0.05 at 3-year follow-up), while parental education and income-to-needs ratio remain significant. [→ Langensee 2024](#langensee-2024)
`q3 i?` Pairwise model comparisons favor the models including cognitive ability (p < 0.001 at both timepoints). [→ Langensee 2024 (2)](#langensee-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Langensee 2024

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cognition-inclusive ordinal regression models on the ABCD sample (N = 5001). Tables 4–5 print ADI OR 0.94 (0.88, 1.02), p = 0.12 at 2-year and OR 0.93 (0.87, 1.00), p = 0.05 at 3-year follow-up; ITN remained significant at OR 1.27 at both timepoints.

> "Neighborhood deprivation (ADI) on the other hand was no longer a signiﬁcant predictor of grades when cognitive ability was factored into the models."

### Langensee 2024 (2)

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model-comparison result reported in the Results section: "Pairwise comparisons of the models for each of two timepoints indicated superior model fit when cognitive abilities were included in the models," with p < 0.001 in both cases as printed in the text.
