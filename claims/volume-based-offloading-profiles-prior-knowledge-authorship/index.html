Evidence¶

Poquet 2026¶

Poquet, O., Nanduri, M. S., Salinas Loyer, M. X., Stadler, M., Sailer, M., Jovanovic, J. (2026). Profiling Cognitive Offloading in LLM-mediated Synthesis Writing: Volume vs. Content. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10434 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.10434

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Exploratory k-means clustering of 97 university students' prompt counts plus prior knowledge test scores yielded four profiles (High Prior Knowledge-Minimal Offloading n=51; High Prior Knowledge-Moderate n=17; Low Prior Knowledge-Low n=22; Heavy Offloading n=7). The clusters "significantly differed both in prior knowledge" and volume of LLM use.

"The clusters significantly differed both in prior knowledge of the learners whose offloading processes they represented, as well as their volume of LLM use."

Poquet 2026 (2)¶

Poquet, O., Nanduri, M. S., Salinas Loyer, M. X., Stadler, M., Sailer, M., Jovanovic, J. (2026). Profiling Cognitive Offloading in LLM-mediated Synthesis Writing: Volume vs. Content. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10434. doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.10434

q2 · i? · associational · r1

In the same volume-based clustering analysis, essay authorship differed significantly across profiles (Table 3: 80%, 81%, 30%, 2%). The article reports that "The more LLM was used, the lower was student authorship score", indicating fewer instances of own knowledge construction.

"The volume of offloading related to the essay authorship. The more LLM was used, the lower was student authorship score."
