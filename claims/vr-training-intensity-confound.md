## Evidence

### Han Shaoyu 2026

Han Shaoyu, Tian Zhen, Cui Bingjun, Wu Lang, Chen Zhixiang. (2026). Virtual reality-based training improves thoracentesis skills in medical interns: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09801-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

This is the authors' interpretation in the Discussion of their RCT design, not a tested result. They note the confound cuts both ways, since VR enables resource-efficient, self-paced learning without such constraints.

> "It is important to note that the VR group practiced independently with unlimited repetitions, while the control group practiced in groups of five, sharing one mannequin. This difference in training intensity—rather than VR per se—may have contributed to the observed differences."
