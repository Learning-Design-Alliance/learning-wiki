# In the higher track, reading enjoyment and sex show main effects on non-leisure readers' school achievement, with enjoyers of both sexes scoring higher

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among higher-track non-leisure readers, main effects emerged for sex (η2 = 0.03) and reading enjoyment (η2 = 0.01); girl enjoyers scored 0.22 and boy enjoyers 0.34 grade points higher. [→ Mol SE and Jolles J 2014](#mol-se-and-jolles-j-2014)

## Evidence

### Mol SE and Jolles J 2014

Mol SE and Jolles J (2014) Reading enjoyment amongst non-leisure readers can affect achievement in secondary school. Front. Psychol. 5:1214. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01214

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

GLM Univariate ANCOVA among 400 higher-track non-leisure readers. The article reports girl enjoyers scored 0.22 and boy enjoyers 0.34 grade points higher than non-enjoyers, with main effects for sex and reading enjoyment.

> "Main effects were found for sex [F(1,393) = 13.40, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.03] and reading enjoyment [F(1,393) = 4.92, p = 0.027, η2 = 0.01] and the covariate classroom type [F(1,393) = 8.75, p = 0.003, η2 = 0.02], but not for mental imagery [F(1,393) = 0.07, p = 0.791, η2 = 0.00]."
