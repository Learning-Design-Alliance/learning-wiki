# Two-thirds of the analysed textbooks explain electromagnetic wave propagation via a self-sustaining mechanism in which time-varying fields generate each other

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only one third of the textbooks explain wave propagation without establishing causal relationships between the electric and magnetic fields (criterion 2.2); two thirds present mutual generation. [→ Álvaro Suárez 2023](#alvaro-suarez-2023)

## Evidence

### Álvaro Suárez 2023

Álvaro Suárez, Arturo C. Martí, Kristina Zuza and Jenaro Guisasola. (2023). Presentation of the electromagnetic field in introductory physics textbooks and consequences for its teaching. arXiv:2305.05431. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020113)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative textbook analysis (Criterion 2.2, Cohen's kappa 0.88 for criterion 2) of the 12 textbooks. Eight texts were rated Does not comply, describing a wave that generates itself through fields inducing each other; four comply, presenting the fields as simultaneous and correlated without causation.

> "two thirds of the textbooks analysed consider that the propagation of electromagnetic waves lies in a supposedly self-sustaining mechanism, originated by a time-varying electric ﬁeld that is supposed to generate a time-varying magnetic ﬁeld"
