# RAP strategy instruction combined with a multicomponent motivational system produced significant reading comprehension gains with very large effect sizes in both peer-tutoring groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2018 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Tutees in group 1 showed significant improvement from t2 to t3 (d = 1.99) and from t1 to t3 (d = 2.17) when RAP instruction was combined with scoring, visual feedback, and attribution-based verbal praise. [→ Leidig 2018](#leidig-2018)
`q2 i3` Tutees in group 2, who received RAP instruction only during the four-week motivational phase, also showed significant improvement from t2 to t3 (d = 1.08) and from t1 to t3 (d = 1.21). [→ Leidig 2018 (2)](#leidig-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Leidig 2018

Leidig, T., Grünke, M., Urton, K., Knaak, T., & Hisgen, S. (2018). The Effects of the RAP Strategy Used in a Peer-tutoring Setting to Foster Reading Comprehension in High-Risk Fourth Graders. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 16(2), 231-253. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1194573

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-tests on reading comprehension scores of 12 group 1 tutees across three measurement points. The article reports very large Cohen's d effect sizes for group 1 (d=1.99 for t2-t3; d=2.17 for t1-t3), described as "can be considered very large".

> "However, there were significant improvements between phases 2 and 3 (RAP strategy instruction plus a motivational system) in both treatment conditions. The same is true for comparisons between t1 and t3."

### Leidig 2018 (2)

Leidig, T., Grünke, M., Urton, K., Knaak, T., & Hisgen, S. (2018). The Effects of the RAP Strategy Used in a Peer-tutoring Setting to Foster Reading Comprehension in High-Risk Fourth Graders. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 16(2), 231-253. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1194573

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired t-tests for group 2 (n=10 tutees) across the same measurement points. The article reports significant improvements with large effect sizes (d=1.08 for t2-t3; d=1.21 for t1-t3), though smaller than group 1's.

> "In both cases, group 1 produced considerably larger effect sizes (d=1.99 and 2.17) than group 2 (d=1.08 and 1.21)."
