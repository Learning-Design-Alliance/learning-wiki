# In Study 1, the revised intervention reduced hypothetical choice of the easy math assignment from 60% to 51% relative to the original (d=.19)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i0` negligible

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` The revised intervention reduced the proportion choosing the easy extra-credit assignment versus the hard one from 60% to 51% compared with the original intervention (d=.19). [→ Yeager 2016](#yeager-2016)

## Evidence

### Yeager 2016

Yeager, D. S., Romero, C., Paunesku, D., Hulleman, C. S., Schneider, B., Hinojosa, C., Lee, H. Y., O'Brien, J., Flint, K., Roberts, A., Trott, J., Greene, D., Walton, G. M., & Dweck, C. S. (2016). Using Design Thinking to Improve Psychological Interventions: The Case of the Growth Mindset During the Transition to High School. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 108*(3), 374–391. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000098

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis in the same Study 1 sample used a dichotomous hypothetical extra-credit choice based on Mueller and Dweck (1998). The revised condition showed "from 60% to 51%" choosing the easy assignment, logistic regression Z=7.951, d=.19 — a small printed effect size.

> "Compared to the original mindset intervention, the revised mindset intervention reduced the proportion of students saying they would choose the “easy” math homework assignment versus the “hard” assignment from 60% to 51%, logistic regression Z=7.951, p<.001, d=.19."
