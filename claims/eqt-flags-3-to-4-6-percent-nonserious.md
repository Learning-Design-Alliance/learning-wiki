## Evidence

### David P. Waters 2019

David P. Waters, Dragos Amarie, Rebecca A. Booth, Christopher Conover and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Investigating students seriousness during selected conceptual inventory surveys. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In the PhysPort actual populations, 'the EQT identified between 3% and 4.6% of students as non-serious for each RBAI,' where non-serious meant answering all four easiest questions (chosen by highest proportion correct) incorrectly.

> "In the actual populations, on the other hand, the UAT identified slightly more than 2% of students as non-serious for each RBAI, and the EQT identified between 3% and 4.6% of students as non-serious for each RBAI."

### David P. Waters 2019 (2)

David P. Waters, Dragos Amarie, Rebecca A. Booth, Christopher Conover and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2019). Investigating students seriousness during selected conceptual inventory surveys. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020118)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Among the simulated random guessers, '41%, 41%, and 51% of the respective sim- ulated populations for the FCI, CSEM, and BEMA were identiﬁed by the EQT as non-serious (green circle),' while most actual test takers answered at least 2 easy questions correctly.

> "41%, 41%, and 51% of the respective sim- ulated populations for the FCI, CSEM, and BEMA were identiﬁed by the EQT as non-serious (green circle)."
