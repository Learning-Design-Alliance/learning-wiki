## Evidence

### García Carpintero-Blas E 2025

García Carpintero-Blas E, Del Pozo-Herce P, Hernández-Cediel MDC, Rodríguez-García M, Navas-Echazarreta N, Chover-Sierra E, Martínez-Sabater A, Juárez-Vela R, Tovar-Reinoso A. (2025). First Contact With Care Through Clinical Simulation in Nursing Students: Qualitative Study. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/81617

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 3 (practical self-confidence) of the qualitative analysis. Participants anticipated future clinical experiences, reducing anxiety; one said "I feel more confident because I have already practiced these situations."

> "The students expressed that, thanks to the simulation, they felt more prepared and less insecure in real situations."

### García Carpintero-Blas E 2025 (2)

García Carpintero-Blas E, Del Pozo-Herce P, Hernández-Cediel MDC, Rodríguez-García M, Navas-Echazarreta N, Chover-Sierra E, Martínez-Sabater A, Juárez-Vela R, Tovar-Reinoso A. (2025). First Contact With Care Through Clinical Simulation in Nursing Students: Qualitative Study. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/81617

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 3 (professional identity) of the qualitative analysis. Students reported strengthened vocation, e.g. "It reaffirms that I have chosen the career I like," and perceived anticipated professionalization.

> "The simulation not only facilitated technical learning but also actively contributed to the development of professional identity by allowing students to project themselves as nurses in realistic clinical contexts."
