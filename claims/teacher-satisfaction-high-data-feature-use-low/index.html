Evidence¶

Brasiel 2016¶

Brasiel, S., Jeong, S., Ames, C., Lawanto, K., Yuan, M., & Martin, T. (2016). Effects of educational technology on mathematics achievement for K-12 students in Utah. Journal of Online Learning Research, 2(3), 205-226. https://www.learntechlib.org/j/JOLR/

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Open-coded survey responses from 2,933 teachers. Table 9 shows 57% satisfied with the provided technology, 11% satisfied with adaptive individualized instruction, and 10% satisfied with student engagement while using the product.

"Overall, 57% of the teachers reported overall general satisfaction (see Table 9). Many (11%) were satisfied with the way the product individualized instruction for the students."

Brasiel 2016 (2)¶

Brasiel, S., Jeong, S., Ames, C., Lawanto, K., Yuan, M., & Martin, T. (2016). Effects of educational technology on mathematics achievement for K-12 students in Utah. Journal of Online Learning Research, 2(3), 205-226. https://www.learntechlib.org/j/JOLR/

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Same teacher survey analysis of performance-management feature use. Table 12 shows 34% used the features to monitor progress and 20% did not use them; the authors call the data collection and reporting features one of the primary strengths of these software programs.

"The most common re - sponse was teachers reporting use of these features to monitor student prog - ress (34%)."
