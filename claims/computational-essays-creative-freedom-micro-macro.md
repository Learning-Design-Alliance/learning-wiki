# Students in a pilot computational-essay project reported that open-ended programming offered creative freedom at both a micro level (varied approaches to sub-tasks) and a macro level (choice of topics and projects)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviewed students distinguished micro-level creative freedom (multiple ways to accomplish similar sub-tasks) from macro-level freedom (pursuing a wide variety of topics and projects). [→ Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero 2020](#tor-ole-b-odden-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020)
`q2 i?` Across all 11 completed projects, no two groups chose exactly the same topic and approach, supporting the claim that the design elicited original solutions. [→ Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)](#tor-ole-b-odden-and-marcos-d-caballero-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). Computational Essays: An Avenue for Scientific Creativity in Physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.odden](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.odden)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of about 10 hours of interviews with 15 of 17 pilot participants in an introductory E&M course. Students reported that "programming allows for multiple avenues to accomplish similar tasks" and enabled them to "make your own problem."

> "students distinguished between two speciﬁc types of creative freedom: freedom on the micro-level, in that programming allows for variation in how individual students accomplish similar sub-tasks, and freedom on the macro-level"

### Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)

Tor Ole B. Odden and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). Computational Essays: An Avenue for Scientific Creativity in Physics. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/perc.2019.pr.odden](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2019.pr.odden)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Essay analysis in the pilot: of two groups investigating cyclotrons, one added special relativity to the provided simulation while the other re-wrote it to study relativity effects on the LHC, showing divergence even on shared topics.

> "across all 11 projects, no two groups chose exactly the same topic and approach"
