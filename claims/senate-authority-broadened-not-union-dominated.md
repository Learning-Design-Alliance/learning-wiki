# Faculty senate authority broadened under collective bargaining without the senate becoming union-dominated

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` At Rutgers, neither the predicted narrowing of senate authority nor union domination of the senate occurred; the senate's range of authority broadened and it remained an independent voice. [→ Bloustein 1973](#bloustein-1973)

## Evidence

### Bloustein 1973

Bloustein, Edward J. (1973). Collective Bargaining and University Governance. Speech presented at the annual meeting of the Association of American Colleges. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED074928

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

A single-institution observation from the author's year at Rutgers with an AAUP bargaining unit: the senate's powers increased and the AAUP "has not dominated the Senate", though he notes occasional concern about some AAUP leaders' senate roles.

> "our University Senate's range of authority has broadened, and this has happened without its having become dominated by the AAUP."
