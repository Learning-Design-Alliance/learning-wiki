## Evidence

### Joanne R. Beames 2023

Joanne R. Beames, Samantha Spanos, Anna Roberts, Lauren McGillivray, Sophie Li, Jill M. Newby, Bridianne O'Dea, Aliza Werner-Seidler. (2023). Intervention Programs Targeting the Mental Health, Professional Burnout, and/or Wellbeing of School Teachers: Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09720-w

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 23 nRCTs of teacher programs versus controls at post-intervention. Effects were "significant and of a moderate size for stress (g=0.50)"; depression, burnout, affect, and resilience were non-significant.

> "Controlled effects at post-intervention were significant and of a moderate size for stress (g=0.50), and of a small size for anxiety ( g=0.38) and wellbeing ( g=0.38). Effects were non-significant for depression, somatisation, professional burnout, positive and negative affect, and resilience."

### Joanne R. Beames 2023 (2)

Joanne R. Beames, Samantha Spanos, Anna Roberts, Lauren McGillivray, Sophie Li, Jill M. Newby, Bridianne O'Dea, Aliza Werner-Seidler. (2023). Intervention Programs Targeting the Mental Health, Professional Burnout, and/or Wellbeing of School Teachers: Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09720-w

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Short-term (1-6 month) follow-up meta-analysis of the nRCTs found no statistically significant controlled effects on any of the five primary outcomes; equivalence was not tested.

> "Controlled effects at short-term follow-up were non-significant for anxiety, depression, stress, professional burnout, and well-being."
