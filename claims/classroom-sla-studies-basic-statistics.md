# Classroom-based SLA studies rely more on interpretive analysis and basic statistics than non-classroom studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Classroom studies tend to be more interpretative and make more limited use of statistics than non-classroom studies, and most surveyed studies used only basic statistical tools. [→ Sadtono 1991](#sadtono-1991)

## Evidence

### Sadtono 1991

Sadtono, Eugenius, Ed. (1991). Language Acquisition and the Second/Foreign Language Classroom. Anthology Series 28. SEAMEO Regional Language Centre. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED367161

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Analysis of the statistical techniques used across the surveyed studies, applying Teleni and Baldauf's classification of basic, intermediate and advanced techniques. The survey reports "the great majority of studies (29 out of 39) employ basic statistical tools", and notes classroom studies were more interpretative than non-classroom ones.

> "Applying their scheme to the studies analysed here, we see that the great majority of studies (29 out of 39) employ basic statistical tools."
