## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Emanuela Carleschi 2021

Emanuela Carleschi, Anna Chrysostomou, Alan S. Cornell, Wade Naylor. (2021). Probing the eﬀect on student conceptual understanding due to a forced mid-semester transition to online teaching. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2101.09908](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2101.09908)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Appendix A statistical analyses of gender means for the paired group (16 female, 32 male): t-test p = 0.02757, two-way ANOVA F = 4.5107, p = 0.03909, and Wilcoxon W = 341, p = 0.03223, all below α < 0.05. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "We found that the mean of the gains for female participants ( µFG = 0.38) was greater than the mean for male participants (µMG = 0.17)."

### Emanuela Carleschi 2021 (2)

Emanuela Carleschi, Anna Chrysostomou, Alan S. Cornell, Wade Naylor. (2021). Probing the eﬀect on student conceptual understanding due to a forced mid-semester transition to online teaching. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2101.09908](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2101.09908)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Table 3 paired results: female pre-test mean 31.0 (SD 12.3) vs male 37.0 (SD 19.4); post-test female 56.9 (SD 25.2) vs male 47.6 (SD 20.5). The authors note the same crossover pattern was found by Alinea & Naylor.

> "the table shows that although male participants did better in the pre-test, female participants had a higher average in the post-test."
