# MITCA homework prescription improved environmental management relative to traditional homework in primary students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` At post-test, the MITCA group scored significantly higher than the control group on environment management (d = 0.22), the largest between-group difference reported. [→ Tania Vieites 2024](#tania-vieites-2024)
`q3 i?` Within the MITCA group, environmental management was the only strategy showing a significant pre-to-post increase. [→ Tania Vieites 2024 (2)](#tania-vieites-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Tania Vieites 2024

Tania Vieites, Eleftheria Gonida, Fátima Díaz-Freire, Susana Rodríguez, Antonio Valle. (2024). Effects of a homework implementation method (MITCA) on self-regulation of learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09376-z

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Post-test between-group t-tests in the twelve-week MITCA intervention study with 533 students. The article reports "environment management (t = 3.243, p < 0.01, d = 0.22)" in favor of the experimental group.

> "significant differences between the two groups in the post-test were found in behavioral and time management (t = 2.245, p < 0.05, d = 0.15), environment management (t = 3.243, p < 0.01, d = 0.22) and self-regulatory deficit (t = -2.557, p < 0.05, d = 0.17) in favor of the experimental group"

### Tania Vieites 2024 (2)

Tania Vieites, Eleftheria Gonida, Fátima Díaz-Freire, Susana Rodríguez, Antonio Valle. (2024). Effects of a homework implementation method (MITCA) on self-regulation of learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09376-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post paired comparisons within the MITCA experimental group, reported alongside Fig. 3. The article states that "significant differences have only been found for environmental management (t = -2.77, p < 0.001)"; no effect size is printed for this within-group change.

> "Although, as can be seen in Fig. 3, the means are higher for information seeking and time management, and are lower for self-reglation defictis for the experimental group, in which the MITCA method was implemented, significant differences have only been found for environmental management (t = -2.77, p < 0.001)."
