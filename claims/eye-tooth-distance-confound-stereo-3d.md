## Evidence

### Kaluschke Maximilian 2023

Kaluschke Maximilian, Yin Myat Su, Haddawy Peter, Suebnukarn Siriwan, Zachmann Gabriel. (2023). The effect of 3D stereopsis and hand-tool alignment on learning effectiveness and skill transfer of a VR-based simulator for dental training. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291389

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking log analysis across the study's 240 trials, comparing mean eye-tooth distance between 3D rendering conditions using the HMD's eye sensors and a cyclops-eye gaze-ray test against the tooth.

> "The monoscopic condition had a significantly lower mean eye-tooth distance (M = 19.83, SD = 8.19) compared to the stereoscopic condition (M = 25.68, SD = 6.82) (t(207.52) = −5.71, p < 0.001)."

### Kaluschke Maximilian 2023 (2)

Kaluschke Maximilian, Yin Myat Su, Haddawy Peter, Suebnukarn Siriwan, Zachmann Gabriel. (2023). The effect of 3D stereopsis and hand-tool alignment on learning effectiveness and skill transfer of a VR-based simulator for dental training. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291389

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis of mean eye-tooth distance per trial against real-world learning gains; the article interprets the accommodation near-point limitation of stereoscopic rendering combined with limited HMD resolution as a possible cause of the counter-intuitive stereo learning effect.

> "Over all data points, there was a weak but statistically significant positive correlation between mean eye-tooth distance and learning gain (R = 0.15, p < 0.05)."
