# Tutors with Tutor CoPilot access use more understanding-fostering strategies (e.g., prompting students to explain) and rely less on giving away answers than control tutors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Treatment tutors used strategies fostering student skills and comprehension significantly more; prompting the student to explain and asking questions to guide thinking appeared roughly 2 standard deviations more frequently on a log-odds scale. [→ Rose E. Wang 2025](#rose-e-wang-2025)
`q3 i?` Control tutors tended to rely on strategies that directly lead students to the solution or provide passive support, such as giving away the answer or providing the solution strategy. [→ Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)](#rose-e-wang-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rose E. Wang 2025

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Classifier-based analysis of 550,000+ chat messages from the RCT sessions: fine-tuned RoBERTa classifiers (F1-scores 0.65–0.90) labeled strategies, and z-scored log-odds ratios compared usage across conditions (Figure 3). The article prints the ~2 standard deviation log-odds difference but no standardized effect size.

> "For example, "prompting the student to explain" and "asking questions to guide thinking" were used approximately 2 standard deviations more frequently on a log-odds scale in treatment sessions compared to control sessions."

### Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Same message-classifier comparison across treatment and control sessions (Figure 3). The article characterizes control tutors' strategies as solution-focused and passive, which help students complete tasks but do less to develop deeper understanding. No effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "In contrast, control tutors tended to rely on strategies that focus on directly leading students to the solution or providing passive support."
