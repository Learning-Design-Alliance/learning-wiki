## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2015

Trevor I. Smith, Warren M. Christensen, Donald B. Mountcastle, and John R. Thompson. (2015). Identifying Student Diﬃculties with Entropy, Heat Engines, and the Carnot Cycle. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Aggregate written EEQ pretest data from five semesters at School 1 and one at School 2 (N = 70 combined), administered after lecture instruction. The article reports "fewer than 30% of students used correct reasoning" for the Carnot-universe sub-question (19 out of 64) and fewer than 20% for the better-than-Carnot implication; no effect size is printed.

> "Fewer than 30% of students used correct reasoning while answering the EEQ to determine that the entropy of the universe would stay the same after one complete cycle of a Carnot engine (19 out of 64), and fewer than 20% of students (11 out of 64) recognized the implication that a heat engine that was more eﬃcient than a Carnot engine would have to violate the laws of thermodynamics"
