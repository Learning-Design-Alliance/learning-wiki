# Nomophobia level is statistically significantly associated with academic achievement among KNUST students, with absence of nomophobia associated with first-class attainment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A chi-square test of independence showed a statistically significant association between nomophobia levels and academic achievement categories. [→ Essel Harry Barton 2021](#essel-harry-barton-2021)
`q2 i?` The absence of nomophobia is associated with better academic achievement, driven by first-class students with absent nomophobia (adjusted standardized residual 3.2, 58%). [→ Essel Harry Barton 2021 (2)](#essel-harry-barton-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Essel Harry Barton 2021

Essel Harry Barton, Vlachopoulos Dimitrios, Tachie-Menson Akosua. (2021). The relationship between the nomophobic levels of higher education students in Ghana and their academic achievement. PLoS ONE 16(6): e0252880. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0252880

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square test of independence relating the four nomophobia levels to the four CWA achievement divisions in the sample of 670 students; the association was "statistically significant, X2(9, N = 670) = 21.9, p = 0.009" at alpha .05.

> "The results indicated that the association between the variables is statistically significant, X2(9, N = 670) = 21.9, p = 0.009."

### Essel Harry Barton 2021 (2)

Essel Harry Barton, Vlachopoulos Dimitrios, Tachie-Menson Akosua. (2021). The relationship between the nomophobic levels of higher education students in Ghana and their academic achievement. PLoS ONE 16(6): e0252880. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0252880

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post-hoc adjusted standardized residual analysis of the chi-square table, tested against a Bonferroni adjusted alpha of p < 0.003; the residual of 3.2 (58%) "showing that the absence of nomophobia is associated with better academic achievement".

> "The adjusted standardized residual of 3.2 (58%) for first class achievement of students with absence of nomophobia contributed to the significant relationship, showing that the absence of nomophobia is associated with better academic achievement."
