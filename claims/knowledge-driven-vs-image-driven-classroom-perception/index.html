Evidence¶

Charlotte E. Wolff 2016¶

Charlotte E. Wolff, Halszka Jarodzka, Niek van den Bogert, Henny P. A. Boshuizen. (2016). Teacher vision: expert and novice teachers' perception of problematic classroom management scenes. Instr Sci 44:243-265. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9367-z

q1 · i? · associational · r1

Authors' interpretive claim (type e) framing their exploratory findings: experts' event-structured knowledge drives monitoring of interactions, while novices' monitoring is guided by location of disruptions and salient features such as bright colours. The eye-tracking results are consistent with this but the interpretation itself is not directly tested.

"In brief, we assert that the classroom perception of expert teachers’ is more likely to be knowledge-driven, whereas novices’ search is likely to be more image-driven."

  • Novice teachers notice more negative classroom events than positive ones, whereas expert teachers show the opposite pattern when reflecting on their own MET videos — related
  • Only a minority of teacher educators interpreted a critical classroom-management incident as theoretically expected, despite marked eye-tracking differences — related
  • Experts detect and correct small changes before events become full-blown, while novices are distracted by possibly irrelevant phenomena — related
