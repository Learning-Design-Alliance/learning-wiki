Smith-Woolley 2018¶

Smith-Woolley, E., Pingault, J.-B., Selzam, S., Rimfeld, K., Krapohl, E., von Stumm, S., Asbury, K., Dale, P. S., Young, T., Allen, R., Kovas, Y. & Plomin, R. (2018). Differences in exam performance between pupils attending selective and non-selective schools mirror the genetic differences between them. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0019-8

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Correlational analysis of EduYears GPS with selection factors and GCSE in the TEDS sample, reported in Supplementary Table S3. Only variance-explained percentages are printed, with no standardised effect size; the 7.6% GCSE figure is described as 'similar to previous analysis of these data'.

"EduYears GPS was positively correlated with each of the selection factors (see Supplementary Table S3), explaining 2.1% of the variance in ability, 5.2% in achievement and 6.6% in family SES. EduYears GPS was also positively correlated with GCSE, explaining 7.6% of the variance in GCSE scores"

Related Claims¶

  • After controlling for selection factors and EduYears GPS, genetic differences between school types disappear and school type explains less than 1% of GCSE variance (down from 7%) — related
  • Students in state non-selective schools have lower EduYears polygenic scores than students in grammar (d = 0.41) and private (d = 0.37) schools, with no significant difference between grammar and private schools — related
  • EduYears polygenic scores predict achievement increasingly with age (about 4% at KS1 to 10% at GCSE) and show largely stable effects, with less than 1% age-specific prediction — related
  • EduYears GPS predicts more variance in GCSE scores (about 10%) than in its GWA target trait, adult years of education (about 4%) — related
