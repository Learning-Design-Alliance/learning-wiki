# Internal medicine physicians and senior physicians had significantly greater overall transfusion medicine knowledge than surgeons and residents, respectively

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Internal medicine physicians scored higher than surgeons on overall knowledge (55 vs. 42, P = 0.02), and senior physicians scored higher than residents (54 vs. 43, P = 0.01). [→ Rahav Koren Roni 2017](#rahav-koren-roni-2017)
`q2 i?` Internal medicine physicians and senior physicians were also more familiar with restrictive blood management (60 vs. 41, P = 0.03; 61 vs. 42, P = 0.03), and multivariate analysis confirmed the overall knowledge differences (P < 0.001 and P = 0.005). [→ Rahav Koren Roni 2017 (2)](#rahav-koren-roni-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Rahav Koren Roni 2017

Rahav Koren Roni, Suriu Celia, Yakir Orly, Akria Luiza, Barhoum Masad, Braester Andrei. (2017). Physicians' lack of knowledge - a possible reason for red blood cell transfusion overuse? Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0173-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariate comparison of questionnaire scores by background group in the 79-physician survey. The article reports mean scores of "55 for internal medicine physicians vs. 42 for surgeons, P = 0.02" and 54 vs. 43 for seniors vs. residents, P = 0.01.

> "Internal medicine and senior physicians had significantly greater overall knowledge than did surgeons and residents, respectively (mean scores: 55 for internal medicine physicians vs. 42 for surgeons, P = 0.02 and 54 for seniors vs. 43 for residents, P = 0.01)."

### Rahav Koren Roni 2017 (2)

Rahav Koren Roni, Suriu Celia, Yakir Orly, Akria Luiza, Barhoum Masad, Braester Andrei. (2017). Physicians' lack of knowledge - a possible reason for red blood cell transfusion overuse? Israel Journal of Health Policy Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13584-017-0173-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate linear regression models controlled for field of specialty, professional status and place of medical school graduation. The specialty and status differences in overall knowledge and restrictive blood management familiarity remained significant (P = 0.001 and P = 0.004 for familiarity).

> "In multivariate analysis, a significant difference in the overall knowledge score was found in favor of internal medicine physicians vs. surgeons (P < 0.001) and senior physicians vs. residents (P = 0.005)."
