# Deep personalization feedback selection yields higher student learning gains than a non-personalized baseline in a dialogue-based ITS

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The deep personalization model led to the highest student learning gains (48.53% all attempts; 60.47% before second attempt) versus the baseline model (39.47%; 37.93%), with the deep-vs-baseline difference significant before second attempt (p=0.03005). [→ Kochmar 2020](#kochmar-2020)

## Evidence

### Kochmar 2020

Kochmar, E., Vu, D.D., Belfer, R., Gupta, V., Serban, I.V., Pineau, J. (2020). Automated Personalized Feedback Improves Learning Gains in an Intelligent Tutoring System. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-52240-7_26](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-52240-7_26)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Platform experiment on Korbit with 796 annotated student–system interactions from 183 students (Jan–Feb 2020), feedback selected uniformly at random among models; learning gain measured as proportion of correct solutions after a personalized hint or explanation, "showing that the deep personalization model leads to the highest student learning gains at 48.53%".

> "the deep personalization model leads to the highest student learning gains at 48.53% followed by the shallow personalization model at 46.51% and the baseline model at 39.47% for all attempts."
