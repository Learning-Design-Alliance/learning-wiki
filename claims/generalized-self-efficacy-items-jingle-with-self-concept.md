# Generalized math self-efficacy items function like self-concept items because they omit an explicit performance criterion

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Generalized math self-efficacy items that do not specify a clear criterion of performing well force students to adopt social or dimensional frames of reference, making them more like self-concept items. [→ Marsh 2019](#marsh-2019)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2019

Marsh, H. W., Pekrun, R., Parker, P. D., Murayama, K., Guo, J., Dicke, T. and Arens, A. K. (2019). The murky distinction between self-concept and self-efficacy: beware of lurking jingle-jangle fallacies. Journal of Educational Psychology, 111 (2). pp. 331-353. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000281

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Conceptual argument from the article's measurement discussion (a theoretical argument, no data in the supplied text). The authors argue such items 'are more like typical self-concept items than self-efficacy items', a jingle fallacy. No effect size is printed.

> "items such as "I am convinced that I can perform well in math homework and on math tests" do not specify a clear criterion of what it means to perform well, and students have to adopt some frame of reference to respond to the item"
