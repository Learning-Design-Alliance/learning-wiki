## Evidence

### Lohre R 2020

Lohre R, Bois AJ, Pollock JW, Lapner P, McIlquham K, Athwal GS, Goel DP. (2020). Effectiveness of Immersive Virtual Reality on Orthopedic Surgical Skills and Knowledge Acquisition Among Senior Surgical Residents: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.31217

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Error analysis of the cadaveric procedure in the randomized trial, covering guide pin insertion, glenoid reaming, and augmented baseplate sizing. The control group's "65% error rate vs 15% error rate" difference was significant (P < .001).

> "Considering these parameters, the control group demonstrated 50% more critical errors in the early procedure than the IVR group (65% error rate vs 15% error rate; difference, 5.5; 95% CI, 3.9-7.0; P < .001)."

### Lohre R 2020 (2)

Lohre R, Bois AJ, Pollock JW, Lapner P, McIlquham K, Athwal GS, Goel DP. (2020). Effectiveness of Immersive Virtual Reality on Orthopedic Surgical Skills and Knowledge Acquisition Among Senior Surgical Residents: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.31217

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Step-level error counts from the same cadaveric assessment: 6 of 9 controls versus 0 IVR residents erred on all 4 first guide-pin steps; all 9 controls versus 2 IVR residents erred on the first 2 reaming steps.

> "Overall, 6 residents (67%) in the control group erroneously performed all 4 of the first key steps in guide pin placement compared with 0 in the IVR group. All control group participants (9 [100%]) erroneously performed the first 2 steps in bone reaming compared with 2 (22%) in the IVR group."
