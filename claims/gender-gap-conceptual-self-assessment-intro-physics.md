# In introductory physics courses, women rate their conceptual problem-solving skills lower than men, with statistically significant gender differences in both pre- and post-surveys

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Women rate their conceptual problem-solving skills lower than men in introductory physics, a statistically significant difference in pre- and post-surveys for all introductory courses (e.g., d = −0.45 in algebra-based Physics 1 pre-survey). [→ Ghimire 2025](#ghimire-2025)

## Evidence

### Ghimire 2025

Ghimire, A. and Singh, C. (2025). Perspectives of Women and Men Students and Faculty on Conceptual and Quantitative Problem-Solving in Physics from Introductory to Graduate Levels. [doi:10.3390/educsci15121602](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci15121602)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Post-survey descriptive results from large introductory algebra-based Physics 1 courses show a gender gap in self-rated conceptual skills: "43% of women and 58% of men rated their conceptual skills as good or excellent". Table 6 reports Cohen's d = −0.45 (pre) and −0.42 (post), both p < 0.001.

> "in algebra-based Physics 1, 43% of women and 58% of men rated their conceptual skills as good or excellent, while 60% of women and 69% of men rated their quantitative skills, similarly, showing women generally rate themselves lower."
