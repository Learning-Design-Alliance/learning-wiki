## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In a case study of the NEXUS/Physics course, students produced high-quality physical reasoning that challenged an unexamined toy model (the infinite charged sheet), revealing tension between teaching simplified models and teaching sense-making. [→ Redish 2014](#redish-2014)

## Evidence

### Redish 2014

Redish, E. F., Sawtelle, V., & Turpen, C. (2014). The role physics can play in a multi-disciplinary curriculum for non-physics scientists and engineers. Proceedings of the Frontiers in Mathematics and Science Education Research Conference, Famagusta, North Cyprus. [doi:10.30935/scimath/9621](https://doi.org/10.30935/scimath/9621)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Case-study analysis of classroom clicker episodes in the first-year NEXUS/Physics course on electric fields near a charged sheet. Of 19 students present, 16 chose the expected field graph, but individual students raised physically correct objections about individual charges and symmetry that the toy model suppressed. The authors conclude that "life science majors are often uncomfortable with toy models" whose value is not made clear.

> "Thanks to the epistemological stances they have been exposed to (especially B1 and B5) life science majors are often uncomfortable with toy models in which the structure of the system is dramatically changed and the value of understanding the simplified system is not made clear."
