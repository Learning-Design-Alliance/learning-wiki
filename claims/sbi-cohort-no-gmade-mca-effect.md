# Cohort was not a significant predictor of GMADE posttest or MCA-III mathematics posttest scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Multilevel analyses showed no significant cohort effect on general mathematical problem solving (GMADE) or state mathematics achievement (MCA-III); pretest scores were the only significant predictors. [→ Jitendra 2016](#jitendra-2016)

## Evidence

### Jitendra 2016

Jitendra, A. K., Harwell, M. R., Karl, S. R., Dupuis, D. N., Simonson, G., Slater, S. C., & Lein, A. E. (2016). Schema-based instruction: The effects of experienced and novice teacher implementers on seventh-grade students' proportional problem solving. Learning and Instruction, 44, 53-64. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2016.03.001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analyses of the GMADE posttest and MCA-III outcome for 1,845 students nested within 73 teachers. The article reports that for GMADE, the only significant predictors were student race (Black) and pretest; for MCA-III, "the only significant predictor was a student's 6th grade MCA mathematics score." Cohort was not significant for either outcome.

> "Multilevel results for the GMADE posttest show that there were only two significant predictors. One indicated whether a student was Black (-1.05, -.06 SDs) meaning that, with other predictors held constant, Black students tended to score 1.05 points lower on the GMADE posttest than White students."
