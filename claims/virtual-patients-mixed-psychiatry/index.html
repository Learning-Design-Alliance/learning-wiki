Evidence¶

Mokhtari Saba 2026¶

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

q2 · i? · review · r3

Non-randomized controlled study of 14 primary care physicians (7 avatar VP, 7 control) working through four standardized MDD/PTSD scenarios; the review reports the avatar group showed greater diagnostic accuracy than the text-based control group. Rated moderate risk of bias (ROBINS-I).

"The findings indicated that the avatar group demonstrated greater diagnostic accuracy for both MDD and PTSD compared to the control group. Furthermore, participants in the avatar group were more likely to initiate appropriate treatment."

Mokhtari Saba 2026 (2)¶

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

q3 · i0 · review · r3

RCT assigning 67 second-year medical students to interactive VP (n = 34) or video module (n = 33), assessed via standardized patient interviews. The review reports no significant differences; printed effect sizes include "Cramer’s V: 0.03" for communication and 0.01 for rapport.

"The results revealed no significant differences between the two groups across several outcome measures, including suicide risk assessment scores, accuracy on the bipolar symptom checklist, SP communication checklist, and rapport ratings. For example, the p-values were 0.16 for communication (Effect size measured with Cramer’s V: 0.03) and 0.40 for rapport (Effect size measured with Cramer’s V: 0.01)."

Mokhtari Saba 2026 (3)¶

Mokhtari Saba, Mokhtari Asieh, Shariat Seyed Vahid, Nazeri Astaneh Ali, Shalbafan Mohammadreza. (2026). Innovative technologies in psychiatry education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09573-1

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Uncontrolled before-after study (RT-SIM) with 32 psychiatry residents completing pre-test, VP simulation, and post-test; the review reports immediate knowledge gains that "did not persist in the follow-up evaluation". Rated moderate quality (JBI).

"The study found a notable improvement in knowledge scores immediately after the simulation, but this improvement did not persist in the follow-up evaluation. Their confidence in handling culturally sensitive cases also increased significantly across various areas, particularly in trauma-related assessments."

Related Claims¶

  • Study design moderates reported effects: RCTs gave more conservative or mixed results while non-randomized and uncontrolled designs more consistently reported positive effects — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Technology-based interventions in psychiatry education mostly show positive short-term effects on knowledge, skills, confidence, and empathy, but evidence of sustained learning is inconsistent — related
  • Controlled GenAI virtual-patient and communication studies reported short-term skill gains, but positive findings were not universal — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In the few trials comparing them, virtual patients showed no significant knowledge differences versus other digital education, and skills differences in only one of three studies — related
  • One RCT found AI-assisted simulation inferior to standardized patient simulation for breast self-examination skills — related
  • AI-generated virtual patients in HPE are used mainly for teaching and learning communication skills, diagnostic reasoning and management reasoning, with assessment a minority use case — related
  • AI-powered simulations show short-term gains in communication confidence and structured knowledge in RCTs, with limited or inferior effects for tactile psychomotor skills — a broader claim this one bears on
  • AI-driven case simulations in ambulatory settings support diagnostic reasoning and anticipatory thinking in nursing education — related
  • VR/AR and metaverse-based approaches were more frequently associated with favorable findings than screen-based virtual patient approaches, which yielded mixed findings with no clear advantage over non-technology-based simulation — a broader claim this one bears on
