# Incidental multimodal exposure produces better explicit translation-recognition performance on previously exposed words than on new words in beginner FL learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants performed better on old words than new words in the translation recognition task in both block 1 and block 2 of explicit learning. [→ Marie-Josée Bisson 2014](#marie-josee-bisson-2014)

## Evidence

### Marie-Josée Bisson 2014

Marie-Josée Bisson, Walter J. B. van Heuven, Kathy Conklin, and Richard J. Tunney. (2014). The Role of Repeated Exposure to Multimodal Input in Incidental Acquisition of Foreign Language Vocabulary. Language Learning 64:4. https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12085

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVAs on the translation recognition task (phase 2) showed a main effect of word type, "indicating that participants performed better on old words" than new words, with ηp2 = .39 (by participants) and ηp2 = .40 (by items) in block 1 and ηp2 = .26 and .28 in block 2. Seventy-eight adults took part; after 10 were excluded for language background, one for a technical problem and one for 58% accuracy in the phase 1 letter-search task, the participant analyses rest on 66 (F1 df = 65).

> "Results revealed a main effect of word type for both block 1, F1(1, 65) = 41.35, p < .001, ηp2 = .39, F2(1, 76) = 51.43, p < .001, ηp2 = .40"
