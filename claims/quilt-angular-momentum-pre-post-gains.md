## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving student understanding of addition of angular momentum in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06165)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-test/post-test evaluation with 26 undergraduates across the 2009 and 2010 spring semesters, QuILT administered after traditional instruction. "their post-test average score increased to 73% and 72%"; a Wilcoxon signed-rank test gave z-value > 3.5 and two-tailed t-tests gave p < 0.001 for all questions except question (a).

> "The average correctness percentage for all the questions in the pre-test was 40% in 2009 (with 9 students) and 27% in 2010 (with 17 students). After the students learned the topic using the QuILT for the addition of angular momentum, their post-test average score increased to 73% and 72% in 2009 and 2010, respectively."

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Improving student understanding of addition of angular momentum in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06165](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06165)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Same 26-student pre/post evaluation, questions (b) and (c) on constructing matrices for H1 and H2. Table 2 prints correctness of 8% pre-test rising to 54% (question b) and 73% (question c) post-test; 21 of 26 students correctly sandwiched the operator with basis vectors in the post-test.

> "The correctness percentages of the students answering questions (b) and (c) in the pre-test and post-test are listed in ta- ble 2. Only two out of the twenty-six students correctly calculated the matrices for both the operators ˆH1 and ˆH2 in the pre-test."
