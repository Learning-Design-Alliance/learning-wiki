Evidence¶

Dajani 2015¶

Dajani, D. R., & Uddin, L. Q. (2015). Demystifying cognitive flexibility: Implications for clinical and developmental neuroscience. Trends in Neurosciences. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2015.07.003

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review of clinical literature. Cognitive inflexibility in ASD "is associated with higher levels of RRBs". Although flexibility deficits may be present in ASD, ADHD and OCD, the review states flexibility may be most profoundly impacted in ASD and is specifically linked with its core symptomatology.

Related Claims¶

  • The four published neuroimaging studies of cognitive flexibility in ASD report inconsistent activation differences, with altered profiles possibly compensatory — related
  • Cognitive flexibility develops protractedly: sharp increase between 7 and 9 years, largely mature by age 10, peaking between 21 and 30
  • Cognitive flexibility uniquely predicts literacy achievement in Grades 4 and 6, above and beyond fluid intelligence, attention, inhibitory control, working memory, and planning
  • Meta-analyses identify a distributed frontoparietal network for flexible switching including vlPFC, dlPFC, ACC, right AI, parietal and subcortical regions
  • Neural variability and flexibility may underlie cognitive flexibility, with an optimal level required across the lifespan
  • Inhibitory control is the EDF subdomain most repeatedly linked to ADHD
