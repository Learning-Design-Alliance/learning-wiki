# On an ATP high-energy-phosphate-bond quiz question, 79% of NEXUS physics students chose the misconception answer C versus 87% in Galley's chemistry class, and about one third chose both B and C

## Evidence

### Redish 2012

Redish, E. F. (2012). The role of context and culture in teaching physics: The implication of disciplinary differences. Keynote address, World Conference on Physics Education 2012, Istanbul. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1207.4840](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1207.4840)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Quiz administered early in the second term of the first trial of the reformed NEXUS physics class (2011-12), using Galley's question from the chemistry education literature. An interview with student Gregor showed he treated choosing C as a framing error, reasoning in the larger biological context of the reaction in water.

> "In our class 79% chose the "misconception" C compared to 87% in the chemistry class; and 47% chose the "correct" answer B compared to 31% in the chemistry class. However, there was an interesting result. Almost 1/3 of the students gave B and C as answers - apparently contradicting themselves."
