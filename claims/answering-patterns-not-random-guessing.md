## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Selecting all answers under the same alphabet letter occurred only for quick responders, and quick responses contained mostly one letter sprinkled with others, indicating pattern-based rather than random guessing. [→ Ene 2019](#ene-2019)

## Evidence

### Ene 2019

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Case analysis of quick responses, including a student coded Kim who took the BEMA four times and used different answering patterns each time (all A-s, all last-option, mostly D-s), supporting pattern-based guessing over random choice.

> "Our data showed that selecting all answers under the same alphabet letter, an approach known as blind guessing, happened for the time category quick but not for time category valid."
