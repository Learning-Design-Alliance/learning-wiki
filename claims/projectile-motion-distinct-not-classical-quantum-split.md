## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The data show no clear split between classical and quantum scenarios, nor between single-value and distribution experiments; instead Projectile Motion responses differ from the other three, whose reasoning is quite similar. [→ Andy Schang 2023](#andy-schang-2023)

## Evidence

### Andy Schang 2023

Andy Schang, Matthew Dew, Emily M. Stump, N. G. Holmes, and Gina Passante. (2023). More or better data: A new perspective on student reasoning about measurement uncertainty. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020105)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-scenario comparison of response distributions and explanation codes from the survey. The authors infer many students see a fundamental distinction between classical and quantum principles for single-valued experiments but not for distribution experiments, so carryover from classical to quantum thinking may be context dependent.

> "these data do not show a clear split between student thinking across classical (Projectile Motion and Brownian Motion) versus quantum (Stern-Gerlach and Single-Slit) mechanical scenarios."
