# Psychosis, ASD, and ADHD-Inattentive Type independently elevate risk for slow processing speed relative to referred youth without diagnoses

## Evidence

### Braaten EB 2020

Braaten EB, Ward AK, Forchelli G, Vuijk PJ, Cook NE, McGuinness P, Lee BA, Samkavitz A, Lind H, O'Keefe SM, Doyle AE. (2020). Characteristics of child psychiatric outpatients with slow processing speed and potential mechanisms of academic impact. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01455-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression (Table 3, n = 751) entered all diagnostic groups together against referred youth with no diagnoses. Psychosis (OR = 3.02, p = .007) and ASD (OR = 1.85, p = .006) were significant; mood, anxiety, conduct, and ODD were not.

> "the odds of slow PS were significantly higher for children with psychosis and ASD compared to the referred youth with no psychiatric diagnoses. The odds for those with ADHD emerged as a statistical trend (OR 1.42, [95% CI 0.97 – 2.07], p = .07)."

### Braaten EB 2020 (2)

Braaten EB, Ward AK, Forchelli G, Vuijk PJ, Cook NE, McGuinness P, Lee BA, Samkavitz A, Lind H, O'Keefe SM, Doyle AE. (2020). Characteristics of child psychiatric outpatients with slow processing speed and potential mechanisms of academic impact. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01455-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Follow-up logistic regression with ADHD subtypes (n = 743) re-ran the model with ADHD-I and ADHD-C categories. ADHD-I was significant while ADHD-C was not; psychosis (OR = 3.03, p = .007) and ASD (OR = 1.80, p = .009) remained significant.

> "youth diagnosed with ADHD-I also showed an elevated risk for slow PS (OR 1.60, [95% CI 1.01 – 2.55], p = .045). The combined subtype of ADHD was not significantly associated with an increased risk for slow PS (OR 1.31, [95% CI 0.85 – 2.03], p = .22)"
