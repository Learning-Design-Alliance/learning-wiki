Evidence¶

Jarodzka 2020¶

Jarodzka, H., Skuballa, I., & Gruber, H. (2020). Eye-Tracking in Educational Practice: Investigating Visual Perception Underlying Teaching and Learning in the Classroom. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09565-7

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The review reports Seidel, Schnitzler, Kosel, Stürmer, and Holzberger's study, which showed video clips of authentic classroom lessons to more- versus less-experienced teachers, combining eye-tracking with diagnoses of pupils' behavior and elaborations. The review attributes the finding to the authors' own report.

"The authors showed that experienced teachers can do so quite proficiently thereby also contributing to our knowledge on teachers'professional vision (see next section), while beginning teachers struggle with it."

Related Claims¶

  • Experienced teachers diagnose underestimating, overestimating, and uninterested student profiles more accurately than novices, with partial eye-tracking support — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Expert teachers' classroom perception appears knowledge-driven while novices' appears image-driven, guided by salient visual features — related
  • Machine learning analysis of regular video recordings can support conclusions on learners' visual perception in lectures — related
  • Expert and novice teachers do not differ significantly in overall judgment accuracy of five student characteristic profiles, though experts judge inconsistent profiles more accurately — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Judgment accuracy varies across student characteristic profiles: struggling and uninterested profiles are diagnosed more accurately than strong, underestimating, and overestimating profiles — related
  • Expert teachers show expertise-related eye movement patterns in some classroom tasks but not others, indicating task and context specificity — related
