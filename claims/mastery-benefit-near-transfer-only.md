## Evidence

### William R. Evans and Mats A. Selen 2016

William R. Evans and Mats A. Selen. (2016). Investigating the Use of Mastery-Style Online Homework Exercises in Introductory Algebra-based Mechanics in a Controlled Clinical Study. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Expert-rating analysis in the first clinical trial: eight physics education experts rated post-test questions 0–5 for nearness of transfer, and questions were split into nearer- and farther-transfer groups. The article reports the mastery condition "signiﬁcantly out- performed the students in the traditional-homework condition on the nearer-transfer ques- tions", significant on 3 of 6 questions (p < 0.05) and marginally significant on another (p < 0.1); no effect size is printed.

> "Figure 2a shows that the students in the mastery-homework condition signiﬁcantly out- performed the students in the traditional-homework condition on the nearer-transfer ques- tions from the post-test."

### William R. Evans and Mats A. Selen 2016 (2)

William R. Evans and Mats A. Selen. (2016). Investigating the Use of Mastery-Style Online Homework Exercises in Introductory Algebra-based Mechanics in a Controlled Clinical Study. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same expert-rating analysis of the first clinical trial, farther-transfer half. The article reports the difference between the two homework conditions "was not judged to be signiﬁcant on any of the six farther-transfer questions"; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "The diﬀerence in performance between students in the two homework conditions was not judged to be signiﬁcant on any of the six farther-transfer questions (see Figure 2b)."
