# Virtual patients blended with traditional education show moderate positive skills effects, but the pooled interval crosses zero

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pooled skills effect for blended virtual patients versus traditional education alone was SMD=0.60 (95% CI −0.07 to 1.27), with significant gains in only 3 of 7 studies. [→ Kononowicz AA 2019](#kononowicz-aa-2019)

## Evidence

### Kononowicz AA 2019

Kononowicz AA, Woodham LA, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/14676

`q3 · i?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of blended-learning skills outcomes (Figure 5). The review reports "SMD=0.60, 95% CI −0.07 to 1.27, I2=83%, n=247"; the CI includes zero, and 2 nursing studies showed no significant difference.

> "The pooled effect for skills outcomes (SMD=0.60, 95% CI −0.07 to 1.27, I2=83%, n=247; Figure 5) suggests that virtual patients blended with traditional education have moderate positive effects in comparison with traditional education alone."
