# Treatment preschoolers significantly improved Spanish receptive and expressive vocabulary and English receptive vocabulary from pre-test to post-test, with moderate effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Treatment children's receptive vocabulary (RVK) rose significantly from pre-test to post-test in English (d = 0.63) and Spanish (d = 0.50). [→ Baker 2016](#baker-2016)
`q2 i2` Treatment children's expressive vocabulary (RNA) rose significantly in Spanish (d = 0.61) but not in English. [→ Baker 2016](#baker-2016)

## Evidence

### Baker 2016

Baker, D. L., Mogna, V., Rodriguez, S., Farmer, D., & Yovanoff, P. (2016). Building the Oral Language of Young Hispanic Children Through Interactive Read Alouds and Vocabulary Games at Preschool and at Home. Journal of International Special Needs Education, 19(2), 81-94. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1195411

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t-tests of pre-test to post-test scores for the 34 treatment children in the quasi-experimental Study 1. The article reports effect sizes (Cohen's d) were 0.63 in English and 0.50 in Spanish on the RVK, and a significant Spanish RNA gain of 1.66 points with d = 0.61; English RNA differences were not statistically significant.

> "the results on the RVK at post-test were, on average, signiﬁcantly higher than at pre-test by 2.02 points on the English version of the assessment (t(33) ¼/C0 3.47, p , .01), and 1.79 points on the Spanish version of the assessment ( t(33) ¼/C0 4.28, p , .01)."
