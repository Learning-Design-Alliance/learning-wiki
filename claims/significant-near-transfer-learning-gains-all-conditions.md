# Students across all conditions demonstrated significant pre-post learning gains on near-transfer physics questions, but not on far-transfer questions

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Killingsworth 2015

Killingsworth, S. S., Clark, D. B., & Adams, D. M. (2015). Self-Explanation and Explanatory Feedback in Games: Individual Differences, Gameplay, and Learning. International Journal of Education in Mathematics, Science and Technology, 3(3), 162-186. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1066360

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA with test administration as within-subjects variable and game condition as between-subjects variable, treating near- and far-transfer questions as separate measures. Only gains on near-transfer questions were significant, with η² = .317 for near-transfer and η² = .026 for far-transfer.

> "the effect of test administration was significant only for near-transfer questions, F(1, 83) = 38.440, p < .0001, η2= .317, with students performing better on near-transfer post-test questions ( M = 53.73%, SD = 23.34) than on pre -test questions ( M = 35.29%, SD = 20.48). The effect for far-transfer questions did not approach significance, F(1, 83) = 2.250, p = .137, η2= .026."
