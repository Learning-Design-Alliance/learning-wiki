Evidence¶

Goetz 2023¶

Goetz, T., Bieleke, M., Yanagida, T., Krannich, M., Roos, A.-L., Frenzel, A. C., Lipnevich, A. A., & Pekrun, R. (2023). Test boredom: Exploring a neglected emotion. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://osf.io/ftr4g/?view_only=beac4ca87987492294aeac4a1bb96c86

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Study 1 latent correlations from hierarchical (multilevel for state) CFA models in 208 8th graders. Boredom scores "showed positive correlations with both overchallenge and underchallenge" and were negatively related to intrinsic and extrinsic value; no effect sizes are printed for these correlations.

"Thus, supporting H2, all correlations for the trait and state assessments were significant and in the expected directions."

Related Claims¶

  • Abundance hypothesis: state test boredom relates negatively to performance on the difficult test part but not the easy part — related
  • Nonlinear CVT extensions show cubic control terms with negative coefficients for negative emotions in the full sample, but the coefficient's sign differs across latent profiles — related
  • Test boredom correlates negatively with math grades but not with the math test score in Study 1 — related
  • Students report statistically significant levels of boredom during mathematics tests, for both trait and state measures — related
  • The experimental manipulation succeeded: most participants reported overchallenge during difficult tasks and underchallenge during easy tasks — related
  • The number of manipulation trials showed little variation across participants and was deliberately not statistically controlled — related
  • A single boredom item correlated strongly with the full TBS-S scale in both the easy and difficult test parts — related
  • Residual covariances were specified between specific item pairs in the Study 1 measurement-invariance test of the TBS-S across difficult and easy test parts — related
