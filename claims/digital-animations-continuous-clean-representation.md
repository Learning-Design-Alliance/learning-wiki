## Evidence

### M.H. Giménez 2007

M.H. Giménez, A. Vidaurre, J. Riera, and J.A. Monsoriu. (2007). Visualizing the Doppler Effect. Universidad Politécnica de Valencia. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702036](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0702036)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' argument from the conclusions: simulations produce the effect of a continuous representation in time, unlike slides or transparencies, and appear free of noise so different aspects can be emphasized; in this sense simulations can be more efficient than the real process itself. No learning outcome data are reported.

> "This problem can be solved with digital animations that provide a continuous time representation of the physical phenomenon and, on the other hand allow us to “clean” the process showing only the aspects we want to emphasize in order to get a better understanding of it."
