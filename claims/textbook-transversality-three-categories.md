# Surveyed textbooks present transversality treatments falling into three categories, and none include physical arguments for transversality

## Evidence

### Lior M. Burko 2008

Lior M. Burko. (2008). Transversality of Electromagnetic Waves in the Calculus-Based Introductory Physics Course. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2993 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The author's textbook survey classifies treatments into three categories: postulating transversality, using only the two dynamical equations, or adding a demonstration that a longitudinal E field is disallowed. The quote names category A; the article defines categories B and C similarly.

> "Category A: textbooks that postulate the transversality (but don't derive it), and then show consistency with the (either all four or just the dynamical) Maxwell equations"

### Lior M. Burko 2008 (2)

Lior M. Burko. (2008). Transversality of Electromagnetic Waves in the Calculus-Based Introductory Physics Course. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2993 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The survey also found that no surveyed textbook gives physical arguments for transversality, and none discusses the impossibility of genuine spherical electromagnetic waves, an omission the author calls surprising given that building physical intuition is a primary course goal.

> "None of the textbooks we have surveyed include physical arguments for the transversality of electromagnetic waves, even though all of them discuss polarization."
