# LACAeP training produced a non-significant increase of 1.34 (out of 20) in correct ADR causality classification among paediatric trainees

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a pilot RCT, paediatric trainees given LACAeP training scored on average 1.34 points higher (95% CI −0.3 to 3.0) on correct classification of 20 ADR cases than untrained controls, a difference that was not statistically significant. [→ Conroy Elizabeth J. 2015](#conroy-elizabeth-j-2015)

## Evidence

### Conroy Elizabeth J. 2015

Conroy Elizabeth J., Kirkham Jamie J., Bellis Jennifer R., Peak Matthew, Smyth Rosalind L., Williamson Paula R., Pirmohamed Munir. (2015). A pilot randomised controlled trial to assess the utility of an e‐learning package that trains users in adverse drug reaction causality. International Journal of Pharmacy Practice. https://doi.org/10.1111/ijpp.12197

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pilot single-blind parallel-group RCT with 35 analysable trainees (18 training, 17 control) assessing 20 ADR cases with the LCAT against expert-panel gold-standard classifications. The intervention arm mean was "9.22 (95% CI, 7.96 to 10.48)" versus 7.88 in control; the printed effect was "1.34 on average (95% CI, −0.3 to 3.0)", with no significance test or effect size reported.

> "The average score by correct classification was 9.22 (95% CI, 7.96 to 10.48) in the intervention arm and 7.88 in the control arm (95% CI, 6.76 to 9.00). The effect of the intervention was to increase the score by 1.34 on average (95% CI, −0.3 to 3.0)."
