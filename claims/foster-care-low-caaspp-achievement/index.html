Evidence¶

Burns 2022¶

Burns, D., Espinoza, D., Adams, J., & Ondrasek, N. (2022). California students in foster care: Challenges and promising practices. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/118.471

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of 2018–19 CAASPP results for students in grades 3–8 and 11 with valid scores. The report also notes 53% of students in foster care scored "standard not met" in ELA versus 26% of non-foster students, and 63% versus 35% in mathematics.

"Just 24% of students in foster care met or exceeded state standards in English language arts (ELA) (compared to 51% of other students), and just 15% met or exceeded state standards in mathematics (compared to 40% of other students)."

Burns 2022 (2)¶

Burns, D., Espinoza, D., Adams, J., & Ondrasek, N. (2022). California students in foster care: Challenges and promising practices. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/118.471

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Subgroup analysis of 2018–19 CAASPP results by foster status, special education eligibility, and English learner status, shown in Figure 2. The report concludes many students in foster care "may need multiple supports to achieve their educational goals."

"Of students in foster care eligible for special education, 7% met or exceeded state standards in ELA, and 5% met or exceeded state standards in mathematics (compared with 16% and 13%, respectively, for non-foster students with disabilities)."
