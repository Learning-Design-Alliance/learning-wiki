# Joint attention is a group phenomenon not reducible to either individual's mental representations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Joint attention involves both members of a dyad gazing with the same intent, and its mutuality is not reducible to either individual's mental representations. [→ Gerry Stahl 2014](#gerry-stahl-2014)

## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl 2014

Gerry Stahl, Nancy Law, Ulrike Cress & Sten Ludvigsen. (2014). Analyzing roles of individuals in small-group collaboration processes. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 9, 365–370. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-014-9204-9

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

This is the editorial's theoretical argument, drawing on Tomasello's comparative research and Vygotsky's analysis of the pointing gesture, about the Schneider and Pea gaze study: joint attention is "a group phenomenon" involving gazing with the same intent and mutual knowledge of gazing together.

> "The mutuality of intersubjective intentionality is not reducible to either individual 's mental representations, but consists in a shared meaning making."
