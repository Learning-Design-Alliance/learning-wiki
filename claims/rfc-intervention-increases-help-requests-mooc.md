## Evidence

### Ralf Teusner 2018

Ralf Teusner, Thomas Hille, Thomas Staubitz. (2018). Effects of Automated Interventions in Programming Assignments: Evidence from a Field Experiment. L@S 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3231644.3231650

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A/B test in a live programming MOOC with randomly assigned students; in the RFC condition 31% (1065/3448) RFCs followed an intervention versus 24% in control — "the RFC intervention signiﬁcantly increased the rate of RFCs by 7 percentage points" (χ2 = 25.32, p < 0.001).

> "However, in the RFC encouragement condition, 31% (1065/3448) RFCs were sent following the intervention. Thus, as a second ﬁnding, the RFC intervention signiﬁcantly increased the rate of RFCs by 7 percentage points relative to the control condition (Chi-squared test χ2 = 25.32, p < 0.001)."

### Ralf Teusner 2018 (2)

Ralf Teusner, Thomas Hille, Thomas Staubitz. (2018). Effects of Automated Interventions in Programming Assignments: Evidence from a Field Experiment. L@S 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3231644.3231650

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive Table 4 metrics from the same A/B test show RFCs per student of 1.0 in the RFC group (+66%) and 0.8 in the break group (+33%) versus a 0.6 baseline — an "increased use of RFCs of up to 66% in the RFC group", reported descriptively.

> "We further see that, despite the fact that most students reported that they were unaffected by the just-in-time interventions, the experimental groups show an increased use of RFCs of up to 66% in the RFC group (see Table 4, RFC per student)."
