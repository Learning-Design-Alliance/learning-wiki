Descriptive analysis of CPS transfer destinations and origins in Chapter 1, disaggregated in Figures 2A-2D. The report finds within-district moves are "between 50 and 60 percent of the total transfers into schools" during the school year and two-thirds during the summer.

"During the school year, these types of moves represent between 50 and 60 percent of the total transfers into schools (Figures 2A and 2B); during the summer, they represent two-thirds of all transfers"

Related Claims¶

  • African American students are the most mobile group of CPS students, and the gap between them and other students widened after 2000-01, especially at the elementary level — related
  • Elementary student mobility in CPS decreased between 1995 and 2007, with school-year stability rising from 87.9 to 91.2 percent and school-year in-mobility falling from 11.4 to 7.1 percent — related
  • Residential mobility is closely associated with elementary school transfers in CPS, with nearly 80 percent of mid-year within-district transfers accompanied by a residential move — related
  • High school stability improvements in CPS were largely driven by declining dropout rates, which fell from 7.1 percent to 3.2 percent during the school year between 1995 and 2006 — related
  • School closings, new school openings, CHA demolitions, and NCLB transfers each affected fewer than 1 percent of CPS students per year, though receiving schools saw summer in-mobility rise by almost 13 percentage points — related
  • Residential mobility explains 55-61 percent of the school-year race gap in mobility, school average achievement an additional 16 percent, and access to school choice options increasingly explains the summer gap — related
  • The study design separately examined recent-year moves and cumulative moves across elementary school in relation to child functioning — related
