## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The populations remaining set-like or remaining mixed did not have significantly different numbers of individual S responses between pre-test and post-test (p = 0.31 for set-like and p = 0.09 for mixed). [→ Benjamin Pollard 2017](#benjamin-pollard-2017)

## Evidence

### Benjamin Pollard 2017

Benjamin Pollard, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2017). Impact of an introductory lab on students’ understanding of measurement uncertainty. arXiv:1707.01979v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1707.01979)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subpopulation analysis of the Fall 2016 CU cohort’s pre/post PMQ responses, comparing students who kept the same overall paradigm. The article prints p = 0.31 for remaining set-like students and p = 0.09 for remaining mixed students, with no effect size for these null findings; equivalence was not tested.

> "The populations of students that did not change their overall paradigm, either remaining set-like or remaining mixed after instruction, did not have signiﬁcantly diﬀerent numbers of individual S responses between pre-test and post-test ( p = 0 .31 for set-like and p = 0 .09 for mixed)."
