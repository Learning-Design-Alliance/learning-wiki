# Completing an E&M course improves performance on negative electric field and potential questions but not on the negative net-charge question

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For Q14 (negative electric field component) and Q16 (negative potential difference), students at the beginning of waves/optics/thermo were more likely to be correct than earlier-sequence students (chi-square p < 0.001), but for Q15 completing an E&M course does not affect students' likelihood of being correct. ## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2019

Trevor I. Smith, Suzanne W. Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Philip Eaton, Paul J. Kelly, Kyle J. Louis, Mitchell A. Nussenbaum, and Louis J. Remy. (2019). Developing a reasoning inventory for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Chi-square analyses comparing course-sequence groups on E&M negativity questions. Q14 and Q16 showed statistically significant differences (p < 0.001) favoring waves/optics/thermo students; for Q15, E&M students beat mechanics students but waves/optics/thermo students were not any better.

> "Our data indicate that this is true for Q14 and Q16, with statistically signiﬁcant differences revealed by chi-square analyses ( p < 0.001), and students in the beginning of their waves/optics/thermo course being more likely to be correct than either students at the beginning of mechanics or students at the beginning of E&M."
