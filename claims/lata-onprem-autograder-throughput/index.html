Evidence¶

Jesse A. Rodríguez 2026¶

Jesse A. Rodríguez. (2026). LaTA: A Drop-In, FERPA-Compliant Local-LLM Autograder for Upper-Division STEM Coursework. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05410 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.05410

q2 · i? · design · r2

Operational evidence collected by the instructor during the Winter 2026 term in ME 373, recorded as the standard output of each grading run. The quote reports the printed per-submission wall-clock time on the single Mac Studio host.

"LaTA graded the 200-student roster in 1–3 minutes of wall-clock time per submission on the Mac Studio M3 Ultra described in §4.1, with the per-assignment total dominated by the number of problems and the length of the worked solutions rather than by class size."

Related Claims¶

  • The between-cohort exam gain is attributable only to a composite intervention (autograding plus corrections plus expanded office hours), not to LaTA alone — related
  • Instructor-confirmed grading errors occurred at roughly 0.02–0.04% of rubric line items across the term — related
  • Student perceptions of the autograder were mixed, with the LaTeX workflow rated more positively than the grader itself — related
  • Students reported first-pass false-positive deductions at a mean of 2.94 per term, normalising to about 0.6% of rubric items — related
  • Student-reported LaTeX time overhead fell about 46% across the term, from 87 to 47 minutes per assignment — related
  • A page-by-page, rubric-guided multimodal LLM workflow graded an authentic handwritten exam in about three hours at roughly $100 in token costs, versus about $3,500 in TA time — related
  • About 400 labeled proofs are sufficient for adequate autograding performance — related
