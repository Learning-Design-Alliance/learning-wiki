# Among accepted students, FAFSA completion is associated with roughly 50 percent higher four-year college enrollment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 review `r3`, 1 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` After controlling for qualifications, background, neighborhood, and reported support, students accepted at a four-year college who completed a federal financial aid application were almost 50 percent more likely to enroll than accepted students who did not. [→ Melissa Roderick 2009](#melissa-roderick-2009)

`q2 i1` Among students accepted to a four-year college, 84 percent of those who completed a FAFSA by year end enrolled in a four-year college in the fall, compared with 55 percent of those who did not file. [→ Jenny Nagaoka 2008](#jenny-nagaoka-2008)

## Evidence

### Melissa Roderick 2009

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, and Vanessa Coca. (2009). College Readiness for All: The Challenge for Urban High Schools. The Future of Children. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/college-readiness-all-challenge-urban-high-schools

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

In the authors' recent Chicago study, FAFSA completion predicted enrollment among students accepted at four-year colleges, controlling for qualifications, family background, neighborhood characteristics, and reported support from parents, teachers, and counselors. The 50 percent figure is an adjusted relative likelihood, not a standardized effect size.

> "students who had been accepted into a four-year college and had completed a federal financial aid application were almost 50 percent more likely to enroll than students who had been accepted but had not completed that application"

### Jenny Nagaoka 2008

Jenny Nagaoka, Melissa Roderick, and Vanessa Coca. (2008). Barriers to College Attainment: Lessons from Chicago. The Consortium on Chicago School Research at The University of Chicago / Center for American Progress. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/barriers-college-attainment-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Part of the CCSR analysis of the path Chicago graduates took toward four-year enrollment. The association held after controlling for student characteristics and support from parents, teachers, and counselors; predicted probabilities were 84 percent versus 56 percent.

> "Among students who had been accepted to a four-year college, some 84 percent of students who completed a FAFSA by the end of the school year attended a four-year college in the fall, compared to only 55 percent of students who did not file a FAFSA."
