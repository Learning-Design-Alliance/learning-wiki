# Primary students taught 'The Representation of the Earth' with AR scored higher on the post-test than students taught traditionally (M = 7.9 vs M = 6.4)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental pre-test/post-test comparison, the experimental group taught with AR obtained higher post-test qualifications (M = 7.9) than the control group taught traditionally (M = 6.4), a difference of 1.5 points. ## Evidence

### Purificacion Toledo-Morales & Jose Manuel Sanchez-Garcia 2018

Purificacion Toledo-Morales & Jose Manuel Sanchez-Garcia. (2018). Use of Augmented Reality in Social Sciences as Educational Resource. Turkish Online Journal of Distance Education-TOJDE, 19(3), Article 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1183349

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of 49 5th-grade students in Seville: experimental group (n = 25) taught with AR materials on tablets, control group (n = 24) taught traditionally, both pre- and post-tested with a 10-item multiple-choice test scored 0-10. The post-test comparison showed "a difference of score of 1.5" favouring the AR group; the article reports t-test analysis but prints no effect size.

> "And those obtained in the post-test of both groups also said that the group experimental obtained qualifications more high (M = 7.9) that the group control (M = 6.4), with a difference of score of 1.5"
