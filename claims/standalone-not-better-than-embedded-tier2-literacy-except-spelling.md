## Evidence

### Foorman 2017

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomized trial in 55 low-performing Florida schools across two cohorts; hierarchical linear models with students nested in small groups nested in schools. The standalone intervention "resulted in significantly improved spelling outcomes relative to the embedded intervention"; the printed effect size is 0.18 standard deviation (17 points, 7 percentile rank points), below d = 0.2.

> "In grade 2 the standalone intervention resulted in significantly improved spelling outcomes relative to the embedded intervention, including among students with a low FRA Spelling baseline score."

### Foorman 2017 (2)

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical linear model analyses by grade with baseline covariates found "no other differences in reading outcomes between students in standalone and embedded intervention schools"; the report also states there were no differences in most language outcomes by cohort or baseline performance between the intervention groups.

> "There were no other differences in reading outcomes between students in standalone and embedded intervention schools for grades K–2."
