# In one eye-tracking experiment, revising math-relevant visuals changes fixation length on them differently by prior knowledge group, with shorter fixations under the revised visuals for low prior knowledge students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Average fixation length on math-relevant visuals showed a condition by prior knowledge interaction, F(2, 51) = 8.58, p < .001, with low prior knowledge students showing longer fixations in the original condition, middle prior knowledge students shorter in the original, and high prior knowledge students longer in the original. ## Evidence

### Clinton 2017

Clinton, V., Cooper, J.L., Michaelis, J., Alibali, M.W., & Nathan, M.J. (2017). How Revisions to Mathematical Visuals Affect Cognition: Evidence from Eye Tracking. In Eye-tracking technology applications in educational research (pp. 195-218). IGI Global. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574984 [doi:10.4018/978-1-5225-1005-5.ch010](https://doi.org/10.4018/978-1-5225-1005-5.ch010)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Same eye-tracking experiment; analyses of average fixation length on math-relevant visuals present in both conditions. The interaction was significant; for low prior knowledge students fixation length "was longer in the original condition than in the revised condition".

> "Similar to the text analyses, there was an interaction of condition and prior knowledge on average fixation length on the math-relevant visuals, F(2, 51) = 8.58, p < .001 (see Figure 2). For participants in the low prior knowledge group, the average fixation length for math-relevant visuals was longer in the original condition than in the revised condition F(1, 21) = 5.53, p = .02."
