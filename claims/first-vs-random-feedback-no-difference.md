# First-error versus random rubric-point feedback ordering produces no significant difference in best submission scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Feedback on the first incorrect rubric point versus a randomly selected incorrect rubric point yielded no significant difference in students' best submission scores. [→ Zhao 2025](#zhao-2025)

## Evidence

### Zhao 2025

Zhao, C., Silva, M., & Poulsen, S. (2025). Autograding Mathematical Induction Proofs with Natural Language Processing. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 35, 3202–3232. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00498-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of two feedback-ordering conditions (59 First, 42 Random students) within the user study, using post-hoc pairwise tests after significant Kruskal-Wallis tests on best submission scores for all three problems. The article reports no standardized effect size for the First-versus-Random contrast.

> "Post-hoc pairwise tests reveal that the best submission scores are signiﬁcantly higher for students in First and Random than Self-eval, while there is no signiﬁcant difference between First and Random."
