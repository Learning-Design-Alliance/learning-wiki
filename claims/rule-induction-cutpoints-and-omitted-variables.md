# Rule induction identifies useful cut-points of continuous predictors and groupings of nominal predictors, and surfaces outcome-related variables omitted from regression models

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Rule induction identified cut-points of continuous predictors and groupings of nominal predictors useful for prediction and further analysis. [→ Iwatani 2019](#iwatani-2019)
`q1 i?` Rule induction easily identified outcome-related variables not included in the regression model, though the author notes this is a feature of any data mining with explicit variable choice, not unique to rulesets. [→ Iwatani 2019 (2)](#iwatani-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2019

Iwatani, E. (2019). How Rule Induction Data Mining Can and Cannot Be Useful for Education Research. Paper presented at the 2019 annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association. https://doi.org/10.302/1438441

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

From the author's reflection on the Study 2 re-analysis: CART-derived 8th grade math subgroups were used as cut-points to create subsamples for subsequent association rule mining.

> "For example, the 8th grade math subgroups identified by CART in study 2 (Figure 7) motivated the next step of using those scores as cut-points for creating subsamples to conduct association rule mining."

### Iwatani 2019 (2)

Iwatani, E. (2019). How Rule Induction Data Mining Can and Cannot Be Useful for Education Research. Paper presented at the 2019 annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association. https://doi.org/10.302/1438441

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Author's observation across both re-analyses; the author cautions this is not a unique benefit of ruleset induction, since stepwise regression might likewise identify different variables.

> "Rule and ruleset induction also easily identified variables that are related to the outcome that were not included in the regression model."
