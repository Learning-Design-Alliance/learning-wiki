# All three structured interdependence conditions produce higher agreement that team members' sense of obligation to contribute aided learning, compared with no structured interdependence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Role, reward, and role-plus-reward groups each agreed significantly more than no-interdependence groups that they learned more because teammates knew it was their job to contribute. [→ Brewer 2003](#brewer-2003)

## Evidence

### Brewer 2003

Brewer, S. A., & Klein, J. D. (2003). Small Group Learning in an Online Asynchronous Environment. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED484997

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Attitude results from the four-condition experiment (Table 2, item significant at p < .01); the no-interdependence group mean was 2.32 versus roughly 3.0 in each structured condition. No effect size printed.

> "participants in all three types of structured interdependence groups (roles, reward, and role s-plus-reward) had significantly higher agreement with the statement "I was able to learn more because my team members knew it was their job to contribute to the group work," compared to groups with no structured interdependence."
