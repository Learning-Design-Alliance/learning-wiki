# Preschool math ability significantly predicts kindergarten math ability on both the ECLS-K Math battery and the Applied Problems subtest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Preschool math scores significantly predicted kindergarten math scores for both measures, with effect sizes β = 0.37 (ECLS-K Math) and β = 0.52 (Applied Problems). [→ Blair 2016](#blair-2016)

## Evidence

### Blair 2016

Blair, Clancy; McKinnon, Rachel D.; the Family Life Project Investigators. (2016). Moderating effects of executive functions and the teacher–child relationship on the development of mathematics ability in kindergarten. Learning and Instruction, 41, 85–93. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.10.001

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Model 1 of the multilevel regression in the Family Life Project sample (N = 1005 with kindergarten math data) established the stability baseline against which the moderation effects were tested, with printed effect sizes β = 0.37 and β = 0.52. Applied Problems was the more stable measure.

> "math scores in preschool significantly predicted math scores in kindergarten for both ECLS-K Math and Applied Problems, b = 0.29, SE = 0.03, p < .001 and b = 0.44, SE = 0.04, p < .001, respectively, with effect sizes of β = 0.37 and β = 0.52"
