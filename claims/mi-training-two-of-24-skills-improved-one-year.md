# Nurses trained in motivational interviewing improved on only 2 of 24 MI skills at one-year follow-up compared to untrained controls

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At one-year follow-up, intervention nurses improved significantly on only two of 24 MI skills: inviting the patient to talk about behaviour change (mean difference=0.39, p=0.009) and assessing patient's confidence in changing lifestyle (mean difference=0.28, p=0.037). ## Evidence

### Jansink Renate 2013

Jansink Renate, Braspenning Jozé, Laurant Miranda, Keizer Ellen, Elwyn Glyn, Weijden Trudy van der, Grol Richard. (2013). Minimal improvement of nurses’ motivational interviewing skills in routine diabetes care one year after training: a cluster randomized trial. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-14-44

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster randomized trial in 58 Dutch general practices (30 trained vs 35 control nurses); five consultation video recordings per nurse at baseline and 14 months later, rated by two blinded judges. The article reports "mean difference=0.39, p=0.009" and "mean difference=0.28, p=0.037" for the two improved skills.

> "At one year follow up, it was demonstrated that the nurses improved on 2 of the 24 MI skills, namely, “inviting the patient to talk about behaviour change” (mean difference=0.39, p=0.009), and “assessing patient’s confidence in changing their lifestyle” (mean difference=0.28, p=0.037)."
