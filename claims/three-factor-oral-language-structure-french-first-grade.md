# In French first-graders, a three-factor structure distinguishing lower level language skills from discourse skills fits better than a two-factor oral language structure at both the beginning and end of first grade

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At T1, confirmatory factor analyses showed a three-factor model (decoding precursors, LLL skills, discourse skills) fit significantly better than a model grouping LLL and discourse skills together. [→ Jessica Massonnié 2018](#jessica-massonnie-2018)
`q3 i?` At T2, the three-factor model fit significantly better than a two-factor model dissociating only decoding from oral language skills. [→ Jessica Massonnié 2018 (2)](#jessica-massonnie-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Jessica Massonnié 2018

Jessica Massonnié, Maryse Bianco, Laurent Lima, Pascal Bressoux. (2018). Longitudinal predictors of reading comprehension in French at first grade: Unpacking the oral comprehension component of the simple view. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.005

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factorial analysis at T1 (September–November 2014) with 556 French first-grade pupils compared Model A (three factors) against a model grouping LLL and discourse skills; Model A fit significantly better (Δ χ2(2) = 46.6, p < .001), with "a good fit to the data".

> "This model yielded a good fit to the data (χ2(24) = 67.64, p < .001; CFI = .95; TLI = .93, SRMR = .04; RMSEA = .06, 90% confidence interval [.04, .07])."

### Jessica Massonnié 2018 (2)

Jessica Massonnié, Maryse Bianco, Laurent Lima, Pascal Bressoux. (2018). Longitudinal predictors of reading comprehension in French at first grade: Unpacking the oral comprehension component of the simple view. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.005

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factorial analysis at T2 (May–June 2015) showed the three-factor Model C (χ2(24) = 41.12; CFI = .99; RMSEA = .04) had "significantly better fit" than the two-factor alternative, replicating the T1 result at the end of first grade.

> "This model had significantly better fit (Δ χ2(2) = 48.33, p < .001) than a two-factor model only dissociating decoding skills from oral language skills"
