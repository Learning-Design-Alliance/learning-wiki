# The scoping review found a lack of high-quality empirical evidence on the measured benefits or harms of ANTT and a lack of empirically derived, tested education and training initiatives for wound dressing

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The reviewed literature lacked high-quality empirical evidence on measured benefits or harms of ANTT in acute surgical wound-dressing procedures. [→ Hegarty Josephine 2019](#hegarty-josephine-2019)
`q2 i?` The reviewed papers shared the theme of lacking empirically derived and tested education and training initiatives surrounding ANTT implementation. [→ Hegarty Josephine 2019](#hegarty-josephine-2019)

## Evidence

### Hegarty Josephine 2019

Hegarty Josephine, Howson Victoria, Wills Teresa, Creedon Sile A., Mc Cluskey Pat, Lane Aoife, Connolly Aine, Walshe Nuala, Noonan Brendan, Guidera Fiona, Gallagher Anthony G, Murphy Siobhan. (2019). Acute surgical wound‐dressing procedure: Description of the steps involved in the development and validation of an observational metric. International Wound Journal. https://doi.org/10.1111/iwj.13072

`q2 · i?`

Scoping review of 13 relevant papers plus 3 manufacturer guidelines and the Royal Marsden Procedural Manual, searched in EBSCO and Cochrane from January 2006 to January 2017. The review reports "a lack of high‐quality empirical evidence on the actual measured benefits or harms of using ANTT".

> "However, there was a lack of high‐quality empirical evidence on the actual measured benefits or harms of using ANTT generally or in the performance of acute surgical wound‐dressing procedures (ie, in reducing mortality, hospital acquired infection rates, and surgical site infections) in the reviewed literature."
