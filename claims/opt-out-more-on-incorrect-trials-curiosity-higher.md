## Evidence

### Sunae Kim 2025

Sunae Kim, Paul L Harris, Benjamin Néher. (2025). Curiosity can influence metacognitive processes. Metacognition and Learning (2025) 20:39. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09444-y

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 2 (final N = 25 Hungarian adults who used the opt-out at least once; 40 trials; accuracy for opted-out trials from forced responses at the end). Mixed-effects regression showed "χ2 = 35.5, df = 1, p <.001" with OR = 0.32; opt-out proportions were 0.1 (correct) vs 0.3 (incorrect).

> "Participants were less likely to opt out on correct than on incorrect trials χ2 = 35.5, df = 1, p <.001 (b = −1.15, SE = 0.19, OR = 0.32)."

### Sunae Kim 2025 (2)

Sunae Kim, Paul L Harris, Benjamin Néher. (2025). Curiosity can influence metacognitive processes. Metacognition and Learning (2025) 20:39. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09444-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Same Experiment 2 mixed-effects regression on curiosity and opt-out choice found "F(1, 998) = 24.6, p <.001" with β = 0.41; mean curiosity was 58.4 overall, higher on opted-out trials. The authors interpret opting out when curious as a strategic decision aimed at improving performance.

> "Curiosity was rated higher on opted out trials than on non-opted out trials, F(1, 998) = 24.6, p <.001 (b = 11.71, SE = 2.36, β = 0.41)."
