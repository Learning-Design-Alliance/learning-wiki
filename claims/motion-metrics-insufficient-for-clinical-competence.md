## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue technical skills not captured by motion metrics (image interpretation, calliper placement) and non-technical skills (communication, report writing, treatment planning) form overall clinical competence that motion metrics alone cannot assess. [→ Dromey BP 2021](#dromey-bp-2021)

## Evidence

### Dromey BP 2021

Dromey BP, Ahmed S, Vasconcelos F, Mazomenos E, Kunpalin Y, Ourselin S, Deprest J, David AL, Stoyanov D, Peebles DM. (2021). Dimensionless squared jerk: An objective differential to assess experienced and novice probe movement in obstetric ultrasound. Prenatal Diagnosis. https://doi.org/10.1002/pd.5855

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretive argument in the Discussion, citing parallels with minimally-invasive surgery literature; no new data are reported for this point. They state an objective skill measure is likely to contribute to, rather than replace, workplace-based assessment.

> "Technical skills which cannot be assessed by motion metrics, such as the interpretation of the obtained images and accurate placement of measurement callipers are combined with non‐technical skills such as communication with patients and colleagues, report writing and treatment planning to form the overall clinical competence. These cannot be assessed by motion metrics alone."
