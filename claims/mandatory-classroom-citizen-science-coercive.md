## Evidence

### Reiheld 2019

Reiheld, A., & Gay, P. L. (2019). Coercion, Consent, and Participation in Citizen Science. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Ethical argument about mandatory classroom assignments: the authors apply the principle that consent without the real possibility of refusal is not genuine consent to required student participation.

> "If given no other way to earn those credits, these students are being coerced: failure to participate has real and unavoidable negative consequences for their grades."

### Reiheld 2019 (2)

Reiheld, A., & Gay, P. L. (2019). Coercion, Consent, and Participation in Citizen Science. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.13061)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article cites prior research (Gray et al. 2012) reporting no demonstrated learning benefit of mandatory citizen science over participatory science alternatives; the review attributes this finding to that work.

> "Furthermore, requiring students to do citizen science as part of their mandatory activities has not been shown to benefit student learning more than other activities (Gray et al. 2012), such as participatory science where students use well-scaffolded, open-ended curricula to perform experiments with known potential outcomes."
