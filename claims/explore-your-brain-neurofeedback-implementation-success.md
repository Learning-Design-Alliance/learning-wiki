# In the neurofeedback lesson, students gained objective control over theta/beta index in three of four exercises (large effects except the reading exercise), and objective control correlated with subjective control (r = −.420).

## Evidence

### Janssen 2025

Janssen, T. W. P., & van Atteveldt, N. (2025). Explore your brain: A randomized controlled trial into the effectiveness of a growth mindset intervention with psychosocial and psychophysiological components. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 280–302. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12572

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Implementation check of the psychophysiological component in the intervention group comparing theta/beta during neurofeedback exercises with resting baseline. Printed effect sizes included ηp2 = .26 and ηp2 = .23, both above the article's large-effect threshold: "these are all large effect sizes (>ηp2 = .14)"; students reported reading while attending to auditory feedback was too difficult.

> "The results showed that students were able to lower theta/beta index by deliberately increasing their focused attention during the line exercise, F(1, 203) = 28.18, p < .001, ηp2 = .12, during the neurons exercise, F(1, 202) = 71.31, p < .001, ηp2 = .26, during the free choice exercise at the end, F(1, 202) = 60.98, p < .001, ηp2 = .23, but not during the reading exercise, F(1, 203) = 1.59, p = .209, ηp2 = .01."

### Janssen 2025 (2)

Janssen, T. W. P., & van Atteveldt, N. (2025). Explore your brain: A randomized controlled trial into the effectiveness of a growth mindset intervention with psychosocial and psychophysiological components. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 280–302. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12572

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational check within the same intervention group after lesson 3, relating averaged objective control (theta/beta change from baseline on working exercises) to self-reported subjective control on a 0–100 scale. The printed correlation was r = −.420 (large by the r coding). "91.7% indicated they were more convinced that they could influence their own brain after lesson 3".

> "Average objective and subjective control were strongly correlated, r(201) = −.420, p < .001, meaning that having more objective control was also experienced that way. Finally, 91.7% indicated they were more convinced that they could influence their own brain after lesson 3."
