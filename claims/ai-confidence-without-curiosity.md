## Evidence

### Morris 2026

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Survey comparison of confidence in explaining concepts across groups in the remote graph-theory study. The article attributes the pattern to "the consistency and factual nature of the AI support."

> "AI participants (M=4.30, SD=0.67) and LQ-PP participants (M=3.18, SD=0.75) reported the highest and lowest confidence respectively (t(21)=-3.62, p=.002, d=-1.56)."

### Morris 2026 (2)

Morris, C., & Maes, P. (2026). When Peers Outperform AI (and When They Don't): Interaction Quality Over Modality. [doi:10.22318/cscl2026.989526](https://doi.org/10.22318/cscl2026.989526)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Between-group t-test on the curiosity survey factor. LQ-PP fell between the extremes (M=3.36), which the authors read as even struggling peer interactions generating more curiosity than AI.

> "HQ-PP participants (M=4.00, SD=0.91) significantly outperformed AI participants (M=3.10, SD=0.99; t(23)=2.16, p=.043, d=1.00)."
