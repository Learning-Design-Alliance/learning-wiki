# Gender effects on e-Learning use ran in opposite directions across measures: females used live chats with colleagues more (p < 0.05) but were less likely than males to write IAs (Coef.=-0.49) or upload assignments (Coef.=-0.66)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` More females than males used the platform for live chats with colleague students, yet being female negatively associated with writing IAs and uploading assignments. [→ Alhassan Robert Kaba 2021](#alhassan-robert-kaba-2021)

## Evidence

### Alhassan Robert Kaba 2021

Alhassan Robert Kaba, Ayanore Martin Amogre, Diekuu John-Bosco, Prempeh Emmanuel B. A., Donkor Ernestina Safoa. (2021). Leveraging e-Learning technology to enhance pre-service training for healthcare trainees in Ghana: evidence from a pilot project and pointers to policy reforms. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-021-07224-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate probit regression from the same 363-respondent survey. "being female did not positively associate" with writing IAs or uploading assignments, while the descriptive cross-tabulation showed females used live chats with colleagues more (p < 0.05).

> "The results of the regression analysis showed that being female did not positively associate with students’ likelihood of using the e-Learning platform for writing IAs ([Coef.=-0.49, (95 % CI=-0.82, -1.5), p < 0.05]) and uploading class assignments ([Coef.=-0.66 (95 % CI=-1.0, -0.30, p < 0.05])"
