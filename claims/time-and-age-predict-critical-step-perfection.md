## Evidence

### Counihan H 2012

Counihan H, Harvey SA, Sekeseke-Chinyama M, Hamainza B, Banda R, Malambo T, Masaninga F, Bell D. (2012). Community Health Workers Use Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) Safely and Accurately: Results of a Longitudinal Study in Zambia. The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene. https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.2012.11-0800

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

GEE logistic regression on panel data identified factors associated with performing all critical steps. Adjusted odds ratios were 2.39 (95% CI 1.13–5.09) at 6 months and 6.42 (95% CI 2.73–15.10) at 12 months versus the 3-month referent.

> "At 6 months post-training, CHWs were 2.4 times more likely to perform all critical steps correctly compared with the 3-month assessment (P = 0.02). At 12 months, they were 6.4 times more likely to perform all critical steps correctly compared with the 3-month assessment (P < 0.001)."

### Counihan H 2012 (2)

Counihan H, Harvey SA, Sekeseke-Chinyama M, Hamainza B, Banda R, Malambo T, Masaninga F, Bell D. (2012). Community Health Workers Use Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) Safely and Accurately: Results of a Longitudinal Study in Zambia. The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene. https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.2012.11-0800

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

The same GEE logistic regression model assessed age effects on perfect critical-step performance. CHWs aged 50 and above had an adjusted odds ratio of 0.38 (95% CI 0.15–0.98) versus those 40 and under.

> "Compared with those 40 and under, CHWs 50 years of age and older were 62% less likely to score 100% on critical steps (P = 0.04)."
