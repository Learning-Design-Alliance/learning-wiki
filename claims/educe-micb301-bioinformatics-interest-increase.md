## Evidence

### Dill-McFarland KA 2021

Dill-McFarland KA, König SG, Mazel F, Oliver DC, McEwen LM, Hong KY, Hallam SJ. (2021). An integrated, modular approach to data science education in microbiology. PLoS Computational Biology. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008661

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Matched pre/post student survey analysis of the first EDUCE course, MICB 301 (2017–2018), using Monte Carlo symmetry tests for paired contingency tables. The article reports "a significant transition from medium to high interest" in bioinformatics (FDR P = 1.82E-2); no effect size is printed. Surveys carried 1% extra credit and ethics approval BREB H17-02416. With no comparison group, the change is observed, not attributed: the authors write that further analyses are needed to determine "if (2) EDUCE curriculum is truly causative of these outcomes."

> "Preliminary analysis of the first EDUCE course, MICB 301, from 2017 to 2018 revealed that student self-reported interest in “bioinformatics” increased with a significant transition from medium to high interest (Monte Carlo false discovery rate [FDR] P = 1.82E-2, Fig 4)."
