## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that prior studies showing no interruption effect used questions answerable from a single line, so success could rely solely on recognition. [→ Foroughi 2015](#foroughi-2015)

## Evidence

### Foroughi 2015

Foroughi, C. K., Werner, N. E., Barragán, D., & Boehm-Davis, D. A. (2015). Interruptions Disrupt Reading Comprehension. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/xge0000074

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Theoretical argument in the introduction: the authors analyze the questions used by Glanzer and colleagues and Oulasvirta and Saariluoma, arguing that "Recognition is often considered a single, automatic process that relies on a familiarity-based decision" and that at best recognition is a crude index of comprehension. No new data are reported for this claim.

> "every question asked in these studies could be answered by reading one single line from a passage. For example, the answer to the question “What is one ingredient in the poisonous compounds found on Jupiter?” came from this line"
