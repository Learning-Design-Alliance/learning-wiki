# Blended learning with online discussion seminars yields higher posttest scores in dermatology than face-to-face teaching alone for second-year medical students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who participated in online discussions associated with face-to-face activities (blended learning) had significantly higher posttest scores than those who only participated in classes (9.0±0.8 vs 7.75±1.8, p<0.01). [→ Silva Cristiana Silveira 2011](#silva-cristiana-silveira-2011)

## Evidence

### Silva Cristiana Silveira 2011

Silva Cristiana Silveira, Souza Murilo Barreto, Filho Roberto Silveira Silva, de Medeiros Luciana Molina, Criado Paulo Ricardo. (2011). E-learning program for medical students in dermatology. Clinics. https://doi.org/10.1590/S1807-59322011000400016

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective quasi-experimental study of 44 second-year medical students at the University of Technology and Science, Salvador, Brazil (20 control, 24 online), with identical content and course duration but non-concurrent semester groups. The blended group outperformed the control group on the posttest, "significantly higher posttest scores (9.0±0.8)".

> "Students who participated in online discussions associated with face-to-face activities (blended learning) had significantly higher posttest scores (9.0±0.8) than those who only participated in classes (7.75±1.8, p <0.01)."
