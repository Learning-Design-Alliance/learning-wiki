## Evidence

### David Gunawan 2022

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian first-order stochastic dominance comparison of the indigenous and non-indigenous HILDA education distributions for 2001, 2006, 2014 and 2017, providing "strong evidence that the education distribution for the non-indigenous population dominates that for the indigenous population" with no evidence of narrowing.

> "The FSD probabilities in Table 5 provide strong evidence that the education distribution for the non-indigenous population dominates that for the indigenous population, and there is no evidence that the gap has narrowed."

### David Gunawan 2022 (2)

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of indigenous and non-indigenous headcount indices in Table 4 shows both declined over time, but "the gap between them has not narrowed", with the difference rising "from 0.11 in 2001 to 0.15 in 2017".

> "we find that the indices for both populations have declined over time , but the gap between them has not narrowed. There has been a slight increase in the difference from 0.11 in 2001 to 0.15 in 2017."

### David Gunawan 2022 (3)

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Restricted FSD over the two lowest education categories strengthens the dominance result: restricted dominance probabilities for the non-indigenous population are "all greater than 0.98" across the four years.

> "The same probabilities for restricted dominance over the first two categories are higher; they are all greater than 0.98."
