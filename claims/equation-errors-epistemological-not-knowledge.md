## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The author reports, from classroom experience, that students frequently replace the constant-rate average equation with a difference and hold strongly to the incorrect equation, indicating reliance on memorization rather than sense-making. [→ Redish 2020](#redish-2020)

## Evidence

### Redish 2020

Redish, E. F. (2020). Using math in physics: 3. Anchor equations. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2011.12761](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.12761)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author's anecdotal classroom observation in a paper arguing for conceptual equation use; no systematic data are reported. He adds that students often lack the epistemological resource that 'If it doesn’t make sense, the equation I used must be wrong.'

> "Students also get this wrong, replacing it by the difference extraordinarily often. When they do this, it’s a clear signal they are relying on memorization and not sense-making (not making the blend)."
