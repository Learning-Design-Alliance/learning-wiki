# No statistically significant differences in moral competence between online and paper-based test administration

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither study field showed a statistically significant difference in MCT C-scores between online and paper-based administration, and the difference in effect sizes between fields was not significant. [→ Zielina M 2025](#zielina-m-2025)

## Evidence

### Zielina M 2025

Zielina M, Pinkasová T, Dostál D, Ivanová K, Macháček M, Matějek J, Řebíková B, Kuchař J, Škoda J, Pospíšilová K, Volejníčková RM, Doležal A. (2025). Assessing moral competence in medical and psychology students: effects on anxiety and test duration in online versus paper-based testing. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08237-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-effects regression comparing MCT C-scores by administration format (online n = 264, paper-based n = 453) with quasi-random assignment. Neither within-field format effect was significant (p = 0.409 and p = 0.423), nor was the between-field difference in format effects (p = 0.266).

> "Psychology students achieved slightly higher scores in the online condition, d = 0.17 (small effect), t(708.72) = 0.83, p = 0.409, whereas medical students performed slightly better in the paper-based condition, d = −0.08 (small effect), t(232.07) = −0.80, p = 0.423. However, the difference in effect sizes was not statistically significant, t(670.45) = −1.11, p = 0.266"
