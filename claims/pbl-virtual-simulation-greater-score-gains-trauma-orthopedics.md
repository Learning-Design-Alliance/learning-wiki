# In the same RCT, the experimental group showed greater score gains than the control group in both written examination (22.24 ± 5.20 vs. 10.64 ± 5.96) and practical operation (21.80 ± 4.71 vs. 12.12 ± 4.79) outcomes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Greater improvements were observed in the experimental group than in the control group after teaching, with significantly larger gains in both written examination and practical operation scores. [→ Xue Gang 2026](#xue-gang-2026)

## Evidence

### Xue Gang 2026

Xue Gang, Zhang Xin, Ji Shengyi, Qi Jin. (2026). Integrating problem-based learning with virtual simulation in trauma orthopedic education: effects on learning outcomes and student satisfaction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09558-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group before-to-after score gains in the same assessor-blinded RCT of 50 clinical medical students, outlined in Table 1 and Fig. 4. The experimental group's gain in written examination scores was "22.24 ± 5.20" versus "10.64 ± 5.96" in the control group, and its gain in practical operation scores was "21.80 ± 4.71" versus "12.12 ± 4.79", both with P < 0.01. The article reports Cohen's d = 2.07 and d = 2.04 for these gains in the Results section.

> "Greater improvements were observed in the experimental group than in the control group after teaching. The gain in written examination scores was 22.24 ± 5.20 in the experimental group and 10.64 ± 5.96 in the control group (mean difference 11.60, 95% CI 8.42 to 14.78, P < 0.01). The gain in practical operation scores was 21.80 ± 4.71 and 12.12 ± 4.79, respectively ( mean difference 9.68, 95% CI 6.98 to 12.38, P < 0.01)"
