## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 73% of MLA students obtained medium and high scores on mathematical understanding versus 66.6% in the conventional class. [→ Maya and Sumarmo 2011](#maya-and-sumarmo-2011)
`q2 i?` On proving ability the pattern reversed: 43.3% of CNV students obtained medium and high scores versus fewer in the MLA class. [→ Maya and Sumarmo 2011](#maya-and-sumarmo-2011)

## Evidence

### Maya and Sumarmo 2011

Rippi Maya, Utari Sumarmo. (2011). Mathematical Understanding and Proving Abilities: Experiment With Undergraduate Student By Using Modified Moore Learning Approach. IndoMS. J.M.E, Vol. 2 No. 2, pp. 231-250. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1078509

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Score-classification analysis (Table 6) of the 56-student experiment. The authors interpret that MLA was little more effective on MUA while CNV was little more effective on MPA; these are descriptive percentages with no test reported.

> "number of students who obtain medium and hig scores on MLA class (73 %) was higher than those number students in CNV class (66,6%). However on students’ MPA, number of students who obtained medium and high scores on CNV (43,3%) was higher than those number of students in MLA class"
