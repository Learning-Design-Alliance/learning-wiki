# Children demonstrate knowledge of others' cognitive processes by assessing peers' comprehension and identifying others' cognitive inadequacies during group work

## Evidence

### Ebru Aydın 2022

Ebru Aydın, Çağlayan Dinçer. (2022). “I did it wrong, but i know it”: Young children’s metacognitive knowledge expressions during peer interactions in math activities. Thinking Skills and Creativity 45 (2022) 101104. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsc.2022.101104

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Episode from the “Shapes on our desk” area activity (Table 6): Dennis noted a child in another group "covered it quickly," assessed her comprehension, then corrected his own mistake; Michael praised him. The article reads this as knowledge of others' cognitive activities.

> "In this example, it is seen that the children had knowledge of the cognitive activities of others in the working process and reflected on it in group interactions."

### Ebru Aydın 2022 (2)

Ebru Aydın, Çağlayan Dinçer. (2022). “I did it wrong, but i know it”: Young children’s metacognitive knowledge expressions during peer interactions in math activities. Thinking Skills and Creativity 45 (2022) 101104. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsc.2022.101104

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Episode from the “Cylinders” volume activity (Table 10): Dennis said filling was easy but counting too many beans would defeat anyone. The authors interpret this as expressing knowledge of others' cognitive inadequacy.

> "When these expressions of Dennis are examined, we see that by taking conservation principles into account in the cognitive process he could make comparisons to identify the differences between the cylinders."
