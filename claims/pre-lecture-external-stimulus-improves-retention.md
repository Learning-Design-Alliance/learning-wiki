# Exposing college students to a topic-relevant 90-second external stimulus before a lecture significantly improves immediate information retention compared with a neutral control

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students exposed to a 90-second external stimulus before a lecture retained significantly more lecture information than students exposed to roll call, with a small-to-medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.35). [→ Rosegard 2013](#rosegard-2013)

## Evidence

### Rosegard 2013

Rosegard, E., & Wilson, J. (2013). Capturing students' attention: An empirical study. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 13, No. 5, December 2013. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1017063

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment over four years with 846 students in 16 sections of a general education course at a large urban university; the arousal group (n=434) heard a poem, game, or puzzle, the control group (n=412) heard roll call, then both heard the same 30-minute lecture and took a 15-point exam. The t-test found "a significant difference in exam scores measuring information retention" between the arousal (M=13.36) and no-arousal (M=12.85) conditions. The article reports Cohen's d = 0.35, between small (.2) and medium (.5).

> "An independent-samples t-test found a significant difference in exam scores measuring information retention between arousal (M=13.36, SD=1.5) and no-arousal (M=12.85, SD=1.4) conditions; t (844)=5.20, p < .001."
