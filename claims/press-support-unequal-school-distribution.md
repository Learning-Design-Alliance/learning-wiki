# Integrated, small, wealthier, and higher-achieving Chicago schools are far more likely to combine strong press and strong support than large, poor, racially isolated, low-achieving schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Small and medium schools were over three times as likely as large schools to combine strong press and support. [→ Valerie E. Lee 1999](#valerie-e-lee-1999)
`q2 i?` Racially integrated schools were about three times as likely as predominantly African-American, Hispanic, or minority schools to have both characteristics. [→ Valerie E. Lee 1999 (2)](#valerie-e-lee-1999-2)

## Evidence

### Valerie E. Lee 1999

Valerie E. Lee, Julia B. Smith, Tamara E. Perry, Mark A. Smylie. (1999). Social Support, Academic Press, and Student Achievement: A View from the Middle Grades in Chicago. Chicago Annenberg Research Project, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/social-support-academic-press-and-student-achievement-view-middle-grades-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive grouping of Chicago K-8 and middle schools by size, racial/ethnic composition, low-income concentration, and 1997 achievement (Figure 5). Large schools (700+ students) showed 7% with the combination versus 25% and 22% for small and medium schools.

> "The proportions of small and medium-sized schools with strong press and strong support are over three times as great as the proportion of large schools with this combination."

### Valerie E. Lee 1999 (2)

Valerie E. Lee, Julia B. Smith, Tamara E. Perry, Mark A. Smylie. (1999). Social Support, Academic Press, and Student Achievement: A View from the Middle Grades in Chicago. Chicago Annenberg Research Project, Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/social-support-academic-press-and-student-achievement-view-middle-grades-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same descriptive analysis (Figure 5): integrated schools (30%+ white) showed 33% with the combination versus 11-14% for racially isolated schools; least disadvantaged schools showed 46% versus 11% for the poorest.

> "the proportion of racially and ethni- cally integrated schools with both strong press and strong support is three times greater than the pro- portions of predominantly Afri- can-American, predominantly Hispanic, and predominantly mi- nority schools with both charac- teristics present."
