## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In observed peer-led groups, some peer facilitators had question and response rates nearly indistinguishable from students, while others engaged in many more talk-turns and question turns than students. [→ Chai A. 2019](#chai-a-2019)

## Evidence

### Chai A. 2019

Chai A., Le J. P., Lee A. S., Lo S. M. (2019). Applying Graph Theory to Examine the Dynamics of Student Discussions in Small-Group Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education 18(2):ar29. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-11-0222

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Observational analysis of four peer-led introductory biology groups in the third data-collection iteration, using questions and responses per hour and normalized talk ratios. The article reports group A's facilitator at a normalized talk ratio of 1.36 versus group D's at 3.08, with the pattern that "in groups A and B, the peer facilitators behaved similarly to the students."

> "in groups A and B, the peer facilitators behaved similarly to the students, whereas in groups C and D, the peer facilitator behaved more like a traditional classroom authority figure."
