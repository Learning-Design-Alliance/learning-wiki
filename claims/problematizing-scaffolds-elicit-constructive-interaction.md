# Problematizing scaffolds elicit constructive, interaction-embedded metacognitive activities, whereas structuring scaffolds elicit application of examples

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Discourse excerpts show structuring scaffolds followed by implementation or elaboration of the example, and problematizing scaffolds triggering chains of co-constructed metacognitive activities. [→ Molenaar 2011](#molenaar-2011)

## Evidence

### Molenaar 2011

Molenaar, I., Chiu, M. M., Sleegers, P., & van Boxtel, C. (2011). Scaffolding of small groups' metacognitive activities with an avatar. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 6, 601-624. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9130-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative discourse analysis of two representative excerpts of triad responses to scaffolds; illustrative, not statistical, so impact is null. The authors call the difference qualitatively different student involvement.

> "A structuring scaffold is typically followed by either the implementation of the scaffold example or a group discussion elaborating on the example. On the other hand, problematizing scaffolds elicit student activities, leading the group to elaborate, share knowledge, resolve a conflict or co-construct new metacognitive activities."
