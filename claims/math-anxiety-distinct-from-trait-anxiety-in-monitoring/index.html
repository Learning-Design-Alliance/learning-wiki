Evidence¶

Scheibe 2022¶

Scheibe, D. A., Fitzsimmons, C. J., Mielicki, M. K., Taber, J. M., Sidney, P. G., Coifman, K., & Thompson, C. A. (2022). Confidence in COVID problem solving: What factors predict adults’ item-level metacognitive judgments on health-related math problems before and after an educational intervention? Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09300-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Hierarchical linear regressions on posttest monitoring judgments controlling for gender, condition, pretest judgments, and trait anxiety. Adding math anxiety improved fit on all three posttest problems (all ΔR2 = 0.01); "β’s between -0.08 and 0.10; all p’s < 0.01".

"math anxiety remained a significant predictor of monitoring judgments (β’s between -0.08 and 0.10; all p’s < 0.01) above and beyond trait anxiety"

Related Claims¶

  • Adults who answered health math problems correctly reported higher monitoring judgments than those who answered incorrectly, with larger differences at posttest than pretest — related
  • Men reported higher monitoring judgments on health math problems than women, even accounting for performance accuracy — related
  • Adults randomly assigned to a worked-example educational intervention made higher post-intervention monitoring judgments on health math problems than control participants — related
  • GenAI literacy dimensions predict critical monitoring beyond traits, use frequency, and perceptions, with critical-ethical awareness the strongest predictor — related
  • Math self-efficacy (first four SNS items) was the only variable significantly predicting monitoring judgments on all four health math problems — related
  • Math anxiety is not significantly correlated with trait anxiety in young children — related
  • Math anxiety has both a direct negative effect and a WM-mediated indirect effect on children's verbal Math Reasoning, but only an indirect effect on Numerical Operations — related
  • Math anxiety has a small-to-moderate negative correlation with math achievement (r = −.28) in a meta-analysis of correlations from 223 studies — related
