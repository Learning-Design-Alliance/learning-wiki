# Lower household income and caregiver unemployment show the strongest associations with poorer parent-rated academic performance in children with CKD

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children whose caregivers had lower household income had lower adjusted odds of average-or-above numeracy (aOR 0.46) and literacy (aOR 0.53) performance; unemployed caregivers' children had aOR 0.52 (numeracy) and 0.44 (literacy). [→ Khalid Rabia 2022](#khalid-rabia-2022)

## Evidence

### Khalid Rabia 2022

Khalid Rabia, Van Zwieten Anita, Kim Siah, Didsbury Madeleine, Francis Anna, Mctaggart Steven, Walker Amanda, Mackie Fiona E., Prestidge Chanel, Teixeira-Pinto Armando, Barton Belinda, Lorenzo Jennifer, Lah Suncica, Howard Kirsten, Nassar Natasha, Au Eric, Tong Allison, Blazek Katrina, Craig Jonathan C., Wong Germaine. (2022). Association between socioeconomic status and academic performance in children and adolescents with chronic kidney disease. Pediatric Nephrology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00467-022-05515-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable logistic regressions on the KCAD cross-sectional cohort (N=377), one model per SES exposure. Lower income showed "0.46 (0.26–0.80)" for numeracy and unemployment "0.44 (0.26–0.73)" for literacy, the strongest associations among the five SES measures.

> "adjusted ORs (95%CI) for average or above average academic performance among children whose caregivers reported lower education level, lower household income, not being in paid employment, poorer financial status, and lack of home ownership were (i) 0.71 (0.44–1.15), 0.46 (0.26–0.80), 0.52 (0.32–0.85), 0.68 (0.41–1.12), and 0.93 (0.53–1.64), respectively, for numeracy, and (ii) 0.75 (0.45–1.23), 0.53 (0.30–0.93), 0.44 (0.26–0.73), 0.59 (0.35–1.00), and 1.00 (0.56–1.79), respectively, for literacy."
