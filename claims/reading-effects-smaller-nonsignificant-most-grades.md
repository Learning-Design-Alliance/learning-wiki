## Evidence

### King 2020

King, G., & Jensen, N. (2020). Looking Back: How Prior-Year Attendance Impacts Starting Achievement. (The Center for School and Student Progress at NWEA Working Paper). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/looking-back-how-prior-year-attendance-impacts-starting-achievement/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same fixed-effects models estimated for reading; the article reports effects "much smaller in magnitude and non-significant in most grade levels" but notes reading effect sizes for highly and chronically absent students were similar to math, suggesting a power limitation.

> "The effects of chronic absenteeism in reading are much smaller in magnitude and non-significant in most grade levels. However, there is some evidence the lack of significance for chronically absent students in reading may be an issue of power in the sample of chronically absent students taking the reading assessment"
