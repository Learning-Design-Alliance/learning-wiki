# Explicit definitions drove the largest definition-recall gains: definition-trained groups recalled 62–63% of meanings (ds > 2) versus 36% for strategy-trained students (d = 0.95)

## Evidence

### Craigo L. 2017

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA on definition-recall posttests across treatment passages in the randomized experiment; the quote prints "d = 2.14" and "d = 2.19", and Bonferroni tests showed both definition-trained groups significantly outperformed the Strategies and Control groups but did not differ from each other.

> "When students worked with definitions, they recalled on average 62% to 63% of the words’ meanings and showed very large effect sizes of d = 2.14 (Strategies plus Definitions group) and d = 2.19 (Definitions group)."

### Craigo L. 2017 (2)

Craigo L., Ehri, L. C., & Hart, M. (2017). Teaching community college students strategies for learning unknown words as they read expository text. Higher Learning Research Communications, 7(1). https://doi.org/10.18870/hlrc.v7i1.350

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same ANCOVA on definition recall; the Strategies group never saw written definitions and had to induce meanings orally from contextual, morphological, and syntactic cues, yet still beat controls with the printed "d = 0.95".

> "When students used cues to induce word meanings, they recalled only 36% of the definitions on average but substantially more than the Control group mean of 17%, with a large effect size (d = 0.95)."
