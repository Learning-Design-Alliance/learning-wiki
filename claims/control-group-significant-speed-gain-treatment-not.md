# The control group improved significantly in completion speed from pretest to posttest while the treatment group showed no significant speed improvement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Within the control group, pre- to posttest completion time improved significantly (t(13) = 3.60, p < .025), whereas the treatment group's change in completion time was not significant (t(13) = 1.50, p > .025). [→ Letwinsky 2017](#letwinsky-2017)

## Evidence

### Letwinsky 2017

Letwinsky, K. M., & Berry, M. D. (2017). Technology Integration and the Effect on Mathematics Fact Fluency in the Middle East. Journal of International Education and Leadership, 7(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1153618

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures MANOVA with paired t-tests (Bonferroni-adjusted alpha of .025) on within-group pre/post changes. The article reports the treatment group's time change was not significant (t(13) = 1.50, p > .025); no effect size is printed, so impact is unquantified.

> "the control group reached statistical significance on pre- and posttest scores (t(13) = -3.41, p < .025) and also was significant on pre- and posttest time (t(13) = 3.60, p < .025)."
