# Academic self-handicapping is moderately negatively correlated with academic achievement in field settings (mean r = -.23)

## Subclaims

`q3 i2` Across 49 independent field-study effect sizes, self-handicapping correlates negatively with academic achievement at a medium magnitude (r = -.23). [→ Schwinger 2014](#schwinger-2014)

`q2 i?` Prior achievement is negatively associated with habitual self-handicapping: low-achieving students show a stronger tendency to use self-handicapping strategies, though some studies report small or nonsignificant correlations. [→ Schwinger 2021](#schwinger-2021)

## Evidence

### Schwinger 2014

Schwinger, M., Wirthwein, L., Lemmer, G., & Steinmayr, R. (2014, February 10). Academic Self-Handicapping and Achievement: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Educational Psychology. Advance online publication. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/a0035832

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 36 field studies with 49 independent samples and N = 25,550 school and university students. The pooled correlation was r = -.23 (p < .001), classified by the authors as a medium effect per Hattie's guidelines.

> "The random-effects model revealed a mean correlation between self-handicapping and achievement of r ⫽ ⫺.23 (p ⬍ .001; range: r ⫽ ⫺.46 to r ⫽ .02; 95% CI [⫺.25, ⫺.20]; k ⫽ 49)."

### Schwinger 2021

Schwinger, M., Trautner, M., Pütz, N., Fabianek, S., Lemmer, G., Lauermann, F., & Wirthwein, L. (2021). Why Do Students Use Strategies That Hurt Their Chances of Academic Success? A Meta-Analysis of Antecedents of Academic Self-Handicapping. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000706

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

The article's narrative review of prior-achievement evidence reports that "low-achieving students show a stronger tendency to use self-handicapping strategies" in school and university contexts, while noting some small or nonsignificant correlations. No pooled effect size for prior achievement is printed in the excerpt.

> "There is compelling evidence in both school and university contexts that low-achieving students show a stronger tendency to use self-handicapping strategies (Martin et al., 2003, 2013) but some studies report small or even nonsignificant correlations with achievement (McCrea et al., 2008)."
