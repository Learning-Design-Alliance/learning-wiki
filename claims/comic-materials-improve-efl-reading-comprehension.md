# Comic reading materials improve EFL reading comprehension more than general texts and pictures

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Adult female pre-intermediate EFL learners taught reading through comic materials scored significantly higher on a reading comprehension test than those taught through general texts and pictures. [→ Roozafzai 2012](#roozafzai-2012)

## Evidence

### Roozafzai 2012

Roozafzai, Z. S. (2012). The Role of Comic Reading Materials in Enhancing the Ability to Read in EFL. Journal of English Language Teaching. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1070200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experimental study with 60 adult female Iranian pre-intermediate EFL learners divided into experimental (comic materials) and control (general texts) groups over 20 sessions. An independent t-test showed the experimental group "significantly higher" reading scores with a mean difference of 1.4333.

> "the mean reading scores of students taught through comic materials (M=14.0667, SD=2.0331) is significantly higher (t=2.550, df= 20, two-tailed p=0.05) than that of students taught through general materials (M=12.6333, SD=2.3116)."
