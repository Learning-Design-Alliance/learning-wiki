### Toy 2022

Toy, S., Huh, D. D., Materi, J., Nanavati, J., & Schwengel, D. A. (2022). Use of neuroimaging to measure neurocognitive engagement in health professions education: a scoping review. Medical Education Online, 27(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.2016357

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's thematic synthesis of included technical-skill neuroimaging studies, most of which were non-experimental. It reports that "Experts show significantly lower activation, especially in the PFC, than do novices", with no effect size printed.

> "Overall, included research studies that used functional neuroimaging technology during technical skill performance indicated that experts and novices show distinct brain activation patterns. Experts show significantly lower activation, especially in the PFC, than do novices."

### Toy 2022 (2)

Toy, S., Huh, D. D., Materi, J., Nanavati, J., & Schwengel, D. A. (2022). Use of neuroimaging to measure neurocognitive engagement in health professions education: a scoping review. Medical Education Online, 27(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.2016357

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this single longitudinal-component finding to reference [51]; the review reports it as a hint that demonstrated competency in a controlled educational environment may not necessarily translate into complex clinical settings.

> "One study with a longitudinal component found that after repeated practice, novices still showed persistent engagement in the PFC even after they demonstrated expert level performance based on observational measures [51]."
