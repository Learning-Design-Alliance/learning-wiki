# The authors judge insufficient instructional intensity (dosage) as the primary factor behind the null result of learning strategies instruction in adult education

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors' post hoc judgment is that insufficient instructional intensity (dosage) was the primary factor affecting study outcomes, since strategy instruction requires explicit, intensive, and systematic delivery that adult education settings could not sustain. [→ Hock Michael F. 2011](#hock-michael-f-2011)

## Evidence

### Hock Michael F. 2011

Hock Michael F., Mellard Daryl F. (2011). Efficacy of learning strategies instruction in adult basic education. Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness, 4(2), 134-153. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2011.555291

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, not a tested result. They note prior research indicates adolescents may require as much as 90 hours to reach mastery of a new reading strategy, while this study delivered 19 to 34 planned hours, of which completers received 68% to 86%.

> "However, after reflecting on the data and our observations, our judgment is that insufficient instructional intensity (dosage) was the primary factor affecting study outcomes."
