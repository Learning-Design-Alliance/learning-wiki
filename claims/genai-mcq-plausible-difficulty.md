## Evidence

### Sze Kai Ping 2026

Sze Kai Ping, Lim Jia Qing, Loke Yng Miin, Ding Teck Yong Gabriel, Wee Wei Chieh, Chen Tongyuan, Wu Jingkai Lawrence, Bansal Vivek, Fong Qi Wei, Chow Minyang. (2026). Human-Edited Generative AI-Assisted Multiple-Choice Questions in Postgraduate Family Medicine: Blinded Cross-Sectional Comparative Psychometric Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/100179

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-level difficulty comparison from the blinded psychometric evaluation of 30 items per origin. The difference was not statistically significant; the printed statistic is Cohen d=−0.28, and higher difficulty index values indicate easier items.

> "The mean difficulty index was 0.64 (SD 0.26) for GenAI-assisted items and 0.70 (SD 0.22) for educator-crafted items, with a mean difference of −0.07 (95% CI −0.19 to 0.06; P=.29; Cohen d=−0.28)"

### Sze Kai Ping 2026 (2)

Sze Kai Ping, Lim Jia Qing, Loke Yng Miin, Ding Teck Yong Gabriel, Wee Wei Chieh, Chen Tongyuan, Wu Jingkai Lawrence, Bansal Vivek, Fong Qi Wei, Chow Minyang. (2026). Human-Edited Generative AI-Assisted Multiple-Choice Questions in Postgraduate Family Medicine: Blinded Cross-Sectional Comparative Psychometric Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/100179

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Difficulty-range classification against thresholds P≤.25, .25<P<.75, and P≥.75. More GenAI-assisted items were in the acceptable band and fewer were too easy; two items per set were too difficult, and one GenAI-assisted diabetes item had no response variance.

> "Eighteen GenAI-assisted items (60.0%) were classified as having appropriate difficulty, compared with 13 educator-crafted items (43.3%). Conversely, 10 GenAI-assisted items (33.3%) and 15 educator-crafted items (50.0%) were classified as too easy"
