## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because most energy relevant in biological systems is chemical energy, and bound atoms have negative energy relative to unbound atoms with a non-arbitrary zero point, negative energy must be accessible from the beginning of the energy unit. ## Evidence

### Benjamin W. Dreyfus 2013

Benjamin W. Dreyfus, Benjamin D. Geller, Julia Gouvea, Vashti Sawtelle, Chandra Turpen, and Edward F. Redish. (2013). Negative Energy: Why Interdisciplinary Physics Requires Multiple Ontologies. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.020)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument from the paper's Interdisciplinarity section about the NEXUS/Physics course design, in which electric potential energy models two interacting atoms. The authors argue that "Zero potential energy has a specific physical meaning here: the energy when the atoms are far enough apart that they are not interacting," so shifting the zero to keep all energies positive would have no limit.

> "The concept of negative energy is essential to this model of chemical bonds. When two atoms are bound, their energy is negative relative to the same atoms if they were unbound."
