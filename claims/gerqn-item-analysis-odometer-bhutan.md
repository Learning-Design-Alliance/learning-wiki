# Item analysis led to removal of the Odometer item and retention of the difficult but valued Bhutan item

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Odometer item was removed after experts judged it too hard, with fewer than 20% of students answering correctly and the only consistently sub-0.3 discrimination value. [→ Charlotte Zimmerman 2025](#charlotte-zimmerman-2025)
`q2 i?` The Bhutan item was kept despite high difficulty because experts valued students selecting more correct answer choices on this multiple-choice-multiple-response item. [→ Charlotte Zimmerman 2025 (2)](#charlotte-zimmerman-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2025

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2025). Assessing physics quantitative literacy development in algebra-based physics. [doi:10.1103/lnd6-pxyt](https://doi.org/10.1103/lnd6-pxyt)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classical test theory item analysis for v1.6 and v3.0 (N = 2778 and N = 1612 across six quarters) targeted difficulties between 0.2 and 0.8 and discrimination above 0.3; item difficulty figures appear in Fig. 5.

> "Finally, most of the experts we interviewed agreed that one item (“Odometer”) was too hard, and it was removed; this is supported by student data, with fewer than 20% of students answering “Odometer” correctly on v1.6 (Fig. 5), and “Odometer” being the only item to consistently have a discrimination value below 0.3 (Fig. 6)."

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2025 (2)

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2025). Assessing physics quantitative literacy development in algebra-based physics. [doi:10.1103/lnd6-pxyt](https://doi.org/10.1103/lnd6-pxyt)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

For the MCMR Bhutan item (N = 4231 across both versions in Fig. 7B), many students selected a subset of correct answers; experts agreed students' ability to reason about scaling in quantities not described by a familiar equation is a valued outcome, so the item was kept.

> "Figure 5 only shows the fraction of completely correct responses for MCMR items; Figure 7(B) suggests that many more students are choosing a subset of correct answers."
