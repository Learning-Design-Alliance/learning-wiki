# Video annotations outperform picture and text-only annotations for learning difficult vocabulary words

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2012 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students learning difficult English words with text-plus-video annotations scored significantly higher than those with text-plus-picture or text-only annotations. [→ Lin 2012](#lin-2012)

## Evidence

### Lin 2012

Lin, C.-C., & Tseng, Y.-F. (2012). Videos and animations for vocabulary learning: A study on difficult words. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 11(4), 346-356. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ989299

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A quasi-experiment with 88 seventh-graders in three intact classes randomly assigned to text-only, text-plus-picture, or text-plus-video annotations on a reading passage with ten difficult target words. Post hoc comparisons showed the "video group (M = 13.032) significantly outperformed the text group (M = 10.967) and the picture group (M = 10.759)"; no effect size was printed.

> "Of the differences among the three different types of annotation, post hoc comparisons showed that the video group (M = 13.032) significantly outperformed the text group (M = 10.967) and the picture group (M = 10.759)."
