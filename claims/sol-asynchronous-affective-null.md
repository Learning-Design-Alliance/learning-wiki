## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across eleven studies, the pooled effect of synchronous versus asynchronous online learning on affective outcomes was positive but not statistically significant, with the confidence interval overlapping zero. [→ Florence Martin 2021](#florence-martin-2021)

## Evidence

### Florence Martin 2021

Florence Martin, Ting Sun, Murat Turk, and Albert D. Ritzhaupt. (2021). A Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Synchronous Online Learning on Cognitive and Affective Educational Outcomes. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, Volume 22, Number 3. https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of eleven studies comparing synchronous with asynchronous online learning on affective outcomes (satisfaction, emotions, attitudes, motivation). The pooled g = 0.32 was marginal (p = .051); equivalence was not tested. Heterogeneity was significant (Q = 50.19, p < .001).

> "The results of the weighted average applying a random model revealed that SOL did not have a statistically significant effect on affective outcomes (g = 0.32, p = .051), with a 95% confidence interval of -0.001 to 0.641."
