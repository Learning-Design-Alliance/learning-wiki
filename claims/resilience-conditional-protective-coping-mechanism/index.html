Zhandire 2026¶

Zhandire, T. T., Bakare-Fatungase, O. D., & Mkhize, N. (2026). Mental health, resilience, and academic outcomes among university students: a global scoping review with particular attention to African and Global South contexts. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-05242-6

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's thematic synthesis (RQ3) of 33 studies examining resilience and academic outcomes reports that "Domain-specific academic resilience showed the most consistent positive associations with performance"; no pooled effect size is printed.

"Domain-specific academic resilience showed the most consistent positive associations with performance. Melaku and Bulcha [50] found that family and teacher support contributed to academic outcomes through resilience in Ethiopia. Self-efficacy emerged as a key linking mechanism across studies."

A separate theme within the same review synthesis reports coping as the modifiable mechanism: "Maladaptive coping including disengagement, self-blame, and substance use was associated with poorer outcomes across studies," with coping style predicting performance more strongly than stress levels alone in one cited study.

"Coping style emerged as the most consistently modifiable proximal factor. Maladaptive coping including disengagement, self-blame, and substance use was associated with poorer outcomes across studies."

Related Claims¶

  • Emerging reading and math resilience literatures suggest candidate resilience factors across cognitive, emotion/motivation, social/environmental, and neural levels, most not yet classified as promotive or protective — related
  • Positive coping styles show larger coefficients than negative coping styles, suggesting improving positive coping strategies may be more beneficial than minimising negative ones — related
  • A systematic review of 32 longitudinal studies identified 96 personal risk and protective factors for psychosocial transition success, consolidated into 16 salient elements across five higher-order domains — related
  • Trait resilience had a promotive role in academic performance for immigrant and non-immigrant students alike, and an additional protective effect mitigating the negative effect of school discrimination specifically among first- and second-generation immigrant students (reported by the editors, citing Huang et al.) — related
  • Resilience should be conceptualized as a process rather than an outcome of development, with environmental factors playing an essential role alongside individual risk and resilience factors (Parrila's discussion, as reported by the editors) — a broader claim this one bears on
