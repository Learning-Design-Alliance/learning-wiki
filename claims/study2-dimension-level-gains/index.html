Evidence¶

Kruse 2013¶

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

Study 2 planned comparisons on individual rubric dimensions. For evaluating evidence the PT section improved (d = .434) and No PT did not; the article similarly reports conclusion (d = .789) and completeness (d = .584) gains only in the PT section, and create gains in both sections.

"planned comparisons showed improvement in the PT section, F(1,51) = 6.054, p = .017, d = .434, but not in No PT section, F(1,51) = 0.628, p = .432."

Kruse 2013 (2)¶

Kruse, G. & Drews, D. (2013). Using Performance Tasks to Improve Quantitative Reasoning in an Introductory Mathematics Course. International Journal for the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, 7(2), Article 19. https://doi.org/10.20429/ijsotl.2013.070219

q3 · i? · causal · r1

For the alternatives dimension, Study 2 planned comparisons showed "no significant change for either" section, with no overall pre/post improvement and no section difference. The authors note this dimension received relatively little emphasis during the course.

"planned comparisons show no significant change for either the PT section, F(1,51) = 1.190, p = .280, or the No PT section, F(1,51) = 2.202, p = .144."
