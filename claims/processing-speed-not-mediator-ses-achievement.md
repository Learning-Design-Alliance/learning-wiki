## Evidence

### Mooney Kate E. 2024

Mooney Kate E., Cheung Rachael W., Blower Sarah L., Allen Richard J., Waterman Amanda. (2024). Do executive functions and processing speed mediate the relationship between socioeconomic status and educational achievement? Analysis of an observational birth cohort study. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-02243-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM Model 1 of the Born in Bradford estimation sample (n = 4201) compared EF and processing speed as simultaneous mediators; the bootstrapped processing-speed indirect effect was "not significant, at B=-0.002 [-0.006 to 0.001]" (p = 0.397). Higher SES was associated with better processing speed, but processing speed was not associated with educational achievement.

> "The path between socioeconomic status and educational achievement via processing speed was not significant, at B=-0.002 [-0.006 to 0.001]."
