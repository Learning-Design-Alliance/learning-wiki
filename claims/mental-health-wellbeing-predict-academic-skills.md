## Evidence

### Devine 2025

Devine, R. T., Gray, L., Edwards, M., Jess, M., Dempsey, C., Heng, J., Mehrotra, M., D'Souza, H., Fink, E., & Hughes, C. (2025). Changes in Children's Well-Being and Mental Health Across the Early School Years: Links With Academic and Social Competence. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001962

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Structural equation models regressing Time 2 academic skills (objective numeracy and reading plus teacher and caregiver ratings) onto initial levels and changes in well-being and mental health, controlling for gender, SES, verbal ability, executive function, and prosocial behavior.

> "Academic skills at Time 2 were significantly negatively associated with initial levels of caregiver-rated mental health but not with initial levels of caregiver- or child-rated well-being."

### Devine 2025 (2)

Devine, R. T., Gray, L., Edwards, M., Jess, M., Dempsey, C., Heng, J., Mehrotra, M., D'Souza, H., Fink, E., & Hughes, C. (2025). Changes in Children's Well-Being and Mental Health Across the Early School Years: Links With Academic and Social Competence. Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001962

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same SEM series: changes in child-rated well-being, but not changes in caregiver-rated mental health or caregiver-rated well-being, were uniquely associated with Time 2 academic skills over initial levels and confounds.

> "In contrast, increases in children's subjective well-being from Time 1 to Time 2 were positively linked with academic skills at Time 2."
