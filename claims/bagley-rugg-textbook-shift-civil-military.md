## Evidence

### J. C. B 1917

J. C. B. (1917). Recent Publications in History. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Documentary content analysis by Bagley and Rugg of twenty-three textbooks divided into four classes (1865–1874, 1881–1888, 1890–1904, 1906–1912), comparing space devoted to nine periods. The period 1783–1812 "increases from 6.4 per cent, in the earlier texts to 14.17 per cent, in the later ones."

> "The most striking change in treatment is found in the period 1783-1812 which increases from 6.4 per cent, in the earlier texts to 14.17 per cent, in the later ones."

### J. C. B 1917 (2)

J. C. B. (1917). Recent Publications in History. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.jstor.org

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

The reviewer's summary of the same textbook comparison across the nine-period scheme; the article reports significant decreases in space for colonial development, the Revolution and the Civil War, concluding "devote less space to military affairs." This is the review's interpretive summary of the space data.

> "It is clear that the tendency is to give greater attention to political, economic and social development and devote less space to military affairs."
