# Step-by-step explanations revealed one at a time, plus removed code auto-insertion and copy buttons, encouraged deeper engagement with generated explanations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the lab study, 8/9 users progressed through step-by-step explanations, clicking the 'next step' button between 3-12 times (M=6.4, SD=3.9), and four participants said they appreciated the breakdown compared to ChatGPT. [→ Tseng 2026](#tseng-2026)
`q2 i?` Participants reported that removing the copy button and auto-insertion made them more intentional, reading generated code line by line rather than copy-pasting without understanding. [→ Tseng 2026 (2)](#tseng-2026-2)

Log analysis from the lab study (n=9) using the updated Flowcode. The article states revealing one step at a time "can help users focus on individual parts and reduce cognitive load," and quotes P7 preferring steps over "walls of text."

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Participant interview quote from the lab study (P6) on the effect of removing the copy-to-clipboard button. The article frames this as friction that helps users "be more intentional with generated code."
