## Evidence

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Fall 2010 Mastering Physics log analysis plotting exam scores against average time per homework problem for medium-low and low aptitude students. The data "do not indicate any relationship", and the authors note only two medium-low students would have met the copying criterion.

> "These data, shown in Fig. 5, do not indicate any relationship between exam scores and the average time it took for students to complete each Mastering Physics problem."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (2)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Sensitivity analysis in which students with homework completion rates above 90% were removed and the line fits were recalculated for all five semesters (Fig. 6). The authors report that "the negative benefit results are essentially unchanged".

> "However, Fig. 6 shows that the negative benefit results are essentially unchanged when the data are re-analyzed in this fashion."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (3)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' conclusion from the two copying checks. The analysis "does not reveal any evidence that homework copying is the reason", with the stated alternative that time spent per problem may not identify copiers in this sample.

> "Consequently, this analysis does not reveal any evidence that homework copying is the reason for lower aptitude students getting negative benefit from completing homework."
