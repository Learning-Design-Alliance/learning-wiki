## Evidence

### Nam Ju Kim 2018

Nam Ju Kim, Brian R. Belland, & Andrew E. Walker. (2018). Effectiveness of Computer-Based Scaffolding in the Context of Problem-Based Learning for STEM Education: Bayesian Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9419-1

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the scaffolding strategies moderator (feedback, question prompts, hints, expert modeling, multiple strategies) in the Bayesian meta-analysis. Expert modeling and feedback effects approached a middle level.

> "the effect size for expert modeling ( g = 0.523, 95% CrI = 0.030 to 0.979) and feedback (g = 0.474, 95% CrI = 0.026 to 0.968) approached a middle level."

### Nam Ju Kim 2018 (2)

Nam Ju Kim, Brian R. Belland, & Andrew E. Walker. (2018). Effectiveness of Computer-Based Scaffolding in the Context of Problem-Based Learning for STEM Education: Bayesian Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9419-1

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Null subgroup result for question prompts within the strategies moderator. The discussion attributes the small effect to studies not considering students' varying prior knowledge and problem-solving skills, and notes multiple strategies in one system were counter-productive.

> "However, in the case of question prompts, there was no evident difference (g = 0.078, 95% CrI = 0.003 to 0.156) as compared to the control group in terms of the effect size."
