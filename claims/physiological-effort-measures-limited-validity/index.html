Evidence¶

Katharina Scheiter 2025¶

Katharina Scheiter. (2025). Commentary: How can We Come to Terms when Discussing the Role of Effort from the Perspective of Cognitive Load Theory and Theories of Self-regulated Learning? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10037-z

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The commentary reports, citing Ayres et al. (2021), that physiological measures (pupillometry, skin conductance, heart rate, brain measures) have some validity for data-driven effort but vary in sensitivity, with subjective ratings showing the best validity.

"Ayres et al. (2021) showed that while these measures have some validity with respect to measuring data-driven effort (i.e., consequences of varying task difficulty), they strongly vary in their ability to detect subtle changes in task difficulty. Subjective ratings appear to have the best validity."

Katharina Scheiter 2025 (2)¶

Katharina Scheiter. (2025). Commentary: How can We Come to Terms when Discussing the Role of Effort from the Perspective of Cognitive Load Theory and Theories of Self-regulated Learning? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10037-z

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

Theoretical argument in the commentary: the same physiological indicators would be used for two constructs that differ at the psychological level, and validity of subjective versus objective goal-driven effort measures may depend on task enjoyment.

"it seems unlikely that physiological measures can be used to distinguish between data-driven and goal-driven effort as all of them assess global processing activity"
