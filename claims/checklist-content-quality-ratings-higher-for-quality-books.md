# Trained raters given quality children's books rate their content qualities significantly higher than raters given non-quality books

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Experimental-group ratings of content qualities (characters, plot, language and narrative, message) were significantly higher than control-group ratings, all p < .001. [→ Çer and Sahin 2016](#cer-and-sahin-2016)

## Evidence

### Çer and Sahin 2016

Erkan Çer, Ertuğrul Şahin. (2016). Validity of a Checklist for the Design, Content, and Instructional Qualities of Children's Books. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.7, No.24. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1112922

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Independent samples t-tests (N = 120, 60 per group) on the checklist's content subscales. Table 3 reports, for example, character M = 34.07 (experimental) vs 11.02 (control), all p < .001; no effect sizes are printed.

> "As seen in Table 3, the independent samples t-test results showed that the rating scores in the experimental group in terms of the content qualities such as the plots, characters, messages, and use of language were higher than those in the control group."
