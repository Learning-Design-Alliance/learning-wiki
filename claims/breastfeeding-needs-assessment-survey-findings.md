## Evidence

### Pessa Valente Emanuelle 2021

Pessa Valente Emanuelle, Cattaneo Adriano, Sola Maria Vittoria, Travan Laura, Quintero Romero Sofia, Milinco Mariarosa, Decorti Cinzia, Giornelli Roberta, Braida Cinzia, Dalmin Patrizia, Giangreco Manuela, Ronfani Luca. (2021). Problem-based learning for in-service training on breastfeeding in Friuli Venezia Giulia, Italy. International Breastfeeding Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13006-021-00439-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous online survey of 343 of about 400 health professionals (142 midwives, 110 nurses, 56 pediatricians, 15 gynecologists, 20 other) in nine maternity hospitals and three local health authorities; the article reports "perceived breastmilk insufficiency, low weight gain and excessive crying were the most cited" infant difficulties.

> "Among common infant difficulties, perceived breastmilk insufficiency, low weight gain and excessive crying were the most cited. Difficulties in expressing or pumping breastmilk were prevalent among maternal challenges, followed by sore or cracked nipples."

### Pessa Valente Emanuelle 2021 (2)

Pessa Valente Emanuelle, Cattaneo Adriano, Sola Maria Vittoria, Travan Laura, Quintero Romero Sofia, Milinco Mariarosa, Decorti Cinzia, Giornelli Roberta, Braida Cinzia, Dalmin Patrizia, Giangreco Manuela, Ronfani Luca. (2021). Problem-based learning for in-service training on breastfeeding in Friuli Venezia Giulia, Italy. International Breastfeeding Journal. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13006-021-00439-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same 343-respondent survey on learning needs (Fig. 2): "The need for effective communication with mothers and families was the most cited", followed by support practices, understanding innate behaviours, and management of common difficulties; continuity of care during the first 1000 days was also prominent. Results fed the Dick and Carey needs-analysis phase.

> "The need for effective communication with mothers and families was the most cited, followed by the use of effective support practices, by a better understanding of innate maternal and neonatal behaviours that favour initiation of breastfeeding, and by the need to improve management of common maternal and infant difficulties."
