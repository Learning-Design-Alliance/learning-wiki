`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's analysis of instrument origins reports that "the instrument that has been adapted most frequently was originally developed in the 1980 s by Stone ( 1987)", with six publications referring to her Autism Knowledge Survey; the older Olley et al. (1981) scale was adapted twice.

> "However, the instrument that has been adapted most frequently was originally developed in the 1980 s by Stone ( 1987)."

### Nick Gerrit Hasche 2026 (2)

Nick Gerrit Hasche, Charlotte Dignath, Sumeja Mustafi, Mareike Kunter. (2026). An International Review of Assessment Tools for Teachers' Professional Competence in Teaching Autistic Children. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10176-x

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review documents adaptive instrument development: many studies based new instruments on existing ones, including three adaptations of Ruble et al.'s (2013) self-efficacy scale and a further development of Mavropoulou and Padeliadu (2000).

> "The Autism Self-Efficacy Scale for Teachers by Ruble et al. ( 2013), for example, has even been adapted three times by other authors (Catalano, 2018; Corona et al., 2017; Love et al., 2019), and the instrument measuring teachers' perceptions of autism by Mavropoulou and Padeliadou (2000) was adapted and further developed by Bjornsson et al. (2019)."
