Evidence¶

W. H. Winch 1910¶

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment in a Standard III municipal girls' class (average age 10 years 4 months) below the author's estimated saturation point in both functions, with group means of 14.5 rising to 17.9 (practised) versus 14.3 to 15.7 (non-practised).

"There certainly appears to be an advantage on the side of the practised group in the 2nd, 3rd, and the lowest sections, but it is somewhat disconcerting to see that the highest section of the practised group is worse than the corresponding section of the non-practised group."

Winch 1910¶

Winch, W. H. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy "transfer"? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0072557

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Matched-groups experiment in School I (1909), a school in a rather good neighbourhood whose arithmetical work was acknowledged low; the practised group worked six rule-sum exercises. Group B averaged 14.5 preliminary and 17.9 final against Group A's 14.3 and 15.7, yet "the highest section of the practised group is worse".

"There certainly appears to be an advantage on the side of the practised group in the 2nd, 3rd, and the lowest sections, but it is somewhat disconcerting to see that the highest section of the practised group is worse than the corresponding section of the non-practised group."
