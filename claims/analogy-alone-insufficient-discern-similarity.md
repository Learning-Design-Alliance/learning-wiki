## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2017

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effect of Scaffolding on Helping Introductory Physics Students Solve Quantitative Problems Involving Strong Alternative Conceptions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intervention 1 quiz data (Table 8, N=34 calculus, N=63 algebra): almost every student solved the reproduced tension problem correctly except minor mistakes such as confusing weight and mass, yet friction-problem performance was much lower.

> "the average score on the tension problem was 9.7 out of 10. On the friction problem, however, the average score was significantly lower (5.9 and 5.1 in the calculus- and algebra-based courses, respectively)."

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2017 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Effect of Scaffolding on Helping Introductory Physics Students Solve Quantitative Problems Involving Strong Alternative Conceptions. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05697)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Process examination of intervention 1 students' written work: some drew no FBD, some drew FBDs for only one problem, and some drew different FBDs (e.g., adding kinetic friction or tension to the friction problem). A separate count found "Only 24% of students explicitly mentioned the similarity between the tension in one problem and the frictional force in the other problem."

> "examining students' problem solving process suggests that only less than 50% of the students drew similar FBDs for the tension problem and the friction problem."
