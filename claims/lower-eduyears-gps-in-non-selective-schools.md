# Students in state non-selective schools have lower EduYears polygenic scores than students in grammar (d = 0.41) and private (d = 0.37) schools, with no significant difference between grammar and private schools

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Smith-Woolley 2018

Smith-Woolley, E., Pingault, J.-B., Selzam, S., Rimfeld, K., Krapohl, E., von Stumm, S., Asbury, K., Dale, P. S., Young, T., Allen, R., Kovas, Y. & Plomin, R. (2018). Differences in exam performance between pupils attending selective and non-selective schools mirror the genetic differences between them. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0019-8

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of a UK-representative genotyped TEDS sample of 4814 unrelated students, using a one-way ANOVA with planned contrasts on EduYears GPS by school type. The authors note the differences 'translate to more than a third of a standard deviation difference ( d = 0.41 and 0.37, respectively)'.

> "Non-selective state school students had significantly lower EduYears GPS scores compared to grammar school students ( t = 4.87, p < 0.001) and private school students ( t = 7.17, p < 0.001)."
