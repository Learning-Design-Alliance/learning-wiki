## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Javier Pulgar 2022

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, Iván Sánchez. (2022). Long-term collaboration with strong friendship ties improves academic performance in remote and hybrid teaching modalities in high school physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

OLS regressions of physics grades on collaboration variables for each activity in the 2021 hybrid quasi-experiment at one urban school. Table VI shows the strong-collaboration coefficient moving from −0.256* (Activity 1) through −0.056 to 0.393** (Activity 3).

> "The eﬀects of strong collaboration (i.e., working ties with friends) however, transition from negative at the end of SEM1 to positive by the end of the year (models 4, 5 and 6)."

### Javier Pulgar 2022 (2)

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, Iván Sánchez. (2022). Long-term collaboration with strong friendship ties improves academic performance in remote and hybrid teaching modalities in high school physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Models 1–3 of the same quasi-experimental regression set regressed grades on collaboration degree centrality: "the number of collaborative ties yields a negative effect over grades" early on, with the effect approaching zero by Activity 3.

> "The ﬁrst three models show that at the beginning of the academic year the number of collaborative ties yields a negative effect over grades, but by Activity 3 such effect got closer to zero."
