# Summative OSCE scores were numerically higher in the peer-led mock OSCE group but did not reach statistical significance

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The intervention group achieved higher summative OSCE scores (median 8.60 vs 8.30), a non-significant trend with a small-to-moderate effect size (Rosenthal's r = 0.277, p = 0.055). [→ Lima Leonardo Mateus de 2026](#lima-leonardo-mateus-de-2026)

## Evidence

### Lima Leonardo Mateus de 2026

Lima Leonardo Mateus de, Favarato Maria Helena, Tibério Iolanda Fátima Lopes Calvo. (2026). Empowering medical students: Peer-Led OSCE reduces anxiety and may enhance test performance. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0340407

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Summative OSCE scores (five stations, checklist-scored 0–10, faculty examiners blind to group assignment) compared between intervention (n = 28) and control (n = 39). Median difference 0.30 points, 95% CI [−0.000019, 0.600], Rosenthal's r = 0.277; the trend was "non-significant".

> "the intervention group achieved higher scores (8.60 ± 0.80, median ± IQR) compared to the control group (8.30 ± 0.90). Although the Mann-Whitney U test did not reach conventional statistical significance under a two-tailed assumption (U = 395, p = 0.055), there was a non-significant trend favoring the intervention group"
