## Evidence

### Ansari 2018

Ansari, A. (2018). The persistence of preschool effects from early childhood through adolescence. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000255

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Propensity-score-matched path models in the ECLS-K 1998 cohort compared preschool attendees with informal-care children on achievement from kindergarten through eighth grade. The kindergarten advantage was "significantly higher" with a printed effect size of E.S. = 0.20 standard deviation units [SD], with later effect sizes of 0.09–0.16 SDs.

> "children who attended preschool at age 4 scored significantly higher on kindergarten assessments of academic achievement (E.S. = 0.20 standard deviation units [SD]). Similar, albeit slightly smaller, academic benefits were documented through the end of eighth grade, with effect sizes ranging from 0.09–0.16 SDs."
