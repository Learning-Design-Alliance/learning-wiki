## Evidence

### Chang Yu-Che 2016

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of 295 paper-format and 604 computer-format EP-rated mini-CEXs in Table 1 (all aspects provided, p = 0.003). The formats had identical content; assessor seniority differed between format groups. Percentages printed; no effect size.

> "Feedback containing all three components was provided by 28.3 % of the mini-CEXs using the computer-based format and 19.0 % of mini-CEXs using the paper-based format (Table 1)."

### Chang Yu-Che 2016 (2)

Chang Yu-Che, Lee Ching-Hsing, Chen Chien-Kuang, Liao Chien-Hung, Ng Chip-Jin, Chen Jih-Chang, Chaou Chung-Hsien. (2016). Exploring the influence of gender, seniority and specialty on paper and computer-based feedback provision during mini-CEX assessments in a busy emergency department. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9682-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same format comparison, EPs using the computer format produced longer agreed action plans by "mean word count" and used that component more often; paper format yielded longer positive feedback (11.40 vs. 8.98 words, p < 0.001). Means and p-values printed, no effect size.

> "more feedback was provided using the computer-based format than paper-based format for agreed action plans (mean word count 12.8 ± 8.88 vs. 9.2 ± 5.1, p < 0.001; frequency of feedback component used 35.8 % vs. 28.1 %, p = 0.023)"
