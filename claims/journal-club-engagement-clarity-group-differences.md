## Evidence

### Almuzaini Ohood K. 2025

Almuzaini Ohood K., Wali Saad M., Harbi Maan H., Azhari Hala F. (2025). Evaluating the impact of a journal club activity on pharmacy students’ learning outcomes and professional skills development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07691-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fisher's Exact Test comparisons in the general experience domain by gender and course (Table 3); the printed proportions and p-values support the engagement and clarity contrasts. No effect sizes were printed.

> "Regarding the perceived engagement of the Journal Club activity, 51.6% of males rated it as very engaging compared to 15.2% of females (p < 0.001). For the clarity of instructions and guidelines, 63.3% of males rated them as very clear, whereas only 22.8% of females gave the same rating (p < 0.001)."

### Almuzaini Ohood K. 2025 (2)

Almuzaini Ohood K., Wali Saad M., Harbi Maan H., Azhari Hala F. (2025). Evaluating the impact of a journal club activity on pharmacy students’ learning outcomes and professional skills development. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07691-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive survey results on the paired-work item (162 valid responses due to 55 missing or invalid records); the pairing benefit difference by gender and course was not statistically significant (p = 0.225, p = 0.487).

> "Additionally, 78.3% of participants believed that working in pairs was beneficial to their learning experience. For those reporting positive beliefs about the benefits of working in pairs, the most common benefits attained were knowledge sharing and learning (46.7%) and collaboration and teamwork (29.0%, Table 3)."
