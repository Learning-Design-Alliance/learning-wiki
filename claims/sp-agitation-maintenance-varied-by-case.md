## Evidence

### Shenai Neeta MD 2024

Shenai Neeta MD, Fulmer Valerie, Gowl Catherine MFA, See Jordan MD, Peterson Ryan MD, Van Deusen Reed MD. (2024). A Verbal De-escalation Standardized Patient Workshop for Third- and Fourth-Year Medical Students. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11417

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

From the video review of 12 encounters, which documented the SP's level of agitation and how quickly they de-escalated. The inpatient case held agitation while "in the outpatient case, their level of agitation dropped much quicker."

> "The SPs maintained their level of agitation throughout the encounter during the inpatient case, but in the outpatient case, their level of agitation dropped much quicker."

### Shenai Neeta MD 2024 (2)

Shenai Neeta MD, Fulmer Valerie, Gowl Catherine MFA, See Jordan MD, Peterson Ryan MD, Van Deusen Reed MD. (2024). A Verbal De-escalation Standardized Patient Workshop for Third- and Fourth-Year Medical Students. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.11417

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Separate SP debriefing sessions gathered SP perspectives on improving case instruction. SPs reported "difficulty with maintaining a high level of agitation" and confusion about when to begin de-escalating; some were surprised by students conceding to inappropriate medication requests.

> "They reported difficulty with maintaining a high level of agitation throughout the case and were confused on the timing at which they should appropriately begin to reward the students’ skill and de-escalate their emotion."
