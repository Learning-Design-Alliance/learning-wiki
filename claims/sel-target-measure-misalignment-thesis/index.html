Evidence¶

Stephanie M. Jones 2017¶

Stephanie M. Jones, Sophie P. Barnes, Rebecca Bailey, and Emily J. Doolittle. (2017). Promoting Social and Emotional Competencies in Elementary School. The Future of Children, Vol. 27, No. 1, Spring 2017. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144815

q2 · i? · review · r2

The article's central conclusion from its review of 11 randomized-trial SEL interventions, mapping each program's targets against its measured outcomes. The review finds that "what appears to be variation in impacts may instead be an artifact" of target-measure misalignment. This is the authors' interpretive synthesis rather than a tested statistical result.

"Our review suggests that what appears to be variation in impacts may instead be an artifact of imprecise program targets misaligned with too-general outcome measures."

Stephanie M. Jones 2017 (2)¶

Stephanie M. Jones, Sophie P. Barnes, Rebecca Bailey, and Emily J. Doolittle. (2017). Promoting Social and Emotional Competencies in Elementary School. The Future of Children, Vol. 27, No. 1, Spring 2017. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1144815

q2 · i? · review · r2

Documented example of the misalignment pattern in the emotion domain: 40 percent of PATHS lessons focus on understanding and communicating emotions and RULER targets five key emotion skills, yet neither study included measures of these basic emotional skills. The article reports this as a review finding about measurement practice, with no effect sizes.

"Programs tend to target basic or fundamental emotion skills, such as emotion knowledge, emotion vocabulary, and emotion expression. But they measure more complex outcomes that build on or use these basic emotion skills."
