## Evidence

### Sten CW 2024

Steen CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pretest–posttest study of 100 social work and nursing students using a VSP intervention, with an online pretest–posttest survey including Brief Substance Abuse Attitudes Scale items. The review reports "no significant effect of VR training on attitudes"; equivalence was not tested.

> "This study found no significant effect of VR training on attitudes and was assessed as having a serious risk of bias."

### Sten CW 2024 (2)

Steen CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The same single-group pretest–posttest study of 100 social work and nursing students assessed perceived competence in screening, brief intervention, and referral to treatment via online survey. The review reports "a significant increase in perceived competence"; no effect size value is printed.

> "The study findings demonstrated a significant increase in perceived competence following the VSP intervention."
