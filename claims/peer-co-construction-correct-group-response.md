## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Impact of peer interaction on conceptual test performance. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07661](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07661)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of pair responses in the IG protocol (148 students, 74 pairs) on the 32-question CSEM, tabulated in Table II. The article reports "Co-construction was observed in 29% of the eligible cases", where co-construction means neither student alone was correct but the group was.

> "Table II displays the percentage of overall cases where neither, o ne, or both group mem- bers chose the correct response individually, and how their choices changed during the group work. Co-construction was observed in 29% of the eligible cases."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Impact of peer interaction on conceptual test performance. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07661](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07661)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article's summary of the co-construction analysis against a guessing baseline; Table IV shows that when both members had the same incorrect response the group was correct in 25% of cases, versus only 8% choosing a third distracter.

> "Students who individually chose an incorrect response were able to ﬁnd the correct respon se working as a group with a frequency that is roughly ten times higher than that predicted by random guessing."
