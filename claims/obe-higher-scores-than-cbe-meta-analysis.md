## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Mizori R 2025

Mizori R, Sadiq M, Ahmad MT, Siu A, Ahmad RR, Yang Z, et al. (2025). STEM exam performance: Open‐ versus closed‐book methods in the large language model era. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13839

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Random-effects meta-analysis of eight peer-reviewed studies (2013 onwards) of university STEM students, using standardised mean differences scaled 0–100. The review reports "an overall mean difference of 5.91 (95% CI: 2.79 to 9.04)" favouring OBEs; Figure 2a shows significance at p < 0.01.

> "The meta‐analysis revealed a notable increase in marks for OBEs compared to CBEs (see Figure 2a ), with an overall mean difference of 5.91 (95% CI: 2.79 to 9.04)."

### Mizori R 2025 (2)

Mizori R, Sadiq M, Ahmad MT, Siu A, Ahmad RR, Yang Z, et al. (2025). STEM exam performance: Open‐ versus closed‐book methods in the large language model era. The Clinical Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1111/tct.13839

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Heterogeneity assessment using I², Cochrane's Q and Tau statistics (REML) across the same eight studies. The review reports "an I 2 value of 97%", indicating the pooled effect rests on highly variable study outcomes.

> "There was substantial heterogeneity in the meta‐analysis results, indicated by an I 2 value of 97%. This reflects significant variability in the study outcomes."
