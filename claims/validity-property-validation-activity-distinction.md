# Validity is a property of tests, whereas validation is an activity, and the two should not be confused

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that validity is a property while validation is the research activity undertaken to find out whether a test has that property, and that most of the validity literature has addressed validation rather than validity. [→ Borsboom et al. 2004](#borsboom-et-al-2004)

## Evidence

### Borsboom et al. 2004

Denny Borsboom, Gideon J. Mellenbergh, and Jaap van Heerden. (2004). The Concept of Validity. Psychological Review. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.111.4.1061

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the Ontology Versus Epistemology section. The authors distinguish the property from the activity, adding that "Validity is about ontology; validation is about epistemology. The two should not be confused." No empirical data are offered; the support is conceptual argument.

> "These terms are sometimes used interchangeably in the literature, but they are not the same. This is clear because validity is a property, whereas validation is an activity."
