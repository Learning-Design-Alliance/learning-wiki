Evidence¶

Marieke de Wijse-van Heeswijk 2025¶

Marieke de Wijse-van Heeswijk, Etienne A. J. A. Rouwette, Sander V. Meijerink. (2025). The learning effects of first, second, and third order interventions in a rule-based and open simulation game. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09715-w

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Qualitative finding from the two-case study comparing an extreme rule-based SG and a positive open SG. The article reports participants "perceived the rule-based serious game as demanding" because learning and applying procedures and rules hindered effort on learning goals.

"Participants perceived the rule-based serious game as demanding, as learning and applying its procedures and rules often hindered them from investing their efforts in achieving their learning goals."

Related Claims¶

  • Struggling participants in the rule-based game initiated emergent facilitation — self-organized peer facilitation — which was fuelled by cognitive load, scope disturbances and high distance and supported first- and second-order learning — related
  • Extraneous cognitive load arising from limited scope and high distance in the rule-based game pushed the facilitator toward first- and second-order interventions instead of the third-order interventions the learning goal required — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In a rule-based simulation game with narrow scope and high distance, facilitator interventions were predominantly first- and second-order and participant learning was mainly first- and second-order — related
  • Increased perceptual fidelity fails to reliably improve serious game learning outcomes (the fidelity fallacy) — related
  • ARPG+ shifts cognitive load composition toward productive effort and reduces overload frequency in simulation — related
