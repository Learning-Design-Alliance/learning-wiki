## Evidence

### Sapci AH 2026

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). Development of Therapeutic Competencies in Health Care Students: Qualitative Focus Group Study Using 360-Degree Video and Virtual Reality Technology. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Focus group thematic analysis (n=12 students) of module 2, in which students observed conversations with families using 360-degree video and VR headsets. Students described observing communication styles with children versus adults as seeing theory applied in practice.

> "The overarching finding from this category showed that students experienced high learning outcomes from observing a conversation between a therapist and a family."

### Sapci AH 2026 (2)

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). Development of Therapeutic Competencies in Health Care Students: Qualitative Focus Group Study Using 360-Degree Video and Virtual Reality Technology. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the focus groups, students reported that the observed conversations gave a practical understanding of tailoring therapeutic techniques to different client groups, comparing the experience to watching a supervisor in an internship.

> "Students discussed how they observed different communication styles in conversations with children versus adults and how the therapist adapted their language and methods according to the client’s age and level of understanding."
