# Most teens at any given time chose the hanging out space, though staff reported a substantial number also engaged in geeking out activities

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kimberly Austin 2011

Kimberly Austin, Stacy B. Ehrlich, Cassidy Puckett, and Judi Singleton. (2011). Reimagining Learning, Literacies, and Libraries: A Snapshot of Year 1. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://youmediachicago.org

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Observational data from nine months of visits to the physical space. The most pronounced pattern was that the majority of teens at any given time were in the hanging out space, where "the majority of teens were playing video games, talking with friends, or checking Facebook."

> "Observations, for instance, note that the majority of teens were playing video games, talking with friends, or checking Facebook."

### Kimberly Austin 2011 (2)

Kimberly Austin, Stacy B. Ehrlich, Cassidy Puckett, and Judi Singleton. (2011). Reimagining Learning, Literacies, and Libraries: A Snapshot of Year 1. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://youmediachicago.org

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Staff reports gathered through interviews and observations indicated that many teens who spent time hanging out also attended workshops, created and posted digital media artifacts, and commented in the online space.

> "staff also reported that a substantial number of these “hanging out” teens also engaged in “geeking out” YOUmedia activities by attending workshops, messing around with tools, creating and posting digital media artifacts, and commenting in the online space."
