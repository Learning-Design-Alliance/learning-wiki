## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Group 2's improvement was significantly larger for individual sounds and sound clusters (segmental) than for suprasegmental aspects such as intonation and pitch. [→ Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna 2016](#yang-gao-and-barbara-e-hanna-2016)

## Evidence

### Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna 2016

Yang Gao and Barbara E. Hanna. (2016). Exploring optimal pronunciation teaching: Integrating instructional software into intermediate-level EFL classes in China. CALICO Journal. https://doi.org/10.1558/cj.v33i2.26054

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t test comparing score gains on the two aspect families for Group 2 (n = 20): segmental gain M = 0.475 vs suprasegmental M = 0.213, "t (19) = 3.18, p < 0.01, r = 0.59". The smallest mean gain was for intonation and pitch (M = 0.150).

> "A paired-samples t test on segmental and suprasegmental scores (Table 5) revealed participants made significantly greater progress on segmental (M = 0.475, SE = 0.29) than on suprasegmental aspects of pronunciation (M = 0.213, SE = 0.20) with a large effect, t (19) = 3.18, p < 0.01, r = 0.59."
