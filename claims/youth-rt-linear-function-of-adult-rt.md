### Kail 1991 (2)

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Figure 1 and Table 2 report fits of Equation 4 for 11 age-group data sets (3-4 through 14-year-olds); "uniformly excellent, with R2s > .90", with slopes (mi) greater than 1 in every age set.

> "The fits, shown in Figure 1 and described in Table 2, were uniformly excellent, with R2s > .90."

### Kail 1991 (3)

Robert Kail. (1991). Developmental Change in Speed of Processing During Childhood and Adolescence. Psychological Bulletin, 109(3), 490-501. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.109.3.490

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The article reports this study by Hale (1990), which tested 10-, 12-, and 15-year-olds and adults on four speeded tasks with eight mean RTs per age; correlations were "greater than .99 at all ages" and slopes approached 1 with age.

> "The correlations between youth and adult RTs were greater than .99 at all ages. Values of m, were 1.82,1.56, and 1.00 for 10-, 12-, and 15-year-olds, respectively, indicating a gradual approximation to adults' processing time."
