Evidence¶

Cousins 2019¶

Cousins, J. N., van Rijn, E., Ong, J. L., Wong, K. F., & Chee, M. W. L. (2019). Does splitting sleep improve long-term memory in chronically sleep deprived adolescents? npj Science of Learning 4:8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0047-z

q3 · i2 · causal · r?

Controlled laboratory-classroom experiment of incidental picture encoding (building/no-building judgments) at 14:45 on day 5 of restriction, with recognition tested after two 9 h recovery nights. The article reports "A′ scores were signiﬁcantly higher in the split sleep group" with t(56) = 2.505, p = 0.015, d = 0.66, and mean A′ of 0.74 versus 0.69.

"A′ scores were signiﬁcantly higher in the split sleep group, t(56) = 2.505, p = 0.015, Cohen's d = 0.66 (Table 2). These ﬁndings indicate a clear afternoon encoding advantage associated with a split sleep schedule."
