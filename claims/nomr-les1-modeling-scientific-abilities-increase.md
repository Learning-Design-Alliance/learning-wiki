## Evidence

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post code-frequency analysis of matched LES1 open-ended responses from 100-level and 200-level students, double-coded to >95% inter-rater agreement. Modeling and Scientific abilities frequencies rose; students "became more likely to indicate a belief that labs are meant to develop their Scientific abilities" and to develop their own models.

> "Comparing pre-post results, we find that the students in our study became more likely to indicate a belief that labs are meant to develop their Scientific abilities and give them opportunities to develop their own models ( Modeling)."

### Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia 2024 (2)

Jared P. Canright and Suzanne White Brahmia (2024). Modeling novel physics in virtual reality labs: An affective analysis of student learning. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same matched pre-post LES1 dataset, analyzed as code frequencies per population. Population-specific decreases were observed: the 100-level population "became less likely to cite Model testing as a purpose of in-class experiments," while the 200-level population became dramatically less likely to cite Supplemental learning.

> "The 100-level population became less likely to cite Model testing as a purpose of in-class experiments, while the 200-level population became dramatically less likely to cite Supplemental learning for the same."
