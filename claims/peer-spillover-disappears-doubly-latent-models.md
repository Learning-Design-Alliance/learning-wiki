# Positive class-level peer spillover effects on mathematics achievement fail to replicate once measurement error is controlled with doubly latent models

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a doubly latent multilevel model, the positive class-level compositional effect of average mathematics achievement on subsequent achievement was eliminated and became non-statistically significant for the USA and New Zealand. [→ Televantou 2023](#televantou-2023)

## Evidence

### Televantou 2023

Televantou, I., Marsh, H. W., Xu, K. M., Guo, J., & Dicke, T. (2023). Peer Spillover and Big-Fish-Little-Pond Effects with SIMS80: Revisiting a Historical Database Through the Lens of a Modern Methodological Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09816-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Re-analysis of SIMS80 longitudinal data (13,656 students in the USA, Canada, New Zealand) applying a multilevel latent variable framework. The doubly latent model estimates for the compositional effect were .040 (USA), .119 (Canada), and -.035 (New Zealand), none reaching significance for USA and New Zealand, so the "positive class-level compositional effect" was "eliminated."

> "the positive class-level compositional effect of average mathematics achievement detected in study 1 was eliminated. It became non-statistically significant for the two countries involved (USA, New Zealand)."
