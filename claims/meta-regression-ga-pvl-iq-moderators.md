## Evidence

### Twilhaar E Sabrina 2018

Twilhaar E Sabrina, de Kieviet Jorrit F, Aarnoudse-Moens Cornelieke SH, van Elburg Ruurd M, Oosterlaan Jaap. (2018). Academic performance of children born preterm: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Archives of Disease in Childhood. Fetal and Neonatal Edition. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2017-312916

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random effects meta-regression of gestational age on between-study variance in academic effect sizes; R2=0.39 (Q(1)=7.49, p=0.006), but "Additional analysis without this study showed non-significant results".

> "GA explained a significant proportion of variance (R2=0.39, Q(1)=7.49, p=0.006). Regression plots are shown in figure 2. One study,24 with a considerably lower mean GA (24.5 weeks) compared with the other studies, played a key role in this effect"

### Twilhaar E Sabrina 2018 (2)

Twilhaar E Sabrina, de Kieviet Jorrit F, Aarnoudse-Moens Cornelieke SH, van Elburg Ruurd M, Oosterlaan Jaap. (2018). Academic performance of children born preterm: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Archives of Disease in Childhood. Fetal and Neonatal Edition. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2017-312916

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression for PVL (R2=0.46, p=0.007) was driven by the same extremely preterm cohort with 16% PVL incidence; the difference in intelligence between preterm and controls explained 46% of variance (Q(1)=8.31, p=0.004).

> "Meta-regression showed a significant result for PVL (R2=0.46, Q(1)=7.33, p=0.007), but additional analysis without this study again was non-significant."
