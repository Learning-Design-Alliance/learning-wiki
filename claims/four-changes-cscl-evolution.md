## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors identify four changes over the past thirty years: collaborative pedagogies mainstreaming, collaboration technology becoming infrastructure, learning redefined to include collective-level learning, and collaboration shifting from small synchronous groups to large, partly asynchronous collectives. ## Evidence

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Editorial essay argument, not an empirical study. The authors assert, with cited background literature, that collaborative pedagogies are mainstream, platforms such as Moodle make technology "infrastructure", and "what counts as learning has changed" as large collectives learn as collectives.
