# Student difficulties using graphs in physics are often epistemological framing errors, not just transferred math difficulties

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Because physics requires combining abstract math and conceptual physics, many student difficulties with graphs are epistemological framing errors, such as seeing graphs as solutions rather than sense-making tools and treating the graph as math rather than physical information. [→ Edward F. Redish 2023](#edward-f-redish-2023)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2023

Edward F. Redish. (2023). Using math in physics: 6. Reading the physics in a graph. The Physics Teacher (series paper, dated 3/14/23). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2303.09589](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2303.09589)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

The article's challenges section argues from the author's experience and PER literature that framing — deciding what knowledge applies to the current problem — is the critical epistemological issue. Observed framing errors include sketching unlabeled axes with no scale marks. The author also argues the transfer framing is misleading, favoring conceptual blending.

> "Because we are asking students to combine knowledge from two distinct domains, abstract math and conceptual physics, many of the difficulties students encounter are epistemological — errors about the nature of the knowledge they are learning."
