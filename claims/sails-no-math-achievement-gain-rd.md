# The high school SAILS remedial math course did not improve math achievement more than a typical senior year math course

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Using a regression discontinuity design, students who completed SAILS showed no greater math achievement than students who took the typical senior year math course. [→ Thomas J. Kane 2021](#thomas-j-kane-2021)

## Evidence

### Thomas J. Kane 2021

Thomas J. Kane, Angela Boatman, Whitney Kozakowski, Christopher Bennett, Rachel Hitch, Dana Weisenfeld. (2021). Is College Remediation a Barrier or a Boost? Evidence from the Tennessee SAILS Program. Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, vol. 40, issue 3. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/is-college-remediation-a-barrier-or-a-boost-evidence-from-the-tennessee-sails-program

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Regression discontinuity analysis of the Tennessee SAILS policy, which waived college math remediation for students completing the computer-based high school course. The study finds "did not improve students' math achievement any more than the typical senior year math course." No effect size is printed.

> "Using a regression discontinuity design, we find that the high school remedial course did not improve students' math achievement any more than the typical senior year math course (although it did allow students to avoid the cost and delay of remedial math in college)."
