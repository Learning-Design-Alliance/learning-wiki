Evidence¶

Lucia Mason 2022¶

Lucia Mason, Angelica Ronconi, Sara Scrimin, Francesca Pazzaglia. (2022). Short-Term Exposure to Nature and Benefits for Students' Cognitive Performance: a Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09631-8

q3 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review following PRISMA of 14 studies (13 articles) selected from 285 database hits, covering students from elementary school to university with exposures of 10–90 minutes. The review reports that "in 12 out of the 14 studies, across educational levels, cognitive benefits emerge."

"The review shows that in 12 out of the 14 studies, across educational levels, cognitive benefits emerge in terms of directed attention restoration from mental fatigue due to contact with nature."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • In elementary school students, short-term green breaks restore attention and working memory, with effects on inhibition mixed — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In secondary school students, breaks with green views or walks in larger green spaces improve attention performance — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In university students, walks or sitting in natural settings after cognitive fatigue improve some attention and working memory measures, with null results for others — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Long-term nature exposure shows inconsistent effects on processing speed, positive in the greened schoolyard study but null in the classroom green wall study — related
  • Effects on sustained/selective attention are mixed across studies, with benefits in some green settings but no effect in others regardless of exposure duration — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
