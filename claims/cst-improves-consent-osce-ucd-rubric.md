## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Senior medical students receiving CST improved from a mean C grade (61%) to a B+ (71%) on the UCD OSCE consent assessment, a 10% increase with a printed effect size of 0.79, while the control group remained at a C grade. [→ McCarrick CA 2025](#mccarrick-ca-2025)

## Evidence

### McCarrick CA 2025

McCarrick CA, Moynihan A, McEntee PD, Boland PA, Donnelly S, Heneghan H, Cahill RA. (2025). Impact of simulation training on communication skills and informed consent practices in medical students- a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07671-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomised cluster-sampling trial of 122 students (61 per arm) in a UCD surgical module; blinded independent senior clinician scored observed simulated-patient consent OSCEs at baseline and after the intervention week. The printed effect size was "0.79, which is considered a medium to large effect for educational interventions"; the control group's effect size was 0.18.

> "the intervention group showed an improvement equivalent to a mean grade increase from a C ( 61%) to a B+ (71%), representing a 10% increase in assessment scores. In contrast, the control group maintained a mean C grade across both assessments"
