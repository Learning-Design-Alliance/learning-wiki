# In a pilot RCT, the TPR-MMF-based game "World + You - World" produced significantly higher interest/enjoyment and perceived competence than the explicit educational game NeuroQuest

## Evidence

### Hsu 2026

Hsu, T.-C., Zhang, Z., Chen, Z., Liu, Y., & Liu, Y. (2026). Designing More Engaging Serious Games to Support Students' Mental Health: A Pilot Study Based on A CBT-Informed Design Framework. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Small-sample randomized controlled trial: 28 first-year university students randomly assigned to play "World + You - World" (N=14) or NeuroQuest (N=14) for 20 minutes, then completed the IMI. Independent-samples t-tests showed the experimental group "significantly higher than the control group" on interest/enjoyment (d=1.73) and perceived competence (d=1.15).

> "the experimental group (E_Group) was significantly higher than the control group (C_Group) in the interest/enjoyment dimension (E_Group M=5.61, SD=0.44; C_Group M=4.78, SD=0.52; p<.001, Cohen's d=1.73) and also significantly higher in the perceived competence dimension (E_Group M=5.48, SD=0.55; C_Group M=4.79, SD=0.65; p=.005, d=1.15)"
