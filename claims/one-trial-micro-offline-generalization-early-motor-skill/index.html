Evidence¶

B. P. Johnson 2023¶

B. P. Johnson, I. Iturrate, R. Y. Fakhreddine, M. Bönstrup, E. R. Buch, E. M. Robertson and L. G. Cohen. (2023). Generalization of procedural motor sequence learning after a single practice trial. npj Science of Learning 8:45. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00194-7

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1 (n = 551, MTurk) randomly assigned participants to 1, 2, 5, or 12 trials of skill A before testing skill B; tapping speed measured generalization. Only the one-trial group showed "the one trial practice group was the only group that started skill B at a higher performance level than they ended skill A ( p = 0.001".

"Relating performance at the end of skill A and the onset of skill B ( GenB0 /C0 Af ), we found that the one trial practice group was the only group that started skill B at a higher performance level than they ended skill A ( p = 0.001, Fig. 1d)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Early generalization is content-specific: skill B onset performance is higher when skills share a parsing rule than when they share only transition structure — related
  • One-trial generalization of parsing rule structure exceeds sensorimotor mapping benefits: onset of skill B is approximately 19% larger in the PARSING RULE than the RANDOM group — related
  • Varying the inter-skill rest interval from 0 to 30 seconds does not modify one-trial generalization from skill A to skill B — related
  • Random termination of practice periods does not reduce micro-offline gains, ruling out preemptive motor slowing — related
  • Performance improvements during early motor skill learning occur predominantly during short rest periods (micro-offline gains), replicated in a large crowdsourced sample and in a laboratory study — related
  • Halving practice-period duration from 10 s to 5 s leaves micro-offline gains unchanged, arguing against recovery from motor fatigue — related
  • Micro-offline gains are inconsistent with a dominant contribution of latent motor slowing, supporting consolidation as their driver — related
  • Early learning of an implicit motor sequence is accounted for by micro-offline gains during brief rest periods rather than by gains during active practice — related
