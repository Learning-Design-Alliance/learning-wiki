The review reports that "When teachers (Marsh et al., 1984) and classmates (Pohlmann et al., 2004) were asked about students’ subject-specific self-concepts, no contrast effects from dimensional comparisons emerged", while Dai (2002) found such effects for parents judging abilities.

Related Claims¶

  • Dimensional comparison effects extend to subjective task values, with contrast effects strongest for intrinsic value and costs and lowest for utility value, often mediated by self-concept — related
  • The net effect of dimensional comparisons is contested: early evidence suggested a positive net effect, but later experiments found downward and upward effects cancel out — related
  • Dimensional comparisons: achievement–self-concept paths are positive in similar subjects but negative in dissimilar subjects — related
  • Subject similarity moderates dimensional comparison effects: far comparisons (e.g., math vs. verbal) yield strong contrast effects, near comparisons yield weaker contrast or assimilation effects — related
