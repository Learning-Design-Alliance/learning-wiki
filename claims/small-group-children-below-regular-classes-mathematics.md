# Children educated in small groups for attention deficits performed below regular-class peers in all mathematics measures, and girls performed at a lower baseline than boys

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At baseline, children in small classes scored below the mean of all children in all three mathematics measures, and Swedish children in regular classes performed significantly better (p < .05). [→ Dahlin 2013](#dahlin-2013)
`q2 i?` Girls (n = 11) performed differently from boys, with boys having higher number ages and BNST scores at T1; the girls' sample was too small to test effects efficiently. [→ Dahlin 2013 (2)](#dahlin-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Dahlin 2013

Karin I. E. Dahlin. (2013). Working Memory Training and the Effect on Mathematical Achievement in Children with Attention Deficits and Special Needs. Journal of Education and Learning, 2(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v2n1p118

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline comparison of z-scores shown in Figure 1: intervention groups (n = 57) versus regular classes (n = 134) in Grades 3–5; regular classes scored mean = 0.29 in all three measures, and t-tests showed regular classes significantly better (p < .05).

> "Children in small classes perform below mean of the total group (all children) in all three measures. The lowest results occur in BNST (mean = - 0.728, SD = 1.05) and subtraction (mean = - 0.624, SD = 0.94)."

### Dahlin 2013 (2)

Karin I. E. Dahlin. (2013). Working Memory Training and the Effect on Mathematical Achievement in Children with Attention Deficits and Special Needs. Journal of Education and Learning, 2(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v2n1p118

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Gender comparison at baseline and across time points; significant boy–girl differences occurred in Digit span forward, subtraction and BNST at T1, T2 and T3 and in training index scores, while improvements in training scores were equal for boys and girls. The article states the girls' sample size was insufficient.

> "We found that the girls ( n = 11) performed differently compared with the boys (e.g., boys had higher number ages, BNST) compared with girls at T1, p < .05."
