## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students with above-average mathematics anxiety appeared to benefit similarly from test practice as students with lower anxiety; point estimates were similar and neither reached statistical significance alone. [→ Avvisati 2020](#avvisati-2020)

## Evidence

### Avvisati 2020

Avvisati, F., & Borgonovi, F. (2020). Learning Mathematics Problem Solving through Test Practice: a Randomized Field Experiment on a Global Scale. Educational Psychology Review, 32, 791–814. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09520-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Moderation analysis (model 9 in Table 4, N = 12,665) using a binary indicator of above-average mathematics anxiety derived from the PISA background questionnaire. "Point estimates were similar for the two groups" and neither subgroup effect was individually significant; the overall test of equal effects had p = 0.766.

> "students who reported above-average levels of math anxiety may have benefited just as much as students with lower levels of math anxiety from test practice. Point estimates were similar for the two groups (none of which reaches statistical significance alone, due, also, to the smaller sample size for this analysis)"
