# All six CPS age cohorts saw a slight 2001 decline in math scores of less than one month, which a Rasch-score re-analysis suggests reflects an actual drop in learning rather than test-form effects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The percent of students at or above national norms in math fell from 46.4 percent in 2000 to 43.6 percent in 2001, and an equated Rasch score analysis replicated the GE decline for all cohorts except fourteen-year-olds. [→ Todd Rosenkranz 2002](#todd-rosenkranz-2002)

## Evidence

### Todd Rosenkranz 2002

Todd Rosenkranz. (2002). 2001 CPS Test Trend Review: Iowa Tests of Basic Skills. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/2001-cps-trend-review-iowa-tests-basic-skills

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Supplementary re-analysis of 2001 CPS math scores using an equated Rasch score (logit), reported in the Decline in 2001 Math Scores sidebar. The same analysis notes the percent at or above norms fell from 46.4 percent in 2000 to 43.6 percent in 2001.

> "The results of the Rasch analysis were the same as the GE trends in 2001 with one exception—the scores for fourteen-year-olds did not drop in Rasch. This suggests that the drop in the GE trends reflects an actual drop in learning and is not the product of the test form progression."
