# READ 180® comprehension effects are inconsistent across cohorts within the same study

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Fitzgerald and Hartry (2008), Cohort 1 showed statistically significant positive comprehension differences, but Cohort 2 showed no statistically significant differences. [→ READ 180® 2016](#read-180-2016)
`q3 i?` The Cohort 2 comprehension effect sizes were not large enough to be substantively important. [→ READ 180® 2016 (2)](#read-180-2016-2)

## Evidence

### READ 180® 2016

READ 180®. (2016). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report: Adolescent Literacy, Updated November 2016. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. http://www.hmhco.com/products/read-180/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized controlled trial in Brockton, Massachusetts afterschool programs (grades 4–6), Cohort 1: statistically significant positive differences on Stanford 10 Vocabulary and Reading Comprehension subtests, with the comprehension result substantively important per WWC criteria.

> "For Cohort 1, the authors reported statistically significant positive differences between the READ 180® Enterprise Edition and comparison groups on both outcomes, and the result for the Reading Comprehension subtest was large enough to be considered substantively important"

### READ 180® 2016 (2)

READ 180®. (2016). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report: Adolescent Literacy, Updated November 2016. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. http://www.hmhco.com/products/read-180/

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Same RCT, second cohort (2007–08): the WWC confirmed "no statistically significant differences between the intervention and comparison groups for Cohort 2," and the effect sizes were not substantively important.

> "The authors also reported, and the WWC confirmed, no statistically significant differences between the intervention and comparison groups for Cohort 2."
