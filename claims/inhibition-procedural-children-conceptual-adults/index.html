Evidence¶

Camilla Gilmore 2015¶

Camilla Gilmore, Sarah Keeble, Sophie Richardson, Lucy Cragg. (2015). The role of cognitive inhibition in different components of arithmetic. ZDM Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-014-0659-y

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Linear regressions with inhibition score as dependent variable run separately for adolescents (R2 = 0.11) and adults (R2 = 0.10), reported in Table 3. The article reports a developmental transition: "only procedural skill was significantly related to inhibition skill" in children, only conceptual understanding in adults.

"At age 11–14 years, only procedural skill was significantly related to inhibition skill, whereas for the group of young adults only conceptual understanding was significantly related to inhibition skill."

Camilla Gilmore 2015 (2)¶

Camilla Gilmore, Sarah Keeble, Sophie Richardson, Lucy Cragg. (2015). The role of cognitive inhibition in different components of arithmetic. ZDM Mathematics Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11858-014-0659-y

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Regression with inhibition score as dependent variable and the three arithmetic components as predictors across all 209 participants. The article reports "procedural skill was a significant predictor (β = −0.260, p = .007)" and conceptual understanding marginally significant; no effect size beyond β printed.

"procedural skill was a significant predictor (β = −0.260, p = .007) and conceptual understanding was a marginally significant predictor (β = −0.134, p = .066) (model R2 = 0.085)"
