## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After controlling for pretest scores, satisfaction and engagement were significantly associated with post-test knowledge, and satisfaction and engagement predicted clinical trials attitudes and career interest; only engagement predicted possible selves. [→ Bowling 2017](#bowling-2017)

## Evidence

### Bowling 2017

Bowling, K. G., Miller, L. M., Patel, R., & Bass, K. M. (2017). Impacts of Virtual Clinical Trials Simulations on Science Knowledge and Attitudes. Electronic Journal of Science Education, 21(7). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1187990

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple regression analyses (N=525) predicting post-test knowledge and attitudes, controlling for pretest scores. Table 6 reports beta values including satisfaction (.284** for attitudes) and engagement (.092* for attitudes), with adjusted R2 of .456 to .773 across outcomes.

> "Significant associations were also found between students’ satisfaction for and engagement with the simulations, and their attitudes toward clinical trials and clinical trials career interest. In fact, the two variables proved to be second and third -strongest predictors of clinical trials attitudes and clinical trials career interest."
