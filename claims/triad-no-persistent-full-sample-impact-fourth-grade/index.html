Evidence¶

Douglas H. Clements 2013¶

Douglas H. Clements, Julie Sarama, Carolyn Layzer, Fatih Unlu, Christopher B. Wolfe, Mary Elaine Spitler. (2013). Longitudinal evaluation of a scale-up model for teaching mathematics with trajectories and technologies: Persistence of effects three years after the treatment. SREE Spring 2013 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED563385

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Two-level cross-sectional HLMs (students nested in schools) estimated separately for Fall 2011 and Spring 2012 REMA 3-5 data from the cluster-randomized trial of 106 pre-K classrooms. Impact estimates were small and non-significant: FT vs CTRL .047 (p=.80) Fall and -.035 (p=.79) Spring; nFT vs CTRL -.089 (p=.62) and -.081 (p=.52). No standardized effect size beyond these impact estimates is printed.

"Table 1 suggests that there is no persistent impact of FT or nFT (when compared with students placed in the control group originally) at either time point."
