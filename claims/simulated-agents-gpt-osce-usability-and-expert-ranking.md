## Evidence

### Zouakia Z 2026

Zouakia Z, Logak E, Szymczak A, Jais J-P, Burgun A, Tsopra R. (2026). AI-Driven Objective Structured Clinical Examination Generation in Digital Health Education: Comparative Analysis of Three GPT-4o Configurations. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/82116

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Categorical usability ratings (n=16 per configuration, two experts x 8 stations, not averaged) analyzed with Kruskal-Wallis tests. The article reports the simulated-agents configuration had "88% (14/16) for usability without major revisions".

> "Overall, usability differed significantly across configurations (P=.02). For simulated-agents GPT, 88% (14/16) of OSCEs were usable without major revisions, while for standard and personalized GPT, 31% (5/16) of OSCEs required major revisions"

### Zouakia Z 2026 (2)

Zouakia Z, Logak E, Szymczak A, Jais J-P, Burgun A, Tsopra R. (2026). AI-Driven Objective Structured Clinical Examination Generation in Digital Health Education: Comparative Analysis of Three GPT-4o Configurations. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/82116

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

After rating, experts ranked groups of three same-topic OSCEs (one per configuration) from first to third, blind to configuration. The simulated-agents GPT was "ranked first in 88% (14/16) of the OSCEs and never placing third".

> "Overall, generated case rankings differed significantly as well (P<.001). The simulated-agents GPT stood out, with 88% (14/16) of OSCEs ranked first and none ranked third."
