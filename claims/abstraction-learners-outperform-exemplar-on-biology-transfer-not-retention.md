# Abstraction learners significantly outperform exemplar learners on transfer exam questions but perform equivalently on retention questions in introductory biology

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Concept-building approach significantly interacted with exam question type: abstraction learners scored higher on transfer questions (M = 0.82 vs. 0.68) but not on retention questions (M = 0.83 vs. 0.81). [→ McDaniel Mark A. 2022](#mcdaniel-mark-a-2022)
`q3 i?` Simple contrasts confirmed equivalent retention performance (t(96) = 0.60, p = 0.550) and significantly better transfer performance for abstraction learners (t(96) = 4.51, p < 0.001). [→ McDaniel Mark A. 2022](#mcdaniel-mark-a-2022)

## Evidence

### McDaniel Mark A. 2022

McDaniel Mark A., Cahill Michael J., Frey Regina F., Limeri Lisa B., Lemons Paula P. (2022). Learning Introductory Biology: Students' Concept-Building Approaches Predict Transfer on Biology Exams. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.21-12-0335

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Mixed-effects analysis of four midterm exams in two introductory biology courses, with 21 exemplar and 46 abstraction learners classified via a laboratory function-learning task. The question type by approach interaction was significant, F(1, 62) = 16.58, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.21; "abstraction learners significantly outperformed exemplar learners on transfer questions, t(96) = 4.51, p < 0.001."

> "abstraction and exemplar learners performed nearly identically on the retention questions (M = 0.83 and 0.81, respectively), but abstraction learners performed notably better on the transfer questions than did exemplar learners (M = 0.82 and 0.68, respectively)"
