# Evidence for physical activity benefits on academic achievement in youth is inconsistent: six of twelve reviews concluded benefits, six mixed or inconclusive

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across twelve systematic reviews of academic outcomes, six concluded physical activity benefits academic performance and six reported mixed or inconclusive evidence. [→ Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020](#wassenaar-thomas-m-2020)
`q3 i?` The single high-confidence review found no evidence for physical activity improvements in maths, reading or language among overweight/obese children. [→ Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020 (2)](#wassenaar-thomas-m-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020

Wassenaar Thomas M., Williamson Wilby, Johansen-Berg Heidi, Dawes Helen, Roberts Nia, Foster Charlie, Sexton Claire E. (2020). A critical evaluation of systematic reviews assessing the effect of chronic physical activity on academic achievement, cognition and the brain in children and adolescents: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-020-00959-y

`q3 · i?`

Synthesis of the twelve systematic reviews that evaluated academic outcomes. The article reports an even split: six reviews concluded benefits and six "mixed or inconclusive evidence for PA-related academic changes".

> "Across all twelve reviews, six concluded that PA benefits academic performance [23, 24, 26, 29, 32, 33], and the remaining six concluded that there was mixed or inconclusive evidence for PA-related academic changes"

### Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020 (2)

Wassenaar Thomas M., Williamson Wilby, Johansen-Berg Heidi, Dawes Helen, Roberts Nia, Foster Charlie, Sexton Claire E. (2020). A critical evaluation of systematic reviews assessing the effect of chronic physical activity on academic achievement, cognition and the brain in children and adolescents: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-020-00959-y

`q3 · i?`

Findings of the one high-confidence review (Martin et al.), based on three RCTs in obese/overweight children. Its meta-analyses reported maths d = 0.49 (−0.04, 1.01) and reading d = 0.10 (−0.30, 0.49), both non-significant.

> "Among three RCTs of obese/overweight children, this review found no evidence for PA-related improvements in maths, reading or language performance."
