# Formulating implementation intentions did not increase university students' use of distributed practice during a four-week course

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no statistically significant effect of implementation intention group on the likelihood of engaging in distributed practice. [→ David 2026](#david-2026)

## Evidence

### David 2026

David, L., Biwer, F., Crutzen, R., & de Bruin, A. (2026). Understanding and supporting university students' use of distributed practice via implementation intentions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00448-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Four-week pre-registered ESM study of 87 university students randomly assigned to an implementation-intention group or an information-only control group. The generalized linear mixed-effects model showed "no statistically significant effect of group on the use of distributed practice". Both groups averaged about 1.61 days per week of distributed practice.

> "There was no statistically significant effect of group on the use of distributed practice (β = 0.25, SE = 0.28,p = 0.371, 95% CI [−0.30; 0.81]), indicating that participants in the II group did not show a signiﬁcantly higher likelihoodof engaging in distributed practice compared to the control group."
