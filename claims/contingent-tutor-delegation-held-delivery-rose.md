# Contingent tutoring held delegation at the level of an answer-on-request assistant while delivering more artifacts, one decision at a time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Learners asked the contingent tutor to build as often as the unrestricted assistant, and far more often than the question-only tutor. [→ Hou 2026](#hou-2026)
`q3 i?` The contingent tutor delivered a tutor-written artifact in more sessions than either baseline and wrote more code per session than the assistant. [→ Hou 2026 (2)](#hou-2026-2)
`q3 i?` Learner turn counts under the contingent tutor matched the question-only tutor and exceeded the assistant's. [→ Hou 2026 (3)](#hou-2026-3)

## Evidence

### Hou 2026

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Session-level GEE analysis clustered on participant from the event log (N=131, 393 sessions). Build-request rates were 50.4% (Tutor C), 51.1% (Tutor A) and 23.7% (Tutor B); the C–A odds ratio was 0.95 and the C–B odds ratio 3.29.

> "At least one build request occurred in 50.4% of Tutor C sessions and 51.1% of Tutor A sessions (𝑂𝑅= 0.95, 95% CI[0.64, 1.42],𝑝=. 82), against 23.7% of Tutor B sessions (𝑂𝑅= 3.29[1.98, 5.48],𝑝<. 001)"

### Hou 2026 (2)

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Event-log delivery counts checked against an authorship probe (κ = .76). The contingent tutor delivered artifacts in 48.1% of sessions versus 23.7% for the assistant and 9.2% for the question-only tutor, and wrote more code per session (RR = 1.64), while complete programs in chat were as frequent under Tutor A as Tutor C (RR = 1.06, p = .77).

> "A tutor-written artifact was delivered into the workspace in 48.1% of Tutor C sessions, against 23.7% for Tutor A (𝑂𝑅= 2.99[1.80, 4.98],𝑝<. 001) and 9.2% for Tutor B ( 𝑂𝑅= 9.71[5.29, 17.85], 𝑝<. 001)"

### Hou 2026 (3)

Hou, X., Weng, Y., Yeh, C. H., Lee, D. L., Zheng, L., Li, F., Wang, W., & Liu, Y. (2026). Adaptive Scaffolding Needs Contingency: An AI Tutor That Escalates and Fades on What the Learner Does. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22993

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dialogue counts per session from the event log in the same within-subjects study. Mean learner turns were 16.8 under the contingent tutor, 14.0 under the question-only tutor and 5.0 under the assistant, with rate ratios as printed.

> "Learners produced a mean of 16.8 turns per session with Tutor C and 14.0 with Tutor B, statistically indistinguishable (𝑅𝑅= 1.16[0.94, 1.44],𝑝=. 17), against 5.0 with Tutor A (𝑅𝑅= 3.30 [2.72, 3.99],𝑝<. 001)"
