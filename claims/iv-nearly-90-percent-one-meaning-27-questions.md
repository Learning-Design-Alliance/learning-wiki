## Evidence

### diSessa et al. 2004

Andrea A. diSessa, Nicole M. Gillespie, Jennifer B. Esterly. (2004). Coherence versus fragmentation in the development of the concept of force. Cognitive Science 28 (2004) 843–900. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cogsci.2004.05.003

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

This is the article's summary of I&V's study, which the present work quasi-replicates; the review-like attribution is the article's own. It reports "four primary meanings" and nearly 90% consistent classification across 27 questions.

> "In a cross-sectional study of 105 subjects from kindergarten to high school, they found only a small universe of meanings for "force": four primary meanings, and a smaller number of coherent but composite meanings. Almost 90% of subjects were unambiguously classifiable as consistently using exactly one of these meanings across a battery of 27 questions"
