## Evidence

### Chou E 2026

Chou E, Cooper D, Goel P, Tang J, Henkle G, Zemeida S, Wolfson H, Shyu YL, Chang E, Abram A, Gerriets VA, Cusick JK. (2026). The use of voiceovers and review games in teaching immunology. ImmunoHorizons. https://doi.org/10.1093/immhor/vlag042

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiyear randomized study of 123 first- and second-year medical students randomly assigned to three groups; single-factor ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test on formative quiz scores. The study reports "performance between these 2 groups did not differ significantly (P = 0.89)" while both pedagogies outperformed the blind control (P < 0.0001).

> "The lecture group and voiceover/review game group scored an average of 63.8% (n = 47) and 61.7% (n = 47) correct on the post-intervention quiz, respectively (Fig. 1). Although performance between these 2 groups did not differ significantly (P = 0.89), both groups performed better than the negative control group, which averaged 30.3% (n = 29; P < 0.0001)."
