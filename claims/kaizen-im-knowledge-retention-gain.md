# Residents' correct responses on retested gamified questions improved by 11.9% (p<0.001), with larger gains for more recently introduced round 2 questions (17.4%) than round 1 questions (10.2%).

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 1046 paired responses from 50 players, correct responses increased 11.9% on retest (p<0.001). [→ Nevin Christa R 2014](#nevin-christa-r-2014)
`q3 i?` Gains were larger for round 2 questions (17.4% increase, p<0.001) than round 1 questions (10.2% increase, p<0.001). [→ Nevin Christa R 2014 (2)](#nevin-christa-r-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Nevin Christa R 2014

Nevin Christa R, Westfall Andrew O, Rodriguez J Martin, Dempsey Donald M, Cherrington Andrea, Roy Brita, Patel Mukesh, Willig James H. (2014). Gamification as a tool for enhancing graduate medical education. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2013-132486

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired t-test analysis of 1046 paired responses from 50 participants who answered retention questions both times, with initial mean 40.5 (SD 20.1) and subsequent 52.3 (SD 23.1). "On average, players’ answers were correct 11.9% (p<0.001) more frequently when a question was reintroduced."

> "On average, players’ answers were correct 11.9% (p<0.001) more frequently when a question was reintroduced."

### Nevin Christa R 2014 (2)

Nevin Christa R, Westfall Andrew O, Rodriguez J Martin, Dempsey Donald M, Cherrington Andrea, Roy Brita, Patel Mukesh, Willig James H. (2014). Gamification as a tool for enhancing graduate medical education. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2013-132486

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired t-test analyses stratified by round of question introduction (Bonferroni-corrected across 12 tests): round 1 questions gained "10.2% increase, p=<0.001" and round 2 questions gained "17.4% increase, p<0.001". The larger gain for more recent questions is attributed to decay of knowledge.

> "Benefits were seen for both reintroduced round 1 questions (10.2% increase, p=<0.001) and round 2 questions (17.4% increase, p<0.001) (table 3)."
