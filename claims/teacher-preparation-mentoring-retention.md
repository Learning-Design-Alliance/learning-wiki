# Well-prepared and well-mentored teachers stay in teaching at more than twice the rate of unprepared entrants, and teacher residency programs typically retain more than 80% over five years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers who are well prepared stay in teaching at rates more than twice as high as those who are not fully prepared when they enter, and the same is true for those who are well mentored. [→ Cook-Harvey 2016](#cook-harvey-2016)
`q2 i?` Teacher residency programs in high-need districts reduce shortages and turnover, with retention rates typically more than 80% over five years. [→ Cook-Harvey 2016 (2)](#cook-harvey-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Cook-Harvey 2016

Cook-Harvey, C.M., Darling-Hammond, L., Lam, L., Mercer, C., & Roc, M. (2016). Equity and ESSA: Leveraging Educational Opportunity Through the Every Student Succeeds Act. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/equity-essa

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The report states in its sidebar on getting and keeping strong teachers in high-need schools that well-prepared and well-mentored teachers stay at more than twice the rate of those not fully prepared; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Teachers who are well prepared stay in teaching at rates more than twice as high as those who are not fully prepared when they enter, and the same is true for those who are well mentored."

### Cook-Harvey 2016 (2)

Cook-Harvey, C.M., Darling-Hammond, L., Lam, L., Mercer, C., & Roc, M. (2016). Equity and ESSA: Leveraging Educational Opportunity Through the Every Student Succeeds Act. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/equity-essa

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report describes teacher residency programs in high-need districts, in which recruits complete credential coursework integrated with a yearlong apprenticeship alongside an expert mentor teacher, with "retention rates typically more than 80% over five years."

> "In exchange for tuition remission and a stipend, as well as two years of mentoring, they commit to teach for three to five years in the districts’ schools, and usually stay much longer, with retention rates typically more than 80% over five years."
