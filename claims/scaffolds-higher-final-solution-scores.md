# Groups' final solution scores were significantly higher on the scaffolded task than the non-scaffolded task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A repeated-measures ANOVA showed a main effect of task on groups' final solution scores, with scaffolded task scores significantly higher on average. [→ Parks 2026](#parks-2026)

## Evidence

### Parks 2026

Parks, T., Shehab, S., & Mercier, E. (2026). Investigating Students' Collaborative Problem-Solving Interactions during Scaffolded and Non-Scaffolded Ill-Structured Tasks. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09816-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on rubric-scored final solutions from 11 groups. Scores were out of 5 for the non-scaffolded task and 6 for the scaffolded task (adjusted to a 5-point scale). "a main effect of task on the groups' scores, F(1,10) = 4.36, p =.044, η2 = 0.713" indicates higher solution quality on the scaffolded task.

> "A repeated measure analysis of variance indicated a main effect of task on the groups' scores, F(1,10) = 4.36, p =.044, η2 = 0.713. These results indicate that the quality of groups' final solutions for the scaffolded task was significantly higher on average than the quality of the groups' final solutions for the non-scaffolded task."
