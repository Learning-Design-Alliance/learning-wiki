# A blended research methodology/biostatistics course improved subjective but not objective numeracy three months later

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a controlled before-and-after study, the intervention group's subjective numeracy increased (median difference 0.5, 95% CI 0.3 to 0.7) while the non-intervention group's decreased (−0.4, 95% CI −0.4 to −0.1), with no corresponding objective-numeracy advantage. [→ Buljan Ivan 2019](#buljan-ivan-2019)

## Evidence

### Buljan Ivan 2019

Buljan Ivan, Tokalić Ružica, Marušić Matko, Marušić Ana. (2019). Health numeracy skills of medical students:cross-sectional and controlled before-and-after study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1902-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled before-and-after study with 113 first-year medical students completing three measurements (54 intervention, 59 non-intervention); the abstract reports the between-group difference as P < 0.001 on the 0–8 subjective scale. No standardized effect size printed.

> "subjective numeracy decreased in the non-intervention group (mean difference (Mddiff) = − 0.4, 95% CI = -0.4 to − 0.1), and significantly increased in the intervention group (Mddiff = 0.5, 95% CI = 0.3 to 0.7)."
