## Evidence

### Luschin-Ebengreuth M 2015

Luschin-Ebengreuth M, Dimai HP, Ithaler D, Neges HM, Reibnegger G. (2015). Situational judgment test as an additional tool in a medical admission test: an observational investigation. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-015-1033-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Aggregated bivariate scatterplots of relative scores across the three admission years (Figure 1). The authors state the SJT relative scores are not nearly symmetrically distributed around 0.5, reflecting that the SJT is the test part with the least difficulty.

> "most observations cluster in the high range above a relative score of 0.6, and apparently they do not depend on the relative score of the other test parts. This behavior of the relative SJT scores nicely reflects the fact that the SJT test part is the one with the least difficulty."
