Evidence¶

Richmond Helen 2017¶

Richmond Helen, Copsey Bethan, Hall Amanda M., Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2017). A systematic review and meta-analysis of online versus alternative methods for training licensed health care professionals to deliver clinical interventions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1047-4

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Review of 14 included RCTs (1089 licensed health professionals) identified from 884 records; risk of bias assessed with the Cochrane tool by two independent reviewers. The review reports "71% (10/14) of studies classified as having unclear or high risk of bias" and reporting quality generally poor.

"The majority of sample sizes were small and ranged from 21 to 183. Methodological quality was poor overall with 71% (10/14) of studies classified as having unclear or high risk of bias."

Richmond Helen 2017 (2)¶

Richmond Helen, Copsey Bethan, Hall Amanda M., Davies David, Lamb Sarah E. (2017). A systematic review and meta-analysis of online versus alternative methods for training licensed health care professionals to deliver clinical interventions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1047-4

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

The review authors' limitations section states effect sizes were "imprecise with wide confidence intervals" and that control interventions often differed from online interventions in factors other than delivery mode, so the cause of any difference could not be determined.

"Despite these strengths, our observed effect sizes were imprecise with wide confidence intervals, as indicated by the GRADE ratings, and thus the results from the meta-analyses should be interpreted cautiously."
