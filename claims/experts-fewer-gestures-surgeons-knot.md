## Evidence

### Vedula S. Swaroop 2016

Vedula S. Swaroop, Malpani Anand O., Tao Lingling, Chen George, Gao Yixin, Poddar Piyush, Ahmidi Narges, Paxton Christopher, Vidal Rene, Khudanpur Sanjeev, Hager Gregory D., Chen Chi Chiung Grace. (2016). Analysis of the Structure of Surgical Activity for a Suturing and Knot-Tying Task. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0149174

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of 135 trials of a surgeon's knot by 18 surgeons (4 experts, 14 trainees) on the dVSS bench-top model, comparing bootstrap interval estimates of gesture counts by skill level. The article reports the expert-novice difference in total gestures as "statistically significant"; the abstract prints expert 26.29 (95% CI 25.21-27.38) vs novice 31.30 (95% CI 29.05-33.55).

> "Expert trials were executed with fewer gestures per task compared with trials in other skill categories. The difference in the total numbers of gestures between trials by expert surgeons (experience-based skill definition) and trials by novice surgeons was statistically significant (Fig 3, Table B in S1 File)."
