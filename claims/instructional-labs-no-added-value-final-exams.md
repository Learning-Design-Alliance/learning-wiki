# Taking an optional introductory physics lab that aims to reinforce lecture content provides no measurable added value to final-exam performance on lab-related questions

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Across nine courses at three institutions, the mean lab benefit on final exams was consistent with zero, with an ANOVA on difference scores that was non-significant (F(1, 2615) = 2.32, p = .128) and a negligible printed effect size (η2partial = 0.00005). [→ N.G. Holmes 2017](#ng-holmes-2017)

## Evidence

### N.G. Holmes 2017

N.G. Holmes, Jack Olsen, James L. Thomas, Carl E. Wieman. (2017). Value added or misattributed? A multi-institution study on the educational benefit of labs for reinforcing physics content. arXiv:1705.03580. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010129)

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on students' difference scores (fractional score on lab-related minus non-lab-related final-exam items) across nine courses at three institutions. The combined average mean lab benefit was 0.24 ± 0.67%, "non-signiﬁcant" per the article, with Group η2partial = 0.00005; equivalence was not otherwise tested.

> "An ANOVA comparing the diﬀerences between lab-enrolled and non-lab-enrolled students on their diﬀerence scores was non-signiﬁcant: F (1, 2615) = 2 .32,p = .128"
