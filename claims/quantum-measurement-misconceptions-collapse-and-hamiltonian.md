## Evidence

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2011

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2011). Surveying students' understanding of quantum mechanics in one spatial dimension. [doi:10.1063/1.3515229](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515229)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

QMS item analysis of Question 13 on consecutive measurements: after a position measurement collapses the wavefunction to a delta function, a higher-order energy can be obtained. 40% mistakenly thought only the initial state's two energies were possible.

> "Only 31% of the students correctly answered Question 13 and realized that the state of the system changed after the position measurement."

### Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh 2011 (2)

Guangtian Zhu and Chandralekha Singh. (2011). Surveying students' understanding of quantum mechanics in one spatial dimension. [doi:10.1063/1.3515229](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515229)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Think-aloud interview finding accompanying Question 11, where 33% incorrectly treated an integral involving the Hamiltonian as the probability of measuring a given energy instead of projecting the state onto the energy eigenstate before integrating.

> "Further interviews indicate that students held a common misconception that the Hamiltonian acting on a state represents an energy measurement."
