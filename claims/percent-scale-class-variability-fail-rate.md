# Class-to-class fail rates were far more variable under the percent scale: a student's chance of a sub-C- grade ranged from about 2% to 30% versus roughly 1.4% on average under the 4-point scale

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The fraction of students with grades below C- varied much more across percent-scale classes (SD = 0.077, mean 0.078) than across 4-point classes (SD = 0.010, mean 0.014). [→ Webb 2020](#webb-2020)

## Evidence

### Webb 2020

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Histograms of class-level fail fractions by grade scale (Fig. 5a), checked against a floor effect using a higher C+ cutoff (Fig. 5b), where CLASP10 also varied more (SD = 0.123 vs 0.068). The appendix shows the variability has little to do with class differences in student GPA.

> "Under CLASP10 grading a student enrolling in a class may have a 2% chance of getting a grade less than C − or they might have a 30% chance. Under CLASP4 that probability varies only a little from the average of 1.4%."
