# Bootstrap analysis found no mediating effects of attention measures or aerobic fitness between physical activity and mathematics achievement

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MVPA had a significant direct effect on mathematics achievement in both sexes, but the indirect effect through each mediating variable was not significant. [→ Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022](#li-lingshu-zhang-li-2022)
`q2 i?` Step count likewise had a significant direct effect on mathematics achievement with no significant indirect effects through the mediating variables. [→ Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022 (2)](#li-lingshu-zhang-li-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022

Li Lingshu; Zhang Li. (2022). The Relationship between Physical Activity and Academic Achievement in Multimodal Environment Using Computational Analysis. Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9418004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Bootstrap mediation analysis (Table 6) with MVPA as independent variable and mathematics achievement as dependent variable, testing aerobic fitness and four attention measures as mediators in the 176-student sample; all indirect-effect confidence intervals crossed zero.

> "The direct effect of MVPA on mathematics achievement in males and females was significant (95% confidence interval: 1.3906–3.4871 and 1.5650–5.4291, respectively). The indirect effect of each mediating variable was not significant; thus, MVPA has no mediating effect on mathematics achievement."

### Li Lingshu; Zhang Li 2022 (2)

Li Lingshu; Zhang Li. (2022). The Relationship between Physical Activity and Academic Achievement in Multimodal Environment Using Computational Analysis. Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/9418004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Bootstrap mediation analysis (Table 7) with step count as independent variable; direct-effect confidence intervals excluded zero for both sexes while every indirect-effect interval crossed zero. No effect sizes are printed for the null indirect effects.

> "The direct effect of step on mathematics achievement of male and female students is significant (95% confidence interval: 0.0034–0.0059 and 0.0039–0.0065, respectively). However, the indirect effect of each mediating variable is not significant; thus, step has no mediating effect on mathematical achievement."
