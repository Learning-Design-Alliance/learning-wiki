# Evidence comparing virtual patient design variants is too sparse to give clear recommendations, with only isolated significant design effects

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most design-variant comparisons (linear vs branched, scaffolded vs nonscaffolded, worked vs unworked, spaced vs nonspaced, solving vs constructing) showed no significant differences in knowledge outcomes; only pedagogic vs problem-solving/high-fidelity design was significant (P<.01). [→ Kononowicz AA 2019](#kononowicz-aa-2019)
`q2 i?` In skills outcomes, empathic feedback (P<.03), narrative structure (P=.03), and representational scaffolding (P=.045) each showed significant benefits in single studies. [→ Kononowicz AA 2019 (2)](#kononowicz-aa-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Kononowicz AA 2019

Kononowicz AA, Woodham LA, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/14676

`q2 · i?`

Narrative synthesis of 8 design-comparison studies for knowledge outcomes; only Friedman et al's pedagogic design beat problem-solving and high-fidelity designs (P<.01). The review states such trials "were not numerous enough to give clear recommendations".

> "Comparing linear and branched virtual patients [92], scaffolded versus nonscaffolded [93], worked and unworked examples [97], virtual patient with usability extensions [94], self-determined versus mandatory integration [98], spaced versus nonspaced release of cases [99], and virtual patient solving versus virtual patient design exercises [100] resulted in no significant differences in knowledge outcomes."

### Kononowicz AA 2019 (2)

Kononowicz AA, Woodham LA, Edelbring S, Stathakarou N, Davies D, Saxena N, Tudor Car L, Carlstedt-Duke J, Car J, Zary N. (2019). Virtual Patient Simulations in Health Professions Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis by the Digital Health Education Collaboration. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/14676

`q2 · i?`

Narrative synthesis of 4 design-comparison studies for skills outcomes; single studies found significant benefits of empathic feedback, narrative over problem-solving structure (P=.03), and representational scaffolding (P=.045), while constructing versus solving showed no difference (P=.54).

> "Foster et al [95] showed that virtual patients with emphatic feedback were significantly better in training communication skills than those virtual patients without feedback (P<.03)."
