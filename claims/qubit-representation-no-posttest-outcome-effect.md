## Evidence

### Linda Qerimi 2025

Linda Qerimi, Sarah Malone, Eva Rexigel, Jochen Kuhn and Stefan Küchemann. (2025). Comparing Visual Qubit Representations in Quantum Education: The Bloch sphere Enhances Task Efficiency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple linear regression in a between-subjects study of N=146 upper secondary students (posttest score as dependent variable; predictors group, pretest, prior knowledge). The model was significant, F(3, 142) = 29.21, p < .001, R2 = .38, but "The representation group, however, had no significant effect ( p = .703)".

> "As shown in Table I, pretest performance ( p < .001) and prior knowledge ( p = .008) were significant predictors of posttest outcomes. The representation group, however, had no significant effect ( p = .703)."

### Linda Qerimi 2025 (2)

Linda Qerimi, Sarah Malone, Eva Rexigel, Jochen Kuhn and Stefan Küchemann. (2025). Comparing Visual Qubit Representations in Quantum Education: The Bloch sphere Enhances Task Efficiency. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.21721)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank pre-post comparisons in the same study showed significant learning gains in both representation groups, so both representations supported conceptual learning; no effect size was printed for these gains.

> "Wilcoxon signed-rank tests revealed significant learning gains in both groups from pretest to posttest in Bloch sphere ( V = 238, p < .001) and in the Quantum Bead ( V = 370, p < .01) group, indicating that students improved regardless of representation."
