Evidence¶

Alharbi 2024¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q1 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Massoth et al., a comparison of high- versus low-fidelity SBL for advanced life support training finding no significant difference; the review also notes Massoth et al. observed overconfidence among high-fidelity participants. This is a second-hand citation, not a study read by this review's synthesis.

"Their findings indicated that improvements in knowledge and skills for those who experienced high-fidelity SBL were not significantly different to those who had undertaken low-fidelity SBL [10]."
