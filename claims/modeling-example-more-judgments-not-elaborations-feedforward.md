## Evidence

### Floris M. van Blankenstein 2019

Floris M. van Blankenstein, Gabriela-Olivia Truțescu, Roeland M. van der Rijst, Nadira Saab. (2019). Immediate and delayed effects of a modeling example on the application of principles of good feedback practice: a quasi-experimental study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09482-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann–Whitney exact tests on the direct measure compared judgment counts between conditions. The modeling condition "generated significantly more positive judgments" and negative judgments, with medians of 7.00 vs 4.00 and 2.00 vs 1.00 respectively.

> "at T1 the modeling example condition generated significantly more positive judgments (U = 584.00, z = 4.187, p < 0.0001, Mdn = 7.00 vs. Mdn = 4.00) and negative judgments (U = 673.00, z = 3.591, p < 0.0001, Mdn = 2.00 vs. Mdn = 1.00) than the practice condition"

### Floris M. van Blankenstein 2019 (2)

Floris M. van Blankenstein, Gabriela-Olivia Truțescu, Roeland M. van der Rijst, Nadira Saab. (2019). Immediate and delayed effects of a modeling example on the application of principles of good feedback practice: a quasi-experimental study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09482-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann–Whitney exact tests on the direct measure found no significant condition differences for feed-forward (p = 0.585) or elaborations (p = 0.190); equivalence was not tested. The authors suggest emulating elaborations and feed-forward "may be more difficult" or a floor effect from limited time.

> "The amount of feedforward (U = 1079.00, z = 0.551, p = 0.585) and number of elaborations (U = 977.00, z = 1.316, p = 0.190) did not differ significantly between the two conditions"
