## Evidence

### Klimas J. 2017

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the structural-concerns domain, the article reports the fellowship was "really was geared to teaching the science behind addiction", yet knowledge was "not always perceived as transferable to daily practice"; a clinical fellow noted patients could not afford specific medications.

> "the 'knowledge' learned through the fellowship was not always perceived as transferable to daily practice because of the nature of practice environment and patient population."

### Klimas J. 2017 (2)

Klimas J., Small W., Ahamad K., Cullen W., Mead A., Rieb L., Wood E., McNeil R. (2017). Barriers and facilitators to implementing addiction medicine fellowships: a qualitative study with fellows, medical students, residents and preceptors. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-017-0086-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

A resident participant described that it is not "always verbalized that we're choosing this medication because this is the evidence-based medication", leaving learners to figure out later whether the decision was most correct.

> "The preceptors applied best-practice guidelines in their decisions intuitively without talking to learners about the evidence, or specific trials, explicitly."
