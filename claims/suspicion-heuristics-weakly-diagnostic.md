### Lixiang Yan 2026

Lixiang Yan, Yueqiao Jin, Xibin Han, Dragan Gasevic. (2026). Socially fluent AI decouples conversational signals from source identity in online interaction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23426 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.23426](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.23426)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Unsupervised BERTopic modelling of 1,568 impression texts extracted eight topics; "a single dominant topic capturing generic humanness and fluency language accounted for 59.6% of texts". Mutual information between topic membership and judgment was low (MI = 0.089).

> "Topic prevalence was strongly skewed: a single dominant topic capturing generic humanness and fluency language accounted for 59.6% of texts (Topic 0, 95% CI [57.1%, 62.0%]), with the remaining topics forming a long tail of smaller, more specific themes"

### Lixiang Yan 2026 (2)

Lixiang Yan, Yueqiao Jin, Xibin Han, Dragan Gasevic. (2026). Socially fluent AI decouples conversational signals from source identity in online interaction. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23426

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Supervised class-based TF-IDF analysis showed incorrect or AI-attributing judgments emphasised "response latency and surface fluency" and "repetition or scriptedness", while correct Human judgments referenced socially interpretable qualities; odds ratios were close to unity for most topics (e.g., Topic 0: OR = 0.853).

> "Distinctive terms in incorrect or AI-attributing categories emphasised response latency and surface fluency (e.g., “fast”, “quick”), repetition or scriptedness (e.g., “repeating”, “script”), and disagreement markers (e.g., “disagree”)."
