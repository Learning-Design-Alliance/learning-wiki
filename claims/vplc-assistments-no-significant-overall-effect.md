# vPLC-augmented ASSISTments did not significantly improve overall middle school math achievement relative to a virtual comparison group

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On average, treatment students scored 0.67 points higher than their virtual comparison group, a difference that was not statistically significant at the .05 level (p = 0.172). [→ Huang 2025](#huang-2025)

## Evidence

### Huang 2025

Huang, C.-W., Feng, M., & Li, L. (2025). Applying a partially nested regression model to evaluate the impact of ASSISTments on middle school students' math achievement. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677665

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental matched-comparison analysis of 2,855 treatment students in grades 6–8 across 36 schools in 24 states, compared with a matched virtual comparison group on MAP Growth posttest scores using a partially nested model. The article reports "this difference is not statistically significant at the .05 level"; Table 3 lists ES = 0.04.

> "the treatment impact was estimated to be 0.67 (the coefficient associated with "treat"), indicating that, on average, the treatment group scored 0.67 points higher than its VCG. However, this difference is not statistically significant at the .05 level, with the p-value equal to 0.172"
