# Mastery learning implementation did not reduce material covered or instructional pace, contradicting the Robin Hood contention

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers reported covering the same amount of material after training as in prior years, and neither pace nor presentation time was adversely affected. [→ Fitzpatrick 1985](#fitzpatrick-1985)

## Evidence

### Fitzpatrick 1985

Fitzpatrick, Kathleen A. (1985). Group-Based Mastery Learning: A Robin Hood Approach to Instruction? Paper presented at the annual meeting of the American Educational Research Association, Chicago, Illinois. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED255556

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Teacher self-reports at semester's end indicated the same amount of material was covered as in prior years. Combined with increased presentation time, the article concludes neither breadth nor depth of instruction was diminished after teachers applied mastery learning procedures.

> "Since there was no difference in the amount ofmaterial covered before and after the training, it would appear thatthe teachers did not alter the scope of instruction to accommodate the learning-for-mastery process."
