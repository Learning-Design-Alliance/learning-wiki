# Child-parent dyads generated more ideas and more original ideas than child-stranger dyads on the AUT, with no significant difference in appropriateness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Child-parent dyads generated more ideas (higher fluency) and more original ideas than child-stranger dyads, while the difference in appropriateness was not significant. [→ Bai 2026](#bai-2026)

## Evidence

### Bai 2026

Bai, H.; Chan, L.; Luo, A.; Kroesbergen, E.H.; Christie, S. (2026). Creativity in dialogues: How do children interact with parents vs. strangers for generating creative ideas? Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102286

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-hoc ANOVAs following a significant MANOVA (Pillai's Trace = 0.15, F(3, 98), p < .001) on dyad-level AUT measures showed "the child-parent dyads generated more as well as more original ideas than the child-stranger dyads"; appropriateness did not differ significantly. No standardized effect size was printed for this contrast.

> "The ANOVA test results (see Table 3) showed that the child-parent dyads generated more as well as more original ideas than the child-stranger dyads, but the difference in their appropriateness was not significant."
