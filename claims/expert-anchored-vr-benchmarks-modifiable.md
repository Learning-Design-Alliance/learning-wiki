## Evidence

### Sridhar Ashwin N. 2017

Sridhar Ashwin N., Briggs Tim P., Kelly John D., Nathan Senthil. (2017). Training in Robotic Surgery—an Overview. Current Urology Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11934-017-0710-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Raison et al.'s analysis of European Association of Urology VR course scores, which identified five assessment exercises and set potential benchmark standards at 60, 75 and 90% of the mean expert score. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Modeling against participant outcome data identified a competency standard of 75% of mean expert score as a suitable standard based on the performance of novice (no robotic surgical experience), intermediate (1–74 robotic procedures performed) and expert participants (>75 robotic procedures performed)."

### Sridhar Ashwin N. 2017 (2)

Sridhar Ashwin N., Briggs Tim P., Kelly John D., Nathan Senthil. (2017). Training in Robotic Surgery—an Overview. Current Urology Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11934-017-0710-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Noureldin et al.'s norm-referenced method, in which experts performed tasks on the da Vinci Surgical Skills Simulator before trainees and a passing score was the experts' average total score minus one standard deviation per task. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Although their benchmark passed only one-third of their trainees, it was able to discriminate between novice and more experienced trainees."
