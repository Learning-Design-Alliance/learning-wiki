# Taking Algebra I instead of remedial math raised Algebra credit attainment but increased failure rates and lowered math grades, with no test-score benefit

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A 20 percentage-point increase in Algebra I enrollment produced a 10% increase in students earning Algebra credit, but math failure rates rose for low- and average-ability students and grades declined. [→ Elaine Allensworth 2008](#elaine-allensworth-2008)
`q2 i?` Math test scores were unaffected by taking Algebra instead of remedial math. [→ Elaine Allensworth 2008 (2)](#elaine-allensworth-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth 2008

Elaine Allensworth, Takako Nomi, Nicholas Montgomery, & Valerie E. Lee. (2008). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Consequences of Ninth-Grade Course Taking in Algebra and English on Academic Outcomes in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Hierarchical interrupted time-series models estimating enrollment effects (RQ2), shown as simulations of a 20 percentage-point enrollment increase. "Math failure rates increased among low-ability students (3.0 percentage points, p<.01)"; grades declined most for average-ability students (0.18 grade points, p<.05).

> "Math failure rates increased among low-ability students (3.0 percentage points, p<.01) and also among average-ability students (8.9 percentage points, p<.05)."

### Elaine Allensworth 2008 (2)

Elaine Allensworth, Takako Nomi, Nicholas Montgomery, & Valerie E. Lee. (2008). College Preparatory Curriculum for All: Consequences of Ninth-Grade Course Taking in Algebra and English on Academic Outcomes in Chicago. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://ccsr.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Same RQ2 enrollment-effect models on ninth-grade TAP math scores; the authors note only seven of 48 TAP questions tested Algebra knowledge, so the null may reflect test insensitivity.

> "Math test scores were unaffected by taking Algebra instead of remedial math, although it is possible that the test was not sensitive to the change in curriculum."
