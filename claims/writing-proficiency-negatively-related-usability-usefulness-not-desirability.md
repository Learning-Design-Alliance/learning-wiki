# Students with higher writing proficiency rated MI Write as less useable and useful, but proficiency was not related to desirability

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Writing proficiency was negatively related to student perceptions of usability (β = −.069 fall, −.068 spring) and usefulness (β = −.096 fall, −.084 spring), but was not a significant predictor of desirability. [→ Wilson 2024](#wilson-2024)

## Evidence

### Wilson 2024

Wilson, J., Zhang, F., Palermo, C., Cordero, T. C., Myers, M. C., Eacker, H., Potter, A., & Coles, J. (2024). Predictors of middle school students’ perceptions of automated writing evaluation. Computers & Education, 211, 104985. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104985

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression results from models predicting usability, usefulness, and desirability. Writing proficiency was measured using the PEG Overall Score (range 6–30). Standardized coefficients for proficiency were small but significant for usability and usefulness, and non-significant for desirability.

> "However, writing proficiency was negatively related to student perceptions of usability: students with higher MI Write scores tended to have lower ratings of MI Write's usability."
