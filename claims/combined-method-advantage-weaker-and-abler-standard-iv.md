# The combined method's advantage over the visual method held across ability levels, for weaker as well as abler pupils

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In every pre-learning ability section of the grouped correlation table, the combined-method Set A post-test average exceeded the visual-method Set B average. [→ Winch 1913](#winch-1913)

## Evidence

### Winch 1913

Winch, W. H. (1913). Experimental Researches on Learning to Spell, Part II. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076021

`q2 · i?`

Grouped correlation table (Table IV) from the Standard IV experiment, sectioning boys by pre-learning Set A score; post-learning averages favor Set A in every section (e.g., 26.3 vs 24.3 in the top section, 8.7 vs 6.7 in the lowest). The author concludes these are "evidently not 'chance' results."

> "All along the line, for the weaker pupils as well as for the abler ones, the simul- taneous combined method with attention directed by the ex- perimenter has shown itself victorious."
