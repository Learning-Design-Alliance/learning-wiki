## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors' experience with different audiences indicates contrasting renditions of the same data generate diverse emotional reactions: young children prefer the friendly droidspeak soundscape, while the dark soundscape frightens young audiences but suits mature audiences. `q1 i?` The slow-vibrato segment in massive-star evolution amuses audiences, making them smile or laugh when presented properly. [→ R. García-Benito and E. Pérez-Montero 2022 (2)](#r-garcia-benito-and-e-perez-montero-2022-2)

## Evidence

### R. García-Benito and E. Pérez-Montero 2022

R. García-Benito and E. Pérez-Montero. (2022). Painting Graphs with Sounds: CosMonic Sonification Project. [doi:10.22201/ia.14052059p.2022.54.06](https://doi.org/10.22201/ia.14052059p.2022.54.06)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' experiential report (type e) from outreach presentations of the two transit renditions: the friendly droidspeak is more likely preferred by young children while the dark soundscape is the choice for adult audiences, composers or sound artists. No systematic data was collected.

> "The “dark” soundscape tends to frighten the young audience, specially if the illumination of the venue is faint (generally, to increase the contrast of the screen)."

### R. García-Benito and E. Pérez-Montero 2022 (2)

R. García-Benito and E. Pérez-Montero. (2022). Painting Graphs with Sounds: CosMonic Sonification Project. [doi:10.22201/ia.14052059p.2022.54.06](https://doi.org/10.22201/ia.14052059p.2022.54.06)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' experiential report (type e) on audience reactions to the 20 solar-mass stellar-evolution case, where the star lingers at low temperature and slow vibrato rate. No quantitative audience data is printed.

> "The contrast between high rate vibrato at the beginning of the life of the star and the low, maintained vibrato rate puts a smile in the public and even makes the audience laugh (if presented properly)."
