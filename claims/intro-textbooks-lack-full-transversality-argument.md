# Surveyed calculus-based introductory textbooks do not include a full theoretical argument that electromagnetic waves must be transverse

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` None of the surveyed introductory textbooks derive that longitudinal components violate the Maxwell equations; they show transverse waves are consistent but not required. [→ Lior M. Burko 2008](#lior-m-burko-2008)

## Evidence

### Lior M. Burko 2008

Lior M. Burko. (2008). Transversality of Electromagnetic Waves in the Calculus-Based Introductory Physics Course. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.2993 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.2993)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The author's survey of calculus-based introductory physics textbooks found that while all discuss transversality, most only "show that transverse waves are consistent with the Maxwell equations, but not that they are required." The author notes the survey was not exhaustive.

> "Most textbooks derive the wave equations for the transverse components of the E and B ﬁelds, but do not show that these wave equations are unique, i.e., that a longitudinal component violates the Maxwell equations."
