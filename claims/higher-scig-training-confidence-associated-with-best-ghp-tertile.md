## Evidence

### Mallick R. 2020

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate logistic regression on IDF survey data from 366 SCIG respondents identified predictors of being in the best GHP tertile. Patients rating confidence after training as 6–7 had "higher odds of being in the best tertile for GHP" (OR = 2.18, p = 0.03).

> "those with > 2 years’ IgG experience prior to SCIG (odds ratio [OR] = 2.36) (p = 0.01), higher confidence after training (OR = 2.18) (p = 0.03), and best tertile Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medication (TSQM) effectiveness scores (OR = 2.73) (p = 0.001) had higher odds of being in the best tertile for GHP"

### Mallick R. 2020 (2)

Mallick R., Henderson T., Lahue B. J., Kafal A., Bassett P., Scalchunes C. (2020). Subcutaneous immunoglobulin in primary immunodeficiency – impact of training and infusion characteristics on patient-reported outcomes. BMC Immunology. [doi:10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-020-00371-y)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression on the same survey cohort modeled GHP on the original 1–7 scale, where "a higher coefficient implies a higher GHP"; confidence after training was retained as a predictor in the multivariate model.

> "The least squares regression models score on a continuous linear scale using the original 1–7 scale, where a higher coefficient implies a higher GHP"
