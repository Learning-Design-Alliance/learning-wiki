Evidence¶

Castro-Alonso 2021¶

Castro-Alonso, J. C., Wong, R. M., Adesope, O. O., & Paas, F. (2021). Effectiveness of Multimedia Pedagogical Agents Predicted by Diverse Theories: a Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09587-1

q4 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-analytic moderator analyses across the 32 pooled effect sizes examined agent characteristics including nonverbal communication, motion, and voice. The review reports these characteristics "appeared not to moderate their effectiveness"; no effect sizes for these moderator tests are printed in the available text.

"As predicted by the computers are social actors hypothesis, most of the agents' characteristics, including nonverbal communication, motion, and voice, appeared not to moderate their effectiveness."

Related Claims¶

  • Agent appearance (iconic vs realistic) shows no significant difference in retention, transfer, or interest — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Effects of agent nonverbal behavior depend on the task: facial expressions were valuable for attitudinal learning but detrimental for procedural learning — reports the opposite
  • Beneficial effects of pedagogical agents are attributed primarily to the agent's voice rather than its visual presence — reports the opposite
  • The review reports no evidence that the mere visual presence of a human-like body produces distinct effects, though likeable appearance improves motivation and transfer — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Redundant speech-gesture cooperation improves recall and social perception more than complementarity — reports the opposite
  • Pedagogical agents significantly improve learners' self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.29), with effects moderated by grade level, facial expressions, and reliability reporting — reports the opposite
  • Learning with multimedia pedagogical agents is more effective than learning without these agents, with a small overall effect (g+ = 0.20) — related
  • Specific pointing by pedagogical agents was more effective than nonspecific pointing, which beat non-pointing gestures (review attributes this to Li et al., 2019) — reports the opposite
