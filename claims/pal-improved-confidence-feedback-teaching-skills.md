## Evidence

### Carr Sandra E. 2016

Carr Sandra E., Brand Gabrielle, Wei Li, Wright Helen, Nicol Pam, Metcalfe Helene, Saunders Julie, Payne John, Seubert Liza, Foley Laurie. (2016). “Helping someone with a skill sharpens it in your own mind”: a mixed method study exploring health professions students experiences of Peer Assisted Learning (PAL). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0566-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-PAL survey across the six pilots reporting agreement percentages on skill attainment; “95 to 100 % agreement” on improved confidence and feedback ability, with 85–90% in classroom settings agreeing PAL met expectations.

> "the quantitative findings relating to the impact of the PAL programmes demonstrated a high proportion of respondents agreeing that PAL had improved their confidence and ability to give feedback (95 to 100 % agreement)"

### Carr Sandra E. 2016 (2)

Carr Sandra E., Brand Gabrielle, Wei Li, Wright Helen, Nicol Pam, Metcalfe Helene, Saunders Julie, Payne John, Seubert Liza, Foley Laurie. (2016). “Helping someone with a skill sharpens it in your own mind”: a mixed method study exploring health professions students experiences of Peer Assisted Learning (PAL). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0566-8

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Evaluation of PAL activities by clinical peer tutors across medicine, nursing and podiatry showing close to 100% agreement on safe environment, clear instructions, learner improvement and increased teaching skills.

> "All agreed that the PAL experience had increased their teaching skills, met their expectations and that they would recommend being a PAL tutor to future students"
