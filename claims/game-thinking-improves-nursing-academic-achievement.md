## Evidence

### Nylén-Eriksen Mats 2025

Nylén-Eriksen Mats, Stojiljkovic Marko, Lillekroken Daniela, Lindeflaten Katrin, Hessevaagbakke Elisabeth, Flølo Tone Nygaard, Hovland Olav Johannes, Solberg Ada Marie Svarstad Hansen Sylvia, Bjørnnes Ann Kristin, Tørris Christine. (2025). Game-thinking; utilizing serious games and gamification in nursing education – a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06531-7

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 70 included studies (27 RCTs, 43 quasi-experimental) with 8348 participants. The article reports a pooled effect size of "0.99, [95%CI 0.53, 1.44]" for academic achievement.

> "The meta-analysis suggested that game-thinking could be beneficial in nursing education, notably improving students’ academic achievement (Pooled ES = 0.99, [95%CI 0.53, 1.44])."
