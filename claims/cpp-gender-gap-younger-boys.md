## Evidence

### Wei 2025

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Grade-by-grade U-tests comparing boys' and girls' motivation-and-interest scores (Figure 4 caption reports p-values of 0.005, 0.131, 0.099 and 0.119 for grades 8–11). Only the 8th-grade contrast is significant; no effect size is printed.

> "U-tests revealed a statistically significant difference in attitudes between boys and girls regarding the contextualization of physics problems among 8th graders. The U-test yields a p-value of 0.005, indicating that younger boys hold a significantly more positive attitude towards CPP than girls."
