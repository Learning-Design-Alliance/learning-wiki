# Family circumstances pile up to worsen preschool attendance, and parent beliefs about attendance importance are related to absence rates

## Evidence

### Ehrlich 2014

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis relating accumulated family circumstances (single-parent family, poorer parent health, emergency room as primary medical care, unemployed or non-college-educated parent) to preschool absence rates; absence rises monotonically as circumstances accumulate.

> "Children in families without any of the aforementioned circumstances missed roughly 5.6 percent of school; those with one obstacle missed 7.5 percent; those with two missed 9.0 percent; and those with three or more missed 12.9 percent."

### Ehrlich 2014 (2)

Ehrlich, S. B., Gwynne, J. A., Pareja, A. S., Allensworth, E. M., Moore, P., Jagesic, S., & Sorice, E. (2014). Preschool Attendance in Chicago Public Schools: Relationships with Learning Outcomes and Reasons for Absences. UChicago CCSR Research Report. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/preschool-attendance-chicago-public-schools-relationships-learning-outcomes-and-reasons

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Survey-based analysis linking parent beliefs about the importance of preschool attendance to children's absence rates; children of parents who did not believe preschool attendance mattered much had the highest rates (13.2 percent).

> "parents who believed that regular attendance in preschool is as important as in later grades had children with the lowest absence rates (7.5 percent); those whose parents said that attendance in preschool is important but would be more important in later years had higher absence rates (10.7 percent)"
