# Interleaving effects are stronger when material is more similar between categories, less similar within categories, and more complex

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Between-category similarity positively influenced the interleaving effect, whereas within-category similarity negatively influenced it, as predicted by the attentional bias framework. [→ Brunmair 2019](#brunmair-2019)

## Evidence

### Brunmair 2019

Brunmair, M., & Richter, T. (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-regression models (Models 1-3) with rater-based material-characteristic ratings as moderators; sensitivity analyses across subsets showed the pattern of effects was very stable across different models and samples. No standardized coefficient is quoted here, so no effect size is reported.

> "As expected from the viewpoint of the attentional bias framework (Carvalho & Goldstone, 2015b), similarity between categories had a positive influence on the effect of interleaving, whereas similarity within categories had a negative influence, which decreased when learning materials were included in the model (Table 3)."
