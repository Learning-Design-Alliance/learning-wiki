## Evidence

### Peter Seban 2025

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Two-way ANOVA on the computed bias index (delayed JOL minus actual score, divided by maximum points) from the Results section. The article prints the approach main effect "F(2, 180) = 14.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.14" — ηp2 = 0.14 — plus a non-significant time effect (ηp2 = 0.00) and a significant interaction (ηp2 = 0.05).

> "This revealed a significant main effect of the reading approach (F(2, 180) = 14.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.14), a non-significant main effect of time of the test (F(1, 180) = 0.77, p = 0.38, ηp2 = 0.00), and a significant effect of interaction (F(2,180) = 4.44, p = 0.013, ηp2 = 0.05)."

### Peter Seban 2025 (2)

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Tukey HSD post-hoc comparison of delayed-JOL bias on the one-day delayed test: MTR learners were "underconfident about the knowledge they acquired" while RR learners were overconfident, with a significant difference (p < 0.01). At one week the trend was non-significant (p = 0.13) as rereaders became more accurate.

> "learners in the multiple text reading group (MTR) were underconfident about the knowledge they acquired while those in the rereading group (RR) were overconfident, and the difference was significant (p < 0.01, 95% C.I. = [0.04, 0.38])."

### Peter Seban 2025 (3)

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Post-hoc contrast of bias on the one-week delayed test: the single-reading group "did not expect they would have forgotten as much as they did and were overconfident in their estimates" relative to the MTR group, significant at p < 0.001 with the printed 95% CI.

> "We have also found a significant difference in the bias index on the one-week (WD) delayed test between the multiple text reading group (MTR) and the single reading group (1R): learners in the 1R group did not expect they would have forgotten as much as they did and were overconfident in their estimates (p < 0.001, 95% C.I. = [0.13, 0.47])."
