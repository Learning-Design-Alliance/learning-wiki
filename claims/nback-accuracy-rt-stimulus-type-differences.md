# N-back behavioral performance differs by condition and stimulus type: 2-back is harder than 0-back, shapes are hardest, and numbers slightly outperform letters at 2-back

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Trial accuracy and reaction times differ substantially between 2-back and 0-back conditions (accuracy d=1.26; RT d=2.04). [→ Leopold D.R. 2024](#leopold-dr-2024)
`q3 i2` Letters and numbers show small differences (d<0.50) at 2-back with superior performance for numbers, and both are somewhat easier than shapes (d=0.41-0.81). [→ Leopold D.R. 2024 (2)](#leopold-dr-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Leopold D.R. 2024

Leopold D.R., Kim H., Carlson K.W., Rowe M.A., Groff B.R., Major M.P., Willcutt E.G., Cutting L.E., Banich M.T. (2024). Stimulus shapes strategy: Effects of stimulus characteristics and individual differences in academic achievement on the neural mechanisms engaged during the N-back task. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101372

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Behavioral analysis of the N-back task in 126 youth. The study reports "trial accuracies (Cohen’s d=1.26, p<.001) and reaction times (d=2.04, p<.001) differ substantially" between conditions, with similar magnitudes across stimulus types.

> "As expected, trial accuracies (Cohen’s d=1.26, p<.001) and reaction times (d=2.04, p<.001) differ substantially between the 2-back and 0-back conditions (see Figure S2)."

### Leopold D.R. 2024 (2)

Leopold D.R., Kim H., Carlson K.W., Rowe M.A., Groff B.R., Major M.P., Willcutt E.G., Cutting L.E., Banich M.T. (2024). Stimulus shapes strategy: Effects of stimulus characteristics and individual differences in academic achievement on the neural mechanisms engaged during the N-back task. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101372

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Within-participant behavioral comparisons among stimulus types in the same sample. The study reports "small differences (d<0.50)" between letters and numbers at 2-back "with superior performance for numbers", and letters and numbers somewhat easier than shapes (d=0.41-0.81).

> "Comparisons among stimulus types indicate that letters and numbers do not differ in the 0-back condition and demonstrate small differences (d<0.50) during the harder 2-back condition, with superior performance for numbers as compared to letters."
