## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Adolescents in the imagined contact condition reported greater academic intentions towards the target than controls, and imagined contact interacted with information type such that combined information was more effective in the contact condition. [→ Eleni Fleva 2014](#eleni-fleva-2014)

## Evidence

### Eleni Fleva 2014

Eleni Fleva. (2014). Imagined Contact Improves Intentions towards a Hypothetical Peer with Asperger Syndrome but not Attitudes towards Peers with Asperger Syndrome in General. World Journal of Education, 5(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v5n1p1

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on SAQ academic scores in the same 416-participant classroom experiment showed "a significant main effect of imagined activity" (η2= .01), with imagined contact participants reporting greater intentions than controls.

> "The analysis of the SAQ academic scores revealed a significant main effect of imagined activity with participants in the imagined contact condition reporting greater intentions than control participants [M= 19.3 vs. M= 18.3, F (1, 400) = 7.34, p= .007, η2= .01]"
