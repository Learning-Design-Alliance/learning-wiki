## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adolescents of authoritative parents achieved significantly higher grades six months later than adolescents of neglectful, permissive and authoritarian parents, after adjusting for socio-demographics. [→ Hayek J 2022](#hayek-j-2022)

## Evidence

### Hayek J 2022

Hayek J, Schneider F, Lahoud N, Tueni M, de Vries H. (2022). Authoritative parenting stimulates academic achievement, also partly via self-efficacy and intention towards getting good grades. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265595

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariate linear regression (Model 2) in a two-wave study of 345 adolescents from private and public schools in Beirut and Mount Lebanon, with parenting style measured at t2 and grades at t3. Authoritative parenting was the reference group; all three contrasts were significant, with "β: -1.07; 95% CI -1.69, -0.45" for neglectful vs authoritative.

> "Adolescents of authoritative parents were significantly more likely to have higher academic achievement at six months follow-up compared to adolescents of neglectful (β: -1.07; 95% CI -1.69, -0.45), permissive (β: -0.68; 95% CI -1.29, -0.07) and authoritarian parents (β: -0.83; 95% CI -1.39, -0.27)."
