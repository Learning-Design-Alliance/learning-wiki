Evidence¶

Ozcelik 2013¶

Ozcelik, E., Ercil Cagiltay, N., & Sahin Ozcelik, N. (2013). The effect of uncertainty on learning in game-like environments. Computers & Education, 67, 12–20. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2013.02.009

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

A randomized experiment with 140 undergraduate students assigned to uncertain (n = 71) or certain (n = 69) versions of a game-like ERD learning tool found the uncertain group "performed signiﬁcantly better than the certain group in the post-test", with "Cohen ’s d ¼ 0.20".

"Whereas, Welch ’s t-test showed that the uncertain group performed signiﬁcantly better than the certain group in the post-test, t(133) ¼ 2.58, p ¼ 0.01 (see Table 3). The effect size was small (Cohen ’s d ¼ 0.20)."

Ozcelik 2013 (2)¶

Ozcelik, E., Ercil Cagiltay, N., & Sahin Ozcelik, N. (2013). The effect of uncertainty on learning in game-like environments. Computers & Education, 67, 12–20. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2013.02.009

q3 · i? · causal · r2

An ANCOVA on post-test scores adjusting for prior knowledge in the same randomized experiment showed the "effect of the group remained signiﬁcant", indicating the uncertainty effect persisted "after controlling for the differences in the prior knowledge".

"The effect of the group remained signiﬁcant, F(1, 134) ¼ 5.46, p ¼ 0.02, suggesting that the effect of uncertainty on the post-test existed even after controlling for the differences in the prior knowledge between the groups."
