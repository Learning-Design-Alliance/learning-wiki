## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in transition from naive to expert understanding use different mental models on different questions that an expert would treat identically, and this inconsistency is a measurable property of the individual student rather than random error. [→ Lei Bao and Edward F. Redish 2002](#lei-bao-and-edward-f-redish-2002)

## Evidence

### Lei Bao and Edward F. Redish 2002

Lei Bao and Edward F. Redish. (2002). Model Analysis: Assessing the Dynamics of Student Learning. Originally published 2001. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207069](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0207069)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article proposes that inconsistent student responses across expert-equivalent questions reflect a fundamental probabilistic character of individual cognition, not sampling error. "We view the individual student who is in a mixed state as simultaneously occupying a number of distinct model states with different probabilities."

> "If the student uses a mixture of distinct mental models in response to the set of questions we say the student is in a mixed model state. We view the individual student who is in a mixed state as simultaneously occupying a number of distinct model states with different probabilities."
