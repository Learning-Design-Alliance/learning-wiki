## Subclaims
`q2 i0` In the first-year lab, the correlation between overall E-CLASS score and final grade is small and not statistically significant (r = 0.01, p = 0.7). [→ Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015](#bethany-r-wilcox-and-h-j-lewandowski-2015)

## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Correlating students’ beliefs about experimental physics with lab course success. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.087](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.087)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of matched post-test E-CLASS scores and final grades within the first-year lab course: the correlation "is small and not statistically signiﬁcant (r = 0.01, p = 0.7)". No effect size beyond the printed r is reported.

> "For students in the ﬁrst-year lab, the correlation be- tween overall E-CLASS score and ﬁnal grade is small and not statistically signiﬁcant (r = 0.01, p = 0.7)."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Correlating students’ beliefs about experimental physics with lab course success. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.087](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.087)

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

The same correlational analysis within the beyond-first-year (second-, third-, and fourth-year) labs: "this correlation increases to r = 0.19 and is statistically signiﬁcant ( p = 0 .02)", a weak association the authors liken to CLASS/MPEX correlations with conceptual gains.

> "However, for the BFY labs, this correlation increases to r = 0.19 and is statistically signiﬁcant ( p = 0 .02)."
