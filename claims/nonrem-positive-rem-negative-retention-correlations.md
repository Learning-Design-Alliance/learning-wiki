# Retention of word pairs across sleep correlates positively with the percentage of NonREM sleep and negatively with the percentage of REM sleep

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Across both sleep conditions, retention correlated positively with NonREM sleep percentage (r = 0.22 Sleep-Wake; r = 0.27 Wake-Sleep) and negatively with REM percentage (r = 0.35 and r = 0.34 as printed magnitudes). [→ Backhaus et al. 2008](#backhaus-et-al-2008)

## Evidence

### Backhaus et al. 2008

Jutta Backhaus, Ralf Hoeckesfeld, Jan Born, Fritz Hohagen, Klaus Junghanns. (2008). Immediate as well as delayed post learning sleep but not wakefulness enhances declarative memory consolidation in children. Neurobiology of Learning and Memory 89, 76-80. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2007.08.010

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of polysomnography from the 27-child study. Table 3 prints REM % correlations of r = 0.35 (Sleep-Wake) and r = 0.34 (Wake-Sleep, p ≤ 0.05); NonREM correlations of r = 0.22 and r = 0.27 (p < 0.1). Only stage 4 in Wake-Sleep (r = 0.31) was individually significant.

> "Retention of word pairs across sleep – deﬁned by retrieval performance after sleep (ﬁrst delayed retrieval) minus performance at learning before sleep in the Sleep–Wake condition and by retrieval after sleep (second delayed retrieval) minus performance at ﬁrst retrieval before sleep in the Wake–Sleep condition—correlated positively with the percentage of NonREM sleep (i.e., summed percentages stages 2, 3 and 4; Fig. 3a and b) and negatively with the percentage of REM sleep"
