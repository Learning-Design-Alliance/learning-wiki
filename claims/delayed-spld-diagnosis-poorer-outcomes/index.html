Review synthesis (citing Gray): many students recognize their SpLD only after entering medical school, when previously effective compensatory strategies may become insufficient; the association between late diagnosis and poorer final-year performance is observational.

Related Claims¶

  • Study demands lead to exhaustion and self-undermining behaviors, instigating a loss spiral — related
  • Psychological distress, particularly depression, consistently predicted poorer academic outcomes among university students across the reviewed literature — related
  • Medical students with SpLDs report persistent academic, emotional, and social challenges including reading and writing difficulties, time pressure, anxiety, and isolation — related
  • With accommodations such as additional examination time, medical students with SpLDs show no statistically significant difference in MCQ performance (d = 0.045) — related
  • Prevalence of SpLDs among medical students averages 5.5%, ranging from 2% to 11.5% across studies — related
  • Parity in MCQ assessments does not extend to other formats: lower performance reported in EMQ, SAQ, OSCE, and delayed progression — related
