# Male top-performing math students report higher math self-efficacy and stronger math-oriented motivation than female top performers

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Male top performers report higher intentions to focus on math (d = 0.27) and higher instrumental math motivation (d = 0.16), math self-concept (d = 0.15), and greater interest in math (d = 0.10) than female top performers. [→ Keller 2021](#keller-2021)

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic synthesis of the remaining math motivation measures. Male top performers showed printed effects of d = 0.27 (math intentions), d = 0.16 (instrumental motivation), d = 0.15 (self-concept), and d = 0.10 (interest) relative to female top performers.

> "Similarly, male students reported higher intentions to focus on math than female students did (i.e., male students reported a higher intention to choose additional math courses in school and beyond compared with additional language or science courses; d = 0.27). Furthermore, male students reported, on average, higher instrumental math motivation (d = 0.16), higher math self-concept (d = 0.15), and greater interest in math (d = 0.10) than female students"
