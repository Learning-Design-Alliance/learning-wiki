## Evidence

### Kumwenda B 2017

Kumwenda B, Cleland JA, Walker K, Lee AJ, Greatrix R. (2017). The relationship between school type and academic performance at medical school: a national, multi-cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-016291

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Retrospective multi-cohort study of 33 UK medical schools using mixed-effects binary logistic regression on EPM rankings. State-funded students had "almost twice as likely (OR=2.01, 95% CI 1.49 to 2.73)" odds of finishing in the highest EPM rank than independent school students.

> "However, students from state-funded schools were almost twice as likely (OR=2.01, 95% CI 1.49 to 2.73) to finish in the highest rank of the EPM ranking than those who attended independent schools."

### Kumwenda B 2017 (2)

Kumwenda B, Cleland JA, Walker K, Lee AJ, Greatrix R. (2017). The relationship between school type and academic performance at medical school: a national, multi-cohort study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-016291

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

The 2013 cohort mixed-effects logistic regression confirmed the direction of the effect with a smaller magnitude: state-funded students had OR=1.36 (95% CI 1.14 to 1.64), being "1.36 times (95% CI 1.14 to 1.64)" more likely to finish in the top 20% of EPM than independently schooled students.

> "There was some consistency regarding students from the state-funded schools in that students from this cohort were 1.36 times (95% CI 1.14 to 1.64) as more likely than independently schooled students to finish in the top 20% of the EPM."
