## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In one fully online physics course, collaborative case-problem solving via breakout rooms and whiteboard annotation took place productively, though slower than in a physical classroom. [→ Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen 2021](#da-yang-tan-jer-ming-chen-2021)

## Evidence

### Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen 2021

Da Yang Tan & Jer-Ming Chen. (2021). Bringing Physical Physics Classroom On line – Challenges of Online Teaching in the New Normal. The Physics Teacher (submitted). Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1119/5.0028641](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0028641)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Practitioner experience report from a 13-week fully online summer physics course of 34 students with two instructors. The authors report from their own observations that discussions in breakout rooms were "productive, albeit at a slower pace" and that instructors could visit rooms as they would groups in a physical classroom.

> "Nonetheless, we found that such collaborative discussions could take place productively, albeit at a slower pace. More importantly, instructors were able to go to the different breakout rooms to observe the ongoing discussions"
