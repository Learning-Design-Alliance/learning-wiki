# Children with ADHD show lower oral contextual and non-contextual reading fluency than controls despite intact word reading

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Jacobson 2011

Jacobson, L. A., Ryan, M., Martin, R. B., Ewen, J., Mostofsky, S. H., Denckla, M. B., & Mahone, E. M. (2011). Working Memory Influences Processing Speed and Reading Fluency in ADHD. Child Neuropsychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2010.532204

`q3 · i?`

Group comparison of 41 ADHD and 21 control children, all screened to exclude word reading deficits (below 25th percentile on WJ-III Basic Reading), found "significantly lower scores on both oral contextual reading fluency" and TOWRE PDE after controlling for sex and GAI. Silent fluency (WJ-III RF, TOWRE SWE) differences were not significant.

> "After controlling for sex and GAI, children with ADHD had significantly lower scores on both oral contextual reading fluency (GORT-IV Fluency, ΔR2 = .064, p = .032) and oral non-contextual fluency (TOWRE Phonetic Decoding; ΔR2 = .075, p = .030)."
