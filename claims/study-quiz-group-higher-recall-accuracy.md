## Evidence

### Taveira-Gomes T 2015

Taveira-Gomes T, Prado-Costa R, Severo M, Ferreira MA. (2015). Characterization of medical students recall of factual knowledge using learning objects and repeated testing in a novel e-learning system. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0275-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized laboratory experiment with 96 medical students (94 completed s0; 47 per group at end) split by simple randomization into quiz and study-quiz groups across three weekly sessions. The study-quiz group "achieved a sharper increase in recall accuracy than the quiz group"; the printed eta-squared values were 0.32 (session) and 0.27 (group).

> "In the study-quiz group, recall accuracy increased from 27% at s0 to 73% at s1 (p <0.001) to 82% at s2 (p <0.001). At session s0, there were no differences in recall accuracy between groups. During s1 and s2, recall accuracy differences between groups were statistically significant (p <0.001)."

### Taveira-Gomes T 2015 (2)

Taveira-Gomes T, Prado-Costa R, Severo M, Ferreira MA. (2015). Characterization of medical students recall of factual knowledge using learning objects and repeated testing in a novel e-learning system. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0275-0

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA with group as between-subjects factor and session and flashcard as within-subject factors, reported in the Results section. The printed F-values were 292.2 (session) and 121.2 (group), with eta-squared 0.32 and 0.27 respectively.

> "F-values were 292.2 for the session and 121.2 for the group. Eta-squared values were 0.32 for the session and 0.27 for the group."
