## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In memory for spoken sentences, first-grade children accessed fewer sentences than sixth-grade children even though sentence-completion knowledge was equal across ages. [→ What is Working Memory? An Introduction and Review 2012](#what-is-working-memory-an-introduction-and-review-2012)

## Evidence

### What is Working Memory? An Introduction and Review 2012

What is Working Memory? An Introduction and Review. (2012). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_memory

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

Review reports the Gilchrist, Cowan, and Naveh-Benjamin study of memory for unrelated spoken sentences, using access rate as the capacity measure; first graders accessed "about 2.5 sentences vs. 4 sentences" for sixth graders.

> "Nevertheless, the number of sentences accessed was considerably smaller in first-grade children than in sixth-grade children (about 2.5 sentences vs. 4 sentences)."

### What is Working Memory? An Introduction and Review 2012 (2)

What is Working Memory? An Introduction and Review. (2012). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_memory

`q2 · i?` · `review · r?`

Same sentence-memory study: the linguistic-knowledge measure, completion rate, was "about 80% for both first and sixth grader children", so the capacity difference was not attributable to knowledge for these simple sentences.

> "This sentence completion rate was about 80% for both first and sixth grader children, suggesting that for these simple sentences, there was no age difference in knowledge."
