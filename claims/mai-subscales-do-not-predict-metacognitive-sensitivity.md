# Self-report metacognitive knowledge (MAI subscales) does not significantly predict metacognitive sensitivity

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the knowledge of cognition nor the regulation of cognition subscale of the MAI significantly predicted metacognitive sensitivity, contrary to the hypothesis of a positive association. [→ Terneusen 2024](#terneusen-2024)

## Evidence

### Terneusen 2024

Terneusen, A., Quaedflieg, C., van Heugten, C., Ponds, R., & Winkens, I. (2024). The many facets of metacognition: comparing multiple measures of metacognition in healthy individuals. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-023-09350-1

`q3 · i?`

Null result from the same multiple regression in the 128-participant study. The article reports the MAI subscales "did not significantly predict metacognitive sensitivity (all p >. 703)"; no effect size is printed, so equivalence was not tested.

> "MAI knowledge of cognition and regulation of cognition subscales did not significantly predict metacognitive sensitivity (all p >. 703)."
