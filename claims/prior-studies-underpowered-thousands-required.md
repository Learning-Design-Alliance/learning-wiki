# Prior small-sample critical-period findings were too noisy to provide precise estimates; thousands of subjects are required

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Bootstrapping simulations show that prior findings, such as Johnson and Newport's correlation contrast, fall within the range expected for N = 69, and that reliable conclusions require thousands of subjects. [→ Hartshorne Joshua K. 2018](#hartshorne-joshua-k-2018)

## Evidence

### Hartshorne Joshua K. 2018

Hartshorne Joshua K., Tenenbaum Joshua B., Pinker Steven. (2018). A critical period for second language acquisition: Evidence from 2/3 million English speakers. Cognition. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6559801/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapping simulation resampling from the authors' own data at N = 69, N = 275, and N = 11,371. Prior correlation findings were "well within what one would expect for N = 69", and variability dropped as power increased.

> "All these prior findings are well within what one would expect for N = 69 (Fig. 8, upper left). As power increased, the variability in the estimates dropped dramatically, with more highly-powered studies being increasingly unlikely to find any substantial difference in the correlations before and after 16 years old."
