## Evidence

### James Wells 2020

James Wells, Rachel Henderson, Adrienne Traxler, Paul Miller, and John Stewart. (2020). Exploring the Structure of Misconceptions in the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation with Modified Module Analysis. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010121)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

MMA analysis of the balanced men's and women's subsamples of the FMCE dataset (3956 pretest, 3719 post-test responses, 80% men overall, downsampled for balance). The article reports "only three out of the nine communities were the same" on the pretest and two of nine on the post-test.

> "The communities identiﬁed for men and women were often diﬀerent; on the FMCE pretest, only three out of the nine communities were the same, while on the FMCE post-test, two out of the nine were the same."

### James Wells 2020 (2)

James Wells, Rachel Henderson, Adrienne Traxler, Paul Miller, and John Stewart. (2020). Exploring the Structure of Misconceptions in the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation with Modified Module Analysis. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010121](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010121)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same MMA analysis, comparing pre- and post-instruction community structure by gender. Women's communities were "completely consistent" across the term, while men's five pretest communities reduced to two post-instruction, partly from Force Sled and Force Graph communities merging.

> "For women, the misconception com- munities identiﬁed were completely consistent from the pretest to the post-test. For men, of the ﬁve commu- nities identiﬁed pre-instruction, only two were identi- ﬁed post-instruction."
