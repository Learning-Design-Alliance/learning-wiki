# VR learning produced higher hedonic-quality and overall user-experience ratings than slideshow learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The VR group rated hedonic quality and overall UEQ score significantly higher than the slideshow group, with no difference in pragmatic quality. [→ Battipede 2025](#battipede-2025)

## Evidence

### Battipede 2025

Battipede, E., Giangualano, A., Boffi, P., Clerici, M., Calvi, A., Cassenti, L., Cialini, R., Van Den Weghe, T. L. A., Addimando, L., Lanzi, P. L., Gallace, A. (2025). Physics Playground: Insights from a Qualitative-Quantitative Study about VR-Based Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-88881-6_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-88881-6_8)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

UEQ-S data from both conditions of the quantitative study (44 participants). The "VR group exhibiting higher evaluations of both HQ" and Overall score; no significant differences were found for pragmatic quality.

> "An independent samples Welch's t -test revealed significant differences between conditions in HQ (t(30.8) = −6.957, p < .001) and Overall score (t(31.2) = −4.481, p < .001), with the VR group exhibiting higher evaluations of both HQ ( M = 2.22, SD = 0.67) and Overall score (M = 2.04, SD = 0.54) compared to the control group"
