# Offering Supplemental Instruction raises first-year students' course grades (d = 0.26) in a randomized field experiment

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Students offered SI obtained significantly higher grades than control students, with a treatment effect of d = 0.26 (0.45 grade points). [→ Dekker 2022](#dekker-2022)

## Evidence

### Dekker 2022

Dekker, I., Luberti, M., & Stam, J. (2022). Effects of supplemental instruction on grades, mental well-being, and belonging: A field experiment. https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6858-4001

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered randomized field experiment: 493 first-year students from 10 courses of study randomly allocated to being offered SI or not; grades analyzed with multilevel regression. The covariate-adjusted treatment effect was "a Cohen’s d of 0.26, and a difference of 0.45 grade points" (p = 0.008).

> "When we controlled for demographic covariates (age, gender, previous education) the treatment explained 1.62% of the variance (r2 = 0.02, p = 0.008), which equals a Cohen’s d of 0.26, and a difference of 0.45 grade points"
