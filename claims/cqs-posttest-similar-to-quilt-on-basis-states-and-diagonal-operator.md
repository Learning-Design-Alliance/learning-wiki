# Upper-level QM students engaging with the addition-of-angular-momentum CQS achieved posttest scores on constructing basis states (question (a)) and on matrix elements of the magnetic field-spin Hamiltonian (question (c)) similar to students who engaged with the QuILT

## Evidence

### Paul Justice 2020

Paul Justice, Emily Marshman, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Development and validation of a sequence of clicker questions for helping students learn addition of angular momentum in quantum mechanics. Physics Education Research Conference proceedings. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.07145](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.07145)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

In-class evaluation comparing an upper-level QM class using the CQS with peer instruction (N=16, Table 1) against previously published QuILT data (N=26, Table 2). The authors report "garnering similar posttest scores to th ose of students who engaged with the QuILT" on questions (a) and (c); no effect size is available for the QuILT comparison.

> "the comparison of the posttest scores of the CQS group and the QuILT group in Tables I and II suggests that the CQS is effective in helping students learn to construct a complete set of basis states (question (a )) and calculate matrix elements for the magnetic field -spin interaction Hamilton ian (question ( c)), garnering similar posttest scores to th ose of students who engaged with the QuILT."
