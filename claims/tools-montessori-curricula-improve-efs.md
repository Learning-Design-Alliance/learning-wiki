## Evidence

### Diamond Adele 2011

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Tools was evaluated against another high-quality program using EF measures requiring transfer of training; benefits required EF supports interwoven into all academic activities all day.

> "Tools 5-year-olds outperformed control children on both EF measures, which taxed all 3 core EFs, and especially on the more EF-demanding conditions. Thus the program with more play produced better EFs than the one with more direct instruction."

### Diamond Adele 2011 (2)

Diamond Adele, Lee Kathleen. (2011). Interventions shown to Aid Executive Function Development in Children 4–12 Years Old. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1204529

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports a lottery-based comparison of children entering an AMI-approved Montessori public school versus lottery peers, assessed at end of Kindergarten and end of Grade 6.

> "At age 5, Montessori children showed better EFs than peers attending other schools. They performed better in reading and math and showed more concern for fairness and justice. No group difference was found in delay of gratification."
