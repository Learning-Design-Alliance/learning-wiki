# Children's age did not moderate the link between bicultural identity and cognitive flexibility

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The three-way Turkish identity × German identity × age interaction on mixed-trial accuracy was not significant (β = 0.01, SE = 0.01, p = 0.643). [→ Spiegler O and Leyendecker B 2017](#spiegler-o-and-leyendecker-b-2017)

## Evidence

### Spiegler O and Leyendecker B 2017

Spiegler O and Leyendecker B (2017) Balanced Cultural Identities Promote Cognitive Flexibility among Immigrant Children. Front. Psychol. 8:1579. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01579

`q3 · i?`

A multivariate regression with a three-way interaction term (Turkish identity × German identity × age), reported in a footnote, tested whether age moderated the biculturalism–flexibility link in the sample of 225 children aged 9–15; it was not significant.

> "This interaction was not significant, β= 0.01, SE= 0.01, p= 0.643, indicating that the link between biculturalism and cognitive ﬂexibility did not depend on children's age."
