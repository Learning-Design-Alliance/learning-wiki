# GRE Quantitative and Verbal scores moderately predict first-semester grades in biomedical graduate coursework

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` A one standard deviation increase in GRE Quantitative was associated with a 0.29 standard deviation increase in First Semester Grades in the bivariate model, attenuating to a 0.16 standard deviation change after controls. [→ Moneta-Koehler L 2017](#moneta-koehler-l-2017)

## Evidence

### Moneta-Koehler L 2017

Moneta-Koehler L, Brown AM, Petrie KA, Evans BJ, Chalkley R. (2017). The Limitations of the GRE in Predicting Success in Biomedical Graduate School. PLoS ONE 12(1): e0166742. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0166742

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Standardized linear regressions on 488 students predicting First Semester Grades (Table 3); the article states standardized coefficients "reflect effect sizes", with β = 0.16 for GRE Quantitative in the full model, and the model accounted for 40% of variance, strongly driven by cohort fixed effects.

> "Standardized regression coefficients reflect effect sizes such that, for example, one standard deviation change in GRE Quantitative is associated with a 0.16 standard deviation change in First Semester GPA."
