# In simulation, effort-moderated scoring (EM-CAT and EG-CAT) yields negligible bias and nominal 95% confidence interval coverage, unlike MLE-CAT scoring

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For simulated test takers with RTE less than .50, MLE-CAT mean bias exceeded 16 RIT points while EM-CAT and EG-CAT showed negligible bias; MLE-CAT confidence intervals captured the true achievement level only 16% of the time versus 95% for the effort-moderated CATs. [→ Wise 2015](#wise-2015)

## Evidence

### Wise 2015

Wise, S. L., & Kingsbury, G. G. (2015). Modeling Student Test-Taking Motivation in the Context of an Adaptive Achievement Test. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Modeling+Student+Test-Taking+Motivation+in+the+Context+of+an+Adaptive+Achievement+Test

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simulation of three CAT types (MLE-CAT, EM-CAT, EG-CAT) with 51,764 simulated test takers, using the real mathematics item pool and motivational templates from Study 1 rapid-guessing patterns. Table 2 reports mean bias, SEM, and 95% confidence interval capture rates by RTE interval.

> "for test takers with RTE less than .50, mean bias exceeded 16 RIT points. In contrast, both the EM-CAT and the EG-CAT exhibited negligible mean bias throughout the RTE range."
