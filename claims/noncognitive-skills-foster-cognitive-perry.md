## Evidence

### Cunha 2007

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper cites the random-assignment evaluation of the Perry Preschool Program (Schweinhart et al., 2005): no adult IQ boost but lasting gains in achievement tests, employment, and reduced crime and social pathologies through age 40.

> "The Perry Preschool Program, which was evaluated by random assignment, did not boost participant adult IQ but enhanced performance of participants on a number of dimensions, including scores on achievement tests, employment and reduced participation in a variety of social pathologies."

### Cunha 2007 (2)

Flavio Cunha and James Heckman. (2007). The Technology of Skill Formation. NBER Working Paper No. 12840. http://www.nber.org/papers/w12840

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The paper cites Duncan et al. (2006) and Raver et al. (2006) showing attention ability, interpreted as a socioemotional skill, facilitates later learning. Animal studies (Suomi, Meaney) document mechanisms relating cortisol, stress, and brain development.

> "Duncan et al. (2006) and Raver et al. (2006) show that a child's ability to pay attention facilitates later learning."
