Evidence¶

Nicolas Rodriguez-Alvarez 2026¶

Nicolas Rodriguez-Alvarez, Alan Martin Blanch-Marsolini, Samuel Vara-Gutierrez, Hugo Gil-Garcia, Javier Calzon-Dueñas, and Fernando Rodriguez-Merino. (2026). The Illusion of Competence: Self-Perceived Digital Literacy and AI Readiness Among European Secondary Students. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26010 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.26010

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Independent samples t-test with Welch's correction on the survey's gender-based comparison (n=241 after excluding negligible non-binary responses) found males scored higher, a difference the authors call statistically significant.

"male students consistently reported higher generalized digital confidence (𝑀= 4.05) compared to their female counterparts (𝑀= 3.89). This difference was found to be statistically significant (𝑝= 0.001)."
