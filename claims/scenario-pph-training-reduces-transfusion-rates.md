# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, post-birth whole blood transfusion rates fell from 3.2% to 1.7% after multi-professional, scenario-based PPH training (47% reduction)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After multi-professional scenario-based PPH training, the proportion of mothers receiving whole blood transfusions fell from 3.2% to 1.7%, a statistically significant 47% reduction. [→ Egenberg 2017](#egenberg-2017)

## Evidence

### Egenberg 2017

Egenberg, S., Masenga, G., Bru, L. E., Eggebø, T. M., Mushi, C., Massay, D., & Øian, P. (2017). Impact of multi-professional, scenario-based training on postpartum hemorrhage in Tanzania: a quasi-experimental, pre- vs. post-intervention study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1478-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Random-sample file review at KCMC comparing 1667 pre-training and 1641 post-training patient records; "The 47% reduction in number of mothers who were transfused, was statistically significant (p < 0.01)" — 53 vs 28 mothers transfused (3.2% vs 1.7%).

> "Compared to pre-training when 53 mothers received a total of 79 units of whole blood transfusions (average 1.5 unit), post-training data showed that 28 mothers received 50 units of whole blood transfusions (average 1.8 unit). The 47% reduction in number of mothers who were transfused, was statistically significant (p < 0.01)."

### Egenberg 2017 (2)

Egenberg, S., Masenga, G., Bru, L. E., Eggebø, T. M., Mushi, C., Massay, D., & Øian, P. (2017). Impact of multi-professional, scenario-based training on postpartum hemorrhage in Tanzania: a quasi-experimental, pre- vs. post-intervention study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1478-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' check on the post-training year: transfusions were spread evenly across the year, so they judged an attention-effect (Hawthorne) explanation unlikely.

> "Out of 28 mothers who received whole blood transfusions the first year after training, they were equally divided throughout the year. A Hawthorne effect explaining the reduction in blood transfusions, was unlikely."
