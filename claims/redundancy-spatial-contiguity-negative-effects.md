# Adherence to the Redundancy and Spatial Contiguity principles shows small negative effects on quiz performance in this physics seminar context

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students performed better on questions for presentations that included redundant on-screen text (d = −0.25) and that did not place corresponding text and pictures close together (d = −0.22). [→ Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz 2024](#steven-w-tarr-and-emily-alicea-munoz-2024)

## Evidence

### Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz 2024

Steven W. Tarr and Emily Alicea-Muñoz. (2024). Reflecting to learn in a physics multimedia communication course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2024.pr.tarr](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2024.pr.tarr)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Table I reports Redundancy d = −0.25 ± 0.06 (p <0.001) and Spatial Contiguity d = −0.22 ± 0.08 (p <0.01), reversing the direction of Mayer's published effects for these principles in this classroom setting.

> "Further, we observed moderately small negative effects from adherence to the Redundancy and Spatial Contiguity principles."
