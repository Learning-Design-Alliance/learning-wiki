## Evidence

### Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach 2015

Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach. (2015). Investigating student understanding of quantum entanglement. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Advanced-level 2014 post-test responses; the article reports "5 students (21%)" stated the given state could not be factorized and was entangled, not recognizing its factorized product form.

> "In the advanced level course, 5 students (21%) stated on question 1 of the post -test (Fig. 3) that 1/√2 (| ↓𝐴〉| ↑𝐵〉  − | ↓𝐴〉|↓𝐵〉) is an entangled state as it could not be factorized, i.e. did not recognize that this is the product state 1/√2 | ↓𝐴〉 (| ↑𝐵〉 −  | ↓𝐵〉)."

### Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach 2015 (2)

Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach. (2015). Investigating student understanding of quantum entanglement. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

2015 introductory post-test responses; the article reports "27 students in total did not factorize states correctly", including responses that incorrectly factorized entangled states. The 2013 activity did not include questions assessing this difficulty.

> "For the 2015 trial, 27 students in total did not factorize states correctly in the post -test (some responses incorrectly factorized entangled states)."
