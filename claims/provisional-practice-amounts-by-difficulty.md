## Subclaims
`q1 i?` For one of the easier bonds, most facilitated by other bonds, the median pupil needs about twelve practices in the week of first learning, twenty-five during the two months following, and thirty well spread later; for bonds of greater difficulty, less facilitated by other bonds, the practice may be from ten to a hundred percent more. ## Evidence

### Thorndike 1921

Thorndike, E. L. (1921). The psychology of drill in arithmetic: The amount of practice. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 12(4). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/edu/12/4

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author hazards these estimates assuming mastery to 199 correct out of 200 by end of grade VI, teaching by an intelligent person in accord with psychological principles. Gifted pupils may suffice with six, twelve and fifteen; less gifted up to thirty, fifty and a hundred. These are explicitly provisional, awaiting suitable experiments.

> "For one of the easier bonds, most facilitated by other bonds (such as 2 X 5 = 10, or 10 — 2 = 8, or the double bond 7 = two threes and 1 remainder) in the case of the median or average pupil, twelve practices in the week of first learning, supported by twenty-five practices during the two months following, and maintained by thirty practices well spread over the later periods."
