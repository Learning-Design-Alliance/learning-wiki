# Blended teaching drives local-attribute knowledge construction: a reversed boundary effect in MFC during LH lecturing versus a standard boundary effect in TPJ during H-only lecturing

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` During LH lecturing, MFC showed a significantly reversed boundary effect (non-boundary higher than boundary), suggesting re-segmentation of knowledge events. [→ X. Feng 2025](#x-feng-2025)
`q3 i2` During H-only lecturing, TPJ showed a significant boundary effect (boundary higher than non-boundary), suggesting representation of the original segmentation. [→ X. Feng 2025 (2)](#x-feng-2025-2)

## Evidence

### X. Feng 2025

X. Feng, X. Xu, Z. Meng, J. Jiang, M. Pei, Y. Zheng, C. Lu. (2025). A Rapid Cortical Learning Process Supporting Students’ Knowledge Construction During Real Classroom Teaching. Advanced Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202416610

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Boundary-effect analysis of fNIRS activity during LH lecturing, comparing 15-s post-offset boundary patterns with mid-event non-boundary patterns. The article reports "Cohen's d = −0.623"; no such effect in TPJ (p > 0.05).

> "the results showed a significantly reversed boundary effect, with the non‐boundary pattern being significantly higher than the boundary pattern (paired sample t‐test: t (20) = −2.854, p = 0.010, Cohen's d = −0.623"

### X. Feng 2025 (2)

X. Feng, X. Xu, Z. Meng, J. Jiang, M. Pei, Y. Zheng, C. Lu. (2025). A Rapid Cortical Learning Process Supporting Students’ Knowledge Construction During Real Classroom Teaching. Advanced Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202416610

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

The same boundary analysis applied to the H-only lecturing phase found the standard effect in TPJ, with "Cohen's d = 0.570", and none in MFC, indicating TPJ represented the original knowledge structure.

> "significant boundary effect was found in the TPJ, that is, higher boundary pattern than non‐boundary pattern (paired two‐sample t‐test: t (20) = 2.612, p = 0.017, Cohen's d = 0.570"
