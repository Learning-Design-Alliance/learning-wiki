# L2 reading speed (SLRT-I) and L2 spelling showed no improvement as a function of the intervention, and no group made significant gains in L2 spelling

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No significant within-group differences emerged between any pre- and posttest on SLRT-I reading speed; reading speed underwent no improvement as a function of the intervention. [→ Pfenninger 2015](#pfenninger-2015)
`q2 i?` None of the groups made significant gains over time in L2 spelling, and differences between dyslexic and non-dyslexic learners in total errors were significant on pretest and posttest (in all cases U = 0.0, p < .001). [→ Pfenninger 2015 (2)](#pfenninger-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Pfenninger 2015

Pfenninger, S. E. (2015). MSL in the digital ages: Effects and effectiveness of computer-mediated intervention for FL learners with dyslexia. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching, 5(1), 109-133. https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2015.5.1.6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-post comparisons on the SLRT-I L2 word and pseudoword reading fluency measures in the intervention study; all 20 learners without dyslexia had significantly higher reading speed than the 20 dyslexic learners on all pre- and posttest measures.

> "no significant within-group differences emerged between any pre- and posttest; in other words, the reading speed underwent no improvement as a function of the intervention"

### Pfenninger 2015 (2)

Pfenninger, S. E. (2015). MSL in the digital ages: Effects and effectiveness of computer-mediated intervention for FL learners with dyslexia. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching, 5(1), 109-133. https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2015.5.1.6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

SLRT-II-L2 spelling of 48 monosyllabic and polysyllabic words; dyslexic learners produced significantly more orthographic errors than non-dyslexic readers on all measures (pre-SPELL-L2 orthographic: U = 0.0, p < .001).

> "None of the groups made signifi- cant gains over time in L2 spelling. The differences in the total number of errors and the total number of misspelled words between the dyslexic learners and the non-dyslexic learners was significant on the pretest as well as the posttest (in all cases U = 0.0, p < .001)"
