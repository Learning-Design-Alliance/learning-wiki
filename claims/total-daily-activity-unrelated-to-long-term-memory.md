# Total percentage of time spent sedentary or in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity during the day is unrelated to long-term memory retention in young adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After controlling for age, sex, and aerobic fitness, neither percent time sedentary nor percent time in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity over the whole day related to the proportion of paired-associate items maintained across 12 hours. [→ Pontifex 2016](#pontifex-2016)

## Evidence

### Pontifex 2016

Pontifex, M. B., Gwizdala, K. L., Parks, A. C., Pfeiffer, K. A. & Fenn, K. M. (2016). The Association between Physical Activity During the Day and Long-Term Memory Stability. Scientific Reports 6, 38148. https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38148

`q3 · i?`

Hierarchical regression on 92 undergraduates' paired-associate retention across a 12-hour interval, controlling for Age, Sex, and Aerobic Fitness. Percent time sedentary showed "No relationship to long-term memory" (β = −0.130, p = 0.19), as did moderate-to-vigorous activity (β = 0.119, p = 0.23). No effect sizes printed.

> "No relationship to long-term memory was observed for either the percentage of time spent sedentary (β = − 0.130, t (87) = 1.3, p = 0.19), or the percentage of time spent in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (β = 0.119, t (87) = 1.2, p = 0.23), after controlling for Age, Sex, and Aerobic Fitness"
