## Evidence

### Cano García 2024

Cano García, E., Halbaut Bellowa, L., Martins Gironelli, L., & Lluch Molins, L. (2024). Online peer assessment in Galenic Pharmacy: enhancing evaluative judgement in higher education. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2409487

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Descriptive comparison of 326 students' ratings across the two Moodle Workshop loops. The article reports the printed percentage differences: "17.8% in favor of feedback given" and "16.0% in favor of feedback received" in the first, reduced to "8.9% for scores of 4 and 3.7% for scores of 5" in the second.

> "in the first peer assessment (Figure 3), the difference in the frequency of scores of 4 between feedback received and feedback given was 17.8% in favor of feedback given, while for scores of 5, the difference was 16.0% in favor of feedback received. However, in the second peer assessment (Figure 4), these differences were notably reduced, being only 8.9% for scores of 4 and 3.7% for scores of 5."
