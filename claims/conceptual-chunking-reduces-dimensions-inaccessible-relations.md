### Graeme S. Halford 1998

Graeme S. Halford, William H. Wilson, Steven Phillips. (1998). Processing capacity defined by relational complexity: Implications for comparative, developmental, and cognitive psychology. Behavioral and Brain Sciences 21, 803-865. https://www.psy.uq.edu.au/people/Department/gsh/index.html

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the section on using capacity efficiently: concepts are recoded into fewer dimensions, and "conceptual chunks save processing capacity, but the cost is that some relations become temporarily inaccessible."

> "Thus conceptual chunks save processing capacity, but the cost is that some relations become temporarily inaccessible."

### Graeme S. Halford 1998 (2)

Graeme S. Halford, William H. Wilson, Steven Phillips. (1998). Processing capacity defined by relational complexity: Implications for comparative, developmental, and cognitive psychology. Behavioral and Brain Sciences 21, 803-865. https://www.psy.uq.edu.au/people/Department/gsh/index.html

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Illustrative example: "The relation between velocity, distance, and time is three dimensional", but chunking velocity to a single dial reading hides those relations; computing how velocity varies requires returning to the three-dimensional representation.

> "The relation between velocity, distance, and time is three dimensional, but velocity can be expressed as a singledimension, such as the position of a pointer on a dial; velocity(60 km/h)."
