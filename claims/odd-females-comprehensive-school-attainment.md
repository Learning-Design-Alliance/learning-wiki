# ODD symptoms in adolescent females predict adult educational attainment extending no further than comprehensive school; this relation is not significant in males after adjustment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among females, 7.9% of the ODD group versus 2.2% of controls stopped at comprehensive school (adjusted OR 2.82, 95% CI 1.12–7.13). [→ Seppä S 2023](#seppa-s-2023)
`q2 i?` The corresponding relation in males did not reach statistical significance after adjustment (adjusted OR 1.42, 95% CI 0.61–3.29). [→ Seppä S 2023 (2)](#seppa-s-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Seppä S 2023

Seppä S, Halt A-H, Nordström T, Hurtig T. (2023). Effects of Symptoms of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD) on Academic Performance and Educational Attainment. Child Psychiatry and Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-023-01598-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multinomial logistic regression of female educational attainment at 32 in NFBC1986, adjusted for parental education, family type, and other psychiatric disorders. Only odds ratios and confidence intervals were printed, no standardized effect size.

> "Among the females, 2.2% of the participants in the control group had comprehensive school as their highest educational attainment, while the figure for the ODD group was 7.9% (adjusted OR 2.82, 95% CI 1.12–7.13)"

### Seppä S 2023 (2)

Seppä S, Halt A-H, Nordström T, Hurtig T. (2023). Effects of Symptoms of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) and Oppositional Defiant Disorder (ODD) on Academic Performance and Educational Attainment. Child Psychiatry and Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-023-01598-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Authors' statement in the Discussion of the sex-specific pattern from the NFBC1986 multinomial regression (Table 5 shows male ODD adjusted OR 1.42, 95% CI 0.61–3.29). The male association was non-significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "symptoms of ODD in adolescent females predicted educational attainment in adulthood that extended no further than the compulsory comprehensive school level, but this relation did not reach statistical significance in the males after adjustment"
