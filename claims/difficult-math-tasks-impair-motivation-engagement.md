# Difficult math exercises impair elementary children's motivational, cognitive and affective task experiences relative to easier exercises

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In a randomized experiment, children solving difficult exercises reported more autonomy and competence frustration, less competence satisfaction, more irritation, less interest/enjoyment, and more cognitive disengagement than children solving easy-medium exercises. [→ Baten 2020](#baten-2020)

## Evidence

### Baten 2020

Baten, E., Vansteenkiste, M., De Muynck, G., De Poortere, E., Desoete, A. (2020). How Can the Blow of Math Difficulty on Elementary School Children's Motivational, Cognitive and Affective Experiences be Dampened? The Critical Role of Autonomy-Supportive Instructions. Journal of Educational Psychology. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000444

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

MANCOVA in a randomized 2x2 experiment with 479 fourth graders; difficulty main effect was significant, F(8, 370) = 10.49, p < .001, ηp² = .19. Children in the difficult condition "reported more autonomy and competence frustration and less 12 competence satisfaction" and more irritation and disengagement.

> "Difficulty level had a significant effect in the expected direction on all outcome 10 variables, except for the non-significant effect on autonomy satisfaction. Children solving the 11 more difficult exercises r eported more autonomy and competence frustration and less 12 competence satisfaction, they felt more irritated and perceived the exercises to be less enjoyable 13 and interesting."
