# Preclass 3D digital simulation before hands-on carving produces significantly higher practical carving test scores than traditional instruction alone in dental morphology courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Dental technology students who received preclass exocad-based 3D digital instruction scored significantly higher on a standardized PM1M plaster carving test than students receiving traditional instruction only (60.3 ± 8.5 vs 47.0 ± 5.9, P < .001). [→ Hsu Ming-Sung 2025](#hsu-ming-sung-2025)

## Evidence

### Hsu Ming-Sung 2025

Hsu Ming-Sung, Yeh Chun-Liang, Cheng Shih-Jung, Lin Chun-Pin. (2025). Transforming Dental Morphology Education: The Impact of Three-Dimensional Digital Simulation on Practical Skill Development. International Dental Journal. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.identj.2025.103904

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 86 second-year dental technology students (n = 43 per group, block randomization). In the Week 3 60-minute practical carving test, the experimental group's mean was "significantly higher mean score (60.3 ± 8.5)" than the control's; no effect size is printed.

> "the experimental group achieved a significantly higher mean score (60.3 ± 8.5) than the control group (47.0 ± 5.9) in the practical PM1M carving test (P < .001)"
