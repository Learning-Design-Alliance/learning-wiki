Evidence¶

Hertzog C 2011¶

Hertzog C, Dunlosky J. (2011). Metacognition in Later Adulthood: Spared Monitoring Can Benefit Older Adults' Self-regulation. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3903298/

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review of experimental studies comparing older adults to college students using extreme age-group designs, reporting that "Younger and older adults have above-chance and equivalent resolution of immediate JOLs for paired-associate items"; delayed JOLs likewise show equivalently high resolution for both ages.

"Younger and older adults have above-chance and equivalent resolution of immediate JOLs for paired-associate items"

Hertzog C 2011 (2)¶

Hertzog C, Dunlosky J. (2011). Metacognition in Later Adulthood: Spared Monitoring Can Benefit Older Adults' Self-regulation. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3903298/

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review summarises the authors' separate cross-sectional study (Hertzog, Sinclair, & Dunlosky, 2010, Developmental Psychology) of adults ages 18 to 81 who studied related and unrelated word pairs, made immediate JOLs, and completed paired-associate recall; regression of gamma correlations on age showed "No age declines in resolution arose across the adult life-span" and significant age-related increases in aggregate resolution.

"No age declines in resolution arose across the adult life-span. In fact, significant age-related increases occurred in the aggregate gamma correlations, as reflected in the positive slope of the black line."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Age did not interact with associative relatedness or effective encoding-strategy use in predicting JOLs, indicating no age differences in the use of these two sources of information when making judgments of learning — related
  • Older adults show lower JOL resolution only when recollection-based responses are contrasted with pooled familiarity and new responses, an outcome attributed to age-related decreases in strong recollective experiences at test — related
  • Older adults show no reliable deficit in assessing item difficulty (EOL accuracy) — related
  • Training metacognitive self-regulation (self-testing plus selective restudy) produced greater paired-associate learning improvements in older adults than traditional encoding-strategy training when study was self-paced — related
  • Reflective judgment scores increased consistently with age and educational level — related
  • Judgments of learning add no new self-assessment information beyond performance estimates for single tasks — related
  • Judgments of learning are higher for easy than difficult items at all ages and increase with age, confirming the difficulty manipulation aligned with subjective difficulty — related
  • JOL magnitudes decline across successive lists in both JOL groups, reflecting learners' belief that recall decreases with continued new learning — related
  • FOK resolution for general-knowledge information is similar for younger and older adults, whereas the literature argues for a deficit in FOK resolution for episodic memory tasks in older adults — related
  • JOL resolution (relative accuracy) did not differ between younger and older adults — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Metacognitive awareness of VSTM performance becomes less accurate with age but remains well above chance across the adult life span — related
