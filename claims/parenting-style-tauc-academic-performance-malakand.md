# Parenting style is significantly associated with secondary students' academic performance: positively for authoritative parenting and negatively for authoritarian and permissive parenting

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Ali Nayab 2023

Ali Nayab, Ullah Asad, Khan Abdul Majid, Khan Yunas, Ali Sajid, Khan Aisha, Bakhtawar, Khan Asad, Din Maaz Ud, Ullah Rahat, Khan Umar Niaz, Aziz Tariq, Ahmad Mushtaq. (2023). Academic performance of children in relation to gender, parenting styles, and socioeconomic status: What attributes are important. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0286823

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square and Kendall's Tau-c analysis of survey data from 448 students in District Malakand cross-tabulating parenting style with academic performance categories. The association was "highly significant (P = 0.000) and positive (Tc = 0.500)" for authoritative parenting; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Results in Table 3 unveil that the association of authoritative parenting style with children’s academic performance was highly significant (P = 0.000) and positive (Tc = 0.500)."

### Ali Nayab 2023 (2)

Ali Nayab, Ullah Asad, Khan Abdul Majid, Khan Yunas, Ali Sajid, Khan Aisha, Bakhtawar, Khan Asad, Din Maaz Ud, Ullah Rahat, Khan Umar Niaz, Aziz Tariq, Ahmad Mushtaq. (2023). Academic performance of children in relation to gender, parenting styles, and socioeconomic status: What attributes are important. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0286823

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same non-parametric analysis of the 448-student sample, reporting the permissive parenting association. The article reports a "highly significant and negative (P = 0.000, Tc = -0.030)" association; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Similarly, the association between permissive parenting style and children’s academic performance was found highly significant and negative (P = 0.000, Tc = -0.030)."
