## Evidence

### N.G. Holmes 2015

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cohort comparison within a calculus-based introductory physics lab course: experimental students (n ≈ 130) had been graded on iterating to improve measurements, and control students (n ≈ 130, prior year, same experiments) had not. The article reports "over half of the experimental group continued to make or propose changes" after instruction ended; no effect size is printed.

> "By the end of the course, when the instructions had been removed, over half of the experimental group continued to make or propose changes to their data or methods."

### N.G. Holmes 2015 (2)

N.G. Holmes, Carl E. Wieman, D.A. Bonn. (2015). Teaching critical thinking. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1505329112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of the measurement-change outcome using generalized linear mixed-effects models: a Type 3 ANOVA of the logistic regression showed condition and the condition-by-week interaction were "highly signiﬁcant in the model, but lab week alone was not signiﬁcant." Only test statistics are printed; no effect size.

> "The logistic regression model was statistically signiﬁcant, χ2(5) = 470.55,p < .001."
