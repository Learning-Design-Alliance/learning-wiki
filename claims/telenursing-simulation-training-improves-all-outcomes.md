# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, nursing students' awareness, knowledge, skills, attitudes, and self-efficacy scores rose and perceived barriers fell from pre-test to post-test after simulation-based telenursing training, with moderate to high effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In one uncontrolled pre-post study, awareness, knowledge, skills, attitudes, and self-efficacy scores rose from pre-test to post-test after simulation-based telenursing training, while perceived barriers fell, with Cohen d values from 0.36 to 1.85. [→ Ali-Saleh Ola 2025](#ali-saleh-ola-2025)

## Evidence

### Ali-Saleh Ola 2025

Ali-Saleh Ola, Massalha Layalleh, Halperin Ofra. (2025). Evaluation of a Simulation Program for Providing Telenursing Training to Nursing Students: Cohort Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/67804

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Cohort pre/post study of 114 third-year nursing students assessed with paired t tests and Cohen d. Table 1 reports, for example, awareness d=1.85 (t113=15.18, P<.001), attitudes d=1.04, knowledge d=0.76, barriers d=0.45, self-efficacy d=0.46, skills d=0.36; the article reports "moderate to high effect sizes".

> "Significant changes were noted in all variables with moderate to high effect sizes (Table 1). Awareness of telenursing, knowledge of telenursing, skills, attitudes, and self-efficacy in telenursing have all significantly increased following participation in the training program, and barriers to telenursing have significantly decreased."
