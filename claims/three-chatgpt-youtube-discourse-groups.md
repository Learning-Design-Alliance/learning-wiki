# Educational YouTube videos about ChatGPT fall into three structurally distinct discourse groups: learning-oriented scaffolding (G1), skill-building retrieval practice (G2), and output/productivity orientation (G3)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ENA of 52 videos identified three structurally distinct epistemic orientations: G1 (n=25) framed ChatGPT as a conceptual scaffold, G2 (n=10) as retrieval practice and skill-building, and G3 (n=17) as an output-generation tool. [→ Shayla Sharmin 2025](#shayla-sharmin-2025)

## Evidence

### Shayla Sharmin 2025

Shayla Sharmin, Mohammad Al-Ratrout, Mohammad Fahim Abrar, and Roghayeh Leila Barmaki. (2025). How YouTube Frames ChatGPT Use in Education: An Epistemic Network Analysis with Supporting Multimodal Metadata. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08698 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.08698](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.08698)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

PRISMA-selected corpus of 52 educational YouTube videos (557 coded chunks) analyzed with ENA using nine binary framing and strategy codes; group sizes were G1 n=25, G2 n=10, G3 n=17. The study "identified three structurally distinct discourse groups" with distinct epistemic orientations.

> "We identified three structurally distinct discourse groups: (𝐺1) videos that positioned ChatGPT as a conceptual scaffold for thinking, (𝐺2) videos oriented toward retrieval practice and skill-building, and (𝐺3) videos that framed ChatGPT as a tool for output generation."
