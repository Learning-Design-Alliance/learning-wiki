# The paper-versus-online participation difference was statistically significant for instructors 1-3 at both pre- and post-test, but for instructors 4-5 only at pre-test, with no significant post-test difference.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For instructors 1-3, online pre- and post-test participation was significantly lower than paper (p < 0.001); for instructors 4 and 5, only pre-test participation differed significantly, and post-test differences between formats were not statistically significant. [→ Manher Jariwala 2016](#manher-jariwala-2016)

## Evidence

### Manher Jariwala 2016

Manher Jariwala, Jada-Simone S. White, Ben Van Dusen, and Eleanor W. Close. (2016). In-class vs. online administration of concept inventories and attitudinal assessments. 2016 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.039

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square tests with Bonferroni corrections compared paper and online participation rates within each instructor's course sections; no statistically significant difference between paper and online post-test participation for instructors 4 and 5, so equivalence was not tested.

> "Instructors 1 -3 had statistically significant  lower participation rates on the online pre and posttests ( p < 0.001) as compared to paper. Instructors 4 and 5 had statistically significant  lower online pre-test participation rates  (p < 0.001) as compared to paper, but did not have statistically significant differences in post -test participation rates between their paper and online conditions."
