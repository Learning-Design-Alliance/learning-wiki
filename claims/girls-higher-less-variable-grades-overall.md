## Evidence

### O’Dea R. E. 2018

O’Dea R. E., Lagisz M., Jennions M. D. & Nakagawa S. (2018). Gender differences in individual variation in academic grades fail to fit expected patterns for STEM. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06292-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 346 effect sizes from 227 studies of teacher-assigned grades (820,158 female and 826,629 male students), using lnRR for means and lnCVR for variances. The analysis found girls' grades "significantly higher grades than boys by 6.3%" with "10.8% less variation among girls than among boys".

> "Overall, girls had significantly higher grades than boys by 6.3% (natural logarithm of response ratio (lnRRoverall(mean): 0.061, 95% confidence interval, CI: 0.052 to 0.070), with 10.8% less variation among girls than among boys"
