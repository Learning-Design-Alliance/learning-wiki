Sarré 2011¶

Sarré, C. (2011). Computer-mediated negotiated interactions: how is meaning negotiated in discussion boards, text chat and videoconferencing? In S. Thouësny & L. Bradley (Eds.), Second language teaching and learning with technology: views of emergent researchers (pp. 189-210). Dublin: Research-publishing.net. https://dx.doi.org/10.14705/rpnet.2011.9781908416001

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Analysis of interactions from 12 groups of four French NNS biology students completing five ESP scenarios online (24 chat logs, 24 discussion board files, 24 video files) shows that "the completion of meaning-focused tasks through computer-mediated communication does foster negotiated interactions" in all three modes.

"The very first conclusion that can be drawn from the analysis of the data collected is the fact that the completion of meaning-focused tasks through computer-mediated communication does foster negotiated interactions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Closed meaning-focused tasks foster more negotiation of meaning and negative feedback than open tasks in CMC — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Videoconferencing was conducive to more negotiation of meaning than text chat and discussion board, measured as proportion of negotiated turns — related
  • Discussion board interactions generated no negative feedback at all, suggesting synchrony is required to foster corrective feedback — related, from the same study: discussion boards produced negotiation but no negative feedback
  • Content-related problems trigger most negotiation routines, and syntactic triggers are absent from text chat where turns are short — related
  • Non-native speaker dyads provided with subtitled videos before tasks produce a significantly higher percentage of negotiated turns in online TBLL than dyads without videos — related
  • Unprompted self-correction dominates negative feedback in NNS-NNS CMC interaction, interpreted as a face-saving preference — related
