# Dual enrollment classes taken on college campuses increase students' likelihood of attending college and earning a bachelor's degree

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who participated in dual enrollment classes on college campuses were 12 percent more likely to go to college than similar non-participants. [→ Cecilia Speroni 2011](#cecilia-speroni-2011)
`q3 i?` Students who participated in dual enrollment classes on college campuses were 7 percent more likely to earn a bachelor's degree than similar non-participants. [→ Cecilia Speroni 2011](#cecilia-speroni-2011)

## Evidence

### Cecilia Speroni 2011

Cecilia Speroni. (2011). Determinants of Students' Success: The Role of Advanced Placement and Dual Enrollment Programs. National Center for Postsecondary Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/determinants-of-students-success-the-role-of-advanced-placement-and-dual-enrollment-programs

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

A statewide observational study tracking all of Florida's 2000-2001 and 2001-2002 high school seniors, comparing dual enrollment participants on college campuses with "similar students who did not participate." It reports participants were "12 percent more likely to go to college and 7 percent more likely to earn a bachelor's degree."

> "This study tracked all of Florida's 2000–2001 and 2001–2002 high school seniors and found that students who participated in dual enrollment classes on college campuses were 12 percent more likely to go to college and 7 percent more likely to earn a bachelor's degree than similar students who did not participate."
