# Error frequency distributions in physical examination skills differ significantly by student gender, with females showing a lower average error frequency that is not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The distribution of PE error frequencies differed significantly between male and female students (p<0.001), though the average error frequency per student (females 0.875 vs males 1.375) did not differ significantly (p=0.167). [→ Li Y 2014](#li-y-2014)

## Evidence

### Li Y 2014

Li Y, Li N, He Q, He S, Bae RS, Lv Z, Lv Y, Shi B. (2014). Performance of Physical Examination Skills in Medical Students during Diagnostic Medicine Course in a University Hospital of Northwest China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109294

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Assessment of 72 fourth-year medical students (32 male, 40 female) after a 17-week diagnostic medicine course, scored by two teachers using a checklist. The error frequency distribution differed by gender (p<0.001); average errors per student were "females (0.875) was less than that in males (1.375)" but not significant (p=0.167).

> "There was a statistically significant difference between the distribution and the percentage in error frequency when separated by gender (p<0.001, Table 1)."
