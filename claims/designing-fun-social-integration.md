# Designing fun explains additional variance in peer interactions and popularity beyond core self-evaluations and PsyCap, whereas designing competition does not

## Evidence

### Costantini 2025

Costantini, A., Bakker, A. B., & Scharp, Y. S. (2025). Playful Study Design: A Novel Approach to Enhancing Student Well-Being and Academic Performance. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10022-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regressions on the full Study 1 sample (N = 753) entered CSE and PsyCap first and PSD dimensions second, with peer interactions and popularity as outcomes. "Designing fun explained additional variance (over and above CSE and PsyCap)" in all three social integration outcomes.

> "designing fun explained additional variance (over and above CSE and PsyCap) in formal and informal peer interactions and popularity, supporting Hypothesis 4a."

### Costantini 2025 (2)

Costantini, A., Bakker, A. B., & Scharp, Y. S. (2025). Playful Study Design: A Novel Approach to Enhancing Student Well-Being and Academic Performance. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10022-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same hierarchical regression analyses, the competition dimension failed to add incremental prediction of the social integration outcomes; the authors state "Hypothesis 4b is rejected" and interpret designing fun as more closely tied to social dynamics.

> "Hypothesis 4b is rejected as designing competition did not meaningfully contribute to social integration outcomes beyond personal resources and designing fun."
