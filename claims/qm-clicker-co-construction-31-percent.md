# Students in one uncontrolled pre-test-to-post-test study: groups in which no member answered the ICT correctly all answered the GCT correctly 31% of the time

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When all group members answered an ICT incorrectly, they all answered the corresponding GCT correctly 31% of the time; when one member was correct individually, all were correct on the GCT 77% of the time; when all were correct individually, all were correct on the GCT 98% of the time. [→ Ryan Sayer 2016](#ryan-sayer-2016)

## Evidence

### Ryan Sayer 2016

Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). The impact of peer interaction on the responses to clicker questions in an upper-level quantum mechanics course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of GCT performance as a function of group members' ICT performance across the 14 comparison questions (Table II). The article reports "co-constructed" knowledge in 31% of cases when all members were initially incorrect, 77% when one member was correct, and 98% when all were correct. No effect size is printed.

> "Row 1 (with data) in Table II shows that when all group members answered an ICT incorr ectly they all answered the  corresponding GCT correctly  (i.e., they “co-constructed” knowledge) 31% of the time."
