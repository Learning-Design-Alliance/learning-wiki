# Pre-session interest in puzzle games predicts completion of puzzle 17, while the gender difference in completion is not statistically significant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants rating their interest in puzzle games 4 or 5 pre-session completed puzzle 17 at significantly higher rates than those rating 1-3 (p=0.019). [→ Nita 2021](#nita-2021)
`q2 i?` The difference in puzzle 17 completion between female and male participants was not statistically significant (p=0.077), though the sample was small. [→ Nita 2021 (2)](#nita-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Nita 2021

Nita, L., Chancellor, N., Mazzoli Smith, L., Cramman, H. & Dost, G. (2021). Inclusive learning for quantum computing: supporting the aims of quantum literacy using the puzzle game Quantum Odyssey. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Questionnaire and log analysis of the 42-participant pilot found that of those completing puzzle 17, "more than three times as many indicated an interest of 4 or 5", a significant difference at p=0.019.

> "A statistically significant difference (p=0.019) was found in completion rates for puzzle 17 between participants that selected 4 or 5 in response to the pre-session questionnaire relating to their “interest in puzzle games” and those that selected 1-3."

### Nita 2021 (2)

Nita, L., Chancellor, N., Mazzoli Smith, L., Cramman, H. & Dost, G. (2021). Inclusive learning for quantum computing: supporting the aims of quantum literacy using the puzzle game Quantum Odyssey. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.07077)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A secondary significance test on puzzle 17 completion by gender (n=8 of 12 female completers vs n=16 of 28 male) found no statistically significant difference; the article explicitly flags the small sample size.

> "A significance of p=0.077 was calculated, which indicates that the difference between genders was not statistically significant (although it should be noted that the sample size was small)."
