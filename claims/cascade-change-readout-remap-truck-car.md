## Evidence

### Wittmann 2006

Wittmann, M. C. (2006). Using resource graphs to represent conceptual change. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article describes the Elby and Hammer truck–car activity as a readout-cascade: after reading the same two resources as connected to changes in velocity, students "can quickly and easily connect to ideas such as acceleration and Newton’s Second Law," producing a cascade of connected changes.

> "After reading out of the situation that “actuating agency” and “compensation” can be connected to changes in velocity, students can quickly and easily connect to ideas such as acceleration and Newton’s Second Law, F = ma and the Ohm’s p-prim (in the context of F = ma).9"

### Wittmann 2006 (2)

Wittmann, M. C. (2006). Using resource graphs to represent conceptual change. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Authors' own classroom observations during small-group facilitation: "students go through a series of connected changes during moments of facilitation," which the article reads as building or restructuring a resource graph in connected fashion.

> "We have often heard students go through a series of connected changes during moments of facilitation in small group learning environments."
