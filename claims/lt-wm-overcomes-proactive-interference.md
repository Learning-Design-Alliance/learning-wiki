# Proactive interference in LT-WM is overcome by temporal distinctiveness and by integrated memory encodings, as when a trained subject recalled most of an earlier digit list despite maximal interference

## Evidence

### Ericsson 1993

K. Anders Ericsson and Walter Kintsch. (1993). Long-Term Working Memory. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Long-Term+Working+Memory

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Chase and Ericsson (1982), a direct maximal-interference test in which a second same-length list was presented and recalled before the first was recalled: DD "recalled the second list virtually perfectly" and recalled most of the first, accuracy ranging from virtually perfect to around 70%. The review attributes this resistance to elaborated semantic structure encodings.

> "Under those conditions DD recalled the second list virtually perfectly, but was also able to recall most of the first list correctly. Recall accuracy for the first list ranged from virtually perfect to around 70%"

### Ericsson 1993 (2)

K. Anders Ericsson and Walter Kintsch. (1993). Long-Term Working Memory. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Long-Term+Working+Memory

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports, citing Walter Schneider and Detweiler (1987), that in the Brown-Peterson paradigm "long intertrial intervals of around 2 m" between trials essentially eliminate interference, supporting temporal distinctiveness as a mechanism keeping the most recent encoding retrievable in LT-WM.

> "long intertrial intervals of around 2 m between successive Brown-Peterson trials essentially eliminate interference and restore recall to the level of the first trial of the session"
