Evidence¶

Stebner 2022¶

Stebner, F., Schuster, C., Weber, X.-L., Greiff, S., Leutner, D., & Wirth, J. (2022). Transfer of metacognitive skills in self-regulated learning: effects on strategy application and content knowledge acquisition. Metacognition and Learning, 17, 715-744. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09322-x

q1 · i? · causal · r2

This is the article's theoretical argument in the introduction for expecting the effect of hybrid metacognitive skill training on content knowledge acquisition to be stronger for near than far transfer. It is a reasoned expectation, not a tested result in the visible text.

"We assume that metacognitive skills increase the quality of cognitive strategy application in far transfer tasks in the same way as in near transfer tasks. However, since metacognitive skill application is more cognitively demanding in far transfer tasks it leaves less free working memory capacity for content knowledge acquisition."

Related Claims¶

  • Whether metacognition is domain-general or domain-specific remains a major unresolved issue with implications for instruction and transfer — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Before metacognitive skill training, fifth graders possessed none of the metacognitive skills later trained, so metacognitive skills are task-specific at first and become domain-general only through transfer — related
  • Hybrid training fosters far transfer of metacognitive skills to a new cognitive strategy, though one hybrid condition did not beat control — related
  • Hybrid training improves fifth-graders' application of metacognitive skills in near-transfer tasks compared to non-hybrid and control training — related
  • Cognitive strategy application in a far-transfer task after hybrid training depends on the specific strategy: text highlighting transferred, conducting experiments did not versus non-hybrid training — related
  • Training in metacognitive regulation combined with a cognitive learning strategy transfers to better metacognitive regulation of mental effort (far transfer), with small effect sizes — related
  • Cognitive learning strategy training yields preliminary evidence of far metacognitive transfer to effort regulation (Wirth et al., 2025) — related
