# Self-scoring improves primary school students' regulation judgment accuracy in mathematics

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students made more accurate regulation judgments after self-scoring than before, on both multiplication and division tasks, though these still deviated substantially from expert judgments. [→ Oudman 2024](#oudman-2024)

## Evidence

### Oudman 2024

Oudman, S., van de Pol, J., Janssen, E., & van Gog, T. (2024). Primary school students' awareness of their monitoring and regulation judgment accuracy. Learning and Instruction, 92, 101907. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101907

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive statistics (Table 1) show absolute regulation accuracy improved from 0.61 (SD = 0.70) to 0.44 (SD = 0.62) for multiplication and from 0.43 (SD = 0.65) to 0.36 (SD = 0.60) for division (values closer to zero indicate more accurate judgments). Statistical analyses are reported in the companion study Oudman et al. (2022) based on the same dataset.

> "The students made more accurate regulation judgments after self-scoring than before, on both tasks (for statistical analyses, see Oudman et al., 2022)."
