# Students in the AI-integrated database course showed large, statistically significant pre/post gains on seven identical database assessment questions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pre/post assessment scores improved from a mean of 4.25 to 6.83 out of 7, a highly significant increase (t(12)≈5.10, p < .001) with a mean score increase of 2.58 points. [→ Hosseini 2026](#hosseini-2026)

## Evidence

### Hosseini 2026

Hosseini, H. (2026). The Pedagogy of AI Mistakes: Fostering Higher-Order Thinking. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05472

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post assessment in an asynchronous undergraduate database design course (n = 13), with seven identical multiple-choice questions administered at course start and embedded in the midterm/final. Mean rose from 4.25 (SD = 1.57) to 6.83 (SD ≈ 0.39); "A paired t-test confirms this improvement is highly significant (t(12)≈5.10, p < .001)". No effect size is printed in this test statistic.

> "A paired t-test confirms this improvement is highly significant (t(12)≈5.10, p < .001), with a mean score increase of 2.58 points."

### Hosseini 2026 (2)

Hosseini, H. (2026). The Pedagogy of AI Mistakes: Fostering Higher-Order Thinking. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05472

`q2 · i3` · `design · r2`

Effect-size analysis of the same pre/post gains in the 13-student course, following a Shapiro–Wilk normality check (W = 0.95, p = 0.64). The authors report "a large effect size (d= 1.49)" and interpret the improvement as practically meaningful.

> "We then examined the magnitude of learning gains using Cohen’sd, which yielded a large effect size (d= 1.49)."
