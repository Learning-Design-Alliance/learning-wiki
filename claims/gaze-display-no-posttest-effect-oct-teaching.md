# Live dynamic gaze displays did not improve students' post-test performance in one-to-one OCT teaching sessions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students taught with a live gaze display did not score higher on the post-test than students in the control condition; Bayesian evidence favored no effect. [→ Korteland 2024](#korteland-2024)

## Evidence

### Korteland 2024

Korteland, R.-J., Kok, E., Hulshof, C., & van Gog, T. (2024). Teaching through their eyes: effects on optometry teachers’ adaptivity and students’ learning when teachers see students’ gaze. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 29(5), 1735–1748. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10325-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-subjects experiment with 49 optometry students randomly allocated to gaze-display (n = 25) or control (n = 24) conditions; Bayesian t-test with pre-test as covariate found "evidence against an effect of condition on the post-test score (BF10 = 0.335)". Post-test means were 5.28 (gaze display) vs 5.58 (control) out of 10.

> "We found evidence against an effect of condition on the post-test score (BF10 = 0.335) with the pre-test score as a covariate: The Bayes Factor shows that it is three times more likely that the gaze displays had no effect on the posttest score than that gaze displays had an effect on the posttest score."
