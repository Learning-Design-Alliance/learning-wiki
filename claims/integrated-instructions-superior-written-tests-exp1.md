# In an industrial training setting, integrated instructions produced superior written test performance to conventional split-source instructions over a 3-month period when material required mental integration to be understood

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First-year apprentices given integrated installation-testing notes scored significantly higher on written tests than apprentices given conventional split-source notes, with the advantage persisting across immediate and 12-week follow-up tests. [→ Chandler 1991](#chandler-1991)

## Evidence

### Chandler 1991

Chandler, P. & Sweller, J. (1991). Cognitive Load Theory and the Format of Instruction. Cognition and Instruction, 8(4). http://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/128

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 2 (Groups) x 2 (Test Periods) ANOVA with repeated measures on 28 first-year trade apprentices in a Sydney company's training center compared conventional split-source and integrated installation-testing notes. The article reports "a significant main effect for groups in the predicted direction" favoring modified (integrated) instructions; the interaction with test period was not significant, indicating the advantage held over both test periods.

> "Results showed a significant main effect for groups in the predicted direction, F(1, 26) == 8.60, MSe == 553.14, suggesting an overall superiority of modified instructions."
