Evidence¶

Boroshok 2022¶

Boroshok, A. L., Park, A. T., Fotiadis, P., Velasquez, G. H., Tooley, U. A., Simon, K. R., Forde, J. C. P., Delgado Reyes, L. M., Tisdall, M. D., Bassett, D. S., Cooper, E. A. & Mackey, A. P. (2022). Individual differences in frontoparietal plasticity in humans. npj Science of Learning 7:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00130-1

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Behavioral analyses of the 46-adult sample relating baseline performance to training gains. The article reports β = −0.284, p = 0.009 for baseline accuracy predicting accuracy gains and β = −0.389, p < 0.001 for baseline response time predicting response-time gains.

"Individuals with lower baseline accuracy improved more on accuracy following training ( β = −0.284, 95% CI [ −0.493, −0.076], p = 0.009). Individuals with slower baseline response times showed swifter response times following training ( β = −0.389, 95% CI [ −0.563, −0.215], p < 0.001)."

Boroshok 2022 (2)¶

Boroshok, A. L., Park, A. T., Fotiadis, P., Velasquez, G. H., Tooley, U. A., Simon, K. R., Forde, J. C. P., Delgado Reyes, L. M., Tisdall, M. D., Bassett, D. S., Cooper, E. A. & Mackey, A. P. (2022). Individual differences in frontoparietal plasticity in humans. npj Science of Learning 7:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00130-1

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Behavioral analysis of the 46-adult sample testing the association between the two learning measures. Accuracy gains and response-time gains were not significantly associated (β = 0.050, p = 0.291); equivalence was not tested. Learning gains were also not significantly associated with age or sex.

"Gains in accuracy were not associated with improvements in response times ( β = 0.050, 95% CI [ −0.045, 0.145], p = 0.291)."
