# A microworld-based intervention produced a modestly greater gain in computational thinking than comparison teaching, with a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50) that was not statistically significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Learners following the microworld-based teaching scheme improved their computational thinking post-test scores by 5.64% more than the comparison group. [→ Jenkins 2015](#jenkins-2015)
`q2 i2` The computational thinking gain difference had a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50) with p = 0.202, not reaching statistical significance. [→ Jenkins 2015](#jenkins-2015)

## Evidence

### Jenkins 2015

Craig Jenkins. (2015). Poem Generator: A comparative quantitative evaluation of a microworlds-based learning approach for teaching English. International Journal of Education and Development using Information and Communication Technology, 11(2), 153-167. http://ijedict.dec.uwi.edu/viewarticle.php?id=1972

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental nonequivalent comparison group study with 27 Year 8 learners in two low-ability English sets; independent t-tests on gain scores. The CT gain difference was t-tested at p = 0.202 with Cohen's d = 0.50, described as "a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50)".
