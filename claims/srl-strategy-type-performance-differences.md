# Metacognitive and composite SRL strategy measures show the strongest relationships with learning performance in continuing education

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Metacognitive strategies (r = .44) and composite SRL scores (r = .48) show moderate to strong and strong relationships with learning performance, exceeding cognitive strategies (r = .16). [→ Yvonne M. Hemmler 2024](#yvonne-m-hemmler-2024)

## Evidence

### Yvonne M. Hemmler 2024

Yvonne M. Hemmler, Dirk Ifenthaler. (2024). Self-regulated learning strategies in continuing education: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100629

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Additional meta-analyses by SRL strategy type for factors with correlations available from at least three studies. The review reports strategy-type differences for learning performance, with "a strong relationship with composite scores of SRL strategies (r= .48)" and weaker coefficients for cognitive strategies.

> "Average correlation coefficients for learning performance showed a weak to moderate relationship with cognitive strategies (r= .16), a moderate relationship with resource management strategies (r= .29), a moderate to strong relationship with metacognitive strategies (r= .44), and a strong relationship with composite scores of SRL strategies (r= .48)."
