Evidence¶

Tessa H. S. Eysink 2020¶

Tessa H. S. Eysink, Alieke M. van Dijk, Ton de Jong. (2020). BE COOL! a digital learning environment to challenge and socially include gifted learners. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09754-9

q2 · i? · design · r3

Exploratory study with 478 fifth and sixth graders of three ability levels comparing groups with prompts available to those without; prompt use and inquiry-process quality came from logfiles and domain knowledge from pre-, post-, and retention tests. The article reports that "children who had support available displayed a more active and effective inquiry process than those who did not."

"Results showed that children who had support available displayed a more active and effective inquiry process than those who did not. Moreover, gifted children used the support frequently, whereas average- and low-ability children hardly used it. However, if they did, their learning process improved."

Related Claims¶

  • A usability study with 37 fourth to sixth graders showed gifted children needed less support and were satisfied with more abstract prompts, while lower-ability children preferred and needed more concrete instructions — related
  • The effectiveness of open-ended prompts varies by grade level and collaboration, with individual written prompts not supporting fifth and sixth grade comprehension but peer discussion and older students' use showing positive outcomes — related
  • In a pilot with 155 graders 4–6, children were overwhelmed by a large non-structured set of inquiry assignments and lacked planning skills, leading to a reduced, predefined-schedule assignment set — related
