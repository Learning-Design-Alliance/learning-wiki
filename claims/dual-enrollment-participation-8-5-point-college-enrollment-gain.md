# Dual enrollment participation is associated with an approximately 8.5 percentage point increase in college enrollment probability, with larger gains for students from low-income and first-generation backgrounds

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dual enrollment participation is associated with an approximately 8.5 percentage point increase in the probability of enrolling in college compared to non-participants, an observational association rather than a causal estimate. [→ Terrell 2026](#terrell-2026)
`q2 i?` The estimated participation gain is larger for students from low-income and first-generation backgrounds (approximately 9.1 percentage points) than for their peers (7.4 percentage points). [→ Terrell 2026](#terrell-2026)

## Evidence

### Terrell 2026

Terrell, J. H., Mathias, J., Blanchard, A., & Roldan-Rueda, D. (2026). The arc of dual enrollment: Access, struggle, and what makes success possible. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681344

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Analysis of California Department of Education CALPADS administrative data covering approximately 1.74 million students in cohorts 2015-16 through 2018-19. The authors report the association descriptively, noting gains of "approximately 9.1 percentage points, compared to 7.4 percentage points for their peers"; no standardised effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "We find that dual enrollment participation is associated with an approximately 8.5 percent- age point increase in the probability of enrolling in college compared to students who do not participate in dual enrollment. For students from low-income and first-generation backgrounds, the estimated gain is approximately 9.1 percentage points, compared to 7.4 percentage points for their peers."
