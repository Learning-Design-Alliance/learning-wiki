# Weekly spaced recall quizzes on fundamental concepts significantly improve 6-month retention of those concepts in a precalculus course

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who took weekly spaced recall quizzes on fundamental concepts scored significantly higher on a retention index of 10 fundamental concepts 6 months later than students quizzed on current material (81.6% vs 77.3%). [→ Lindquist 2024](#lindquist-2024)

## Evidence

### Lindquist 2024

Lindquist, D. S., Sparrow, B. E., & Lindquist, J. M. (2024). Spaced recall reduces forgetting of fundamental mathematical concepts in a post high school precalculus course. Instructional Science, 52, 859-878. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09680-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized classroom experiment in 17 sections of a precalculus course at USMAPS with N=128 students (60 control, 68 treatment). Treatment took weekly quizzes on 12 fundamental concepts cycling every 28 days; control was quizzed on current material. The article reports "The p-value of 0.012" for the retention index difference; no effect size is printed.

> "the mean performance (retention index) of the treatment group (81.6%) is higher than that of the control group (77.3%) . The p-value of 0.012 provides statistically significant evidence that the control and treatment group did not perform the same on those 24 specific problems."
