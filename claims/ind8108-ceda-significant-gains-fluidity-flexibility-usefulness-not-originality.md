# A 13-week creativity course produced significant pre-post CEDA gains in fluidity, flexibility and usefulness but not originality among engineering students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` T-test analysis of pre-post CEDA scores indicated a significant increase for fluidity, flexibility and usefulness, while originality did not show an important increase. [→ Sophie Morin 2016](#sophie-morin-2016)

## Evidence

### Sophie Morin 2016

Sophie Morin, Jean-Marc Robert, Liane Gabora. (2016). Creativity Training for Future Engineers: Preliminary Results from an Educative Experience. Proceedings of the 7th International Conference on Education and New Learning Technologies. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.02643](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.02643)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-post experiment using the CEDA test administered on the 2nd and 14th class, 13 weeks apart, to 31 graduate and undergraduate students across two semesters (9 in Fall 2014, 22 in Winter 2015); t-test analysis of the differences. The article reports "a significant increase for fluidity, flexibility and usefulness" with no effect size printed.

> "Although our results did not show an important increase in creativity (especially for originality) in the students who attended the course, the t-test produced still indicates a significant increase for fluidity, flexibility and usefulness."
