## Evidence

### Carver-Thomas 2024

Carver-Thomas, D., Leung-Gagné, M., & Jeannite, D. (2024). Tackling teacher shortages: What we know about California's teacher workforce investments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/137.196

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of California Department of Education Teaching Assignment Monitoring Outcomes data for 2022–23 comparing priority and non-priority schools. The report finds "83% of teacher full-time equivalents (fTEs) in priority schools were fully credentialed" versus 87% in non-priority schools.

> "In 2022–23, the most recent year with available data, 83% of teacher full-time equivalents (fTEs) in priority schools were fully credentialed with clear or preliminary credentials for their teaching positions (see figure 3). In comparison, 87% of teacher fTEs were fully credentialed in non-priority schools."

### Carver-Thomas 2024 (2)

Carver-Thomas, D., Leung-Gagné, M., & Jeannite, D. (2024). Tackling teacher shortages: What we know about California's teacher workforce investments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/137.196

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same 2022–23 assignment-monitoring analysis comparing highest-need (top 10% unduplicated pupil count) and lowest-need schools. Highest-need schools had "just 84% fully credentialed teacher fTEs compared to 90% in the lowest-need schools."

> "The state's highest-need schools had just 84% fully credentialed teacher fTEs compared to 90% in the lowest-need schools (see figure 4). In the highest-need schools, 11% of teacher fTEs were interns or ineffective compared to just 4% in the lowest-need schools, nearly three times the proportion."

### Carver-Thomas 2024 (3)

Carver-Thomas, D., Leung-Gagné, M., & Jeannite, D. (2024). Tackling teacher shortages: What we know about California's teacher workforce investments. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/137.196

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of credential-type distribution among inexperienced (2 or fewer years) math teacher FTEs in 2022–23, shown in Figure 6. Disparities were "especially pronounced among inexperienced math teachers," with similar but less pronounced patterns in science.

> "fewer than half (49%) of inexperienced math teacher fTEs were fully credentialed in the highest-need schools compared to 70% in the lowest-need schools (see figure 6). A full 42% of inexperienced math teachers in the highest-need schools were identified as interns or ineffective compared to 19% of inexperienced teachers in the lowest-need schools."
