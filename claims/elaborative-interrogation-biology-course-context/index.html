Evidence¶

Dunlosky 2013¶

Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Classroom-embedded experiment by B. L. Smith et al. (2010) described in the monograph's educational-contexts section: 21 prompts (roughly one per 150 words) were interspersed in a course text, followed by 105 true/false questions. The prompted group scored 76% versus 69%; no effect size is printed.

"Performance was better for the elaborative-interrogation group than for the control group (76% versus 69%), even after controlling for prior knowledge and verbal ability."
