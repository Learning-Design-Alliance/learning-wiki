# Interaction type did not significantly moderate the relationship between perception of workload and measured performance

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` The relationship between students' perception of workload and measured performance remained the same across the two interaction groups, and perceived workload alone did not predict performance. [→ Hauert 2026](#hauert-2026)

## Evidence

### Hauert 2026

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Stepwise multiple regression in the randomized trial (n = 66) with performance as dependent variable; the Step 3 interaction term yielded "F (1, 62) = 0.46, p = .500, ΔR2 = .01". Step 1 also showed no predictive relationship between perception of workload and performance, F(1, 64) = 1.08, p = .303, ΔR2 = .02.

> "The results did not suggest a moderator effect of interaction type on the relationship between students' perception of workload and their measured performance, F (1, 62) = 0.46, p = .500, ΔR2 = .01."
