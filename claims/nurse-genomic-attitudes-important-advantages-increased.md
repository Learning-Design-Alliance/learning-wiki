## Evidence

### Calzone 2026

Calzone, K., Stokes, L., Peterson, C., Yee, L. M., Liewehr, D., and Badzek, L. (2026). Evaluation of the Integration of Genetics and Genomics Into Nursing Practice. Journal of Nursing Scholarship 58, no. 1: e70056. https://doi.org/10.1111/jnu.70056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Attitudes/receptivity section of the 2023 GGNPS descriptive survey of US registered nurses. The article reports "it was important (N = 705, 66%) or very important (N = 300, 28%)" and notes the data are largely unchanged from 2010 (68% and 27%).

> "Most respondents indicated that it was important (N = 705, 66%) or very important (N = 300, 28%) for nurses to become more educated about the genetics of common diseases. These data are largely unchanged from 2010"

### Calzone 2026 (2)

Calzone, K., Stokes, L., Peterson, C., Yee, L. M., Liewehr, D., and Badzek, L. (2026). Evaluation of the Integration of Genetics and Genomics Into Nursing Practice. Journal of Nursing Scholarship 58, no. 1: e70056. https://doi.org/10.1111/jnu.70056

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of advantage endorsements between the 2010 and 2023 GGNPS surveys; 2023 rates were 96%, 94%, and 92% for the top three advantages versus 85%, 79%, and 69% in 2010, which the article describes as slightly lower opinions in 2010.

> "The data from 2010 showed slightly lower opinions on the advantages: better decisions about recommendations for preventative services (N = 525, 85%); better treatment decisions (N = 490, 79%); and improved services to patients (N = 427, 69%)"
