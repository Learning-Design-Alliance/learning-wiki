## Evidence

### Wikaire Erena 2016

Wikaire Erena, Curtis Elana, Cormack Donna, Jiang Yannan, McMillan Louise, Loto Rob, Reid Papaarangi. (2016). Patterns of privilege: A total cohort analysis of admission and academic outcomes for Māori, Pacific and non-Māori non-Pacific health professional students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0782-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of early academic outcomes (first-year course passes) in the 2686-student cohort, tested by chi-square with nMnP as reference.

> "Māori or Pacific students were less likely to have passed all courses in their first year of bachelor study (p < 0.0001, 61 % for Māori, p < 0.0001, 41 % for Pacific) when compared with nMnP students (78 %)"

### Wikaire Erena 2016 (2)

Wikaire Erena, Curtis Elana, Cormack Donna, Jiang Yannan, McMillan Louise, Loto Rob, Reid Papaarangi. (2016). Patterns of privilege: A total cohort analysis of admission and academic outcomes for Māori, Pacific and non-Māori non-Pacific health professional students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0782-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same observational analysis of first-year bachelor GPA (0–9 scale) across eight courses, tested by ANOVA with pairwise comparisons against the nMnP reference group.

> "had an average first year bachelor GPA that was significantly lower (mean GPA = 3.63, SD = 1.71, p < 0.0001 for Māori, mean GPA = 2.83, SD = 1.64, p < 0.0001 for Pacific) when compared to nMnP students (mean GPA = 4.69, SD = 1.94)"
