## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Plotting specialist teaching rate against estimated specialist availability separates subjects into a problematic low-availability cluster (including physics) and a high-availability cluster; the authors interpret physics as needing more teachers with physics content knowledge rather than redeployment. ## Evidence

### Robert Fischer 2013

Robert Fischer, Elton Fireman, José Renan Gomes. (2013). Who teaches science in Alagoas? A quantitative analysis of non-specialist teaching at primary and secondary schools in Brazil. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1304.8055](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1304.8055)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Numerical analysis plotting specialist teaching rate against specialist availability (specialist registrations divided by total subject registrations, Figure 1) shows two clusters separated by a gap; the authors conclude "physicists are already focused on teaching “their” subject".

> "However, the graph makes clear that physicists are already focused on teaching “their” subject, so that an increase in the total number of teachers with a suitable physics content knowledge is needed to improve the subject specialist rate."
