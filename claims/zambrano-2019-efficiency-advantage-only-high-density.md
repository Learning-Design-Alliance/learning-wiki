## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the short-term retention test, experienced groups were more efficient than inexperienced groups for high information density tasks (η²p = .03) but not for low information density tasks. [→ Jimmy Zambrano 2019](#jimmy-zambrano-2019)
`q3 i2` The same interaction pattern held in the delayed retention test for instructional efficiency, with a small-to-medium effect at high information density (η²p = .04). [→ Jimmy Zambrano 2019 (2)](#jimmy-zambrano-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Jimmy Zambrano 2019

Jimmy Zambrano, Femke Kirschner, John Sweller, Paul A. Kirschner. (2019). Effects of group experience and information distribution on collaborative learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09495-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA interaction in the short-term retention phase. The interaction showed an efficiency advantage for experienced groups only where task information density was high (η²p = .03); at low density the groups "are not significantly different", so the advantage is conditional rather than general.

> "Regarding instructional efficiency, the significant interaction between main effects indicated that for the task with high information density, experienced groups are more efficient than inexperienced groups, (p = .02, /u1D7022 p = .03). However, for the task with low information density, experienced groups and inexperienced groups are not significantly different (p = .37, /u1D7022 p = .01)."

### Jimmy Zambrano 2019 (2)

Jimmy Zambrano, Femke Kirschner, John Sweller, Paul A. Kirschner. (2019). Effects of group experience and information distribution on collaborative learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-019-09495-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Delayed retention phase efficiency interaction: experienced groups were more efficient only for high information density tasks (η²p = .04, a small-to-medium effect); at low information density the difference was not significant (η²p = .00).

> "The significant interaction between main effects indicated that for the task with high information density, experienced groups are more efficient than inexperienced groups, (p = .01, /u1D7022 p = .04). However, for low information density, experienced and inexperienced groups are not significantly different (p = .90, /u1D7022 p = .00)."
