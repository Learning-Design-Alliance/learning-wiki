## Evidence

### Martin-Storey 2014

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Structural equation modeling of SECCYD data (n = 924) predicting age-15 school attachment, controlling for prior school functioning, demographics, school factors, and three external neighborhood measures. The full model showed the printed association: "34.8% of a standard deviation increase in school attachment". No standardised effect size (d, r, eta-squared) was printed.

> "perceived neighborhood safety was associated with higher levels of school attachment, with each standard deviation increase in perceived neighborhood safety being associated with 34.8% of a standard deviation increase in school attachment"
