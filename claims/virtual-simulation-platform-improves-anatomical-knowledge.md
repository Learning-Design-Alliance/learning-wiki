## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Third-year medical undergraduates who used the virtual simulation platform scored significantly higher on a written gastrointestinal anatomy test than students taught traditionally. [→ Liu Jie 2025](#liu-jie-2025)

## Evidence

### Liu Jie 2025

Liu Jie, Hu Tingting, Xu Ke, Cheng Zihui, Bu Wenjie, Lin Aiqin. (2025). Does a virtual simulation teaching platform improve surgical training for medical undergraduate students? BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-025-03029-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-group comparison of 128 third-year medical undergraduates (66 experimental, 62 control) with comparable baseline characteristics. The experimental group, which received additional virtual simulation platform practice, scored "79.44 ± 7.43 points" versus 73.95 ± 6.25 for controls (P < 0.001; t = 4.508). No effect size printed.

> "Students in the experimental group obtained significantly higher scores on the test of anatomical knowledge of gastrointestinal diseases (79.44 ± 7.43 points) than did those in the control group (73.95 ± 6.25 points) (P < 0.001)."
