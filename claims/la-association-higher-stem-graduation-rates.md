# Students who had a Learning Assistant in at least one of the ten courses had higher STEM graduation odds, with 5-year and 6-year graduation differences statistically significant (6-year: 61%±5% without LA vs 70%±5% with LA)

`q2 i1` Freshman-admitted STEM students who saw an LA somewhere had odds ratios greater than 1 for 4-, 5-, 6-, and 7-year graduation, with 5-year (OR = 1.40) and 6-year (OR = 1.47) differences statistically significant at a level higher than 99.9%. ## Evidence

### Cassandra A. Paul and David J. Webb 2026

Cassandra A. Paul and David J. Webb. (2026). Grades, equity and graduation rates: a quantitative analysis of the association of learning assistants with improved student outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.27006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.27006)

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Three-level multilevel logistic modeling of four standard graduation rates (Table I reports odds ratios 1.27, 1.40, 1.47, and 1.41 for 4-, 5-, 6-, and 7-year graduation), comparing students who had seen an LA with students who had not; the 6-year odds ratio is 1.47.

> "It seems like having an LA in a student's course is correlated with higher graduation rates (odds ratio > 1) for all of these graduation definitions with 5-year and 6-year graduation rate differences being statistically significant at a level higher than 99.9%."

### Cassandra A. Paul and David J. Webb 2026 (2)

Cassandra A. Paul and David J. Webb. (2026). Grades, equity and graduation rates: a quantitative analysis of the association of learning assistants with improved student outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.27006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.27006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Using the fitted Eq. 2 coefficients, the article computes the 6-year STEM graduation probability as "61%±5%" without an LA versus "70%±5%" with an LA; these are model-derived probabilities, not a standardized effect size.

> "the no-LA 6-year STEM graduation probability works out to be 61%±5% and the rate when a student has seen an LA is 70%±5%."
