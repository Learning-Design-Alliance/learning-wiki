Evidence¶

Shi 2025¶

Shi, H., Bi, R., Lin, X., & Dai, Y. (2025). Decreasing Digital Distraction in College Students: Associated Online Learning Strategies Identified by Unsupervised Data Mining Approaches. https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/P85S3

q2 · i? · associational · r1

ARM on the 530-student survey: demographics did not appear in significant rules under the stated thresholds, so "demographics did not play a role in the behavioral profiles that characterize lower distraction". The same held in the two-antecedent step.

"We found no significant rules that included student demographic characteristics (i.e., gender, age, race) and majors, which filtered out demographics as meaningful predictors because they did not co-occur with lower distraction patterns under our significant rule extraction thresholds (support > .20 and confidence > .50)."

Shi 2025 (2)¶

Shi, H., Bi, R., Lin, X., & Dai, Y. (2025). Decreasing Digital Distraction in College Students: Associated Online Learning Strategies Identified by Unsupervised Data Mining Approaches. https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/P85S3

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Independent samples t-test comparing digital distraction between male and female participants in the full sample of 530; the test was not significant (t = 0.86, p = .39). Equivalence was not tested; the result addresses possible gender-imbalance bias.

"an independent samples t-test indicated that no significant difference in digital distraction between male and female students (t = 0.86, p = .39), suggesting that gender imbalance in the sample is unlikely to bias the findings"
