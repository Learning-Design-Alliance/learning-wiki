## Evidence

### Alexis Olsho 2022

Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2022). A framework for characterizing covariational reasoning in physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2022.pr.olsho](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2022.pr.olsho)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article re-analyzes Sherin's interview study of advanced-introductory physics students determining how a dropped object's terminal velocity depended on its mass, through the CoRP lens. All students recognized the air-resistance–velocity dependence (PMA 1) and its increasing trend (PMA 2); the article notes students often needed interviewer prompting.

> "In working on this task, all of the students recognized that the force of air resistance is dependent on the velocity of the ob- ject (PMA 1), and that the air resistance increases as the ve- locity of the object increases (PMA 2)."

### Alexis Olsho 2022 (2)

Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2022). A framework for characterizing covariational reasoning in physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2022.pr.olsho](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2022.pr.olsho)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article describes student Jack, who listed candidate proportionalities (v, v squared, v cubed) for air resistance but, per the article, "is not able to choose one as being the most appropriate for the situation" — an incomplete version of function choosing (EB IIB).

> "While Jack is able to generate a number of possibilities, he is not able to choose one as being the most appropriate for the situation"
