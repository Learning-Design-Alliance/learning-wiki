## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Trainees randomized to a virtual patient case on upper gastrointestinal bleed scored similarly on a 10-item multiple-choice post-test to trainees randomized to a PowerPoint presentation, for both medical expert and non-medical expert CanMEDS topics. [→ Samira J 2018](#samira-j-2018)

## Evidence

### Samira J 2018

Samira J, Wang JY, Richardson L. (2018). Evaluation of virtual patient cases for teaching diagnostic and management skills in internal medicine: a mixed methods study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-018-3463-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of a VP case (n = 23) versus PowerPoint (n = 29) among 52 University of Toronto trainees, with a 10-item multiple-choice post-test analyzed by Mann–Whitney U test; the article reports "did not significantly affect knowledge acquisition" on all question topics (Table 2).

> "Our results indicated that VP cases did not significantly affect knowledge acquisition, for both medical expert and non-medical expert CanMEDS topics (Table 2)."
