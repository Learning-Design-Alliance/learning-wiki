# Prior mathematical knowledge correlates very strongly with later mathematics achievement (r = .63), largely reflecting stable differences between learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)

Prior mathematical knowledge correlates r = .63 with later mathematics achievement (Simonsmeier et al. 2022, k = 950, corrected for unreliability), the second-largest of 66 correlates. These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

## Evidence

### Breit et al. 2025

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. *Learning and Individual Differences, 118*, 102621. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621)

`q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses` · `i3 · large effect, r = .63 (k = 950)` · `n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)` · `review · r3`

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. The estimate comes from Simonsmeier et al. (2022), 950 effect sizes across mixed ages, corrected for unreliability. The authors caution that most of the underlying studies relate prior knowledge to later knowledge rather than to knowledge growth, so the correlation largely reflects how stable mathematics achievement is over time rather than an effect of prior knowledge on how much is learned.
