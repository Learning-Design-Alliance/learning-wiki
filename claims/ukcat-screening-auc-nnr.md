## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ROC analysis treating UKCAT total score as a screening test for failing at least one year at first attempt, with missing outcomes (including unsuccessful applicants) singly imputed (Fig. 8, Table 5). Sensitivity at z ≤ 0 was 62.1% and specificity 50.5%.

> "the ROC curve is rather flat, although the area under the curve (AUC) is, at 0.61, significantly different to 0.5 (95 % confidence intervals 0.60 to 0.61)"

### Tiffin Paul A. 2016 (2)

Tiffin Paul A., Mwandigha Lazaro M., Paton Lewis W., Hesselgreaves H., McLachlan John C., Finn Gabrielle M., Kasim Adetayo S. (2016). Predictive validity of the UKCAT for medical school undergraduate performance: a national prospective cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0682-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-by-two contingency analysis (Table 6) at the medium threshold (z ≤ 0) using real and singly imputed outcomes. The article reports an absolute risk reduction of 15%, a number needed to screen of seven, and an NNR of 1.18 (5790:4903), rising to 1.5 at a higher threshold.

> "46 % (4903/10,693; in effect the positive predictive value of the test) of those who screen positive are estimated to fail at least one of their year’s at first attempt. In contrast, only 31 % (4370/13,930) of those that are above the screening threshold are estimated to fail"
