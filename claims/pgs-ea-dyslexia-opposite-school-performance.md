## Evidence

### Graell M. et al 2026

Graell M. et al. (2026). Towards disentangling the polygenic contribution of dyslexia to school performance. Translational Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41398-026-04118-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal mixed-effect models in the INSchool cohort (n = 4274) tested PGS associations with school grades in mathematics, primary language and foreign language. PGSEA OR > 1.38; PGSDYS OR = 0.79 for first language, 0.80 for foreign language, 0.89 for mathematics. Variance explained ranged from 2.69–3.88% for EA and 0.46–1.65% for dyslexia.

> "PGSEA were associated with higher odds of having a better performance in all three subjects studied in the 4274 school-aged participants from the INSchool cohort (OR > 1.38, P < 4.41E-24). In contrast, PGS for dyslexia (PGSDYS) were associated with worse performance across all three subjects, with stronger effect in language-related subjects"
