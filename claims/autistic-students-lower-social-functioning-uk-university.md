## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared to non-autistic students, students with autism scored significantly lower on the social functioning component (p < 0.001), reporting more difficulty socialising, less involvement in social activities, and less meaningful relationships. [→ Gurbuz Emine 2018](#gurbuz-emine-2018)

## Evidence

### Gurbuz Emine 2018

Gurbuz Emine, Hanley Mary, Riby Deborah M. (2018). University Students with Autism: The Social and Academic Experiences of University in the UK. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3741-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Questionnaire comparison of 26 autistic and 158 non-autistic UK university students; PCA-derived social functioning scores were significantly lower in the autism group (p < 0.001), with items showing "more difficulties in socialising and making friends" and less involvement in social activities. No effect size printed.

> "Compared to the non-autistic students, those with autism had significantly lower scores both in social (p < 0.001) and academic components (p < 0.001)."

### Gurbuz Emine 2018 (2)

Gurbuz Emine, Hanley Mary, Riby Deborah M. (2018). University Students with Autism: The Social and Academic Experiences of University in the UK. Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-018-3741-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive item-level percentages from the same questionnaire study show comparable friendship motivation between groups (70% vs 88.5%; 77% vs 89%); these are descriptive percentages, not tested effects.

> "students with and without autism reported similar motivation levels for friendships such as pleasure talking to friends (70% in ASD; 88.5% in TD) and having fun times being with friends (77% in ASD; 89% in TD)"
