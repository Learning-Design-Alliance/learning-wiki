## Evidence

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis comparing per-block homework benefit on the three midterms against whole-semester homework benefit on the final exam, for four semesters (fall 2009 to spring 2011), shown in Fig. 4. The authors report "very little difference in benefit" on the first two midterms.

> "There is very little difference in benefit between students in different aptitude groups on the first two midterms."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (2)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Final-exam analysis from the Fig. 4 data for the four semesters with three midterms; spring 2009 was excluded because that semester had only two midterm exams. The authors report a "statistically significant negative benefit" on the final exam for both lower-aptitude groups.

> "On the final exam, students in both the medium-low and low aptitude groups had a statistically significant negative benefit from completing homework."

### F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry 2013 (3)

F. J. Kontur and N. B. Terry. (2013). The benefits of completing homework for students with different aptitudes in an introductory physics course. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1305.2213)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Authors' interpretation of the Fig. 4 midterm-versus-final pattern, not a separately tested result. They write that "the effects of doing homework are indeed cumulative", with high-aptitude students building on existing schemas while lower-aptitude students accumulated confusion.

> "These data suggest that the effects of doing homework are indeed cumulative."
