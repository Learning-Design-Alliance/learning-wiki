## Evidence

### UMANODAN Rino 2014

UMANODAN Rino, SHIMAZU Akihito, MINAMI Masahide, KAWAKAMI Norito. (2014). Effects of Computer-based Stress Management Training on Psychological Well-being and Work Performance in Japanese Employees: A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. Industrial Health 52(6). https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2013-0209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Additional analysis excluding the 'dashed study group' (participants who finished in 1–2 days; 40% of the intervention group), using mixed-model repeated measures ANOVA. The article reports the three significant interactions with the quoted F and p values; seeking social support was marginally significant (F=2.784, p=0.063).

> "Specifically, there was a significant group × time interaction for “problem-solving”, “avoidance and suppression”, and “knowledge about stress management” (F=5.029, p=0.007; F=3.171, p=0.043; F=7.819, p<0.001, respectively)."

### UMANODAN Rino 2014 (2)

UMANODAN Rino, SHIMAZU Akihito, MINAMI Masahide, KAWAKAMI Norito. (2014). Effects of Computer-based Stress Management Training on Psychological Well-being and Work Performance in Japanese Employees: A Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. Industrial Health 52(6). https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2013-0209

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple main effects of time in the same ≥3-day analysis: the article reports significant increases in problem-solving and seeking social support for the intervention group only, with the quoted p-values for T0–T1 and T0–T2 contrasts.

> "For the intervention group, “problem-solving” and “seeking social support” scores significantly increased from T0 to T1 (p=0.002; p<0.001, respectively) and from T0 to T2 (p=0.004; p=0.007, respectively)."
