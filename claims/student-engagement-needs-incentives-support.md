## Evidence

### Chen 2025

Chen, Z.; Singh, C. (2025). Opportunities and Challenges in Harnessing Digital Technology for Effective Teaching and Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.3390/higheredu4010006](https://doi.org/10.3390/higheredu4010006)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Interviews with 37 students in college introductory physics courses at the end of fall 2020 during remote pandemic teaching. The study found that 'when students had no grade incentive for doing out -of-class or in-class activities in a ﬂipped course' with solutions posted after each class, many students had difficulty self-regulating and stopped doing the weekly activities early in the semester, cramming before exams; students in otherwise similar courses with grade incentives kept up.

> "when students had no grade incentive for doing out -of-class or in-class activities in a ﬂipped course, and if the solutions to all problems and in -class assignments were posted on the course website after e ach class,"

### Chen 2025 (2)

Chen, Z.; Singh, C. (2025). Opportunities and Challenges in Harnessing Digital Technology for Effective Teaching and Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.3390/higheredu4010006](https://doi.org/10.3390/higheredu4010006)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r1`

A study in which students used web-based adaptive tutorials in introductory physics outside class as optional self-study and then took quizzes with problems sharing the same physics principles but different contexts. Many self-study students 'engaged with them superﬁcially and struggled to transfer', while students working the same tutorials in supervised one-on-one situations 'performed sig niﬁcantly better than them' regardless of their course physics grade.

> "students who worked on the same web-based tutorials in supervised, one -on-one situations performed sig niﬁcantly better than them regardless of their physics grade in the course at the time they engaged with the web-based tutorial"
