# Incremental conceptual change adds or deletes single resources, and the two incremental changes in the light-and-shadow tutorial can be independent of each other

## Evidence

### Wittmann 2006

Wittmann, M. C. (2006). Using resource graphs to represent conceptual change. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Examination of student responses in a recently taught University of Maine course using materials slightly modified from the UW light-and-shadow materials. The article reports that "some students did not stop using “masks invert” and did not use “adding effects” in their responses," supporting independent incremental changes.

> "We found that some students did not stop using “masks invert” and did not use “adding effects” in their responses."

### Wittmann 2006 (2)

Wittmann, M. C. (2006). Using resource graphs to represent conceptual change. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Same examination of course responses: some students combined both resources, in that "they inverted the image due to the single point source and then added the effects due to each point source on an extended bulb," showing the two changes need not co-occur.

> "More importantly, some used both, in that they inverted the image due to the single point source and then added the effects due to each point source on an extended bulb (see Figure 6)."

### Wittmann 2006 (3)

Wittmann, M. C. (2006). Using resource graphs to represent conceptual change. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.2.020105)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Authors' report from their own teaching of the UW-based materials: even with explicit development of the long filament bulb as point sources, "many students have a great difficulty incorporating the idea of “adding” in their reasoning."

> "Even when helping students explicitly develop the model of a long filament light bulb as a series of several small point-like light sources, we have found many students have a great difficulty incorporating the idea of “adding” in their reasoning."
