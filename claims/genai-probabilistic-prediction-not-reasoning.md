### Prado 2026

Prado, Y.; Ruiz, P.; Fusco, J.; DuVall, D.; Hinkel, D.; Perkoff, M.; Rocha, O. (2026). AI in Education: A Set of Four Essential Resource Briefs for Educators. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/291

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

This is an explanatory assertion in the practitioner brief, offered without empirical evidence. It states GenAI produces content by predicting sequences and patterns and that it works "based on probability/statistics, not understanding."

> "GenAI is a type of machine learning that arrives at responses and creates content by predicting sequences and patterns, rather than by reasoning or verifying truth."

### Ulrike Cress · Joachim Kimmerle 2023

Ulrike Cress · Joachim Kimmerle. (2023). Co-constructing knowledge with generative AI tools: Reflections from a CSCL perspective. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09409-w

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument in the article's introduction framing generative AI tools like ChatGPT. The authors assert that these tools 'do not possess any conceptual knowledge' and may produce output lacking truth or validity. No empirical evidence is offered for this assertion.

> "Generative AI tools, however, do not possess any conceptual knowledge and have no conscious understanding of the world. They might produce text that might look adequate at first glance but lacks truth and validity or refers to entities that do not exist."
