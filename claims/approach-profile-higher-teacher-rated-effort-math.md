# In mathematics, teachers rated students with an approach-oriented goal profile as making more effort than students with a multiple-goals profile, and students who moved from a moderate to an approach-oriented profile gained more in teacher-rated effort than those who did not change

## Evidence

### Hornstra 2017

Hornstra, Lisette; Majoor, Marieke; Peetsma, Thea. (2017). Achievement goal profiles and developments in effort and achievement in upper elementary school. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 87(4), 606-629. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12167

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel analysis of teacher-rated general school effort across three waves for 722 students in mathematics. The article reports the approach-vs-multiple difference as large (d = .69) and the moderate-vs-multiple difference as large (d = .68) despite the latter not reaching significance.

> "students with an approach‐oriented profile in math were rated significantly higher on effort by their teachers than students with a multiple goals profile (b = 0.37, p = .033), whereas the difference between students with a moderate/indifferent profile compared to multiple goals students did not reach significance (b = 0.31, p = .053)"

### Hornstra 2017 (2)

Hornstra, Lisette; Majoor, Marieke; Peetsma, Thea. (2017). Achievement goal profiles and developments in effort and achievement in upper elementary school. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 87(4), 606-629. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12167

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Transition analysis of teacher-rated effort in mathematics. The article interprets this as an increase in mastery goals combined with a decrease in performance-avoidance goals accompanying effort gains substantial enough to be detected by teachers. The design is observational, so the transition is associated with the effort change rather than shown to cause it.

> "When students transitioned from a moderate to an approach‐oriented profile, their teachers’ ratings of effort increased compared to students who did not make a transition (b = 0.25, p = .032), which can be considered a medium‐sized effect (d = .51)"
