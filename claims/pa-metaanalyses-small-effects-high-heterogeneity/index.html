Evidence¶

Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020¶

Wassenaar Thomas M., Williamson Wilby, Johansen-Berg Heidi, Dawes Helen, Roberts Nia, Foster Charlie, Sexton Claire E. (2020). A critical evaluation of systematic reviews assessing the effect of chronic physical activity on academic achievement, cognition and the brain in children and adolescents: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-020-00959-y

q3 · i1 · quant-synthesis · r2

The review of reviews synthesised meta-analytic results from 18 low or critically low quality reviews. It reports "small effect sizes (0.1–0.3)" accompanied by high heterogeneity; individual meta-analyses in Table 2 and 3 report d or g values in this range with I2 often above 50%.

"Eighteen (critically) low-quality reviews presented mixed favourable and null effects, with meta-analyses showing small effect sizes (0.1–0.3) and high heterogeneity."

Related Claims¶

  • Evidence for physical activity benefits on academic achievement in youth is inconsistent: six of twelve reviews concluded benefits, six mixed or inconclusive
  • Eight of thirteen reviews concluded physical activity benefits cognitive performance, five reported mixed or inconclusive evidence, and none suggested worse cognition
  • A single bout of acute physical activity increases overall academic outcomes in school-aged youth (Hedge's g = 0.35)
  • Acute physical activity improves mathematics performance in school-aged youth (Hedge's g = 0.29 multivariate)
  • Chronic physical activity shows a medium positive effect on academic achievement while acute physical activity shows a null effect — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Mathematics is the subject where positive associations with physical activity are found most consistently in systematic reviews, though meta-analytic effects range from null to medium — related
  • Children with lower baseline achievement, lower IQ or lower school engagement may benefit more from school-based PA interventions, though evidence is mixed — related
  • Effect sizes across included studies show high heterogeneity (all I² above 75%) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Effect sizes across the included studies are highly heterogeneous (I² = 91.37%), indicating moderating factors — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Physical activity has null or small-to-medium positive effects on school-age children's and adolescents' academic achievement according to meta-analyses — related
