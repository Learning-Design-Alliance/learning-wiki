# After self-scoring, the monitoring-accuracy gap between low- and high-performing students remains on multiplication (small) but disappears on division

## Evidence

### Sophie Oudman 2021

Sophie Oudman, Janneke van de Pol, Tamara van Gog. (2021). Effects of self-scoring their math problem solutions on primary school students' monitoring and regulation. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:213–239. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09281-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multilevel regression comparing skill groups after self-scoring (Table 2: multiplication B = −0.07, p < 0.05; division B = 0.02, non-significant). Self-scoring appears to close the accuracy gap on division but not fully on multiplication.

> "on the multiplication task, high-performing students made significantly more accurate monitoring judgments after self-scoring than low-performing students, although this difference was only 0.07 on a seven-point scale. For the division task, high- and low-performing students' monitoring accuracy after self-scoring did not differ significantly."
