# Kindergarten convergence is not attributable to pre-K children's kindergarten classroom experiences but is attributable to preexisting individual differences

## Evidence

### Erik Ruzek 2020

Erik Ruzek, Arya Ansari, Robert Pianta, Jessica Whittaker, Virginia Vitiello. (2020). Persistence and convergence: The end of kindergarten outcomes of pre-K graduates and their nonattending peers. Developmental Psychology, 56(11), 2027–2039. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001115

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The study reports that "convergence in the groups’ performance in kindergarten was not attributed to pre-K children’s classroom experiences in kindergarten," ruling out kindergarten classroom quality as the explanation in this sample of 2,581 children.

> "convergence in the groups’ performance in kindergarten was not attributed to pre-K children’s classroom experiences in kindergarten"

### Erik Ruzek 2020 (2)

Erik Ruzek, Arya Ansari, Robert Pianta, Jessica Whittaker, Virginia Vitiello. (2020). Persistence and convergence: The end of kindergarten outcomes of pre-K graduates and their nonattending peers. Developmental Psychology, 56(11), 2027–2039. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001115

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r?`

The authors report convergence was "attributable to preexisting individual differences" and found "support for the notion" that longer-term outcomes are impacted by factors outside the scope of early schooling, despite pre-K improving children's skills.

> "Convergence was, however, attributable to preexisting individual differences, and there was support for the notion that even though children’s skills are susceptible to improvement as a result of pre-K, their longer-term outcomes are likely to be impacted by factors that are outside the scope of early schooling"
