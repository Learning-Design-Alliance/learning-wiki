## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants with good NR knowledge (score 80% or higher on a 25-question knowledge test) had significantly higher odds of skill retention than those with poor knowledge (AOR 3.31, 95% CI 1.41–7.73). [→ Sintayehu Y 2020](#sintayehu-y-2020)

## Evidence

### Sintayehu Y 2020

Sintayehu Y, Assefa D, Biftu G, Getahun T, Haymanot M, Kasiye S, Teshale M. (2020). Basic neonatal resuscitation skills of midwives and nurses in Eastern Ethiopia are not well retained: An observational study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0236194

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable logistic regression on 427 participants; 40.5% of those with good knowledge retained skills versus 8.1% with poor knowledge, and "those with good knowledge in NR (AOR, 3.31 [95% CI: 1.41, 7.73]) were three times more likely to retain their NR skill when compared with those with poor knowledge". The article prints an adjusted odds ratio but no standardized effect size.
