Evidence¶

Al‐Me'ani Mohammad 2026¶

Al‐Me'ani Mohammad, Alqaisi Omar, Dibas Mohammed, Al‐Aaraj Heyam, Rubbai Yousef. (2026). Effectiveness of Educational Programmes on Knowledge and Practice Related to Diabetic Foot Care Among Nurses: A Systematic Review. Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism. https://doi.org/10.1002/edm2.70324

q3 · i? · review · r3

The review's own GRADE assessment across nine studies (589 participants for knowledge, 436 for practice, per Table 4), rating overall certainty 'very low' for both outcomes; the review states the true effect may differ substantially from reported estimates.

"the risk of bias was judged to be serious; all nine included studies utilized quasi‐experimental designs, predominantly lacking randomization and concurrent control groups, which significantly restricts causal inference"

Related Claims¶

  • The evidence base on inclusion effects is methodologically weak: no randomised studies and nearly all synthesised studies at serious risk of bias — related
  • An interactive workshop produced higher post-test knowledge scores than an e-learning course of equivalent content among nurses — related
  • Most included studies carried a high risk of bias or some concerns, particularly for confounding and outcome measurement, limiting certainty — related
  • No included randomized comparison was at low risk of bias, and all 17 non-randomized comparisons were rated serious risk under ROBINS-I — related
  • Nurses begin with low baseline knowledge of diabetic foot care, and educational qualifications may moderate post-training knowledge gains — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Certainty of evidence for all outcomes was rated low due to serious risk of bias and heterogeneity — related
  • Certainty of evidence for narrative medicine education effects is rated very low under GRADE — related
  • Included active-methodology studies had a high overall risk of bias — related
