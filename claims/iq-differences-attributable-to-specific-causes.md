### Lincoln 1922

Lincoln, Edward A. (1922). The Constancy of Intelligence Quotients (A Case Study). The Journal of Educational Psychology, 14, 484-495. https://archive.org/details/journalofeducat14

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative case analysis of Number 3's four successive Stanford-Binet records. The 1920 slump is explained by "he failed three tests which he had passed the previous year," and passing those would have raised his IQ from 94 to 101.

> "In this test he failed three tests which he had passed the previous year, and he was not given the chance to make points on the form board as previously."

### Lincoln 1922 (2)

Lincoln, Edward A. (1922). The Constancy of Intelligence Quotients (A Case Study). The Journal of Educational Psychology, 14, 484-495. https://archive.org/details/journalofeducat14

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case analysis of Number 24, whose first IQ of 114 with wide scatter and a Doll Short Test IQ of 125 contrasted with later scores of 112 and 124. The author writes "It seems reasonable to suppose that the lower IQ's resulting from the first examinations were due to the failure of the subject to put forth his full effort during the testing."
