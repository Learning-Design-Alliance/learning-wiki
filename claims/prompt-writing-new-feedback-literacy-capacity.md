# Prompt-writing ability is a new feedback-eliciting capacity that shapes GenAI feedback engagement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In the paper's illustrative examples, a student who wrote specific, well-structured prompts obtained useful ChatGPT feedback while a student with vague prompts deemed the feedback useless, suggesting prompt writing should be added to feedback-eliciting capacities. [→ Ying Zhan 2025](#ying-zhan-2025)

## Evidence

### Ying Zhan 2025

Ying Zhan, David Boud, Phillip Dawson & Zi Yan (2025) Generative artificial intelligence as an enabler of student feedback engagement: a framework, Higher Education Research & Development, 44:5, 1289-1304, https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2025.2476513

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Illustrative contrast of two students in a trial use of ChatGPT 3.5 for IELTS writing feedback at the first author's university. Student B asked a specific examiner-role prompt and received useful information; Student A's broad question yielded information 'Student A deemed useless'.

> "Student B's ability to write prompts aligns well with the personalised feedback feature of ChatGPT, while Student A lacked such ability."
