## Evidence

### Javid 2014

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Student interview item on classroom Arabic use; all 34 interviewed students agreed they use Arabic in classrooms and seldom use English when talking to each other or to their Arab teachers. Reported mean Arabic use was 59.2% (SD 13.9).

> "The reported percentage of Arabic use in the classroom varied from 30% to 90% of the total teaching time. The mean was calculated as 59.2% with a high SD rate (13.9)."

### Javid 2014 (2)

Javid, C. Z. (2014). EMP Needs of Medical Undergraduates in a Saudi Context. Kashmir Journal of Language Research, 14(1), 89-110. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1410.6469)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Classroom observation of 11 classes (6 theoretical lectures, 3 small-group discussions, 2 practical sessions), each 30 minutes, with Arabic Speaking Time (AST) calculated from recordings; AST totalled 98 minutes.

> "Another very important finding has been the substantial amount of time, 98 minutes, dedicated to Arabic speaking in the classroom both by the teachers as well as by the students."
