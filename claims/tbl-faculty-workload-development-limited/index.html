Evidence¶

Alizadeh Maryam 2024¶

Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Umbrella review synthesis: one study reported TBL required more time (12 h versus 5 h) than lecture; three studies reported positive educator attitudes toward the TBL class atmosphere.

"Out of these four reviews, three reported an increase in workload in the beginning [17, 19, 34]. However, these reviews also highlighted that with experience and familiarity, faculty members were able to optimize their workload related to TBL."

Alizadeh Maryam 2024 (2)¶

Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Umbrella review synthesis: only one review mentioned facilitator or educator training prior to implementing TBL, finding training "limited in duration and scope".

"This particular review reported that the training provided to facilitators was minimal, often limited to reading about the TBL method or attending a one-day workshop."
