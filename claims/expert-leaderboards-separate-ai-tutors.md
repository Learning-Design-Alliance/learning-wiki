# Expert tutors' lesson-planning and practice-design leaderboards separated AI models, and expert-rated practice design correlated with fewer student answer disputes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 2,028 pairwise rubric reviews by 51 expert tutors, leaderboards distinguished models, Anthropic models (particularly Opus) performed strongly, and expert practice-design preference correlated with fewer student answer disputes (ρ=0.71 and 0.77 in Quantitative and Combined). ## Evidence

### Curtis Northcutt 2026

Curtis Northcutt, Inaara Hasmani, Kevin Feng, Trevor Khangi, Andreas Plesner, Jonas Mueller. (2026). StudentBench: AI and human tutoring yield equivalent GRE learning gains. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28470 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.28470](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.28470)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second study: 51 expert human tutors completed 2,028 pairwise reviews of AI lesson plans and practice problems based on 381 student pre-tests; correlations with student flag rates were "Spearmanρ=0.71 and0.77". Correlational, exploratory; flags record disputes, not verified errors.

> "Interestingly, AI tutors scoring highly on practice problem design (Figure3B), i.e., AI tutors preferred by experts for practice-problem creation, also tended to receive fewer student answer disputes in Quantitative and Combined (Spearmanρ=0.71 and0.77;p=.035andp=.033; Appendix F.2)."
