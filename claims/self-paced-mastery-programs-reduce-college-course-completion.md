## Evidence

### Kulik et al. 1990

Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299. [doi:10.3102/00346543060002265](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)

`q4 · meta-analysis (32 PSI studies reporting completion)` · `i? · small effect, h = -0.14 on attrition (not a d metric)` · `n=32 studies` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Thirty-two evaluations compared course completion in mastery and conventional classes; all were of PSI, the self-paced variant. Nine found higher completion in the mastery class and 23 in the conventional class; the average effect (Cohen's h for attrition) was -0.14, small and favouring conventional instruction. The examination advantage was not an artefact of weaker students dropping out: it was 0.46 SD where PSI completion was lower and 0.55 SD where it was higher.

> "Nine of the 32 studies found a higher completion rate in the mastery class, and 23 studies found a higher completion rate in the control class. The average h for attrition for these 32 studies was -0.14, t(31) = 2.86, p < .01."

## Discussion

The cost belongs to self-pacing, not to mastery gating as such: group-based mastery evaluations report lower attrition ([In one synthesis of group-based mastery learning, … lower attrition](mastery-improves-engagement-attrition.md)). A self-paced or adaptive course built on mastery gating should plan for procrastination and non-completion, with deadlines or pacing support.
