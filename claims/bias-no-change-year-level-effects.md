# Bias scores did not improve over the school year, but school year level mattered: year 1 students became less overconfident while year 3 students improved their underconfidence

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Bias scores showed no statistically detectable change over time, and reflection support did not matter for bias. [→ Nederhand 2020](#nederhand-2020)
`q3 i?` Year level predicted bias and interacted with time: year 1 students became less overconfident, year 3 students improved their underconfidence. [→ Nederhand 2020 (2)](#nederhand-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nederhand 2020

Nederhand, M. L., Tabbers, H. K., Jongerling, J., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2020). Reflection on exam grades to improve calibration of secondary school students: a longitudinal study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09233-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Three-level multilevel model of bias scores (signed estimate-minus-grade difference) in the French course across the school year, with intervention groups dummy-coded against Practice. The article reports no statistically detectable change in bias over time.

> "results showed no statistically detectable change in bias scores over time, b = −0.03, t(8) = −0.60, p = .567, nor a statistically detectable random effect of bias scores over time,χ2(205) = 232.17, p = .094."

### Nederhand 2020 (2)

Nederhand, M. L., Tabbers, H. K., Jongerling, J., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2020). Reflection on exam grades to improve calibration of secondary school students: a longitudinal study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09233-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same three-level bias model, Year was a statistically detectable predictor and interacted with Time. The article states "Students in year 1 showed more overconfidence than students in year 2 and year 3" and describes divergent improvement directions by year.

> "Students in year 1 showed more overconfidence than students in year 2 and year 3. Furthermore, there was an interaction between Time and Year."
