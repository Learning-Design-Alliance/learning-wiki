## Evidence

### Melissa Dancy 2002

Melissa Dancy, Wolfgang Christian and Mario Belloni. (2002). Teaching with Physlets: Examples from Optics. The Physics Teacher. [doi:10.1119/1.1526622](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1526622)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' observational claim from classroom experience with Physlet problems, contrasting interactive applets with static pictures which students cannot interact with. No data are reported; the authors state they have "seen the benefit of interactivity in three very specific ways."

> "We have seen the benefit of interactivity in three very specific ways:  the ability of the students to be creative, the learning opportunities provided, and the likelihood of students demonstrating misconceptions."

### Melissa Dancy 2002 (2)

Melissa Dancy, Wolfgang Christian and Mario Belloni. (2002). Teaching with Physlets: Examples from Optics. The Physics Teacher. [doi:10.1119/1.1526622](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1526622)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' interpretation of student behavior on the mirror problem: some students declared a shifting convergence point (x = 0.62 cm) to be the focal point. The authors note such students "could easily dispel it themselves by noting that the point of convergence changed as the point source was moved."

> "Had the students been given a traditional problem that did not allow them to interact, this misconception would not have been so apparent."
