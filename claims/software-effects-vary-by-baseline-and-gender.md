## Evidence

### Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli 2014

Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli. (2014). Teachers’ Helpers: Experimental Evidence from Costa Rica on Computers for English Language Learning. SREE Spring 2014 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562994

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup difference-in-difference analyses (Tables 5 and 6) testing treatment-by-subgroup interactions in the randomized trial. The article reports DynEd's effects "did not vary significantly by schools’ baseline test scores", while Imagine's were significantly lower for low-scoring schools and students (e.g., t*Imagine*Low of -1.116** on verbal analogies, Table 5b).

> "In contrast, Imagine Learning’s effects were significantly lower in low-scoring schools and for low-scoring students on some subtests in both years."

### Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli 2014 (2)

Sarah Humpage and Horacio Álvarez-Marinelli. (2014). Teachers’ Helpers: Experimental Evidence from Costa Rica on Computers for English Language Learning. SREE Spring 2014 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562994

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Gender-subgroup analyses (Tables 7a-7c) in the same trial. Table 7c shows t*DynEd*Female coefficients of 0.424** (picture vocabulary), 0.397* (understanding directions) and 0.390* (oral language) at end of year two.

> "Neither software’s effects varied significantly by gender, but Dyned’s advantage over Imagine is significantly greater for girls on some subtests in the second year."
