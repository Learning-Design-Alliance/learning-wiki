Claims

In a comparison of two consecutive cohorts, adding peer role-play and peer assessment was associated with higher scores on non-technical clinical competencies (doctor-patient communication, humanistic care, preoperative preparation)

In a historical two-cohort comparison of international medical students, the cohort taught with peer role-play and peer assessment scored higher on doctor-patient communication, humanistic care and preoperative preparation, with comparable technical proficiency; the design cannot establish a causal effect
