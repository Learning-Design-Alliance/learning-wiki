## Evidence

### White Brahmia 2021

White Brahmia, S. W., Olsho, A., Smith, T. I., Boudreaux, A., Eaton, P., and Zimmerman, C. (2021). The Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy: A tool for assessing mathematical reasoning in introductory physics. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Comparison of online, unproctored PIQL administrations (run on Institution 1's survey/quiz platform during COVID-19) with in-person administrations suggests comparable performance and serious engagement; the article reports no effect size for this comparison.

> "overall results from online administrations of the PIQL suggest that students take the online-version of the assessment seriously and per- form at roughly the same level as for in-person administration [54]."

### White Brahmia 2021 (2)

White Brahmia, S. W., Olsho, A., Smith, T. I., Boudreaux, A., Eaton, P., and Zimmerman, C. (2021). The Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy: A tool for assessing mathematical reasoning in introductory physics. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The article reports small but significant performance differences on MCMR items between administration methods, with a preliminary investigation attributing them to the difference in administration method; no effect size is printed, and the analysis is described as preliminary.

> "We note that small but signiﬁcant differences are seen in student performance on MCMR items [54]."
