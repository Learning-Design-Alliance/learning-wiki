# Peer pressure within project groups was often more motivational than faculty guidance, and allowing group splits rescued motivated members

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Faculty mentors observed that peer pressure within project groups was often more motivational than faculty guidance, and in every case where a motivated member split from unmotivated members, a project paper of excellent quality resulted. [→ Michael Courtney and Amy Courtney 2012](#michael-courtney-and-amy-courtney-2012)

## Evidence

### Michael Courtney and Amy Courtney 2012

Michael Courtney and Amy Courtney. (2012). Impossible? Publication Quality Research with the Weakest 10% of Incoming Freshmen. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1211.7194](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1211.7194)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative case observations by faculty mentors in the cadet research program, including an occasion where a motivated member slammed a graded draft on a partner's desk, after which both classroom work and the Part 3 product rapidly improved. The authors also report that group splits always yielded excellent papers.

> "Faculty mentors have seen numerous occasions where peer pressure is more motivational than faculty guidance."
