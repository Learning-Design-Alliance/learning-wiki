## Evidence

### Minju Park 2026

Minju Park, Ivan Orozco Vasquez, and Cristina Conati. (2026). Characterizing Students' LLM Usage Behaviors and Their Association with Learning in Critical Thinking Tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04534 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.04534](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.04534)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive analysis of per-assignment usage rates across 13 papers (Figure 1) in the two-term course dataset. Usage varied by assignment and task, with an increase for later assignments, particularly discussion points; the authors interpret this as students growing more comfortable integrating LLMs.

> "A notable trend is the increase in LLM usage for later assignments, particularly for the discussion points task, suggesting that students may have grown more comfortable integrating LLMs into their workflow as the course progressed."

### Minju Park 2026 (2)

Minju Park, Ivan Orozco Vasquez, and Cristina Conati. (2026). Characterizing Students' LLM Usage Behaviors and Their Association with Learning in Critical Thinking Tasks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04534

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive trajectory analysis of the 16 Low-Reliance students across assignments (Figures 1-2). The article reports "an average of three students using LLMs per assignment before Midterm 1", falling to one after Midterm 2, which the authors read as students reducing usage as they grew more comfortable with the tasks.

> "Students in the Low-Reliance group showed more con- centrated usage early in the term, with an average of three students using LLMs per assignment before Midterm 1."
