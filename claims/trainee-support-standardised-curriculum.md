## Evidence

### Mooney SS 2022

Mooney SS, Hiscock RJ, Hicks L, Narula S, Maher PJ, Readman E, Pendlebury A, Ellett L. (2022). We live in a virtual world: Training the trainee using an integrated visual reality simulator curriculum. Aust N Z J Obstet Gynaecol. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajo.13521

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire VAS ratings from the intervention groups: mean usefulness 7.7 (SD 1.66) for novice-trained and 8.4 (SD 0.74) for experienced-trained trainees (P = 0.30), with "100% of intervention participants reporting a need for a standardised laparoscopic curriculum".

> "The NT group reported a mean usefulness of the curriculum of 7.7 (1.66) out of ten. ET reported similar usefulness with a mean VAS of 8.4 (0.74) (P = 0.30). Trainees strongly supported the program, with 100% of intervention participants reporting a need for a standardised laparoscopic curriculum."

### Mooney SS 2022 (2)

Mooney SS, Hiscock RJ, Hicks L, Narula S, Maher PJ, Readman E, Pendlebury A, Ellett L. (2022). We live in a virtual world: Training the trainee using an integrated visual reality simulator curriculum. Aust N Z J Obstet Gynaecol. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajo.13521

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Free-text questionnaire responses identifying "quality of simulation, the use of case scenarios, competitions and rewards, and live operating ‘incentives’" as key drivers of curriculum use; the authors note external motivators were trialled elsewhere with limited success.

> "Trainees suggested key drivers to motivate use of a curriculum: quality of simulation, the use of case scenarios, competitions and rewards, and live operating ‘incentives’ for diligent trainees."
