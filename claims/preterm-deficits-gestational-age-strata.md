# Mathematics impairments are more pronounced than reading deficits for extremely preterm children, and deficits appear across all gestational age strata including late preterm

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Extremely preterm children (<28 weeks) showed larger mathematics deficits (MD, −11.92) than reading deficits (MD, −8.54) relative to term-born peers. [→ McBryde M 2020](#mcbryde-m-2020)
`q3 i?` Very preterm (28-32 weeks) and late-preterm (33-37 weeks) children also showed significant mathematics and reading deficits, suggesting all preterm children are at risk. [→ McBryde M 2020 (2)](#mcbryde-m-2020-2)

## Evidence

### McBryde M 2020

McBryde M, Fitzallen GC, Liley HG, Taylor HG, Bora S. (2020). Academic Outcomes of School-Aged Children Born Preterm: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.2027

`q3 · i?`

Subgroup meta-analysis by gestational age. Extremely preterm children (aggregated sample of 2460) had significantly impaired reading (MD, −8.54; 95% CI, −10.52 − 6.55; I2 = 79%) versus term-born peers (aggregated sample of 1955).

> "Mathematics impairments were more pronounced than reading deficits for children born within the same extremely preterm category of less than 28 weeks' gestation (MD, −11.92; 95% CI, −14.60 to −9.24; I2 = 85%)"

### McBryde M 2020 (2)

McBryde M, Fitzallen GC, Liley HG, Taylor HG, Bora S. (2020). Academic Outcomes of School-Aged Children Born Preterm: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2020.2027

`q3 · i?`

Subgroup meta-analyses by gestational age. Very preterm children showed significant mathematics impairments; late-preterm children (n = 162 vs 168 term-born) showed marked reading deficits (MD, −8.07; 95% CI, −14.29 to −1.84).

> "For an aggregated sample of 710 very preterm children born from 28 to 32 weeks' gestation, significant mathematics impairments were also apparent when compared with an aggregated sample of 618 term-born peers (MD, −7.60; 95% CI, −9.25 to −5.96; I2 = 1%)."
