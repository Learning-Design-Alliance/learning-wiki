# Advanced counting competencies predict fifth grade mathematics achievement whereas basic counting does not when both are included together

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` In a model including both basic and advanced counting, advanced counting was predictive (β = 0.33, p < 0.001) while basic counting was not significant (β = 0.08, n.s.), and the two coefficients differed significantly (F = 7.28, p < 0.01). [→ Nguyen 2016](#nguyen-2016)

## Evidence

### Nguyen 2016

Nguyen, T., Watts, T. W., Duncan, G. J., Clements, D. H., Sarama, J. S., Wolfe, C., & Spitler, M. E. (2016). Which Preschool Mathematics Competencies Are Most Predictive of Fifth Grade Achievement? Early Childhood Research Quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2016.02.003

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r3`

Post-hoc regression (Model 5) on the 781-child sample recoded counting and cardinality items into basic (verbal counting, one-to-one correspondence, number recognition) and advanced (cardinality, counting forward/back, conceptual subitizing) competencies with full controls and classroom fixed effects.

> "we included both the basic and advanced counting competencies and found that advanced counting (β = 0.33, SE = 0.05, p < 0.001) is much more predictive of later achievement than basic counting (β = 0.08, SE = 0.05, n.s.). The coefficients for the basic and advanced counting competencies were statistically significantly different from each other (F = 7.28, p < 0.01)."
