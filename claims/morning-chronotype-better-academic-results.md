# Students with a morning chronotype achieve better academic results than evening-chronotype students, with a small effect size

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Morning-chronotype students (M = 7.41, SD = 0.89) score higher academically than evening-chronotype students (M = 7.15, SD = 0.76), d = 0.31. [→ Toscano-Hermoso M.D. 2020](#toscano-hermoso-md-2020)

## Evidence

### Toscano-Hermoso M.D. 2020

Toscano-Hermoso M.D., Arbinaga F., Fernández-Ozcorta E.J., Gómez-Salgado J., Ruiz-Frutos C. (2020). Influence of Sleeping Patterns in Health and Academic Performance Among University Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17082760

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of categorized chronotype (evening below the 10th percentile, morning above the 90th percentile of the Composite Scale of Morningness) on average academic score in the 855-student sample; differences observed at t = 2.127, p = 0.035, with "a small effect size (d = 0.31)".

> "students with a morning chronotype achieve better academic results (M = 7.41, SD = 0.89) than their evening counterparts (M = 7.15, SD = 0.76), although these differences have a small effect size (d = 0.31)"
