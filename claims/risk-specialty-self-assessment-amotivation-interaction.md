## Evidence

### Bansbach Joachim 2024

Bansbach Joachim, Bentele Michael, Bollinger Matthias, Bentele Stefanie, Langenhan Ronny, Gerber Bianka, Trifunovic-Koenig Milena, Bushuven Stefan. (2024). Self-assessment and learning motivation in emergency point-of-care ultrasound: an online pilot investigation in German physicians. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-024-01154-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Post hoc moderation analysis (SPSS PROCESS model 3, bootstrapping with deviation correction based on 5000 samples) with risk perception as predictor, specialty as moderator, and self-assessment as second moderator on amotivation. Table 2 prints the three-way trauma-surgeon interaction B = -0.96 with a CI of -1.88 to -0.03.

> "Simple slopes analysis revealed that the higher risk awareness concerning POCUS for anaesthesiologists resulted in lower amotivation, independent of their self-assessment. For surgeons, higher risk stratification for POCUS leads to higher amotivation, especially in the case of poor self-assessment. No interaction was found for internal medicine physicians (see Table 2; Fig. 2a, b and c)."
