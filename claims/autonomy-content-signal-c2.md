# Autonomy shows a strong content signal: 53 of 64 outcomes satisfy the a priori autonomy rule, with exceptions for self-efficacy (competence) and collegial support (relatedness)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across all 64 correlates, 53 satisfied the a priori rule that autonomy satisfaction is stronger for resources/positive outcomes and autonomy frustration stronger for demands/negative outcomes. [→ Marsh 2025](#marsh-2025)
`q3 i?` Sensible exceptions exist: self-efficacy aligned more strongly with competence and support from colleagues/community with relatedness, so the autonomy conclusion varies with the correlate set. [→ Marsh 2025](#marsh-2025)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2025

Marsh, H. W., Ryan, R. M., Dicke, T., Pekrun, R., Guo, J., Bradshaw, E. L., Reeve, J., Lüdtke, O., Clarke, T., & Waterschoot, J. (2025). Basic Psychological Needs Under Constrained Autonomy: A Substantive–Methodological Reflection and Analysis of School Leaders' Needs from a Self-Determination Theory Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10079-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Orthogonal-contrast GLM (C2: autonomy vs competence + relatedness) over the 64-variable nomological network from 1,950 Australian school leaders. The article reports "53 satisfy the a priori rule" and domain means of r = .07 to r = −.11.

> "Across all 64 outcomes, 53 satisfy the a priori rule, so autonomy has a strong content signal in this taxonomy. The mean values for C2 at the bottom of Table 4 mirror this pattern: resources (r = .07) and positive outcomes (r = .09) favor autonomy satisfaction, whereas demands (r = −.11) and negative outcomes (r = −.03) favor autonomy frustration."
