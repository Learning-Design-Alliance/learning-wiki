## Evidence

### Puolakanaho 2018

Puolakanaho, A., Lappalainen, R., Lappalainen, P., Muotka, J. S., Hirvonen, R., Eklund, K. M., Ahonen, T. P. S., & Kiuru, N. (2018). Reducing Stress and Enhancing Academic Buoyancy among Adolescents Using a Brief Web-based Program Based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-018-0973-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Moderation analysis using the per-protocol sample (n = 123), conducted in MPLUS with the Wald test (one-tailed) and gender as covariate. The article reports that "higher baseline overall stress (p < 0.001)" predicted greater gains; no effect size was printed.

> "those who had higher baseline overall stress (p < 0.001) and higher baseline school stress (p < . 05) had greater positive gains than those who had a lower level of stress in the baseline state"

### Puolakanaho 2018 (2)

Puolakanaho, A., Lappalainen, R., Lappalainen, P., Muotka, J. S., Hirvonen, R., Eklund, K. M., Ahonen, T. P. S., & Kiuru, N. (2018). Reducing Stress and Enhancing Academic Buoyancy among Adolescents Using a Brief Web-based Program Based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: A Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-018-0973-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same per-protocol moderation analysis (n = 123). The article reports buoyancy gains "were not connected to the initial level of buoyancy (p > 0.05)", a null moderation with no printed effect size.

> "In turn, the gains in academic buoyancy were not connected to the initial level of buoyancy (p > 0.05)."
