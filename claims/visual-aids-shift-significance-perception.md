## Evidence

### Aaron Fisher 2014

Aaron Fisher, G. Brooke Anderson, Roger Peng, Jeff Leek. (2014). A randomized trial in a massive online open course shows people don't know what a statistically significant relationship looks like, but they can learn. arXiv. [doi:10.7717/peerj.589](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.589)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regression analysis with person-specific random intercepts comparing plot categories against the 100-point reference category in the MOOC trial. Adding "best-ﬁt line, lowess curve" aids raised sensitivity odds ratios to 1.62 and 1.26 while lowering specificity odds ratios to .600 and .699.

> "Adding visual aids (best-ﬁt line, lowess curve) signiﬁcantly improved sensitivity (OR=1.62 and 1.26 respectively, with 95%CIs: 1.38-1.89 and 1.08-1.47), but signiﬁcantly reduced speciﬁcity (OR=.600 and .699 respectively, with 95%CIs: .508-.709 and .590-.829)."
