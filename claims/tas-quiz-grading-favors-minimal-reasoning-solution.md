# In a quiz context, TAs graded a minimal-reasoning solution (SSE) significantly higher than a detailed-reasoning solution with canceling mistakes (SSD)

## Evidence

### Yerushalmi 2014

Yerushalmi, E., Marshman, E., Maries, A., Henderson, C., & Singh, C. (2014). Grading Practices and Considerations of Graduate Students at the Beginning of their Teaching Assignment. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.068

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Worksheet-based study of 43 graduate TAs grading specially designed student solutions. In the quiz context the mean SSE grade was 8.3 vs 7.1 for SSD, with a 'p-value calculated by a t-test: 0.010'; no effect size is printed.

> "In a quiz context, TAs graded SSE significantly higher than SSD (<SSE>=8.3 compared to <SSD>=7.1, p-value calculated by a t-test: 0.010) while in a homework, the averages are comparable (<SSE>=7.1 and <SSD>=6.7)."

### Yerushalmi 2014 (2)

Yerushalmi, E., Marshman, E., Maries, A., Henderson, C., & Singh, C. (2014). Grading Practices and Considerations of Graduate Students at the Beginning of their Teaching Assignment. 2014 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.068

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Distribution of quiz-context grades assigned by the 43 TAs on the worksheet task: 65% (N=28) graded SSE>SSD versus 23% (N=10) grading SSD>SSE, which the authors call 'counterproductive to promoting the use of prescribed PS strategies'.

> "In the quiz context, many more TAs graded SSE>SSD (N=28, 65%) compared to SSD>SSE (N=10, 23%), transmitting a message that is counterproductive to promoting the use of prescribed PS strategies and providing explication of reasoning."

### Emily Marshman 2016

Emily Marshman, Alexandru Maries, Ryan T. Sayer, Charles Henderson, Chandralekha Singh, and Edit Yerushalmi. (2016). Physics postgraduate teaching assistants’ grading approaches: Conflicting goals and practices. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.08210](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.08210)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Simulated grading task at the start of a TA professional development program: 43 first-year physics TAs graded two student solutions in a quiz context; 'many more T As graded SSE higher than SSD' (65% vs 23%), with average scores SSE 8.3 vs SSD 7.1, t-test p = 0.010.

> "In the quiz context, many more T As graded SSE higher than SSD (N = 28, 65%) compared to those who graded SSE lower than SSD (N = 10, 23%) and, as a result, the average score for SSE was higher than for SSD (SSEavg = 8.3 compared to SSD avg = 7.1, p-value (t-test) = 0.010)."
