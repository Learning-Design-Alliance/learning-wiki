## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The review reports, citing Dimeo et al., that knowledge improved remarkably after the virtual escape room but showed no significant improvement after the lecture among 30 EM residents. [→ Rafi Alireza 2025](#rafi-alireza-2025)
`q2 i?` The motivation advantage of the escape room over the lecture held at one site but not another: Prisma Health-Upstate residents preferred the ER, while University of California-Irvine residents found both formats equally engaging. [→ Rafi Alireza 2025 (2)](#rafi-alireza-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rafi Alireza 2025

Rafi Alireza, Habibi Amin, Kalantarion Masomeh. (2025). Traditional medical education under siege: the revolutionary integration of virtual reality and gamification in medical escape rooms. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07367-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports a study of 30 EM residents from two programs comparing a Google Forms/Zoom virtual ER on infectious disease puzzles with a lecture, using pre- and post-quizzes; "a remarkable amelioration in knowledge after the ER, but no significant improvement after the lecture" was found.

> "Results revealed a remarkable amelioration in knowledge after the ER, but no significant improvement after the lecture."

### Rafi Alireza 2025 (2)

Rafi Alireza, Habibi Amin, Kalantarion Masomeh. (2025). Traditional medical education under siege: the revolutionary integration of virtual reality and gamification in medical escape rooms. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07367-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

In the same study, motivation measured by the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory differed by site: "Residents at Prisma Health-Upstate reported higher motivation for the ER", whereas the other site found the formats equally engaging, so the motivation effect does not replicate across sites.

> "Residents at Prisma Health-Upstate reported higher motivation for the ER, while the residents at University of California-Irvine found both formats equally engaging."
