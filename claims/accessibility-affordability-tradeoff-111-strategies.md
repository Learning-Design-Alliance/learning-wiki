# Among 111 strategies within ±30% budget change, higher accessibility correlates with higher enrollment and revenue but also worse unmet need, though some strategies are budget-neutral or budget-reducing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Strategies grouped by accessibility level (averaging 111%, 141%, 179%) show higher accessibility accompanying higher enrollment, revenue and budget change, but also worse unmet need ($478, $2066, $3322 respectively). [→ Vinhthuy Phan 2022](#vinhthuy-phan-2022)
`q2 i?` Some strategies yield approximately 110% accessibility increases while remaining budget neutral, and group A* strategies reduce the budget 20%–30% while maintaining around 110% increased accessibility. [→ Vinhthuy Phan 2022 (2)](#vinhthuy-phan-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Vinhthuy Phan 2022

Vinhthuy Phan, Laura Wright, Bridgette Decent. (2022). Optimizing Financial Aid Allocation to Improve Access and Affordability to Higher Education. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 14, No 3. https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/609

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of 111 optimized strategies within ±30% of the current budget, grouped into three accessibility levels in Table 3. The article reports "higher accessibility seemed to correlate with worse unmet need", with students paying an average of $478, $2066, or $3322 more under groups A, B, and C.

> "Strategies in these 3 groups have averaged expected change in accessibility of 111%, 141%, and 179%, respectively."

### Vinhthuy Phan 2022 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Closer examination of strategy diversity shown in Figure 4, plotting budget change against accessibility. The article notes group A strategies vary widely in budget demands and that some (group A*) "can reduce the budget significantly (between 20% to 30%)" while keeping accessibility increases around 110%.

> "there are a number of strategies that yield approximately 110% increase in accessibility while remaining budget neutral"
