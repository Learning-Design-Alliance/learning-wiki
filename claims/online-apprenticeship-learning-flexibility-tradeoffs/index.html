A key takeaway from the virtual discussion groups with state apprenticeship administrators, comparing online and in-person learning modalities as reported by participants.

"Online learning offers greater flexibility than in-person learning, despite certain challenges in implementing hybrid or remote learning options."

Related Claims¶

  • Training providers and instructors faced logistical challenges moving in-person apprenticeship training online but adapted quickly — related
  • Online professional development offers potential advantages of reduced travel costs, increased convenience, and community building among participants — related
  • State apprenticeship administrators leveraged technology to pivot work online, streamline processes, and expand reach and responsiveness during the pandemic — related
  • Reliable Internet access was a larger barrier to remote and hybrid learning than device access, especially in urban areas — related
  • Virtual meeting options gave parents more flexible scheduling opportunities to communicate with educators during remote schooling — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Local leaders supported student learning in similar and different ways across in-person, hybrid, and remote instructional modalities — related
  • Virtual pre-K observations posed substantial logistical challenges despite being practical
  • In its first 3-year grant cycle, 21CSLA served approximately 8,300 California leaders through 333 communities of practice and localized professional learning offerings plus coaching for hundreds of leaders — related
  • Staying motivated was the most pervasive challenge of learning remotely, and students missed instructor feedback, peer collaboration, and hands-on experiences — related
  • Two educational modalities were identified: face-to-face classes using PowerPoint presentations and online training accessible 24/7 via institutional websites. — related
