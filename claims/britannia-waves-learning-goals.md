# The activity is designed so that students learn that longitude can be derived from time measurements and that determining longitude was a historical navigation problem

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The lesson's stated goals include understanding that longitude can be derived from time measurements, that reliable longitude determination was an unsolved navigation problem with many ships lost, and that persistence like Harrison's can lead to great achievements. [→ Nielbock 2017](#nielbock-2017)

## Evidence

### Nielbock 2017

Nielbock, M. and Müller, T. (2017). Britannia Rule the Waves. AstroEDU manuscript no. astroedu1646. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1708.08332](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1708.08332)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Stated goals in Section 3 of the lesson plan; this is a design statement, not a tested result. A further goal states that "the astronomical (local) noon does not coincide with noon on the clock" and that Harrison's example shows persistence can lead to great achievements.

> "With this activity, the students will learn that – determining longitude reliably was an increasing problem in marine navigation of the 17th and 18th century with a large number of ships lost at sea. – longitude can be derived from time measurements."
