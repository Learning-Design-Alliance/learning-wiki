## Evidence

### Manuel G. Forero and H. J. Herrera-Suárez 2023

Manuel G. Forero and H. J. Herrera-Suárez. (2023). ChatGPT in the Classroom: Boon or Bane for Physics Students' Academic Performance?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2312.02422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.02422)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey question on the usefulness of ChatGPT for physics-related queries, administered to 41 students in Physics I, II, and III; 14.6 % rated it very useful and 43.9 % useful, described as "a general consensus on ChatGPT's utility for answering physics-related questions".

> "A combined total of 58.5 % of respondents categorize the tool as either "Very Useful" (14.6 %) or "Useful" (43.9 %), highlighting a general consensus on ChatGPT's utility for answering physics-related questions."

### Manuel G. Forero and H. J. Herrera-Suárez 2023 (2)

Manuel G. Forero and H. J. Herrera-Suárez. (2023). ChatGPT in the Classroom: Boon or Bane for Physics Students' Academic Performance?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2312.02422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2312.02422)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final survey question on recommendation of ChatGPT; combining "Definitely yes" (34.1 %) and "Yes" (46.3 %) gives 80.4 % who would recommend it, while the article reports 14.7 % would not.

> "Combining the categories "Definitely yes" (34.1 %) and "Yes" (46.3 %), 80.4 % of participants would recommend the use of ChatGPT as a support tool in physics courses."
