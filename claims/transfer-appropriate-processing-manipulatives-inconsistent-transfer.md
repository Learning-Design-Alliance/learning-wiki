# Transfer-outcome format can favor symbolic controls over manipulative conditions (transfer-appropriate processing), potentially explaining inconsistent transfer findings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When transfer of learning is measured with symbolic-format tasks, symbolic control conditions tend to outperform manipulative-based treatment conditions; the reverse may occur when transfer tasks require manipulating objects, a phenomenon the commentary identifies as transfer-appropriate processing. [→ Scott C. Marley & Kira J. Carbonneau 2014](#scott-c-marley-kira-j-carbonneau-2014)

## Evidence

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Commentary discussion in the Transfer of Learning section proposing transfer-appropriate processing (citing Morris et al. 1977) as an explanation for inconsistent transfer results: "control conditions will tend to perform better than manipulative-based treatment conditions" on symbolic-format measures.
