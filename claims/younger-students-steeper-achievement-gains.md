# Achievement progress is steeper for students tested at younger ages, especially in math

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Decadal math gains are largest for students tested at age 9/4th grade (0.23sd–0.39sd across surveys) and smallest for older students (no more than 0.06sd). [→ M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson 2022](#m-danish-shakeel-paul-e-peterson-2022)
`q3 i?` Reading gains are modest at all ages, with little evidence that cohort advances vary by testing age in reading. [→ M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson 2022 (2)](#m-danish-shakeel-paul-e-peterson-2022-2)

## Evidence

### M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson 2022

M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson. (2022). A Half Century of Progress in US Student Achievement: Agency and Flynn Effects, Ethnic and SES Differences. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09657-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Differential Trends by Age results, shown in Fig. 1 and Tables 2–3, report math change per decade by age group: younger students gain most, early-adolescent gains range from –0.10sd (PISA) to 0.27sd (NAEP), and older students gain "no more than 0.05sd on the NAEP and 0.06sd on the LTT".

> "The steepest upward trend is observed among students tested in m at a younger age (Fig. 1). For those age 9 or in the 4th grade, the decadal rate of change in this subject varies between 0.23sd on the LTT to 0.39sd on the NAEP."

### M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson 2022 (2)

M. Danish Shakeel · Paul E. Peterson. (2022). A Half Century of Progress in US Student Achievement: Agency and Flynn Effects, Ethnic and SES Differences. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09657-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same Results section reports reading trends of 0.05sd–0.09sd for younger students, about the same or negative for early adolescence, and "hardly any change at all on the LTT test" for older students.

> "On rd tests, it is less clear that cohort advances vary by the age at which a student is tested. In fact, there is only modest progress detected for any age group."
