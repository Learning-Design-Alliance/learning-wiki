## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The review distinguished 14 intervention component categories plus an 'other' category, but could not meta-analyze component effects because most evidence was descriptive or anecdotal. [→ Winstone 2017](#winstone-2017)

## Evidence

### Winstone 2017

Winstone, N. E., Nash, R. A., Parker, M., & Rowntree, J. (2017). Supporting Learners' Agentic Engagement with Feedback: A Systematic Review and a Taxonomy of Recipience Processes. *Educational Psychologist, 52*(1), 17–37. [doi:10.1080/00461520.2016.1207538](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2016.1207538)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative summary across the 105 intervention papers in the systematic review. Because of heterogeneous reporting standards, the authors state it "was not plausible to conduct a quantitative meta-analysis or qualitative meta-synthesis to compare the effects of different intervention components."

> "a large proportion of the evidence on different interventions came only from descriptive analysis, and anecdotal reports from learners and from the researchers themselves"

### Winstone 2017 (2)

Winstone, N. E., Nash, R. A., Parker, M., & Rowntree, J. (2017). Supporting Learners' Agentic Engagement with Feedback: A Systematic Review and a Taxonomy of Recipience Processes. *Educational Psychologist, 52*(1), 17–37. [doi:10.1080/00461520.2016.1207538](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2016.1207538)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Hovardas et al. (2014), limitations of peer-assessment interventions, alongside self-report evidence that undergraduates and graduates reported improved reflection, perspective-taking, and appreciation of grading criteria, and that Cartney's (2010) undergraduates remained more proactive months later.

> "peers do not always identify flaws in each other's work and can be less likely than experts to suggest amendments (Hovardas, Tsivitanidou, & Zacharia, 2014)"
