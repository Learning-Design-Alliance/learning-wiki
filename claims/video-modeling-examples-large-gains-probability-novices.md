# Novice secondary students show large pretest-to-posttest gains in learning and near transfer of probability calculation after studying a video modeling example, regardless of model or observer gender

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Learning scores improved massively from pretest to posttest after studying one video modeling example (no no-video control) (gp 2 = .807). [→ Vincent Hoogerheide 2016](#vincent-hoogerheide-2016)
`q3 i3` Near transfer scores also improved significantly from pretest to posttest (gp 2 = .495). [→ Vincent Hoogerheide 2016 (2)](#vincent-hoogerheide-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Vincent Hoogerheide 2016

Vincent Hoogerheide, Sofie M. M. Loyens, Tamara van Gog. (2016). Learning from video modeling examples: does gender matter? Instructional Science, 44, 69-86. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9360-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA (Test Moment within; Gender Model and Gender Observer between) on learning items in a 2x2 experiment with 163 secondary students remaining after exclusions, 162 of them in the test-performance analyses (one left during the posttest). Posttest learning (M = 5.56) far exceeded pretest (M = 0.30), with a large printed effect size "gp 2 = .807". The experiment manipulated model and observer gender; every condition studied a video, and there was no no-video or alternative-instruction control, so the gain shows improvement after the study phase rather than an effect of the video itself.

> "The scores obtained on the test items that measured learning showed a signiﬁcant main effect of Test Moment, F(1, 158) = 658.79, p \ .001, gp 2 = .807. Participants performed signiﬁcantly better on the Posttest (M = 5.56, SD = 2.55) than on the Pretest (M = 0.30, SD = 0.64)."

### Vincent Hoogerheide 2016 (2)

Vincent Hoogerheide, Sofie M. M. Loyens, Tamara van Gog. (2016). Learning from video modeling examples: does gender matter? Instructional Science, 44, 69-86. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9360-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same mixed ANOVA on the two near transfer items: posttest (M = 1.85) significantly higher than pretest (M = 0.28), with large printed effect size "gp 2 = .495". No gender main effects or interactions were significant. With no no-video control, the gain cannot be attributed to the video itself.

> "With regard to near transfer, the main effect of Test Moment was signiﬁcant, F(1, 158) = 154.96, p \ .001, gp 2 = .495. Per- formance was signiﬁcantly higher on the Posttest ( M = 1.85, SD = 1.66) than on the Pretest (M = 0.28, SD = 0.54)."
