# Arbitrarily assigned point values in the Yerkes-Bridges scale yield implausible growth-rate results, such as intellectual ability increasing more than fifty per cent during the fourth year and only approximately fifteen per cent during the seventh.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Point-scale-derived growth rates (more than fifty per cent in the fourth year, scarcely more than thirty in the fifth, approximately fifteen in the seventh) indicate an erroneous scale construction rather than genuine growth. ## Evidence

### ARTHUK S. OTIS (Stanford University) 1917

ARTHUK S. OTIS (Stanford University). (1917). A Criticism of the Yerkes-Bridges Point Scale, with Alternative Suggestions. The Journal of Educational Psychology, Vol. VIII, No. 3. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Otis+criticism+Yerkes-Bridges+point+scale

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article reports, quoting Yerkes and Wood (p. 604), these yearly increases in point-scale-measured ability. Otis attributes the pattern to giving one point to each of too many small increments in the easy part of the scale, calling the authors' interpretation an unwarranted confidence in arbitrarily assigned points. Secondhand reported data, analyzed critically.

> "During the fourth year of life, intellectual ability (always understood as measured by the point scale) increases more than fifty per cent.; during the fifth year of life, by scarcely more than thirty per cent.; during the seventh year, by approximately fifteen per cent."
