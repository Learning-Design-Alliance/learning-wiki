## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Jim regarded equations as law-like, fact-bearing truths more trustworthy than perception, and saw the connection between equations and physical experience as indirect and non-obvious, so he did not attempt reconciliation. [→ Gupta 2010](#gupta-2010)

Beyond deficit-based models of learners' cognition: Interpreting engineering students' difficulties with sense-making in terms of fine-grained epistemological and conceptual dynamics. Submitted to Journal of Engineering Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1003.4288](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1003.4288)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview transcript segment at 28:20–29:15 in which Jim explains choosing the mathematical answer over the experience-based answer on an exam. He calls equations "fact-bearing" and law-like, and elsewhere says the link to experience is "not obvious."

> "for an equation to be given to you it has to be like theory and it has to be fact-bearing. So, fact applies for everything. It is like a law. It applies to every single situation you could be in."
