## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Failure to account for previous test scores misattributes variance in student achievement to classroom interactions when it may reflect pre-existing student proficiencies, since students are tracked into higher and lower-achieving groups. ## Evidence

### Donald E Grant Jr. & Jerell B. Hill 2020

Donald E Grant Jr. & Jerell B. Hill. (2020). Activating Culturally Empathic Motivation in Diverse Students. Journal of Education and Learning, 9(5). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v9n5p45

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The article argues, drawing on value-added approaches (Allen et al., 2013; Rothstein, 2010), that end-of-year scores are highly dependent on pre-existing proficiency and highly correlated with prior-year scores. This is an analytical argument, not a new empirical test.

> "Failure to account for the previous test scores would thus misattribute variance in student achievement that would be more directly be considered for by pre-existing student proficiencies, not to be confused with intelligence."
