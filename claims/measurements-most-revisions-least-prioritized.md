## Evidence

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer 2018

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, Laura Ríos, Benjamin Pollard, Jacob T. Stanley, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2018). Characterizing lab instructors' self-reported learning goals to inform development of an experimental modeling skills assessment. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020118)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview study of 35 optics and electronics lab instructors; 80% identified making measurements as an important outcome, with "no major differences between optics and electronics instructors" on this subtask.

> "Most (80%) of the instructors in our study said that learning how to make measurements is an important outcome of their course."

### Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer 2018 (2)

Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, Laura Ríos, Benjamin Pollard, Jacob T. Stanley, and H. J. Lewandowski. (2018). Characterizing lab instructors' self-reported learning goals to inform development of an experimental modeling skills assessment. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020118)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Descriptive results from the same 35-instructor interview study reported alongside Fig. 2: making measurements drew the most instructor endorsements and enacting revisions the fewest; "63% of instructors listed three or more subtasks as important".

> "and about two-thirds (63%) of instructors listed three or more subtasks as important. Making measurements was identiﬁed as important by the largest number of instructors, and enacting revisions by the smallest."
