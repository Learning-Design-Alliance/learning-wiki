## Evidence

### Basu Andrea 2014

Basu Andrea, Kennedy Lynne, Tocque Karen, Jones Sharn. (2014). Eating for 1, Healthy and Active for 2; feasibility of delivering novel, compact training for midwives to build knowledge and confidence in giving nutrition, physical activity and weight management advice during pregnancy. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2393-14-218

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-training questionnaire responses from the same 32 midwives; the article reports 83% indicated confidence to explain risks of raised BMI was much or somewhat better (95% CI 66 to 93) and 89% for discussing eating habits and physical activity (95% CI 73 to 97).

> "Statistically significant improvements in confidence were observed for questions b-h (Table 2), indicating participants felt more confident in raising the sensitive issue of weight, discussing the risks associated with a raised BMI, and reassuring women about safe approaches to eating well and being physically active during pregnancy."

### Basu Andrea 2014 (2)

Basu Andrea, Kennedy Lynne, Tocque Karen, Jones Sharn. (2014). Eating for 1, Healthy and Active for 2; feasibility of delivering novel, compact training for midwives to build knowledge and confidence in giving nutrition, physical activity and weight management advice during pregnancy. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2393-14-218

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The one non-significant confidence change concerned measuring weight and height at first contact; pre-training confidence for this statement was significantly higher (median = 10) than all other statements, so less improvement was expected. Equivalence was not tested.

> "Confidence intervals overlapped for question 3a which explored changes in confidence levels related to measuring weight and height at the first antenatal contact. 60% of the midwives trained reported their knowledge had ‘stayed the same’."
