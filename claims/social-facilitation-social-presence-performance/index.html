Evidence¶

Sascha Schneider 2021¶

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review attributing this finding to Mayer (2014b): even non-interactive materials raised perceived social presence and learning performance. The article reports no effect size and no sample details for this cited result.

"It was shown that even non-interactive learning materials like videos or audio messages increase the perception of social presence (Mayer 2014b), thereby increasing learning performance when social cues are included."

Related Claims¶

  • Human embodiment in video: perceived social presence benefits learning ratings, but instructor-face inclusion shows no significant learning-performance difference, and learners prefer human over robot presenters with mixed recall — related
  • The authors argue instructor presence is unlikely to improve learning outcomes unless the instructor's visible behavior provides information learners can directly use for the task — related
  • A 2024 vendor white paper cites research that avatars provide social presence and that realistic avatars in intelligent tutoring systems can increase student-tutor engagement — related
  • Oral non-interactive teaching can enhance learning more than written teaching for complex materials — related
  • Minimal human-like cues suffice to trigger automatic social reactions to embodied agents, though the role of visual social cues remains contested — related
  • Manipulating anthropomorphism shows no clear causal effect on trust in controlled post-generative-AI studies, though long-term use and cue type condition its effects — related
  • Prior knowledge moderates non-interactive teaching: low conceptual knowledge learners profit more, but very low-knowledge learners may not benefit at all — related
  • The low- and high-coercion versions of NTool did not differ on perceived sociability or social presence — related
  • Learners feel a stronger social connection with positive instructors than with negative instructors. — related
