## Evidence

### Qing X. Ryan 2015

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of exam solutions at ACER element C1 (writing complex field expressions) for an oblique-incidence problem. The article reports "Half of the solutions (18 out of 36) did not even consider ⃗k·⃗ r" and only 4 correct; no effect size printed.

> "In the case of oblique incidence, the dot product becomes kxx +kyy +kzz. Half of the solutions (18 out of 36) did not even consider ⃗k·⃗ r. Out of those who considered the dot product, only 4 worked it out correctly."

### Qing X. Ryan 2015 (2)

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of normal-incidence exam solutions (N=128 solutions, two semesters) at element C1. The article reports 18% (23/128) missed the negative sign for k_refl and 13% (16/128) did not differentiate k_refl and k_trans; no effect size printed.

> "almost a ﬁfth (18%, 23 out of 128) of the solutions missed the negative sign forkref l, and more than a tenth (13%, 16 out of 128) of the solutions did’t diﬀerentiate between kref l and ktrans."
