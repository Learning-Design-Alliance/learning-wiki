## Evidence

### Barzagar Nazari and Ebersbach 2019

Barzagar Nazari, K., & Ebersbach, M. (2019). Distributing mathematical practice of third and seventh graders: Applicability of the spacing effect in the classroom. *Applied Cognitive Psychology, 33*(2), 288–298. [doi:10.1002/acp.3485](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3485)

`q2 · classroom comparison of two practice schedules; how students were assigned is not stated in the abstract` · `i? · Bayesian analyses; the abstract prints no effect size` · `n=213 third and seventh graders` · `causal · r?`

Third and seventh graders (N = 213) in school first received an introduction to a mathematics topic taken from their curriculum, then practised in one of two conditions: massed (three practice sets in one day) or [distributed](../elements/spaced-learning.md) (one set a day on three consecutive days). Follow-up tests came one and six weeks after the last practice set. The authors conclude that distributed practice is a powerful learning tool for both elementary and secondary school students in the classroom. This entry was written from the published abstract: the full text (open access under CC BY) could not be retrieved for this ingest, so group sizes, assignment, the topics, effect sizes and the authors' stated limitations are not recorded here.

## Discussion

The spacing here is short (one day between sets) and the test delays are one and six weeks. The weaker and shorter-lived benefit for third graders is reported by the authors; the abstract does not say why.
