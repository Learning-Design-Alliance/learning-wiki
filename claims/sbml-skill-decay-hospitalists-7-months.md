## Evidence

### Hale Caleb 2021

Hale Caleb, Crocker Jonathan, Vanka Anita, Ricotta Daniel N, McSparron Jakob I, Huang Grace C. (2021). Cohort study of hospitalists' procedural skills: baseline competence and durability after simulation-based training. BMJ Open. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8370503/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Delayed reassessment of 10 hospitalists who could be scheduled, a mean of 7 months post-training; videotaped simulator performances scored by two blinded trained raters. Only descriptive percentages and means are printed.

> "The number of hospitalists achieving passing scores was 40% and 40% for LP and AP, respectively. They attained average scores of 16 and 20 for LP and AP, respectively."

### Hale Caleb 2021 (2)

Hale Caleb, Crocker Jonathan, Vanka Anita, Ricotta Daniel N, McSparron Jakob I, Huang Grace C. (2021). Cohort study of hospitalists' procedural skills: baseline competence and durability after simulation-based training. BMJ Open. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8370503/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Wilcoxon rank sum tests compared mean checklist scores at baseline and delayed reassessment and χ2 or Fisher's exact tests compared proportions meeting the MPS; the printed p-values were not significant, so the delayed scores did not statistically differ from baseline.

> "Percent passing, pre vs delayed post 0.193 0.698  Scores, pre vs delayed post 0.472 0.268"
