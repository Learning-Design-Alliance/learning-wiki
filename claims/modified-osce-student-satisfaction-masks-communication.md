## Evidence

### O'Malley A 2025

O'Malley A, Fitzgerald N, Moylett E, Gaffney G, McCarthy G, Murphy A.W., Geoghegan R, Hallahan B. (2025). A comparison of objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs) for medical students, modified during the COVID-19 pandemic. Irish Journal of Medical Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11845-025-03976-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A previously validated anonymous 7-item questionnaire analysed with the Mann–Whitney U test; respondents were 92 students (45.5%) in 2020 and 63 respondents (30.7% of those surveyed) in 2021. All item comparisons were non-significant (e.g. safety U=3185.0, p=0.84); no effect sizes are printed.

> "A majority of respondents at both OSCE sittings stated that they felt that the examination was safe (2020 = 89.1%, 2021 = 96.8%), well coordinated (2020 = 88.8%, 2021 = 88.9%)"

### O'Malley A 2025 (2)

O'Malley A, Fitzgerald N, Moylett E, Gaffney G, McCarthy G, Murphy A.W., Geoghegan R, Hallahan B. (2025). A comparison of objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs) for medical students, modified during the COVID-19 pandemic. Irish Journal of Medical Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11845-025-03976-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Questionnaire item Q7 from the same anonymous survey of modified OSCE respondents in 2020 and 2021, comparing agreement that masks impaired patient communication; the Mann–Whitney test showed no significant difference (U=2912.5, p=0.96).

> "A similar percentage of participants at both OSCEs felt that face masks impacted their ability to communicate with patients (2020 = 47.8%, 2021 = 52.4%)."
