## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teachers perceive that misconceptions in foundational concepts persist into later grades and that misconceptions held by prospective teachers may be transferred to their future students. [→ Mutmainna 2025](#mutmainna-2025)

## Evidence

### Mutmainna 2025

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding (RQ1, impact category): participant Dani explained physics concepts are continuous, so an early misconception in Newton's laws affects understanding in grades 11 and 12; Budi added a flawed foundation means the structure built on it "may collapse".

> "if the concept of Newton’s laws is misunderstood early on, the misconception will persist through grade 11 and 12, affecting later understanding."

### Mutmainna 2025 (2)

Mutmainna, Istiyono, E., Haryanto, Retnawati, & Setiawan, C. (2025). Physics Teachers' Perceptions about Diagnostic Assessment of Students' Physics Misconceptions: A Phenomenological Study. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2501.10422)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same interview study: teachers also perceived impact on prospective teachers, who may propagate incorrect concepts and produce flawed assessments; the authors relate this to the teacher as a source of misconceptions (Suprapto, 2020).

> "Regarding the impact of misconceptions on prospective Physics teachers, Budi noted "...and if they become Physics teachers, it could be dangerous because they might teach incorrect concepts""
