# Bilingual children score higher in English than Spanish, with the language effect larger in the expressive modality

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A significant language-by-modality interaction reflected a greater effect of language (English > Spanish) in the expressive than the receptive modality. [→ Gross Megan 2014](#gross-megan-2014)

## Evidence

### Gross Megan 2014

Gross Megan, Buac Milijana, Kaushanskaya Margarita. (2014). Conceptual scoring of receptive and expressive vocabulary measures in simultaneous and sequential bilingual children. American Journal of Speech-Language Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1044/2014_AJSLP-13-0026

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Repeated-measures ANCOVA on the two bilingual groups with SES covariate; the language-by-modality interaction (ηp2 = .15) showed the English advantage concentrated in expressive vocabulary, consistent with reduced expressive skills in the minority language.

> "A significant interaction between language and modality, F (1, 47) = 8.58, p = .005, ηp2 = .15, reflected a greater effect of language in the expressive modality (English Madjusted marginal = 93.4, SE = 1.6 vs. Spanish Madjusted marginal = 88.5, SE = 2.3) than in the receptive modality (English Madjusted marginal = 101.6, SE = 2.2 vs. Spanish Madjusted marginal = 102.2, SE = 2.3)."
