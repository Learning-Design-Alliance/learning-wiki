## Evidence

### Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018

Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal. (2018). Challenges Arising from Prerequisite Testing in Cybersecurity Games. Proceedings of SIGCSE '18: The 49th ACM Technical Symposium on Computing Science Education. https://doi.org/10.1145/3159450.3159454

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of G1 data reported with the descriptive statistics and Figure 2 boxplots. Significant Pearson and Spearman correlations, "ranging from 0 .36 to 0 .60" (r = 0.36 to 0.60), linked the pretest (both scoring methods) with game score and with levels completed.

> "statistically significant ( p≤ 0.02) Pearson and Spearman correlations (ranging from 0 .36 to 0 .60) were reported between the score and pretest and between levels completed and pretest (both scoring methods in both cases)"

### Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018 (2)

Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal. (2018). Challenges Arising from Prerequisite Testing in Cybersecurity Games. Proceedings of SIGCSE '18: The 49th ACM Technical Symposium on Computing Science Education. https://doi.org/10.1145/3159450.3159454

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of G1 data with post-game perceived difficulty (rated 1 trivial to 5 impossible; 46 participants after a technical error). The article reports that all performance predictors and skill descriptors "negatively correlate with how diﬃcult the game is perceived", with coefficients r = −0.37 to −0.52.

> "there was strong evidence that all performance predictors and skill descriptors negatively correlate with how diﬃcult the game is perceived (p≤ 0.02, coeﬃcients ranging from−0.37 to−0.52)"
