## Evidence

### Wijnen-Meijer M 2015

Wijnen-Meijer M, ten Cate O, van der Schaaf M, Burgers C, Borleffs J, Harendza S. (2015). Vertically integrated medical education and the readiness for practice of graduates. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0514-z

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Standardized-patient CARE-questionnaire ratings (transformed to a 3-point scale to correct cross-country scale differences) in the UHTRUST assessment favoured German non-VI candidates on "making you feel at ease" (r = .42) and "really listening" (r = .29).

> "The German candidates received significantly higher scores for "making you feel at ease" (M = 2.86; SD = .18; U = 639.50; z = 3.22; p = .00, r = .42) and "really listening" (M = 2.88; SD = .12; U = 573.50; z = 2.21; p = .03, r = .29), in comparison with the Dutch candidates"

### Wijnen-Meijer M 2015 (2)

Wijnen-Meijer M, ten Cate O, van der Schaaf M, Burgers C, Borleffs J, Harendza S. (2015). Vertically integrated medical education and the readiness for practice of graduates. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0514-z

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Same CARE ratings: the German advantage on "really listening" was r = .29 against Dutch means of M = 2.79 (SD = .17) for that aspect. Ratings came from five SPs plus one SP simulating a relative per candidate.

> "and "really listening" (M = 2.88; SD = .12; U = 573.50; z = 2.21; p = .03, r = .29), in comparison with the Dutch candidates (M = 2.69; SD = .21 and M = 2.79; SD = .17 respectively)."

### Wijnen-Meijer M 2015 (3)

Wijnen-Meijer M, ten Cate O, van der Schaaf M, Burgers C, Borleffs J, Harendza S. (2015). Vertically integrated medical education and the readiness for practice of graduates. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0514-z

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

The same CARE ratings showed the Dutch VI candidates higher on "being positive" (r = .32), reversing the direction of the other CARE differences.

> "For the aspect "being positive" the Dutch candidates had significantly higher scores (M = 2.80; SD = .19) than the German candidates (M = 2.58; SD = .36; U = 279; z = -2.43; p = .02. r = .32)."
