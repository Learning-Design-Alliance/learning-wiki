## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Quantum-proficient roles combine domain expertise (e.g., nanofabrication, cryogenics) with working quantum mechanics knowledge, while quantum-expert roles require primarily theoretical knowledge or highly sophisticated hands-on skills with deep theoretical understanding. [→ A.R. Piña 2025](#ar-pina-2025)
`q2 i?` The authors interpret that as expertise level increases, the degree of role specialization increases, contrasting hands-on with computational/theory roles at higher expertise levels. [→ A.R. Piña 2025 (2)](#ar-pina-2025-2)

### A.R. Piña 2025

A.R. Piña, Shams El-Adawy, H. J. Lewandowski, and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Industry Insights into Quantum Knowledge Needed for the Quantum Information Science and Engineering Workforce. arXiv:2508.15039 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.1119/perc.2025.pr.pina](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2025.pr.pina)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative interview analysis of role descriptions at the proficient and expert levels. The article reports proficient roles need "advanced knowledge in a specific technical domain... combined with a working knowledge of quantum mechanics," and expert roles require "primarily theoretical knowledge" or sophisticated hands-on skills with deep theory.

> "Among roles requiring quantum proficiency, some require advanced knowledge in a specific technical domain, such as nanofabrication, cryogenics, or fabrication, combined with a working knowledge of quantum mechanics."
