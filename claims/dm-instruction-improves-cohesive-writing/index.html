Evidence¶

Nader Assadi Aidinlou & Hakimeh Shahrokhi mehr 2012¶

Nader Assadi Aidinlou & Hakimeh Shahrokhi mehr. (2012). The Effect of Discourse Markers Instruction on EFL Learners' Writing. World Journal of Education, Vol. 2, No. 2. http://dx.doi.org/10.5430/wje.v2n2p10

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Classroom-based quasi-experiment with 20 Iranian intermediate EFL learners (10 per group) at Shoukoh Iran English Institute; experimental group received DM treatment sessions, control did not. The article reports "a value of -6.8 with 5 d.f. is significant at the .001 level" on the post-test cloze.

"Through SPSS software, it be concluded a value of -6.8 with 5 d.f. is significant at the .001 level."

Related Claims¶

  • Post-test cloze means were 15.01 (control) versus 18.75 (experimental) after DM instruction — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Learners' overuse and misuse of discourse markers is attributed to first-language translation, and uninstructed learners may overuse DMs believing this makes text more cohesive — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Supplementary exercises targeting outlining, coherence, and unity developed experimental-group students' writing skills to some extent in a controlled pre/post-test experiment — related
  • Teaching the textual metafunction significantly improved Iranian EFL learners' essay coherence and cohesion ratings compared with essay-writing instruction alone — related
  • Drama-based instruction produced statistically significant gains on all five writing aspects: thesis statement, unity, completeness and coherence, wording and cohesion, and organization — related
  • Drama-based instruction improved Jordanian EFL eighth-grade students' overall writing performance more than traditional textbook-based instruction — related
