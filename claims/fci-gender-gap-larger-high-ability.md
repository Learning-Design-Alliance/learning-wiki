# The FCI gender gap in algebra-based courses is more pronounced at higher ability levels based on pretest scores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In both algebra-based LB and EBAE courses, the FCI gender gap appears more pronounced among students in the top 1/3 by pretest score than at lower ability levels. [→ Nafis I. Karim 2018](#nafis-i-karim-2018)

## Evidence

### Nafis I. Karim 2018

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do evidence-based active-engagement courses reduce the gender gap in introductory physics? https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010103

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis (Table VI(a)) combining all algebra-based LB and EBAE courses, with students divided into pretest-score thirds within gender. In the LB course the top 1/3 male students averaged 18% (19%) higher than top 1/3 female students on pretest (posttest).

> "Interestingly, in both type of courses, it appears that the gender gap on the FCI was more pronounced at higher ability levels (based on FCI pretest scores)."
