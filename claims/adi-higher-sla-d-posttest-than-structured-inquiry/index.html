Evidence¶

Gülüzar Eymur · Pınar Seda Çetin 2024¶

Gülüzar Eymur · Pınar Seda Çetin (2024). Investigating the role of an inquiry-based science lab on students' scientific literacy. Instructional Science (2024) 52:743–760. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09672-w

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Quasi-experiment with two intact classes (34 ADI, 33 comparison) taught by the same instructor over ten weeks. The independent t-test gave "t (65) = 3.20, p < .05" and the printed effect size in Table 3 was d = 0.83 (post means 11.5 vs 8.60).

"Moreover, the students in the experimental group scored significantly higher than the students in the comparison group in the post- SLA-D (t (65) = 3.20, p < .05)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The ADI group showed significant within-group gains on demonstrated scientific literacy from pre- to post-test, while the structured-inquiry group did not
  • ADI students' item-level gains concentrated on items about determining variables for a research question and on scientific inquiry questions
  • ADI students scored significantly higher than structured-inquiry students on the personal epistemology of science subscale, but not on value of science or self-efficacy subscales
  • Interviewed ADI students reported engaging in scientific practices such as identifying variables, finding patterns in data, and developing methods, while no comparison-group students did
  • Scientific literacy post-test scores, critiquing performance, and inquiry performance were closely related
  • Critiquing problematic inquiry cases before virtual-lab inquiry augments the effect of virtual labs on middle school students' scientific literacy more than teacher demonstration — related
