## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In the extended-study condition of Experiment 4, bolded items were recalled better (M = 45%) than unbolded items (M = 39%), d = 0.43. [→ Richland 2009](#richland-2009)

## Evidence

### Richland 2009

Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The Pretesting Effect: Do Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Learning? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 15(3), 243–257. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 4 within-subjects comparison in the extended-study condition only: bolded vs. unbolded items. "a smaller difference between bolded items and unbolded," d = 0.43, described as bolding being "an effective typographical tool for directing attention."

> "There was also a smaller difference between bolded items and unbolded when the extended study condition was examined separately, t(78) ⫽ 1.9, p ⬍ .04, d ⫽ 0.43 (M ⫽ 45%, SE ⫽ 2.2, and M ⫽ 39%, SE ⫽ 2.6, respectively), revealing that bolding was an effective typographical tool for directing attention."
