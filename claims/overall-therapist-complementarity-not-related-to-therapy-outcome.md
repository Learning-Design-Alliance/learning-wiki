# Overall therapist complementarity across the full therapeutic relationship does not differ significantly between successful and unsuccessful outcome groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When complementarity was examined across the entire therapeutic relationship, successful and unsuccessful outcome groups showed no significant difference, though successful therapists trended somewhat lower. [→ Dietzel 1974](#dietzel-1974)

## Evidence

### Dietzel 1974

Dietzel, C. S.; Abeles, Norman. (1974). Client-Therapist Complementarity and Therapeutic Outcome. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED091646

`q2 · i?`

Behavioral analysis of 120 rated tape segments from 20 late-adolescent clients (10 successful, 10 unsuccessful outcome by clinician MMPI ratings) compared Complementarity Index values across all sessions. The study reports "no significant differences (t = 1.309, p > .05)" between groups, with only a tendency toward lower complementarity in successful therapists.

> "When the level of therapist complementarity during the entire therapeutic relationship was examined, there were no significant differences (t = 1.309, p > .05) between the successful and unsuccessful outcome groups."
