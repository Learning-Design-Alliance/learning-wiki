## Evidence

### Mooney SS 2022

Mooney SS, Hiscock RJ, Hicks L, Narula S, Maher PJ, Readman E, Pendlebury A, Ellett L. (2022). We live in a virtual world: Training the trainee using an integrated visual reality simulator curriculum. Aust N Z J Obstet Gynaecol. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajo.13521

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Six-month questionnaire and free-text responses from the intervention groups: "rostering issues (60%), rotation to other sites (20%), life commitments (10%), leave (5%) and lack of incentive (5%)" were the reported reasons for inadequate VRS usage.

> "Only one NT and one ET reported a belief that they had spent adequate time using the VRS. The remaining trainees indicated their reasons for inadequate VRS usage: rostering issues (60%), rotation to other sites (20%), life commitments (10%), leave (5%) and lack of incentive (5%)."

### Mooney SS 2022 (2)

Mooney SS, Hiscock RJ, Hicks L, Narula S, Maher PJ, Readman E, Pendlebury A, Ellett L. (2022). We live in a virtual world: Training the trainee using an integrated visual reality simulator curriculum. Aust N Z J Obstet Gynaecol. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajo.13521

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Trainee questionnaire data on training conditions: simulator use was "predominantly after hours", and the minority who trained in-hours mostly lacked protected time and faced frequent interruptions.

> "All trainees reported use of the simulator predominantly after hours and for those who managed in‐hours training, the majority was unprotected with frequent interruptions."
