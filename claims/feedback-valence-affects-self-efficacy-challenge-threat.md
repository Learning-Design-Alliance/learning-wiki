# Feedback valence affects post-task self-efficacy, challenge, and threat: negative feedback lowers self-efficacy and challenge and raises threat (Experiments 1 and 2)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Self-efficacy after problem solving was significantly lower in the negative feedback condition than in the positive feedback condition, with large effects in both experiments. [→ Tamara van Gog 2024](#tamara-van-gog-2024)
`q3 i?` Participants in the negative feedback condition reported higher threat and lower challenge than participants in the positive feedback condition. [→ Tamara van Gog 2024 (2)](#tamara-van-gog-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog 2024

Tamara van Gog, Eva Janssen, Florence Lucas, Maaike Taheij (2024). A Motivational Perspective on (Anticipated) Mental Effort Investment: The Biopsychosocial Model of Challenge and Threat. Educational Psychology Review (2024) 36:54. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09861-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs in the two between-subjects feedback-valence experiments show "a significant difference between the predominantly positive and negative feedback conditions" in post-task self-efficacy, with η²p = 0.37 and η²p = 0.53, described as large effects.

> "As for self-efficacy after problem solving, there was a significant difference between the predominantly positive and negative feedback conditions, both in Experiment 1, F(1, 129) = 75.29, p < 0.001, /u1D7022p = 0.37, and in Experiment 2, F(1, 120) = 136.68, p < 0.001, /u1D7022p = 0.53, indicating large effects."

### Tamara van Gog 2024 (2)

Tamara van Gog, Eva Janssen, Florence Lucas, Maaike Taheij (2024). A Motivational Perspective on (Anticipated) Mental Effort Investment: The Biopsychosocial Model of Challenge and Threat. Educational Psychology Review (2024) 36:54. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09861-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In both experiments, ANOVAs showed challenge and threat differed significantly between the randomly assigned feedback conditions; the article reports the direction of the differences for threat and challenge as printed.

> "participants in the negative feedback condition reported significantly higher levels of threat and significantly lower levels of challenge compared to participants in the positive feedback condition"
