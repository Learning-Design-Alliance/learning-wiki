# Publication bias analysis suggests the overall intelligence–DT correlation is slightly underestimated (r = .27 after trim-and-fill)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Funnel-plot asymmetry was confirmed, and trim-and-fill imputation of 169 missing coefficients yielded a corrected correlation of r = .27, slightly above the uncorrected r = .25. [→ Gerwig Anne 2021](#gerwig-anne-2021)

## Evidence

### Gerwig Anne 2021

Gerwig Anne, Miroshnik Kirill, Forthmann Boris, Benedek Mathias, Karwowski Maciej, Holling Heinz. (2021). The Relationship between Intelligence and Divergent Thinking—A Meta-Analytic Update. Journal of Intelligence. https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence9020023

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Publication-bias assessment of the 849 coefficients using a funnel plot, trim-and-fill imputation, and an Egger-type test (z = −2.37, p = .018). The corrected estimate was "r = .27, 95% CI [.25, .29]"; imputed values fell in the region of significant effect sizes, so the asymmetry mechanism was not classical publication bias.

> "When taking the 169 imputed effect sizes into account, a significant correlation of r = .27, 95% CI [.25, .29], p < .001, appears, indicating that the true correlation was only slightly underestimated because of publication bias (recall that the overall estimate reported above was found to be r = .25)."
