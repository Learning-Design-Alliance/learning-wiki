Evidence¶

Peña 2024¶

Peña, M., Vásquez-Venegas, C., Cortés, P., Pittaluga, E., Herrera, M., Pino, E. J., Escobar, R. G., Dehaene-Lambertz, G., & Guevara, P. (2024). A brief tablet-based intervention benefits linguistic and communicative abilities in toddlers and preschoolers. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00249-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Pre-post equivalent-group experiment with 101 toddlers (~24 months); Study group (n=53 for ASQ-3) showed greater Communication gain than Control (n=39), Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney Z = -2.42, P = 0.015, effect size r = -0.25. "the Study group showed greater gain than the Control group in the raw data of the Communication domain"

"When comparing the groups, the Study group showed greater gain than the Control group in the raw data of the Communication domain only (Table 1 and Fig. 2a)."

Peña 2024 (2)¶

Peña, M., Vásquez-Venegas, C., Cortés, P., Pittaluga, E., Herrera, M., Pino, E. J., Escobar, R. G., Dehaene-Lambertz, G., & Guevara, P. (2024). A brief tablet-based intervention benefits linguistic and communicative abilities in toddlers and preschoolers. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00249-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Category-change analysis in the same toddler experiment: in the Study group "15 toddlers improved, none dropped, and 38 remained at the same level" versus 4 improved, 2 dropped, 28 unchanged among Controls; Fisher exact probability = 0.026.

"In contrast, in the Study group, 15 toddlers improved, none dropped, and 38 remained at the same level (Fisher exact probability = 0.026)."

Peña 2024 (3)¶

Peña, M., Vásquez-Venegas, C., Cortés, P., Pittaluga, E., Herrera, M., Pino, E. J., Escobar, R. G., Dehaene-Lambertz, G., & Guevara, P. (2024). A brief tablet-based intervention benefits linguistic and communicative abilities in toddlers and preschoolers. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00249-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

CDI gain comparison in the toddler experiment: Study group (n=52) showed greater gain in the Adverbs and Verbs subset than Control (n=45), t-test P = 0.030, Cohen d = -0.45. "the Study group showed greater gains, particularly in the Adverbs and Verbs subset"

"When comparing the groups, however, the Study group showed greater gains, particularly in the Adverbs and Verbs subset (Table1 and Fig. 2b), unveiling a subtle yet significant advantage in favor of the Study group."
