## Evidence

### Tiraboschi Gabriel Arantes 2025

Tiraboschi Gabriel Arantes, Garon-Carrier Gabrielle, Madigan Sheri, Smith Jonathan, Surprenant Rachel, Fitzpatrick Caroline. (2025). Longitudinal associations between gaming and academic motivation during middle childhood. Psychological Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291725101153

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics and Welch's t-tests (Table 1) from the QLSCD sample across ages 7, 8, and 10. Gaming also increased (mean difference = .214 h/day) and motivation decreased (mean difference = −.287) from ages 7 to 10 for both sexes; no standardized effect sizes were printed.

> "At all time points, boys spent significantly more time playing video games than girls (mean difference = .413 h/day, 95% confidence interval [CI] = .347 to .479), and girls had significantly higher academic motivation compared to boys (mean difference = .398, 95% CI = .242 to .553)."
