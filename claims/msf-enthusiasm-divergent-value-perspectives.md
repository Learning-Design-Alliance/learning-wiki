## Evidence

### Yama Brie A. 2018

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the Results' initial impressions, all focus groups first identified everyone as potential feedback providers when introduced to the MSF concept; "Enthusiasm was expressed, with residents anticipating professional growth" and AHCPs predicting better teamwork. Deeper discussion then surfaced barriers.

> "Enthusiasm was expressed, with residents anticipating professional growth and AHCPs predicting improved interdisciplinary teamwork as potential outcomes of the process."

### Yama Brie A. 2018 (2)

Yama Brie A., Hodgins Michael, Boydell Katherine, Schwartz Sarah B. (2018). A qualitative exploration: questioning multisource feedback in residency education. BMC Medical Education 18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1270-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In concluding impressions, residents hesitated to accept MSF, questioning its value, while AHCPs agreed on its importance because providing feedback signals that their opinions matter; both groups valued group feedback for team function.

> "These divergent perspectives regarding the value of MSF represent significant challenges. Despite this apparent division, each group identified the value of providing group feedback with respect to team function."
