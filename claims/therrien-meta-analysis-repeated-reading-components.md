## Evidence

### Still 2012

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article reports, citing Therrien (2004), a meta-analysis of 18 repeated reading articles; the review attributes "a moderate mean increase in fluency" and a somewhat smaller comprehension increase to that analysis. Reported second-hand; no effect sizes printed here.

> "The results of this analysis showed that repeated reading improves the reading fluency and comprehension of nondisabled students and students with a learning disability."

### Still 2012 (2)

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article reports, citing Therrien (2004), that adult implementation beat peer implementation and that cueing for speed and comprehension, three to four readings, corrective feedback, and performance criterion were important components. Second-hand report; no effect sizes printed.

> "The results of the important components showed that adult implementation was higher in both areas than when peers implemented the repeated reading program."
