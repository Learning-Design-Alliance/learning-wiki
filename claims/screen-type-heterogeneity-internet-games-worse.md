# Internet/game time is more detrimental to mathematics, English and overall performance than television time, showing heterogeneity by screen exposure type

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both television time and internet/game time were significantly negatively correlated with performance (p < 0.01), and Z-tests showed significant coefficient differences for mathematics (Z = 1.945, p = 0.026), English (Z = 2.400, p = 0.008) and overall performance (Z = 1.993, p = 0.023), but not Chinese (Z = 0.354, p = 0.363). ## Evidence

### Feng Xiaosu 2025

Feng Xiaosu, Ren Shuanquan, Shi Peng. (2025). The relationship and mechanism of screen time and academic performance among adolescents: an empirical study based on CEPS. Frontiers in Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1533327

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Regression models comparing television time and internet/game time in the CEPS sample, with Z-tests of coefficient differences, found "heterogeneity in the type of screen exposure"; internet/game coefficients were more negative for mathematics, English and overall scores, with no significant difference for Chinese.

> "there was heterogeneity in the type of screen exposure in the relationship between screen time and adolescents’ academic performance, with time spent surfing the internet and playing games having more detrimental effects on adolescents’ Mathematics (β = −0.063), English (β = −0.047), and Overall performance (β = −0.148)."
