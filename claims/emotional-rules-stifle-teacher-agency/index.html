Related Claims¶

  • The role of affect in STEM teacher learning remains seriously underexplored despite growing research on teacher emotion
  • Strict emotional regimes contribute to the production of vulnerability that can precipitate deficit narratives from asset-oriented teachers
  • Contextual resources shape teachers' agentic responses in challenging situations, which in turn shape their interpretations of student behavior and possibilities for responsiveness
  • Epistemic empathy, an affective phenomenon, dovetails with science teachers' learning of responsive teaching — related
  • Consultation with trusted colleagues offered new interpretations of a student's behavior, lessening a teacher's emotional labor and shifting her from surface acting to deep acting (Linda case) — related
  • LLMs redistributed educational labor while family and institutional rules left parents and professionals responsible for interpreting outputs and deciding how they entered practice — related
  • In Case 2, persistent strong negative self-critique led teachers to assign deficit narratives to students as having missed opportunities, serving as foils for teachers — related
  • Organized PD groups reported fewer negative emotions than teachers learning informally — related
