## Evidence

### Matthew Dew 2021

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

T-tests on transformed data pooled across 10 instructors and 27 lecture sections of calculus-based E&M (Table IV) found no significant exam differences; equivalence was not tested.

> "No signiﬁcant diﬀerences were observed between male and female students on any exam for this course."

### Matthew Dew 2021 (2)

Matthew Dew, Jonathan Perry, Lewis Ford, William Bassichis, and Tatiana Erukhimova. (2021). Gendered Performance Diﬀerences in Introductory Physics: A Study from a Large Land-Grant University. arXiv:2009.09536. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010106)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pooled t-test on final letter grades for calculus-based E&M reported no significant difference (Table II: p = 0.334); the study did not test equivalence.

> "As seen in Table II, there was no signiﬁcant diﬀerence observed when a t-test was applied to ﬁnal letter grades based on student gender for the pooled data from all instructors and sections."
