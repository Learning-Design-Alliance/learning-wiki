## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Validation and analysis of the coupled multiple response Colorado upper-division electrostatics (CUE) diagnostic. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item-difficulty analysis of average scores by question (Fig. 3, N=421). The authors extend reasoning from dichotomous items (acceptable range 30-90%) to argue no single item is too easy or too hard to contribute to discrimination.

> "Item diﬃculties for all questions fall between 30-75%. We are not aware of a well-established range of acceptable values for item diﬃculty on polytomously scored items."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and Steven J. Pollock. (2015). Validation and analysis of the coupled multiple response Colorado upper-division electrostatics (CUE) diagnostic. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item discrimination analysis comparing each item score to the rest of the test (Pearson correlations, Fig. 4). The authors adopt the standard cutoff of r = 0.2 used for dichotomously scored items to argue all items demonstrate acceptable discriminatory power.

> "Item-test correlations for all 16 items are shown in Fig. 4, and all correlation coeﬃcients fall between 0.28-0.55 and are statistically signiﬁcant given N=421 [20]."
