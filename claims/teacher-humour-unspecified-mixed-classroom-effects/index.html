Evidence¶

Hannah Robinson 2024¶

Hannah Robinson, Sarah E. Rose, Jade M. Elliott, Romina A. Vivaldi. (2024). Teachers' Humour Use in the Classroom: A Scoping Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09913-x

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of classroom-observation studies in the review (theme five), reporting observed positive effects on learning processes; the review notes these studies "did not measure actual student learning or improvement in students' academic achievement".

"Studies have reported positive effects of teachers' humour use on students' learning by improving teachers' explanations (Miura & Jones, 2005), students' attention (DiCamillo, 2010; Horng et al., 2005), and critical thinking (Ryoo, 2019)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Affiliative humour is associated with positive classroom climate, enjoyment and willingness to participate among adolescent students
  • Aggressive humour has mixed effects on adolescent students' educational experiences, with reported increases in anger, boredom and resistance but some students reporting sarcasm improved rapport
  • Observed teacher humour differs from self-reports: sarcasm and mocking accounted for nearly two-thirds of observed teacher humour although teachers and students reported aggressive humour as least common
