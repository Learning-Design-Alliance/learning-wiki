### Kerr Brendan 2013

Feasibility of scenario-based simulation training versus traditional workshops in continuing medical education: a randomized controlled trial. `q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical argument in the discussion, citing the expertise reversal effect literature: strategies "beneficial for novice learners may not always be beneficial for expert learners", so simulation's value in CME requires direct empirical testing. The article also notes only 6% of studies in a cited simulation review involved physicians in practice.

> "Because instructional strategies that are beneficial for novice learners may not always be beneficial for expert learners (expertise reversal effect) (22–24), it is important that the effectiveness for the use of simulation in the CME setting be established empirically rather than inferred from non-CME studies."
