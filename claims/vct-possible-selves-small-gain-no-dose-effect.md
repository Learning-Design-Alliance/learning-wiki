## Evidence

### Bowling 2017

Bowling, K. G., Miller, L. M., Patel, R., & Bass, K. M. (2017). Impacts of Virtual Clinical Trials Simulations on Science Knowledge and Attitudes. Electronic Journal of Science Education, 21(7). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1187990

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA after consolidating arms one and two versus arm three (N=525). The article states students exposed to any simulation slightly increased perceptions of possible selves, but two simulations did not confer additional benefits; the dose interaction was non-significant (F(1,523) = 0.16, p = .899).

> "The analyses did, however, detect a small but significant main effect of time for the possible selves variable across the two simul ation conditions (F(1, 523) = 13.01, p < .000, effect size (partial eta squared) = .024)."
