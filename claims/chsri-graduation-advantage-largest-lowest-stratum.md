# The CHSRI graduation advantage was concentrated among students least likely to graduate, exceeding six percentage points in the lowest stratum versus about two in the highest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the 2004 cohort, CHSRI students were more likely to graduate than similar non-CHSRI peers across all five graduation-probability strata, but the advantage was largest for the lowest stratum (slightly more than six percentage points) and smallest for the highest (about two percentage points). [→ Susan Sporte 2010](#susan-sporte-2010)

## Evidence

### Susan Sporte 2010

Susan Sporte, Marisa de la Torre. (2010). Chicago High School Redesign Initiative: Schools, Students, and Outcomes. Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago, Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/chicago-high-school-redesign-initiative-schools-students-and-outcomes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Stratified analysis of the 2004 cohort: students were grouped into five equal strata by pre-high school probability of graduating, then CHSRI and non-CHSRI graduation rates compared within strata. CHSRI students graduated at higher rates in every stratum. No effect size printed.

> "Among the lowest quintile (Stratum 1), the difference in graduation between CHSRI and non-CHSRI students was slightly more than six percentage points; the difference in the fifth quintile (Stratum 5) was about two percentage points"
