# People can selectively remove no-longer-relevant information from WM, but removal of an arbitrary subset of items is inefficient

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Removing an entire memory set is rapid and seamless, but people find it difficult to remove a random subset of several items from a memory set unless the subset was clearly marked at encoding. [→ Oberauer 2019](#oberauer-2019)

## Evidence

### Oberauer 2019

Oberauer, K. (2019). Working Memory and Attention – A Conceptual Analysis and Review. Journal of Cognition, 2(1): 36. https://doi.org/10.5334/joc.58

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of removal evidence from updating paradigms and forget-cue experiments. Successful removal of a subset of three words from a six-word list occurred only when the subset was clearly marked at encoding (Oberauer, 2018). The review states people "find it difficult to remove a random subset" and that removal efficiency is limited by ability to discriminate to-be-maintained from to-be-removed contents.
