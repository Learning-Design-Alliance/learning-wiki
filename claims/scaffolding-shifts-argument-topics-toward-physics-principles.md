# Semesters with explicit argumentation scaffolds show essay topics concentrated around physics-principle themes, while minimally scaffolded semesters spread evenly across conceptual, procedural, and surface-feature topics

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In minimally scaffolded semesters (Fall 2022, Spring 2023) student arguments were distributed fairly evenly across topics, with substantial focus on mathematical steps and surface features. [→ Winter Allen 2025](#winter-allen-2025)
`q2 i?` In heavily scaffolded semesters (Fall 2023, Spring 2024) topic distributions showed large peaks at topics focused primarily on energy principles. [→ Winter Allen 2025 (2)](#winter-allen-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Winter Allen 2025

Winter Allen, Carina M. Rebello, N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Assessing Physics Students' Scientific Argumentation using Natural Language Processing. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.08910](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.08910)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

NMF topic modeling of Fall 2022 student argumentation essays (minimal scaffolding) showed fairly even topic distribution; representative words included "energy", "kinetic", and "force" for principle topics but "solution", "used", "equation" and "find" for mathematical-step topics.

> "We found the topics to be fairly evenly distributed with minor peaks at Topics 0, 4, and 8. Our overall takeaway from these representative words is that there was some focus on explicit physics principles"

### Winter Allen 2025 (2)

Winter Allen, Carina M. Rebello, N. Sanjay Rebello. (2025). Assessing Physics Students' Scientific Argumentation using Natural Language Processing. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.08910](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.08910)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

NMF topic modeling of Fall 2023 essays (extensive scaffolding) showed a large peak at Topic 1, which the authors tagged as focusing primarily on energy; the most representative essay argued via conservation of energy. Spring 2024 showed a similar peak at an energy-focused Topic 2.

> "We found the topics to be not as evenly distributed with a large peak emerging at Topic 1. Our overall takeaway from these representative words is that there was some focus on the energy physics principle"
