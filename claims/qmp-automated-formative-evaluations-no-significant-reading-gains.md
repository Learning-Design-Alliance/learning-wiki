# Automated formative evaluations via QMP produced no statistically significant reading comprehension improvement compared to printed materials

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There were no statistically significant differences between the mean grade differences (post-test minus pre-test) of the experimental group using QMP online modules and the control group using printed materials. [→ Lazzeri 2015](#lazzeri-2015)

## Evidence

### Lazzeri 2015

Lazzeri, S., Cabezas, X., Ojeda, L., & Leiva, F. (2015). Automated formative evaluations for reading comprehension in an English as a foreign language course: benefits on performance, user satisfaction, and monitoring of higher education students in Chile. In F. Helm, L. Bradley, M. Guarda, & S. Thouësny (Eds), Critical CALL – Proceedings of the 2015 EUROCALL Conference, Padova, Italy (pp. 355-361). Dublin: Research-publishing.net. http://dx.doi.org/10.14705/rpnet.2015.000358

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-test/post-test randomized controlled study with 57 freshmen kinesiology students at UACh randomly divided into G1-experimental (11 online reading comprehension modules with automated immediate feedback) and G2-control (printed materials). Comparison used Student's t-test for independent samples on grade differences (1-7 scale). The study reports no statistically significant difference between groups.

> "The results show that there were no statistically significant differences between the mean grade differences (post-test grade - pre-test grade) of G1 and G2."
