## Evidence

### Afzali Monika 2017

Afzali Monika, Kvisselgaard Ask Daffy, Lyngeraa Tobias Stenbjerg, Viggers Sandra. (2017). Intraosseous access can be taught to medical students using the four-step approach. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0882-7

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pilot study: 19 students attended a four-hour cadaver course (30-min lecture plus workshop); 15 sat the OSCE on a cadaver case. "This resulted in 60% (n = 9) of the participants obtaining the highest attainable points with all three raters." No effect size is printed.

> "The essentials of the IO access procedure can be taught to medical students as part of a four-hour human cadaver course using a modified version of the Walker and Peyton's four-step approach. A modified version of a previously validated checklist to assess the performances of 15 medical students in gaining IO access was used. This resulted in 60% (n = 9) of the participants obtaining the highest attainable points with all three raters."
