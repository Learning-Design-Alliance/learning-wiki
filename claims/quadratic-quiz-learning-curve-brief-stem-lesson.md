## Evidence

### Cetron 2026

Cetron, J.S., Hillis, M.E., Diamond, S.G., May, V.V. & Kraemer, D.J.M. (2026). Neural patterns reflect conceptual grasp of novice students following first class learning in physics. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00394-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Results analysis of two written fact-retrieval quizzes (linear forces and moments) administered at four timepoints to 50 novices, using a mixed-effects polynomial regression with random intercepts by participant ID. The article reports "a significant positive linear effect" and a "significant negative quadratic effect" as printed; no effect size is reported, so no magnitude is asserted.

> "On average, there was a significant positive linear effect of experimental timepoint (𝛽 = 0.36, SE = 0.13, p = 0.006), coupled with a significant negative quadratic effect (𝛽 = -0.82, SE = 0.12, p = 1.08x10-10)."
