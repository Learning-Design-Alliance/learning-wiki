# Among institutions that increased Black enrollment from 2000 to 2020, more than half (56%) increased it more than their state-based demographic benchmark rose, while at 65% of declining institutions enrollment fell even as the Black resident population increased.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among the 74% of institutions that increased Black enrollment, 56% increased it more than the increase in the Black resident share in their recruiting states, 24% increased it less, and 20% increased it while the Black resident share decreased. [→ Gabriel Montague 2023](#gabriel-montague-2023)
`q2 i?` Among the 26% of institutions that decreased Black enrollment, about 65% saw enrollment decrease while the Black resident population ages 18-24 increased in their recruiting states. [→ Gabriel Montague 2023 (2)](#gabriel-montague-2023-2)

### Gabriel Montague 2023

Gabriel Montague, Ed.M., Higher Education Research Analyst. (2023). 'Segregation Forever'?: The Continued Underrepresentation of Black Undergraduates at the Nation's 122 Most Selective Private Colleges and Universities. The Education Trust, October 2023. https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/the.education.trust/viz/SegregationForeverPrivatesGraphicsAppendices/FullDashboard

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ed Trust comparison of institutional enrollment changes against state demographic benchmarks (Figure 5), using IPEDS and Census 2000/2020 data. The report reports 56% outpaced the benchmark increase, 24% lagged it, and 20% grew while state shares fell.

> "more than half (56%) increased the percentage of Black students more than the increase in the percentage of Black residents ages 18-24 in the states from which first-time students came."

### Gabriel Montague 2023 (2)

Gabriel Montague, Ed.M., Higher Education Research Analyst. (2023). 'Segregation Forever'?: The Continued Underrepresentation of Black Undergraduates at the Nation's 122 Most Selective Private Colleges and Universities. The Education Trust, October 2023. https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/the.education.trust/viz/SegregationForeverPrivatesGraphicsAppendices/FullDashboard

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same benchmark-comparison analysis for the 32 declining institutions: 23% declined faster than the state share fell, and 13% declined less than the state share fell. The report concludes many institutions missed or moved away from relatively steady benchmarks.

> "at about 65% of these institutions, Black undergraduate student enrollment decreased while the population of Black residents ages 18-24 increased in the states from which first time students came."
