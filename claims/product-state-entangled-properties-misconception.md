## Evidence

### Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach 2015

Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach. (2015). Investigating student understanding of quantum entanglement. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

2015 introductory post-test responses coded for choice and reasoning; the article reports "23 students (29%)" incorrectly disagreed with statement II, with 11 (14%) giving reasoning that product states can show perfect correlations along X and Z.

> "Also, 23 students (29%) in the 2015 post-test incorrectly disagre ed with statement II of question 2 (Fig. 3).  Of these students, 11 (14%) used reason ing similar to “[statement] II is not correct because product states can exist where there is perfect anticorrelation or correlation along X and Z .”"

### Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach 2015 (2)

Antje Kohnle and Erica Deffebach. (2015). Investigating student understanding of quantum entanglement. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.038)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

2015 activity responses to the revised simulation, where students were asked to rewrite a Z-basis state in the X basis and asked whether a product state along Z is also a product state along X; the article reports 74% and 78% correct.

> "These two questions were amongst the most poorly answered, with 74% and 78% correct respectively."
