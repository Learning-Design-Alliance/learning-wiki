## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schools where the highest physics/astronomy degree is an MS have about 40% Pell-grant undergraduates versus roughly 20% at Ph.D.-granting institutions, with medians of 39% and 24%. [→ Michael B. Lund 2019](#michael-b-lund-2019)

## Evidence

### Michael B. Lund 2019

Michael B. Lund, Savannah R. Jacklin, and David Ciardi. (2019). Enabling Terminal Master's Degrees as a Step Towards a Ph.D. Astro2020 White Paper. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1909.02103)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis combining AIP department rosters (190 Ph.D.-highest and 55 MS-highest schools) with Department of Education College Scorecard data for the 2016 undergraduate body; Figure 1 shows median Pell fractions of 24% (Ph.D.) and 39% (MS).

> "The average school that only offers a master's degree has about 40% of its undergraduate students meet the necessary criteria to receive a Pell grant, whereas only ∼ %20 students at Ph.D. granting institutions meet the same criteria, suggesting a notable difference in the fraction of the student body that is lower-income."
