# Subjective competence in brain death diagnosis increased significantly after the VR simulation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students' self-rated competence rose from 2.21 (±0.85) before to 2.98 (±0.97) after the VR simulation, a significant increase. [→ Junga A 2025](#junga-a-2025)

## Evidence

### Junga A 2025

Junga A, Kockwelp P, Valkov D, Schulze H, Bozdere P, Hätscher O, Ahrens H, Marschall B, Risse B, Holling M. (2025). Teach the Unteachable with a Virtual Reality (VR) Brain Death Scenario – 800 Students and 3 Years of Experience. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1427

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Pre/post survey in the most recent course run (cohort 6), analysed with a paired samples t-test. Mean subjective competence was "2.21 (±0.85)" at t0 and 2.98 (±0.97; n = 91) at t1, t(90) = –6,69, p < 0,001.

> "The mean score for subjective competence was (M ± SD) 2.21 (±0.85) [scale 1–5]. After the brain death simulation had been carried out (t1), the students were surveyed again. The score was significantly better at 2.98 (±0.97; n = 91), t(90) = –6,69, p < 0,001."
