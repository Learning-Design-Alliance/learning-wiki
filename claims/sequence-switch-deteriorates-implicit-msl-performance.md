## Evidence

### Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group repeated-measures comparisons in experiment 1 (bimanual implicit SRTT). Performance "was significantly reduced compared to the end of session 3" for New, Compatible, and Incompatible groups, while the Same group did not change (p = 1.). No effect size printed.

> "When the new, compatible, or incompatible sequence was introduced at the start of session 4, the performance was signiﬁcantly reduced compared to the end of session 3 (p = 0.002, p < 0.001, p = 0.001, for the New, Compatible, and Incompatible group, respectively), while the performance in the Same group did not change between the end of session 3 and the start of session 4 (p =1 . ) ."

### Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025 (2)

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc comparisons at the start of session 4 in experiment 1: "The performance in the Same group was significantly higher compared to all other groups (allp < 0.001)", while Compatible and Incompatible did not differ (p = 1.). No effect size printed.

> "The performance in the Same group was signiﬁcantly higher compared to all other groups (allp < 0.001). Only the Compatible and Incompatible groups showed no difference in performance (p = 1.)."

### Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes 2025 (3)

Susanne Dyck & Christian Klaes. (2025). Faster implicit motor sequence learning of new sequences compatible in terms of movement transitions. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00296-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post hoc comparisons at the start of session 2 in experiment 2: "a significant difference in Δ compound values between the Same and all other experimental groups (all p ≤ 0.001)". No effect size printed.

> "Post-hoc tests showed a signiﬁcant difference in Δ compound values between the Same and all other experimental groups (all p ≤ 0.001)."
