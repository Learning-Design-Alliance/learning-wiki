## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Unresolved discrepancy after high-confidence errors predicted higher frustration; resolved discrepancy predicted stronger enjoyment. [→ Berweger 2023](#berweger-2023)
`q3 i?` Both emotions were unrelated to confidence under the opposite condition. [→ Berweger 2023 (2)](#berweger-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Berweger 2023

Berweger, B., Kracke, B., & Dietrich, J. (2023). Preservice Teachers' Epistemic and Achievement Emotions When Confronted With Common Misconceptions About Education. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian Model 2, restricted to incorrect answers, testing the discrepancy resolution x confidence interaction on frustration and enjoyment among the same 275 participants; printed coefficients are unstandardized.

> "when participants were not able to resolve their discrepancy after high-confidence errors (solid line in Figure 3A), they showed higher frustration (.154 [.085, .225])."

### Berweger 2023 (2)

Berweger, B., Kracke, B., & Dietrich, J. (2023). Preservice Teachers' Epistemic and Achievement Emotions When Confronted With Common Misconceptions About Education. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000792

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same Model 2 analysis of 275 preservice teachers: the confidence-enjoyment link was significant only when the discrepancy was resolved, and no confidence-frustration link appeared when it was resolved.

> "when participants could resolve the discrepancy (dotted line in Figure 3B), they showed stronger enjoyment (.070 [.017, .125])."
