# In a self-regulated solving phase, S JOSs predicted solving success and AS and NS JOSs predicted effort regulation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2`–`r3` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` When solving time was self-regulated and unsolvable anagrams were included, the proportion of anagrams solved was greater for those receiving S JOSs than NS JOSs. [→ Burton 2023](#burton-2023)
`q3 i3` Not-solvable responses were more likely for anagrams given NS JOSs than AS + S JOSs, providing a measure of effort regulation predicted by JOSs. [→ Burton 2023 (2)](#burton-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Burton 2023

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3 (297 participants) modified solving phase with self-regulated time and both solvable and unsolvable anagrams. JOS main effect on proportion solved: F(2, 400) = 168.01, p < .001, η²p = .46. "the proportion of anagrams solved was greater for anagrams receiving S JOSs than NS JOSs".

> "Unlike in Experiments 1 and 2, here the proportion of anagrams solved was greater for anagrams receiving S JOSs than NS JOSs (p < .001)."

### Burton 2023 (2)

Burton, O. R., Bodner, G. E., Williamson, P., & Arnold, M. M. (2023). How accurate and predictive are judgments of solvability? Explorations in a two-phase anagram solving paradigm. Metacognition and Learning, 18, 1–35. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-022-09313-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 3 mixed-factor ANOVA on proportion of not-solvable responses: JOS main effect F(1, 266) = 739.65, p < .001, η²p = .74. "not-solvable responses were more likely for anagrams given NS JOSs than AS + S" JOSs, indicating NS JOSs predicted effort regulation.

> "There was a significant main effect of JOS; not-solvable responses were more likely for anagrams given NS JOSs than AS + S"
