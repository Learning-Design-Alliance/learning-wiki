Evidence¶

El Ashry M 2026¶

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

q2 · i? · review · r3

Prospective cohort (4 surgeons / 80 screws, one experienced surgeon plus one novice per group) using HoloLens 2 with Vuforia 3D registration versus freehand; the review reports 95% versus 77.5% accuracy (P<.05).

"Kong et al [10] demonstrated AR's equalizing effect on novice-expert performance disparity in pedicle screw placement: overall AR accuracy 95% versus 77.5% freehand (P<.05 for both linear and angular deviation)."

El Ashry M 2026 (2)¶

El Ashry M, El Ashry A, Khalique H, Abdalle Y, Yeung T. (2026). Augmented Reality in Surgical Training: Systematic Review of Its Impact on Technical Performance in Surgical Trainees. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/71572

q1 · i? · review · r2

The review's own interpretive synthesis, attributed to cognitive load theory and the expertise reversal effect; it is an interpretation, not a directly tested result of this review.

"The observed expertise reversal pattern, AR providing the greatest benefit to novices with diminishing returns at higher levels of proficiency, is consistent with predictions from both cognitive load theory [12] and the expertise reversal effect [14]."
