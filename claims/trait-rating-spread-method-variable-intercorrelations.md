# Intercorrelations among analyzed trait ratings show a spread of the general impression, and their size varies extremely with the rating method, from central tendencies of 0.3 to 0.9

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Three rating methods for the same fact yielded central tendencies of trait intercorrelations of 0.3 to 0.9, making the general factor impossible to diagnose and partial out. [→ Knight 1921](#knight-1921)

## Evidence

### Knight 1921

Knight, F. B., & Franzen, R. H. (1921). Pitfalls in rating schemes. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073817

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparisons across a Boice Teachers Rating Card (over 100 teachers, central tendency +0.5), a rank-order card in a Massachusetts system (100 teachers, many correlations 0.9), and competent judges rating 100 university instructors (average +0.32) show method-dependent variability.

> "Using three different methods for obtaining the same fact, we obtain central tendencies of inter-correlations of 0.3 to 0.9, which means that inter-correlations of traits is a highly variable function of the method of obtaining them."
