## Subclaims
`q3 i?` One-way ANOVA found a significant difference in listening success depending on the number of words in the videos (F(11,1500)=135.997, p<0.05), with post-hoc Tukey tests showing short clips outperforming long clips. [→ Tekin and Parmaksız 2016](#tekin-and-parmaksz-2016)

## Evidence

### Tekin and Parmaksız 2016

İnan Tekin, Ramazan Şükrü Parmaksız. (2016). Impact of Video Clips on the Development of the Listening Skills in English Classes: A Case Study of Turkish Students. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v4i9.1698

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA on scores grouped by clip length (short: 1-5 words; long: 6-20 words) with 126 students; the article reports F(11,1500)=135.997 and concludes that "the amount of words created a difference on the skill to understand."

> "there was a significant difference between the number of words in the videos watched within the scope of implementation and their level of success F (11, 1500)= 135.997; p > 0.05. The success of students in the implementation changed according to the number of words in the video."
