# Neither feedback mode produced differential improvements in syntactic complexity; a general time effect appeared only in the first task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the first task a significant time main effect (F = 6.184, p = .016) showed general syntactic improvement (composite mean 2.03 to 2.14), with no significant group or interaction effects; in the second task all effects were non-significant. [→ Tang J 2026](#tang-j-2026)

## Evidence

### Tang J 2026

Tang J, Li P, Luo R (2026) Teacher feedback VS AI-assisted peer feedback in L2 writing: A quasi-experimental study in a Chinese University. PLoS One 21(6): e0345976. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0345976

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mixed repeated-measures ANOVA on Coh-Metrix 3.0 syntactic complexity indicators (SYNLE, SYNNP, SYNMEDpos, SYNSTRUTt) across both tasks; the article prints no effect size in this sentence. Second-task results: time F(1,59)=0.234, p = .630; group F(1,59)=1.991, p = .165; interaction F(1,59)=0.417, p = .522.

> "a significant main effect of time was found (F = 6.184, p = .016), indicating that feedback and revision behavior brought about a general improvement in syntactic complexity (composite mean increased from 2.03 to 2.14). However, neither the main effect of group nor the interaction effect was significant (p > .05)"
