## Evidence

### Dahlberg Johanna 2018

Dahlberg Johanna, Nelson Marie, Dahlgren Madeleine Abrandt, Blomberg Marie. (2018). Ten years of simulation-based shoulder dystocia training- impact on obstetric outcome, clinical management, staff confidence, and the pedagogical practice - a time series study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-018-2001-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 1 reports 11, 20 and 29 cases among 11,064, 11,122 and 11,667 deliveries (p = 0.005) over the full birth cohort of about 3000 births per year.

> "Among the deliveries, there was a statistically significant increase of diagnosed shoulder dystocia between the prePROBE and the early and late postPROBE periods, 0.9/1000 deliveries prePROBE compared to 1.8 and 2.5/1000 postPROBE (Table 1)."

### Dahlberg Johanna 2018 (2)

Dahlberg Johanna, Nelson Marie, Dahlgren Madeleine Abrandt, Blomberg Marie. (2018). Ten years of simulation-based shoulder dystocia training- impact on obstetric outcome, clinical management, staff confidence, and the pedagogical practice - a time series study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-018-2001-0

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Table 2 prints non-significant p-values (0.15-0.99) for maternal age, parity, BMI, diabetes, gestational weight gain and gestational age, supporting the comparison as a risk-factor balance check.

> "There were no significant differences in maternal age, parity, BMI, gestational weight gain, or prevalence of diabetes mellitus type 1 between deliveries complicated with shoulder dystocia in prePROBE compared to the postPROBE groups (Table 2)."
