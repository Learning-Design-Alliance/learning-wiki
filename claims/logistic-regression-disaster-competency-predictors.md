## Evidence

### Ličen Sabina 2025

Ličen Sabina, Prosen Mirko. (2025). Disaster Nursing Competencies in a Time of Global Conflicts and Climate Crises: A Cross‐Sectional Survey Study. International Nursing Review. https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.70052

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple logistic regression (N = 379) with Cox and Snell R² = 0.086 and Nagelkerke R² = 0.137; 2 Log likelihood = 273.30. The article calls 13.7% "the moderate predictive power of these factors".

> "The results of the multiple logistic regression show that training in emergency management, frequency of participation in emergency drills in the last 10 years, possession of a formal qualification for emergency situations and the nurse's leadership role are statistically significant predictors of nurses' disaster management competencies."

### Ličen Sabina 2025 (2)

Ličen Sabina, Prosen Mirko. (2025). Disaster Nursing Competencies in a Time of Global Conflicts and Climate Crises: A Cross‐Sectional Survey Study. International Nursing Review. https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.70052

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Drill participation coefficient from the same logistic regression (Wald = 13.305, B = 0.121). The article prints Exp(B) = 1.129 with a 95% CI of 1.058–1.205; no standardised effect size is printed, so no magnitude is coded.

> "The frequency of participation in emergency drills measures how often the nurse has participated in such drills in the last ten years, with each additional unit of participation significantly increasing the likelihood of higher competencies (Exp(B) = 1.129, 95% CI: 1.058–1.205, p < 0.001)."
