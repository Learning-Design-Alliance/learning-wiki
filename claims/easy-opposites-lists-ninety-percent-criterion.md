# Standardized easy opposites lists can be built from words answered correctly by ninety percent or more of subjects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The authors offer standardized easy stimulus-word lists consisting only of words to which ninety percent or more of the 100 subjects gave the correct opposite. [→ Irving King and Hugo Gold 1911](#irving-king-and-hugo-gold-1911)

## Evidence

### Irving King and Hugo Gold 1911

Irving King and Hugo Gold. (1911). A Tentative Standardization of Certain "Opposites Tests". University of Iowa. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=A+Tentative+Standardization+of+Certain+Opposites+Tests

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Normative tabulation of oral responses from 100 University of Iowa subjects (faculty, graduate students, seniors, juniors) to eight 20-word lists. The authors state the lists "ought to give fairly uniform results" and identify easy-list words with 90% or more correct responses, e.g. good, up, wet, wrong.

> "On the basis of our results, we offer, as standardized lists, certain words selected from our frequency tables which ought to give fairly uniform results in an opposites test, when given under standard conditions."
