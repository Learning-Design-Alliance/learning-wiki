## Evidence

### Lyra 2016

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Randomized controlled study with 27 undergraduate students in an online platform; paired t-test over all students across Stages A and B. The article reports pretest µ = 20.85, σ = 3.5 and post-test µ = 33.96, σ = 5.11, a "statistically significant difference".

> "A paired samples t-test compared the number of correct answers between the pretest and the post-test and found a statistically significant difference (t(52) = -10.9; p < .001)"

### Lyra 2016 (2)

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Stage C delayed post-test one week after Stage B in the same online-platform study; the authors conclude "learners maintained the acquired knowledge" despite the descriptive decrease to µ = 31.44 (σ = 6.55).

> "Despite the slight average decrease from the post-test to the delayed post-test (µ = 31.44, σ = 6.55), the difference was not statistically significant (t(52) = 1.5; p = .12)"
