## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cognitive performance improved with age and grade level, whereas younger and lower-grade children outperformed older peers in mathematics and literature. [→ Tia Achil 2025](#tia-achil-2025)

## Evidence

### Tia Achil 2025

Tia Achil, Konan Amoin G., Hauser Jonas, Ndri Kouassi Y., Ciclet Olivier, Esso Lasme E., Nindjin Charlemagne. (2025). A Cross-Sectional Study of the Relationship Between Dietary Micronutrient Intake, Cognition and Academic Performance Among School-Aged Children in Taabo, Côte d’Ivoire. Nutrients 17(22):3602. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17223602

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Kruskal–Wallis group comparisons across age groups in the cross-sectional sample of 252 children, showing an academic trend "in contrast to the trends noted in cognitive performance". Only p-values printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Regarding academic performance, significant differences were also observed between age groups and grades. In contrast to the trends noted in cognitive performance, younger children (6–8 years) achieved higher scores in both mathematics (p < 0.001) and literature (p = 0.002) compared with children aged 9–10 and 11–12 years."
