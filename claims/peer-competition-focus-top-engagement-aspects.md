## Evidence

### Pettit RK 2015

Pettit RK, McCoy L, Kinney M, Schwartz FN. (2015). Student perceptions of gamified audience response system interactions in large group lectures and via lecture capture technology. BMC Medical Education 15:92. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0373-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of 91 first-year medical students rating engagement/flow statements about gamified TP games on 5-point Likert scales, analyzed as grouped positive vs neutral/negative responses. The article reports "at least 85 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they enjoyed the friendly peer competition" alongside focus, energy, and variety items.

> "at least 85 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they enjoyed the friendly peer competition, were focused on the activity, their energy level rose, and they enjoyed the variety of interactions"

### Pettit RK 2015 (2)

Pettit RK, McCoy L, Kinney M, Schwartz FN. (2015). Student perceptions of gamified audience response system interactions in large group lectures and via lecture capture technology. BMC Medical Education 15:92. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0373-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same survey's engagement category: the prize-focus item was the lowest-rated, at "Only 55 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed"; the authors interpret that students were more motivated by competition than by the nominal prizes such as microbe-shaped candy.

> "Only 55 % of the respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they were focused on the prize during TP games, the lowest positive response in the engagement category."
