## Evidence

### Humphrey 2016

Humphrey, N., Barlow, A., Wigelsworth, M., Lendrum, A., Pert, K., Joyce, C., Stephens, E., Wo, L., Squires, G., Woods, K., Calam, R., & Turner, A. (2016). A cluster randomized controlled trial of the Promoting Alternative Thinking Strategies (PATHS) curriculum. Journal of School Psychology, 58, 73–89. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsp.2016.07.002

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup (secondary-effect) analyses of the cluster RCT among children classified as at-risk at baseline showed "statistically significant increases in Pro-Social Behavior" (d = 0.16, p = .03) and, marginally, Engagement (d = 0.19, p = .06) in PATHS schools.

> "increases in Pro-Social Behavior, d = 0.16, 95% CI [− 0.00, 0.33], p = .03 (see Table 4b) and (marginally) Engagement, d = 0.19, 95% CI [− 0.05, 0.43], p = .06 (see Table 5b), among children classified as at-risk at baseline"
