Systematic mapping of discourse across the 96 transitions articles. The paper reports the distribution as printed: 63% negative, 26% mixed, 9% neutral, 2% predominantly positive, and argues unbalanced negative discourse may bias findings and cause unnecessary anxiety for children and families.

> "Additionally, discourse about primary-secondary school transitions was unbalanced with 63% studies using a negative discourse, 26% a mixed discourse (of these 16% were more negative than positive), 9% neutral, and only 2% using a predominantly positive discourse"
