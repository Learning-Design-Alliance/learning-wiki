# For features such as Mean Velocity and Handwriting Density, Cyrillic improves faster than Latin, producing an increasing gap that indicates incomplete transfer

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean velocity increased significantly with grade in both alphabets (Latin U(562), p < 0.05; Cyrillic U(565), p < 0.01), but increased more rapidly for Cyrillic. [→ Thibault Asselborn 2021](#thibault-asselborn-2021)
`q2 i?` Children wrote significantly smaller (denser) in Cyrillic than Latin in grade 3, with a similar but non-significant tendency in grade 4 (p = 0.074). [→ Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)](#thibault-asselborn-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Thibault Asselborn 2021

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Grade 1 vs grade 4 comparison of Mean Velocity distributions within each alphabet; the Cyrillic mean was always higher and rose faster, which the authors attribute to incomplete learning transfer between the alphabets.

> "a Mann – Whitney U test has been performed between the ﬁrst and last grade and shows statistically signi ﬁcant differences between the distributions: for Latin, U (562), p < 0.05, for Cyrillic, U(565), p < 0.01"

### Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Handwriting Density comparison across alphabets: writing became smaller with grade in both alphabets (an indicator of quality), but the between-alphabet difference grew; the grade-4 difference was not statistically significant.

> "children wrote signi ﬁcantly smaller in Cyrillic than in Latin in grade 3 and this tendency seems to be the same for the last grade even if no statistically signi ﬁcant results could be extracted in grade 4 ( p = 0.074)"
