# Students unanimously report positive effects of metacognitive strategy use on writing in both L1 and L2, with strategies compensating for limited L2 linguistic knowledge

`q2 i?` Stimulated-recall interview participants reported metacognitive strategies helped writing performance in both languages, with a more important compensatory role in L2 writing. [→ Wandong Xu 2024](#wandong-xu-2024)

## Evidence

### Wandong Xu 2024

Wandong Xu, Xinhua Zhu. (2024). Examining metacognitive strategy use in L1 and L2 task-situated writing: effects, transferability, and cross-language facilitation. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09387-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stimulated-recall interviews with 20 students (10 English majors, 10 non-English majors of different L2 proficiency levels) found participants "unanimously reported the positive effects of metacognitive strategy use on the writing process and outcomes"; one participant described purposively managing limited linguistic knowledge to enhance L2 text quality.

> "they unanimously reported the positive effects of metacognitive strategy use on the writing process and outcomes, which was the same in L1 and L2 contexts."
