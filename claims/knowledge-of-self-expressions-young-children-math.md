## Evidence

### Ebru Aydın 2022

Ebru Aydın, Çağlayan Dinçer. (2022). “I did it wrong, but i know it”: Young children’s metacognitive knowledge expressions during peer interactions in math activities. Thinking Skills and Creativity 45 (2022) 101104. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsc.2022.101104

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Episode from the “Measuring Rods” length activity (Table 3): a peer pointed out the inverted five, and Michael replied he knew the correct form. The article states "Being aware of one’s own inadequacy is important metacognitive knowledge."

> "While creating his measuring rod, Michael symmetrically inverted the “5” symbol, and upon his friend’s notice, he stated that he was aware of both the misdrawing and the correct form."

### Ebru Aydın 2022 (2)

Ebru Aydın, Çağlayan Dinçer. (2022). “I did it wrong, but i know it”: Young children’s metacognitive knowledge expressions during peer interactions in math activities. Thinking Skills and Creativity 45 (2022) 101104. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsc.2022.101104

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Episode from the “Making a puzzle” area activity (Table 4): with no instruction on assembling pieces, Michael said he forgot to paste them after a friend's warning. The authors interpret this as articulating weaknesses despite preschool memory limitations.

> "Although children have moderately deficient skills in memory processes such as recalling, they can articulate their weaknesses in learning processes, which is considered metacognitive knowledge of self (Whitebread et al., 2009)."
