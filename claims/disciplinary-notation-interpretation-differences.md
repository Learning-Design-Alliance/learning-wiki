## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Physicists initially identified x with spring elongation and wanted Fx as a force in the same direction; engineers rapidly used measuring tapes and invented their own notation; mathematicians were puzzled by the subscript and expected capital F to represent a function rather than a force. ## Evidence

### David Roundy 2014

David Roundy, Allison Dorko, Tevian Dray, Corinne A. Manogue, and Eric Weber. (2014). Experts' understanding of partial derivatives using the Partial Derivative Machine. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of interview transcripts on how experts interpreted the prompt symbols. The authors report "the physicists initially identiﬁed x with the elongation of the spring" while mathematicians expected F to be a function and were confused by the subscript notation.

> "The physicists initially identiﬁed x with the elongation of the spring and then wanted Fx to represent a force in the same direction as the spring. Their mental focus was clearly on the internal mechanics of the system and they (at ﬁrst) ignored the measuring tapes on the PDM as a potential method for measuring a position x."
