## Evidence

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

An administrator responsible for allocating teaching time explained "There is no way you're going to prioritize Sim-IPE when it's not in the curriculum," timetabling curriculum courses first and simulation only in free slots. Thematic analysis identified lack of curriculum integration as a crucial barrier.

> "There is no way you're going to prioritize Sim-IPE when it's not in the curriculum. The curriculum is very clear it talks about these are the courses which are there."

### Najjuma Josephine N. 2024 (2)

Najjuma Josephine N., Muhumuza Albert, Santorino Data, Sekyere Samuel Owusu, Ocheke Amaka N., Yiltok Simon J., Bajunirwe Francis. (2024). Barriers and facilitators to interprofessional simulation-based learning in a Ugandan medical school: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06521-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

The timetabling administrator stated "the timetable I have done for simulation really I've not seen it timetabling nursing students and medicine students at the same time. Actually, it's always separate." Participants recommended harmonizing schedules so both professions are free simultaneously.

> "if we are specifically talking about simulation, the timetable I have done for simulation really I've not seen it timetabling nursing students and medicine students at the same time. Actually, it's always separate."
