# The podcast increased students' confidence in nephrology clinical skills but did not change intention to specialize in nephrology

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Dupont V 2025

Dupont V, Guerrot D, Bouazzi L, Figueres L, Pers Y-M, Guenou E, Barbe C, Yauy K. (2025). Evaluating podcasts as a tool for OSCE training: a randomized trial using generative AI-powered simulation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06675-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-study survey of all 50 randomized students compared perception outcomes between groups using Chi² or Fisher exact tests; the podcast group reported higher comfort with clinical skills, "41.7% vs. 16%; p = 0.04". Confidence was self-reported.

> "However, it significantly improved the proportion of students feeling comfortable with nephrology clinical competencies (41.7% vs. 16%; p = 0.04)."

### Dupont V 2025 (2)

Dupont V, Guerrot D, Bouazzi L, Figueres L, Pers Y-M, Guenou E, Barbe C, Yauy K. (2025). Evaluating podcasts as a tool for OSCE training: a randomized trial using generative AI-powered simulation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06675-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary survey outcome in the same trial: intention to intern (44.0% vs. 32.0%; p = 0.38) and to specialize in nephrology (4.2% vs. 4.0%; p = 0.99) did not differ between the podcast and control groups. No effect size was printed for this null finding.

> "The use of the OSCE preparation podcast did not impact the intention to do an internship in a nephrology department or the will to become a nephrologist."
