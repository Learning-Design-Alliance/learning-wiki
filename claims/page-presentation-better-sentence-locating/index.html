Evidence¶

Annie Piolat 1997¶

Annie Piolat, Jean-Yves Roussey and Olivier Thunin. (1997). Effects of screen presentation on text reading and revising. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies. https://doi.org/10.1006/ijhc.1997.0145

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1, a controlled lab experiment in which 54 undergraduates read a 574-word expository text in Page or Scroll mode and then pointed to sentence locations. The article reports "a significantly higher degree of accuracy than Scroll" with F(1, 68) = 15.92, p < 0.001.

"Page allowed participants to locate test sentences with a significantly higher degree of accuracy than Scroll (mean distance in lines: 7.68vs. 10.56;F(l, 68) = 15.92, p < 0.001)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Only Page readers tended to locate boundary-zone sentences more accurately than central-zone sentences
  • The effect of page-break response cues on locating accuracy depends on the presentation mode read: they help Page readers and hurt Scroll readers
  • Reading time did not differ between Page and Scroll, though Page readers tended to backtrack more
  • Browsing term lists (selection mode) helps searchers who do not know the exact query term — related
  • Page readers tended to include more details in their summaries than Scroll readers (a marginal result, p = .056), while recall of main ideas did not differ significantly — related
