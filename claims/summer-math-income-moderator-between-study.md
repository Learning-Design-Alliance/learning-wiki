## Subclaims
`q3 i0` In a between-study random effects moderator analysis, low-income samples benefited from summer math programs (d=0.11, significant) while mixed-income samples did not (d=-0.02, not significant). [→ Quinn 2014 (2)](#quinn-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Quinn 2014

Quinn, D. M., Lynch, K., & Kim, J. S. (2014). Replicating the Moderating Role of Income Status on Summer School Effects across Subject Areas: A Meta-analysis. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562949

`q3 · i0` · `quant-synthesis · r1`

Random effects moderator analysis (Table 1) coding samples with over 50% FRL students as low income (k=8) versus less than 50% (k=3). The article reports a "significant positive treatment effect of d=0.11" for low-income samples versus a negatively-signed d=-0.02.

> "In low income samples, students enjoyed a significant positive treatment effect of d=0.11, while in mixed income samples, the pooled effect size was negatively-signed and not statistically different from zero (d=-0.02)."

### Quinn 2014 (2)

Quinn, D. M., Lynch, K., & Kim, J. S. (2014). Replicating the Moderating Role of Income Status on Summer School Effects across Subject Areas: A Meta-analysis. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562949

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The Qbetween test for the math income moderator (Table 1: Qbetween=2.802, p=0.094) was not statistically significant; the authors attribute this to "limited statistical power" from the small number of studies.

> "Due to the small number of studies, however, we have limited statistical power for detecting whether effects were statistically different for each group (p=0.09)."
