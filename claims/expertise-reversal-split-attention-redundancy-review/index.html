Kalyuga 2003¶

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The Expertise Reversal Effect. Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes this to Kalyuga et al. (1998): in experiments with electrical trainees, novices needed integrated text but more experienced trainees did significantly better with diagrams alone and reported lower mental effort, so "The split-attention effect for novices was replaced by the redun -dancy effect for experts."

"Kalyuga, Chandler, and Sweller (1998) found that inexpe-rienced electrical trainees benefitted from textual explana -tions integrated into the diagrams of electrical circuits (to reduce split attention). They were not able to comprehend a diagram-only format. However, more experienced trainees performed significantly better with the electrical circuit dia-gram-only format."

Kalyuga 2003 (2)¶

Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The Expertise Reversal Effect. Educational Psychologist, 38(1), 23-31. https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Yeung, Jin, and Sweller (1998), a textual study showing that integrated explanatory notes helped low language-competence learners but "retarded learning for more knowledgeable learners."

"Integrating explanatory notes into the primary text assisted learners with low levels of language competence. The same format retarded learning for more knowledgeable learners because the integrated notes, al- though redundant, were difficult to ignore when integrated into the primary text."
