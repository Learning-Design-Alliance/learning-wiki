## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students from closed schools experienced a long-term negative impact on math test scores lasting four years, but reading scores returned to expected levels by year two. [→ Gordon 2018](#gordon-2018)

## Evidence

### Gordon 2018

Gordon, M.F., de la Torre, M., Cowhy, J.R., Moore, P.T., Sartain, L.S., & Knight, D. (2018). School closings in Chicago: Staff and student experiences and academic outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-closings-chicago-staff-and-student-experiences-and-academic-outcomes

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multi-year administrative data analysis comparing closed-school students to similar comparison students. The report finds "the gap in math test scores remained for four years post-closings" at roughly two months behind, while reading rebounded without improving faster than comparison students.

> "However, the gap in math test scores remained for four years post-closings, the last year in our analyses. The size of the effect was similar to the effect during the year of the announcement, which was approximately two months behind in math."
