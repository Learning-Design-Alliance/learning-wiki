## Evidence

### Andrade Italo Rigoberto Cavalcante; de Queiroz Johny Carlos; Farias Maria Sinara; Maia Nicole Paulo da Silva; da Silva Lúcia de Fátima 2025

Andrade Italo Rigoberto Cavalcante; de Queiroz Johny Carlos; Farias Maria Sinara; Maia Nicole Paulo da Silva; da Silva Lúcia de Fátima. (2025). Clinical simulation in the teaching of cardiovascular physical examination with nursing students: a systematic review. Revista Brasileira de Enfermagem. https://doi.org/10.1590/0034-7167-2023-0429

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Quasi-experimental mixed-method study of 52 nursing students randomized to a high-fidelity simulator or standardized patients, as reported in the review's Chart 4; knowledge scores favored standardized patients, with no printed effect size.

> "Knowledge and performance scores for all students increased after the simulation activities; however, students who worked with standardized patients achieved significantly higher knowledge scores than those who worked with the high-fidelity simulator."

### Andrade Italo Rigoberto Cavalcante; de Queiroz Johny Carlos; Farias Maria Sinara; Maia Nicole Paulo da Silva; da Silva Lúcia de Fátima 2025 (2)

Andrade Italo Rigoberto Cavalcante; de Queiroz Johny Carlos; Farias Maria Sinara; Maia Nicole Paulo da Silva; da Silva Lúcia de Fátima. (2025). Clinical simulation in the teaching of cardiovascular physical examination with nursing students: a systematic review. Revista Brasileira de Enfermagem. https://doi.org/10.1590/0034-7167-2023-0429

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Same mixed-method study of 52 students: post-simulation performance scores were similar between groups, with the printed t-test statistic quoted; equivalence was not tested.

> "Regarding skill acquisition, students in both groups achieved similar performance scores in the post-simulation evaluation test (t = 0.767, p = 0.447)."
