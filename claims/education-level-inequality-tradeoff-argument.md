## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors conclude that rising inequality is acceptable, and not necessarily bad, if it is accompanied by increases in the level of education. [→ David Gunawan 2022](#david-gunawan-2022)

## Evidence

### David Gunawan 2022

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretive argument in the conclusions, offered without a formal test: because zero inequality would require everyone in the same category, "increasing inequality is not necessarily bad if it is accompanied by increases in the level of education".

> "Hence, increasing inequality is not necessarily bad if it is accompanied by increases in the level of education."

### David Gunawan 2022 (2)

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The premises of the argument: leaving too many individuals behind in the bottom part of the distribution is undesirable, yet "having all individuals in the same category" — the requirement for zero inequality — is "also undesirable and clearly not realistic".

> "Having increasing inequality caused by too many individuals being left behind in the bottom part of the distribution is clearly undesirable. However, having all individuals in the same category – the requirement for zero inequality – is also undesirable and clearly not realistic."
