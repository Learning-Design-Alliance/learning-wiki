Evidence¶

Yu 2015¶

Yu, W.-C. W., Lin, C. C., Ho, M.-H., & Wang, J. (2015). Technology facilitated PBL pedagogy and its impact on nursing student's academic achievement and critical thinking dispositions. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 14(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1057343

q3 · i? · causal · r2

ANOVA on posttest comprehensive exam scores for 120 nursing students across three instructional conditions (PBL n=40, B-PBL n=39, traditional n=41) revealed a statistically significant between-group difference, "F = 10.83, p < .05". The B-PBL group had the highest posttest mean (79.13) and the traditional group the lowest (65.34). No effect size was reported.

"However at the end of the study, based upon posttest scores, as illustrated in table 3, the groups were significantly different, F = 10.83, p < .05."

Yu 2015 (2)¶

Yu, W.-C. W., Lin, C. C., Ho, M.-H., & Wang, J. (2015). Technology facilitated PBL pedagogy and its impact on nursing student's academic achievement and critical thinking dispositions. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 14(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1057343

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Post hoc Tukey HSD multiple comparisons at posttest showed "a significant difference between the B-PBL group and the PBL group at the posttest with p < .05". B-PBL also significantly outperformed traditional (mean difference 13.78, p = .00), while PBL vs. traditional was not significant (mean difference 5.02, p = .23). No effect size was reported.

"There was a significant difference between the B-PBL group and the PBL group at the posttest with p < .05. The B-PBL teaching methodology was shown to have a significant impact on the students' academic achievement posttest scores."
