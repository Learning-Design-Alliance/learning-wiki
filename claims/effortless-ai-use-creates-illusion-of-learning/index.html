Evidence¶

Mario Brcic 2026¶

Mario Brcic, Stjepan Frljic. (2026). The Effortless Trap: Productive Struggle, AI, and the Illusion of Learning. Preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26181

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The article reports, citing Deslauriers et al. (2019), a controlled study in which students in effortful active classes felt they learned less while objectively learning more, grounding the effortless trap. No effect size is printed in the article.

"Students in effortful active classes judged that they were learning less even as they objectively learned more (Deslauriers et al., 2019), so fluent, AI-smoothed practice can feel most productive exactly when it teaches least."

Mario Brcic 2026 (2)¶

Mario Brcic, Stjepan Frljic. (2026). The Effortless Trap: Productive Struggle, AI, and the Illusion of Learning. Preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26181

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The article reports, citing the Shen and Tamkin (2026) preprint (a randomized coding task, N=52 per its reference list), that engineers with an AI assistant performed the task well but learned markedly less afterward. No effect size is printed in the article.

"software engineers given an AI assistant on a coding task performed it well but learned markedly less, scoring far lower afterward on the concepts behind the code they had just produced (Shen and Tamkin, 2026)"
