# Students' favourable attitudes towards sports were positively associated with higher academic achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Compared to students disliking sports, students with general or fond attitudes towards sports scored significantly higher in reading, mathematics and language in adjusted models. [→ Wang Junyu 2025](#wang-junyu-2025)

## Evidence

### Wang Junyu 2025

Wang Junyu, Yang Yong, Wu Jun, Xie Ting, Guo Xuejun, Yuan Xun, Li Ting, Zhang Yu, Zhao Huiying, Zhuang Jie. (2025). Association between moderate-to-vigorous physical activity trajectories and academic achievement in Chinese primary school children: a 3-year longitudinal study. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-21487-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

GEE models adjusting for demographics, sleep and parental education in the 202-child cohort. Student attitudes (general vs. do not like; like vs. do not like) were significant in all three subjects; parent attitudes were not significant. No standardised effect size printed.

> "Compared to those disliking sports, participants expressing general or specific fondness for sports were more likely to show increased scores in reading (β = 8.8, 95% CI: 6.68–10.92 and β = 9.29, 95% CI: 6.98–11.6), mathematics (β = 9.47, 95% CI: 7.46–11.47 and β = 8.76, 95% CI: 6.55–10.98), and language (β = 8.69, 95% CI: 6.64–10.73 and β = 8.27, 95% CI: 6.04–10.49)."
