Evidence¶

Pfenninger 2015¶

Pfenninger, S. E. (2015). MSL in the digital ages: Effects and effectiveness of computer-mediated intervention for FL learners with dyslexia. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching, 5(1), 109-133. https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2015.5.1.6

q2 · i3 · associational · r1

Correlational analysis of the stimulated vocabulary retrieval task scores in the intervention study; the article reports very strong positive correlations between L2 and L3 retrieval performance, with "pre-RETRIEVE: r = .86, p < .0001; post-RETRIEVE: r = .87, p < .0001".

"In general, the learners who had higher scores on the L2 stimulated vocabulary retrieval task achieved higher scores on the corresponding L3 test (pre-RETRIEVE: r = .86, p < .0001; post-RETRIEVE: r = .87, p < .0001)"

Pfenninger 2015 (2)¶

Pfenninger, S. E. (2015). MSL in the digital ages: Effects and effectiveness of computer-mediated intervention for FL learners with dyslexia. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching, 5(1), 109-133. https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2015.5.1.6

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Group comparisons on the stimulated vocabulary retrieval tasks in L2 German and L3 English in the intervention study; the article attributes the dyslexic learners' lower scores to an underdevelopment of the size and depth of German and English vocabulary.

"While there were no significant differences between the Test Dyslexic group and the Control Dyslexic group, or between the Test Nondyslexic group and the Control Nondyslexic group, the nondyslexic learners performed significantly better than the dyslexic learners on all measures (in all cases p < .001)"

Related Claims¶

  • The training proved effective irrespective of reading ability and was more effective for the Test Dyslexic group than for the Test Nondyslexic group, which made greater gains on rapid naming, L3 receptive vocabulary and L3 picture naming — related
  • The Test Dyslexic group outperformed the Control Dyslexic group on the post-TOWRE L3 word decoding measures despite equivalent pretest performance, but did not catch up with either nondyslexic group — related
  • Three months of daily computer-based MSL training in L3 English produced significant pre-post gains for the Test Dyslexic group on L2 and L3 rapid naming and L3 receptive vocabulary tasks, while control groups did not improve — related
  • MANCOVA showed significant treatment effects for 11 of 15 dependent measures with consistently high effect sizes, while L2 reading speed, L2 spelling, L2 rapid digit naming and L3 stimulated vocabulary retrieval did not change as a function of the intervention — related
