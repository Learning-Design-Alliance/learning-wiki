# Familiarity with technology gained from prior face-to-face tablet use does not significantly contribute to learning activities, cognitive activation, or effort in digital distance teaching

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically significant group differences between high and low familiarity groups on learning activities, perceived cognitive activation, or effort in either subject. [→ Fütterer 2023](#futterer-2023)

## Evidence

### Fütterer 2023

Fütterer, T., Hoch, E., Lachner, A., Scheiter, K., & Stürmer, K. (2023). High-quality digital distance teaching during COVID-19 school closures: Does familiarity with technology matter? Computers & Education, 199, 104788. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2023.104788

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple group models comparing 517 high-familiarity and 212 low-familiarity students. The study reports "no statistically significant difference between the high and the low familiarity group" on any construct, with all |d| values below 0.30 in Table 4.

> "The mean differences (ΔM) for student-observed learning activities (manifest) in digital distance teaching showed no statistically significant difference between the high and the low familiarity group as did the means for student-perceived cognitive activation and students' effort in learning (i.e., academic effort, and cognitive engagement; latent)"
