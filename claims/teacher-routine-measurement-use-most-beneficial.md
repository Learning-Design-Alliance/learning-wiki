This is the editorial's core interpretive claim. It frames measurements as "the teacher's useful tools, whereby she can determine quickly and accurately the needs of her pupils and the most effective adjustment of her energies," so that when staff unite to study children's needs, "much of the drudgery and grind of teaching will disappear." No evidence is offered beyond the argument itself.

> "Instead of more work for the teacher measurements mean in the long run less work, and much easier and more successful work."
