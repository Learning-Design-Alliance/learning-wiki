Evidence¶

Armah 2018¶

Armah, R.B., Cofie, P.O., & Okpoti, C.A. (2018). Investigating the effect of van Hiele Phase-based instruction on pre-service teachers' geometric thinking. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(1), 314-330. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.383201

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Chi-square test on the 2×2 contingency table of group by van Hiele level (VHL0,1&2 vs VHL3&4) for 150 pre-service teachers after the intervention; the article reports "only 19.5% of PTs in the control group attained VHL3&4, whereas 80.5% of PTs in the experimental group attained VHL3&4".

"Table 8 presents the results of the chi -square test for PTs post -test scores and as can be seen from this table the difference highlighted above was statistically significant (χ2 = 58.949; df = 1; p < 0.05 )."

Armah 2018 (2)¶

Armah, R.B., Cofie, P.O., & Okpoti, C.A. (2018). Investigating the effect of van Hiele Phase-based instruction on pre-service teachers' geometric thinking. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(1), 314-330. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.383201

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Post-test bar-chart analysis (Figure 4) of van Hiele levels in the experimental group: Level 3 rose from 8% to 64% and 18.67% reached Level 4, while the control group reached 20% at Level 3 and 0% at Level 4. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

"However, the percentage number of PTs at VHL 3 increased significantly from 8% to 64%. Also, it was interesting to see 18.67% of PTs operating at VHL 4 in the experimental groups' posttest."
