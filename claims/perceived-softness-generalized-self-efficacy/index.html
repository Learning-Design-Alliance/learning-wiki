Evidence¶

Tsuda 2026¶

Tsuda, A., Manalo, E., Teramae, T., & Noda, T. (2026). Unveiling the Impact of Goal Types: Enhancing Self-Efficacy in Rehabilitation Robotics. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00444-4

q1 · i1 · causal · r2

Authors' Discussion-level report of the association between perceived softness and generalized task self-efficacy; the printed residual correlation in Table 2 is small (r = 0.18) and its 95% CrI includes zero, so the association is weak and the SEM-based abstract statement should be read cautiously.

"Participants who perceived the robot as soft also reported greater confidence in transferring the skills acquired in boccia to other contexts and in responding ef- fectively when the task became more difficult"

Tsuda 2026 (2)¶

Tsuda, A., Manalo, E., Teramae, T., & Noda, T. (2026). Unveiling the Impact of Goal Types: Enhancing Self-Efficacy in Rehabilitation Robotics. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00444-4

q1 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion, offered without a direct soft-versus-rigid comparison in this study; the article explicitly cautions these relationships should be interpreted cautiously.

"when individuals experience low self-efficacy, perceiving the robot as soft may enhance their sense of capability in the task at hand, increase their willingness to engage with more challenging tasks in the future"
