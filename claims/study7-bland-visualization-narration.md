# When text is read aloud (reducing split visual attention), undergraduates generalize more broadly from bland than rich visualizations, but the text and visualization cues' effects do not compound

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Study 7, undergraduates generalized more broadly with bland than rich visualizations (b = -0.16). [→ David Menendez 2023](#david-menendez-2023)

## Evidence

### David Menendez 2023

David Menendez. (2023). Cues to generality: Integrating linguistic and visual information when generalizing biological information. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000812

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-registered Bayesian analysis of Study 7 (146 undergraduates after exclusions; narrated videos) found an effect of visualization type, b = -0.16 [-0.29, -0.02], favoring bland images; the b is a log-odds posterior median, no standardized effect size printed.

> "there was evidence for an effect of visualization type, b = -0.16 [-0.29, -0.02], such that undergraduate students generalized more broadly when the visualization was bland (M = 3.61, SD = 1.24) than when it was rich (M = 3.53, SD = 1.22)."

### David Menendez 2023 (2)

David Menendez. (2023). Cues to generality: Integrating linguistic and visual information when generalizing biological information. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000812

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 7's Bayesian model found a visualization × text-type interaction (Generic vs Nongeneric: b = 0.17 [0.03, 0.31]); the discussion states "Undergraduate students seemed to generalize more broadly when at least one element of the lesson promoted generalization."

> "There was also an interaction between visualization type and text type, Generic vs Nongeneric: b = 0.17 [0.03, 0.31], and Exemplified vs Nongeneric: b = 0.09 [-0.05, 0.23], probability of direction 89.57%, suggesting that the greater generalization with the bland visualization occurs only when the statement does not use generic language."
