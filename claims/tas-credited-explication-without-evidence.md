## Evidence

### Edit Yerushalmi 2016

Edit Yerushalmi, Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman, Charles Henderson and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Physics graduate teaching assistants' beliefs about a grading rubric: Lessons learned. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.097)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of how the 15 TAs applied the rubric's explication category to the brief solution SSE, compared with the investigators' expert application of the rubric, found TAs credited explication "even though there was no evidence of explicit invoking or justifying of physics in the brief solution SSE".

> "over half of the TAs selected that “useful principles are invoked” and “useful principles are justified” when grading the brief solution SSE, even though there was no evidence of explicit invoking or justifying of physics in the brief solution SSE."

### Marshman 2018

Marshman, Sayer, Henderson, Yerushalmi, and Singh. (2018). The challenges of changing teaching assistants’ grading practices: Requiring students to show evidence of understanding. Physical Review Physics Education Research. [doi:10.1139/cjp-2017-0030](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjp-2017-0030)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of Week 2 rubric grading of the brief solution SSE (and analogously SSG) by 15 TAs. The article reports "There was no evidence of explicit invoking or justifying of physics in SSE, though the majority of TAs gave this solution credit for those two criteria," in disagreement with expert raters.

> "There was no evidence of explicit invoking or justifying of physics in SSE, though the majority of TAs gave this solution credit for those two criteria."
