# A pilot study found no divergence effect of optimal versus suboptimal test conditions on bimodality of conservation scores

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the expectation that optimal test conditions would yield more bimodal score distributions, a pilot study found strongly bimodal distributions in both optimal and suboptimal conditions; no divergence effect was detected. [→ Hartelman 1998](#hartelman-1998)

## Evidence

### Hartelman 1998

Hartelman, P. A. I., van der Maas, H. L. J., & Molenaar, P. C. M. (1998). Detecting and modelling developmental transitions. British Journal of Developmental Psychology, 16, 97-122. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-835X.1998.tb00751.x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pilot study testing optimal test conditions as a splitting control variable: children were tested individually (optimal conditions) and in the classroom (suboptimal conditions). Contrary to expectation, 'In both cases, however, the score distributions were strongly bimodal'; the study did not detect the expected divergence.

> "We expected that the scores in the optimal condition would be more bimodal than in the suboptimal condition. In both cases, however, the score distributions were strongly bimodal."
