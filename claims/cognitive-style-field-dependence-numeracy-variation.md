## Evidence

### Gbenga-Akanmu & Jegede 2019

Gbenga-Akanmu, T. O., & Jegede, P. O. (2019). Influence of Meta-cognitive Training and Cognitive Styles on Numeracy Achievement of Nigerian Pupils’ in Computer-mediated Classrooms. World Journal of Education, 9(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v9n1p221

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA of the quasi-experiment (Table 1) reports the field-dependent/independent effect as significant (F = 11.16; p = 0.0114). The printed effect size is partial eta square = 0.12; the article states cognitive style "accoun ted for 12% of the variation in the pupils’ performance". The text and abstract also present F = 11.16 as the effect of meta-cognitive training, although the table prints it on the cognitive-style row (the Group row is F = 13.15, p = 0.0023). Cognitive style was measured, not assigned, so this is a difference between groups of learners, not a shown cause.

> "Furthermore, the partial eta square for the group embedded figure test is 0.12. This showed that (CGEFT) (f ield-dependent and independent) accoun ted for 12% of the variation in the pupils’ performance."
