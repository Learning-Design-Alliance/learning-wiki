Evidence¶

Anthony B. Fong 2015¶

Anthony B. Fong, Neal D. Finkelstein, Laura M. Jaeger, Rebeca Diaz, Marie E. Broek. (2015). Evaluation of the Expository Reading and Writing Course: Findings from the Investing in Innovation Development Grant. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED559522

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Fidelity-of-implementation assessment during the 2013/14 study period, using implementation feedback instruments and a fidelity rubric. The report states "10 (17.9 percent) taught at least 8 modules with fidelity" under the six-strand criterion.

"Of the 56 ERWC teachers who participated in the study, 10 (17.9 percent) taught at least 8 modules with fidelity. This low percentage is due to the stringent requirement that, for a teacher to be considered as having taught any module with fidelity, he or she needed to teach at least one activity in each of the module's six strands"

Anthony B. Fong 2015 (2)¶

Anthony B. Fong, Neal D. Finkelstein, Laura M. Jaeger, Rebeca Diaz, Marie E. Broek. (2015). Evaluation of the Expository Reading and Writing Course: Findings from the Investing in Innovation Development Grant. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED559522

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Alternative fidelity criterion analysis of the same 56 study teachers, counting at least five activities per module regardless of strand. The report states "35 teachers (62.5 percent) would have been defined as having taught the course with fidelity."

"If, instead, the criterion had been defined as teaching at least five activities in a given module, irrespective of the strand — an approach that would provide a better indication of the number of teachers who completed 8 modules over the course of the year — 35 teachers (62.5 percent) would have been defined as having taught the course with fidelity."
