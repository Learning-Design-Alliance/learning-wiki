# Dynamically generated natural-language feedback was not demonstrated to be superior to a non-NLP feedback condition that told students the answer

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The article reports being unable to demonstrate that dynamically generated (ELICIT) feedback produced better outcomes than a TELL condition that immediately gave the correct answer without analysis. [→ Dzikovska 2014](#dzikovska-2014)

## Evidence

### Dzikovska 2014

Dzikovska, M., Steinhauser, N., Farrow, E., Moore, J., & Campbell, G. (2014). BEETLE II: Deep Natural Language Understanding and Automatic Feedback Generation for Intelligent Tutoring in Basic Electricity and Electronics. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 24, 284–332. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0017-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

System evaluation in which participants were randomly assigned to the ELICIT version (adaptive natural language feedback) or the TELL version (neutral acknowledgment plus correct answer); the only difference was feedback type. The article reports it was "unable to d emonstrate" the dynamic feedback's superiority. No effect size for this contrast is printed in the available text.

> "However, we are unable to d emonstrate that dynamically generated feedback is superior to a non-NLP feedback condition."
