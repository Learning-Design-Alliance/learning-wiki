## Evidence

### Cacey S. Stevens 2015

Cacey S. Stevens, Michael Marder, and Sidney R. Nagel. (2015). Patterns in Illinois Educational School Data. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Time-series analysis of aggregate Math PSAE passing rates for Chicago charter and neighborhood 11th graders, 2005-2013 (Fig. 6, with per-year counts of schools and students tested). Passing rates for both remained about 18% to 33%, compared to about 60% in other Illinois communities.

> "Up to 2008, charter schools consistently performed at the same level as other high schools in the percent of students passing the exam. However, in the last several years, there has been a shift in performance; between 25% and 30% of charter students passed the exam each year since 2009."

### Cacey S. Stevens 2015 (2)

Cacey S. Stevens, Michael Marder, and Sidney R. Nagel. (2015). Patterns in Illinois Educational School Data. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.02057)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fit slopes of annual passing rates shown in Fig. 6, determined from the slopes of each fit. The authors note the 2008-2009 growth of charter 11th graders by 65% during the Renaissance 2010 school closures may have contributed to the apparent 2009 shift.

> "Charter school scores have increased by about 0.8% per year from 2009 to 2013. Although neighborhood high schools in Chicago have improved by about 1.5% each year, the traditional programs have not reached the performance of charter schools."
