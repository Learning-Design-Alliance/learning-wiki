## Evidence

### Libertus Melissa E. 2013

Libertus Melissa E., Feigenson Lisa, Halberda Justin. (2013). Is Approximate Number Precision a Stable Predictor of Math Ability? Learning and Individual Differences. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2013.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of ANS acuity task performance (Results §3.1) in the preschool sample; trials were drawn from four ratio bins (1:2, 2:3, 3:4, 6:7), and "children's accuracy decreased as the numerical ratio increased" as predicted by Weber's Law.

> "As predicted by Weber's Law, children's accuracy decreased as the numerical ratio increased (i.e., as the ratio of blue to yellow dots approached equality)."

### Libertus Melissa E. 2013 (2)

Libertus Melissa E., Feigenson Lisa, Halberda Justin. (2013). Is Approximate Number Precision a Stable Predictor of Math Ability? Learning and Individual Differences. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2013.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of ANS task performance across the two testing times (Table 2 shows mean accuracy rising from 65.10% to 76.10% and mean RT falling from 3205.13 ms to 2534.10 ms); the article states "children's average accuracy increased and RT decreased between Time 1 and Time 2."

> "As expected, children's average accuracy increased and RT decreased between Time 1 and Time 2"
