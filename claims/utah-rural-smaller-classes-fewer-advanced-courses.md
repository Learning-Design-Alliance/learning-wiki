# Rural Utah districts had smaller average class sizes and student-to-teacher ratios, but far fewer unique advanced courses per secondary school, than non-rural districts from 2012 to 2017

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In elementary and secondary schools, rural districts had smaller average class sizes compared to non-rural districts. [→ Rice 2018](#rice-2018)
`q2 i?` Each year, the average number of unique advanced courses offered in non-rural secondary schools offering any was greater than in rural ones (4.4 vs. 16.2 in 2015). [→ Rice 2018 (2)](#rice-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Rice 2018

Rice, J., Huang, M., & Derby, K. (2018). Characteristics of rural and non-rural districts in Utah from 2012 to 2017. San Francisco, CA: REL West @ WestEd. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs/regions/west

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of USBE data (table 2) for 18 rural and 23 non-rural districts. For example, average median elementary class size was 20.5 in rural versus 24.4 in non-rural districts in 2012; the report states "rural districts had smaller average class sizes compared to non-rural districts."

> "In elementary and secondary schools, rural districts had smaller average class sizes compared to non-rural districts (table 2)."

### Rice 2018 (2)

Rice, J., Huang, M., & Derby, K. (2018). Characteristics of rural and non-rural districts in Utah from 2012 to 2017. San Francisco, CA: REL West @ WestEd. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs/regions/west

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of advanced course offerings (Advanced Placement, International Baccalaureate, concurrent/dual enrollment) from USBE data. In 2015 rural schools offering any advanced courses averaged 4.4 unique courses versus 16.2 in non-rural districts.

> "For example, in 2015 ( the most recent year that data were available), across rural districts there was an average of 4.4 unique advanced courses available at each secondary school that offered any advanced courses. In contrast, in the same year, across non-rural districts there was an average of 16.2 unique advanced courses available at each secondary school that offered any such courses."
