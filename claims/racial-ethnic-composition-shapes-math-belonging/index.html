Evidence¶

Harris-Thomas 2026¶

Harris-Thomas, B., Nicolai, K. D., Chen, X.-Y., & Areizaga Barbieri, C. (2026). Examining Black and Latine Students' Sense of Belonging within the Mathematics Classroom: A Narrative Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10181-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review reporting Morales-Chicas and Graham (2021) on perceptions of incongruence between classroom and school racial/ethnic makeup. The review states students "are likely to report a lower sense of belonging" under mismatch, and greater belonging with more same-race peers in class than in school. No effect sizes printed.

"When the racial/ethnic diversity of math classrooms does not match the school's racial/ethnic makeup, students are likely to report a lower sense of belonging. Likewise, perceiving more same-race peers in class than in school is associated with a greater sense of belonging"

Harris-Thomas 2026 (2)¶

Harris-Thomas, B., Nicolai, K. D., Chen, X.-Y., & Areizaga Barbieri, C. (2026). Examining Black and Latine Students' Sense of Belonging within the Mathematics Classroom: A Narrative Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10181-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review reporting Graham et al.'s (2022) synthesis of four longitudinal studies following students from elementary school to ninth grade. The review states greater overall school diversity "served as a buffer against the negative effects of lower in-group representation in classrooms." No effect sizes printed.

"It is important to note that although balanced diversity across school settings would be ideal, greater diversity in the school overall served as a buffer against the negative effects of lower in-group representation in classrooms (Graham et al., 2022)."
