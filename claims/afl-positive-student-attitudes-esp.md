## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 84% of subjects agreed or strongly agreed that immediate feedback helps them discover mistakes early, and 88% agreed it reduces the tension and anxiety of final exams. [→ Al-Tayib Umar 2018](#al-tayib-umar-2018)
`q2 i?` Majorities also endorsed self-assessment (81% reported increased self-confidence and autonomy), peer assessment (82% reported teamwork benefits), and motivation (75%). [→ Al-Tayib Umar 2018 (2)](#al-tayib-umar-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Al-Tayib Umar 2018

Abdul Majeed Al-Tayib Umar. (2018). The Impact of Assessment for Learning on Students' Achievement in English for Specific Purposes: A Case Study of Pre-Medical Students at Khartoum University: Sudan. English Language Teaching, 11(2). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n2p15

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Questionnaire administered to the experimental group (n = 27) after the course, processed in SPSS 17.0; the largest endorsements were for immediate feedback (84%) and reduced final-exam tension (88%).

> "it is found that (84%) of the subjects agree or even strongly agree that immediate feedback helps them to know or discover their mistakes early enough."

### Al-Tayib Umar 2018 (2)

Abdul Majeed Al-Tayib Umar. (2018). The Impact of Assessment for Learning on Students' Achievement in English for Specific Purposes: A Case Study of Pre-Medical Students at Khartoum University: Sudan. English Language Teaching, 11(2). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n2p15

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same questionnaire data: 81% credited self-assessment with increased self-confidence and autonomy, 82% said peer assessment helped them practice team work, 75% said the approach motivated them, and 78% said it helped improve their English performance.

> "(81%) of the sample believe that self-assessment which is practiced during assessment for learning has increased their self-confidence and autonomy"
