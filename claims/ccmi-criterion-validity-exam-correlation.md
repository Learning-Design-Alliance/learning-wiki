## Evidence

### Marcos D. Caballero and Steven J. Pollock 2013

Marcos D. Caballero and Steven J. Pollock. (2013). Assessing Student Learning in Middle-Division Classical Mechanics/Math Methods. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.008](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.008)

`q3 · i3` · `design · r3`

Criterion validity analysis at CU comparing CCMI post-test scores to exam performance. Each class took three exams (two hour exams and a final), whose averages were z-scored to allow comparisons across instructors; the article reports r = 0.71 and that the linear model accounts for "50% of the variance in exam scores associated with CCMI scores." Similarly high correlations were observed on the CUE.

> "CCMI post-test scores were strongly correlated with these z-scored exam averages (r = 0.71, p < 0.05); a linear model can thus account for 50% of the variance in exam scores associated with CCMI scores."

### Marcos D. Caballero 2016

Marcos D. Caballero, Leanne Doughty, Anna M. Turnbull, Rachel E. Pepper, and Steven J. Pollock. (2016). Assessing Learning Outcomes in Middle-Division Classical Mechanics: The Colorado Classical Mechanics/Math Methods Instrument. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010118](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.13.010118)

`q3 · i3` · `design · r3`

Criterion-validity analysis of CM 1 courses at CU Boulder, where each class took three exams and exam averages were normalized to z-scores. The study reports "strongly correlated with these z-scored exam averages (r = 0.71)". The CCMI, unlike exams, does not affect students' grades.

> "CCMI post-test scores were strongly correlated with these z-scored exam averages (r = 0.71); a linear model can thus account for 50% of the variance in exam scores associated with CCMI scores."
