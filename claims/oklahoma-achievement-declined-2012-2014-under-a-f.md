# Oklahoma achievement declined rather than grew across 2012–2014 under the A–F letter grade system, with the largest drops in F schools

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Math and reading scores declined for students in each letter grade category from 2012–2014, with Non-FRL and FRL students in F schools showing the largest average drops. [→ Adams 2017](#adams-2017)

## Evidence

### Adams 2017

Adams, C. M., Ford, T. G., Forsyth, P. B., Ware, J. K., Olsen, J. J., Lepine, J. A., Sr., Barnes, L. B., Khojasteh, J., & Mwavita, M. (2017). Next generation accountability: A vision for improvement under ESSA. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/next-generation-accountability

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Three-year growth analysis of Oklahoma math and reading scale scores by 2011–12 school grade and FRL status (Figures 4–6) found declines across grade categories; the authors report F-school students had the largest average drops despite the greatest room for growth.

> "We do not find any meaningful gains in student achievement for 2012–2014. Instead of average “growth,” achievement has declined for students in each letter grade category."
