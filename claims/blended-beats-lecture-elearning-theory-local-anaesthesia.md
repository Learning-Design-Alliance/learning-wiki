# Blended learning produces significantly better theoretical knowledge gains than face-to-face lecture or e-learning alone in teaching local anaesthesia

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomised three-group study, the blended group scored significantly higher on a 20-point multiple-choice theory test than both the lecture group (p = 0.013) and the e-learning group (p = 0.006). [→ Bock Anna 2021](#bock-anna-2021)

## Evidence

### Bock Anna 2021

Bock Anna, Kniha Kristian, Goloborodko Evgeny, Lemos Martin, Rittich Anne Barbara, Möhlhenrich Stephan Christian, Rafai Nicole, Hölzle Frank, Modabber Ali. (2021). Effectiveness of face-to-face, blended and e-learning in teaching the application of local anaesthesia: a randomised study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02569-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomised study of 37 dental students allocated to lecture (n = 12), e-learning (n = 12) and blended (n = 13) groups; a 20-question multiple-choice test followed the theory phase. The blended group's mean was 17 (SD 1.5) versus 14.8 (SD 2.3) and 14.7 (SD 2.2), and "the blended group showed significantly better results than the two other groups".

> "Comparing the test scores of all three groups, the blended group showed significantly better results than the two other groups (p = 0.013 and p = 0.006). There was no statistical significance between the results of the lecture group and the e-learning group (p = 0.863)."
