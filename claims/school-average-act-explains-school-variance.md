# School average ACT scores predict college graduation for students with the same individual HSGPA and ACT score, reducing between-school variance by 42 percent, while school poverty is not significant once average ACT is included

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The odds of graduating college increase by 60 percent for every standard deviation increase in school average ACT scores, for students with the same HSGPA and ACT score. [→ Allensworth 2019](#allensworth-2019)
`q2 i?` School average ACT reduces high school variation in graduation rates by 42 percent (0.324 vs 0.622); school poverty is not significant in that model because it is highly correlated with school average ACT (r=0.70). [→ Allensworth 2019 (2)](#allensworth-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2019

Allensworth, Elaine M., & Clark, Kallie. (2019). Are GPAs an Inconsistent Measure of College Readiness across High Schools? Examining Assumptions about Grades versus Standardized Test Scores. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/are-gpas-inconsistent-measure-achievement-across-high-schools-examining-assumptions

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Final hierarchical model adding school-level predictors (school average ACT, school poverty) to the combined student-level model (n=17,753). The article reports an unstandardized odds increase of 60 percent per standard deviation of school average ACT.

> "The odds of graduating college increase by 60 percent for every standard deviation increase in school average ACT scores, for students with the same HSGPA and ACT score."

### Allensworth 2019 (2)

Allensworth, Elaine M., & Clark, Kallie. (2019). Are GPAs an Inconsistent Measure of College Readiness across High Schools? Examining Assumptions about Grades versus Standardized Test Scores. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/are-gpas-inconsistent-measure-achievement-across-high-schools-examining-assumptions

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

School-level predictor models. The intercept variance falls from 0.622 to 0.324 when school average ACT is added; school poverty is not significant once average ACT is controlled, with a printed correlation of r=0.70 between the two.

> "Average ACT scores in the school reduce the high school variation in college graduation rates by 42 percent (0.324 vs. 0.622). The school poverty level is not significant in this model, but that is because it is highly correlated with school average ACT scores (r=0.70)."
