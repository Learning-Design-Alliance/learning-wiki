# Overlaying a grid onto the MIT-P display reifies the attentional anchor and abruptly shifts students into a new unit-based interaction routine

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Once the grid is introduced, the imaginary attentional anchor is materialized externally and students shift to iteratively raising the left hand 1 unit and the right hand 2 units to make green. [→ Dor Abrahamson & Raúl Sánchez–García 2015](#dor-abrahamson-raul-sanchezgarcia-2015)

## Evidence

### Dor Abrahamson & Raúl Sánchez–García 2015

Dor Abrahamson & Raúl Sánchez–García. (2015). A Call to Action: Towards an Ecological-Dynamics Theory of Mathematics Learning, Teaching, and Design. Proceedings of the 37th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. http://www.tinyurl.com/FreeMITP

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative micro-analysis of MIT-P interview episodes: after the grid overlay materialized the attentional anchor as a 'perceptually stable, externally present, deictically referable, bounded entity', students adopted the unit-based routine. No effect sizes reported.

> "Yet this frame of reference shifted students abruptly into a new interaction routine: raise the left hand 1 unit, then raise the right hand 2 units, iteratively, to make green."
