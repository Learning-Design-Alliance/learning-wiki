## Evidence

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Study 1 hierarchical linear modeling of check-in data across the six-week intervention found that “their focus improved across sessions” with b = 0.04, p < .001, when stabilizing for teacher, student, and session random effects.

> "Furthermore, their focus improved across sessions, with greater real-time focus as they completed more sessions over time, b = 0.04, SE = 0.004, t (1702) = 9.62, p < .001 (Fig. 2)."

### Michael D. Mrazek 2026 (2)

Michael D. Mrazek, Dustyn S. Ransom, Amy R. Texter, Calvin V. Tower, Bryce A. Rutila, Thomas F. Kennedy, Elijah J. Woodson, Jonathan W. Schooler, Alissa J. Mrazek. (2026). Theory-driven design and validation of a tool to enhance academic focus. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10730-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Study 2 replicated the across-session effect with b = 0.03, p < .001. Sensitivity analyses in both studies found the effect remained significant down to the first two sessions, suggesting focus “continued to improve with ongoing use of the tool.”

> "Furthermore, students’ real-time focus increased as they completed more sessions over time, b = 0.03, SE = 0.005, t (3045) = 6.16, p < .001 (Fig. 2)."
