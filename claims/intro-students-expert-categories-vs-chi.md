## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The percentage of introductory students selecting ramps or pulleys as categories was significantly less than in the Chi study, and expert-like categories (Newton's second law, energy principles, circular motion, linear kinematics) were selected by 18–51% of students even on the Chi problems. ## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Expertise in Introductory Physics Using Categorization Task. Originally published 1974. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.08781](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.08781)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of category choices in Table 3 across the study's groups and the Chi study's eight novices and experts. The article reports, for version II, 18% selecting Newton's second law for the 7 Chi-problems (22% for all), 31% energy principles (42% for all), 28% circular motion (29% for all), and 44% linear kinematics (51% for all), versus none of the Chi novices choosing those categories.

> "Table 3 shows that the percentage of introductory students in our study who selected “ramps” or “pulleys” as categories (based mainly upon the surface features of the problem rather than the physics principle required to solve the problem) is significantly less than in the Chi study."
