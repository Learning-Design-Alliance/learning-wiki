## Evidence

### Jens Möller 2024

Jens Möller. (2024). Ten Years of Dimensional Comparison Theory: On the Development of a Theory from Educational Psychology. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09918-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Gaspard et al. (2018) analyzed dimensional achievement comparison effects on self-concepts and task values across German, English, biology, physics, and math, finding "stronger evidence for dimensional comparisons in self-concept than in values" with facet-specific variation.

> "finding stronger evidence for dimensional comparisons in self-concept than in values. However, the results varied substantially across the value facets under investigation. Contrast effects were strongest for intrinsic value and costs, and lowest for utility value."

### Jens Möller 2024 (2)

Jens Möller. (2024). Ten Years of Dimensional Comparison Theory: On the Development of a Theory from Educational Psychology. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09918-6

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's summary of studies including mediation findings (Gniewosz et al., 2015; Schurtz et al., 2014; Dörendahl et al., 2021) in which negative cross-domain effects on intrinsic values, interest, and achievement goals were mediated through academic self-concepts.

> "we can assume that negative paths from achievement in one domain to STVs in another indicate that dimensional comparisons are significant, but smaller than those to self-concept of ability, and are often mediated by self-concept."
