Evidence¶

Sara Coemans 2017¶

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of reported strengths, situating findings in McKean's three types of transformation: personal, institutional and audience. Participants reported "feelings of pride and increased wellbeing and confidence after participation"; arts-based inquiry also offered a safe space for dialogue on sensitive issues.

"Hence, the use of arts-based methods led to empowerment in many studies. Several times, participants described a marked change following their participation."

Sara Coemans 2017 (2)¶

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Tolia-Kelly (2007), that arts-based data captured "alternative vocabularies and visual grammars" beyond oral interviews; Kaptani and Yuval-Davis (2008) are cited noting arts-based methods produced experiential and emotive moments where interviews produced chronological stories or normative perceptions.

"Tolia-Kelly (2007) argued for example that it captured “alternative vocabularies and visual grammars that are not always encountered or expressible in oral interviews” (p. 340)."

Sara Coemans 2017 (3)¶

Sara Coemans, Karin Hannes. (2017). Researchers Under the Spell of the Arts: Two Decades of Using Arts-Based Methods in Community-Based Inquiry with Vulnerable Populations. Educational Research Review. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2017.08.003

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's own critical assessment of the strength reports: because few articles specified outcomes or evaluation procedures, the authors "assume that many of these statements are perceptions of the authors instead of results from evaluations" and call for more evaluative studies.

"However, it was unclear whether these statements were actually informed by the data collected and by an evaluation of the process. Few articles in this database specified outcomes or evaluation procedures."
