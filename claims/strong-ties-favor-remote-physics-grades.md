# During remote teaching, strong friendship-based working ties positively predict physics grades while weak ties show no effect

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` In the exploratory regression, strong collaborative ties (among friends) positively predicted physics grades, whereas weak ties had a null effect. [→ Javier Pulgar 2022](#javier-pulgar-2022)

`q2 i?` Collaborative ties between friends (strong ties) positively predict physics performance (.27, p < .05), whereas weak ties between non-friends show a null effect. [→ Javier Pulgar 2021](#javier-pulgar-2021)

## Evidence

### Javier Pulgar 2022

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, Iván Sánchez. (2022). Long-term collaboration with strong friendship ties improves academic performance in remote and hybrid teaching modalities in high school physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

OLS regression model B from the 2020 exploratory analysis differentiated tie types: "model B shows a null effect for weak ties" while strong ties carried a positive coefficient of .22 (p < .05) for physics grades.

> "When differentiating the effects of types of collaboration, model B shows a null effect for weak ties, while having strong relationships indicates a posi- tive predictive value over physics performance (.22,p < . 05)."

### Javier Pulgar 2021

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, and Iván Sánchez. (2021). Student networks on online teaching due to COVID-19: Academic effects of strong friendship ties and perceived academic prestige in physics and mathematics courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.06245](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.06245)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression model B from the same OLS analysis of remote physics grades separated collaboration into strong (friend) and weak (non-friend) ties. Strong ties showed a "positive predictive value over physics performance (.27, p < . 05)"; weak ties were null. No standardized effect size printed.

> "model B shows a null effect for weak ties, while having strong relationships indicates a positive predictive value over physics performance (.27, p < . 05)."
