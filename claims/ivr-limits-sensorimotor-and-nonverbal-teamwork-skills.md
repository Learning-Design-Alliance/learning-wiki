## Evidence

### Sohlin AM 2025

Sohlin AM, Kjærgaard J, Hoffman IM, et al. (2025). Immersive virtual reality training: Addressing challenges and unlocking potentials. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15748

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From the strategies-in-action theme of the 10-interview qualitative study; debriefers recommended avoiding learning objectives related to sensorimotor skills and acknowledging modality limitations in pre-briefing and debriefing. No effect size reported.

> "the debriefers perceived the differences in navigating the virtual environment compared to the clinical environment, such as positioning in the room through teleportation and navigating with controllers, as a barrier to learning sensorimotor skills"

### Sohlin AM 2025 (2)

Sohlin AM, Kjærgaard J, Hoffman IM, et al. (2025). Immersive virtual reality training: Addressing challenges and unlocking potentials. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.15748

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same qualitative study; debriefers compensated by shifting focus to verbal communication and vocal prosody, and judged verbal communication goals such as transparent thinking and closed loop feasible to train and debrief in IVR. No effect size reported.

> "The lack of non‐verbal cues between learners wearing IVR headsets was also perceived as a barrier to training non‐verbal teamwork skills"
