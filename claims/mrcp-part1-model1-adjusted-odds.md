# After adjusting for sociodemographic covariates and medical school clustering, odds of passing MRCP Part 1 at first sitting are greater for men and graduate-entry candidates and lower with increasing age, for minority ethnic candidates and for gateway-year candidates

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Men had higher adjusted odds of passing than women (OR = 1.606, 95% CI 1.422-1.814), and odds fell with each year of age (OR = 0.873, 95% CI 0.845-0.902). [→ Johnston Peter W. 2025](#johnston-peter-w-2025)

## Evidence

### Johnston Peter W. 2025

Johnston Peter W., Vieira Rute, Cameron Isobel M., Kumwenda Ben, Walker Kim A., Cleland Jennifer A. (2025). Big data analysis: examination of the relationship between candidates' sociodemographic characteristics and performance in the UK's Membership of the Royal College of Physicians Part 1 examination. Advances in Health Sciences Education 30(1), 53-68. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10406-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Mixed-effects logistic regression (Model 1, N = 5365) with medical school as random effect, testing sociodemographic covariates against first-sitting pass/fail. The printed odds ratios are age OR = 0.873, minority ethnic OR = 0.613 and "it was greater for men (OR = 1.606)"; ethnicity, parental occupation and school type are named covariates.

> "Model 1: The odds of passing MRCP Part 1 at first sitting were significantly lower as age increased (OR = 0.873, 95% CI = 0.845, 0.902) and for those from a minority ethnic background (OR = 0.613, 95% CI 0.530, 0.708), while it was greater for men (OR = 1.606, 95% CI = 1.422, 1.814)"

### Johnston Peter W. 2025 (2)

Johnston Peter W., Vieira Rute, Cameron Isobel M., Kumwenda Ben, Walker Kim A., Cleland Jennifer A. (2025). Big data analysis: examination of the relationship between candidates' sociodemographic characteristics and performance in the UK's Membership of the Royal College of Physicians Part 1 examination. Advances in Health Sciences Education 30(1), 53-68. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10406-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same Model 1 regression, contrasting programme types against standard entry. Gateway candidates had lower odds (OR = 0.489) and graduate-entry candidates "had greater odds to pass at first sitting than those on a standard entry programme (OR = 1.442)"; the preliminary-year contrast was not significant (OR = 0.767, p = 0.541).

> "Students from a Gateway to Medicine programme had significant lower odds to pass MRCP Part 1 at first sitting than students from a standard entry programme (OR = 0.489, 95% CI = 0.265, 0.904) and those on a graduate entry programme had greater odds to pass at first sitting than those on a standard entry programme (OR = 1.442, 95% CI = 1.045, 1.990)."
