## Evidence

### Kellman 2009

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Bryan & Harter (1897), a study of telegraphic receiving in which characters received per minute plotted against weeks of practice showed a negatively accelerated curve reaching asymptote, then second and third curves.

> "With continued practice, however, many subjects produced a new learning curve, rising from the plateau of the first. And for some subjects, after even more practice, a third learning curve ultimately emerged. Each learning curve raised performance to substantially higher levels than before."

### Kellman 2009 (2)

Kellman, P. J., & Garrigan, P. (2009). Perceptual Learning and Human Expertise. Physics of Life Reviews, 6(2), 53-84. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2008.12.001

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports the cited test experiments confirming subjects' reports about which information was being processed at each stage, showing acquisition of new bases of response (discovery effects).

> "To test these introspective reports, Bryan & Harter presented learners in the second phase with sequences of letters that did not make words. Under these conditions, performance returned to the asymptotic level of the first learning curve. When the most advanced learners were presented with sequences of words that did not make messages, their performance returned to the asymptotic levels of the second learning curve."
