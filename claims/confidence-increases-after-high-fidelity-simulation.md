## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Confidence total scores rose from M = 26.59 (SD = 6.93) to M = 31.92 (SD = 7.25) after the first simulation and from M = 29.76 (SD = 5.81) to M = 32.51 (SD = 4.89) after the second, with paired t-tests significant at p < 0.001 and p < 0.01 respectively. [→ Yu Ji Hye 2021](#yu-ji-hye-2021)

## Evidence

### Yu Ji Hye 2021

Yu Ji Hye, Chang Hye Jin, Kim Soon Sun, Park Ji Eun, Chung Wou Young, Lee Su Kyung, Kim Miran, Lee Jang Hoon, Jung Yun Jung. (2021). Effects of high-fidelity simulation education on medical students' anxiety and confidence. PLoS ONE 16(5): e0251078. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0251078

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same 37-student pre-post analysis; confidence was measured with 11 reversely coded Cato-derived items (higher total = higher confidence). Paired t-tests: first session t = -5.22, p < 0.001; second t = -3.82, p < 0.01. The article reports "students felt more confident post-simulation" with printed means.

> "In both the first and second sessions, students felt more confident post-simulation (M = 31.92, SD = 7.25; M = 32.51, SD = 4.89) compared to pre-simulation (M = 26.59, SD = 6.93; M = 29.76, SD = 5.81)."
