# Message content mattered: loss framing and avoiding social pressure produced significantly larger test-score effects, ruling out experimenter-demand explanations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Framing staying in school in terms of avoiding losses increased standardized test scores by significantly more, driven by Portuguese scores. [→ Lichand 2024](#lichand-2024)
`q3 i?` Not resorting to social pressure increased test scores by a much larger magnitude, mainly in math, but only for students not exposed to the loss-framing variation (negative interaction). [→ Lichand 2024](#lichand-2024)

## Evidence

### Lichand 2024

Lichand, G., Christen, J., & Van Egeraat, E. (2024). Neglecting students’ socio-emotional skills magniﬁed learning losses during the pandemic. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00235-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Student-level cross-randomized content experiments within the treatment group (framing and social-pressure arms, classroom fixed effects). Loss-framing coefficient 0.175 on the summary measure (p<0.1) and 0.231 in Portuguese (p<0.05); No Social Pressure 0.238 (p<0.05); their interaction was negative (-0.253, p<0.1).
