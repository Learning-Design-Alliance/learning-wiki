# School level moderates the correlation non-linearly, with grades 7–12 showing the most negative correlation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` School level is a significant categorical moderator, with grades 7–12 showing a more negative correlation than grades 1–6 or higher education; mean age is not a significant predictor. [→ Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020](#martine-baars-lisette-wijnia-anique-de-bruin-fred-paas-2020)

## Evidence

### Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas 2020

Martine Baars & Lisette Wijnia & Anique de Bruin & Fred Paas. (2020). The Relation Between Students' Effort and Monitoring Judgments During Learning: A Meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09569-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Categorical moderator analysis (Table 1: grades 1–6 r = −.349; grades 7–12 r = −.553; higher education r = −.296) was significant, while meta-regression on mean age was not (b = .009, p = .086).

> "However, results from the moderator analysis with school level as a categorical variable showed that school level was a significant moderator of the relationship between effort and monitoring,Q(2) = 14.66,p = .001."
