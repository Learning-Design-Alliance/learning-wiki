# Within low-SES samples, quality of mother–child communication predicts children's later expressive language more strongly than input quantity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 60 low-income families, measures of communication quality (joint engagement, routines, fluency) accounted for more variance in expressive language at 36 months than maternal words per minute. [→ Schwab Jessica F. 2016](#schwab-jessica-f-2016)

## Evidence

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?`

The review reports Hirsh-Pasek and colleagues' longitudinal study of 60 low-income families, in which hierarchical regression showed quality measures at 24 months predicted expressive language at 36 months better than quantity. No effect sizes are printed.

> "within this low-SES sample, measures of the quality of mother–child communication more strongly predicted children’s expressive language abilities one year later than did the total number of words spoken by mothers."

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016 (2)

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?`

The review reports a longitudinal study of low-SES families between 14 and 36 months in which mothers varied significantly in amount and diversity of speech, and "diversity of maternal lexical input predicted vocabulary growth" while amount did not. No effect sizes are printed.

> "diversity of maternal lexical input predicted vocabulary growth, as did maternal literacy skills, but amount of maternal talk did not."
