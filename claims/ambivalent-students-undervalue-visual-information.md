# Ambivalent-profile students undervalued visual information and rigidly applied print-reading strategies, undermining their OMR comprehension

## Evidence

### Zhu 2026

Zhu, S., Yao, Y., Cheong, C. M., & Zhu, X. (2026). Latent motivational-emotional profiles and their effects on strategy use and performance in online multimodal reading tasks: A mixed-methods investigation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102386

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Qualitative content analysis of semi-structured group interviews with 27 selected students (13 boys, 14 girls) representing the three profiles; students reported glancing at images and believing the knowledge resides in the text.

> "G3 students consistently undervalued visual information, viewing images as secondary aids rather than integral to comprehension."

### Zhu 2026 (2)

Zhu, S., Yao, Y., Cheong, C. M., & Zhu, X. (2026). Latent motivational-emotional profiles and their effects on strategy use and performance in online multimodal reading tasks: A mixed-methods investigation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102386

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same interview analysis: conceptual rigidity combined with overconfidence hindered adjustment to multimodal reading demands, limiting comprehension and strategic flexibility despite frequent strategy use.

> "when students discovered that print reading strategies, such as locating topic sentences, could not fully apply to OMR texts, they did not adapt reflectively. Instead, they maintained overconfidence, insisting that "OMR is no different from print reading.""
