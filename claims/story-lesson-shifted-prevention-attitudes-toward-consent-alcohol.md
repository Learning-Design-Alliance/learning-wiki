## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Open-ended pre-lesson responses emphasized law enforcement and legal punishment, while post-lesson responses emphasized consent and avoiding heavy drinking. [→ Erbe 2016](#erbe-2016)

## Evidence

### Erbe 2016

Erbe, R. G., & Kearns, K. D. (2016). Using story structure for lesson design in teaching about sexual assault. The Health Educator, 48(2), 23-27. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1152994

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative thematic coding of open-ended responses from the same pilot pre/post instrument; pre-lesson responses predominantly called for more sexual assault penalties and punishments, while post-lesson responses focused on consent and alcohol.

> "In comparison, post-lesson responses focused on the importance of consent and avoiding drinking too much alcohol to prevent assaults."

### Erbe 2016 (2)

Erbe, R. G., & Kearns, K. D. (2016). Using story structure for lesson design in teaching about sexual assault. The Health Educator, 48(2), 23-27. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1152994

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Word association task (ten words per participant) counted and ordered from coded responses. Pre-lesson top words included rape (9.4%), forced (6,4%), crime (4.1%), violence (2.9%); post-lesson rape (8.5%), consent (6.8%), alcohol (3.4%), violence (2.8%).

> "Common words that were consistent in pre- and post-lesson responses included rape and violence, while common words that were different included forced and crime in the pre-lesson responses versus consent and alcohol in the post-lesson responses."
