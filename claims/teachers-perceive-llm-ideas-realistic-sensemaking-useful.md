## Evidence

### Nguyen 2026

Nguyen, H., & Cao, J. (2026). Exploring the Capacity of Large Language Models to Simulate Students' Scientific Thinking: Insights for Responsive Teaching. Journal of Science Education and Technology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10956-026-10333-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Thematic analysis of 45-minute semi-structured interviews with six teachers who evaluated GPT-4o-generated ideas against their own lesson plans. Example: Rylie reasoned about an incorrect roller-coaster energy idea, "This is incorrect and is important for me to think about."

> "Teachers stated that the responses were overall realistic and aligned with their lessons' objectives. They mentioned that the responses were most useful when they sparked teachers' sensemaking about what students knew and did not know."

### Nguyen 2026 (2)

Nguyen, H., & Cao, J. (2026). Exploring the Capacity of Large Language Models to Simulate Students' Scientific Thinking: Insights for Responsive Teaching. Journal of Science Education and Technology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10956-026-10333-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Teachers appreciated suggestions like asking for evidence, reframing questions, and providing counterexamples; John noted unexpected penguin examples in a bird-movement modeling lesson. Three teachers said the suggestions could save time and help anticipate student responses.

> "Additionally, all teachers found value in the LLM-generated instructional prompts to scaffold student ideas and surface unexpected insights."
