## Evidence

### Muhittin ÇALIŞKAN & Ali Murat SÜNBÜL 2011

Muhittin ÇALIŞKAN & Ali Murat SÜNBÜL. (2011). The Effects of Learning Strategies Instruction on Metacognitive Knowledge, Using Metacognitive Skills and Academic Achievement (Primary Education Sixth Grade Turkish Course Sample). Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 11(1), 148-153. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ919894

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative interview analysis using the Metacognitive Skills Standardized Open-Ended Interview Form after the 15-week intervention; the authors report the teaching "was effective in students' selecting and applying a strategy" at home.

> "it was understood that the teaching that was performed was effective in students' selecting and applying a strategy while they were studying at home, and that half of the students checked the result of the strategy that they selected and tried a new strategy."

### Muhittin ÇALIŞKAN & Ali Murat SÜNBÜL 2011 (2)

Muhittin ÇALIŞKAN & Ali Murat SÜNBÜL. (2011). The Effects of Learning Strategies Instruction on Metacognitive Knowledge, Using Metacognitive Skills and Academic Achievement (Primary Education Sixth Grade Turkish Course Sample). Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 11(1), 148-153. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ919894

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same interview analysis also found a limitation: "students did not possess knowledge" about which strategies fit different topics, qualifying the skill-use gains.

> "However, it was observed that students did not possess knowledge about what kind of strategies they can use to learn different topics."
