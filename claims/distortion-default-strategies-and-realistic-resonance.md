## Evidence

### Hsu 2026

Hsu, T.-C., Zhang, Z., Chen, Z., Liu, Y., & Liu, Y. (2026). Designing More Engaging Serious Games to Support Students' Mental Health: A Pilot Study Based on A CBT-Informed Design Framework. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of Exploring Motivation responses (Q1) from the 14 experimental-group participants, shown in a sunburst chart per level. The Perfectionism level's dominant first reaction was "collect all stars" (55%).

> "In the “Perfectionism” level, most participants first choose “collect all stars” (55%), reflecting an intuitive goal of equating “complete collection” with “success.”"

### Hsu 2026 (2)

Hsu, T.-C., Zhang, Z., Chen, Z., Liu, Y., & Liu, Y. (2026). Designing More Engaging Serious Games to Support Students' Mental Health: A Pilot Study Based on A CBT-Informed Design Framework. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15662)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Thematic analysis of Realistic Resonance responses (Q3) from the same 14 participants. Perfectionism-level associations drew mainly on "academic pressure, such as college entrance exam pressure (70%)"; other levels drew on interpersonal interpretation and social evaluation anxiety.

> "“Perfectionism” was more often associated with academic pressure, such as college entrance exam pressure (70%) and university final exam pressure (15%)."
