## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The probability of a tone's task-irrelevant frequency significantly impacts the speed of duration decisions, with RTs slower for low- than high-probability frequencies, graded by distance from the mode. [→ Luthra 2025](#luthra-2025)

## Evidence

### Luthra 2025

Luthra, S., Luor, A., Tierney, A. T., Dick, F., & Holt, L. L. (2025). Statistical learning dynamically shapes auditory perception. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00328-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Exp 2a-c (400-trial duration decisions, 50/90 ms tones equiprobable, frequency 80% vs 5% trials), tone frequency probability significantly affected RTs (η² = 0.017). Adjacent low-probability frequencies were slower than the mode (d = 0.387), and far frequencies slower still (d = 0.258).

> "Response times (RTs) are slower for tones with low, compared to high, probability frequencies (linear contrast, unpooled error, p = 1.628 × 10−17,C o h e n's d = 0.516)"
