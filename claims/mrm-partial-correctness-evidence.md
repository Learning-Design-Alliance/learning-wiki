## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2019

Trevor I. Smith, Suzanne W. Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Philip Eaton, Paul J. Kelly, Kyle J. Louis, Mitchell A. Nussenbaum, and Louis J. Remy. (2019). Developing a reasoning inventory for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Four-tiered correctness analysis (All Correct, Some Correct, Both, Only Incorrect) of the three MRM questions (Q9, Q12, Q15) shown in Fig. 3. Q9 had about 50% of students choosing some but not all correct responses; Q12 and Q15 each about 20%.

> "The fraction of All Correct responses to each question is consistent with Fig. 2(a) at about 25% for each question, but the distribution of partially correct answers shows that at least 75% of students are choosing one of the correct responses, even if they also choose an incorrect response."
