Evidence¶

Gold 2020¶

Gold, T. (2020). Pipeline and Retention of Teachers of Color: Systems and Structures Impeding Growth and Sustainability in the United States. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.org/2020/03/24/pipeline-and-retention-of-teachers-of-color-systems-and-structures-impeding-growth-and-sustainability-in-the-united-states/ doi:10.51388/20.500.12265/96

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative review citing Carver-Thomas & Darling-Hammond (2017) on turnover: "The turnover rate for teachers of color (TOC) is about 19 percent, compared to white teachers at 15 percent." No standardized effect size is printed, so the magnitude is reported as descriptive rates only.

"Teachers of color also leave at a higher rate than white teachers. The turnover rate for teachers of color (TOC) is about 19 percent, compared to white teachers at 15 percent (Carver-Thomas & Darling-Hammond, 2017)."

Davis Dixon 2019¶

Davis Dixon, Ashley Griffin, and Mark Teoh. (2019). If You Listen, We Will Stay: Why Teachers of Color Leave and How to Disrupt Teacher Turnover. The Education Trust & Teach Plus. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED603193 doi:10.3102/1445541

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

The report cites prior research (footnoted) for this workforce trend; the review reports that turnover rates diverged from similar rates in the late 1990s to "15 percent for White teachers and close to 19 percent for teachers of color."

"But by the early 2000s, teachers of color started leaving the classroom at higher rates than their White counterparts — 15 percent for White teachers and close to 19 percent for teachers of color."
