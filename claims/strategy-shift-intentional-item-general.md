## Evidence

### Haider and Frensch 2002

Hilde Haider & Peter A. Frensch. (2002). Why Aggregated Learning Follows the Power Law of Practice When Individual Learning Does Not: Comment on Rickard (1997, 1999), Delaney et al. (1998), and Palmeri (1999). Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.2.392

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment with 30 participants trained on the AVT for a varying number of blocks (maximum eight) followed by two transfer blocks of new structure-equivalent strings; shifts were detected online via latency declines exceeding 1,500 ms. The discriminant analysis "correctly categorized 25 of the 30 participants", which the authors read as evidence for an intentional strategy adoption.

> "A discriminant function analysis, with latency differences (second transfer block minus last practice block) as the independent variable and detected discontinuity as the grouping variable, correctly categorized 25 of the 30 participants."

### Haider and Frensch 2002 (2)

Hilde Haider & Peter A. Frensch. (2002). Why Aggregated Learning Follows the Power Law of Practice When Individual Learning Does Not: Comment on Rickard (1997, 1999), Delaney et al. (1998), and Palmeri (1999). Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.28.2.392

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same 30-participant transfer experiment: despite shorter practice phases (M = 6 vs. M = 8 practice blocks), participants with a detected shift were significantly faster by the end of practice than participants without one, so "speed of task performance by the end of training is at least partly predicted by the occurrence of a strategy shift".

> "the results suggest that in this experiment speed of task performance by the end of training is at least partly predicted by the occurrence of a strategy shift as operationalized by a sizable latency shift."
