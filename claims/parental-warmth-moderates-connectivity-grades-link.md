## Evidence

### Yang 2025

Yang, B., Zhou, Z., Chen, Y.-Y., Devakonda, V., Cai, T., Lee, T.-H., & Qu, Y. (2025). Parental warmth buffers the negative impact of weaker fronto‐striatal connectivity on early adolescents' academic achievement. Journal of Research on Adolescence, 35, e12949. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12949

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r3`

Moderation analysis in the ABCD longitudinal models testing FPN-striatum connectivity × parental warmth predicting T2 school grades, controlling for prior grades and demographics. The article reports the interaction "β = −.020, p = .043".

> "parental warmth moderated the longitudinal association between FPN‐striatum connectivity and school grades (interaction term: β = −.020, p = .043)."

### Yang 2025 (2)

Yang, B., Zhou, Z., Chen, Y.-Y., Devakonda, V., Cai, T., Lee, T.-H., & Qu, Y. (2025). Parental warmth buffers the negative impact of weaker fronto‐striatal connectivity on early adolescents' academic achievement. Journal of Research on Adolescence, 35, e12949. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12949

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r3`

Simple-slope probing (1 SD below and above the mean of warmth) of the connectivity-grades link, shown in Figure 4 Panel A. The article reports "β = .042, p = .002" at low warmth versus "β = .004, p = .732" at high warmth.

> "the longitudinal association between FPN‐striatum connectivity and school grades was significant when parental warmth was low (β = .042, p = .002), but not when parental warmth was high (β = .004, p = .732)."
