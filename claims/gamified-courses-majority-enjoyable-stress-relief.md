## Evidence

### Bajko et al. 2016

Robert Bajko, Jaigris Hodson, Katie Seaborn, Pamela Livingstone, Deborah Fels. (2016). Edugamifying Media Studies: Student Engagement, Enjoyment, and Interest in Two Multimedia and Social Media Undergraduate Classrooms. Information Systems Education Journal 14(6). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135365

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive overall-enjoyment ratings from the survey in both gamified courses: "72.5% rated their enjoyment of the class as either enjoyable or very enjoyable"; the article also prints 73% for ITM 445 and 91% for CMN 450. No inferential test reported.

> "Many students reported an increased overall enjoyment with ITM 445 and CMN 450, particularly when compared to other, non -gamified classes (72.5% rated their enjoyment of the class as either enjoyable or very enjoyable)."

### Bajko et al. 2016 (2)

Robert Bajko, Jaigris Hodson, Katie Seaborn, Pamela Livingstone, Deborah Fels. (2016). Edugamifying Media Studies: Student Engagement, Enjoyment, and Interest in Two Multimedia and Social Media Undergraduate Classrooms. Information Systems Education Journal 14(6). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135365

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Qualitative open-ended survey commentary: students reported the gaming elements "helped to relieve school related stress". This is self-reported perception from a subset of commenting students, not a measured stress outcome.

> "Students also commented on the fact that, unlike a traditional educational environment, they found that the gaming elements helped to relieve school related stress, rather than contributing to it,"
