# The parental involvement–achievement relationship is stronger for global achievement indicators (e.g., GPA) than for subject-specific indicators (e.g., math grade)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The relationship between parental involvement and academic achievement is stronger when achievement is measured by a global indicator such as GPA than by a subject-specific indicator such as a math grade. [→ Fan 2001](#fan-2001)

## Evidence

### Fan 2001

Fan, X., Chen, M. (2001). Parental Involvement and Students' Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1009048817385

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

A moderator analysis of achievement-measure type in the meta-analysis found the involvement–achievement relationship "is stronger when academic achievement is represented by a global indicator (e.g., GPA)" than a subject-specific one. No effect sizes by indicator type are printed.

> "In addition, the relationship is stronger when academic achievement is represented by a global indicator (e.g., GPA) than by a subject-specific indicator (e.g., math grade)."
