# Short-term (~100 min) character training rapidly enhances N170 amplitude to trained characters in the right hemisphere of preliterate 4–5-year-old Chinese children in the visual identification group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After four training sessions, visual identification training significantly increased right-hemisphere N170 amplitude to trained characters, while face and tool amplitudes did not change. [→ Zhao Pei 2015](#zhao-pei-2015)

## Evidence

### Zhao Pei 2015

Zhao Pei, Li Su, Zhao Jing, Gaspar Carl M., Weng Xuchu. (2015). Training by visual identification and writing leads to different visual word expertise N170 effects in preliterate Chinese children. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2015.09.002

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Mixed-design ERP ANOVA of 29 preliterate children randomly assigned to visual identification (VL) or free writing (FW) training; right-hemisphere (T6) analysis showed "the N170 amplitude of characters increased significantly after training" (p < 0.01) in the VL group only, with no significant change for faces or tools.

> "Post hoc test revealed that, in the VL group, the N170 amplitude of characters increased significantly after training compared to the before training (p < 0.01) and the N170 amplitudes of faces and tools did not change significantly in the Post-test (p > 0.10)."
