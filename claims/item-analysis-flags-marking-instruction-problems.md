# Item analysis flags items whose marking instructions mislead peer assessors

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On CA-2007 question Q7, peers were far more severe (p=0.74) than the teacher (p=0.59), traced to marking instructions that did not preserve independence of the marking scheme's parts; Q7 was excluded from the validity analysis. [→ Bouzidi 2009](#bouzidi-2009)

## Evidence

### Bouzidi 2009

Bouzidi, L., & Jaillet, A. (2009). Can Online Peer Assessment be Trusted?. Educational Technology & Society, 12(4), 257-268. https://www.ifets.info/

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item analysis of the CA-2007 exam (13 items, 94 sheets): difficulty indexes for Q7 diverged between peers and teacher, and the authors attribute this to "a poor indication of the marking instructions for this item", so Q7 was disregarded in the validity analysis.

> "the students marked the answers to question Q7 severely (p=0.74), whereas the teacher marked the answers to the same question with average severity (p=0.59). The difference is too large to be explained by chance alone."
