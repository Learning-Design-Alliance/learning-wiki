Cepeda 2006¶

Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354-380. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-analytic lag analyses binning accuracy differences and absolute accuracy by ISI and retention interval (Figures 3 and 6, Tables 3 and 5). Within-study analyses of studies spanning sub- and supraday ISIs (e.g., Cepeda et al., 2005; Gordon, 1925) support the same pattern.

"the results show a tendency for the greatest increases in final-test recall to be found at longer ISI differences, the longer the retention interval. The qualitative pattern that optimal ISI difference increases as retention interval increases is supported by quantitative analyses of the bin data"

  • In continuous recognition memory, the lag function is inverted U-shaped after a short retention interval and monotonically increasing after a long retention interval — related
  • Recent research identifies time intervals during which spaced study should occur to maximize memory retention — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In paired-associate paradigms, the lag function is decreasing at very short retention intervals, inverted U-shaped at slightly longer intervals, and monotonically increasing at long intervals — related
  • The testing effect is largest at a retention interval of 1–6 days (g = 0.82) and robust across all intervals — related
  • Eight-day review improved a five-week science test versus one-day review in a simulated university classroom — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
