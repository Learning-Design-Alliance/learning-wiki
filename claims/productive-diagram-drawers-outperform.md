# Students who drew productive diagrams significantly outperformed students who did not on both electrostatics problems, with large effect sizes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across both problems and all intervention conditions combined, students who drew a productive diagram significantly outperformed students who did not (both p < 0.001; effect sizes 0.84 for problem 1 and 0.91 for problem 2, large effects). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do students benefit from drawing productive diagrams themselves while solving introductory physics problems? The case of two electrostatics problems. Eur. J. Phys. 39 015703. https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/aa9038

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Comparison pooling all intervention groups on both problems (table 6: problem 1, 58 productive-diagram students averaging 8.3 versus 62 averaging 6.3; problem 2, 91 averaging 6.6 versus 27 averaging 4.1). Effect sizes printed as 0.84 and 0.91, described as large effects.
