# Training produced a non-significant 11% increase in STI tasks completed during unannounced standardized patient visits relative to control, with lecture up 26%, paper-based up 17% and computer-based down 13%

## Evidence

### Weaver Marcia R 2016

Weaver Marcia R, Pillay Erushka, Jed Suzanne L, de Kadt Julia, Galagan Sean, Gilvydis Jennifer, Marumo Eva, Mawandia Shreshth, Naidoo Evasen, Owens Tamara, Prongay Vickery, O'Malley Gabrielle. (2016). Three methods of delivering clinic-based training on syndromic management of sexually transmitted diseases in South Africa: a pilot study. Sex Transm Infect. https://doi.org/10.1136/sextrans-2015-052107

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-difference Poisson analysis of five STI tasks completed during unannounced standardized patient encounters in 40 cluster-randomized clinics; "there was an 11% increase in the number of STI tasks completed relative to the control arm".

> "When all training arms were combined, there was an 11% increase in the number of STI tasks completed relative to the control arm (RRR=1.11, 95% CI 0.67 to 1.84)"

### Weaver Marcia R 2016 (2)

Weaver Marcia R, Pillay Erushka, Jed Suzanne L, de Kadt Julia, Galagan Sean, Gilvydis Jennifer, Marumo Eva, Mawandia Shreshth, Naidoo Evasen, Owens Tamara, Prongay Vickery, O'Malley Gabrielle. (2016). Three methods of delivering clinic-based training on syndromic management of sexually transmitted diseases in South Africa: a pilot study. Sex Transm Infect. https://doi.org/10.1136/sextrans-2015-052107

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same analysis, arm-specific contrasts; computer-based training was associated with "a 13% decrease (RRR=0.87, 95% CI 0.40 to 1.90) in the computer arm". None of the effects were statistically significant.

> "there was a 26% increase (RRR=1.26, 95% CI 0.77 to 2.06) in STI tasks completed in the lecture arm relative to the control arm, a 17% increase (RRR=1.17, 95% CI 0.59 to 2.28) in the paper-based arm and a 13% decrease (RRR=0.87, 95% CI 0.40 to 1.90) in the computer arm"
