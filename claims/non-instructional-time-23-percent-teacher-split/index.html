q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observation-log analysis of over 1,000 instruction periods in language arts, mathematics, and social studies found a 23 percent non-instructional rate, "somewhat higher than 18 percent average found in comparably designed observation studies."

"On average, the rate of non-instructional time (time spent in management tasks or time doing nothing) in the observed elementary school classrooms was 23 percent. This is somewhat higher than 18 percent average found in comparably designed observation studies."

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The distribution of teacher non-instructional rates (Figure 2) splits observed teachers into an effective group averaging 14 percent and a less effective group averaging 30 percent non-instructional time.

"Nearly half of the teachers spend 20 percent or less of their classroom time in non-instructional activities. These CPS teachers appear to manage their classrooms quite effectively. The average rate of non-instruction of these effective teachers is 14 percent. The other half of CPS teachers seem much less effective. They have a group average of 30 percent."
