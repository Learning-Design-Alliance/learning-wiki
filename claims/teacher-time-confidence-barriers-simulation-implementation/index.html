The review reports, citing Yaman et al. (2024), teacher-stated barriers of time, curriculum alignment, and low confidence. It further cites Kebritchi et al. (2010) that teacher attitude and self-efficacy strongly influence whether games are implemented.

"Across K-12 game-based implementations, teachers state that insufficient prep and planning time, difficulty adapting experiences to required curriculum and/or standards, and low confidence with new technologies and pedagogies are all barriers to implementation (Yaman et al., 2024)."
