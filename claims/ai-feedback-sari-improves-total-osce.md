## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students receiving AI-generated feedback after each VP encounter achieved significantly higher total OSCE scores than controls (mean difference 0.70 points; d=0.74). [→ Alexander Borg 2026](#alexander-borg-2026)

## Evidence

### Alexander Borg 2026

Alexander Borg, Jonathan Schiött, William Ivegren, Cidem Gentline, Viking Huss, Anna Margareta Hugelius, Benjamin Jobs, Mini Ruiz, Samuel Edelbring, Carina Georg, Gabriel Skantze, Ioannis Parodis. (2026). AI-generated Feedback Following Social Robotic Virtual Patient Interactions and Medical Student Performance: Nonrandomized Quasi-Experimental Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/90368

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study of 115 sixth-semester medical students allocated by hospital site to feedback (n=61) or control (n=54); primary outcome analyzed with an independent samples t test after confirming normal distribution. The feedback group scored "mean 7.39, SD 0.86 vs mean 6.68, SD 1.04 points", a medium to large effect (Cohen d=0.74).

> "Students who received AI-generated feedback achieved significantly higher total OSCE scores compared with the control group (mean 7.39, SD 0.86 vs mean 6.68, SD 1.04 points), representing a mean difference of 0.70 points (95% CI 0.35-1.06; t113=3.96; P<.001)."
