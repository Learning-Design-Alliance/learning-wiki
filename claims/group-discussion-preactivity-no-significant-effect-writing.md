# Group discussion as a pre-activity task has no significant effect on the writing ability of Iranian intermediate EFL learners

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After 16 sessions, posttest writing scores of the group discussion group did not differ significantly from the control group (t=1.410, df=25, p=.171). [→ Khoshsima et al. 2016](#khoshsima-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Khoshsima et al. 2016

Hooshang Khoshsima, Esmail Zare-Behtash, Mahboobeh Khosravani. (2016). Examining the Impact of Novel Pre-activity Tasks on Macroskills: The Case of Group Discussion on Writing Ability. Advances in Language and Literary Studies 7(1). https://doi.org/10.7575/aiac.alls.v.7n.1p.148

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experiment with 27 intermediate Iranian EFL learners: 14 in the group discussion experimental group and 13 in the conventional-instruction control group, taught over 16 sessions with TOEFL writing pretests and posttests analyzed in SPSS. The independent samples t-test on posttests was not significant, "significant value is .171"; no effect size is printed.

> "In table 5, df= 25, t= 1.4, and significant value is .171 which is more than .05. As a result, group discussion pre -activity does not affect significantly on writing ability. So the null hypothesis is accepted."
