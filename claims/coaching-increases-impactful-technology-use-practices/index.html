Evidence¶

Mahsa Bakhshaei 2020¶

Mahsa Bakhshaei, Angela Hardy, Jason Ravitz, John Seylar. (2020). Instructional Coaching Holds Promise as a Method to Improve Instruction with Technology. SITE 2020 - Online, April 7-10, 2020. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/5465e54a-b290-4210-97fb-514a3aa13e21

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Year 2 teacher post-survey from the DLP, comparing coached and non-coached teachers on the six-indicator ITU index; the creativity example is limited to core academic subjects, with the article printing "effect sizes = . 4, p < .001" without a standardized metric, and 51% vs. 38% monthly creative use.

"Coached teachers also reported more frequent ITU practices to develop their student skills (effect sizes = . 4, p < .001). As an illustrative example of using technology for creativity (one of the six ITU indicators), when limiting analysis to teachers of “core academic” subjects (math, science, social studies, English, general subject), 51% of coached teachers said their students use technology at least monthly to “come up with different ideas to see how they work and then improve them” compared to 38% of non-coached teachers."
