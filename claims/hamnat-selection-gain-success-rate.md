# Selecting the top 25% of applicants by HAM-Nat-BPC is expected to raise the two-year success rate from 70.1% to about 83.1%

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Extrapolations for different selected proportions showed expected success rates rising from 70.1% to between 74.1% and 85.5%; at a 25% selected proportion using HAM-Nat-BPC the absolute gain was 13.0%, an effect size of h = .32. [→ Hissbach Johanna C 2011](#hissbach-johanna-c-2011)

## Evidence

### Hissbach Johanna C 2011

Hissbach Johanna C, Klusmann Dietrich, Hampe Wolfgang. (2011). Dimensionality and predictive validity of the HAM-Nat, a test of natural sciences for medical school admission. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-83

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Extrapolation from the cohort data (Table 4) over selected proportions of 100%, 75%, 50% and 25% for HAM-Nat-raw, -uni and -BPC. The article projects a gain of 26 successful students in a scenario selecting 200 of 800 applicants.

> "With the HAM-Nat-BPC and a selected proportion of ¼, an absolute gain of 13.0% in the proportion of successful applicants is expected and this corresponds to an effect size of h = .32"
