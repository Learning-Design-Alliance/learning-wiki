# Among university students, poorer calibration accuracy is accompanied by lower confidence in the performance estimate, independent of performance level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` University students' second-order judgements correlated negatively with calibration accuracy (r = -.197, p = .002): less confident students showed higher miscalibration. [→ Marloes L. Nederhand 2021](#marloes-l-nederhand-2021)

## Evidence

### Marloes L. Nederhand 2021

Marloes L. Nederhand, Huib K. Tabbers, Anique B.H. De Bruin, Remy M. J. P. Rikers. (2021). Metacognitive awareness as measured by second-order judgements among university and secondary school students. Metacognition and Learning, 16, 1-14. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09228-6

`q3 · i1`

Postdiction study of 251 second-year psychology students who estimated their educational psychology exam grade and rated confidence in the estimate on a five-point scale. Calibration was the absolute difference between estimated and actual grade; the correlation was "r = −.197, p = .002".

> "Results among university students showed a significant negative correlation between subjective confidence (SOJ) and calibration accuracy r = −.197, p = .002, confirming our hypothesis."
