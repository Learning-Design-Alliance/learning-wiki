# After rule learning, low flexibility learners show latency differences across probability conditions while high flexibility learners do not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i1`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Only the low flexibility group showed significantly different post-learning latencies across probability pairs (η²p = 0.343), responding faster to larger (2/3) than smaller (1/3) probability differences (Cohen's d = 0.24). [→ Feng X 2020](#feng-x-2020)

## Evidence

### Feng X 2020

Feng X, Perceval GJ, Feng W and Feng C (2020) High Cognitive Flexibility Learners Perform Better in Probabilistic Rule Learning. Front. Psychol. 11:415. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00415

`q3 · i3`

Simple-effects latency analysis in the coin-searching task: "only the low ﬂexibility group had signiﬁcantly diﬀerent latencies in the diﬀerent probability pairs" (F(3,32) = 5.571, p < 0.05, η²p = 0.343).

> "Simple eﬀects analysis of the four probability pairs for the two ﬂexibility groups showed that only the low ﬂexibility group had signiﬁcantly diﬀerent latencies in the diﬀerent probability pairs [F(3,32) = 5.571, p < 0.05, η2 p = 0.343]."

### Feng X 2020 (2)

Feng X, Perceval GJ, Feng W and Feng C (2020) High Cognitive Flexibility Learners Perform Better in Probabilistic Rule Learning. Front. Psychol. 11:415. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00415

`q3 · i1`

Paired t-test within the low flexibility group on post-learning latencies: 1/3 difference M = 498.20 ms vs 2/3 difference M = 419.97 ms, t(11) = 3.406, p < 0.05, Cohen's d = 0.24.

> "A paired sample t-test showed a signiﬁcant diﬀerence between 1/3 ( M = 498.20 ms, SD = 176.54 ms) and 2/3 ( M = 419.97 ms, SD = 135.34 ms) probability diﬀerences [ t(11) = 3.406, p < 0.05, Cohen’s d = 0.24]. After successful rule learning, the low ﬂexibility subjects responded faster to stimuli with a higher probability diﬀerence."
