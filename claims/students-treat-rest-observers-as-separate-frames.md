## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2001

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2001). Student understanding of time in special relativity: simultaneity and reference frames. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1371254

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The Seismologist question was administered to introductory and advanced students as examination questions, ungraded written questions, and interview tasks. "Only between 15% and 40% of the students answered correctly on the written questions." Even advanced students had difficulty.

> "Relatively few students at any level answered correctly about the time order of events in the frame of the assistant. Only between 15% and 40% of the students answered correctly on the written questions."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2001 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2001). Student understanding of time in special relativity: simultaneity and reference frames. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1371254

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Student responses to the Seismologist question revealed that "many students treated a reference frame as being local to the position of an observer." Some graduate students explicitly stated that observers at rest relative to one another are in different reference frames because they measure different signal reception times.

> "Many students treated a reference frame as being local to the position of an observer."
