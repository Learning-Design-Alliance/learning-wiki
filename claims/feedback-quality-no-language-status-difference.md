# Feedback quality did not differ by students' language status for either human evaluators or ChatGPT

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Feedback given to EO/IFEP students was not statistically different from feedback given to EL and RFEP students, for both AI and human raters (Scheffe-corrected ANOVAs). [→ Steiss 2023](#steiss-2023)

## Evidence

### Steiss 2023

Steiss, Jacob; Tate, Tamara; Graham, Steve; Cruz, Jazmin; Hebert, Michael; Wang, Jiali; Moon, Youngsun; Tseng, Waverly; Warschauer, Mark. (2023). Comparing the Quality of Human and ChatGPT Feedback on Students' Writing. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Comparing+the+Quality+of+Human+and+ChatGPT+Feedback+on+Students+Writing

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scheffe-corrected ANOVAs on coded feedback ratings for the blocked sample (50 ELs, 50 RFEP, 100 fluent speakers) found no statistically significant language-status differences "with respect to both the AI and the human raters."

> "Feedback given to students who were either initially fluent or English-only speakers (collectively, EO/IFEP) was not statistically different compared to that given to students classified as English learners (ELs) and reclassified fluent speakers (RFEP) (using Scheffe correction for multiple comparisons)."
