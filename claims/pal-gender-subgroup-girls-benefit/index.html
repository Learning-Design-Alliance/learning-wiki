Evidence¶

Miller 2015¶

Miller, S., Gildea, A., Sloan, S., & Thurston, A. (2015). Physically Active Lessons: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581164

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory sub-group analysis adding a Group-by-Gender interaction term to the literacy regression model in the pilot trial. The interaction "Beta (interaction term) = -0.18, p = 0.02" indicated better literacy outcomes for intervention girls (Figure 2).

"In relation to literacy, girls in the intervention group performed the best overall compared to girls in the control group and compared to boys overall (Beta (interaction term) = -0.18, p = 0.02) (Figure 2)."

Miller 2015 (2)¶

Miller, S., Gildea, A., Sloan, S., & Thurston, A. (2015). Physically Active Lessons: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581164

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Exploratory sub-group analysis of the numeracy outcome using the same Group-by-Gender interaction term. The numeracy interaction was "Beta (interaction term) = - 0.13, p=0.06", marginal and not statistically significant (Figure 3).

"With respect to numeracy, girls in the control group performed worst compared to girls in the intervention group and boys overall (Figure 3) (Beta (interaction term) = - 0.13, p=0.06)."
