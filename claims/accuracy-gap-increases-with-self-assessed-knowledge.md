## Evidence

### Dahlkemper 2023

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

ANOVA on the gap metric (sample solution minus mean of bot responses) with self-assessment as between-subjects factor, question 1. The article reports "a higher self-assessment score is correlated with a higher discrepancy in perceived scientiﬁc accu- racy" with η² = 0.14.

> "we observed a statistically signif- icant main eﬀect of the self-assessment score on the per- formance gap metric ( F (4, 91) = 3 .42,p = 0.007,η 2 = 0.14), i.e. a higher self-assessment score is correlated with a higher discrepancy in perceived scientiﬁc accu- racy between the sample solution and ChatGPT re- sponses."

### Dahlkemper 2023 (2)

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

For question 3 (fluid dynamics), the article reports that no relationship between self-assessment score and the gap metric was present; students' ratings were almost independent of self-assessment for this unknown topic.

> "However, in the third question, no such relationship was present."
