# Active children show higher self-regulation than inactive children, and children with under 2 h/day screen time show higher self-regulation than those with 2 h or more

## Evidence

### López-Gil 2020

López-Gil, J.F., Oriol-Granado, X., Izquierdo, M., Ramírez-Vélez, R., Fernández-Vergara, O., Olloquequi, J., & García-Hermoso, A. (2020). Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors and Their Association with Self-Regulation in Chilean Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17165676

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA comparison of self-regulation across physical activity categories (active vs inactive) in 1561 children aged 8–12, adjusted for age, sex, BMI z-score and sleep quality. Active children "had higher SR than inactive counterparts (p < 0.001)".

> "Children who are classified as active had higher SR than inactive counterparts (p < 0.001)."

### López-Gil 2020 (2)

López-Gil, J.F., Oriol-Granado, X., Izquierdo, M., Ramírez-Vélez, R., Fernández-Vergara, O., Olloquequi, J., & García-Hermoso, A. (2020). Healthy Lifestyle Behaviors and Their Association with Self-Regulation in Chilean Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17165676

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANCOVA comparison of self-regulation across screen time categories (less than 2 h/day vs 2 h/day or more) in the same adjusted sample. Children with "less than 2 hours per day of ST had higher SR" (p = 0.044).

> "Also, children who reported less than 2 hours per day of ST had higher SR than counterparts with 2 hours per day or more per day (p = 0.044)."
