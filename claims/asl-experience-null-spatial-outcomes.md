## Subclaims
`q2 i?` More experience with ASL was not associated with decreased MR commission error rates, spatial habits, or any of the three academic anxieties, contrary to the hypothesis that spatial language experience confers spatial-cognition benefits. [→ Pizzie 2025](#pizzie-2025)

## Evidence

### Pizzie 2025

Pizzie, R. G., Sortino, R. M., Kim, C. E.-Y., & Inghram, R. (2025). Math anxiety and science anxiety are associated with spatial cognition and STEM interest in deaf, hard of hearing, and hearing people. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00336-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Zero-order correlations across the full sample (N = 115) using the ASL-Summary measure found no significant associations with MR error rate, spatial habits, or anxieties. The article reports "more experience with ASL was not associated with decreased commission error rates on the MR Task".

> "more experience with ASL was not associated with decreased commission error rates on the MR Task, p > 0.05. Similarly, more ASL experience was not related to differences in spatial habits or visuospatial tendencies."
