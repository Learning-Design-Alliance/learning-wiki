# Cognitive offloading to AI during early learning can stifle development of stable knowledge and skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When effortful processing in early learning is substituted with external AI aid, development of knowledge and skills can be stifled, whereas later-stage offloading may free capacity for higher-level thinking. [→ S P 2026](#s-p-2026)

## Evidence

### S P 2026

S P, Joseph J, Jose M, S M A, N R and Joseph J (2026) Artificial intelligence as a cognitive partner: a developmental framework for human–AI co-regulation in learning. Front. Dev. Psychol. 4:1835258. https://doi.org/10.3389/fdpys.2026.1835258

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Conceptual analysis of cognitive offloading risks (Section 5.4). The authors argue the developmental outcome of offloading depends on timing and frequency of use: early substitution of effortful processing can stifle development, while later offloading "can enable people to employ higher level thinking."

> "Forming stable knowledge and skills during early years of learning is frequently accompanied by eﬀortful processing. When this eﬀort is substituted with the outside aid, the development can be stiﬂed"
