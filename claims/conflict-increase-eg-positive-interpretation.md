# The experimental group's conflict level increased by 6.4 points (20%), which the authors interpret as greater readiness to resist asocial actions rather than heightened interpersonal conflict

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the experimental group the average conflict score increased by 6.4 points (20%, from 20.3 to 13.9 on a scale where fewer points mean more conflict), while the control group's level practically did not change. [→ Nagovitsyn et al. 2018](#nagovitsyn-et-al-2018)
`q2 i?` Qualitative analysis indicated the conflict increase was due to attitude to violations of social norms, not personal mood, and no respondent reached the critical low-conflict level. [→ Nagovitsyn et al. 2018 (2)](#nagovitsyn-et-al-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Nagovitsyn et al. 2018

Roman S. Nagovitsyn, Dana K. Bartosh, Aleksandr Y. Ratsimor, Yuri G. Maksimov. (2018). Formation of Social Tolerance Among Future Teachers. European Journal of Contemporary Education, 7(4). https://doi.org/10.13187/ejced.2018.4.754

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Rogov 'Are you a conflict person?' test results (Table 5) in the 72-student experiment; EG moved from 20.3 to 13.9 points (Wilcoxon Temp = 1, p<0.01), CG from 18.9 to 19.5 (Temp = 160, p<0.01).

> "In the course of experimental work, it was found that in the EG the average level of conflict increased by 6.4 points, which is 20 %."

### Nagovitsyn et al. 2018 (2)

Roman S. Nagovitsyn, Dana K. Bartosh, Aleksandr Y. Ratsimor, Yuri G. Maksimov. (2018). Formation of Social Tolerance Among Future Teachers. European Journal of Contemporary Education, 7(4). https://doi.org/10.13187/ejced.2018.4.754

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Qualitative analysis of the conflict test reported by the authors; they state no respondent reached the critical low-conflict level and that moral qualities rose simultaneously, so the increase toward negative phenomena is read as a positive result.

> "the conflict has increased due to the attitude to violations of social norms, and not due to perso nal mood, living conditions"
