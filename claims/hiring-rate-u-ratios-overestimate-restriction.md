# Estimating U ratios from hiring rates overestimates range restriction, as shown by SAT data where the actual mean U of 1.2 contrasts with a hiring-rate-implied U of 1.54

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Converting company-reported hiring rates to U ratios rests on two strong assumptions (the predictor is the sole basis for selection; all offers are accepted) and overestimates restriction: SAT data show a mean U of 1.2 versus a hiring-rate-implied 1.54. [→ Sackett 2022](#sackett-2022)

## Evidence

### Sackett 2022

Sackett, P. R.; Zhang, C.; Berry, C. M.; and Lievens, F. (2022). Revisiting Meta-Analytic Estimates of Validity in Personnel Selection: Addressing Systematic Overcorrection for Restriction of Range. Journal of Applied Psychology, 107(11), 2040-2068. https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/6894

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Empirical illustration from Sackett, Sharpe, and Kuncel (2021) across 174 colleges and universities: computed SAT U ratios averaged 1.2 while the hiring-rate assumption implied 1.54. The authors judge the overestimation from hiring-rate-based U ratios likely large.

> "the assumption that all selectivity is “spent” on the predictor of interest (in this case, the SAT) would translate a 62% selection ratio to a U ratio of 1.54, suggesting much greater restriction than is actually present."
