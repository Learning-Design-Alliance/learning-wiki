## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Expert median GOALS scores were significantly higher than novice scores for nondominant-to-dominant peg transfer, simulated appendicectomy, and intracorporeal and extracorporeal knot tying on the fresh frozen cadaver model. [→ Sharma Mitesh MS 2012](#sharma-mitesh-ms-2012)

## Evidence

### Sharma Mitesh MS 2012

Sharma Mitesh MS, Macafee David MA MD, Pranesh Nagarajan MD, Horgan Alan F. MD. (2012). Construct Validity of Fresh Frozen Human Cadaver as a Training Model in Minimal Access Surgery. JSLS : Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/108680812X13462882735818

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Construct validity analysis comparing 10 novices and 2 experts using Mann-Whitney U tests on blinded video-scored GOALS ratings. The study reports "significantly higher" expert medians for four tasks, with printed P values of .006, .005, .001 and .033; no effect sizes are printed.

> "The median (IQR) performance scores of experts was significantly higher than those of novices for nondominant to dominant hand peg transfer [15(13) vs 12(2); P = .006]; simulated appendicectomy [18(3) vs 14(3); P = .005]; intracorporeal knot tying [15.5(5) vs 12(3); P = .001]; and extracorporeal knot tying [16.5(3.75) vs 14(3.25); P = .033]."
