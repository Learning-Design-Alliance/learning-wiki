## Evidence

### Lai 2025

Lai, S., Choi, J., Kim, J., Kunievsky, N., Potter, Y., & Evans, J. (2025). Biased AI improves human performance but reduces perceived helpfulness. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Nine-week field experiment in a University of Chicago graduate course (39 students, 312 student-weeks) with a Claude Code tutor whose working style was randomized in two weeks per student; grades blind to treatment. The unadjusted novelty advantage was marginal (adj. p = 0.093); template-volume reweighting raised it to Δ = 0.663 (p = 0.014).

> "Coding grades were highest under novelty-biased assistance, 6.1% above the default ([0.1%, 12.1%]; Δ = 0.541, g = 0.48, adj. p = 0.093), with a smaller, insignificant difference under reliability bias"
