# Recording the loop's direction as an arrow, not merely gesturing or drawing the loop, enabled Larry to simplify the Ampere's law integral

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r3` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Neither gesturing the loop orientation (hand for loop) nor drawing the loop (square as loop) let Larry figure out the relative orientation between the magnetic field and the unit length on the loop; adding an arrow for the loop direction did. Episode 3 case analysis of the oral exam: Larry realized unprompted that he needed to pick a loop direction, recorded it counter-clockwise, and then reasoned the unit length on the l legs was parallel to the magnetic field, obtaining 2Bl for the integral.

> "But the inclusion of arrow as vector to represent the direction on the loop helps Larry to continue with a valid argument."
