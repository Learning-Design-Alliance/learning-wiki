# Student-based instruction enhanced students' attitudes toward probability relative to teacher-based instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A greater percentage of students taught with student-based learning disagreed with negative attitude items and agreed with positive items about probability than students taught with teacher-based instruction. [→ Pale 2016](#pale-2016)

## Evidence

### Pale 2016

Joseph W. Pale. (2016). Teacher and Student Based Instructions on Probability Achievement Outcomes and Attitudes of Secondary School Students in Bungoma North, Kenya. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.7, No.24. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1112852

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of a 20-item probability attitude survey (10 positive, 10 negative Likert items, Cronbach's alpha 0.81) administered to both groups after six weeks of two-hour instruction sessions. Sample items show e.g. "Probability is interesting" mean 4.26 (student based) vs 2.0 (teacher based).

> "From table 3, it was found out that the percentage of students who agreed with the negative items from  “student based” learning method was much smaller as compared to the percentage of those who agreed with the same items from the “teacher based” method."
