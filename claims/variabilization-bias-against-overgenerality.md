# Identifier variabilization biases chunks against over-generality, and the resulting overspecialization has not caused problems in practice

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Variabilizing identifiers so that identical identifiers require equality and distinct identifiers require inequality assures chunks will not be overly general, though they may be overly specific. [→ Laird et al. 1986](#laird-et-al-1986)

## Evidence

### Laird et al. 1986

John E. Laird, Paul S. Rosenbloom, Allen Newell. (1986). Chunking in Soar: The Anatomy of a General Learning Mechanism. Machine Learning 1: 11-46. https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00116249

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The article's analysis of the identifier variabilization step of chunk creation, in which all identifiers are replaced by pattern-match variables while constants are left unchanged. The authors report that "In practice, these modifications have not led to overly specific chunks."

> "Biasing the generalization in these ways assures that the chunks will not be overly general (at least because of these modifications), but they may be overly specific. The only problem this causes is that additional chunks may need to be learned if the original ones suffer from overspecialization."
