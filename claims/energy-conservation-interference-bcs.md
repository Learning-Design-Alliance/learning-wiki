# Conservation-of-energy reasoning interferes with applying boundary conditions, with students equating wave amplitude with energy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students wrote |E0,in| = |E0,refl| + |E0,trans|, invoking energy conservation by replacing energy with wave amplitude, which hindered correct BC application. [→ Ryan 2017](#ryan-2017)
`q2 i?` One student showed interference from quantum mechanics, using probability reasoning to decide the sign between incoming and reflected fields. [→ Ryan 2017 (2)](#ryan-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Ryan 2017

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Observed in exam solutions and interviews: students arrived at |E0,in| = |E0,refl| + |E0,trans| with the justification 'This makes sense since the incident wave gets partially reﬂected and partially transmitted'.

> "Here in classical E&M context, some students treat energy and wave amplitude interchangable, which hinders them from applying BCs correctly."

### Ryan 2017 (2)

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interview observation of one student concurrently taking quantum mechanics, who used probability reasoning from that course when deciding the sign between fields, saying 'I'm also taking quantum mechanics and I remember those from my quantum class'.

> "When a student was trying to decide if there should be a plus or minus sign between ⃗Ein and ⃗Eref l, they wrote down: 1 = Prob (R) +Prob (T ) , stat- ing that the total probablity of reﬂection and transmis- sion should add up to be 1."
