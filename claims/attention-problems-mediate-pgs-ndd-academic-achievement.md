## Evidence

### Larose 2025

Larose, Marie‐Pier; Schuurmans, Isabel; Barker, Edward D.; Garcia Mondragon, Liliana; Tiemeier, Henning; Waldman, Irwin; Cecil, Charlotte. (2025). Associations between genetic predisposition to mental health problems and academic achievement: a developmental perspective using two population‐based cohorts. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.70043

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Structural equation modeling in the Generation R cohort (N = 2,992) tested indirect pathways from PGS-NDD to the Cito test at age 12 via maternal-reported symptoms; the authors report that "attention problems mediated the PGS‐NDD and academic achievement association at 12 years old". No standardized effect size was printed for the indirect effect.

> "we found that attention problems mediated the PGS‐NDD and academic achievement association at 12 years old (B indirect effect = −0.014, p < .001)"

### Larose 2025 (2)

Larose, Marie‐Pier; Schuurmans, Isabel; Barker, Edward D.; Garcia Mondragon, Liliana; Tiemeier, Henning; Waldman, Irwin; Cecil, Charlotte. (2025). Associations between genetic predisposition to mental health problems and academic achievement: a developmental perspective using two population‐based cohorts. Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.70043

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM in ALSPAC (N = 5,099) with ADHD symptoms (ages 7–13) mediating PGS-NDD effects on age-16 achievement; the final two-mediator model showed "academic achievement decreases by 0.011 standard deviations for every one standard deviation in PGS‐NDD via ADHD problems" (indirect effect p < .001, Table 4).

> "academic achievement decreases by 0.011 standard deviations for every one standard deviation in PGS‐NDD via ADHD problems"
