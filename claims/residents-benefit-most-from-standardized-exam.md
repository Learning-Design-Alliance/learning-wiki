## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Junior and senior residents showed significant before-after improvement in fracture probability ratings (e.g., midfacial fracture group p = 0.01 and p = 0.002). [→ van der Zaag P 2025](#van-der-zaag-p-2025)

## Evidence

### van der Zaag P 2025

van der Zaag P, Rozema R, Reininga IHF, van Minnen B. (2025). Standardized clinical examination of maxillofacial injury: fracture diagnostic accuracy across experience levels. European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00068-025-02927-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Intra-assessor repeated-measures analysis in the midfacial fracture group (n = 187): "Both the junior and senior residents showed a significant difference in their probability ratings before and after the clinical examination (p = 0.01 and p = 0.002, respectively)." Only p-values printed.

> "Both the junior and senior residents showed a significant difference in their probability ratings before and after the clinical examination (p = 0.01 and p = 0.002, respectively)."

### van der Zaag P 2025 (2)

van der Zaag P, Rozema R, Reininga IHF, van Minnen B. (2025). Standardized clinical examination of maxillofacial injury: fracture diagnostic accuracy across experience levels. European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00068-025-02927-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' observational note in the Discussion on interns in the midfacial fracture group: "the interns in training showed a decline in assessment accuracy with the standardized clinical examination, with the median probability rating decreasing from 7 to 5.5." Statistical testing was not feasible for interns in the mandibular group due to a single participant.

> "it is noteworthy that the interns in training showed a decline in assessment accuracy with the standardized clinical examination, with the median probability rating decreasing from 7 to 5.5."
