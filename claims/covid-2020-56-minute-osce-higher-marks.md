## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The 2020 OSCE had a higher total mean mark (62.95%) than the 2019 (59.35%) and 2021 (58.89%) OSCEs, with significantly more first-class honours marks in 2020 (11.5% vs 1% in each other year). [→ O'Malley A 2025](#omalley-a-2025)

## Evidence

### O'Malley A 2025

O'Malley A, Fitzgerald N, Moylett E, Gaffney G, McCarthy G, Murphy A.W., Geoghegan R, Hallahan B. (2025). A comparison of objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs) for medical students, modified during the COVID-19 pandemic. Irish Journal of Medical Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11845-025-03976-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ANOVA and chi-square comparison of anonymised results from three cohorts of fourth-year students (2019 n=200, 2020 n=200, 2021 n=205) sitting the ACS OSCE. The study reports "more first-class honours marks also attained in 2020 (11.5%)" against 1% in each other year; no effect size is printed.

> "the total mean mark was higher at the 2020 OSCE (62.95%) compared to the 2019 (59.35%) and 2021 (58.89%) OSCEs (F = 31.83, p < 0.001), with more first-class honours marks also attained in 2020 (11.5%) compared to 2019 and 2021 (both 1%) (p < 0.001)"
