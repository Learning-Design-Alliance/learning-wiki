## Evidence

### Li J 2026

Li J, Lin Z and Qiu C (2026) The personalization paradox: how AI-driven adaptive learning environments are associated with college students' academic emotions and self-regulation learning—a moderated mediation model. Front. Psychol. 17:1915839. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1915839

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent interaction modeling (Mplus XWITH) testing Hypothesis 3 in the 486-student sample. The article reports the enjoyment interaction as "positive and significant" and also reports significant negative interactions for anxiety (β = −0.11) and boredom (β = −0.13).

> "For enjoyment, the interaction was positive and significant ( β = 0.14, SE = 0.05, p < 0.01, 95% CI [0.04, 0.24]), indicating that higher AI literacy attenuated the negative impact of the environment on this emotion."

### Li J 2026 (2)

Li J, Lin Z and Qiu C (2026) The personalization paradox: how AI-driven adaptive learning environments are associated with college students' academic emotions and self-regulation learning—a moderated mediation model. Front. Psychol. 17:1915839. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1915839

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped moderated mediation indices (5,000 resamples) for the three emotion pathways. The article reports conditional indirect effects diminishing from low (−1 SD) to high (+1 SD) AI literacy, e.g., enjoyment pathway −0.12 at low versus −0.04 at high literacy.

> "The moderated mediation indices (Table 4) were significant for three pathways, with conditional indirect effects diminishing as AI literacy increased, which fully support Hypothesis 3."
