# Students receiving simulation-based education rate the teaching higher than traditionally taught students for learning interest, imaging thinking, teamwork, and communication skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On 10-point questionnaires, the SBE group rated the teaching significantly higher than the TBE group on learning interest (8.00 vs. 5.00), imaging thinking (8.00 vs. 5.00), teamwork (8.00 vs. 4.00), and communication skills (8.00 vs. 2.00). [→ Yu Huajun 2025](#yu-huajun-2025)

## Evidence

### Yu Huajun 2025

Yu Huajun, Zhao Jin, Su Lina, Zhang Jianjun. (2025). Application of simulation-based education in the instruction of medical students from non-radiology specialties. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07572-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Self-report questionnaire on a 10-point scale administered to both groups after teaching. All four rated domains favored SBE with all p < 0.001; no standardized effect size is printed for these contrasts in the quoted text.

> "Questionnaire results further demonstrated that students in the SBE group scored significantly higher than their counterparts in the TBE group in terms of increasing interest in learning (8.00 vs. 5.00, Z = -13.23, P < 0.001), developing imaging thinking (8.00 vs. 5.00, Z = -13.71, P < 0.001), enhancing teamwork (8.00 vs. 4.00, Z = -13.77, P < 0.001), and improving communication skills (8.00 vs. 2.00, Z = -13.86, P < 0.001)"
