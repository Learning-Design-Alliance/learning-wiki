# Participants who consciously notice a generation advantage after a first test tend to eliminate it on a second test

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1B, 17 of 31 participants reported noticing a generation advantage after the first test, and most of these tied or showed a read advantage on the second test, more than expected by chance. [→ deWinstanley 2004](#dewinstanley-2004)

## Evidence

### deWinstanley 2004

deWinstanley, P. A., & Bjork, E. L. (2004). Processing strategies and the generation effect: Implications for making a better reader. Memory & Cognition, 32(6), 945-955. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03196872

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Replication with 31 educational psychology students using different textbook paragraphs, plus a written question about what participants noticed after the first test. Responses were coded for noticing a generation advantage [chi-square(1) = 4.77, p < .05]; participants who did not comment showed no such pattern [chi-square(1) = 0.29, p < .50].

> "Of the 17 participants who had noticed the generation advantage, 13 either tied or showed a read advantage in their performance on the second test, which, according to a chi-square test is more than would be expected by chance"
