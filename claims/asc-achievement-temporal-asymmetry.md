# Effects of academic self-concept on achievement are lagged (lag1) whereas effects of achievement on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The directional ordering is asymmetric: self-concept effects on achievement emerge over time (lag1, not lag0), while achievement effects on self-concept are more contemporaneous (lag0, not lag1). [→ Marsh 2024](#marsh-2024)

## Evidence

### Marsh 2024

Marsh, H. W., Guo, J., Pekrun, R., Lüdtke, O., & Núñez-Regueiro, F. (2024). Cracking Chicken-Egg Conundrums: Juxtaposing Contemporaneous and Lagged Reciprocal Effects Models of Academic Self-Concept and Achievement's Directional Ordering. Educational Psychology Review, 36. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09887-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Reported result of the model taxonomy applied to the PALMA data (five annual waves, math self-concept from six items, achievement from school grades). The article states the asymmetry verbatim; no test statistics or effect sizes are printed in the available text, so impact is uncoded.

> "effects of ASC on achievement evolved over time (lag1, not lag0 effects), whereas effects of achievement on ASC effects were more contemporaneous (lag0, not lag1 effects)."
