## Evidence

### Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl 2025

Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Exploring Subfield Interest Development in Undergraduate Physics Students through Social Cognitive Career Theory. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/cgcb-4k6z](https://doi.org/10.1103/cgcb-4k6z)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Comparative qualitative analysis (Research Question 2) of students interested in astrophysics versus biomedical physics found distinct learning-experience patterns: astrophysics captured attention before college via popular culture and via coursework, while "interest in biomedical physics was primarily influenced by engaging in research and practical experiences".

> "In contrast to astrophysics, biomedical physics was not commonly encountered through popular science media or high school or college coursework. Instead, interest in biomedical physics was primarily influenced by engaging in research and practical experiences within the medical field."

### Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl 2025 (2)

Dina Zohrabi Alaee and Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Exploring Subfield Interest Development in Undergraduate Physics Students through Social Cognitive Career Theory. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/cgcb-4k6z](https://doi.org/10.1103/cgcb-4k6z)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Comparison of outcome expectations between the two subfields found astrophysics students driven by large-scale research and discovery and confidence in finding good jobs, while "students pursuing careers in biomedical physics prioritized making a positive impact on others and society".

> "In contrast, students pursuing careers in biomedical physics prioritized making a positive impact on others and society as a desired outcome expectation."
