# IDSE-exposed interns showed statistically significant pre-post improvements in five of seven interdisciplinary attitude areas on a 10-point Likert survey

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` IDSE interns improved significantly in comfort learning with other disciplines, perceived interdisciplinary work interactions, collegiality, views of other disciplines, and perceived respect, with no printed effect sizes. [→ Bullard Mark J. 2019](#bullard-mark-j-2019)

## Evidence

### Bullard Mark J. 2019

Bullard Mark J., Fox Sean M., Wares Catherine M., Heffner Alan C., Stephens Casey, Rossi Laura. (2019). Simulation-based interdisciplinary education improves intern attitudes and outlook toward colleagues in other disciplines. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1700-1

`q2 · i?`

Quasi-experimental pre-post survey of 60 IDSE interns (58 paired) using a novel 10-point Likert instrument; mean changes with p-values as printed, e.g. comfort "x̅ = 1.164; p < 0.0001". No standardized effect sizes were printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "there were statistically significant post-curricular increases in the positive perceptions of: 1) comfort level of learning with others from different disciplines [x̅ = 1.164; p < 0.0001], 2) extent to which different disciplines worked well with one another [x̅ = 0.691; p = 0.0069], 3) rating that learning together improved collegiality between medical disciplines [x̅ = 0.855; p = 0.0002], 4) views of other medical disciplines [x̅ = 0.764; p = 0.0031], 5) respect from other disciplines within the hospital system [x̅ = 0.436; p = 0.0312]"
