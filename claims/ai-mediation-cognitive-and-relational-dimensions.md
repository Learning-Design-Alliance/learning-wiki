# Participants in a probe study and co-design workshop perceived AI mediation in interdisciplinary student teams along both cognitive and relational dimensions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` University students from technical, business, and design backgrounds experienced AI mediation as cognitive support reorganizing fragmented discussion and as a relational buffer softening interpersonal pressure around disagreement. [→ themes-1](#themes-1)

## Evidence

### themes-1

Ching-Jung Cheng, Yu-Chan Chung, Bing-Chen Chiu, Yu-Hsuan Lin, and Jia-Wei Liao. 2026. Exploring AI-Supported Disciplinary Mediation in Student Project Teams' Text-Based Communication: A Probe-Grounded Co-Design Study. OzCHI '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07503

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Inductive thematic analysis of co-design workshop transcripts and written artifacts from 12 university students in four interdisciplinary groups who first completed a 15-minute Discord probe session; the analysis yielded two overarching themes, one of which was that beyond cognitive organization, participants "valuedSpritz for softening the interpersonal pressure surrounding disagreement". No effect sizes are reported.
