# For elementary children above the fifth grade, the written group vocabulary test is as accurate a chronological-age measure as the oral test

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the elementary group, the written test matched the oral test as a measure of chronological age in all cases except one eighth-grade pupil, and the written index agreed with the school record in that case. [→ Weeks 1919](#weeks-1919)

## Evidence

### Weeks 1919

Weeks, A. L. (1919). Terman Vocabulary as a Group Test. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A written-then-oral comparison in 35 elementary children (grades 3-8, average age 9.8 years). The single disagreement was a nearly sixteen-year-old eighth grader; there the written index agreed with the school record.

> "When the vocabulary ages obtained in the tests were compared with the chronological ages, the written test was found to be as accurate a measure of chronological age as the oral test in all cases except one."

### Weeks 1919 (2)

Weeks, A. L. (1919). Terman Vocabulary as a Group Test. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Vocabulary-index table for the elementary group: oral test average 42.5, written test average 37.0, with a written-oral correlation printed as +0.899 and correlations with school standing of +0.230 (oral) and +0.345 (written).
