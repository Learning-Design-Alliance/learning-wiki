## Evidence

### Zhao J 2026

Zhao J, Yang K, Gao H and Jiang G (2026). Generative artificial intelligence and the transformation of medical education: a scoping review. Front. Educ. 11:1907589. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1907589

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Wang et al. and Borg et al., that controlled simulation studies showed short-term gains; "Several controlled studies reported short-term objective gains" in this 47-report category.

> "A GPT history-taking trial reported a large post-test difference and lower anxiety (Wang et al., 2025), while other trials found improvements in gynecologic history taking, communication domains, or OSCE performance (Borg et al., 2026a; Gao et al., 2026b)."

### Zhao J 2026 (2)

Zhao J, Yang K, Gao H and Jiang G (2026). Generative artificial intelligence and the transformation of medical education: a scoping review. Front. Educ. 11:1907589. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1907589

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports countervailing findings in the same category, including that automated total scores approximating faculty ratings showed weaker agreement for communication than information-gathering domains.

> "However, positive findings were not universal. Some single-arm implementations measured confidence or usability without objective change. Human or conventional cases were sometimes rated as more educational or authentic than AI cases (Fąferek et al., 2025; Karampinis et al., 2025; Sheth et al., 2025)."
