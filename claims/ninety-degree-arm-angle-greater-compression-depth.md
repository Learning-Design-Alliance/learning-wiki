## Evidence

### Mayrand Kelsey P. 2015

Mayrand Kelsey P., Fischer Eric J., Ten Eyck Raymond P. (2015). A Simulation-based Randomized Controlled Study of Factors Influencing Chest Compression Depth. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.28167

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary endpoint analysis of the same 55-subject randomized mannequin simulation, with arm angle rated from video screen shots by two blinded investigators (Cohen's kappa 0.87). The 90° group mean was "41.4 (95% CI [37.5–45.2])mm" versus 32.2 mm, p<0.003.

> "The group of 29 subjects (18 from the intervention group and 11 from the control group) forming a 90° angle between their arms and the mannequin’s chest wall achieved a mean compression depth of 41.4 (95% CI [37.5–45.2])mm compared to 32.2 (95% CI [28.6–35.8])mm achieved by the 26 subjects in the group compressing at an angle less than 90° (p<0.003)"

### Mayrand Kelsey P. 2015 (2)

Mayrand Kelsey P., Fischer Eric J., Ten Eyck Raymond P. (2015). A Simulation-based Randomized Controlled Study of Factors Influencing Chest Compression Depth. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.28167

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive post hoc observation from the same simulation study: among the 11 subjects reaching the recommended ≥50mm mean depth, 10 had achieved a 90° arm angle, supporting the association between shoulder position and adequate depth.

> "However, of the 11 subjects attaining a mean compression depth of ≥50mm, 10 achieved a 90° angle."
