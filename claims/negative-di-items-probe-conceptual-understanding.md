## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Questions with very low or negative DI and correspondingly low Diff-I were deliberately designed to probe conceptual understanding, and conventional analysis would judge them poor quality. [→ Joshi 2019](#joshi-2019)

## Evidence

### Joshi 2019

Joshi, P.K., Jain, Y., Khunyakari, R. and Basu, S. (2019). A novel alternative to analyzing multiple choice questions via discrimination index. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

While engaging with an Olympiad screening dataset, the authors observed that questions with very low or negative DI also had low Diff-I, and that "The questions with a negative value were those that probed conceptual understanding," designed with deliberate intent to probe conceptual grasp.

> "The questions with a negative value were those that probed conceptual understanding. In other words, the item was designed with a deliberate intent to bring out the conceptual understanding or grasp of the respondent."
