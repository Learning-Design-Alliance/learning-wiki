## Evidence

### Vanessa Ferdinand 2019

Vanessa Ferdinand, Simon Kirby, Kenny Smith. (2019). The cognitive roots of regularization in language. Preprint submitted to Cognition. [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2018.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.12.002)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Participants were classified as regularizers, probability matchers, or variabilizers against simulated probability-matching confidence intervals; e.g., 42 (66%) regularizers in words6 vs 30 (47%) in marbles6. The printed χ2 statistics appear in the quote.

> "There is a signiﬁcant eﬀect of cognitive load ( χ2(2) = 151.63,p < .001) and domain ( χ2(2) = 31 .49,p < .001) on the distribution of frequency learning strategies, mean- ing that the experimental manipulations elicit diﬀerent frequency learning strategies from participants."

### Vanessa Ferdinand 2019 (2)

Vanessa Ferdinand, Simon Kirby, Kenny Smith. (2019). The cognitive roots of regularization in language. Preprint submitted to Cognition. [doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2018.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2018.12.002)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Among high-load participants, 22 produced a maximally regular set in words6 versus 6 in marbles6. The paper states "Participants are more likely to maximally regularize in the linguistic condition" with χ2(1) = 10.2857, p = .001.

> "Participants are more likely to maximally regularize in the linguistic condition (χ2(1) = 10.2857,p = .001)."
