## Evidence

### Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman 2024

Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman. (2024). Biomedical students’ self-efficacy and academic performance by gender in a flipped learning haematology course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05421-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the gender subgroup analysis of the quasi-experiment, female students' self-efficacy increase differed significantly between intervention and control groups. A linear model with an interaction term estimated the intervention coefficient at 4.945 (SE 1.108) and the intervention-by-male interaction at −1.68, "not significant possibly due to the small sample sizes (t82 = 1.11, p =.27)".

> "For the female students, the mean increase in self-efficacy was 5.42 in the intervention group (t18 = 7.46, p <.05) and 0.48 in the control group (t20 = 0.72, p =.48); the difference was significant (t38 = 5.03, p <.05)."

### Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman 2024 (2)

Algarni Abdulrahman Abdulrahman. (2024). Biomedical students’ self-efficacy and academic performance by gender in a flipped learning haematology course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05421-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same gender subgroup analysis found that male students in the flipped group also significantly increased self-efficacy relative to male controls. The overall F test for the self-efficacy model was significant (F3,82 = 11.02, p <.05), while the knowledge model F test was not (F3,82 = 1.45, p =.24). No effect size was printed for the subgroup contrast.

> "For the male students, the mean increase in self-efficacy was 3.18 in the intervention group (t21 = 4.12, p <.05) and − 0.083 in the control group (t23 = 0.10, p =.92); again, the difference was significant (t44 = 2.90, p =.0058)."
