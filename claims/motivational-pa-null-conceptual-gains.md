# Motivational pedagogical agents did not significantly improve posttest scores or error rates in an adaptive algebra system after controlling for pretest

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Condition (Agent vs. Non-Agent) had no significant effect on posttest scores or error rates after controlling for pretest scores, though pretest was a significant predictor of both. [→ Man Su 2025](#man-su-2025)

## Evidence

### Man Su 2025

Man Su, Belle Dang, Andy Nguyen & Tomohiro Nagashima. (2025). Choice-making in an adaptive learning system with motivational pedagogical agents. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00366-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A between-groups classroom comparison of 49 9th- and 10th-grade students in the Agent (n = 24) or Non-Agent (n = 25) condition, analyzed with ANCOVA. The condition effect was null on both outcomes "after controlling for pretest scores"; pretest itself significantly predicted posttest (p < 0.001) and error rates (p = 0.030).

> "ANCOVA results revealed that the intervention condition (Non-Agent vs. Agent) did not have a signi ﬁcant effect on either posttest scores ( p = 0.189) or error rates (p = 0.668) after controlling for pretest scores."
