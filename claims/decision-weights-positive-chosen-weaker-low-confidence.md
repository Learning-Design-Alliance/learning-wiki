# Reverse-correlation decision weights are positive for the chosen stimulus dimension and weaker on low-confidence trials across confidence-assessment methods

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across the three confidence-assessment conditions, stimulus information positively influenced choice for the selected dimension and negatively for the non-selected dimension, with a large selection main effect (generalized eta squared = 0.6). [→ Lorenz Weise 2022](#lorenz-weise-2022)
`q3 i1` Decision weights were smaller on low-confidence than high-confidence trials, a small confidence-level main effect (generalized eta squared = 0.02). [→ Lorenz Weise 2022](#lorenz-weise-2022)

## Evidence

### Lorenz Weise 2022

Lorenz Weise, Saskia D. Forster, Siegfried Gauggel. (2022). Reverse-correlation reveals internal error-corrections during information-seeking. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:321–335. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09286-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on time-averaged decision weights from 27 participants' reverse correlation analysis of the numerical comparison task. The article reports a selection main effect with effect size generalized eta squared (ɳG2) = 0.6 and a confidence-level main effect with ɳG2 = 0.02.

> "there were significant main effects for selection ( F(1, 26) = 112.88, p < 0.001, ɳG2 = 0.6) and confidence level (F(1, 26) = 9.99, p = 0.004, ɳG2 = 0.02)."
