# In a cross-sectional survey analysed with SEM, self-efficacy's direct path to learning engagement was non-significant, and its association with engagement ran indirectly through self-regulation, teaching presence and cognitive presence (statistical mediation, which cannot show cause)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The direct path from self-efficacy to learning engagement was non-significant (β = 0.056, ns), while the indirect paths were significant (β = 0.597, p < 0.05); these are SEM paths in cross-sectional survey data, so they describe associations, not causal effects. [→ Doo 2023](#doo-2023)

## Evidence

### Doo 2023

Doo, M. Y., Bonk, C. J., & Heo, H. (2023). Examinations of the relationships between self-efficacy, self-regulation, teaching, cognitive presences, and learning engagement during COVID-19. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10187-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

SEM results from the survey of 1435 undergraduates showed the direct path "β = 0.056, ns" while indirect effects were "β = 0.597, p < 0.05", which the authors read as full mediation through self-regulation, teaching presence, and cognitive presence. The survey was voluntary and taken at one time point at one Korean university, so the model shows a pattern of associations and cannot establish that self-efficacy causes engagement or that the mediators carry a causal effect.

> "Whereas the direct effects of self-efficacy on learning engagement were not significant (β = 0.056, ns), the indirect effects were significant (β = 0.597, p < 0.05). Therefore, the total effects of self-efficacy on learning engagement were significant (β = 0.654, p < 0.05)."
