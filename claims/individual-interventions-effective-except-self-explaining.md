# All seven situation-model interventions except self-explaining significantly improved relative metacomprehension accuracy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Moderator analysis showed significant positive average effects for rereading, delayed-keywords listing, delayed-summary writing, delayed-diagram completion, and setting a comprehension-test expectancy, a marginally significant effect for concept mapping (p = .097), and a non-significant effect for self-explaining. [→ Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer 2020](#anja-prinz-stefanie-golke-jorg-wittwer-2020)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer 2020

Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer. (2020). To What Extent Do Situation-Model-Approach Interventions Improve Relative Metacomprehension Accuracy? Meta-Analytic Insights. Educational Psychology Review (2020) 32:917–949. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09558-6

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis across the seven intervention categories (Table 2). The article states "The average effect for self-explaining was not significant" and concept mapping was marginally significant (p = .097).

> "the moderator analysis with intervention revealed a significant positive average effect of small-to-medium size for rereading and delayed-keywords listing, of medium size for delayed-summary writing, of medium-to-large size for delayed-diagram completion, and of large size for setting a comprehension-test expectancy"
