# Reading Recovery has potentially positive effects on comprehension, with mixed findings across two trials

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two RCTs reported findings in the comprehension domain—one statistically significant positive effect (vocabulary) and one indeterminate effect—with an average improvement index of +14 percentile points. [→ Reading Recovery® 2013](#reading-recovery-2013)

## Evidence

### Reading Recovery® 2013

Reading Recovery®. (2013). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report, Beginning Reading. Updated July 2013. http://www.readingrecovery.org

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

In the vocabulary development construct, the 1988 RCT showed a positive and statistically significant effect on the Reading Vocabulary subtest of the CTBS. Table 1 reports an average comprehension improvement index of "+14" (range +6 to +26) across two studies (145 students); no standardized effect size is printed, so impact is null.

> "Pinnell, DeFord, & Lyons (1988) found, and the WWC confirmed, a positive and statistically significant effect of Reading Recovery® on the Reading Vocabulary subtest of the CTBS."

### Reading Recovery® 2013 (2)

Reading Recovery®. (2013). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report, Beginning Reading. Updated July 2013. http://www.readingrecovery.org

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

A null reading comprehension result: the 2005 RCT found no statistically significant or substantively important effect on the Degrees of Reading Power Test; equivalence was not established, only no detected effect.

> "Schwartz (2005) reported neither a statistically significant nor a substantively important effect of Reading Recovery® on the Degrees of Reading Power Test."
