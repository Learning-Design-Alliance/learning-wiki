# Cognitive ability interacts with parental education in predicting self-reported grades at both timepoints, but the authors judge the effect size negligible and not practically relevant

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` At both the 2-year and 3-year follow-up, composite cognition and parental education interact (Cognition*Education OR 1.08, p < 0.05 at both timepoints), such that children with better cognitive ability benefit more from having well-educated parents. [→ Langensee 2024](#langensee-2024)
`q3 i0` The authors state the interaction effect size was small and negligible, so the study does not provide strong evidence for robust SES-grade associations as a function of cognitive ability. [→ Langensee 2024 (2)](#langensee-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Langensee 2024

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Ordinal regression models adding cognition-by-SES interactions to the ABCD sample (N = 5001). Tables 4–5 print Cognition*Education OR 1.08 (1.00, 1.16), p = 0.04 at 2-year and OR 1.08 (1.01, 1.16), p = 0.02 at 3-year follow-up; cognition-by-ITN and cognition-by-ADI interactions were not significant.

> "The results, shown in Tables 4 and 5, indicate that at both timepoints cognitive ability and parental education interact with one another in their effect on the odds of reporting higher grades in school."

### Langensee 2024 (2)

Langensee, L., Rumetshofer, T. & Mårtensson, J. (2024). Interplay of socioeconomic status, cognition, and school performance in the ABCD sample. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00233-x

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the same interaction result: "While the interaction was statistically significant, the size of the effect was small, making it hard to conceive of it as having practical relevance." The conclusion section calls the effect size negligible.

> "While the interaction was statistically significant, the size of the effect was small, making it hard to conceive of it as having practical relevance, even on a populational level."
