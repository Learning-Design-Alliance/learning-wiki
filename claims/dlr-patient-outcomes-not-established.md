# Direct effects of digital learning on patient outcomes are rarely studied and not consistently detected

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In six articles studying patient-level outcomes, two cluster-randomized trials of online training found reduced patient preference for antibiotics (relative risk 0.48 and, for combined interventions, 0.38), while three found no effects on prescribing errors. [→ Bakkum 2019](#bakkum-2019)

## Evidence

### Bakkum 2019

Bakkum, M.J., Tichelaar, J., Wellink, A., Richir, M.C. & van Agtmael, M.A. (2019). Digital Learning to Improve Safe and Effective Prescribing: A Systematic Review. Clinical Pharmacology and Therapeutics. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.1549

`q3 · i?`

Review synthesis of six articles reporting patient-level outcomes (Kirkpatrick level IV). A cluster-randomized trial of online shared decision-making training found "a relative risk of antibiotics being preferred by the patient of 0.48 (95% confidence interval 0.34–0.68)"; a combined CRP-test and communication-training trial reported RR 0.38 (95% CI 0.25–0.55).
