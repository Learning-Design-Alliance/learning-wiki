## Evidence

### Suppes 1963

Suppes, P., & Ginsberg, R. (1963). A fundamental property of all-or-none models, binomial distribution of responses prior to conditioning, with application to concept formation in children. Psychological Review, 70(2), 139-161. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/rev/70/2/139

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Preliminary discussion summarizing the binomial distribution tests across all experimental groups: nine of ten results were nonsignificant, with the single highly significant exception from a Geometric Forms subgroup.

> "The binomial goodness of fit tests were applied to 10 groups and of the 10 test results 9 were not significant and 1-from a subgroup of a children's concept formation experiment (Geometric Forms)-was again highly significant."

### Suppes 1963 (2)

Suppes, P., & Ginsberg, R. (1963). A fundamental property of all-or-none models, binomial distribution of responses prior to conditioning, with application to concept formation in children. Psychological Review, 70(2), 139-161. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/rev/70/2/139

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Identity of Sets experiment (48 first-grade children, 56-trial sessions): with maximum likelihood estimate p = .803, the binomial goodness-of-fit test on blocks of four trials yields a result that "supports ... the binomial assumption" at the .05 level.

> "result of the goodness of fit test method directly from the empirical (x 2 5.99, df 2, P = .05) supports data on the distribution with four the binomial assumption."

### Suppes 1963 (3)

Suppes, P., & Ginsberg, R. (1963). A fundamental property of all-or-none models, binomial distribution of responses prior to conditioning, with application to concept formation in children. Psychological Review, 70(2), 139-161. https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/rev/70/2/139

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Galanter-Bush T-maze reanalysis (21 and 19 rats, noncorrection procedure, 10-successive-correct criterion): both the binomial distribution test and the specific-sequences test on the combined data are nonsignificant, supporting the constant guessing assumption.

> "The statistical tests for binomial distribution of responses before the final error (x 2 = 2.24, df = 3, P > .50) and for specific sequences of errors and successes (x 2 = 9.35, df = 14, P > .80), estimated on the combined data of Experiments III and IV with 202 observations, are also nonsignificant."
