# Students were identified for special education after failing the promotion gate, not before taking it

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` First-time third, sixth, and eighth graders were no more likely to be classified eligible after the promotion policy than before, while identification of retained students jumped dramatically (third grade: 5.6 to 13.3 percent; sixth: 3.7 to 13.1; eighth: 1.7 to 4.2). [→ Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002](#shazia-rafiullah-miller-and-robert-m-gladden-2002)
`q2 i?` Identification of first-time gate-grade students showed no increase after the policy, supporting the interpretation that identification followed gate failure rather than preceding it. ## Evidence

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002

Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden. (2002). Changing Special Education Enrollments: Causes and Distribution Among Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-special-education-enrollments-causes-and-distribution-among-schools

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparative trend analysis of identification rates for first-time versus repeating gate-grade students (Figure 4). The report finds identification "after they were retained jumped dramatically" across third, sixth, and eighth grades between 1993–94 and 1999–00, partly due to Board encouragement to evaluate students retained twice or more.

> "the percent of students identified as having a disabil-ity after they were retained jumped dramatically between the 1993–94 and 1999–00 school years—from 5.6 percent to 13.3 in third grade, from 3.7 to 13.1 in sixth, and from 1.7 to 4.2 in eighth"

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden 2002 (2)

Shazia Rafiullah Miller and Robert M. Gladden. (2002). Changing Special Education Enrollments: Causes and Distribution Among Schools. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/changing-special-education-enrollments-causes-and-distribution-among-schools

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis contrasting first-time gate-grade enrollees before and after the policy (Figure 4). The report concludes "Analysis supports the latter explanation" — that identification occurred after gate failure and retention — and reports no printed effect size for the null comparison.

> "Students enrolled for the first time in the third, sixth, and eighth grades were no more likely to be classified as eligible for special education services after the pro-motion policy was implemented than before"
