## Evidence

### Rey 2011

Günter Daniel Rey, Florian Buchwald. (2011). The Expertise Reversal Effect: Cognitive Load and Motivational Explanations. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022243

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Transfer contrast from the same randomized experiment's Results section: the novice advantage on the 10-item transfer test is reported with t(50) = 2.27, p = .03, and "The effect size (Cohen's d) was 0.63 for transfer."

> "Novices who received additional information scored significantly, t(50) ⫽ 2.27, p ⫽ .03, higher on the transfer test (M ⫽ 8.15, SD ⫽ 1.99) than novices who did not receive additional information (M ⫽ 6.85, SD ⫽ 2.15)."

### Rey 2011 (2)

Günter Daniel Rey, Florian Buchwald. (2011). The Expertise Reversal Effect: Cognitive Load and Motivational Explanations. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022243

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Expert contrast on transfer from the same Results section: experts with additional text scored worse, t(50) = 2.30, p = .03, and "The effect size (Cohen's d) was 0.64 for transfer," reversing the novice pattern.

> "Contrary to novices, experts who received additional information scored significantly, t(50) ⫽ 2.30, p ⫽ .03, worse on the transfer test (M ⫽ 7.27, SD ⫽ 1.78) than those who did not receive additional information (M ⫽ 8.31, SD ⫽ 1.46)."
