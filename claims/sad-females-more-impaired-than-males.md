# Females with social anxiety disorder are more educationally impaired than males with social anxiety disorder at most educational levels, particularly starting a university degree

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Females with SAD had significantly lower odds than males with SAD across all educational levels except finishing postgraduate education, with the largest sex difference in starting a university degree (aOR 0.43 for females v. 0.54 for males). [→ Vilaplana-Pérez 2021](#vilaplana-perez-2021)
`q2 i?` For vocational eligibility, females with SAD had a lower adjusted odds ratio than males with SAD (0.28 v. 0.36). [→ Vilaplana-Pérez 2021 (2)](#vilaplana-perez-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Vilaplana-Pérez 2021

Vilaplana-Pérez, A., Pérez-Vigil, A., Sidorchuk, A., Brander, G., Isomura, K., Hesselmark, E., Kuja-Halkola, R., Larsson, H., Mataix-Cols, D., & Fernández de la Cruz, L. (2021). Much more than just shyness: the impact of social anxiety disorder on educational performance across the lifespan. Psychological Medicine, 51(5), 861–869. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291719003908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Sex-stratified adjusted logistic regressions (Table 2) within the register cohort. The article states females were significantly more impaired than males across all educational levels except finishing post-graduate education, printing "aOR for females = 0.43 (95% CI 0.40–0.45)" v. 0.54 for males.

> "The educational level presenting the largest sex difference was starting a university degree [aOR for females = 0.43 (95% CI 0.40–0.45) v. aOR for males = 0.54 (95% CI 0.51–0.58)]"

### Vilaplana-Pérez 2021 (2)

Vilaplana-Pérez, A., Pérez-Vigil, A., Sidorchuk, A., Brander, G., Isomura, K., Hesselmark, E., Kuja-Halkola, R., Larsson, H., Mataix-Cols, D., & Fernández de la Cruz, L. (2021). Much more than just shyness: the impact of social anxiety disorder on educational performance across the lifespan. Psychological Medicine, 51(5), 861–869. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291719003908

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Sex-stratified analysis of eligibility to access a vocational programme in the compulsory-education subcohort. The article prints "aOR for females = 0.28 (95% CI 0.26–0.30) v. aOR for males = 0.36 (95% CI 0.33–0.38)" and reports no significant sex difference for academic-programme eligibility.

> "females with SAD were significantly less likely to be eligible for a vocational programme, compared to males with SAD [aOR for females = 0.28 (95% CI 0.26–0.30) v. aOR for males = 0.36 (95% CI 0.33–0.38)]"
