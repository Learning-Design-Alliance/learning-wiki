# Nurses' self-reported comfort with ultrasound-guided PIV placement increased after asynchronous training, while comfort without ultrasound did not change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 15 nurses completing both surveys, comfort with PIV using US increased significantly pre- to post-training (p = 0.03, Friedman test), while comfort without US showed no change (p = 0.71). [→ Schott CK 2024](#schott-ck-2024)
`q2 i?` Among the 8 nurses who recorded PIV placements, comfort with US increased significantly (p = 0.02) with no change without US (p = 0.65). [→ Schott CK 2024 (2)](#schott-ck-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Schott CK 2024

Schott CK, Devore JA, Kelly MYB, Mayr FB. (2024). Asynchronous training for ultrasound-guided peripheral IV placement among critical care nurses. The Journal of Vascular Access. https://doi.org/10.1177/11297298221129675

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Kirkpatrick level 1 (Reaction) pre/post Likert surveys analyzed with Friedman tests among nurses contributing to both surveys. The same participants showed "no change in comfort level regarding PIV placement without US guidance" (p = 0.71).

> "there was a significant increase in comfort level with PIV using US from pre- to post-implementation (median pre = 4, IQR 3-5 vs post = 4, IQR 3-4; p = 0.03)"

### Schott CK 2024 (2)

Schott CK, Devore JA, Kelly MYB, Mayr FB. (2024). Asynchronous training for ultrasound-guided peripheral IV placement among critical care nurses. The Journal of Vascular Access. https://doi.org/10.1177/11297298221129675

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Friedman test on pre/post Likert comfort ratings for the subset of eight nurses who logged clinical PIV placements. Comfort placing PIV without US did not change in this subset (p = 0.65).

> "participants who recorded PIV placements and contributed to both pre and post implementation surveys (n = 8) demonstrated a significantly increased comfort level with use of US from pre- to post-timepoints (median pre = 4, IQR 3-5, post = 4.5, IQR 4-5; p = 0.02)"
