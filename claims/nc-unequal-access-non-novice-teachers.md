# Black and Latino students in North Carolina have less access to non-novice and certified teachers than their peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Schools serving the most Black students have higher percentages of novice teachers (10% vs. 4%) than schools serving the fewest Black students, and Black students are more often in schools with high percentages of novice and uncertified teachers. [→ EdTrust 2022](#edtrust-2022)
`q2 i?` Latino students similarly attend schools with higher percentages of novice teachers (8% vs. 6%) and are more often in schools with high percentages of uncertified teachers (51% vs. 44%). [→ EdTrust 2022 (2)](#edtrust-2022-2)

## Evidence

### EdTrust 2022

EdTrust. (2022). Educator Diversity State Profile: NORTH CAROLINA. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED622852

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison of schools serving the most versus fewest Black students: 10% versus 4% of students attend schools with high (≥20%) percentages of novice teachers, and 50% versus 44% attend schools with high (≥10%) percentages of uncertified teachers. Percent novice teachers in most-Black schools is 10% versus 4% in fewest-Black schools.

### EdTrust 2022 (2)

EdTrust. (2022). Educator Diversity State Profile: NORTH CAROLINA. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED622852

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive comparison for Latino students: 6% versus 5% attend schools with high (≥20%) novice-teacher percentages, and 51% versus 44% attend schools with high (≥10%) uncertified-teacher percentages. Schools serving the most Latino students have 8% novice teachers versus 6% in schools serving the fewest.
