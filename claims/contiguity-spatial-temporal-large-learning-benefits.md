# Presenting related material contiguously in space and time yields large learning benefits

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Contiguous presentation of related materials produced large learning benefits overall (g = 0.74). [→ Noetel 2022](#noetel-2022)
`q3 i2` Spatial contiguity (g = 0.63) and temporal contiguity (g = 0.78) each showed large significant benefits. [→ Noetel 2022 (2)](#noetel-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Noetel 2022

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Synthesis of three reviews of contiguity (integration); the pooled estimate from Ginns (2006) across 46 effect sizes showed large benefits of presenting materials contiguously (g = 0.74, CI [0.67, 0.82]).

> "Overall, there were large benefits of having materials presented contiguously (g = 0.74, 95% CI [0.67, 0.82], k = 46; Ginns, 2006)"

### Noetel 2022 (2)

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Harris, N. R., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2022). Multimedia design for learning: An overview of reviews with meta-meta-analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The larger review (Schroeder & Cenkci, 2018) found spatial contiguity significantly improved learning versus spatially distant designs (g = 0.63, k = 58); the article separately reports temporal contiguity moderation analyses showing strong benefits (g = 0.78, CI [0.64, 0.92], k = 13; Ginns, 2006).

> "The larger of these reviews found that spatial contiguity led to significant improvements in learning compared to spatially distant designs (g = 0.63, 95% CI [0.55, 0.71], k = 58; Schroeder & Cenkci, 2018)"
