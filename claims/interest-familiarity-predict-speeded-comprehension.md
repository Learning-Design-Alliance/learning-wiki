## Evidence

### Ashley Chen 2024

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 1's random intercept multilevel mixed-effects model with topic, speed, interest, familiarity and interactions as fixed effects (interest and familiarity centered within persons), both "interest and familiarity positively predicted" accuracy, each with p < 0.001 and /u1D6FD = 0.03.

> "In addition, both interest and familiarity positively predicted perfor -mance, F(1, 191) = 17.83, p < 0.001, /u1D6FD = 0.03 and F(1, 191) = 19.03, p < 0.001, /u1D6FD = 0.03, respectively."

### Ashley Chen 2024 (2)

Ashley Chen, Suchita E. Kumar, Rhea Varkhedi, Dillon H. Murphy. (2024). The Effect of Playback Speed and Distractions on the Comprehension of Audio and Audio-Visual Materials. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09917-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

The same multilevel model in Experiment 1 tested the speed × familiarity interaction on comprehension accuracy; the article reports it was significant, F(1, 192) = 6.74, p = 0.010, with the benefit of familiarity decreasing as speed increased.

> "The interaction between speed and familiarity was significant, F(1, 192) = 6.74, p = 0.010, /u1D6FD = -0.05, indicating that the benefit of familiarity on performance decreased as speed increased."
