Evidence¶

Hock 2012¶

Hock, H., & Isenberg, E. (2012). Methods for Accounting for Co-Teaching in Value-Added Models. Working Paper 6. Washington, DC: Mathematica Policy Research. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/methods-for-accounting-for-coteaching-in-valueadded-models

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The paper's comparative analysis of three co-teaching value-added methods concludes that "the latter two methods provide a more stable approach to estimating teacher effects on student achievement" than the Partial Credit Method. No effect sizes or test statistics are printed in the available text.

"This paper considers and compares three methods—Partial Credit Method, Teacher Team Method, and Full Roster Method—to estimate teacher effects. Based on the analysis, the authors conclude that the latter two methods provide a more stable approach to estimating teacher effects on student achievement."
