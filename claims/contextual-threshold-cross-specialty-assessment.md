## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Trainees were reluctant to accept that even basic procedural skills could be appropriately assessed by outsiders, and some suggested the strategy might suit medical students more than residents. [→ Burm Sarah 2020](#burm-sarah-2020)

## Evidence

### Burm Sarah 2020

Burm Sarah, Sebok-Syer Stefanie S., Van Koughnett Julie Ann, Watling Christopher J. (2020). Are we generating more assessments without added value? Surgical trainees’ perceptions of and receptiveness to cross-specialty assessment. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-020-00594-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group data from the cross-specialty assessment study. A general surgery trainee doubted an outsider could meaningfully complete checklists for technically difficult procedures, and trainees felt checklists failed to capture the nuance of tasks like wrist movement that comes from experience.

> "Throughout the four focus groups, trainees alluded to the fact that there might be a contextual threshold for cross-specialty assessments."
