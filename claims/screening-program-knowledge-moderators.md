# Knowledge of the DDH screening program is higher among physicians who follow DDH patients, screen more babies, attend congresses, are family medicine specialists, are female, use more educational resources, and feel proficient

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Screening-program knowledge correlates with number of babies screened (r = 0.216), self-perceived proficiency (r = 0.233), and diversity of educational resources (r = 0.155), and is higher in those following DDH patients, congress attendees, specialists, and female physicians. [→ Şükrü Gönen E 2025](#sukru-gonen-e-2025)

## Evidence

### Şükrü Gönen E 2025

Şükrü Gönen E, Taşkın Egici M, Gönen E. (2025). Knowledge and Awareness of Family Physicians as Key Stakeholders Regarding Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip and the Nationwide Screening Program. Acta Orthop Traumatol Turc. https://doi.org/10.5152/j.aott.2025.24034

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Survey results on the screening-program subtopic among 401 family physicians, with Spearman correlations for screened-baby volume (r = 0.216), proficiency (r = 0.233), and resource diversity (r = 0.155).

> "Knowledge and awareness about the DDH screening program significantly increased among those who have followed a child with DDH (regardless of the number of children) (P < .001), those working in units with a high number of screened babies in the last year (r = 0.216, P < .001), attendees of congresses and symposiums (P = .006), family medicine specialists (P = .005), considering themselves proficient in DDH knowledge (r = 0.233, P < .001), female physicians (P = .012), and with increased diversity of educational and information resources used (r = 0.155, P = .002)"
