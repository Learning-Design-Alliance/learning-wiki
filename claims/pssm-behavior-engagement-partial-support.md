## Evidence

### Hagborg 1994

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discussion-section integration of Study 1 findings from 50 eighth-graders whose responses were matched to school records; the authors report "partial support for the PSSM relationship to school atten- dance" and a significant association with Behavioral Conduct, a subscale the article says "measures self-control, rule compliance, and kindness to others."

> "findings included partial support for the PSSM relationship to school atten- dance (absences during seventh grade) and a significant association with the SPPC's Behavioral Conduct subscale."

### Hagborg 1994 (2)

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discussion-section summary of the two studies' correlates of school membership; the authors note homework-time support was "high school only", and elsewhere report the High group spent "approximately 16 minutes more per night completing homework"; no effect size is printed.

> "With respect to engagement, support is found in several areas, including the PSSM's relationship to grades, academic achievement (partial correlational sup- port with the New York achievement tests), time spent on homework (high school only), several domains of self-esteem"

### Hagborg 1994 (3)

Winston J. Hagborg. (1994). An Exploration of School Membership among Middle- and High-School Students. Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=An+Exploration+of+School+Membership+among+Middle-+and+High-School+Students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Study 1 Results report that independent t-tests comparing Low and High PSSM contrast groups (median split of 50 eighth-graders) found "No statistically significant differences were found between the groups in grades or attendance"; equivalence was not tested.

> "No statistically significant differences were found between the groups in grades or attendance based on independent t-tests."
