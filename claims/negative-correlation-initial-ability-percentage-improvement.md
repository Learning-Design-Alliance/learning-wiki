# Children below average in initial ability improve relatively more with practice than those above average, in both feeble-minded and normal groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The linear correlation between initial ability and percentage of improvement is negative in both groups: r = -.36 (P.E. .13) for the feeble-minded group and r = -.40 (P.E. .14) for the normal group. [→ Woodrow 1915](#woodrow-1915)

## Evidence

### Woodrow 1915

Woodrow, H. (1915). Practice and transference in normal and feeble-minded children. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Practice+and+Transference+in+Normal+and+Feeble-Minded+Children%22

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Correlational analysis of individual practice data from the four-group experiment; the printed correlations are r = -.36 and r = -.40, the latter a medium effect. "On the average then, there was a tendency in both groups for the children who were below the average in initial ability to improve, relatively, more than" those above average.

> "the linear correlation between the initial abilities of the children and the percentages of improvement is negative, the correlation being —.36 (P. E., .13) for the feeble-minded group and —.40 (P. E., .14) for the normal group"
