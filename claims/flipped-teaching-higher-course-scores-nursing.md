# Undergraduate nursing students taught with a flipped teaching strategy scored significantly higher in the therapeutic communication course than students taught traditionally, though the article characterizes the magnitude of the difference as very small

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Students in the flipped teaching intervention group achieved significantly higher mean course scores (M=83.34, SD=9.81) than the traditional teaching control group (M=75.57, SD=9.82), p ≤ 0.001, with the article reporting the magnitude of the difference as very small (Eta squared = 0.00035). ## Evidence

### Andargeery Shaherah Yousef 2024

Andargeery Shaherah Yousef, Bahri Hibah Abdulrahim, Alhalwani Rania Ali, Alahmedi Shorok Hamed, Ali Waad Hasan. (2024). Using a flipped teaching strategy in undergraduate nursing education: students’ perceptions and performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05749-9

`q2 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study with eight randomly assigned course sections (intervention n=182, control n=173); an independent t-test compared cumulative assessment scores out of 100. The article reports "Eta squared = 0.00035" and calls the mean difference of -7.77% very small.

> "The significant level value (2-tailed) is p ≤ 0.001, indicating that there is a statistically significant difference in the mean scores of students’ academic performance for the intervention group (M = 83.34, SD = 9.81) and control group (M = 75.57, SD = 9.82)."
