The chapter quotes Gee's own argument, in which the 'creativity of capitalism' leads to 'better and better designs for good learning.' The author characterizes this as a pseudo-Hegelian model of inevitable improvement, an interpretive claim rather than an empirical result.

> "If a game has poor learning principles built into its design, then it won't get learned or played and won't sell well."
