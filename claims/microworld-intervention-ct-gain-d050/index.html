Evidence¶

Jenkins 2015¶

Craig Jenkins. (2015). Poem Generator: A comparative quantitative evaluation of a microworlds-based learning approach for teaching English. International Journal of Education and Development using Information and Communication Technology, 11(2), 153-167. http://ijedict.dec.uwi.edu/viewarticle.php?id=1972

q2 · i2 · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental nonequivalent comparison group study with 27 Year 8 learners in two low-ability English sets; independent t-tests on gain scores. The CT gain difference was t-tested at p = 0.202 with Cohen's d = 0.50, described as "a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50)".

"Following the microworld intervention, a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50) was reported in computational thinking"

Related Claims¶

  • Improvement in computational thinking was positively correlated with improvement in poetic thinking (r = 0.341, p = 0.082), though this may merely indicate high gains in both tests — related
  • A microworld-based intervention produced a modestly greater gain in poetic thinking than comparison teaching, with a modest effect size (d = 0.46) that was not statistically significant — related
  • In a multiple regression, improvement in computational thinking (standardized Beta = 0.301) had a stronger effect on poetic thinking gain than microworld group membership (standardized Beta = 0.157), but the combined model fit the data poorly and was not statistically significant — related
  • AI-supported PBL students reported higher computational thinking on a self-report scale than traditional-lecture students, controlling for pretest scores — related
