# College enrollment gaps by race, gender, and socioeconomic background among CPS graduates were driven primarily by differences in four-year enrollment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Black and Latino graduates in 2015 had overall enrollment rates 15 to 25 percentage points lower than their White and Asian counterparts, a wider gap than seen nationally. [→ Coca 2017](#coca-2017)
`q2 i?` Latino graduates had the lowest four-year enrollment among their gender groups and the highest two-year enrollment, while the gender gap was most pronounced for Black graduates and least for Latino graduates. [→ Coca 2017 (2)](#coca-2017-2)
`q2 i?` Graduates from low median family income census blocks enrolled in four-year colleges at rates 16 percentage points lower than peers from high-income blocks (39 versus 55 percent), while two-year rates were nearly the same (18 versus 17 percent). [→ Coca 2017 (3)](#coca-2017-3)

## Evidence

### Coca 2017

Coca, V.M., Nagaoka, J., & Seeskin, A. (2017). Patterns of two-year and four-year college enrollment among Chicago Public Schools graduates. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive disaggregation of 2015 CPS enrollment by race/ethnicity in Chapter 2, Figure 3. The report states the Chicago gap was much wider than the roughly 2-to-7-point gaps seen nationally.

> "Black and Latino graduates from the class of 2015 had rates of overall college enrollment that ranged from 15 to 25 percentage points lower than their respective White and Asian counterparts"

### Coca 2017 (2)

Coca, V.M., Nagaoka, J., & Seeskin, A. (2017). Patterns of two-year and four-year college enrollment among Chicago Public Schools graduates. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chapter 2 analysis of enrollment by race/ethnicity and gender. The report also notes more Latino graduates attended two-year colleges than Black, White, and Asian graduates, and that the gender gap ranged from 10 points for Black to 6 points for Latino graduates.

> "The differences in CPS graduates' enrollment rates by race/ethnicity were primarily driven by four-year college enrollment rates. Latino graduates had the lowest rates of four-year college enrollment in comparison to the rates of other racial/ethnic groups in their respective gender group."

### Coca 2017 (3)

Coca, V.M., Nagaoka, J., & Seeskin, A. (2017). Patterns of two-year and four-year college enrollment among Chicago Public Schools graduates. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://toandthrough.uchicago.edu/tool

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chapter 2 analysis using census-block median family income as a proxy for socioeconomic background, shown in Figure 4. The report cautions this proxy likely makes the enrollment gap an underestimate.

> "The share of CPS graduates from low median family income census blocks who enrolled in a four-year college was 16 percentage points lower than their peers from high median family income census blocks (39 percent vs. 55 percent, respectively), while two-year enrollment rates were nearly the same (18 percent vs. 17 percent, respectively)."
