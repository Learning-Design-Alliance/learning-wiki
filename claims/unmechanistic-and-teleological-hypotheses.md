## Evidence

### Sensibaugh Cheryl A. 2017

Sensibaugh Cheryl A., Madrid Nathaniel J., Choi Hye-Jeong, Anderson William L., Osgood Marcy P. (2017). Undergraduate Performance in Solving Ill-Defined Biochemistry Problems. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(4):ar63. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0106

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Content analysis of unsatisfactory Hypothesize responses (39/47; 114 coded segments) found most hypotheses were not mechanistic, failing to explain how the observations might have arisen. Percentages are descriptive proportions of coded segments, not effect sizes.

> "Taken together, the unmechanistic hypotheses accounted for 49% of all the coded segments (Table 6). Surprisingly, another 9% of segments were inconsistent with given information."

### Sensibaugh Cheryl A. 2017 (2)

Sensibaugh Cheryl A., Madrid Nathaniel J., Choi Hye-Jeong, Anderson William L., Osgood Marcy P. (2017). Undergraduate Performance in Solving Ill-Defined Biochemistry Problems. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(4):ar63. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0106

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Within the same content analysis, teleological thinking, characterized per Coley and Tanner as causal reasoning based on a goal, purpose, or function, appeared in 7% of coded Hypothesize segments, framing outcomes as causes rather than mechanisms.

> "Such thinking appeared in 7% of segments (Supplemental Table S1). In these cases, students hypothesized that the observations were somehow due to the lorrat needing to adapt to its environment, use energy efficiently, or proliferate"
