## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Plateau (r = -.25, p < .05) and close multi-spike (r = -.31, p < .01) curve-form frequencies correlated significantly negatively with pretest scores, suggesting students with these forms started with lower knowledge. [→ Baker et al. 2013](#baker-et-al-2013)

## Evidence

### Baker et al. 2013

Ryan S. Baker, Arnon Hershkovitz, Lisa M. Rossi, Adam B. Goldstein & Sujith M. Gowda (2013). Predicting Robust Learning With the Visual Form of the Moment-by-Moment Learning Curve. Journal of the Learning Sciences, 22:4, 639-666. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2013.836653

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Secondary correlational analysis of curve-form frequencies against pretest scores, using Benjamini and Hochberg's original false discovery rate procedure because the QVALUE package did not work for so few tests. Printed correlations were r = -.25 for plateau and r = -.31 for close multi-spike; the authors interpret the negative posttest associations as partly due to "having lower initial knowledge".

> "only two relationships between a curve form and pretest scores are statistically significant: the plateau form (r = –.25, p < .05) and the close multi-spike form (r = –.31, p < .01). Hence, students who had curve forms of these types likely had lower posttest scores because of having lower initial knowledge."
