# Narrowly focused media production lessons increase university students' perceived media literacy competencies across all four dimensions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After three video design lessons focused only on mediated message communication, students' self-assessed competencies in media access, creation, sharing, and analysis each increased significantly (p < .001, two-tailed). [→ Schmidt 2014](#schmidt-2014)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 2014

Hans Schmidt. (2014). Helping Students Understand Media: Examining the Efficacy of Interdisciplinary Media Training at the University Level. Journal of Media Literacy Education 7(2), 50-68. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1074678

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-group pretest-posttest quasi-experiment with 118 undergraduates from 10 campuses and 10 fields who received three standardized 60-minute video design lessons. Table 1 shows the average of all items rose from M = 4.57 to M = 6.32, with each dimension significant at p < .001, two-tailed.

> "An independent samples t-test showed significant differences between the pretest and posttest regarding perceived media literacy competencies associated with media access (p < .001, two-tailed), media creation (p < .001, two-tailed), media sharing (p < .001, two-tailed), and media analysis (p < .001, two-tailed)."
