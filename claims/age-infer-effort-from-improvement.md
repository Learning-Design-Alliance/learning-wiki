## Evidence

### Ying Hu 2026

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Preregistered Study 1 with 4- to 10-year-old children comparing an improving and a constant character with matched final performance. Simple slopes show the age effect on hardworking selections was significant (B = 0.43, p < 0.001) but not on smart selections (p = 0.200), a significant age-by-measure interaction.

> "Simple slope tests revealed that the age effect was significant for the hardworking question (B = 0.43, SE =0 . 1 2 ,p < 0.001), but not for the smart question (B = −0.17, SE = 0.13, p = 0.200)."

### Ying Hu 2026 (2)

Ying Hu, Yuhang Shu & Xin Zhao. (2026). Children consider changes in performance over time when reasoning about academic achievements. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00401-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Study 2 replication using honor-roll rankings across three exams found the same pattern: the age effect on hardworking inferences was significant (B = 0.98, p < 0.001) while the smart-question effect was not (p = 0.459). Johnson–Neyman analyses placed significant hardworking selection of the improver from about 6.2 years.

> "Simple slope tests revealed that the age effect was significant for the hardworking question (B = 0.98, SE = 0.26, p < 0.001), but not for the smart question (B = 0.12,SE = 0.16,p = 0.459)."
