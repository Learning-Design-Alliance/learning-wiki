# Pro-verbs become configuration-bound function words whose meaning comes from their sentential frame

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When verbs such as take, make, give, have and do act as pro-verbs, they lose their individual grammatico-semantic features and take their meaning from the constituents in their sentential frame. [→ Babcock 1967](#babcock-1967)

## Evidence

### Babcock 1967

Babcock, Sandra Scharff. (1967). Syntactic Dissimilation. ERIC. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED024020

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument from analysis of pro-verb substitution in English cognate constructions. The paper argues that in 'make a suggestion' the pro-verb make does not carry the semantic load of basic make, since it is interchangeable with give, showing its meaning is "configuration-bound."

> "whenever the meaning of a verb is a function of the whole sentence, or is governed by one or more participants in its frame, um must assume that it is behaving, not as an independent lexical morpheme, but as a configuration-bound function-word."
