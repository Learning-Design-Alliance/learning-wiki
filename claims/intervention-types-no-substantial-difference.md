# Intervention types (cue-generation encoding-based, retrieval-based, cue-attention, cue-selection) do not substantially differ in effectiveness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` All four intervention types showed significant positive effects, but the between-type moderator test was not significant, with only cue-selection tending to exceed cue-attention (p = .029). [→ Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer 2020](#anja-prinz-stefanie-golke-jorg-wittwer-2020)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer 2020

Anja Prinz & Stefanie Golke & Jörg Wittwer. (2020). To What Extent Do Situation-Model-Approach Interventions Improve Relative Metacomprehension Accuracy? Meta-Analytic Insights. Educational Psychology Review (2020) 32:917–949. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09558-6

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Mixed-effects moderator analysis over four intervention-type categories (Table 4). The article reports the moderator test was not significant, Q(3) = 4.07, p = .254, R2 = .10, and no Bonferroni-corrected pairwise difference.

> "the moderator analysis with intervention type revealed a significant positive average effect of small-to-medium size for the cue-generation encoding-based interventions and the cue-attention intervention, of medium size for the cue-generation retrieval-based interventions, and of large size for the cue-selection intervention"
