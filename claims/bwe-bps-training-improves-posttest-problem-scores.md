# Students trained with both backward worked examples and backward problem solving significantly outperformed control and BWE-only students on posttest problem scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Posttest problem scores were significantly associated with training treatment, with the BWE+BPS group consistently outperforming both other groups. [→ Preya Shabrina 2024](#preya-shabrina-2024)

## Evidence

### Preya Shabrina 2024

Preya Shabrina, Behrooz Mostafavi, Mark Abdelshiheed, Min Chi, Tiffany Barnes. (2024). Investigating the Impact of Backward Strategy Learning in a Logic Tutor: Aiding Subgoal Learning Towards Improved Problem Solving. Int J Artif Intell Educ 34:825–861. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-023-00338-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental assignment of 168 completers (59 C, 55 T1, 54 T2) in a discrete mathematics course to Deep Thought treatments; mixed-effect regression on posttest scores gave p=0.02 and “T2 consistently outperformed the other training groups in all problems” with significantly higher scores in 7.3–7.6.

> "Subsequently, when we performed a similar mixed-eﬀect regression analysis for the posttest problems, the p-value was 0.02 demonstrating a signiﬁcant association between training treatment and posttest scores. In the posttest phase [notice the ‘Posttest’ segment in Fig.5], T2 consistently outperformed the other training groups in all problems."
