# Pragmatic recasts improved learners' request performance in one study but showed no significant effects in another, leaving recast effectiveness mixed

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Fukuya et al. (1998) found no statistically significant differences among focus-on-form, focus-on-formS, and control groups on written DCT posttests for sociopragmatic requests. [→ Yousefi 2021 (2)](#yousefi-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Yousefi 2021

Yousefi, M., & Nassaji, H. (2021). Corrective Feedback in Second Language Pragmatics: A Review of Research. Teaching English as a Second Language Electronic Journal (TESL-EJ), 25(1). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej97/a6.pdf

`q2 · i3` · `review · r2`

The review reports Fukuya and Zhang's (2002) study in which Chinese EFL learners role-played request scenarios and the experimental group received recasts on request Head Acts; gains were measured with a DCT task and yielded "large (d = 0.83) effect sizes for the pragmatic recast group."

> "The results yielded large (d = 0.83) effect sizes for the pragmatic recast group, proving that pragmalinguistic recasts were effective for teaching both pragmatically and grammatically appropriate requests."

### Yousefi 2021 (2)

Yousefi, M., & Nassaji, H. (2021). Corrective Feedback in Second Language Pragmatics: A Review of Research. Teaching English as a Second Language Electronic Journal (TESL-EJ), 25(1). https://tesl-ej.org/pdf/ej97/a6.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Fukuya et al.'s (1998) study of focus on form versus focus on formS instruction with role-play scenarios for requests; the written DCT pre-and posttest "showed no statistically significant differences among the three groups." Equivalence was not tested.

> "Results from the written DCT pre-and posttest showed no statistically significant differences among the three groups."
