# Design majors outperform matched non-design majors on visual artistic creativity task performance, with medium to large effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a propensity-score-matched quasi-experiment, the design group scored higher than the non-design group on most product design and book cover design metrics, with the largest effects on aesthetics (d = 2.06) and elaboration (d = 2.29) in the product task. [→ Jing Teng 2025](#jing-teng-2025)

## Evidence

### Jing Teng 2025

Jing Teng, Xinuo Qiao, Kelong Lu, Tuo Liu, Xinyue Wang, Zhenni Gao, Tingting Yu & Ning Hao (2025). Neural mechanisms underpinning the association between visual arts education and creativity. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00388-1

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Weighted independent-samples t-tests (FDR-corrected) on creativity task ratings after propensity score matching compared design majors (n = 39) with matched non-design majors (n = 39). The quote shows the design group's advantages in the product design task, with the printed effects ranging from d = 0.81 to d = 2.06. The article reports "medium to large effect sizes" overall and no significant difference for functional fluency (d = 0.25).

> "the design group scoring higher in functional usefulness (t(74) = 3.58, pcorr < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.81), functional novelty (t(74) = 4.06, pcorr < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.92), appearance novelty ( t (76) = 5.06, pcorr < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.15), aesthetics ( t (74) = 9.11, pcorr < 0.001, Cohen's d = 2.06)"
