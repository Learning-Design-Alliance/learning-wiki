# Student mistakes in axiomatic proofs fall into three categories and can largely be covered by buggy rules

## Evidence

### Josje Lodder 2021

Josje Lodder, Bastiaan Heeren, Johan Jeuring, Wendy Neijenhuis. (2021). Generation and Use of Hints and Feedback in a Hilbert-Style Axiomatic Proof Tutor. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:99-133. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00222-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Qualitative error analysis of homework from 40 students, classifying mistakes into oversights (19), conceptual errors (11), and creative rule adaptations (9), such as missing parentheses in Axiom b. The authors link this to repair theory, noting students typically make these mistakes when they do not know how to proceed.

> "From the homework of 40 students participating in our course we collected a set of mistakes, and classified these mistakes in three categories"

### Josje Lodder 2021 (2)

Josje Lodder, Bastiaan Heeren, Johan Jeuring, Wendy Neijenhuis. (2021). Generation and Use of Hints and Feedback in a Hilbert-Style Axiomatic Proof Tutor. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education 31:99-133. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-020-00222-2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The article states that with a few improvements in the design of the buggy-rule set, LOGAX will cover about 80% of student mistakes; the buggy rules were defined from homework solutions, mostly concerning Modus Ponens applications. No effect size is printed.

> "With a few improvements in the design of the set of buggy rules, LOGAX will cover about 80% of the mistakes made by students by buggy rules."
