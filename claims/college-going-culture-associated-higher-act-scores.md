# Schools with stronger college-going cultures have higher student ACT scores, regardless of student backgrounds

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The more schools develop a college-going culture—teachers preparing all students for college, students feeling pushed to prepare, and class work felt to prepare them for college—the higher their students' ACT scores. [→ Elaine Allensworth 2008](#elaine-allensworth-2008)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth 2008

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Observational analysis across CPS high schools relating survey-based measures of college-going culture to ACT scores; the association holds "regardless of the backgrounds of the students they serve," per the report.

> "the more that schools develop a college-going culture where teachers work to prepare all students to succeed in college, where students feel that all students are being pushed to prepare for the future, and where students report that their class work is preparing them for college, the higher their students’ ACT scores."
