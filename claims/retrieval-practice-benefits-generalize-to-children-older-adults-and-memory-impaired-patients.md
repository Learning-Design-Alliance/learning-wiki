# Retrieval practice benefits have been observed in children, healthy older adults, and memory-impaired patient groups, not only college students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The chapter reports, citing Karpicke et al. (2016), that children aged 9-11 who practiced retrieval of word pairs without feedback showed benefits on final free recall and recognition tests. [→ Karpicke 2017](#karpicke-2017)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports that a few studies suggest benefits become more evident with older children (e.g., age 8) than younger children (e.g., age 6). [→ Karpicke 2017 (2)](#karpicke-2017-2)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports, citing Meyer and Logan (2013), consistent benefits of multiple-choice retrieval practice over restudying for adults aged 18-25 and 55-65 at 5 min and 2 days. [→ Karpicke 2017 (3)](#karpicke-2017-3)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports promising results using retrieval practice as memory remediation in patients with multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injuries, and Alzheimer's-type dementia. [→ Karpicke 2017 (4)](#karpicke-2017-4)
