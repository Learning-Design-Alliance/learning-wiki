## Evidence

### Lin 2017

Lin, L.-F. (2017). Impacts of the Problem-based Learning Pedagogy on English Learners’ Reading Comprehension, Strategy Use, and Active Learning Attitudes. Journal of Education and Training Studies, 5(6). https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i6.2320

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent sample t test comparing two intact classes (PBL n = 26, non-PBL n = 30) on the reading comprehension post-test. The PBL group's "total mean score on the post-test (M = 28.15)" exceeded the non-PBL group's 26.20, a significant difference (t = 2.206, p < .05).

> "The PBL group’s total mean score on the post-test (M = 28.15) was higher than that of the non-PBL group ( M = 26.20), and the independent sample t test yielded a significant result between the two groups in the total score of the RC post-test (t = 2.206, p < .05)."

### Lin 2017 (2)

Lin, L.-F. (2017). Impacts of the Problem-based Learning Pedagogy on English Learners’ Reading Comprehension, Strategy Use, and Active Learning Attitudes. Journal of Education and Training Studies, 5(6). https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v5i6.2320

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired t test within the PBL group (n = 26): post-test mean 28.15 vs pre-test 24.65, significant at p = .001. The non-PBL group's paired comparison was not significant (t = -1.693, p = .096).

> "There was a significant difference between the PBL group’s pre- and post-tests (t = -3.586, p = .001). That is, the participants in the PBL group made significant progress in their RC ability after the instructional treatment."
