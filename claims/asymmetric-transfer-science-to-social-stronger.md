# Transfer of counterargument skill across domains is asymmetrical: science-condition participants transferred to the social topic as strongly as social-condition participants, but social-condition participants transferred less to the science topic

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q3` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2010 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` On the science topic the science condition raised overall counterargument usage more than the social condition did (56.1% vs 32.92%; partial η² = .66). [→ Iordanou 2010](#iordanou-2010)
`q3 i?` Social-condition participants nevertheless transferred counterargument skill to the nonintervention science topic, outperforming the control condition. [→ Iordanou 2010 (2)](#iordanou-2010-2)
`q3 i?` At the individual level, almost all science-condition participants reached mastery of Counter-C on the transfer social topic, whereas only about half of social-condition participants did so on the transfer science topic. [→ Iordanou 2010 (3)](#iordanou-2010-3)

## Evidence

### Iordanou 2010

Kalypso Iordanou. (2010). Developing Argument Skills Across Scientific and Social Domains. Journal of Cognition and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2010.485335

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Two-way Condition x Time ANOVA on the science topic showed a significant Time x Condition interaction, F(2, 53) = 51.73, p \< .001, with printed effect size "partial g2 = .66" (partial η² = .66). The condition difference was significant at p \< .001: "the SCI was more effective in raising overall counterargument usage" than the SOC.

> "As seen in Figure 2, the SCI was more effective in raising overall counterargument usage (M = 56.1%, SD = 15.18) on the science topic than the SOC (M = 32.92%, SD = 20.4)."

### Iordanou 2010 (2)

Kalypso Iordanou. (2010). Developing Argument Skills Across Scientific and Social Domains. Journal of Cognition and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2010.485335

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Bonferroni post-hoc comparison on the science topic showed "the SOC produced significantly more counterarguments compared with the control condition (M = 2.42%, SD = 7.39)"; no effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "However, Bonferroni post-hoc analysis revealed that the SOC produced significantly more counterarguments compared with the control condition (M = 2.42%, SD = 7.39), demonstrating the ability of the SOC participants to transfer their counterargument skills to the nonintervention science topic."

### Iordanou 2010 (3)

Kalypso Iordanou. (2010). Developing Argument Skills Across Scientific and Social Domains. Journal of Cognition and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2010.485335

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Individual-level analysis of participants producing at least three Counter-Cs at final assessment. The asymmetry is printed as counts: "11 of 19" of the SOC versus "18 of 20" of the SCI on their transfer topics; no effect size is printed.

> "Only about half—11 of 19—of the SOC participants were able to produce at least three Counter-Cs on the science topic (transfer topic), whereas almost all—18 of 20—of the SCI participants did so on the social topic (transfer topic) at the final assessment."
