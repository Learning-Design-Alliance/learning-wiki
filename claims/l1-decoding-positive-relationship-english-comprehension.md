# L1 decoding is positively related to English reading comprehension for Spanish- and Chinese-speaking MLs, with the correlation decreasing as children get older

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A meta-analysis found a positive linear relationship between L1 decoding and English reading comprehension (r = 0.24, p = 0.01), and the magnitude of the relationship decreased as children got older. [→ Kittle 2024](#kittle-2024)

## Evidence

### Kittle 2024

Kittle, J. M., Amendum, S. J., & Budde, C. M. (2024). What Does Research Say About the Science of Reading for K-5 Multilingual Learners? A Systematic Review of Systematic Reviews. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09942-6

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis pooling six studies with independent correlations consisting of 1067 children; five had Spanish L1 and one Chinese L1. The review reports r = 0.24, p = 0.01, and that age had a significant impact, the magnitude decreasing as children got older.

> "A meta-analysis by Melby-Lervåg and Lervåg (2011) found a positive linear relationship between L1 decoding and English reading comprehension (r = 0.24, p = 0.01)."
