# Misconceptions impair comprehension of a standard text, and both a refutation text and a think sheet compensate for this detrimental effect

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized experiment, the negative effect of misconceptions on comprehension in the control condition was compensated when learners read a refutation text or completed a think sheet, with no difference between the two methods. [→ Anja Prinz 2021](#anja-prinz-2021)
`q3 i?` In the control group, misconceptions had a marginally significant negative effect on comprehension. [→ Anja Prinz 2021 (2)](#anja-prinz-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Anja Prinz 2021

Anja Prinz, Stefanie Golke, Jörg Wittwer. (2021). Counteracting detrimental effects of misconceptions on learning and metacomprehension accuracy: The utility of refutation texts and think sheets. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09535-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression analysis in the immediate session of the randomized between-subjects experiment (N = 92) testing misconceptions as predictor and instructional method as multicategorical moderator. The omnibus interaction was marginally significant, with "∆R 2 = .06, F(2, 86) = 3.03, p = .053"; no effect size beyond ΔR² was printed.

> "The analysis on comprehension revealed that there was a marginally significant overall interaction between misconceptions and instructional method, ∆R 2 = .06, F(2, 86) = 3.03, p = .053."

### Anja Prinz 2021 (2)

Anja Prinz, Stefanie Golke, Jörg Wittwer. (2021). Counteracting detrimental effects of misconceptions on learning and metacomprehension accuracy: The utility of refutation texts and think sheets. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09535-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Conditional effect in the same regression analysis for the control group reading the standard text with a misconception list: "b = −0.40, SE = 0.20, t(91) = −1.99, p = .050". Effects in the refutation-text and think-sheet groups were not significant.

> "In the control group, misconceptions had a marginally significant negative effect on comprehension, b = −0.40, SE = 0.20, t(91) = −1.99, p = .050."
