## Evidence

### Kiuru 2020

Kiuru, N., Wang, M.-T., Salmela-Aro, K., Kannas, L., Ahonen, T., & Hirvonen, R. (2020). Associations between Adolescents' Interpersonal Relationships, School Well-being, and Academic Achievement during Educational Transitions. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-019-01184-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Combined cross-lagged SEM (N = 848). Table 3 prints the indirect effect school satisfaction→closeness to parents→achievement = 0.009* (SE 0.004). The hypothesis that well-being would predict achievement via friend relationship quality was not supported.

> "adolescents' high school satisfaction in the fall of the sixth grade indirectly promoted the subsequent academic achievement in the spring of the seventh grade through increased closeness to parents in the fall of the seventh grade"

### Kiuru 2020 (2)

Kiuru, N., Wang, M.-T., Salmela-Aro, K., Kannas, L., Ahonen, T., & Hirvonen, R. (2020). Associations between Adolescents' Interpersonal Relationships, School Well-being, and Academic Achievement during Educational Transitions. Journal of Youth and Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-019-01184-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same combined SEM. Table 3 prints the indirect effect school stress→conflict with teachers→achievement = −0.027*** (SE 0.007). The article notes stress effects on achievement were indirect, operating through interpersonal conflicts.

> "school stress in the fall of the sixth grade indirectly hindered subsequent academic achievement in the spring of the seventh grade through increased conflict with teachers immediately after their transition in the fall of the seventh grade"
