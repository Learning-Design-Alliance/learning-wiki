## Evidence

### Zhang Yongshuo 2026

Zhang Yongshuo, Li Wenbo, Zhang Chuchen, Wang Yawei, Feng Shuo. (2026). Reforming hospital management education with the P-MASE pedagogy: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08948-8

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Learning-process behavioral outcomes (Table 6) from the RCT: platform logins and valid discussion posts were used as engagement proxies since formal attendance was not systematically recorded; both favored the P-MASE group (P < 0.001).

> "students in the experimental group showed higher engagement, with more weekly platform logins (18.3 ± 2.1 vs. 9.7 ± 3.4, P < 0.001) and valid discussion posts (24.5 ± 5.2 vs. 8.3 ± 3.7, P < 0.001)"

### Zhang Yongshuo 2026 (2)

Zhang Yongshuo, Li Wenbo, Zhang Chuchen, Wang Yawei, Feng Shuo. (2026). Reforming hospital management education with the P-MASE pedagogy: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08948-8

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the standardized ED workflow optimization simulation scenario used for educational assessment, the P-MASE group's waiting-time reduction was 33.3 ± 4.7% vs. 11.2 ± 3.6% (P < 0.001); the article states this is not a real-world operational metric.

> "their simulation-based ED waiting-time reduction rate was also higher and was interpreted as a performance indicator of process-optimization decision-making within the learning tasks (33.3 ± 4.7% vs. 11.2 ± 3.6%, P < 0.001)"
