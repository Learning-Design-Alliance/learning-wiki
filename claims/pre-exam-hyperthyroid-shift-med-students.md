## Evidence

### Hettiarachchi Manjula 2014

Hettiarachchi Manjula, Lakmal Fonseka Chathuranga, Gunasekara Priyanka, Jayasinghe Prasanjanie, Maduranga Dasun. (2014). How does the quality of life and the underlying biochemical indicators correlate with the performance in academic examinations in a group of medical students of Sri Lanka? Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v19.22772

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Paired pre/post serum sampling in the same 78-student prospective study; TSH was log transformed for analysis and presented as median (IQR). The Results report TSH "significantly (p=0.001) lower" before the exam.

> "The median serum TSH level prior to the exam (0.9 mIU/L; IQR 0.74–1.4 mIU/L) was significantly (p=0.001) lower when compared with after-exam status (median of 2.7 mIU/L; IQR 1.90–3.60)."

### Hettiarachchi Manjula 2014 (2)

Hettiarachchi Manjula, Lakmal Fonseka Chathuranga, Gunasekara Priyanka, Jayasinghe Prasanjanie, Maduranga Dasun. (2014). How does the quality of life and the underlying biochemical indicators correlate with the performance in academic examinations in a group of medical students of Sri Lanka? Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v19.22772

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same paired pre/post serum analysis; free T4 was significantly higher at study entry than after the examination (p<0.001), reported with mean±SD in the Results text.

> "The fT4 level prior to the exam (19.48±0.4 pmol/L) was higher when compared with the fT4 level after the exam (17.43±0.3 pmol/L; p<0.001; Table 1)."
