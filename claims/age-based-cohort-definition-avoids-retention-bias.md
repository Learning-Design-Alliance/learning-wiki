# Defining dropout cohorts by age 13 rather than by ninth-grade entry avoids composition bias from the CPS promotion policy's grade retentions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Cohorts defined by age are not affected by new patterns of grade retention, unlike ninth-grade-defined cohorts whose composition changed under the 1996 promotion policy. [→ Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton 2001](#elaine-allensworth-and-john-q-easton-2001)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton 2001

Elaine Allensworth and John Q. Easton. (2001). Calculating a Cohort Dropout Rate for the Chicago Public Schools: A Technical Research Report. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/calculating-cohort-dropout-rate-chicago-public-schools

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Methodological argument based on CPS enrollment and retention records: in 1996 about 1,800 first-year eighth graders were retained, roughly three times the previous year's rate, so about 1,200 students were excluded from the 1996 ninth-grade cohort. This is an analytical/definitional justification, not a tested comparison.

> "Defining cohorts by age has several advan- tages. Most importantly, cohorts are not af- fected by the new patterns of grade retention."
