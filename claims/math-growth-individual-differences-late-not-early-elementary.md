# Math skills show the opposite two-stage pattern: no individual differences in early growth but significant individual differences in late elementary growth for two of three measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Slope variance was not significant for TEMA-early math ability in K-2 or Applied Problems in grades 3-5, indicating no inter-individual differences in growth rates on these measures. [→ Little 2021](#little-2021)
`q3 i?` Slope variance estimates for math fluency and calculation were significant in grades 3 through 5, indicating individual differences in growth rates on these measures. [→ Little 2021 (2)](#little-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Little 2021

Little, C. W., Lonigan, C. J., & Phillips, B. M. (2021). Differential Patterns of Growth in Reading and Math Skills during Elementary School. Journal of Educational Psychology, 113(3), 462-476. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000635

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent-growth-curve models of math outcomes in the same 921-child longitudinal sample, estimated separately for K-2 (TEMA) and grades 3-5 (WJ-III subtests) because measures changed across grades. The study reports non-significant slope variances for these two measures; no effect size was printed.

> "Values for slope variance were not significant for the TEMA-early math ability in kindergarten through grade 2 or for Applied Problems in grade 3 to grade 5, which indicated that although scores increased on the measures over time, there were no inter-individual differences in the rate of growth on these measures"

### Little 2021 (2)

Little, C. W., Lonigan, C. J., & Phillips, B. M. (2021). Differential Patterns of Growth in Reading and Math Skills during Elementary School. Journal of Educational Psychology, 113(3), 462-476. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000635

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same latent-growth-curve analysis of WJ-III math subtests in grades 3-5. The study reports significant slope variances for Math Fluency and Calculation, indicating inter-individual differences in growth; no effect size was printed.

> "In contrast, the slope variance estimates for math fluency and calculation were significant in grade 3 through grade 5, indicating that there were individual differences in the rate of growth on these measures"
