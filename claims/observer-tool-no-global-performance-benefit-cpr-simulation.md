# Using an observer tool during CPR simulation did not improve medical students' global performance immediately after training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Immediately after CPR simulation training, global performance scores of dyads using an observer tool were not significantly different from dyads observing without one (median 24 vs 23 out of 25, p = 0.052). [→ Goulamhoussen Ammar 2024](#goulamhoussen-ammar-2024)

## Evidence

### Goulamhoussen Ammar 2024

Goulamhoussen Ammar, Havard Caroline, Gille Benoit, François Bob, Benhamou Dan, Blanié Antonia. (2024). An observer tool to enhance learning of medical students during simulation training of cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomised controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05658-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In this randomised controlled trial of second-year medical students, 40 dyads used an observer tool and 41 did not; dyads were assessed at end of day on a 2-min CPR scenario scored 0-25 on the OT checklist. Medians were "24 [23 - 25]" vs "23 [21 - 24]", p = 0.052 — not significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "Immediately after training, the global performance scores of the dyads were not significantly different between the OT + and OT- groups (Fig. 2)."
