Niklas Humble. (2026). Ethical Implications of Prompt Injection in AI-mediated Grading: An Adversarial Red-Team Evaluation. Journal of Academic Ethics. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10805-026-09758-9

Security check (6 iterations re-using attempt-5 files): Copilot found and reported the hidden instructions "in all instances (Fig. 6)". The authors caution self-checking "is not a robust security strategy" and may not work against all tools or advanced attacks.

Related Claims¶

  • Basic prompt injection attempts failed or were detected: instruction manipulation alone failed in all 9 iterations, role-playing without concealment was detected in output, and metadata-hidden injections failed in all 9 iterations — related
  • Copilot's detection of one advanced injection did not persist: after reporting an embedded instruction once, the same file's injection succeeded in six subsequent iterations without warning — related
  • Indirect prompt injections combining instruction manipulation, role-playing, and obfuscation altered AI-assigned grades with attack success rates of 94–100% while remaining undetected — related
  • A revised injection combining instruction manipulation and role-playing with an explicit do-not-mention instruction succeeded in all 9 iterations across docx, pdf, and htm formats — related
