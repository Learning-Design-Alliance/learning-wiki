Evidence¶

Serrano-Villar 2016¶

Serrano-Villar, M., & Calzada, E. J. (2016). Ethnic identity: Evidence of protective effects for young, Latino children. Journal of Applied Developmental Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appdev.2015.11.002

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive analysis of the Early Childhood Ethnic Identity Interview in a sample of 674 MA and DA children in NYC public schools. The study reports that "about 20% showed ethnic knowledge" and 75% self-identified; no effect sizes are printed for these descriptive rates.

"The majority of the sample (75%) was able to self-identify; about half of the children showed understanding of ethnic constancy; about 20% showed ethnic knowledge; and 27% expressed ethnic preference."

Related Claims¶

  • Ethnic identity components are more prevalent among kindergarten than pre-k children, consistent with developmental sequencing — related
  • Ethnic constancy and preference are higher among Mexican American than Dominican American children — related
  • Ethnic identity associations with functioning are robust across child gender and ethnic group, and ethnic identity scores are uncorrelated with IQ — related
  • Ethnic constancy and knowledge are associated with better adaptive behavior at home, with no moderation by gender or ethnicity — related
  • At school, ethnic constancy and knowledge predict better adaptive behavior, and constancy predicts fewer externalizing and internalizing problems — related
