# Sciences-oriented students appreciated the CT-promoter e-learning experience more overall, but humanities-oriented students' appreciation rose significantly during the doing phases, narrowing the group gap

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across the whole learning process, sciences-oriented students' appreciation scores were significantly higher than humanities-oriented students' (MANOVA p = .0009). [→ Katai 2020](#katai-2020)
`q2 i?` Humanities-oriented students' appreciation increased significantly from the hearing/seeing phases (5.63 points) to the doing phases (6.55 points), significantly diminishing the group differences. [→ Katai 2020 (2)](#katai-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Katai 2020

Katai, Z. (2020). Promoting computational thinking of both sciences- and humanities-oriented students: an instructional and motivational design perspective. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09766-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

MANOVA on questionnaire scores (statements q2–9/1) with students' orientation as grouping variable, in the Results section. The article reports the group difference significant at "p = .0009"; no effect size is printed.

> "Test results confirmed our expectation that S-students mostly appreciate this kind of learning experience (p = .0009)."

### Katai 2020 (2)

Katai, Z. (2020). Promoting computational thinking of both sciences- and humanities-oriented students: an instructional and motivational design perspective. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09766-5

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired samples t-test comparing H-students' means for q2–4/1 ("hearing and seeing phases": 5.63 points, 77.17%) with q5–7/1 ("doing phases": 6.55 points, 92.5%). The increase occurred despite many errors during the reconstruction task.

> "we detected a significant increase (p = .0000) in their appreciation level. This increase significantly diminished the size of the differences between groups."
