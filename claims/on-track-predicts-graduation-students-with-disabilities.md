# The freshman on-track indicator predicts five-year graduation equally or more strongly for students with disabilities than for students without disabilities, with on-track students two to six times more likely to graduate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On-track students with emotional disturbances are almost six times more likely to graduate in five years than off-track students with emotional disturbances, despite a lower overall graduation rate. [→ Julia Gwynne 2009](#julia-gwynne-2009)
`q2 i?` Similar on-track graduation patterns hold for students with learning disabilities, students with mild cognitive disabilities, and students two or more years below grade level. [→ Julia Gwynne 2009 (2)](#julia-gwynne-2009-2)

## Evidence

### Julia Gwynne 2009

Julia Gwynne, Joy Lesnick, Holly M. Hart, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2009). What Matters for Staying On-Track and Graduating in Chicago Public Schools: A Focus on Students with Disabilities. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/what-matters-staying-track-and-graduating-chicago-public-schools-focus-students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chapter 3 analysis of the 2001 CPS freshman cohort relating freshman on-track status to five-year graduation. Figure 11 prints on-track versus off-track graduation rates for each of five categories, e.g. 87% versus 31% for students without disabilities.

> "although only 57 percent of on-track students with emotional disturbances graduate in five years (compared with 87 percent of students without disabilities), on-track students with emotional disturbances are almost six times more likely to graduate than off-track students with emotional disturbances."

### Julia Gwynne 2009 (2)

Julia Gwynne, Joy Lesnick, Holly M. Hart, Elaine M. Allensworth. (2009). What Matters for Staying On-Track and Graduating in Chicago Public Schools: A Focus on Students with Disabilities. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago Urban Education Institute. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/what-matters-staying-track-and-graduating-chicago-public-schools-focus-students

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chapter 3 reports the on-track indicator is "equally or more predictive of graduation for students with disabilities" than in prior CCSR research, with Figure 11 showing on-track students across all groups two to six times more likely to graduate.

> "Similar patterns exist for students with learning disabilities, students with mild cognitive disabilities, and students who enter high school two or more years below grade level, as shown in Figure 11."
