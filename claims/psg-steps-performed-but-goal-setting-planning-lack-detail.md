# In daily physiotherapy practice, the PSG method's steps were almost always performed, but the 'setting goals' and 'planning treatment' steps were not performed in detail

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Observed consultations showed the PSG steps almost always present, but detailed components such as explaining the reason for a step and checking patient understanding were often absent. [→ Stevens A 2017](#stevens-a-2017)
`q2 i?` In patient files, steps 1 and 4 were recorded in about 99% of files while step 5 (planning treatment) was recorded in only 52%. [→ Stevens A 2017 (2)](#stevens-a-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Stevens A 2017

Stevens A, Köke A, van der Weijden T, Beurskens A. (2017). Ready for goal setting? Process evaluation of a patient-specific goal-setting method in physiotherapy. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-017-2557-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Structured observation of 38 recorded consultations (12 video, 26 audio) by two trained research assistants using standardized forms. The description reports that detailed components like 'explaining reason for the relevant step in all steps' were often absent.

> "Table 2 shows that the steps were almost always present, but detailed components of these steps were often absent."

### Stevens A 2017 (2)

Stevens A, Köke A, van der Weijden T, Beurskens A. (2017). Ready for goal setting? Process evaluation of a patient-specific goal-setting method in physiotherapy. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-017-2557-9

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Assessment of 218 anonymized electronic patient files from 44 physiotherapists, with two assessors agreeing on more than 90% of files. Step 5 was 'only present in half of the files', and treatment goals were often formulated as a future PSG score.

> "The results in Table 2 show that, overall, steps 1 and 4 were correctly recorded and that step 5 was only present in half of the files."
