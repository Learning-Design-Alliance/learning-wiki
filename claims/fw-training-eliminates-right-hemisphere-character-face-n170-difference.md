# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, the right-hemisphere N170 amplitude difference between characters and faces was present before free writing training but not after training, while character N170 amplitude did not significantly change

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the FW group, character N170 differed from face N170 in the right hemisphere before training but not after training, although character amplitude itself did not change significantly. [→ Zhao Pei 2015](#zhao-pei-2015)

## Evidence

### Zhao Pei 2015

Zhao Pei, Li Su, Zhao Jing, Gaspar Carl M., Weng Xuchu. (2015). Training by visual identification and writing leads to different visual word expertise N170 effects in preliterate Chinese children. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2015.09.002

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Planned comparisons in the free writing group's right-hemisphere ERP data: characters smaller than faces before training (p = 0.01) but "did not significantly differ from that of faces in the Post-test" (p = 0.52); character, face and tool amplitudes individually showed no significant pre-to-post change (all p > 0.10).

> "the N170 amplitude of characters was smaller than that of faces (p = 0.01, Fig. 6B) in the Pre-test. While the N170 amplitude of characters did not significantly differ from that of faces in the Post-test (p = 0.52, Fig. 6D)."
