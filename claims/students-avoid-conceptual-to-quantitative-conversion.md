# Students seldom convert conceptual questions into quantitative ones, even when explicitly encouraged by an interviewer

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students given only conceptual questions almost never converted them into quantitative problems to reason about the solution, and persisted in guessing from gut feeling even when the interviewer explicitly encouraged conversion. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` Students who were only given conceptual questions did not convert them into quantitative problems, and this persisted when the interviewer explicitly encouraged conversion. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part II: Effect of Some Potential Factors on Problem Solving and Transfer. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 one-on-one interviews and scratch-work examination in the introductory physics courses: students given only conceptual questions tried to guess based upon gut feeling rather than converting to a quantitative problem. The authors suggest the conversion may cause mental overload, citing Simon's bounded rationality.

> "However, we ﬁnd that students avoided turning conceptual questions into quan- titative ones, even when explicitly encouraged to do so."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Coupling Conceptual and Quantitative Problems to Develop Expertise in Introductory Physics Students. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.02945](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.02945)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observations from scratch-work examination and one-on-one interview discussions in the introductory physics courses; students "often used gut feeling to reason about the conceptual questions", and the tendency persisted under explicit encouragement. No effect size printed.

> "those who were only given the corre- sponding conceptual question did not automatically con- vert it into a quantitative problem as an aid for rea- soning correctly. Examination of students’ scratch work suggests that they seldom attempted such conversion"
