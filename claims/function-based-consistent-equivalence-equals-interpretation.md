## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Function-based algebra gives a consistent interpretation of equivalence (graphs coinciding over the whole domain) versus equals (functions intersecting at particular values), where traditional instruction prescribes rules for simplifying and solving. [→ Laxman 2012](#laxman-2012)

## Evidence

### Laxman 2012

Kumar Laxman. (2012). Conceptualizing Alternative Ways of Curricular Teaching Through Technology. li-manager's Journal on School Educational Technology. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1101699

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Analytical/conceptual exposition in the section on networked generative activities using function-based algebra. The article illustrates that equivalent expressions like x + x + 3 and 2x + 3 yield graphs with all points in the domain coinciding, whereas f(x) = 2x and g(x) = x + 3 are equal only at one x value. No empirical test is reported for this distinction in this article.

> "Instead of prescriptively remembering rules for simplifying and solving, function-based algebra affords a consistent intepretation of both equivalence and equals."
