# A parallel-process implementation validates cross-domain (not within-domain) coupling, disfavoring a Matthew-effect account

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the parallel-process model, vocabulary intercepts positively drove reasoning slopes and vice versa; constraining either path worsened fit, and freeing within-domain effects did not improve fit, favoring cross-domain coupling over a Matthew-effect account. [→ Kievit R. A. 2019](#kievit-r-a-2019)

## Evidence

### Kievit R. A. 2019

Kievit R. A., Hofman A. D., & Nation K. (2019). Mutualistic Coupling Between Vocabulary and Reasoning in Young Children: A Replication and Extension of the Study by Kievit et al. (2017). Psychological Science, 30(8), 1245–1252. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797619841265

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Robustness analysis re-estimating the model as a bivariate latent growth (parallel-process) model, χ2(9) = 18.72, p = .028, RMSEA = 0.069. Freeing within-domain driving effects worsened fit (ΔAIC = 3.6, ΔBIC = 10.4).

> "The path of vocabulary intercepts on reasoning slopes was strong and positive (b = 0.196, SE = 0.051, β = 0.43), and constraining this path led to a considerable drop in model fit, χ2(1) = 18.72, p < .0001"
