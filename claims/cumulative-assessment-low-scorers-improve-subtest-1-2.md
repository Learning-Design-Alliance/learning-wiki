# In a cumulative assessment program, initially low-scoring students' scores improved significantly more than initially high-scoring students' scores decreased between the first and second subtests in all four modules

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Between subtests 1 and 2, the average improvement of initially low-scoring students was significantly higher than the average decrease of initially high-scoring students in modules 1, 3 and 4, and in module 2 high-scoring students' scores decreased significantly more than low-scoring students' scores. [→ Kerdijk Wouter 2013](#kerdijk-wouter-2013)

## Evidence

### Kerdijk Wouter 2013

Kerdijk Wouter, Tio René A, Mulder B Florentine, Cohen-Schotanus Janke. (2013). Cumulative assessment: strategic choices to influence students’ study effort. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-172

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of test scores in four second- and third-year modules (n = 494, 436, 383, 345) using independent-sample t-tests comparing absolute mean score change of lowest and highest quartiles on subtest 1. In modules 1, 3 and 4 "the average improvement of low-scoring students to be significantly higher than the average decrease in high-scoring students' scores"; in module 2 high scorers decreased significantly more (t = 6.49, p = .00).

> "In these modules, we found the average improvement of low-scoring students to be significantly higher than the average decrease in high-scoring students’ scores, which is in line with our expectations"
