## Evidence

### Torbergsen HKS 2025

Torbergsen HKS, Paulsby TE, Haugan G, Utvær BK. (2025). Students’ motivation, study-effort and perceptions of teachers’ goals when engaging in a learning design within the flipped classroom. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07729-z

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional questionnaire study of 351 nursing students at a Norwegian university. Pearson correlations show positive significant associations between autonomous motivation for each activity and perceived learning outcomes, with "The simulation showed the strongest association (r = 0.38)" and r = 0.31 and 0.30 for MCQ and TBL.

> "The correlations between students’ autonomous motivation to perform the four learning activities and their perceived learning outcomes in CPR are positive and significant (p ≤ 0.01) (Table 2). The simulation showed the strongest association (r = 0.38), whereas multiple-choice questions and team-based learning demonstrated somewhat lower correlations, with r = 0.31 and 0.30, respectively."

### Torbergsen HKS 2025 (2)

Torbergsen HKS, Paulsby TE, Haugan G, Utvær BK. (2025). Students’ motivation, study-effort and perceptions of teachers’ goals when engaging in a learning design within the flipped classroom. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07729-z

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Same cross-sectional study (N = 351): the correlation between autonomous motivation for the RQI skills-training activity and perceived learning outcomes was "r = 0.18", the weakest of the four activities.

> "The RQI disclosed an r = 0.18 with students’ perceived learning outcomes."
