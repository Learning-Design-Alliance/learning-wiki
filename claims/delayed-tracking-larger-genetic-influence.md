# Delaying definite tracking is associated with a larger genetic influence and a lower shared-environmental influence on secondary educational attainment among Dutch twins

## Evidence

### Antonie Knigge 2022

Antonie Knigge, Ineke Maas, Kim Stienstra, Eveline L. de Zeeuw and Dorret I. Boomsma. (2022). Delayed tracking and inequality of opportunity: Gene-environment interactions in educational attainment. npj Science of Learning 7:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00122-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ACE modeling of twin data from the Netherlands Twin Register (N = 8847) comparing immediate versus delayed tracking groups. The results section reports "Genes account for 55% of the variance (0.72 of 1.31) in case of immediate tracking" against 74% under delayed tracking; the authors note 95% confidence intervals are just overlapping.

> "Genes account for 55% of the variance (0.72 of 1.31) in case of immediate tracking and 74% (0.69 of 0.93) in case of delayed tracking."

### Antonie Knigge 2022 (2)

Antonie Knigge, Ineke Maas, Kim Stienstra, Eveline L. de Zeeuw and Dorret I. Boomsma. (2022). Delayed tracking and inequality of opportunity: Gene-environment interactions in educational attainment. npj Science of Learning 7:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00122-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same ACE decomposition of attainment for the two tracking groups in the results section. The article reports "a substantial impact (27%; 0.36) for immediately tracked children, but no significant impact when tracking occurs at a later age (2%; 0.02)"; no effect size is printed for the non-shared environment (18% vs 24%).

> "The shared environment has a substantial impact (27%; 0.36) for immediately tracked children, but no significant impact when tracking occurs at a later age (2%; 0.02)."

### Antonie Knigge 2022 (3)

Antonie Knigge, Ineke Maas, Kim Stienstra, Eveline L. de Zeeuw and Dorret I. Boomsma. (2022). Delayed tracking and inequality of opportunity: Gene-environment interactions in educational attainment. npj Science of Learning 7:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00122-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Likelihood-ratio comparison of the constrained versus unconstrained multigroup ACE models on the same twin sample, reported in the results. The article states the differences "could not be ignored statistically" with Chi 2(22) = 312.1, p < 0.001; only the test statistic is printed, so no effect size.

> "The differences between the variance components of immediate versus delayed tracking could not be ignored statistically: Model 1a ﬁts the data signiﬁcantly worse than Model 1b (Chi 2(22) = 312.1, p < 0.001, two-sided)."
