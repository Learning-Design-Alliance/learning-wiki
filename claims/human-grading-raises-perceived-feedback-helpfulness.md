# Human grading modestly increases the probability that students rate feedback as helpful

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Tests with any human-graded SAQ receive a statistically significant but modest increase in the probability of a helpful retrospective feedback evaluation. [→ Heinrich Tobias 2025](#heinrich-tobias-2025)

## Evidence

### Heinrich Tobias 2025

Heinrich Tobias, Baily Spencer, Chen Kuan-Wu, DeOliveira Jack, Park Sanghoon, Wang Navida Chun-Han (2025). AI-assisted grading and personalized feedback in large political science classes: Results from randomized controlled trials. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0328041

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Test-level analysis limited to students who took both the current and previous tests regressed a binary any-helpful outcome on an indicator for whether any SAQ on the last test was human-graded; the increase was "statistically significant but modest".

> "increases the probability of a helpful evaluation in a statistically significant but modest way (mean: 2.13, SE: 0.64)"
