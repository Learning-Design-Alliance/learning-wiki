# One year after training, psychomotor CPR component skills (hand placement, rate/depth, chest recoil, activating emergency response) show significant decreases in students performing them sufficiently, while some skills do not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2024 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At 1 year vs post-training, significant decreases in sufficient performance occurred for activating emergency response (P6 = 0.028), correct CPR hand placement (P6 = 0.001), correct CPR rate and depth (P6 = 0.005) and complete chest recoil (P6 = 0.001). [→ Kochhar S 2024](#kochhar-s-2024)
`q3 i?` For other skills the 1-year decrease was not significant: scene safety (P6 = 0.065), responsiveness assessment (P6 = 0.126), airway opening (P6 = 0.243), and breath delivery showed no change (P6 = 1.00). [→ Kochhar S 2024 (2)](#kochhar-s-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Kochhar S 2024

Kochhar S, Samagh N, Sharma J, Kaur A, Verma M. (2024). Knowledge and Skill Retention in First-Year MBBS Students After Basic Life Support Training: A One-Year Longitudinal Study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05922-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Detailed per-skill analysis of DOPS checklist items across time points in the 100-student longitudinal study; the discussion reports significant one-year decreases for the four compressions-related and emergency-activation skills, identifying the most affected skills.

> "there was a significant decrease in the number of students who could perform the skill sufficiently at 1 year as compared to post-skill score for skills including activation of emergency response i: e, call for help/AED (P6 = 0.028), correct CPR hand placement and positioning (P6 = 0.001), correct CPR rate and depth ((P6 = 0.005) and allowing for complete recoil of the chest between compressions. (P6 = 0.001)"

### Kochhar S 2024 (2)

Kochhar S, Samagh N, Sharma J, Kaur A, Verma M. (2024). Knowledge and Skill Retention in First-Year MBBS Students After Basic Life Support Training: A One-Year Longitudinal Study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05922-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

From the same per-skill DOPS analysis (Table 1): scene safety showed a non-significant 1-year change (P6 = 0.065); the discussion reports non-significant decreases for responsiveness assessment, airway opening, and unchanged breath delivery (P6 = 1.00).

> "There was an increase in the number of students who could perform the skill sufficiently during post 1-year skill score assessment compared to Post- skill score but it was not statistically significant. (P6 = 0.065)"
