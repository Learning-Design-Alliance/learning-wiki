# During practice, 100% feedback frequency was superior to 50% frequency for compression scores, and concurrent feedback was superior to terminal for ventilation scores

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For practice-session compression scores, the 100% feedback condition (mean 92.0%, 95% CI 89.8–94.1) was superior to the 50% condition (mean 89.2%, 95% CI 86.9–91.4), with a significant frequency main effect (p = 0.035). [→ Akizuki Kazunori 2019](#akizuki-kazunori-2019)
`q3 i?` For practice-session ventilation scores, the concurrent condition (mean 91.5%) was superior to the terminal condition (mean 85.9%, p = 0.002). [→ Akizuki Kazunori 2019](#akizuki-kazunori-2019)

## Evidence

### Akizuki Kazunori 2019

Akizuki Kazunori, Yamamoto Ryohei, Yamaguchi Kazuto, Yabuki Jun, Ohashi Yukari. (2019). The effects of feedback timing and frequency on the acquisition of cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills of health sciences undergraduate students: A 2 x 2 factorial quasi randomized study. PLoS ONE 14(7): e0220004. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0220004

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Practice-session three-way ANOVAs on subscores: compression scores showed significant timing (p < 0.001), frequency (p = 0.035), and timing x sequence interaction (p = 0.004); ventilation scores showed a significant timing main effect (p = 0.002). No standardized effect sizes printed.

> "The 100% feedback condition (mean: 92.0%, 95% CI: 89.8–94.1) was superior to the 50% feedback condition (mean: 89.2%, 95% CI: 86.9–91.4)."
