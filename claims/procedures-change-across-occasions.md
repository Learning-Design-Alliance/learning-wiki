### Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo 1993

Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo, Gail P. Baxter, and Richard J. Shavelson. (1993). On the Stability of Performance Assessments. Journal of Educational Measurement, 30(1), 41-53. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/17453984

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of procedural sequence codes across occasions (Table 4) showed agreement ranging from 24% (paper towels observed) to 67% (electric mysteries notebooks). Changes were usually not haphazard; students typically changed only one procedure component.

> "For all the investigations, the low to moderate agreement coefficients indicated that students used different procedures on each of the two occasions (Table 4)."

### Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo 1993 (2)

Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo, Gail P. Baxter, and Richard J. Shavelson. (1993). On the Stability of Performance Assessments. Journal of Educational Measurement, 30(1), 41-53. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/17453984

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Across investigations, notebook procedural agreement exceeded observed agreement, attributed to scoring rules for vague notebook reports and, for electric mysteries, to notebooks recording only the final circuit rather than the full sequence.

> "In general, procedural agreement was higher for notebooks than observed investigations. In the notebooks, students reported procedures in a general way, and the rater often had difficulty determining what they did from what they wrote"
