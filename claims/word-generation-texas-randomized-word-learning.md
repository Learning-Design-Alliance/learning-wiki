# In a randomized Texas study, an adapted ten-week Word Generation version produced significantly greater word learning than control, and control-condition teachers' later students also learned more

## Evidence

### Snow 2014

Catherine E. Snow. (2014). Extended discourse in first and second language acquisition: A challenge and an opportunity! Journal of Japanese Linguistics 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED571513

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Experimental study in Texas with teachers randomly selected to receive Word Generation professional development and materials; the ten-week version added writing practice and teaching about roots, morphology, and Spanish-English cognates while preserving small-group work and weekly debate. Results showed "significantly greater word learning" for Word Generation students.

> "Results from this experimental study showed significantly greater word learning for the students receiving Word Generation than for those in the control group"

### Snow 2014 (2)

Catherine E. Snow. (2014). Extended discourse in first and second language acquisition: A challenge and an opportunity! Journal of Japanese Linguistics 30. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED571513

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Crossover year: teachers originally randomly assigned to control were given Word Generation professional development and materials the following year. Their students showed significantly greater learning of target words than the same teachers' students the prior year without the program.

> "Their students showed significantly greater learning of the target words than those same teachers’ students had shown in the previous year, when they were not using Word Generation."
