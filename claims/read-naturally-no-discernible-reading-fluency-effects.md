# Read Naturally® shows no discernible effects on reading fluency for students with learning disabilities, with small negative effect sizes on GORT-III accuracy and rate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a randomized controlled trial with 20 dyslexic students in grades 4–6, Read Naturally® produced no statistically significant effects on either GORT-III Reading Accuracy or Reading Rate, and the WWC-calculated average effect size (domain average –0.16) was not substantively important. [→ Read Naturally® 2010](#read-naturally-2010)

## Evidence

### Read Naturally® 2010

Read Naturally®. (2010). WWC Intervention Report: Read Naturally®. What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. https://www.readnaturally.com

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r?`

Randomized controlled trial (Chenault et al., 2006) reviewed under WWC standards: 20 dyslexic students in grades 4–6 randomly assigned to Read Naturally® or Pay Attention! in ten 25-minute sessions. The WWC confirmed "no statistically significant effects" on both GORT-III subtests; tabled effect sizes were –0.22 (Accuracy) and –0.11 (Rate), both ns, domain average –0.16, improvement index –6 percentile points.

> "Chenault et al. (2006) found no statistically significant effects of Read Naturally ® on either of two measures of reading fluency: the Reading Accuracy and Reading Rate subtests from the Gray Oral Reading Test–III. The WWC confirmed these findings. Furthermore, the WWC-calculated average effect size across the two outcomes was not large enough to be considered substantively important."
