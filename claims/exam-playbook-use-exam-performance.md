# Self-administered Exam Playbook use predicts higher exam scores in real-world college classes (d = 0.18)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (4 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q3` · `i0` negligible
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` Across 14 classes, students who used the Exam Playbook at least once scored on average 2.17 percentage points higher (Cohen's d = 0.18) than non-users. [→ Chen 2022](#chen-2022)
`q3 i0` At the exam level within classes, users on a given exam scored 2.91 percentage points higher (d = 0.22) than non-users on that exam. [→ Chen 2022 (2)](#chen-2022-2)
`q3 i0` Controlling for college entrance exam scores, users scored 1.65 percentage points higher (d = 0.14) than non-users. [→ Chen 2022 (3)](#chen-2022-3)
`q3 i0` Excluding the Introductory Statistics course for which the intervention was designed, users still scored 1.60 percentage points higher (d = 0.13) than non-users. [→ Chen 2022 (4)](#chen-2022-4)

## Evidence

### Chen 2022

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Random-effects mini meta-analysis across 14 classes (N = 12,065), treating each class as a separate experiment, found "2.17 ([95% CI: 1.13, 3.21], p < 0.001) percentage points higher", translating to Cohen's d = 0.18; the effect was positive in 13 of 14 classes.

> "Our meta-analysis, summarized in Fig. 1, revealed that students who used the Exam Playbook in their class scored 2.17 ([95% CI: 1.13, 3.21], p < 0.001) percentage points higher than non-users, for their average exam score"

### Chen 2022 (2)

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Robustness check at the exam level across all 40 exams showed users "scored an average of 2.91" percentage points higher on that exam (d = 0.22, p < 0.001).

> "A mixed-effects meta-analysis (with exam as a ﬁxed effect within each class, and class as a random effect) across all 40 exams observed showed that students who used the Exam Playbook on a given exam scored an average of 2.91 ([1.81, 4.01], Cohen 's d = 0.22, p < 0.001) percentage points higher"

### Chen 2022 (3)

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

Robustness check controlling college entrance exam scores as a covariate found users "scored an average of 1.65 ([0.55, 2.75], Cohen 's d = 0.14, p = .003) percentage points higher".

> "Exam Playbook users scored an average of 1.65 ([0.55, 2.75], Cohen 's d = 0.14, p = .003) percentage points higher than non-users on their average exam score."

### Chen 2022 (4)

Chen, P., Teo, D. W. H., Foo, D. X. Y., Derry, H. A., Hayward, B. T., Schulz, K. W., Hayward, C., McKay, T. A., & Ong, D. C. (2022). Real-world effectiveness of a social-psychological intervention translated from controlled trials to classrooms. npj Science of Learning 7:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00135-w

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Conservative generalizability test excluding Introductory Statistics (12 classes) found users scored "an average of 1.60 ([1.00, 2.19], d = 0.13, p < 0.001) percentage points higher"; controlling entrance scores reduced it to 1.07 (d = 0.09).

> "students who used the Exam Playbook scored an average of 1.60 ([1.00, 2.19], d = 0.13, p < 0.001) percentage points higher than non-users. When controlling for college entrance exam scores, we observed a 1.07 percentage points difference ([0.29, 1.85], d = 0.09, p = 0.007)."
