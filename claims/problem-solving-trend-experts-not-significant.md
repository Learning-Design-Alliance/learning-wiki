# For more knowledgeable learners, problem solving showed only a non-significant trend over worked examples (f = 0.36)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For high-knowledge learners, the problem-solving format (M = 10.82, SD = 5.79) produced better results than worked examples (M = 7.20, SD = 4.64), but the difference was not statistically significant, F(1, 19) = 2.46, with Cohen's f = 0.36. [→ Kalyuga 2004](#kalyuga-2004)

## Evidence

### Kalyuga 2004

Slava Kalyuga and John Sweller. (2004). Measuring Knowledge to Optimize Cognitive Load Factors During Instruction. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.96.3.558

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Simple-effect test in Experiment 3 within the high-knowledge group. The article reports the problem-solving advantage was "not statistically significant" despite a medium-to-large Cohen's f of 0.36; the authors suggest a possible floor effect could have reduced statistical significance.

> "Although this effect was not statistically significant, F(1, 19) ⫽ 2.46, MSE ⫽ 27.86, a Cohen’s f index of 0.36 indicated a medium to large effect size."
