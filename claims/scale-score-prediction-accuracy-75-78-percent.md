# When scale scores are used, prediction accuracy of grade 8 algebra I proficiency does not vary substantially across grade 6 CST, grade 7 CST, and MDTP assessments (75–78 percent)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Logistic regression models using grade 6 CST scale scores alone, grade 7 CST scale scores alone, grade 7 MDTP, or combinations all predicted algebra I proficiency accurately between 75 and 78 percent of the time. [→ Chun-Wei Huang 2016](#chun-wei-huang-2016)

## Evidence

### Chun-Wei Huang 2016

Chun-Wei Huang, Jason Snipes, Neal Finkelstein, WestEd. (2016). Using assessment data to guide math course placement of California middle school students. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567123

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression comparison of several predictor combinations on the 2,579-student sample (table B2 lists accuracies of 75, 77, 76, 77, and 78 percent); the study reports all models "resulted in accurate predictions of success between 75 and 78 percent of the time."

> "Models using the grade 6 CST scale scores alone, the grade 7 CST scale scores alone, the grade 7 MDTP, or CST scale scores in combination with the MDTP scores all resulted in accurate predictions of success between 75 and 78 percent of the time."
