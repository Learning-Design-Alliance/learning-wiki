# Alternating observation and performance in dyads yielded better transfer than individual practice or a control condition in a video-game task

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a video-game task study, dyads in which one learner performed and the other observed while switching roles showed better transfer scores than learners who only practiced or a control group. [→ Wouters 2007](#wouters-2007)

## Evidence

### Wouters 2007

Wouters, P., Tabbers, H. K., & Paas, F. (2007). Interactivity in Video-based Models. Educational Psychology Review, 19, 327–342. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9045-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Shea et al.'s (2000) video-game study comparing role-switching dyads with individual practice and a control group on transfer scores; the review attributes the better transfer of the dyad arrangement to alternation between observing and practicing.

> "A study with a video-game task revealed better transfer scores for dyads in which one learner performed and the other observed while switching roles than for a situation in which learners only practiced or for a control group (Shea et al. 2000)."

### Wouters 2007 (2)

Wouters, P., Tabbers, H. K., & Paas, F. (2007). Interactivity in Video-based Models. Educational Psychology Review, 19, 327–342. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9045-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review also reports a second dyad arrangement (Shebilske et al. 1992) in which each learner performed part of the task and observed the other; working memory demands decreased without affecting learning performance relative to individual performers.

> "Results showed that the demands on working memory decreased without affecting learning performance compared to learners that performed the task individually."
