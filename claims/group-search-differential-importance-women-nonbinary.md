## Evidence

### Mike Verostek 2024

Mike Verostek, Casey W. Miller, Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2024). Physics PhD student perspectives on the importance and difficulty of finding a research group. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of interviews with 20 physics PhD students found non-male students screened groups using indicators including faculty diversity and underrepresented students' experiences; "only one man (1 of 7) described a similar concern." The authors interpret this as granting male students more research opportunities.

> "whereas a majority of women (7 of 11) and all non-binary interviewees (2 of 2) described having to pare down their list of prospective research groups due to worries that the advisors would not foster inclusive environments, only one man (1 of 7) described a similar concern."

### Mike Verostek 2024 (2)

Mike Verostek, Casey W. Miller, Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2024). Physics PhD student perspectives on the importance and difficulty of finding a research group. arXiv. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010136)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the interview data, two women (Tabitha and Pauline) left groups over advisor misconduct allegations, each abandoning research topics they "really wanted to pursue" for less preferred topics under different advisors.

> "More alarmingly, two of the 11 women interviewees re- ported that they were compelled to leave their groups after discovering that their advisors had been investi- gated for misconduct allegations related to their treat- ment of graduate students."
