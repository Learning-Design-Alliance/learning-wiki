# The ASSISTments homework intervention effect on TerraNova math scores varied by gender: boys benefited more, narrowing the gender gap

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In an HLM with a treatment-by-gender interaction, the intervention effect varied by gender (γ21=7.476, t(42)=2.232, p = 0.031), with boys in treatment scoring 14.21 points higher than boys in control versus 6.73 points for girls. [→ Mingyu Feng 2016](#mingyu-feng-2016)
`q3 i?` The intervention helped close the gender gap in standardized test achievement: boys in control scored 9.61 points lower than girls in control, but only 2.13 points lower in treatment. [→ Mingyu Feng 2016](#mingyu-feng-2016)

## Evidence

### Mingyu Feng 2016

Mingyu Feng, Jeremy Roschelle, Craig Mason, Ruchi Bhanot. (2016). Investigating Gender Differences on Homework in Middle School Mathematics. Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Educational Data Mining, June 29-July 2, 2016, Raleigh, NC. https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2016/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Augmented 3-level HLM on 2,756 students from 44 schools (treatment and control), predicting TerraNova scaled scores from prior achievement, gender, condition and their interaction; Table 5 shows boys gained 14.21 points vs 6.73 for girls, so "boys have benefited more from the intervention than girls".

> "In this model, the effect of ASSISTments intervention was found to vary by gender ( γ21=7.476, t(42)=2.232, p = 0.031)."
