## Evidence

### Ishii Kaori 2020

Ishii Kaori, Aoyagi Kenryu, Shibata Ai, Javad Koohsari Mohammad, Carver Alison, Oka Koichiro. (2020). Joint Associations of Leisure Screen Time and Physical Activity with Academic Performance in a Sample of Japanese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030757

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal observational study of 261 Japanese children aged 7–10; screen time and physical activity were questionnaire-assessed in 2016 and academic performance (teacher grades in four subjects) evaluated in 2017. Logistic regression adjusted for sex, grade, and degree of obesity found an odds ratio of OR = 2.04 (95% CI: 1.11–3.78, p < 0.05), i.e., "2.04 (95% CI: 1.11–3.78, p < 0.05) times greater odds".

> "children in the low screen time/low physical activity group had 2.04 (95% CI: 1.11–3.78, p < 0.05) times greater odds of having high academic performance after one year compared with those engaged in high screen time/low physical activity."
