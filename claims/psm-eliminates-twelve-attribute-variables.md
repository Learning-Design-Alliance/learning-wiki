# Propensity score matching eliminates the statistically significant differences of 12 research attribute variables between low and high effect size studies

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before adjustment, 13 attribute variables differed significantly at the .05 level between high and low effect size groups; after propensity score matching, 12 of them no longer differed significantly. [→ Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022](#itsarangkul-na-ayutthaya-2022)

## Evidence

### Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya 2022

Itsarangkul Na Ayutthaya, T., & Damrongpanit, S. (2022). A Meta-Analysis of Instructional Management Model for Students’ Creative Thinking Development: An Application of Propensity Score Matching. European Journal of Educational Research, 11(4), 2429-2444. https://doi.org/10.12973/eu-jer.11.4.2429

`q2 · i?`

Comparison of 26 research attribute variables before and after propensity score matching across 400 studies (Table 3 prints per-variable t, F and F* tests). The article states the matching "eliminated the differences in the extraneous variables of the samples".

> "After the propensity score adjustments, it was found that the 12 variables of the research attribute variables are probable and different with no statistical significance at the . 05 level. They were manufacturing research institutions, year of publication, educational institutions, curriculum, creative thinking indicators, instructional materials, types of res earch, research objectives, research groups, research protocols, statistics used in research, and quality of research."
