# Including late pretest data in RCT analyses can bias post-test impact estimates because pretests are collected after random assignment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 theoretical `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` When pretest data are collected after random assignment for logistical reasons, including them in the analysis could bias post-test impact estimates. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2010](#peter-z-schochet-2010)

`q2 i?` Because pretest data are often collected after random assignment for logistic reasons, including them in the analysis could bias post-test impact estimates. [→ Peter Z. Schochet 2008](#peter-z-schochet-2008)

## Evidence

### Peter Z. Schochet 2010

Peter Z. Schochet. (2010). The Late Pretest Problem in Randomized Control Trials of Education Interventions. Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics, vol. 35, no. 4. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/journal-article-the-late-pretest-problem-in-randomized-control-trials-of-education-interventions

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical analysis of the late pretest problem in education RCTs. The article states that "pretest data often are collected after random assignment, so including them in the analysis could bias post-test impact estimates."

> "For logistical reasons, however, pretest data often are collected after random assignment, so including them in the analysis could bias post-test impact estimates."

### Peter Z. Schochet 2008

Peter Z. Schochet. (2008). The Late Pretest Problem in Randomized Control Trials of Education Interventions. Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical analysis in the paper's framing of the problem: pretests collected after random assignment may be contaminated by treatment, so using them "could bias the post-test impact estimates". No effect size is reported; this is the paper's motivating analytical claim.

> "For logistic reasons, however, pretest data are often collected after random assignment, so that including them in the analysis could bias the post-test impact estimates."
