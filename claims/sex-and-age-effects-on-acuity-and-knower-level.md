# Female preschoolers showed significantly better ANS acuity than males at Session 5, and knower level increased significantly with age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` ANCOVA showed ANS acuity was significantly better for females (M = 0.43) than males (M = 0.65) at S5 (F(2,37) = 4.27, p = .02), and only marginally so at S2 (p = .09). [→ Shusterman Anna 2016](#shusterman-anna-2016)
`q3 i?` Children's knower level significantly increased with age (rs(41) = .55, p < .01 at S2; rs(38) = .51, p < .01 at S5). [→ Shusterman Anna 2016 (2)](#shusterman-anna-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Shusterman Anna 2016

Shusterman Anna, Slusser Emily, Halberda Justin, Odic Darko. (2016). Acquisition of the Cardinal Principle Coincides with Improvement in Approximate Number System Acuity in Preschoolers. PLoS ONE 11(4): e0153072. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153072

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis with age as covariate in the cross-sectional sample; the S2 difference was marginal only. Age and sex were therefore controlled in subsequent analyses.

> "ANS acuity at S2 was slightly better for females (n = 21, M = 0.48) than males (n = 17, M = 0.75) at S2 (F(2, 37) = 2.62, p = .09), and significantly better for females (n = 20, M = 0.43) than males (n = 18, M = 0.65) at S5 (F(2,37) = 4.27, p = .02)."

### Shusterman Anna 2016 (2)

Shusterman Anna, Slusser Emily, Halberda Justin, Odic Darko. (2016). Acquisition of the Cardinal Principle Coincides with Improvement in Approximate Number System Acuity in Preschoolers. PLoS ONE 11(4): e0153072. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153072

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`
