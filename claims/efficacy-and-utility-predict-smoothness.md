# Both self-efficacy and utility-value predict task-general teaching quality (smoothness) in technology-based lessons

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both self-efficacy and utility-value significantly predicted the smoothness of technology integration, indicating both are linked to maintaining classroom management in technology-based environments. [→ Backfisch 2021](#backfisch-2021)

## Evidence

### Backfisch 2021

Backfisch, I., Lachner, A., Stürmer, K., & Scheiter, K. (2021). Variability of teachers’ technology integration in the classroom: A matter of utility!. Computers & Education, 166, 104159. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104159

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed effect models found both self-efficacy and utility-value predicted task-general teaching quality (smoothness). The article reports "both utility-value and self-efficacy predicted the task-general teaching quality" with printed estimates and confidence intervals excluding zero.

> "Interestingly, both utility-value and self-efficacy predicted the task-general teaching quality (i.e., smoothness of technology integration): self-efficacy, Estimate = 0.448, SE = 0.165, 95% CI [0.124, 0.771]; utility-value, Estimate = 0.417, SE = 0.128, 95% CI [0.166, 0.669]."
