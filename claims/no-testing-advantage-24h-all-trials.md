## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Phase 3, accuracy was significantly better for trials following restudy than for those following a test (d = 1.24). [→ Liu 2016](#liu-2016)
`q3 i3` At the Day 2 final test, accuracy was significantly higher after one restudy plus one test and after two restudies than after two tests (d = 0.91 and d = 0.68). [→ Liu 2016 (2)](#liu-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Liu 2016

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Behavioral accuracy analysis of all trials in the within-subject experiment (20 subjects, three training conditions). At Phase 3, "accuracy was significantly better for trials following restudy (SST-T condition) than for those following a test (STT-T condition)", d = 1.24.

> "For Phase 3, accuracy was significantly better for trials following restudy (SST-T condition) than for those following a test (STT-T condition), t 19 = 5.441, P < 0.001, d = 1.24."

### Liu 2016 (2)

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Day 2 final-test accuracy across the three conditions in the same behavioral analysis. Accuracy was "significantly higher following either one restudy and one test (SST-T) or following two restudies (SSS-T) as compared to trials that followed two tests (STT-T)" (d = 0.91; d = 0.68).

> "Accuracy was significantly higher following either one restudy and one test (SST-T) or following two restudies (SSS-T) as compared to trials that followed two tests (STT-T) (t 19 = 4.02, P = 0.001, d = 0.91; t 19 = 3.10, P = 0.006, d = 0.68)."
