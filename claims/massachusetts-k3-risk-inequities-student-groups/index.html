Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Socioeconomic Status — predictor: outcomes differ by group: a gap reflecting circumstances and treatment, not a cause
  • English-Learner Status — predictor: learners who differ on it differ in outcomes

Related Claims¶

  • School test score averages are strongly related to the income of the families a school serves — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Students from low income backgrounds, English learners, students receiving special education services, and Black and Hispanic students were more often identified as at significant risk — related
  • Formal early childhood experience reduces the likelihood of being identified as significantly below benchmark, most pronounced among English learner students and Black English learner students — related
  • The likelihood of being identified as significantly below benchmark in early literacy screening increases as historically underserved background characteristics intersect — related
  • Low income, English learner, special education, Black, and Hispanic students were more likely to be classified significantly below benchmark at each time period and more than once — related
  • School and district factors relate to risk identification: low income concentration correlates with repeated risk identification, and segregation modestly increases it, though some schools outperform model predictions — related
  • The likelihood of being identified as at risk increases as historically underserved background characteristics intersect, with variation by gender and race/ethnicity, and school characteristics also matter — related
  • Economically disadvantaged, English learner, special education, Black, and Hispanic students were more likely than peers to be ever below benchmark — related
  • Children from all racial/ethnic groups were projected to gain more in higher-tier programs, but most racial/ethnic differences were not statistically significant — related
