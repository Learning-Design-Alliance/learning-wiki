# In nonrandom studies where state-specific variables influence instruction, neither the ANCOVA nor the CHANGE approach reports the correct instruction effect

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Under data-generating models in which time-variant variables affect both the outcome at T1 and the instruction variable, both approaches yield biased instruction effects. [→ Köhler 2021](#kohler-2021)

## Evidence

### Köhler 2021

Köhler, C., Hartig, J., & Naumann, A. (2021). Detecting Instruction Effects—Deciding Between Covariance Analytical and Change-Score Approach. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09590-6

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical deduction illustrated with the Holzberger et al. (2013) study of reciprocal effects between teacher self-efficacy and instructional quality. The ANCOVA partials out too much variance, underestimating the effect; the CHANGE score contains inseparable state-at-T1 and δ variance, so "neither of the approaches reports the correct effect".

> "Unfortunately, neither of the approaches reports the correct effect of teacher self-efficacy under the data-generating models in Fig. 5."
