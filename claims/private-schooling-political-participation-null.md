# Private schooling shows no statistically significant association with political participation in the four-category analysis.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The pooled association between private schooling and political participation is positively signed (0.033 SD) but not statistically significant; equivalence was not tested. [→ Shakeel 2024](#shakeel-2024)

## Evidence

### Shakeel 2024

Shakeel, M. D., Wolf, P. J., Johnson, A. H., Harris, M. A., & Morris, S. R. (2024). The Public Purposes of Private Education: a Civic Outcomes Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09874-1

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

RVE confirmatory analysis (Table 2): political participation pooled ES 0.033 (SE 0.037, 152 effects, 14 databases, df 2.265), not significant. The article notes that when df < 4, p values should be interpreted with caution per Tipton (2015).

> "When divided across our four general types of civic outcomes, the effect is positive for political tolerance (0.120 SD, 99% CI), positively signed but null for political participation (0.033 SD), positive for political knowledge and skills (0.121 SD, 99% CI), and positive for voluntarism and social capital (0.043 SD, 90% CI)."
