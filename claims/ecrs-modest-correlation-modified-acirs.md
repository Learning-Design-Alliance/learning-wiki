## Evidence

### Terregino Carol A. 2019

Terregino Carol A., Copeland H. Liesel, Sarfaty Suzanne C., Lantz-Gefroh Valeri, Hoffmann-Longtin Krista. (2019). Development of an empathy and clarity rating scale to measure the effect of medical improv on end-of-first-year OCSE performance: a pilot study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2019.1666537

`q2 · i2` · `design · r2`

Concurrent-validity analysis: a single actor-trained rater used the final ECRS to rate 128 archived videotaped OSCE encounters previously scored with the modified ACIRS; Pearson r = 0.262, p = 0.003, a small correlation.

> "The validity coefficient between the two measures of communication skill, the ECRS total score and the total score for the modified ACIRS was significant though small (r = 0.262, p = 0.003)."

### Terregino Carol A. 2019 (2)

Terregino Carol A., Copeland H. Liesel, Sarfaty Suzanne C., Lantz-Gefroh Valeri, Hoffmann-Longtin Krista. (2019). Development of an empathy and clarity rating scale to measure the effect of medical improv on end-of-first-year OCSE performance: a pilot study. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2019.1666537

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same 128-encounter dataset: scores divided into weak, average and strong groups; 53/128 students matched categories exactly, 62 fell within one category, and 13 showed true mismatch between the two scales.

> "we found that 41% percent of the students (53/128) scored at the same level on both scales"
