# Eighth-grade geometry students taught with the think-talk-write strategy show higher mathematical representation ability than students taught with the expository strategy

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students taught with the think-talk-write strategy achieved significantly higher mathematical representation ability scores than students taught with the expository strategy (marginal means 76.88 vs 63.32). [→ Supandi 2018](#supandi-2018)

## Evidence

### Supandi 2018

Supandi, S., Waluya, B., Rochmad, R., Suyitno, H., & Dewi, K. (2018). Think-Talk-Write Model for Improving Students' Abilities in Mathematical Representation. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 77-90. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1136a

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental 2x3 factorial study with 64 eighth-grade students (32 experimental, 32 control) studying geometry and spatial relations; two-way ANOVA gave Fcount 63.82 for strategy (Ftable 4.01) and Scheffe's test Fcount 268.39. Marginal means were "76.88 (A2)" versus "63.32 (A1)". No effect size is printed.

> "The findings of Scheffe’s post-hoc test between A1 and A2 showed that H0 is rejected, indicating that differences exist in the mathematical representation abilities between the students exposed to the expository strategy and those exposed to the think-talk-write strategy."
