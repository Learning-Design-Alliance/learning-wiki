# Among students with low performance expectations, self-first variety (a self-focused essay followed by other-focused letters) produced higher grades than other-first variety

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Performance expectations moderated the self-first versus other-first contrast on grades (β = −0.08, p = .045); low-expectation students did better with self-first variety (β = 0.11, p = .017), while high-expectation students performed equally in both (β = −0.05, p = .231). [→ Priniski 2019](#priniski-2019)

## Evidence

### Priniski 2019

Priniski, S. J., Rosenzweig, E. Q., Canning, E. A., Hecht, C. A., Tibbetts, Y., Hyde, J. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2019). The Benefits of Combining Value for the Self and Others in Utility-Value Interventions. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000343

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the RCT's performance analyses, the interaction between baseline performance expectations and the self-first contrast was significant ("Œ≤ = ‚àí0.08, p = .045"). Simple effects showed low-expectation students (−1 SD) benefited from self-first variety, β = 0.11, p = .017; high-expectation students did not differ, β = −0.05, p = .231.
