## Evidence

### González-Sanz A 2026

González-Sanz A, López-García JC, Sutil-Rodríguez E, Juárez-Vela R, Santos-Sánchez JÁ, Navas-Echazarreta N, Martínez-Sabater A, Sancho-Sanchez C. (2026). Simulation-Based Training for Nursing Students to Improve Patient Safety: Systematic Review. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/87898

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Systematic review (20 studies) of simulation-based training for nursing students' patient safety. Its narrative conclusion is quoted below, but the review's own study tables describe the underlying study (Li et al. 2023, quasi-experimental, n=205 nursing students) as an online patient-safety course (SPOC) for all students, with two simulation cases added for the intervention group and the online course alone for the control group. So the comparison is online training plus simulation versus online training alone, not blended training versus simulation alone. Patient safety competence scores were significantly higher in the intervention group (P<.001).

> "Simulation training complemented by other techniques (lectures, presentations, debates, and demonstrations) achieves better results than simulation training alone for improving patient safety competency among nursing students"

### González-Sanz A 2026 (2)

González-Sanz A, López-García JC, Sutil-Rodríguez E, Juárez-Vela R, Santos-Sánchez JÁ, Navas-Echazarreta N, Martínez-Sabater A, Sancho-Sanchez C. (2026). Simulation-Based Training for Nursing Students to Improve Patient Safety: Systematic Review. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/87898

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Systematic review (20 studies) of simulation-based training for nursing students' patient safety. This finding comes from one included study, Wai et al. (2021), a mixed-methods pilot with 46 interprofessional medical and nursing students comparing combined classroom plus clinical simulation against clinical simulation alone; the review reports that both groups improved teamwork attitudes (P=.04) with no added benefit from classroom teaching.

> "However, the effects of a blended classroom plus clinical simulation versus clinical simulation alone on teamwork attitudes did not further improve teamwork attitudes, perceptions, and performance in medical and nursing students compared with clinical simulation alone"
