## Evidence

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group interviews with 13 interested students surfaced barriers including the genius perception (e.g., Reva calling quantum something super geniuses talk about), beliefs that a PhD is needed, perceived job risk, and the lack of women in the field.

> "One of the challenges that must be overcome if a broader group of students is going to pursue quantum is the idea that quantum is only accessible to “geniuses.”"

### Jessica L. Rosenberg 2024 (2)

Jessica L. Rosenberg, Nancy Holincheck, Michele Colandene. (2024). Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Undergraduates’ Knowledge and Interest in Quantum Careers: Barriers and Opportunities to Building a Diverse Quantum Workforce. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010138)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Focus group participants also identified opportunities: anticipated growth in quantum jobs and the intrinsic appeal of quantum, with one student describing how normal physics gets thrown out the window as really interesting.

> "Some of the opportunities in quantum were also evident from the interviews, including the possibility that there would be more quantum jobs in the future."
