# The evidence base for youth physical activity effects shows sample, geographical and publication bias, with few adolescent, brain-imaging, or South American and African studies

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Most primary studies sampled children aged 7–12 with far fewer studies on adolescents, and none were conducted in South America or Africa except South Africa. [→ Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020](#wassenaar-thomas-m-2020)
`q3 i?` Systematic reviews indicated publication bias: studies reporting significant, often positive effects are more likely to be published, and all included brain imaging studies had at least one significant outcome. [→ Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020 (2)](#wassenaar-thomas-m-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020

Wassenaar Thomas M., Williamson Wilby, Johansen-Berg Heidi, Dawes Helen, Roberts Nia, Foster Charlie, Sexton Claire E. (2020). A critical evaluation of systematic reviews assessing the effect of chronic physical activity on academic achievement, cognition and the brain in children and adolescents: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-020-00959-y

`q3 · i?`

Geographical analysis of the 118 unique primary studies included across the 19 reviews, predominantly conducted in developed countries, especially the USA and Australia. The article reports no primary studies from South America or Africa except South Africa.

> "None of the primary studies were conducted in countries in South-America or Africa, with the exception of South Africa."

### Wassenaar Thomas M. 2020 (2)

Wassenaar Thomas M., Williamson Wilby, Johansen-Berg Heidi, Dawes Helen, Roberts Nia, Foster Charlie, Sexton Claire E. (2020). A critical evaluation of systematic reviews assessing the effect of chronic physical activity on academic achievement, cognition and the brain in children and adolescents: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-020-00959-y

`q3 · i?`

The review of reviews identifies publication bias as a form of bias in the evidence base, noting in the same discussion that all brain imaging studies included at least one significant outcome, and recommending preregistration and publishing non-significant findings.

> "Systematic reviews indicated the presence of publication bias: studies that report on significant, often positive, effects of PA interventions are more likely to be published."
