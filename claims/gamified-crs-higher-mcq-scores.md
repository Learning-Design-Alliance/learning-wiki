# MCQ test scores were higher with both gamified systems than with the non-gamified SIDRA, but ranking plus teams plus points without badges did not differ from badges.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` MCQ scores varied significantly by system, with RB-G-SIDRA highest (M = 6.67) and SIDRA lowest (M = 3.98), and no significant difference between the two gamified systems. [→ López-Jiménez 2021](#lopez-jimenez-2021)

## Evidence

### López-Jiménez 2021

López-Jiménez, J. J., Fernández-Alemán, J. L., García-Berná, J. A., López González, L., González Sequeros, O., Nicolás Ros, J., Carrillo de Gea, J. M., Idri, A., & Toval, A. (2021). Effects of Gamification on the Benefits of Student Response Systems in Learning of Human Anatomy: Three Experimental Studies. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182413210

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis test of MCQ scores across the three cohorts reported "Kruskal–Wallis chi-squared (2) = 96.217, p < 0.001"; both gamified systems beat SIDRA (both adjusted p < 0.001) but RB-G-SIDRA vs R-G-SIDRA was not significant (adjusted p = 1). No effect size printed.

> "The highest average score was obtained by students using RB-G-SIDRA in the academic course 2019/2020 (M = 6.67; SD = 1.11) and the lowest average score by students using SIDRA in the academic course 2017/2018 (M = 3.98; SD = 1.42)."
