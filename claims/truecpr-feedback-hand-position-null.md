# Feedback device use shows no significant difference in correct hand positioning during simulated CPR

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Correct hand position did not differ significantly between compression with TrueCPR (95%, IQR 89–98) and manual compressions (94%, IQR 82–96). [→ Majer Jolanta 2019](#majer-jolanta-2019)

## Evidence

### Majer Jolanta 2019

Majer Jolanta, Jaguszewski Miłosz J., Frass Michael, Leskiewicz Marcin, Smereka Jacek, Ładny Jerzy R., Robak Oliver, Szarpak Łukasz. (2019). Does the use of cardiopulmonary resuscitation feedback devices improve the quality of chest compressions performed by doctors? A prospective, randomized, cross-over simulation study. Cardiology Journal. https://doi.org/10.5603/CJ.a2018.0091

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result from the same randomized cross-over simulation study; Table 1 reports correct hand position of 94 [IQR 82–96] manual vs 95 [IQR 89–98] with TrueCPR, p = 0.185. Equivalence was not tested.

> "In the case of proper hand positioning on the chest, no statistically significant differences were observed in the examined devices (Table 1)."
