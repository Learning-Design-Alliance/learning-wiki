# Teacher satisfaction with the clinical encounter was significantly higher in the serious game group than in the control group

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Adjusted teacher satisfaction at post-test differed significantly between groups (MD = 3.16, 95% CI 2.48–3.84, P < 0.0001, η² = 0.560), favoring the intervention group. [→ Deldar Kolsoum 2026](#deldar-kolsoum-2026)

## Evidence

### Deldar Kolsoum 2026

Deldar Kolsoum, Tavousi Seyed Hasan, Ahmadabadi Ali, Rakhsha Mohsen, Khodabandeloo Farnoosh, Froutan Razieh. (2026). Enhancing clinical competence in burn management through a serious educational game: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09970-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

In the randomized trial, teacher satisfaction was rated on the Mini-CEX form's built-in 1–9 satisfaction item; the adjusted between-group difference "remained statistically significant" with a printed effect size of η² = 0.560 (intervention 7.17 ± 1.40 vs control 3.97 ± 1.27).

> "After adjusting for the baseline difference using ANCOVA and correcting for multiple comparisons, the between-group difference at post-test remained statistically significant (P-value < 0.0001). The adjusted mean difference was 3.16 (95% CI: (2.48 to 3.84), indicating a large intervention effect (η2 = 0.560)"
