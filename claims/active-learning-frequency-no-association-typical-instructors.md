# In a random sample of introductory college biology courses, the weekly frequency of active-learning exercises is not associated with student learning gains on natural selection

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Weekly frequency of active-learning exercises was not associated with learning gains on the CINS-abbr (p = 0.058) or the cheetah question (p = 0.669), with negative regression coefficients in both models. [→ Andrews T. M. 2011](#andrews-t-m-2011)

## Evidence

### Andrews T. M. 2011

Andrews T. M., Leonard M. J., Colgrove C. A., Kalinowski S. T. (2011). Active Learning Not Associated with Student Learning in a Random Sample of College Biology Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.11-07-0061

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression models of course-level effect sizes (Cohen's d) on the CINS-abbr (n = 33 courses) and cheetah question (n = 29 courses), controlling for instructor experience, class size, attendance, difficulty, interest, and misconception factors. The article reports "the regression coefficients for active learning in both models were negative, although not statistically significant."

> "An instructor's use of active learning was not associated with learning gains on the CINS-abbr (p = 0.058) or the cheetah question (p = 0.669), and the regression coefficients for active learning in both models were negative, although not statistically significant"

### Andrews T. M. 2011 (2)

Andrews T. M., Leonard M. J., Colgrove C. A., Kalinowski S. T. (2011). Active Learning Not Associated with Student Learning in a Random Sample of College Biology Courses. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.11-07-0061

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Sensitivity analyses repeating the regressions with four learning-gain calculations and an alternative categorical active-learning measure; the article states "we obtained the same result" in every specification. Figure 3 shows no evidence of a positive relationship under any calculation.

> "When we calculated learning gains as average normalized gain, percent change, or raw change, we obtained the same result (Figure 3 and Table 6; Supplemental Material). When we replaced the weekly frequency of specific active-learning exercises with an instructor's more general use of any active-learning methods, we again obtained the same result."
