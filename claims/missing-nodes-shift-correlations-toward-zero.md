# Simulated node removal shifts measured centrality–performance correlations toward zero, and fewer than three percent of non-significant complete networks became significant after node removal, indicating the measured correlations are not spurious

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dropping nodes from networks with significant correlations (Mines) tended to decrease the measured correlation, and dropping nodes from simulated networks without significant correlations shifted correlations toward zero, with under three percent becoming spuriously significant. ## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2023

Nathan Crossette, Lincoln D. Carr, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2023). Correlations between student connectivity and academic performance: a pandemic follow-up. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010106)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Simulation study using random networks generated with the DD method plus node removal from the Mines network, testing five node-dropping distributions (by high/low grades, high/low centrality, random). "dropping nodes tended to shift the correlation" toward zero; no effect size is printed.

> "dropping nodes tended to shift the correlation measured in the reduce d network towards zero, and the proportion of complete networks with a non-signiﬁcant correlation that became reduced networks with a signiﬁcant correlation when dropping nodes was less than three percent."
