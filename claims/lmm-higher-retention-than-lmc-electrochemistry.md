# Students taught electrochemistry with lecture method supplemented with music (LMM) had significantly higher retention scores than students taught with lecture method supplemented with computer animation (LMC).

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students taught electrochemistry using LMM had higher retention scores than their counterparts taught using LMC, and the difference was statistically significant. [→ Akpoghol 2016](#akpoghol-2016)

`q2 i?` ANCOVA showed a statistically significant main effect of instructional method on mean retention score, with the effect in favour of LMM. [→ Akpoghol 2016](#akpoghol-2016)

## Evidence

### Akpoghol 2016

Akpoghol, T.V, Ezeudu, F.O, Adzape, J.N, & Otor, E.E. (2016). Relative Effect of Lecture Method Supplemented with Music and Computer Animation on Senior Secondary School Students’ Retention in Electrochemistry. Journal of Education and Practice, 7(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1092390

Quasi-experimental pre-test post-test non-equivalent control group study of 165 SS2 chemistry students in Makurdi; LMM group (n=80) scored higher on delayed retention test than LMC group (n=85), with "higher retention score (41.55 ± 3.74) than thei r counterparts taught using LMC (40.00 ± 3.69)".
