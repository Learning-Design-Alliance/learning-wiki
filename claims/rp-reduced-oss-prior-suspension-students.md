## Evidence

### Huang 2023

Huang, F., Gregory, A., & Ward-Seidel, A. R. (2023). The Impact of Restorative Practices on the Use of Out-of-School Suspensions: Results from a Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. [doi:10.1007/s11121-023-01507-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11121-023-01507-3)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction model from the CRCT multilevel linear probability analysis (n = 5,878). The intervention-by-prior-OSS interaction was statistically significant (B = -0.14, p = .04), and the marginal R2 increased from .108 to .142, which the authors call "substantial". For students with a prior OSS, the predicted probability of suspension was 33% in control schools and 11% in intervention schools (d = 0.77). No standardized effect size is printed for the interaction term itself, so impact is coded i? here.

> "The final model (see model 3, Table 3) showed that the intervention effect varied as a function of whether the student had received a prior OSS or not (B = -0.14, p = .04)."

### Huang 2023 (2)

Huang, F., Gregory, A., & Ward-Seidel, A. R. (2023). The Impact of Restorative Practices on the Use of Out-of-School Suspensions: Results from a Cluster Randomized Controlled Trial. [doi:10.1007/s11121-023-01507-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11121-023-01507-3)

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Illustration of the interaction (Figure 1) from the same CRCT: among previously suspended students, predicted suspension probability fell from 33% (control) to 11% (intervention), with the article printing "d = 0.77", a large effect size.

> "students in the control school with a prior OSS had a 33% probability of being suspended; in the intervention schools, the probability was only 11% (d = 0.77)."
