## Evidence

### LearnLM Team 2024

LearnLM Team, Google. (2024). LearnLM: Improving Gemini for Learning. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2412.16429](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2412.16429)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Stage 3 expert assessment of 2360 collected role-played tutoring conversations, analyzed with hierarchical Bayesian regression. The results section reports that comparative preference ratings "reveal a strong preference toward LearnLM over GPT-4o for all five comparative assessment categories", with similar but smaller preferences over Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini 1.5 Pro.

> "First, comparative preference ratings (Figure 4) reveal a strong preference toward LearnLM over GPT-4o for all five comparative assessment categories."

### LearnLM Team 2024 (2)

LearnLM Team, Google. (2024). LearnLM: Improving Gemini for Learning. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The report's summary of the same evaluation prints the aggregate preference strengths: "average preference strength of+31% over GPT-4o,+11% over Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and+13% over the original Gemini 1.5 Pro". These are preference-strength percentages, not standardized effect sizes.

> "educators and pedagogy experts preferred this version of LearnLM with an average preference strength of+31% over GPT-4o,+11% over Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and+13% over the original Gemini 1.5 Pro (see Figure 1)."
