## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students and teachers perceived communication with each other as more intensive and more connected in the online format, aided by a personal first-name approach, cameras switched on, and chat functions. [→ Reinhart A 2021](#reinhart-a-2021)
`q2 i?` Teachers found it difficult to assess students' engagement and progress when student cameras were switched off, causing teacher unease. [→ Reinhart A 2021 (2)](#reinhart-a-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Reinhart A 2021

Reinhart A, Malzkorn B, Döing C, Beyer I, Jünger J, Bosse HM. (2021). Undergraduate medical education amid COVID-19: a qualitative analysis of enablers and barriers to acquiring competencies in distant learning using focus groups. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1940765

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group finding from the Communication theme of the qualitative study. Participants reported that 'The communication with the teacher is perceived as more intensive and more connected by both students and teachers', and named cameras-on, first-name address and chat as enhancing measures.

> "The communication with the teacher is perceived as more intensive and more connected by both students and teachers, as all explicitly take their time. Specific measures may enhance productive communication between teachers and students: a personal first-name approach, the motivation of the teacher, all switching their cameras on, and a complementary chat function."

### Reinhart A 2021 (2)

Reinhart A, Malzkorn B, Döing C, Beyer I, Jünger J, Bosse HM. (2021). Undergraduate medical education amid COVID-19: a qualitative analysis of enablers and barriers to acquiring competencies in distant learning using focus groups. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1940765

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same focus group results, teacher perspective: teachers reported that without visual feedback 'it is difficult for teachers to assess whether and how well the students follow the course', leading to high unease among them.

> "without visual feedback from students with cameras switched off it is difficult for teachers to assess whether and how well the students follow the course, to determine who is focused, and to monitor the students’ learning progress leading to high unease among the teachers."
