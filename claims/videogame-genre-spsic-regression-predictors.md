## Evidence

### Yılmaz 2022

Yılmaz, E., Yel, S., & Griffiths, M. D. (2022). Comparison of children's social problem-solving skills who play videogames and traditional games: A cross-cultural study. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104548

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression on the 523-child sample: videogame genres by scenario related to SPSIC (R=.293, p<.001) and genres by primary production purpose (R=.222, p<.001). The article also reports serious (β=.145) and educational (β=.126) purposes predicted SPSIC significantly and positively.

> "the SPSIC scores of the children were negatively predicted by action-adventure videogames (β= -.191, SE=.732, p<.001) and positively by simulation videogames (β=.1 53, SE=1.115, p<.001) and puzzle videogames (β=.1 14, SE=1.2 57, p<.01)."
