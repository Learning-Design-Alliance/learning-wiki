## Evidence

### Alexander Koch 2015

Alexander Koch, Julia Nafziger, Helena Skyt Nielsen. (2015). Behavioral economics of education. Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization 115 (2015) 3–17. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2014.09.005

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Gneezy et al.'s (2011) survey of field experiments on extrinsic incentives in schools: incentives 'work well in increasing attendance and enrollment' but have 'mixed results on effort and achievement', working for some students but not others, with gender and ability playing a role.

> "Secondly, they summarize that incentives have mixed results on effort and achievement"

### Alexander Koch 2015 (2)

Alexander Koch, Julia Nafziger, Helena Skyt Nielsen. (2015). Behavioral economics of education. Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization 115 (2015) 3–17. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2014.09.005

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Field experiment reported by the review: Levitt et al. (2012) rewarded improvement on a low-stakes standardized test, varying framing, timing, and financial versus non-financial rewards; 'immediate high ﬁnancial incentives ($20) and non-ﬁnancial rewards (a trophy) improve performance'. Gain versus loss framing made no difference.

> "They ﬁnd that immediate high ﬁnancial incentives ($20) and non-ﬁnancial rewards (a trophy) improve performance, while low ﬁnancial rewards ($10) do not."
