# Dynamic systems theory and sociocultural theory are incommensurable because language is a complex adaptable, not adaptive, system

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r1` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Lantolf argues a fundamental difference makes DST and SCT incommensurable: DST treats language as a complex adaptive system, while SCT treats language as a complex adaptable, functional system created for human purposes. [→ Karimi-Aghdam 2020](#karimi-aghdam-2020)

## Evidence

### Karimi-Aghdam 2020

Karimi-Aghdam, S., & Lantolf, J. P. (2020). Dialectical Emergence of Language and Consciousness in Society: An Interview with Professor James P. Lantolf. The Journal of Applied Linguistics and Applied Literature: Dynamics and Advances, 8(2), 3-21. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED610078

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

A theoretical argument offered in the interview. Lantolf grounds it in the claim that humans modify symbolic tools over history to meet communicative and psychological goals, so language's purpose comes from human needs rather than environmental adaptation. He hedges that his understanding of DST may be off base.

> "Thus, for me, language is not a complex adaptive system, but is instead a complex adaptable system, just as any tool or human artifact is. In Luria s (1973) terminology, language, as with most other humanly created systems, is a complex functional system."
