## Evidence

### Vegada Bhavisha 2016

Vegada Bhavisha, Shukla Apexa, Khilnani Ajeetkumar, Charan Jaykaran, Desai Chetna. (2016). Comparison between three option, four option and five option multiple choice question tests for quality parameters: A randomized study. Indian Journal of Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.190757

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Concurrent validity was checked by Pearson correlation between internal test score and each format's score; all were "moderate" and significant but pairwise comparisons showed no differences (e.g., P = 0.631). Cronbach's alphas were 0.61, 0.67, and 0.75 with no significant difference.

> "These correlation coefficients were not significantly different from each other (three option vs. four option, P = 0.631, three option vs. five option, P = 0.401, four option vs. five option, P = 0.359)."

### Vegada Bhavisha 2016 (2)

Vegada Bhavisha, Shukla Apexa, Khilnani Ajeetkumar, Charan Jaykaran, Desai Chetna. (2016). Indian Journal of Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.190757

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item analysis (Table 3) compared mean discrimination index and point biserial (Rpbis) values across the three randomized groups; the article reports "There was no significant difference in item discrimination index and point biserial coefficient."

> "There was no significant difference in item discrimination index and point biserial coefficient."
