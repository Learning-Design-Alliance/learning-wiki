# Comparing datasets with and without repeated values in the ISNL perturbed a PSMT's definition of mode and prompted its revision

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` After modeling and comparing two datasets (one with a repeated value, one without) on the ISNL, Alex revised his definition to allow no mode, one mode, or more than one mode. [→ Md Amiruzzaman 2020](#md-amiruzzaman-2020)

## Evidence

### Md Amiruzzaman 2020

Md Amiruzzaman, Karl W. Kosko, Stefanie R. Amiruzzaman. (2020). Preservice Mathematics Teachers Understanding of Mode. Proceedings of the 39th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.10304](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.10304)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In episode 4 of the teaching experiment, the teacher-researcher asked Alex to compare the datasets 1, 3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9 and 1, 2, 3, 4 using ISNL models. Alex observed "in the first data set, there was a repeated number which was the mode… in the second data set there were no repeated numbers," then modeled additional datasets before revising his definition to state a data set may have no mode, one mode, or more than one mode.

> "After analyzing both data sets via the ISNL, he decided to revise his definition of mode"
