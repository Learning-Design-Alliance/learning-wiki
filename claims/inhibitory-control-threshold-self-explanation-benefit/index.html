Evidence¶

Killingsworth 2015¶

Killingsworth, S. S., Clark, D. B., & Adams, D. M. (2015). Self-Explanation and Explanatory Feedback in Games: Individual Differences, Gameplay, and Learning. International Journal of Education in Mathematics, Science and Technology, 3(3), 162-186. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1066360

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Exploratory regression analysis with physics post-test scores as dependent variable and ANT network scores, ANT neutral RT, physics pre-test score, actions per trial, and game condition as predictors. The Johnson-Neyman technique showed students with ANT executive scores below 46.72ms were predicted to have higher post-test scores in the self-explanation game.

"Moderation analyses showed a significant interaction between condition and ANT executive score, b = -.312, SEb = .123, p = .013, 95% CI ( -.557, -.068). Analyses treating condition as a moderator showed that executive scores did not predict differences in post-test scores for the explanatory feedback game, b = .094, SEb = .086, p = .276, 95% CI (-.077, .266), but that lower executive scores predicted significantly higher post -test scores for the self-explanation game, b = -.218, SEb = .085, p = .013, 95% C I (-.388, -.048)."
