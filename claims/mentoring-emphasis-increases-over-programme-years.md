# Mentees in successive years of the programme experienced more mentor emphasis on reflection, personal issues and professional identity development, with mentor presence roughly constant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-way ANOVA across Year 1–3 mentee groups showed significant increases in experienced mentor stimulation of reflection (e.g. Situation 28: Year 1 3.56 ± 0.18 to Year 3 4.48 ± 0.11, F = 8.56, p = 0.000), addressing personal issues and professional identity development, while mentor presence remained largely constant. ## Evidence

### Heeneman Sylvia 2019

Heeneman Sylvia, de Grave Willem. (2019). Development and initial validation of a dual-purpose questionnaire capturing mentors’ and mentees’ perceptions and expectations of the mentoring process. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1574-2

`q2 · i?`

One-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc tests comparing Year 1 (n = 38), Year 2 (n = 31) and Year 3 (n = 33) mentee groups on experienced mentoring situations; e.g. Situation 28 rose from 3.56 ± 0.18 to 4.48 ± 0.11 (F = 8.56, p = 0.000).

> "In Table 4a, it is shown that the mentees in different year groups experienced that more emphasis was given by the mentor on stimulation of reflection (Scale 1), the addressing of personal issues (Scale 3) and the stimulation of professional identity development as a medical doctor (Scale 5). Mentor presence (Scale 2) seemed constant, except that the mentor was more available for contact during the years."
