## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Posttest scores differed significantly across high, medium and low achievers taught with IWB, with high achievers scoring highest. [→ Gambari 2014](#gambari-2014)
`q2 i?` Scheffe post-hoc tests confirmed high achievers (X = 72.21) significantly outperformed medium (X = 61.52) and low achievers (X = 47.10), and medium significantly outperformed low achievers. [→ Gambari 2014 (2)](#gambari-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Gambari 2014

Gambari, I. A., Balogun, S. A., & Alfa, A. S. (2014). Efficacy of Interactive Whiteboard on Psychomotor Skills Achievement of Students in Isometric and Orthographic Projection. Contemporary Educational Technology, 5(4), 316-330. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105500

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA of posttest scores across three achievement levels in the IWB group. The level main effect was significant, "F (1, 41) = 9.456, p = < .05", indicating achievement level influenced outcomes when pretest was controlled.

> "Table 6 indicates that F (1, 41) = 9.456, p = < .05 for the main effect was significant at 0.05 alpha level. This indicates that there were significant difference in the posttest mean scores of the high, medium and low achiever students."

### Gambari 2014 (2)

Gambari, I. A., Balogun, S. A., & Alfa, A. S. (2014). Efficacy of Interactive Whiteboard on Psychomotor Skills Achievement of Students in Isometric and Orthographic Projection. Contemporary Educational Technology, 5(4), 316-330. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105500

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scheffe post-hoc analysis located the direction of differences among the three achievement levels. All pairwise contrasts were significant at the 0.05 level, favoring higher-achieving groups in each comparison.

> "The result in Table 8 indicates significant difference in the posttest mean scores of high achievers (X = 72.21) and medium achievers (X = 61.52) in favor of high achievers. It also indicates significant difference in the posttest scores between medium achievers (X = 61.52) and low achievers (X = 47.10) in favor of medium achievers."
