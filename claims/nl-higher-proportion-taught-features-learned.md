## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in the natural language condition learned a significantly higher proportion of the features they taught (M = 50%) compared to sentence-selection (M = 41.6%). [→ Rachel Love 2025](#rachel-love-2025)

## Evidence

### Rachel Love 2025

Rachel Love, Edith Law, Philip R. Cohen, Dana Kulić. (2025). Teaching a Conversational Agent using Natural Language: Effect on Learning and Engagement. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-025-00461-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Exploratory two-way repeated measures ANOVA on the proportion of taught features answered correctly at post-test but not pre-test. The natural language condition showed a significantly higher proportion learned, which "may indicate that this teaching modality improves recall of content that was covered".

> "participants in the natural language condition learned signiﬁcantly more of the features they taught compared to sentence-selection (natural language: M = 50% (SD = 21.9); sentence- selection: M = 41.6% (SD = 21.7)), F(1, 39) = 4.176, p = .048."
