## Evidence

### Slepkov 2021

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive statistics from a survey of 182 classroom multiple-choice tests deployed at a primarily undergraduate Canadian university between 2013 and 2019, reporting the distribution of test lengths shown in Figure 1B, where "test sizes ranged from less than 20 items to more than 100 items."

> "The mean number of multiple-choice items in a given test is 62 ± 22 (mean ± standard deviation), with the smallest MCT component being 17 items, and the largest being 106."

### Slepkov 2021 (2)

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Distribution of average test scores across 182 classroom multiple-choice tests, shown in Figure 4, indicating that "most of the surveyed tests show a class average in the range of 60%-70%."

> "The mean test score is 65% ± 8%, and the most common test score is nominally 65%. The range of test averages is large, spanning 42% to 89%."

### Slepkov 2021 (3)

Slepkov, A. D., Van Bussel, M. L., Fitze, K. M., & Burr, W. S. (2021). A baseline for multiple-choice testing in the university classroom. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1177/21582440211016838](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440211016838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Length-adjusted Cronbach's alpha (normalized to 50 items via the Spearman-Brown prediction formula) across 182 classroom tests, reported as "an excellent match to previously reported results from Frisbie (1988)" of 0.78 ± 0.08 for classroom chemistry tests.

> "When adjusted to a normalized length of 50 items, the mean reliability value observed is 〈𝛼/+〉 = 0.77 ± 0.12, and spans values from 0.20 to 0.92."
