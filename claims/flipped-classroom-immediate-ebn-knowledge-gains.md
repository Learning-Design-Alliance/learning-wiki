# Flipped classroom teaching produces significantly higher immediate post-course EBN knowledge scores than traditional lecture-based instruction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Immediately after the course, the flipped classroom group scored significantly higher on EBN knowledge than the traditional instruction group (p = 0.015). [→ Chu Tsung-Lan 2019](#chu-tsung-lan-2019)

## Evidence

### Chu Tsung-Lan 2019

Chu Tsung-Lan, Wang Jeng, Monrouxe Lynn, Sung Yu-Chih, Kuo Chen-li, Ho Lun-Hui, Lin Yueh-E. (2019). The effects of the flipped classroom in teaching evidence based nursing: A quasi-experimental study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0210606

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest nonequivalent control group study with 151 nurses (75 control, 76 experimental). Independent t-tests compared groups at posttest. The experimental group's posttest knowledge mean was 80.92±14.62 versus 75.07±14.55 for controls, described as "significantly higher scores than the control group."

> "T1 scores revealed that all the participants presented better performance, with the experimental group had significantly higher scores than the control group (knowledge p = 0.015; self-efficacy p = 0.011)."
