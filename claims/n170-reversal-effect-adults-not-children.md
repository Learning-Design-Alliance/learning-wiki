# Adults, but not children, show a differential N170 response to typical versus reversed letters

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The N170 group x condition interaction was significant; adults showed a more negative-going wave for reversed than typical letters, whereas children's conditions did not differ significantly. [→ Blackburne LK 2014](#blackburne-lk-2014)

## Evidence

### Blackburne LK 2014

Blackburne LK, Eddy MD, Kalra P, Yee D, Sinha P, Gabrieli JDE. (2014) Neural Correlates of Letter Reversal in Children and Adults. PLoS ONE 9(5): e98386. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0098386

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

ERP letter reversal task (12 adults, 12 children), N170 measured at occipito-temporal electrode sites. The group x condition interaction was significant (F(1,22) = 5.15, p = .03); follow-ups showed the adult condition effect (F(1,11) = 11.44, p = .006) and no significant child difference. No standardized effect size is reported here.

> "The N170 in adults was characterized by a more negative going wave for reversed letters than typical letters across the posterior electrodes (main effect of condition: F(1,11) = 11.44, p = .006, gp2 =. 5 1 ) , whereas in the children the reversed and typical letter conditions did not differ significantly in amplitude (F ,0.41, p..5)."
