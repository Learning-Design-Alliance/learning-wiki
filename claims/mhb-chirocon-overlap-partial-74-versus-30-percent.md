# High magical thinking and high chiropractic conservatism overlap only partially: 74% of high-ChiroCon students scored high on magical beliefs, but only 30% of high-MHB students were high on ChiroCon

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The two constructs are significantly associated but not equivalent: 74% of the 43 high-ChiroCon students scored high on MHB, whereas only 30% of the 107 high-MHB students scored high on ChiroCon (χ² = 16.311, p < 0.0001). [→ Innes SI 2022](#innes-si-2022)

## Evidence

### Innes SI 2022

Innes SI, Goncalves G, Leboeuf-Yde C. (2022). Who are the chiropractic students favouring a limitless scope of practice? Exploring the relationship with personality, magical thinking, and academic achievement. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-022-00440-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Post hoc cross-tabulation in the same 243-student survey testing whether ChiroCon is a variant of magical thinking. The asymmetry (74% vs 30%) shows "all magical thinkers did not belong to the ChiroCon group"; no effect size is printed.

> "A cross-tabulation of the two dichotomised variables MHB and ChiroCon confirmed that although 74% of the 43 students who scored high on ChiroCon scored high also on MHB, only 30% of the 107 who scored high on MHB scored high also on ChiroCon (Pearson Chi-Square, df (1) = 16.311, p = < 0.0001)."
