# VR participants report more technical issues and cybersickness symptoms than slideshow participants

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Tech issues were more frequent in the VR group (10 of 24 reported breaks due to tech issue vs 1 in slideshow), and VR participants reported cybersickness symptoms, though most were no or minimal. [→ Steele Justin 2026](#steele-justin-2026)

## Evidence

### Steele Justin 2026

Steele Justin, Haus Jeff, Renner Ann, Schmidt Cory, Synhorst David, Mariscal Joseph, Whyte Janae, Foxworthy Christian, Sisson-Smith Ryan, Priddy Alexis, Costello Jillian, Mischler Matthew. (2026). Using virtual reality or slideshow to teach ultrasound-guided lumbar puncture: immersion increases fun. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09611-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Break-reason and symptom data from the randomized trial. In the VR group, 23 (82.14%) participants had no or minimal cybersickness symptoms; only one student (3.57%) reported severe symptoms (headaches).

> "Tech issues were more frequent in the VR group, with 10 (18.18%) students reporting this as a reason for their break. Distraction was listed as a reason for break in 5 (9.09%) of the slideshow group participants and none of the VR participants."
