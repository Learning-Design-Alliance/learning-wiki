# Students commonly confuse superposition with entanglement, overgeneralizing that any multi-qubit superposition state is entangled, and the tutorial improves performance on this distinction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students commonly overgeneralize that any multi-qubit state that is a linear combination of basis states is an entangled state, though only states not writable as a product of single-qubit states are entangled. [→ Hu 2024](#hu-2024)

## Evidence

### Hu 2024

Hu, P., Li, Y., & Singh, C. (2024). Investigating and improving student understanding of the basics of quantum computing. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020108](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020108)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of student responses to Question 4d in both courses. The authors report pre-test scores around 50% for Physics students improving to around 80% post-tutorial, with students learning that the set of all superposition states is larger than the set of all entangled states.

> "many students easily make the overgeneralization that any multi-qubit state that is a linear combination (i.e., superposition) of the possible basis states is an entangled state. Only multi-qubit states that cannot be written as a product of states of each qubit are entangled."
