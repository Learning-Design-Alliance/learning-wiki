# Openness shows larger correlations with achievement in language than in STEM subject domains, a pattern that holds for both grades and test scores

## Subclaims
`q4 i2` The correlation between openness and achievement is larger in language (ρ = 0.25) than in STEM (ρ = 0.13) subject domains. [→ Meyer 2023](#meyer-2023)
`q4 i2` The language-over-STEM pattern for openness holds for grades (ρ = 0.25 vs. 0.12) and for test scores (ρ = 0.27 vs. 0.16), with no significant domain × measure interaction. [→ Meyer 2023 (2)](#meyer-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Meyer 2023

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis with robust variance estimation of 247 openness effect sizes from 60 studies (N = 229,504), corrected for measurement error, found "larger correlations in language compared to STEM subject domains" (ρ = 0.25 vs. 0.13).

> "for openness, we found a domain-specific effect, with larger correlations in language compared to STEM subject domains (ρ = 0.25 vs. 0.13; z = –7.80; p < 0.001)"

### Meyer 2023 (2)

Meyer, J., Jansen, T., Hübner, N., & Lüdtke, O. (2023). Disentangling the Association Between the Big Five Personality Traits and Student Achievement: Meta-Analytic Evidence on the Role of Domain Specificity and Achievement Measures. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09736-2

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Within the domain × measure subgroup analyses, openness correlations with language test scores (ρ = 0.27) exceeded those with STEM test scores (ρ = 0.16); the overall interaction was not significant (t[43] = –0.84; p = 0.405).

> "we found a significant difference between test scores in language and STEM subject domains (ρ = 0.27 vs. 0.16; z = –5.25; p < 0.001), with larger correlations found for languages compared to STEM test scores"
