## Evidence

### Stone Alicia 2026

Stone Alicia, Vicente Maria Asuncion, Xia Oudong, He Yuchen, Chen Chen, Yin Rong, Zhang Wuyang, Chang Haoli, Yang Wei, Li Fei, Li Xinhua, Xia Zhuying, Xie Xiaoyun, Huang Jing, Zeng Qiuming, Yang Guang, Wu Jing. (2026). Effect of Large Language Model–Powered Virtual Standardized Patients on History-Taking Among Undergraduate Medical Students: Propensity-Matched Cohort Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/92486

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Generalized linear model with a group × baseline score interaction term, adjusted for sex, major, and final test case, in matched and overall samples. The article reports the interaction as nonsignificant; the main effect of group was significant in both samples (matched mean difference 3.92, P<.001; overall 3.61, P<.001).

> "the interaction between group assignment and baseline status did not reach statistical significance in either the matched or overall samples (matched sample: P=.63; overall sample: P=.20)"

### Stone Alicia 2026 (2)

Stone Alicia, Vicente Maria Asuncion, Xia Oudong, He Yuchen, Chen Chen, Yin Rong, Zhang Wuyang, Chang Haoli, Yang Wei, Li Fei, Li Xinhua, Xia Zhuying, Xie Xiaoyun, Huang Jing, Zeng Qiuming, Yang Guang, Wu Jing. (2026). Effect of Large Language Model–Powered Virtual Standardized Patients on History-Taking Among Undergraduate Medical Students: Propensity-Matched Cohort Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/92486

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory descriptive subgroup analysis by baseline tertiles (33rd and 67th percentiles) with adjusted means. The article reports high-tertile differences of "5.93 (95% CI 2.17-9.70)" matched and "7.04 (95% CI 3.84-10.25)" overall, versus 2.94-2.99 matched and 1.29-1.64 overall for medium and low tertiles.

> "the between-group mean difference in the high baseline subgroup appeared to be 5.93 (95% CI 2.17-9.70) in the matched sample and 7.04 (95% CI 3.84-10.25) in the overall sample"
