## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Elstein, Shulman and Sprafka (1978) studies found no evidence that expert clinicians were better at generating hypotheses than novices, and instead found that experts' domain-specific prior knowledge drove their problem-solving advantage. [→ Servant-Miklos 2018](#servant-miklos-2018)

## Evidence

### Servant-Miklos 2018

Servant-Miklos, V. F. C. (2018). Problem solving skills versus knowledge acquisition: the historical dispute that split problem-based learning into two camps. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 24(3), 619–635. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-018-9835-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Historical analysis of the 1978 Elstein, Shulman and Sprafka medical problem-solving research as reported in the article. The authors "forced to acknowledge that the expertise was not one of deductive ability, but of content knowledge", yet retained the idea of trainable heuristics.

> "The authors' initial contention was that expert clinicians would fare better at hypothetico-deduction than novices, but their research found no evidence that expert clinicians were indeed better at generating hypotheses than novices."
