## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When students were asked whether a precise 4.5 star university rating was good or bad, they gave varying linguistic feedback between the two extremes, showing that words mean different things to different people. [→ Gupta 2018](#gupta-2018)

## Evidence

### Gupta 2018

Gupta, P. K., and Muhuri, P. K. (2018). Computing with words for student strategy evaluation in an examination. Granular Computing 4, no. 2, 167-184. [doi:10.1007/s41066-018-0109-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41066-018-0109-2)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Small survey conducted by the authors among a group of students using university ranking data, reported in Section 2 to motivate fuzzy-set modeling of linguistic uncertainty: though the rating was the precise value 4.5, "students gave varying feedback between these two extremes". No sample size or effect size is printed.

> "When we asked the students whether they considered 4.5 to be a ‘good’ or ‘bad’ rating, we found that students gave varying feedback between these two extremes viz. students considered rating to be ‘very good’, ‘moderately good’, ‘fair’, etc."
