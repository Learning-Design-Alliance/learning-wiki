# Emotion dynamics during problem-solving predict learning outcomes in a manner that depends on scaffolding design

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i1` small · n=132 university students (59% male), novices to data science, 43-45 per condition

Automated frame-by-frame facial-emotion coding of 132 university students across three randomized problem-solving-before-instruction scaffolding conditions found that failure-driven scaffolding produces more shame and more diverse emotion sequences than success-driven scaffolding, and that the same emotions (e.g., disgust, contempt) correlate with opposite learning-outcome directions depending on which scaffolding condition a student was in.

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Failure-driven and success-driven scaffolding both produced more surprise than unscaffolded productive failure (d=0.32-0.34 and d=0.13-0.21 respectively), while unscaffolded productive failure produced more confusion than either scaffolded condition (d=0.24-0.43). [→ Sinha 2022](#sinha-2022)

`q3 i1` Failure-driven scaffolding and unscaffolded productive failure both produced more shame than success-driven scaffolding (d=0.32 and d=0.41 respectively); hostile emotions (anger, disgust, contempt) and happiness showed no meaningful difference across conditions (all d<0.2). [→ Sinha 2022](#sinha-2022)

`q3 i1` Failure-driven scaffolding produced the widest variety of discovered emotion sequences (n=12,927 sequential patterns) and, uniquely among the three conditions, showed frequent shame-repeating sequences (e.g., shame→shame→shame) despite similar overall shame incidence to unscaffolded productive failure — indicating a difference in emotional dynamics, not just frequency. [→ Sinha 2022](#sinha-2022)

`~M i1` Within the failure-driven condition, disgust incidence correlated positively with transfer and non-isomorphic posttest scores (ρ=0.41, ρ=0.47), while contempt and happiness correlated negatively with transfer (ρ=-0.43, ρ=-0.47); within the unscaffolded productive-failure condition, the same emotions (contempt, disgust) correlated negatively with the same outcomes — the direction of association reverses depending on scaffolding design, so no single emotion is generically "good" or "bad" for learning in this design. [→ Sinha 2022](#sinha-2022)

## Evidence

### Sinha 2022

Sinha, T. (2022). Enriching problem-solving followed by instruction with explanatory accounts of emotions. *Journal of the Learning Sciences, 31*(2), 151-198. [https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2021.1964506](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2021.1964506)

`q3 · randomized three-condition experiment (n=132) with automated multimodal emotion detection (14.8 million video frames, three independent facial-coding schemes) and PANAS/curiosity self-report validation; single site, single domain, no replication` · `i1-i2 · emotion-incidence differences between randomized conditions are modest-to-medium (d~0.13-0.43) and directly attributable to the manipulation; emotion-outcome correlations (ρ~0.35-0.47) are observational within each condition, not causally isolated` · `n=132 university students (59% male), novices to data science, 43-45 per condition`

132 university students were randomly assigned to failure-driven scaffolding, success-driven scaffolding, or unscaffolded [Problem-Solving Followed by Instruction](../theories/problem-solving-followed-by-instruction.md) using [Failure-Driven Scaffolding](../strategies/failure-driven-scaffolding.md) on a bivariate data-ranking task. Automated facial action coding across the full session (14.8 million frames) inferred 28 emotion categories per frame; sequential pattern mining identified which emotions co-occurred and in what order. Emotion-incidence differences between the randomized conditions (surprise, confusion, shame) are causally attributable to the scaffolding manipulation. The correlations between emotion incidence and posttest performance, however, are observational associations computed *within* each condition after random assignment — they show that the same emotion predicts learning in opposite directions depending on instructional context, but they do not establish that any specific emotion causally drives learning.

## Discussion
The paper's most important methodological caution is built into its own framing: correlations between emotion incidence and posttest scores are not causal evidence that manipulating emotion would change learning, even though the scaffolding conditions themselves were randomized. The context-dependent reversal of the disgust/contempt-outcome relationship across conditions is the paper's most novel and best-supported finding, and argues against treating any emotion as uniformly facilitative or debilitating for learning. Self-report data (PANAS, state curiosity, cognitive dissonance) converges with the automated coding in ordering the three conditions, which increases confidence that the facial-coding measure is capturing something real rather than an artifact of the classifier.
