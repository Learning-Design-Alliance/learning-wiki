# Reactive inhibition speed and accuracy improve with age between 8 and 11 years, while proactive response slowing does not increase with age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Stop-signal reaction time decreased with age (r = −0.38) and inhibition accuracy improved with age, with no sex differences. [→ Pas P. 2021](#pas-p-2021)
`q2 i?` Proactive response slowing was not associated with age (p = 0.09) or sex (p = 0.49). [→ Pas P. 2021 (2)](#pas-p-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Pas P. 2021

Pas P., Hulshoff Pol H.E., Raemaekers M., Vink M. (2021). Self-regulation in the pre-adolescent brain. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101012

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Behavioral regression across the cohort found SSRT "was significantly associated with age F(1, 636) = 104.84, p < 0.001 (r = −0.38)"; inhibition accuracy also improved with age (F(1, 636) = 26.67, p < 0.001). The printed r = −0.38 indicates a large effect.

> "reactive inhibition latency (SSRT) was significantly associated with age F(1, 636) = 104.84, p < 0.001 (r = −0.38), but not with sex F(1, 637) = 3.08, p = 0.08, indicating that older children where faster at inhibiting responses than younger children."

### Pas P. 2021 (2)

Pas P., Hulshoff Pol H.E., Raemaekers M., Vink M. (2021). Self-regulation in the pre-adolescent brain. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2021.101012

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same behavioral analysis, proactive slowing showed no age association: "This proactive response slowing was not associated with age F(1, 636) = 2.97, p = 0.09". No effect size was printed; equivalence was not tested.

> "This proactive response slowing was not associated with age F(1, 636) = 2.97, p = 0.09, nor with sex F(1, 637) = 0.48, p = 0.49."
