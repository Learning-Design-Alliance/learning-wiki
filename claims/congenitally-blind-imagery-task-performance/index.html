Evidence¶

Bértolo 2005¶

Bértolo, H. (2005). Visual imagery without visual perception? Psicológica, 26, 173-188. https://www.uv.es/psicologica

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports Aleman et al.'s (2001) study of congenitally blind subjects on a pictorial task (comparing object contour shapes) and a spatial task (imagining a path through 2D and 3D matrices). It states "blind subjects performed worse than sighted they were able to perform both tasks"; no effect size is printed.

"Although blind subjects performed worse than sighted they were able to perform both tasks."

Bértolo 2005 (2)¶

Bértolo, H. (2005). Visual imagery without visual perception? Psicológica, 26, 173-188. https://www.uv.es/psicologica

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Zimler and Keenan's (1983) three experiments comparing congenitally blind and sighted adults and children on imagery-mediated memory tasks, including Neisser and Kerr's imaging task where "the blind, like the sighted, recalled more pictorial than concealed targets". The review notes this suggests haptic images maintain occlusion like visual images.

"the blind, like the sighted, recalled more pictorial than concealed targets."
