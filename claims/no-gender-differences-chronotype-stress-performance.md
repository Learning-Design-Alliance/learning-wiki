## Evidence

### Manjareeka Magna 2025

Manjareeka Magna, Dasgupta Subhronil, Kanungo Pratyush, Das Rama Chandra. (2025). Perceived stress and academic achievement among medical students with different chronotypes: a cross sectional study on first year medical students from India. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07281-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Gender comparison across 80 female and 68 male students using t-tests of total marks and PSS. The article reports "p =.05" for marks and "p =.06" for stress, neither reaching significance; chronotype scores were also similar (p =.26).

> "No statistically significant differences were found in total percentage marks (p =.05) or perceived stress scores (p =.06) between males and females."

### Manjareeka Magna 2025 (2)

Manjareeka Magna, Dasgupta Subhronil, Kanungo Pratyush, Das Rama Chandra. (2025). Perceived stress and academic achievement among medical students with different chronotypes: a cross sectional study on first year medical students from India. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07281-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square test of gender against chronotype category among all 148 participants, reported as "χ² = 0.60, p =.44". Equivalence was not tested; the study simply did not detect a significant association.

> "No significant association was observed between gender and chronotype (χ² = 0.60, p =.44)."
