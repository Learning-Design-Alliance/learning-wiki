# Hours in center-based child care become increasingly positively associated with cognitive scores and negatively associated with behavior scores starting around age 4–4½.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Center-based care hours were near-zero predictors of outcomes in early childhood, but from about age 4–4½ became increasingly positively related to cognitive/reading and math scores and negatively related to behavior scores. [→ Mollborn 2014](#mollborn-2014)

## Evidence

### Mollborn 2014

Mollborn, S., Lawrence, E., James-Hawkins, L., & Fomby, P. (2014). When Do Socioeconomic Resources Matter Most in Early Childhood? Advances in Life Course Research. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.alcr.2014.03.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Supplemental growth curve models interacted weekly center-care hours with age and age squared (Figure 3). The authors report that care hours "became increasingly strongly related to cognitive/reading and math scores" from about age 4–4½, with a parallel negative pattern for behavior scores.

> "Starting about at age 4 to 4½, the amount of time spent in center-based child care (which includes preschool) became increasingly strongly related to cognitive/reading and math scores."
