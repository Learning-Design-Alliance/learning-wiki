# A revised low-stress TBL format produces significantly higher individual summative assessment (iSAT) scores than the original high-stakes format

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dental students in the revised low-stress TBL format scored significantly higher on the iSAT than medical students in the original format (5.2% increase, p < .05). [→ Cynthia J. Miller 2015](#cynthia-j-miller-2015)

## Evidence

### Cynthia J. Miller 2015

Cynthia J. Miller, Jeff C. Falcone & Michael J. Metz. (2015). A Comparison of Team-Based Learning Formats: Can We Minimize Stress While Maximizing Results? World Journal of Education, 5(4). http://dx.doi.org/10.5430/wje.v5n4p1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of two cohorts: 161 medical students (6 TBLs, 966 scores) vs 119 dental students (2 pilot TBLs, 238 scores), analyzed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc test. The "revised, low-stress format had significantly higher scores on the iSAT"; the discussion reports a 5.2% increase.

> "However, the revised, low-stress format had significantly higher scores on the iSAT (n=119-161, p <.05)."
