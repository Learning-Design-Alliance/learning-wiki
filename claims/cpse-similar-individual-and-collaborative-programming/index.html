Evidence¶

Ting-Chia Hsu 2021¶

Ting-Chia Hsu, Hal Abelson, Evan Patton, Shih-Chu Chen, Hsuan-Ning Chang. (2021). Self-efficacy and behavior patterns of learners using a real-time collaboration system developed for group programming. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-021-09357-3

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Behavioral experiment in which 13 adult learners completed two individual projects then two RTC collaborative projects, with CPSE (16 items, Cronbach's α = 0.96) surveyed after each phase. The overall average of the 13 cases "did improve, but did not achieve statistical significance".

"this study confirmed that the Computer Programming Self-Efficacy (CPSE) was similar for individual and multi-user programming overall. The CPSE of the homogeneous CS-major groups engaged in programming within the RTC was higher"

Related Claims¶

  • Adult learners using RTC showed a two-way BMO–BCC revision loop, a DEL–DCR interface cycle, and more OI interaction than in individual programming
  • Different group compositions adopted different collaboration strategies: homogeneous groups split interface and programming work, while the heterogeneous CS/non-CS pair showed the most divergent behavior patterns
  • Homogeneous CS-major groups reported higher CPSE in collaborative programming than homogeneous non-CS and heterogeneous groups, with no significant difference between the latter two — related
  • Whether individual or peer practice works better with ASR-based pronunciation training is unsettled: one study found individual work best, a meta-analysis found peer practice gave larger effects — related
  • Pre-service teachers' collaborative diagnostic accuracy does not per se exceed individual diagnostic accuracy, but collaboration support can significantly enhance the added value of collaborative diagnosis — related
  • In the collaborative MIT App Inventor study, significant interaction patterns signified coordination of behavior between team participants, and no lower self-efficacy emerged for non-CS-background participants — related
