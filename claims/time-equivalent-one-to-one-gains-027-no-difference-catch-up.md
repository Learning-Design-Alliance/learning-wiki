# The equivalent-time one-to-one group outperformed control (+0.27) with no significant difference from the Catch Up group, suggesting gains came from one-to-one teaching rather than Catch Up Numeracy itself

## Evidence

### National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER) 2014

National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER), evaluation team led by Simon Rutt. (2014). Catch Up® Numeracy: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://www.educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/evaluation

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Main ITT multilevel analysis of the trial: control pupils averaged post-test scores 3.54 points below the time-equivalent group, while the Catch Up group averaged 0.79 points below the time-equivalent group, a difference that was not significant; no effect size is printed for this contrast.

> "Table 6 shows that there is a significant difference  in the post-test maths standardised score, between the pupils in the control group and those in the time equivalent group, but there is no significant difference between the time equivalent group and the Catch Up group."

### National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER) 2014 (2)

National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER), evaluation team led by Simon Rutt. (2014). Catch Up® Numeracy: Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://www.educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/evaluation

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

The same ITT analysis reports the time-equivalent versus control effect with a printed effect size of "0.27 (CI 0.49 – 0.06)" using Hedges' g, a small effect, roughly four months of estimated progress.

> "The effect size for the significant effect  identified above is 0.27 (CI 0.49 – 0.06), based on Cohen’s d adjusted for small sample sizes using Hedges’ g."
