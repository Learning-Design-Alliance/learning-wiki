# No common underlying proficiency emerges for oral language: bifactor models of expressive and definitional vocabulary did not converge

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Bifactor models of expressive and definitional vocabulary did not converge, indicating English and Spanish vocabulary items measured only language-specific variance. [→ Goodrich J. Marc 2017](#goodrich-j-marc-2017)
`q3 i?` Expressive vocabulary factors correlated negatively across languages (r = −.12, p < .05) and definitional vocabulary factors were uncorrelated (r = −.02, p = .72). [→ Goodrich J. Marc 2017 (2)](#goodrich-j-marc-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Goodrich J. Marc 2017

Goodrich J. Marc, Lonigan Christopher J. (2017). Language-Independent and Language-Specific Aspects of Early Literacy: An Evaluation of the Common Underlying Proficiency Model. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000179

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factor analysis of vocabulary items from the TOPEL and Pre-CTOPPP-Spanish in 858 language-minority preschoolers; "A bifactor model of expressive vocabulary did not converge". The definitional vocabulary bifactor model also did not converge.

> "A bifactor model of expressive vocabulary did not converge, indicating that English and Spanish expressive vocabulary items only measured variance unique to the language of the item."

### Goodrich J. Marc 2017 (2)

Goodrich J. Marc, Lonigan Christopher J. (2017). Language-Independent and Language-Specific Aspects of Early Literacy: An Evaluation of the Common Underlying Proficiency Model. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000179

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Two-factor model results for expressive vocabulary; "The correlation between the English and Spanish Expressive Vocabulary factors was negative and statistically significant (r = −.12, p < .05)". Definitional vocabulary correlation was not statistically significant.

> "The correlation between the English and Spanish Expressive Vocabulary factors was negative and statistically significant (r = −.12, p < .05)."
