# Ship speed calculated from a Google Earth wake image (31 km/h) closely matches the boat's known cruise speed (33 km/h)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applying v = √(gλ/2π)/sin θ to wake measurements (λ = 22 m, θ = 43°) from a Google Earth image yields a ferry speed of 31 km/h, close to the identified boat's cruise speed of 33 km/h. [→ C E Aguiar and A R Souza 2009](#c-e-aguiar-and-a-r-souza-2009)

## Evidence

### C E Aguiar and A R Souza 2009

C E Aguiar and A R Souza. (2009). Google Earth Physics. https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3333 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0907.3333](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0907.3333)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Worked example by the authors on a Google Earth picture of a ferry boat near Rio de Janeiro, using ImageJ measurements and the 50 m scale fixed by Google Earth. The calculated speed of 31 km/h compares with the identified catamaran Pégasus cruise speed of 33 km/h, described as quite close.

> "we measured the wavelength and angle indicated in the ﬁgure, obtaining λ = 22 m and θ = 43 ◦. With these results, and taking g = 9 .8 m/s2, we ﬁnd from (3) that the velocity of the ferry boat is v = 31 km /h."
