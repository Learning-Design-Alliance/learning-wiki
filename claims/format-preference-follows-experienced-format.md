## Evidence

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square test comparing preferred learning format across the two randomized groups after training found "a statistically significant correlation between the training format and the preferred learning format"; the authors interpret the pattern as a mere exposure effect.

> "The results demonstrated a statistically significant correlation between the training format and the preferred learning format (χ² [2] = 226.313, p <.001, p_adj = 0.013, Cramér's V = 0.466)."

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025 (2)

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparison of preferred formats across the two randomized groups (Fig. 2B and C): "the paramedics who participated in the webinar demonstrated a preference for the synchronous learning format, whereas those using the learning platform favored the asynchronous learning format".

> "A comparison of the two study groups indicated that the paramedics who participated in the webinar demonstrated a preference for the synchronous learning format, whereas those using the learning platform favored the asynchronous learning format (Fig. 2B and C)."
