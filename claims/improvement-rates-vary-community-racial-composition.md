# School improvement rates varied by community type: Latino, racially diverse, and integrated schools improved disproportionately, while stagnation concentrated in predominantly African-American low-income communities

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Improving and stagnating schools existed in all kinds of communities, but improving schools were disproportionately Latino, racially diverse, and racially integrated schools. [→ Sebring 2006](#sebring-2006)
`q2 i?` Stagnating schools were more common among predominantly African-American schools in low-income or very poor communities; among African-American schools of moderate income, roughly equal numbers improved and stagnated. [→ Sebring 2006](#sebring-2006)

Community-context analysis of Chicago elementary schools' 1990-1996 achievement trends, contrasting improvement and stagnation rates across community racial composition and income types.
