# Readers with an entertainment goal generate more associative inferences than readers with a study goal, though the participant-level test was marginal

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Readers reading for entertainment produced more associations connecting text to personal experience than readers reading to study, with the effect marginal against subject variability. [→ P. van den Broek 2001](#p-van-den-broek-2001)

## Evidence

### P. van den Broek 2001

P. van den Broek, R. F. Lorch, Jr., T. Linderholm, & M. Gustafson. (2001). The effects of readers' goals on inference generation and memory for texts. Memory & Cognition, 29(8), 1081-1087. https://link.springer.com/journal/13414

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Think-aloud ANOVA from the same experiment: entertainment-goal readers made more associative inferences (retrieving information unrelated to text coherence) than study-goal readers. The item-level test was reliable, but "the effect was marginal in the test against subject variability."

> "Table 1 shows that the readers in the entertainment conditionproduced more associations than did the readers in the study condition, although the effect was marginal in the test against subject variability [ F1(1,55) = 3.86, MSe = .006, .05 , p , .1, F2(1,135) = 20.57, MSe = .0107]."
