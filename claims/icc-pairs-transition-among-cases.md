# Inventing students transition among contrasting cases far more often than told-then-practicing students, who apply the formula case by case

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Videotaped ICC pairs shifted attention between cases an average of 20.1 times versus 6.0 for T&P pairs (d = .92). [→ Schwartz 2011](#schwartz-2011)

## Evidence

### Schwartz 2011

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Behavioral coding of 24 randomly selected videotaped pairs working on the clown worksheet; a transition was coded when at least one student shifted referencing from one company to another.

> "The ICC pairs transitioned an average of 20.1 times (SD = 10.3) compared to 6.0 (SD = 4.7) for the T&P pairs; F(1, 22) = 18.7, p < .001, d = .92."

### Schwartz 2011 (2)

Schwartz, D. L., Chase, C. C., Oppezzo, M. A., & Chin, D. B. (2011). Practicing versus inventing with contrasting cases: The effects of telling first on learning and transfer. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://aaalab.stanford.edu/transfer.html. [doi:10.1037/a0025140](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025140)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary correlational analysis of the videotaped pairs; for ICC the correlation between transitions and structural recall was non-significantly negative, r(12) = -.25, p = .38.

> "Within the T&P condition nine of ten pairs exhibited ten or less transitions, and there was no correlation between number of transitions and a pair's average structural recall, r(12) = .05; p = .87."
