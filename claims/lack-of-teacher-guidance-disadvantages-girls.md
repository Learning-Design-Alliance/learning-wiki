# The absence of a teacher-guided consolidation phase was disadvantageous notably for girls, who yielded lower retention scores in CALC than in CALT or TAL.

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In CALC, gender effects on knowledge scores were reduced: there was no significant gender effect at retention (K3: Z = −0.949, p = 0.343) and hardly any at post-test (K2: Z = −2.065, p = 0.039). [→ Conradty 2016](#conradty-2016)

## Evidence

### Conradty 2016

Conradty, C. & Bogner, F. X. (2016). Hypertext or Textbook: Effects on Motivation and Gain in Knowledge. Education Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030029

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U tests comparing girls' and boys' knowledge scores within the CALC group at post-test and retention, showing the gender gap largely disappeared without teacher-guided consolidation.

> "Accordingly, gender effects were reduced in CALC: there is no gender effect in K3 (MWU: Z = −0.949, p = 0.343) and hardly any in K2 (MWU: K2 Z = −2.065, p = 0.039)."

### Conradty 2016 (2)

Conradty, C. & Bogner, F. X. (2016). Hypertext or Textbook: Effects on Motivation and Gain in Knowledge. Education Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030029

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney U test on girls' post-test scores comparing TAL with other conditions, reported as significant, while no comparable treatment effect was found for boys.

> "The treatment effect is significant for girls with TAL (K2 MWU: Z = −2.858, p = 0.004). But there is no treatment effect with the boys."
