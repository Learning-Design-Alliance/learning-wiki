## Evidence

### Albert Munoz 2026

Albert Munoz, Mercedez Hinchcliff, Cameron Langfield and Ann Rogerson. (2026). How strong is the evidence in generative AI-related academic misconduct allegations? A mixed-methods analysis. International Journal for Educational Integrity. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40979-026-00235-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of institutional case records at one regional Australian university found GenAI-related allegations rose from 11.9% of cases in 2023 to 36.9% in 2025; the chi-square test gave "V = 0.250, approaching a moderate effect". The study reports this as proportional growth, not a causal claim.

> "A chi-square test confirmed that this proportional growth across the years is not attributable to random variation (χ²(2) = 320.93, p < .001), with Cramér's V reported as the effect size measure given its ability to standardise the chi-square statistic against both sample size and table dimensions (V = 0.250, approaching a moderate effect; Cohen, 2013)."
