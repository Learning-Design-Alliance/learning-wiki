## Evidence

### Innes SI 2020

Innes SI, Kimpton A. (2020). Are Councils on Chiropractic Education expectations of chiropractic graduates changing for the better: a comparison of similarities and differences of the graduate competencies of the Chiropractic Council on Education-Australasia from 2009 to 2017. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-020-00315-8

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Summative content analysis of 100 key words across the two documents, reported in Results with Table 4. The article states the frequency of "evidence-based" "has been shown to be an indicator of the quality of accreditation standards and their regulation", citing other work. Word counts are descriptive, not effect sizes.

> "Words that increased in frequency in the 2017 graduate competencies, despite being smaller, that indicated a move toward a contemporary mainstream health care approach were collaboration (3 in 2009 versus 7 in 2017), evidence-based (1 vs. 9), professional (30 vs. 43), patient-centred (0 vs. 3), and research (1 vs. 5)."
