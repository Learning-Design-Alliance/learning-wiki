Related Claims¶

  • Controlled GenAI virtual-patient and communication studies reported short-term skill gains, but positive findings were not universal — related
  • Overall 3E, Empower, and Empathize scores were higher in the second AI-patient encounter, but the uncontrolled design precludes attributing this to training — related
  • AI-powered simulations show short-term gains in communication confidence and structured knowledge in RCTs, with limited or inferior effects for tactile psychomotor skills — related
  • Student satisfaction with virtual patients is generally positive, with item-level comparisons dominating and some confidence exceptions — related
  • AI-generated virtual patients in HPE are used mainly for teaching and learning communication skills, diagnostic reasoning and management reasoning, with assessment a minority use case — related
  • Outcome evaluation of AI-generated virtual patients in HPE is concentrated at Kirkpatrick level 1, with no studies reporting level 3 or 4 outcomes — related
  • Repeated exposure to AI-VPs is associated with improved perceived self-efficacy and more positive attitudes toward communication training — reports the opposite
  • Evidence of transfer from AI-VP training to real clinical practice is scarce, and one cohort study found lower field assessment scores after AI-VP training — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Virtual patient-based learning produces greater learning performance, self-efficacy and communication skills than conventional learning in a quasi-experimental study — reports the opposite
