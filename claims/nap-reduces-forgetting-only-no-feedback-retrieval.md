# Napping reduces forgetting only for items learned by retrieval practice without feedback, not for restudied or feedback-retrieved items

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a nap-vs-wake experiment, the nap group showed significantly lower recall change rates (less forgetting) than the wake group only under retrieval practice without feedback; no significant differences appeared under restudy or feedback retrieval. [→ Jing Zhang 2025](#jing-zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Jing Zhang 2025

Jing Zhang, Xiaoning Huo, Hongbo Lv, Jiahua Xu & Xiaofeng Ma. (2025). Offline consolidation mechanisms of the retrieval practice effect: an analysis based on EEG signal characteristics. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00349-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Behavioral repeated-measures ANOVA of the recall change rate (delayed minus immediate over immediate) in the 48-participant nap/wake experiment; the simple-effect analysis showed "the nap group showing lower rates than the wake group" only for no-feedback retrieval practice (p < 0.001).

> "only under the retrieval practice without feedback condition were there signiﬁcant differences in word pair recall change rates between the nap and wake groups (p < 0.001), with the nap group showing lower rates than the wake group"
