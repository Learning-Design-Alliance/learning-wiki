# A self-coding MI exercise is not superior to passive video observation for building therapeutic empathy in an 8-hour introductory MI training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized pilot of 55 undergraduates, the interaction of training time and group allocation on HRQ empathy scores was non-significant (F1,51 = 2.12, p = .151, partial η² = .040), so the self-coding intervention was not measurably superior to video viewing. ## Evidence

### Simper Trevor 2020

Simper Trevor, Agley Jon, DeSalle Mallori, Todd Jennifer, Dutta Tapati. (2020). Pilot study of the influence of self-coding on empathy within an introductory motivational interviewing training. BMC Medical Education 20:43. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-1956-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pilot randomized controlled trial with 1:1 allocation at two universities (UK and USA); mixed ANOVA on the HRQ empathy measure. The "partial ƞ2 statistic of .040" indicates a small, non-significant interaction effect; equivalence was not tested.

> "The interaction effect measured the degree to which the change in therapeutic empathy over time was different for the experimental and control groups. This effect was non-significant (F1,51 = 2.12, p = .151), with a partial ƞ2 statistic of .040"
