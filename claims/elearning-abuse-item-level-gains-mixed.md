# Item-level percentage-correct scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test for eight test items but did not change significantly for seven others, in one group with no comparison group; most of the latter were already answered correctly at pretest

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In one group with no comparison group, percent correct rose significantly from pre-test to post-test for eight of the 15 knowledge items, including the item on the law defining abuse (p < 0.001). [→ Baba Kaori 2023](#baba-kaori-2023)
`q2 i?` In one group with no comparison group, the percentage of correct answers did not change significantly from pre-test to post-test for seven items; most were already answered correctly in the pretest. [→ Baba Kaori 2023 (2)](#baba-kaori-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Baba Kaori 2023

Baba Kaori, Kataoka Yaeko. (2023). An E-Learning Program for Continuing Midwifery Education on Handling High-Risk Abuse Cases: A Pretest–Posttest Design. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20136317

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item-level McNemar's tests on the 15-item knowledge test (N = 71) in the same pretest–posttest study. The article reports "statistically significant differences were found for eight items"; largest gains included the DV-victim percentage item (74.7 percentage points). No effect sizes are printed.

> "McNemar’s test was conducted on the percentage of correct answers for each item in the pre-test and post-test, and statistically significant differences were found for eight items, including “What is the law that establishes the definition of abuse?” (p < 0.001)."

### Baba Kaori 2023 (2)

Baba Kaori, Kataoka Yaeko. (2023). An E-Learning Program for Continuing Midwifery Education on Handling High-Risk Abuse Cases: A Pretest–Posttest Design. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph20136317

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same item-level McNemar's analyses (N = 71): seven items showed no significant increase, including the notification-obligation and risk-factor items. The authors attribute this to prior knowledge, suggesting "students may have acquired the knowledge before the program"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Most of the seven items for which the percentage of correct answers did not increase significantly after the program were answered correctly in the pretest, which suggests that students may have acquired the knowledge before the program."
