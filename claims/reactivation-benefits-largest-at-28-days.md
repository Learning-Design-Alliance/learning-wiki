# At the 28-day retention interval, both descriptive and component reactivations outperform both controls, with relative gains of 58.8–67.2% for images and over 100% for details

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the 28-day condition, Descriptive and Component groups recalled significantly more images and details than both the Distractor and Control groups, and relative enhancements exceeded 100% for details. [→ MacLeod 2018](#macleod-2018)

## Evidence

### MacLeod 2018

MacLeod, S., Reynolds, M. G. & Lehmann, H. (2018). The mitigating effect of repeated memory reactivations on forgetting. npj Science of Learning 3:9. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0025-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Relative-score analysis versus the no-reactivation Control average in the 28-day condition showed these percentage gains, all significantly above chance (ps < 0.05) except the Component group on details per image (p > 0.05).

> "image recall was enhanced by 58.8% (SEM = 18.4) for the Descriptive group and 67.2% (SEM = 23.9) for the Component group. Similarly, for both groups the number of details recalled increased by 134.5% (SEM = 27.34) and 113.1% (SEM = 43.4), respectively."
