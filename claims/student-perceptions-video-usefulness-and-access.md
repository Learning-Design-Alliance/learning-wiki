## Evidence

### Hibbert Emily J 2013

Hibbert Emily J, Lambert Tim, Carter John N, Learoyd Diana L, Twigg Stephen, Clarke Stephen. (2013). A randomized controlled pilot trial comparing the impact of access to clinical endocrinology video demonstrations with access to usual revision resources on medical student performance of clinical endocrinology skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post-OSCE evaluation questionnaires completed by 21 of 22 assessed students (95.5%) in the pilot trial captured perceptions; for the thyroid video, 55.6% (5/9) strongly agreed and 44.4% (4/9) tended to agree it was useful.

> "Eleven of 12 students (91.7%) who viewed both the LLE and DMH videos strongly agreed on a 5 point Likert scale (strongly agreed - tend to agree - neither agree or disagree - tend to disagree - strongly disagree) that the videos were useful for them in learning the clinical skill."

### Hibbert Emily J 2013 (2)

Hibbert Emily J, Lambert Tim, Carter John N, Learoyd Diana L, Twigg Stephen, Clarke Stephen. (2013). A randomized controlled pilot trial comparing the impact of access to clinical endocrinology video demonstrations with access to usual revision resources on medical student performance of clinical endocrinology skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-135

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Self-report and website hit data in the pilot trial: 87 total hits on the video website, 47 by iPhone or iPod download and 40 by computer mp4 file download, indicating substantial mobile use.

> "All students randomized to the diabetes videos reported accessing them. One student randomized to the TE video reported not accessing it. The LLE and DMH videos were viewed up to 3 times on student self-report and the TE video once or twice (Table 3)."
