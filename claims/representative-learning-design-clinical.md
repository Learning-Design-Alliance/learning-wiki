# Representative learning design preserves perception-action coupling needed for real-world clinical skill

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r2`–`r3` · `q1`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Learning must occur in contexts representative of the real-world performance environment so learners perceive and act on real-world-relevant information; representative design means essential elements of the real-world context are accurately mirrored in the learning environment. [→ Brymer 2022](#brymer-2022)
`q1 i?` Practising decomposed parts of an activity out of context breaks perception-action coupling and produces less functional behaviours that may not fit the performance context. [→ Brymer 2022 (2)](#brymer-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Brymer 2022

Brymer, E., & Schweitzer, R. D. (2022). Learning clinical skills: an ecological perspective. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 27(3), 691-707. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-022-10115-9

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical exposition, not a physical-fidelity requirement: the article specifies that social and emotional components of clinical relationships, not physical replication, "should be accurately mirrored" in the learning environment.

> "Representative design refers to the process of ensuring that the essential elements of the real-world context (e.g., the therapeutic context) are accurately represented in the learning environment"

### Brymer 2022 (2)

Brymer, E., & Schweitzer, R. D. (2022). Learning clinical skills: an ecological perspective. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 27(3), 691-707. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-022-10115-9

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument about perception-action mutuality being strengthened through attunement to relevant information; the article states the resulting out-of-context practice yields "an unproductive development of less functional behaviours".

> "Reducing an intended behaviour to decomposed parts and practicing an activity out of context breaks up this perception–action coupling"
