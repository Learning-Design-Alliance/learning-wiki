## Evidence

### Song 2025

Song, M., Li, L., He, D. & Cai, Q. (2025). Reading experience reveals shared and idiosyncratic neural patterns during text comprehension. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00390-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Paired-samples t-test on manually scored 24-item multiple-choice comprehension performance from 39 participants, six items per text. The article reports expository accuracy of .917 versus narrative accuracy of .829, with the paired difference significant at p < .001.

> "Participants’ performance for expository texts (M = .917, SD = .063) was better (p < .001) than that for narrative texts (M = .829, SD = .098)."

### Song 2025 (2)

Song, M., Li, L., He, D. & Cai, Q. (2025). Reading experience reveals shared and idiosyncratic neural patterns during text comprehension. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00390-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Spearman rank correlation between comprehension accuracy and overall tART print exposure in the 39-participant analysis sample; the printed coefficient is rho = -.109 with p = .508, so no significant association was detected. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The comprehension performance did not significantly correlate with print exposure quantity (Spearman rho = -.109, p = .508)."
