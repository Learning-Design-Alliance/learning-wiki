Evidence¶

Floris M. van Blankenstein 2011¶

Floris M. van Blankenstein, Diana H. J. M. Dolmans, Cees P. M. van der Vleuten, Henk G. Schmidt. (2011). Which cognitive processes support learning during small-group discussion? The role of providing explanations and listening to others. Instructional Science 39, 189–204. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9124-7

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Planned contrast on immediate recall in the randomized three-condition experiment: the combined relevant-discussion conditions outscored the irrelevant-discussion control, "t(67) = 2.31, p\ .05 (two-tailed), r = 0.27".

"Planned contrasts revealed that, directly after the group discussion, the participants who had given explanations (M = 57.50, SD = 22.72) or listened (M = 54.63, SD = 15.01) had higher recall scores than the participants in the control condition (M = 44.86, SD = 17.99), t(67) = 2.31, p\ .05 (two-tailed), r = 0.27."

Floris M. van Blankenstein 2011 (2)¶

Floris M. van Blankenstein, Diana H. J. M. Dolmans, Cees P. M. van der Vleuten, Henk G. Schmidt. (2011). Which cognitive processes support learning during small-group discussion? The role of providing explanations and listening to others. Instructional Science 39, 189–204. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9124-7

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

Planned contrast on delayed recall in the same experiment: the combined relevant-discussion conditions did not outscore the control at one month, "t(67) = 1.49, p = .14, r = 0.18"; the immediate advantage of mere participation faded.

"the participants who had provided explanations (M = 49.29, SD = 19.71) or listened during the radar discussion (M = 39.04, SD = 13.56) had no higher recall scores than the participants in the control condition (M = 38.05, SD = 13.72), t(67) = 1.49, p = .14, r = 0.18."
