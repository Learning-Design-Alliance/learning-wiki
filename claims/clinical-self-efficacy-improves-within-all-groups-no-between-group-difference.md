## Evidence

### Yıldız Rıdvan 2026

Yıldız Rıdvan, Ni̇şanci Onur Seçgin. (2026). The effect of artificial intelligence–based scenarios on the clinical education of rehabilitation students: an anatomy-based randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09220-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group paired t-tests in the randomized controlled study of 90 rehabilitation students, reported in the Results section. A "significant increase was observed in clinical self-efficacy" in all three groups.

> "In the intra-group analyses, a significant increase was observed in clinical self-efficacy and artificial intelligence attitude scores in all groups (p < .05)."

### Yıldız Rıdvan 2026 (2)

Yıldız Rıdvan, Ni̇şanci Onur Seçgin. (2026). The effect of artificial intelligence–based scenarios on the clinical education of rehabilitation students: an anatomy-based randomized controlled study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09220-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group ANOVA of change scores reported in the Results section. The study found "no significant between-group differences" for clinical self-efficacy, indicating equivalence was not tested.

> "No significant between-group differences were observed for clinical self-efficacy (p=.060) or attitude towards AI (p=.998) (Table 6)."
