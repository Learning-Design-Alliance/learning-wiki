# Equations of the form a/x = b are the most difficult equation type, reflecting procedural complexity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r2`, 1 associational `r2` · `q3` · `i2`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Equation type affected accuracy, effect size η2 p = 0.169, with all participants less accurate on C equations (a/x = b) than A and B equations. [→ Susac A 2014](#susac-a-2014)
`q3 i2` Equation type affected RTs, effect size η2 p = 0.113, and inverse efficiency indicated C equations were the most difficult. [→ Susac A 2014 (2)](#susac-a-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Susac A 2014

Susac A, Bubic A, Vrbanc A and Planinic M (2014) Development of abstract mathematical reasoning: the case of algebra. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 8:679. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00679

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Two-way mixed-design ANOVA, Age × Equation type (A vs. B vs. C), on accuracy; printed effect size η2 p = 0.169 for Equation type. "All participants were less accurate on the C equations compared to both the A and B equations."

> "a signiﬁcant main effects of both Age [ F(3,327) = 8.37, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.071] and Equation type [ F(2,654) = 66.59, p < 0.001, η2 p = 0.169] were found, as well as their interac­tion [ F(6,654) = 2.53, p < 0.05, η2 p = 0.023]"

### Susac A 2014 (2)

Susac A, Bubic A, Vrbanc A and Planinic M (2014) Development of abstract mathematical reasoning: the case of algebra. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 8:679. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00679

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Inverse-efficiency ANOVA (Age × Equation type) on equations with letters: Equation type main effect F(2,650) = 43.72, printed effect size η2 p = 0.119; "the C equations were the most dif­ﬁcult" and A and B did not differ significantly.

> "the results suggest that the C equations were the most dif­ﬁcult. There was no statistically signiﬁcant difference between the A and the B equations."
