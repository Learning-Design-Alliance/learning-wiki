Evidence¶

Benden 2026¶

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Cross-classified multilevel models (Model 2) of situation-level data showed within-person deviations in test performance predicted all four motivational beliefs; the article reports "small to moderate effects, with the strongest relation observed for self-concept."

"students who performed better in a given achievement situation—relative to their personal average across all situations—evaluated their test-specific self-concept more positively, found the math content in that situation more interesting and useful for their studies, and experienced lower psychological and effort costs (0.14 ≤ |b| ≤ 0.35, ps < .001)"

Benden 2026 (2)¶

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

q2 · i? · associational · r3

At the student level of the same cross-classified models, students' average performance across the four achievement situations predicted their motivational beliefs, accounting for "8% to 55% of the between-student variance in motivational beliefs."

"test performance showed moderate to large between-person effects on motivational beliefs, again with particularly strong associations for self-concept (0.32 ≤ |b| ≤ 0.82, ps < .001)"
