# Combined letter name and sound instruction produced greater letter name production gains than letter sound only instruction in preschoolers

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Preschool children taught letter names and sounds together made larger letter name production gains than children taught letter sounds only (d = 0.53). [→ Piasta 2010](#piasta-2010)

## Evidence

### Piasta 2010

Piasta, S. B., Purpura, D. J., & Wagner, R. K. (2010). Fostering Alphabet Knowledge Development: A Comparison of Two Instructional Approaches. Reading and Writing, 23(6), 607-626. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-009-9174-x

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment with 58 preschoolers blocked by letter knowledge and assigned to LNLS, LS, or number conditions; repeated measures ANOVA interaction contrast showed the letter name production gain difference "favoring the LNLS condition (d = 0.53)".

> "Interaction contrasts indicated differential gains in letter name production between children in the LNLS condition and those in the LS condition, F(1, 52) = 3.129, p = .041, favoring the LNLS condition (d = 0.53)."
