# Retrieval practice mostly increases susceptibility to misinformation (retrieval-enhanced suggestibility)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across most reviewed studies, taking a practice test increased participants' susceptibility to misinformation (RES), even though testing benefited memory for non-contradicted control items. [→ Rau 2025](#rau-2025)
`q3 i?` Boundary conditions exist: verbatim and criterion testing reduced suggestibility, whereas gist testing produced an RES effect. [→ Rau 2025 (2)](#rau-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rau 2025

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's narrative synthesis of 19 of the 40 misinformation-effect articles examining retrieval practice reports that "taking a practice test increased participants' susceptibility to misinformation (known as retrieval-enhanced suggestibility, RES)", while participants showed better memory for control items that had not been contradicted.

> "The results of most articles point towards negative effects of retrieval practice; that is, taking a practice test increased participants' susceptibility to misinformation (known as retrieval-enhanced suggestibility, RES)."

### Rau 2025 (2)

Rau, M. A., & Premo, A. E. (2025). Systematic Review of Educational Approaches to Misinformation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10012-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports boundary conditions of RES related to the nature of the practice test: "verbatim testing reduced suggestibility, hence showing a protective effect of testing", and criterion-testing reduced susceptibility compared to one-trial tests in studies with pre-school children.

> "Pansky and Tenenboim (2011) found an RES effect for gist testing, verbatim testing reduced suggestibility, hence showing a protective effect of testing."
