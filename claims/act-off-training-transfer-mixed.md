## Evidence

### Ockelmann 2025

Ockelmann, J., Scherpiet, S., Stropahl, M. & Giroud, N. (2025). Personalized and gamified auditory-cognitive training improves naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in older adults with hearing loss. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00369-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM analysis of lab-conducted off-training tasks completed by both groups pre- to post-training. Across-group improvements appeared for phonological short-term memory, working memory, and focused attention, but "no signiﬁcant group session inter- actions were detected", so the gains were not statistically specific to ACT; divided attention showed no change (d = 0.08 overall).

> "Across all cognitive domains, no signiﬁcant group session inter- actions were detected, indicating improvements were not statistically spe- ciﬁc to the auditory-cognitive training condition."

### Ockelmann 2025 (2)

Ockelmann, J., Scherpiet, S., Stropahl, M. & Giroud, N. (2025). Personalized and gamified auditory-cognitive training improves naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in older adults with hearing loss. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00369-4

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc simple-effects analysis of the same off-training lab battery: experimental-group gains in working memory ("d = 0.58") and focused attention (d = 0.55), while the control group's WM change was non-significant and its focused-attention gain a non-significant trend.

> "For WM and FA, signiﬁcant gains were observed only in the experimental group (WM: β = 10.73, 95% CI [4.54, 16.91], p = 0.001, d = 0.58; FA: β = 15.27, 95% CI [5.89, 24.65], p = 0.002, d = 0.55)"
