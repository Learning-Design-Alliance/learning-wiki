# The association between inhibition and first-grade achievement in math, reading, and writing is fully mediated by LRBs

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across math, reading, and writing models, when LRBs were included the direct effect of inhibition on achievement was not significant, indicating total mediation for all three domains. [→ Rivella 2025](#rivella-2025)
`q2 i?` Inhibition significantly predicted LRBs, with higher inhibition scores associated with higher LRB scores. [→ Rivella 2025 (2)](#rivella-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Rivella 2025

Rivella, C., & Viterbori, P. (2025). Executive functions and school achievement: The mediating role of learning‐related behaviour in primary school children. British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70053

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' summary of Models 2a–2c in the Results of the correlational first-grade study; in each inhibition model the indirect path through LRBs was significant while the direct effect on the outcome was not (e.g., math: β = .98, p = .481). No standardized effect sizes are printed for the indirect paths per se.

> "In contrast, the relationship between inhibition and school performance is fully mediated by LRBs. This suggests that when LRBs are included in the models, inhibition is no longer associated with learning outcomes."

### Rivella 2025 (2)

Rivella, C., & Viterbori, P. (2025). Executive functions and school achievement: The mediating role of learning‐related behaviour in primary school children. British Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70053

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path a of Model 2a in the same study, showing inhibition's regression coefficient on teacher-rated LRB (β = .567, p < .001); as a regression coefficient this is not treated as a standardized effect size for impact coding.

> "In Model 2a (Math), inhibition significantly predicted LRB, with higher Inhibition scores associated with higher LRB scores (β = .567, p < .001, CI [2.539, 5.406])."
