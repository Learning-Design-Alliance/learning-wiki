## Evidence

### Junga A 2026

Junga A, Kockwelp P, Dabel J, Scherzer SH, Risse B, Holling M, Marschall B, Friederichs H. (2026). Olfactory Cues and Patient Safety Behavior in Virtual Reality Medical Simulation: Controlled Trial. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/93148

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation (type e) offered in the discussion to explain the low control-group baseline; they argue the intervention "does not enhance hygiene behavior beyond real-world baselines but rather restores cue-triggered behavior under impoverished sensory conditions". This interpretation was not directly tested.

> "the environmental cues that normally prompt hand hygiene in real settings, such as ambient scent, tactile feedback from the dispenser, and the physical proximity of cleansed surfaces, are absent or only weakly present in a purely audiovisual VR scene."
