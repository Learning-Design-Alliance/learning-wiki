# A one-factor unidimensional model fits MAP computerized adaptive Reading and Mathematics tests across grades, terms, and ten states

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` CFA of goal-level scores shows the one-factor (unidimensional) model is the most reasonable model for MAP tests in the ten studied states. [→ Shudong Wang 2013](#shudong-wang-2013)

## Evidence

### Shudong Wang 2013

Shudong Wang, Marty McCall, Hong Jiao, & Gregg Harris. (2013). Construct Validity and Measurement Invariance of Computerized Adaptive Testing: Application to Measures of Academic Progress (MAP) Using Confirmatory Factor Analysis. Journal of Educational and Developmental Psychology, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jedp.v3n1p88

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

CFA (Proc TCALIS, maximum likelihood) on goal scores from MAP Reading and Mathematics tests in ten states, grades 3-9, five academic terms. Fit indexes met Hu and Bentler criteria, and "the one-factor (unidimensional) model is the most reasonable model". Michigan Mathematics RMSEAs were exceptions.

> "All values of fit indexes (except RMSEAs for Michigan MAP Mathematics tests) satisfy the Hu and Bentler (1999) criteria and show that each model fits data extremely well for different content areas, grades, terms, and states. Overall results suggest that the one-factor (unidimensional) model is the most reasonable model for MAP tests in these 10 states."
