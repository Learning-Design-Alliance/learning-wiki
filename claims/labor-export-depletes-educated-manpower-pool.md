# Labor export policy depletes the educated segment of the Philippine manpower pool, explaining the remaining workforce's under-education

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The deployment of overseas contract workers depletes the Philippine manpower pool of people with relatively high educational qualifications, helping to explain why the remaining domestic labor force is characterized as predominantly under-educated and rural-based — an analysis the author offers as a departure from the official EDCOM characterization that ignores OCW deployment. `q2 · i?`

Interpretive analysis in Part II drawing on 1990 Census data, EDCOM Report statistics, and National Economic Development Authority occupational distribution data for OCWs from 1975-1981. The author argues that exported labor is predominantly college-educated, which explains why the remaining domestic labor force is 49.78% completed only Grade 6 or less. No inferential test is reported.

> "This analysis is a departure from the usual and official analysis (including EDCOM) of the Philippine manpower pool which does not take into account the size of the OCW deployment and the effects of the present labor export policy."
