# Practitioner-implemented Fusion produced a statistically significant positive effect on the proximal ProFusion mathematics measure in a quasi-experimental study

## Subclaims

`q2 i2` At-risk first-grade students receiving Fusion from school staff made significantly greater gains than comparison students on the proximal ProFusion measure (g = .61). [→ Strand Cary 2017](#strand-cary-2017)

`q2 i?` First-grade at-risk students receiving Fusion from school staff made significantly greater fall-to-spring gains on the proximal ProFusion measure than comparison students (p = .046, g = .61). [→ Mari G. Strand Cary 2017](#strand-cary-2017)

## Evidence

### Strand Cary 2017

Strand Cary, M. G., Clarke, B., Doabler, C. T., Smolkowski, K., Fien, H., & Baker, S. K. (2017). A practitioner implementation of a Tier 2 first grade mathematics intervention. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(4), 211–224. https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948717714715

`q2 · i2`

Quasi-experimental time-by-condition (gain-score) analysis of 253 first-grade students in ten Hawaii schools, with students most at risk assigned to Fusion (n = 154) and less at-risk peers to comparison (n = 99). The article reports a significant positive effect on the proximal measure, "p = .046; g = .61".

> "Results of the time by condition analyses suggest a positive, statistically significant effect for the proximal ProFusion measure (p = .046; g = .61)."
