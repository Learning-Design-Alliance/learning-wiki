Evidence¶

Equitable & Inclusive Hiring Practices: A Reference Guide of Strategies to Grow & Diversify the Educator Workforce 2023¶

Equitable & Inclusive Hiring Practices: A Reference Guide of Strategies to Grow & Diversify the Educator Workforce. (2023). Rhode Island Department of Education, Office of Educator Excellence. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED633258

q1 · i? · design · r1

The guidance reports, citing Brill and McCartney (2008), that high-quality induction and mentoring in California schools "reduced attrition by twenty-six percent in just two years." Second-hand; no effect size printed.

"Brill and McCartney (2008) found that high quality induction and mentoring programs in California schools reduced attrition by twenty-six percent in just two years."

Equitable & Inclusive Hiring Practices: A Reference Guide of Strategies to Grow & Diversify the Educator Workforce 2023 (2)¶

Equitable & Inclusive Hiring Practices: A Reference Guide of Strategies to Grow & Diversify the Educator Workforce. (2023). Rhode Island Department of Education, Office of Educator Excellence. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED633258

q1 · i? · design · r1

The guidance states that coaching promotes retention and that "Early career teachers are twice as likely to leave the profession without coaching," also noting retention improvements linked to coaching in Tennessee and Louisiana. Second-hand; no effect size printed.

"Coaching is another effective strategy to promote teacher retention. Early career teachers are twice as likely to leave the profession without coaching."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Mentoring strongly influences new teachers' decisions to stay in or leave teaching (review attribution) — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • New teachers who receive little mentoring are twice as likely to leave the classroom as well-mentored beginners — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Well-prepared and well-mentored teachers stay in teaching at more than twice the rate of unprepared entrants, and teacher residency programs typically retain more than 80% over five years — related
  • Comprehensive induction supports keep new teachers in teaching at more than twice the rate of unsupported novices — related
  • Ohio meets criteria on high-retention teaching pathways and early-career induction with mentoring — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Teachers without mentors reached the impact level more often than mentored teachers, raising the question that mentoring may inhibit development — reports the opposite
  • On average, more effective early-career teachers remain in the teaching profession and stay in their initial schools — related
  • Few teachers receive regular mentoring, and early-career teachers of color report less access to mentoring and induction than White peers — related
  • Participation in an induction program alone does not improve the chances teachers will remain; induction quality matters, and almost 20 percent of new teachers report not participating — reports the opposite
