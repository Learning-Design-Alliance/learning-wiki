# Factor-analytic studies identify distinct curiosity dimensions, including depth versus breadth and interest versus deprivation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Ainley's factor analysis of five curiosity measures in college students yielded depth and breadth factors. [→ Jirout 2011](#jirout-2011)
`q2 i?` Litman found epistemic curiosity items load onto distinct interest and deprivation factors. [→ Jirout 2011 (2)](#jirout-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Jirout 2011

Jirout, J. & Klahr, D. (2011). Children's scientific curiosity: In search of an operational definition of an elusive concept. Developmental Review (in press, draft). https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/developmental-review

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Ainley (1987), a factor analysis of five curiosity measures; the two-factor model distinguished "depth" from "breadth" factors across scales.

> "Data from 227 college students on all five measures were best fit by a two-factor model that included "depth", associated with items that described investigation of objects, ideas, etc., in order to better understand them"

### Jirout 2011 (2)

Jirout, J. & Klahr, D. (2011). Children's scientific curiosity: In search of an operational definition of an elusive concept. Developmental Review (in press, draft). https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/developmental-review

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Litman (2008), that Epistemic Curiosity Scale and Feeling-of-Deprivation scale items loaded onto two distinct factors, interest and deprivation.

> "Within epistemic curiosity, he found the items of the two scales to load onto two distinct factors, interest (including items like, "I enjoy exploring new ideas") and deprivation"
