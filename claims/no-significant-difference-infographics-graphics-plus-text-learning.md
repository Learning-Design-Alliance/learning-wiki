# No statistically significant difference in learning between students using infographics and students using graphics+text

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-test score means were significantly higher than pretest within each condition (infographics: t(26) = -6.8, p < .001; graphic+text: t(24) = -9.02, p < .001), but the article reports "little variation between learning by graphic+text or infographic". [→ Lyra 2016](#lyra-2016)
`q3 i?` Infographic users scored higher on average than graphic+text users at all stages, though this difference is not statistically significant. [→ Lyra 2016](#lyra-2016)

## Evidence

### Lyra 2016

Lyra, K. T., Isotani, S., Reis, R. C. D., Marques, L. B., Pedro, L. Z., Jaques, P. A., & Bitencourt, I. I. (2016). Infographics or Graphics+Text: Which Material is Best for Robust Learning?. Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Advanced Learning Technologies (ICALT), to appear. [doi:10.1109/icalt.2016.83](https://doi.org/10.1109/icalt.2016.83)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-condition comparisons in the randomized study (14 infographic users, 13 graphic+text users). Table I reports post-test means of 34 (infographic) and 33.92 (graphic+text); the article states no significant difference between the materials.

> "a significantly higher score mean was found in the posttest both for the infographics condition (t(26) = -6.8, p < .001) and for the graphic+text condition (t(24) = -9.02, p < .001)"
