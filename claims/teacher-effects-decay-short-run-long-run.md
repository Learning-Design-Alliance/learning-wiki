## Evidence

### Rothstein 2008

Rothstein, Jesse. (2008). Teacher Quality in Educational Production: Tracking, Decay, and Student Achievement. NBER Working Paper No. 14442. http://www.nber.org/papers/w14442

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cumulative-effect analysis (Table 7): sampling-error-adjusted standard deviations of 4th grade teachers' effects are much larger on 4th grade scores than on 5th grade scores — the standard deviation "falls in the year after contact", consistent with decay of teacher effects.

> "First, the standard deviation of teachers' estimated "effects" falls in the year after contact – there is much more variation in 4th grade teachers' effects on 4th grade scores than in those same teachers' effects on 5th grade scores."

### Rothstein 2008 (2)

Rothstein, Jesse. (2008). Teacher Quality in Educational Production: Tracking, Decay, and Student Achievement. NBER Working Paper No. 14442. http://www.nber.org/papers/w14442

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r3`

The author's summary of the cumulative-effect estimates: the printed correlation between the in-year effect and the two-year cumulative effect is r = 0.3 to 0.5 — "correlated only 0.3 to 0.5" — and even less with the three-year effect, so short-run value added weakly proxies long-run effects.

> "A teacher's effect in the year of exposure – the universal focus of value added analyses – is correlated only 0.3 to 0.5 with her cumulative effect over two years, and even less with her effect over three years."
