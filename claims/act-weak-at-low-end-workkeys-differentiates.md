# The ACT does not distinguish well among low-achieving students, while WorkKeys differentiates achievement levels among these students

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Stephen M. Ponisciak 2005

Stephen M. Ponisciak. (2005). Understanding the Prairie State Achievement Exam: A descriptive report with analysis of student performance. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/understanding-prairie-state-achievement-exam-descriptive-report-analysis-student

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Box-plot analysis of 2004 CPS ACT versus WorkKeys scores in math and reading (Figure 11). The report states "the ACT does not distinguish well among students at the lower end of the achievement spectrum, while the WorkKeys does differentiate achievement levels among these students."

> "However, as Figure 11 shows, the range (and median) of ACT scores does not vary much for WorkKeys scores of less than 3, 3, and 4. This suggests that the ACT does not distinguish well among students at the lower end of the achievement spectrum, while the WorkKeys does differentiate achievement levels among these students."

### Stephen M. Ponisciak 2005 (2)

Stephen M. Ponisciak. (2005). Understanding the Prairie State Achievement Exam: A descriptive report with analysis of student performance. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/understanding-prairie-state-achievement-exam-descriptive-report-analysis-student

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of standardized PSAE and ACT reading scores by school type in 2004 (Figure 13), covering selective-enrollment, probation, vocational, and neighborhood schools. The report states "average standardized PSAE scores are higher than ACT scores for schools on probation."

> "Figure 13 shows that average standardized PSAE scores are higher than ACT scores for schools on probation, which demonstrates that the ACT does not measure performance at the low end of the achievement spectrum as well as the WorkKeys."
