q2 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 5 analysis of whether students with disabilities benefit as much from rigorous study habits and lower absences as non-disabled peers, using the 2004 freshman cohort. The report notes these groups report more rigorous study habits on average than non-disabled students.

"when students with learning disabilities reported more rigorous study habits, their course failures decreased by only two-thirds as much as students without identified disabilities."

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Chapter 5 null finding for the mild cognitive disability group: the expected relationship between self-reported study rigor and course failures was absent, unlike for students without identified disabilities.

"For students with mild cognitive disabilities, there was no relationship between study habits and course failures; self reports of more rigorous study habits did not result in fewer course failures."
