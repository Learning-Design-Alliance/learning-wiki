# Grade-based difficulty index is computed by converting class average to a 5-point scale and subtracting from 5

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The actual difficulty index is derived from the class average grade via the printed formula DI = 5 - (Class Average/100)*5, e.g. an average of 35 yields a difficulty index of 3.25. [→ Premalatha M 2021](#premalatha-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Premalatha M 2021

Premalatha M, Suganya G, Viswanathan V, G Jignesh Chowdary. (2021). Course Difficulty Estimation Based on Mapping of Bloom's Taxonomy and ABET Criteria. ring-programs-2017-2018/#outcomes. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2112.01241](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2112.01241)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Definitional/implementation statement in Section 3 on estimating difficulty from grades: "The class average is converted into 5 and subtracted with 5" to yield the Difficulty Index, using lower grades to indicate higher difficulty.

> "If the class average of a course is 35 out of 100, the difficulty index will be 5 – 1.75, that is 3.25. Similarly, all the courses are estimated with be estimated for the difficulty index to the point of 5 as specified in Table 3."
