# The binocular table microscope outperformed all digital microscopes and the smartphone on overall training-suitability score in a 31-participant subjective assessment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a questionnaire completed by 31 participants, the binocular table microscope scored significantly better than each digital microscope and the smartphone on overall score (Dunn's adjusted p < 0.0001 for PDM, KDM, and smartphone; p = 0.0046 for PLDM). [→ Awad L 2024](#awad-l-2024)

## Evidence

### Awad L 2024

Awad L, Abdi Z, Langridge BJ, Karoshi A, Butler PEM. (2024). A Comparison of Commercially Available Digital Microscopes for Their Use in Bench-Model Simulation of Microsurgery. Journal of Reconstructive Microsurgery. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1787980

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Subjective questionnaire study at Royal Free Hospital (December 2021–February 2022) with 31 participants (two consultants, nine registrars, fourteen junior trainees, six medical students) who performed interrupted sutures and end-to-end anastomosis on a synthetic model, graded on an 11-criteria 1–5 scale and analyzed with the Kruskal–Wallis test; "the binocular table microscope performed better in most domains." Table 2 reports overall-score mean rank differences of 86.67 (PDM), 66.68 (KDM), 36.23 (PLDM), and 66.25 (smartphone), all significant.

> "Overall, the binocular table microscope performed better in most domains reviewed through the questionnaire."
