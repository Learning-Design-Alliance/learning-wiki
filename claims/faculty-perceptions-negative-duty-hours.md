## Evidence

### Jamal Mohammad H 2014

Jamal Mohammad H, Wong Stephanie, Whalen Thomas V. (2014). Effects of the reduction of surgical residents' work hours and implications for surgical residency programs: a narrative review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-S1-S14

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Griner and colleagues, a survey comparing attending surgeons' views of graduates trained entirely under the 80-hour week with pre-restriction graduates: "55% of attending surgeons had less trust" and "68% had less confidence".

> "Comparing surgeons graduating in 2008 (that is, having trained entirely under the 80-hour work week) with those who had graduated before the restrictions, they found that 55% of attending surgeons had less trust in the 2008 graduates with respect to patient care and that 68% had less confidence in the ability of these residents to operate."

### Jamal Mohammad H 2014 (2)

Jamal Mohammad H, Wong Stephanie, Whalen Thomas V. (2014). Effects of the reduction of surgical residents' work hours and implications for surgical residency programs: a narrative review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-S1-S14

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Antiel and colleagues, a survey of 719 program directors with a 65% response rate finding General Surgery directors significantly more likely to perceive harm to patient care quality and safety.

> "Antiel and colleagues [4] surveyed 719 program directors in Internal Medicine, Pediatrics, and General Surgery. With a response rate of 65%, the study noted a pronounced difference between Internal Medicine and General Surgery program directors' perceptions of the work hour restrictions with respect to their impact on patient care"
