# The PBP group completed significantly more ISBAR steps post-intervention, and its pre-to-post gain was 1.7 steps larger than either comparison group

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Post-intervention mean steps completed differed significantly between groups (p<0.001): PBP 9.5 (SD 1.2), S 7.7 (SD 1.8), HSE 7.5 (SD 1.6). [→ McCarthy Nora 2023](#mccarthy-nora-2023)
`q4 i?` In a linear mixed model the group-by-time interaction was significant (p<0.001); the increase in steps was 1.7 higher in the PBP group than in the HSE group (95% CI 0.9 to 2.6, p<0.001) and 1.7 higher than in the S group (95% CI 0.8 to 2.5, p<0.001). ## Evidence

### McCarthy Nora 2023

McCarthy Nora, Neville Karen, Pope Andrew, Barry Lee, Livingstone Vicki. (2023). Effectiveness of a proficiency-based progression e-learning approach to training in communication in the context of clinically deteriorating patients: a multi-arm randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open 13(8):e072488. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-072488

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA at each timepoint in the same RCT, with pairwise t-tests. Post-intervention means: PBP 9.5 (1.2), S 7.7 (1.8), HSE 7.5 (1.6); PBP vs S and PBP vs HSE both p<0.001; S vs HSE p=0.532.

> "Post-intervention, the average number of 'steps' completed in the low-fidelity simulation environment improved in each of the groups and there was a statistically significant difference between the three groups (p<0.001). The average number of 'steps' completed was highest in the (PBP) group (mean (SD) 9.5 (1.2))"

### McCarthy Nora 2023 (2)

McCarthy Nora, Neville Karen, Pope Andrew, Barry Lee, Livingstone Vicki. (2023). Effectiveness of a proficiency-based progression e-learning approach to training in communication in the context of clinically deteriorating patients: a multi-arm randomised controlled trial. BMJ Open 13(8):e072488. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-072488

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Linear mixed model with group, time and group-by-time fixed effects and subject as random effect. The PBP group's mean steps rose from 6.6 (95% CI 6.1 to 7.1) to 9.5 (95% CI 9.0 to 10.0), a significant increase of 2.9 steps (95% CI 2.3 to 3.6, p<0.001).

> "In the linear mixed model, the interaction between group and time was statistically significant (p<0.001), indicating that the increase in 'steps' over time differed by group."
