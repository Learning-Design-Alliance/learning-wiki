## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the Physics 5B analysis, the female/male score gap was significantly smaller on the Problem Project than on traditional exams, with project performance the same between the groups within statistical uncertainty. [→ Bruce A. Schumm 2022](#bruce-a-schumm-2022)

## Evidence

### Bruce A. Schumm 2022

Bruce A. Schumm, Joy Ishii, and Colin G. West. (2022). Student-created physics problems as an independent and equitable assessment tool. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2202.07091](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2202.07091)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparative analysis of 245 students' course assessment scores by demographic subgroup (67 female, 171 male). Exams showed a difference of -8.3 ± 2.4 while the Problem Project showed "- 3.0 ± 2.9" percentage-point difference, i.e. the gap was "significantly reduced for the Problem Project".

> "for two of the four classification schemes (female/male and URG/non-URG), the gap between the groups is seen to be significantly reduced for the Problem Project."
