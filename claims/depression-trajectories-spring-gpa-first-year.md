## Evidence

### Yunusova Asal 2026

Yunusova Asal, Huang Caitlin, Price Stephen, Joshi Kunal, Dutcher Janine M., Brown Kirk Warren, Villalba Daniella K., Tumminia Michael J., Creswell Kasey G., Cohen Sheldon, Creswell J. David. (2026). Depression trajectories and their associations with academic performance and sleep patterns among first-year college students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04404-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA on registrar-obtained spring GPA among N = 271 first-year students across the four trajectory groups. The article reports "F(3, 267) = 3.64, p = 0.01" with the group means above; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "However, trajectory membership was significantly associated with spring term GPA (F(3, 267) = 3.64, p = 0.01). As shown in Fig. 2, mean spring term GPA was higher in the low-stable (M = 3.44, SE = 0.05) and decreasing trajectory groups (M = 3.39, SE = 0.06) than in the increasing (M = 3.22, SE = 0.07), and high-stable groups (M = 3.18, SE = 0.09)."

### Yunusova Asal 2026 (2)

Yunusova Asal, Huang Caitlin, Price Stephen, Joshi Kunal, Dutcher Janine M., Brown Kirk Warren, Villalba Daniella K., Tumminia Michael J., Creswell Kasey G., Cohen Sheldon, Creswell J. David. (2026). Depression trajectories and their associations with academic performance and sleep patterns among first-year college students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04404-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Dunnett post hoc comparisons with the low-stable group as reference, reported in Table 4: increasing vs. low-stable mean difference -0.22 (p = 0.02) and high-stable vs. low-stable -0.26 (p = 0.03); decreasing vs. low-stable was not significant (p = 0.90).

> "Post hoc Dunnett tests indicated that the low-stable group had a significantly higher GPA than the increasing and high-stable groups but did not differ significantly from the decreasing group (see Tables 3 and 4)."
