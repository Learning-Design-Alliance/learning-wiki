Evidence¶

K. Paige Harden 2020¶

K. Paige Harden, Benjamin W. Domingue, Daniel W. Belsky, Jason D. Boardman, Robert Crosnoe, Margherita Malanchini, Michel Nivard, Elliot M. Tucker-Drob and Kathleen Mullan Harris. (2020). Genetic associations with mathematics tracking and persistence in secondary school. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0060-2

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Ordered logistic regression of 9th-grade math course level on education-PGS in N = 3,635 Add Health students, reported in Results. The association "attenuated... only by about 20%" (from b = 0.583 to b = 0.469) after adding family SES and school-SES covariates, and was roughly comparable in magnitude to the family-SES association (b = 0.419).

"Students with higher polygenic scores were more likely to be tracked into more advanced math courses in 9th grade (Fig. 1a, b = 0.583, SE = 0.035, 95% CI = [0.516, 0.656], Supplementary Table 3)."
