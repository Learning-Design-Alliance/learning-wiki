## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Adolescent video game playing is negatively correlated with academic achievement (r = -0.152). [→ Gu Xiaoxia 2024](#gu-xiaoxia-2024)

## Evidence

### Gu Xiaoxia 2024

Gu Xiaoxia, Hassan Norlizah Che, Sulaiman Tajularipin, Wei Zhixia, Dong Jingyi. (2024). The impact of video game playing on Chinese adolescents’ academic achievement: Evidence from a moderated multi-mediation model. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0313405

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of the CEPS 2014–2015 follow-up sample of 824 Chinese junior high students who visit net bars or video arcades. Video game playing correlated negatively with achievement, "academic achievement (r = -0.152, p < 0.001)".

> "adolescent video game playing was negatively correlated with self-educational expectation (r = -0.119, p < 0.01), learning attitude (r = -0.205, p < 0.001), parent-child relationship (r = -0.116, p < 0.01), and academic achievement (r = -0.152, p < 0.001)"

### Gu Xiaoxia 2024 (2)

Gu Xiaoxia, Hassan Norlizah Che, Sulaiman Tajularipin, Wei Zhixia, Dong Jingyi. (2024). The impact of video game playing on Chinese adolescents’ academic achievement: Evidence from a moderated multi-mediation model. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0313405

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression Model 1 in the PROCESS macro parallel mediation analysis, controlling for gender, only-child status, Grade 7 score, family and school characteristics. The article reports "video game playing (β = -0.096, p < 0.001)"; no effect size is printed.

> "According to Model 1 (Table 2), academic achievement was significantly predicted by video game playing (β = -0.096, p < 0.001). Meanwhile, the results of Model 4 with mediating variables reconfirmed this negative relationship."
