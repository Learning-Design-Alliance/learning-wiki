## Evidence

### Taveira-Gomes T 2015

Taveira-Gomes T, Prado-Costa R, Severo M, Ferreira MA. (2015). Characterization of medical students recall of factual knowledge using learning objects and repeated testing in a novel e-learning system. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0275-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Random effects model estimated by Restricted Maximum Likelihood with flashcard, session and student as random variables, reported for the study-quiz group in the Results section. Percentages of total variance are printed as stated.

> "In respect to the components of variance for recall accuracy in the study-quiz group, the most prominent factor was the session (49.6%). The participant and flashcard components explained a small proportion of variance (5.1% and 15.3%, respectively)."

### Taveira-Gomes T 2015 (2)

Taveira-Gomes T, Prado-Costa R, Severo M, Ferreira MA. (2015). Characterization of medical students recall of factual knowledge using learning objects and repeated testing in a novel e-learning system. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0275-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same random effects variance decomposition reported for the quiz group. The residual component accounted for 41.2% of total variance in this group.

> "Regarding the components of variance for recall accuracy in the quiz group, the largest one was the flashcard (34.7%). The participant and session components explained a small proportion of variance (15.1% and 8.2%, respectively)"
