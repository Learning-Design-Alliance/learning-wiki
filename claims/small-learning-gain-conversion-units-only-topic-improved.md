## Evidence

### Simonsen Bjoerg O 2014

Simonsen Bjoerg O, Daehlin Gro K, Johansson Inger, Farup Per G. (2014). Improvement of drug dose calculations by classroom teaching or e-learning: a randomised controlled trial in nurses. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-006025

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intention-to-treat analysis of 183 nurses before and after the courses: knowledge score rose from 11.1 (2.0) to 11.8 (2.0) (p<0.001); faultless tests rose from 10.9% to 20.2%. The authors note the gain was smaller than defined as clinically relevant.

> "The overall knowledge score improved from 11.1 (2.0) to 11.8 (2.0) (p<0.001). Before and after the course, 20 (10.9%) and 37 (20.2%) participants, respectively, completed a faultless test."

### Simonsen Bjoerg O 2014 (2)

Simonsen Bjoerg O, Daehlin Gro K, Johansson Inger, Farup Per G. (2014). Improvement of drug dose calculations by classroom teaching or e-learning: a randomised controlled trial in nurses. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-006025

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Topic-level analysis (table 3) across the four calculation topics: conversion of units improved most (73.9% to 81.8% correct, p<0.001), while infusions and dilutions were unchanged. The authors attribute the pattern to poor conceptual understanding rather than arithmetic difficulty.

> "the classroom group scored significantly better after the course in conversion of units: 86% correct answers vs 78% (p<0.001), with no difference in the other topics."
