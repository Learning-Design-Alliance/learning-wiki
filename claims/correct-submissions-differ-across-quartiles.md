# Frequency of correct quiz submissions differs across performance quartiles, with high performers submitting correct answers most often

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Correct submission frequency differed significantly across the four performance quartiles with a medium effect size (η² = 0.07), and the high-performing group had significantly more correct submissions than the mid-high, mid-low, and low groups. [→ Hou 2025](#hou-2025)

## Evidence

### Hou 2025

Hou, C., Kuhlmann, S. L., Greene, J. A., Bernacki, M. L., & Hogan, K. A. (2025). Process mining measures students' help-seeking transitions when completing assignments in an online learning and assessment platform. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-025-09442-0

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis analysis of trace event frequencies across the four quartiles of 482 biology students found correct submissions differed across groups, η² = 0.07, a "medium effect size (Richardson, 2011)". Post-hoc tests showed the high group significantly exceeded the other three groups.

> "A Kruskal-Wallis test (Table 5) showed a statistically significant difference among the groups in the frequency of their correct submissions, c2(3) = 38.89, p <.001, h2 = 0.07, with a medium effect size (Richardson, 2011)"
