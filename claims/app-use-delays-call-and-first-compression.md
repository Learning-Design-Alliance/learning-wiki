## Evidence

### Metelmann Camilla 2021

Metelmann Camilla, Metelmann Bibiana, Schuffert Louisa, Hahnenkamp Klaus, Vollmer Marcus, Brinkrolf Peter. (2021). Smartphone apps to support laypersons in bystander CPR are of ambivalent benefit: a controlled trial using medical simulation. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-021-00893-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled simulation trial: 200 pupils trained in hands-only CPR were assessed six weeks later in a standardised simulated cardiac arrest, with generalised linear regression on 1,000 bootstrap samples adjusting for gender, age, first aid knowledge and prior emergency experience. The trial reports the call for help and first compression were "significantly delayed compared with the control group (p < .001)".

> "In the groups that used the app (facultative group, mandatory group), the call for help and the first chest compression were significantly delayed compared with the control group (p < .001)."

### Metelmann Camilla 2021 (2)

Metelmann Camilla, Metelmann Bibiana, Schuffert Louisa, Hahnenkamp Klaus, Vollmer Marcus, Brinkrolf Peter. (2021). Smartphone apps to support laypersons in bystander CPR are of ambivalent benefit: a controlled trial using medical simulation. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-021-00893-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same controlled simulation trial, regression-adjusted primary outcome. Total hands-off time was significantly longer in the facultative group (β = 29.22 s) and mandatory group (β = 38.02 s) than in the control group; the β values are unstandardised coefficients, so no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The total hands-off time (time until first compression + time of all pauses during compression) was significantly longer in the facultative group (β = 29.22 s; 95 %-CI: [20.79; 37.65]; p < .001) and in the mandatory group (β = 38.02 s; 95 %-CI: [29.55; 46.50]; p < .001) than in the control group."
