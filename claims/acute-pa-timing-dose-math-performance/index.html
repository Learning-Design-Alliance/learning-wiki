Evidence¶

Sneck S. 2019¶

Sneck S., Viholainen H., Syväoja H., Kankaapää A., Hakonen H., Poikkeus A.-M., Tammelin T. (2019). Effects of school-based physical activity on mathematics performance in children: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-019-0866-6

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Within-subjects randomised experiment (n = 96, age 10.7) comparing 5-, 10- and 20-min PA breaks against a 10-min sedentary lesson, measured with a one-minute math fluency test. The review reports higher scores after "the 10-min and 20-min exercise breaks".

"Math scores were higher after the 10-min and 20-min exercise breaks compared with the control, but not after the 5-min exercise break"

Sneck S. 2019 (2)¶

Sneck S., Viholainen H., Syväoja H., Kankaapää A., Hakonen H., Poikkeus A.-M., Tammelin T. (2019). Effects of school-based physical activity on mathematics performance in children: a systematic review. The International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-019-0866-6

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Review synthesis of four acute-effect studies in which 20–30-min PA sessions took place right before mathematics testing, varying in intensity. Two studies showed positive effects and two neutral effects on math scores.

"Two of these studies indicated positive effects of PA sessions on math scores [61, 76] and two indicated neutral effects [75, 77]"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Large-scale Scandinavian multicomponent PA interventions showed no significant positive effects on mathematics performance, raising a possible upper-limit question
  • Intervention effect sizes on mathematics are negatively associated with participants' age and intervention duration, while intervention type does not explain heterogeneity
  • Dose-response evidence suggests 10-minute active breaks, moderate intensity, and twice-daily frequency favour academic-related outcomes — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Duration and intensity of PA bouts may moderate attention effects, consistent with an inverted-U hypothesis — related
