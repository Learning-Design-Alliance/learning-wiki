# College students who study integrated annotated illustrations alongside science text generate approximately 50% more creative solutions on transfer problems than students who view the same illustrations separately after reading

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` An integrated group that viewed illustrations adjacent to corresponding text paragraphs with annotations generated approximately 50% more creative solutions on transfer problems than a separated group that viewed the same illustrations on a separate page without annotations after reading. [→ Mayer 1995](#mayer-1995)

## Evidence

### Mayer 1995

Mayer, R.E., Steinhoff, K., Bower, G., & Mars, R. (1995). A generative theory of textbook design: Using annotated illustrations to foster meaningful learning of science text. ETR&D, 43, 31-41. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02300480

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Across three experiments, college students read a text explaining how lightning works and took problem-solving transfer tests; the integrated group "generated approximately 50% more creative solutions on transfer problems than the separated group." No effect size is printed in the available text.

> "The integrated group generated approximately 50% more creative solutions on transfer problems than the separated group, and this pattern was stronger for students who lacked experience in meteorology than for high-experience students."
