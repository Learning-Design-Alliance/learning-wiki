## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a pilot study, transfer increased with more intensive comparison activity: separate-cases showed no increase, comparison instruction increased contingent contracts from 5% to 15%, and guided-analogy training increased them from 5% to 25%. [→ Gentner 2003](#gentner-2003)

## Evidence

### Gentner 2003

Gentner, D., Loewenstein, J., & Thompson, L. (2003). Learning and Transfer: A General Role for Analogical Encoding. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A pilot study with 120 undergraduates engaging in two simulated face-to-face negotiations found that "Those receiving separate-cases instruction showed little linking of cases, little grasp of the principle, and no evidence of transfer" while comparison instruction increased contingent contracts from 5% to 15% and guided-analogy training from 5% to 25%.

> "Those receiving separate-cases instruction showed little linking of cases, little grasp of the principle, and no evidence of transfer—that is, no increase in tendency to form contingent contracts from their first to their second negotiations. The same 10% formed contingencies in both rounds. Those receiving comparison instruction evidenced schema abstraction, as in Experiment 2. However, this resulted in only a minimal transfer benefit. The rate of forming contingent contracts increased from 5% initially to 15% after instruction. Finally, those receiving the guided-analogy training had a slightly larger increase in using contingencies: 5% to 25%"
