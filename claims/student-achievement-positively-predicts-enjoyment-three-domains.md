# Student-level achievement positively predicts enjoyment in math, science, and reading among fourth graders

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Domain-specific student-level achievement positively predicted enjoyment in each of the three subject domains, largest for reading (β = .241), then math (β = .169), smallest for science (β = .098). [→ Basarkod 2023](#basarkod-2023)

## Evidence

### Basarkod 2023

Basarkod, G., Marsh, H. W., Guo, J., Parker, P. D., Dicke, T., & Pekrun, R. (2023). The Happy-Fish-Little-Pond Effect on Enjoyment: Generalizability Across Multiple Domains and Countries. https://osf.io/kb29h/

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Three-level multilevel models (students in classes in countries) on TIMSS-PIRLS 2011 data controlling for SES, gender, and age. The article reports "largest for reading enjoyment (β = .241), followed by math (β = .169), and smallest for science enjoyment (β = .098)".

> "The fixed effect estimates show that domain-specific student-level achievement positively predicted student-level enjoyment in each of the three subject domains, confirming Hypothesis 1. This predictive effect was largest for reading enjoyment (β = .241), followed by math (β = .169), and smallest for science enjoyment (β = .098)."

### Basarkod 2023 (2)

Basarkod, G., Marsh, H. W., Guo, J., Parker, P. D., Dicke, T., & Pekrun, R. (2023). The Happy-Fish-Little-Pond Effect on Enjoyment: Generalizability Across Multiple Domains and Countries. https://osf.io/kb29h/

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Zero-order correlations across the whole sample using group-mean centering at the country level; the article notes the math and science correlations were "very small (r = .08 and .06, respectively)" and reading "small" (r = .19).

> "We note, however, that while statistically significant (given the large sample size), the magnitude of these correlations in the domains of math and science was very small (r = .08 and .06, respectively). The correlation was small for the domain of reading (r = .19)."
