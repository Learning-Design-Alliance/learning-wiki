# Initial retrieval conditions that provide less cue support, such as free recall rather than recognition or fewer letter cues, tend to produce better retention despite lower initial success

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 studies · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The chapter reports, citing Carpenter and DeLosh (2006, Experiment 1), that initial free recall enhanced retention more than initial recognition even though subjects recognized 89% and freely recalled 69% of words initially. [→ Karpicke 2017](#karpicke-2017)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports that in Carpenter and DeLosh (2006, Experiments 2 and 3), providing fewer letter cues during initial retrieval enhanced final free recall. [→ Karpicke 2017 (2)](#karpicke-2017-2)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports, citing Finley et al. (2011), that a diminishing cues condition outperformed study-only and accumulating cue conditions on final cued recall without feedback. [→ Karpicke 2017 (3)](#karpicke-2017-3)
`q2 i?` The chapter reports, citing Carpenter (2009), that retrieval practice effects on final cued recall were larger for weakly than for strongly associated word pairs. [→ Karpicke 2017 (4)](#karpicke-2017-4)
