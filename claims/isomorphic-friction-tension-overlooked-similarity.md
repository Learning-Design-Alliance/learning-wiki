# Introductory students overlooked the similarity between isomorphic friction and tension problems: 28% correct on the friction problem versus 72% on the tension problem

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When introductory students received two isomorphic Newton's second law problems back-to-back, only 28% answered the friction problem correctly although 72% answered the tension problem correctly, because a strong misconception about static friction overrode the logical mapping. [→ Singh 2013](#singh-2013)

## Evidence

### Singh 2013

Singh, C., and Marshman, E. (2013). Analogous Patterns of Student Reasoning Difficulties in Introductory Physics and Upper-Level Quantum Mechanics. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.010

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Introductory students were given two isomorphic equilibrium-on-an-incline problems back-to-back, with identical free-body diagrams except tension replaced by friction. A majority did not recognize the similarity despite doing the tension problem correctly, and launched into calculating maximum static friction although it was not at its maximum value.

> "Only 28% of the students provided the correct response to the friction problem (72% correct for tension problem right before the friction problem)."
