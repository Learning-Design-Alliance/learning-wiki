## Evidence

### Cavanagh A. J. 2018

Cavanagh A. J., Chen X., Bathgate M., Frederick J., Hanauer D. I., Graham M. J. (2018). Trust, Growth Mindset, and Student Commitment to Active Learning in a College Science Course. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-06-0107

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Survey of N = 245 undergraduates in an anatomy and physiology course; multivariate regressions tested trust and growth mindset as predictors within the same model. Trust was "a strong and positive predictor" of commitment with β = 0.38, t(238) = 6.29, p < 0.001, while mindset was nonsignificant.

> "trust is a strong and positive predictor of each of the three outcomes, including commitment to active learning, β = 0.38, t(238) = 6.29, p < 0.001; engagement, β = 0.35, t(239) = 5.66, p < 0.001"

### Cavanagh A. J. 2018 (2)

Cavanagh A. J., Chen X., Bathgate M., Frederick J., Hanauer D. I., Graham M. J. (2018). Trust, Growth Mindset, and Student Commitment to Active Learning in a College Science Course. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-06-0107

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same survey dataset's combined multiple regression model for commitment. The article reports "17% of the variance in student commitment to active learning, R2 = 0.17, F(2, 238) = 24.67, p < 0.001"; no standardized effect size beyond R² is printed.

> "Together, trust and mindset account for 17% of the variance in student commitment to active learning, R2 = 0.17, F(2, 238) = 24.67, p < 0.001"
