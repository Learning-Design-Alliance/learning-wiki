## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students described honest, constructive feedback after simulations as crucial to their learning, and valued actors' feedback on how the patient experienced the encounter. [→ Antila Asta Kristiina 2024](#antila-asta-kristiina-2024)

## Evidence

### Antila Asta Kristiina 2024

Antila Asta Kristiina, Lindblom Sari, Louhiala Pekka, Pyörälä Eeva. (2024). Creating a safe space: medical students’ perspectives on using actor simulations for learning communication skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06184-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Content analysis of 208 students' written reflections; the feedback theme included categories such as corrective and constructive feedback being crucial and actors' feedback being important (Table 1). Students "expressed a desire for honest, constructive feedback that helped them improve their communication skills".

> "The feedback from professional actors, offering the patients’ viewpoint, was highly valued."

### Antila Asta Kristiina 2024 (2)

Antila Asta Kristiina, Lindblom Sari, Louhiala Pekka, Pyörälä Eeva. (2024). Creating a safe space: medical students’ perspectives on using actor simulations for learning communication skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06184-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same qualitative dataset. Students found it "challenging to provide constructive or corrective feedback to friends", yet considered feedback an indispensable component of the simulations.

> "Simulations also served as a platform for students to learn how to give and receive feedback."
