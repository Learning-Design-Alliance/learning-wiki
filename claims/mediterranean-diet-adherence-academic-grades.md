## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The article reports a significant difference in average grades by Mediterranean diet adherence (d = 0.53), but the printed text and Table 2 contradict each other on which group scores higher. [→ Fernández-Medina 2020](#fernandez-medina-2020)

## Evidence

### Fernández-Medina 2020

Fernández-Medina, I. M., Ruíz-Fernández, M. D., Hernández-Padilla, J. M., Granero-Molina, J., Fernández-Sola, C., Jiménez-Lasserrotte, M. d. M., Lirola, M.-J., Cortés-Rodríguez, A. E., & López-Rodríguez, M. M. (2020). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet and Self-efficacy as Mediators in the Mediation of Sleep Quality and Grades in Nursing Students. Nutrients, 12(11), 3265. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12113265

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey of 334 nursing students; t test of average grade by Mediterranean diet adherence group. The text states poor adherers obtain "significantly lower average scores" (Table 2 prints t = 3.32, p = 0.001, d = 0.53).

> "In terms of adherence to the Mediterranean diet, students with poor adherence to the Mediterranean diet are those who obtain significantly lower average scores, compared to those with good adherence."
