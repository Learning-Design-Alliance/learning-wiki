### Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork 2009

Elizabeth Ligon Bjork and Robert A. Bjork. (2009). Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way: Creating Desirable Difficulties to Enhance Learning. Psychology and the Real World. https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/research/

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter's synthesis of the distributed-practice literature describes the spacing effect as "one of the most general and robust effects from across the entire history of experimental research on learning and memory," demonstrated across materials, learner types, and time scales.

> "Although massing practice (for example, cramming for exams) supports short-term performance, spacing practice (for example, distributing presentations, study attempts, or training trials) supports long-term retention."
