Evidence¶

Barth 2000¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The article reports national patterns (citing the National Commission on Teaching and America's Future) mirrored in California data from the Public Policy Institute of California, where low-experience teacher medians range from 24% in the most-disadvantaged to 17% in the least-disadvantaged school populations. No standardized effect size is printed.

"Inthe U.S. as a whole, children in high-poverty schools are more likely than students in low-poverty schools to be taught by inexperienced teachers (those with 0-3 years of experience). The same patterns hold true when the data are examined by race."

Barth 2000 (2)¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The article reports this retention pattern (citing NCES Digest of Education Statistics 1998) as part of its argument that high-poverty and high-minority schools suffer a hemorrhaging of good teachers to other schools. No standardized effect size is printed.

"Each year, teachers in high-minority schools are twice as likely as teachers in predominantly white schools not to leave the profession, but to leave their school for another school."
