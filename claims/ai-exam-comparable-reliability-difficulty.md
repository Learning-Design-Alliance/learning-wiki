## Evidence

### Ma Xiang 2025

Ma Xiang, Pan Wei, Yu Xiao-ning. (2025). Evaluating AI-generated examination papers in periodontology: a comparative study with human-designed counterparts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07706-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Split-half reliability of both papers computed with the Spearman-Brown formula from student responses in the trial. Corrected reliability was 0.81 (95% CI 0.76–0.86) for the AI paper and 0.84 (95% CI 0.79–0.89) for the human paper, "suggestive of good test stability".

> "Corrected total reliability results indicate high reliability for both examinations (AI Test Paper = 0.81 [95% CI: 0.76–0.86]; Human Test Paper = 0.84 [95% CI: 0.79–0.89]), suggestive of good test stability."

### Ma Xiang 2025 (2)

Ma Xiang, Pan Wei, Yu Xiao-ning. (2025). Evaluating AI-generated examination papers in periodontology: a comparative study with human-designed counterparts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07706-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square comparison of difficulty distributions between the two papers in the same trial. Distributions did not differ significantly (χ²(2) = 2.589, p = 0.274), but the AI paper's easy-item proportion (40.0%) exceeded the expected 30% syllabus target.

> "The proportion of easy, moderate, and difficult questions in the AI Test Paper (40.0%, 46.7%, 13.3%) and Human Test Paper (30.0%, 50.0%, 20.0%) showed no significant distributional difference (χ²(2) = 2.589, p = 0.274)"
