# Skilled EFL writers possess declarative knowledge that topic familiarity influences how much and how well they can write about a topic.

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Skilled EFL writers are metacognitively aware that their familiarity with a topic determines the amount and quality of content they can produce. [→ Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe 2001](#yu-ling-you-shih-guey-joe-2001)

## Evidence

### Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe 2001

Yu-Ling You; Shih-Guey Joe. (2001). Investigating the Metacognitive Awareness and Strategies of English-Majored University Student Writers. ERIC Document ED465281. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED465281

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview transcription analysis in the declarative-knowledge section of the study of nine skilled EFL writers. A subject reported writing more when familiar with a topic and less when not, showing awareness that topic knowledge shapes content; the article also reports all subjects would gather material from library or Internet when unfamiliar.

> "Examining the above transcription indicates that the subject was aware of that how much he knew about a topic would determine how much and how well he could write about the topic."
