# Children's baseline motor skills do not correlate with reading-related outcomes or with their use of movement when naming letter-sounds

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No significant correlations were found between balance and dexterity measures and academic outcomes, nor between motor skill scores and prevalence of using movement while naming letter-sounds. [→ Linn Damsgaard 2022](#linn-damsgaard-2022)

## Evidence

### Linn Damsgaard 2022

Linn Damsgaard, Anne-Mette Veber Nielsen, Anne Kær Gejl, Anne Sofie Bøgh Malling, Søren Kildahl Jensen, Jacob Wienecke. (2022). Effects of 8 Weeks with Embodied Learning on 5–6-Year-Old Danish Children's Pre-reading Skills and Word Reading Skills: the PLAYMORE Project, DK. Educational Psychology Review (2022) 34:1709–1737. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-022-09671-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory Spearman rank correlations in the RCT sample using flamingo balance and 9-hole pegboard scores; the discussion adds that "no correlations were found between balance and dexterity measures and academic outcomes." No effect sizes printed.

> "An exploratory Spearman correlation showed no significant correlation (p > 0.05) between the children's motor skills performance score and the prevalence of using movement while naming letter-sounds."
