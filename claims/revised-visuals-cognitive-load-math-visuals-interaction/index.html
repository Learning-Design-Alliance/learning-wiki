Clinton 2017¶

Clinton, V., Cooper, J.L., Michaelis, J., Alibali, M.W., & Nathan, M.J. (2017). How Revisions to Mathematical Visuals Affect Cognition: Evidence from Eye Tracking. In Eye-tracking technology applications in educational research (pp. 195-218). IGI Global. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574984 doi:10.4018/978-1-5225-1005-5.ch010

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Same eye-tracking experiment; analyses of average fixation length on math-relevant visuals present in both conditions. The interaction was significant; for low prior knowledge students fixation length "was longer in the original condition than in the revised condition".

"Similar to the text analyses, there was an interaction of condition and prior knowledge on average fixation length on the math-relevant visuals, F(2, 51) = 8.58, p < .001 (see Figure 2). For participants in the low prior knowledge group, the average fixation length for math-relevant visuals was longer in the original condition than in the revised condition F(1, 21) = 5.53, p = .02."
