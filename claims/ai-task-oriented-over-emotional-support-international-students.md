# International students use conversational AI predominantly for task-oriented academic and logistical support rather than for psychological or chronic emotional challenges

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q1`–`q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 86% of AI-using students reported using AI for academic and logistical tasks, while only 20% used it for psychological challenges. [→ Nourian 2026](#nourian-2026)
`q1 i?` Students describe AI as an on-demand first-aid tool for immediate problems. [→ Nourian 2026 (2)](#nourian-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Nourian 2026

Nourian, L.; Callis, A.; Patterson, S.; Miao, J.; Heard, J.; Tigwell, G. W. (2026). Understanding How International Students in the U.S. Are Using Conversational AI to Support Cross-Cultural Adaptation. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15127 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.15127](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.15127)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Online survey of 60 international students at US institutions (56 AI users), testing a hypothesis that AI is used for immediate task-oriented problems. In the usage findings, "48 (86%) of students reported using AI" for academic and logistical tasks; a separate sentence reports only 11 (20%) used AI for psychological challenges.

> "Out of 56 AI users (from the previous question), 48 (86%) of students reported using AI to help with tasks such as using English in academic settings (e.g., writing essays or communicating with professors), handling visa restrictions, immigration logistics, or paperwork."

### Nourian 2026 (2)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Open-ended survey response from participant S14 in the survey's open-ended items, illustrating why students turn to AI first: it is "The go-to place to get quick, trustworthy, and to the point information or solution to a problem." Qualitative, no effect size reported.

> "The go-to place to get quick, trustworthy, and to the point information or solution to a problem. Its like a first-aid"
