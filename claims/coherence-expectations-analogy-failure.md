# A coherence/expectations framework explains the analogy failure: students who do not value consistency in physics resort to memorized formulas and disregard the paired problem.

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The framework holds that because introductory students do not expect physics to be a coherent body of knowledge, they resort to memorized formulas and thus fail to exploit deep analogies. [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Challenges in Analogical Reasoning. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06423)

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical argument attributed to prior work (citing Redish, Hammer, and others): because "students have no expectation of deep similarities, they resort to memorized formulas", which explains why the promising analogy did not transfer. No new data test the framework in this paper.

> "introductory physics students often don't value consistency and that they tend to think of the subject as a jumble of disconnected formulas"
