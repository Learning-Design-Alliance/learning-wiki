# A two-week otolaryngology simulation and human factors module did not significantly increase medical students' interest in surgical or otolaryngology careers

## Evidence

### McInerney NJ 2025

McInerney NJ, Sexton GP, Roche A, Donnelly M, Skinner L. (2025). Development of an undergraduate otorhinolaryngology simulation education and human factors module: its impact on students' attitudes and perceptions. The Journal of Laryngology and Otology. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022215125103009

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post questionnaire analysis of 47 medical students completing the two-week module, using Mann–Whitney U tests. The study reports no significant increase in career interest: "in pursuing a career in surgery (p = 0.6) or otolaryngology (p = 0.17)". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Although the students did not demonstrate an increased interest in pursuing a career in surgery (p = 0.6) or otolaryngology (p = 0.17) following the rotation, they exhibited heightened comfort levels with the specialty"
