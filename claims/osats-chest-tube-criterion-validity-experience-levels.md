# A modified OSATS for chest tube insertion shows criterion validity by distinguishing four self-estimated experience levels in blinded video-rated performance on a porcine model

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Kruskal-Wallis testing found significant differences in OSATS scores across four experience-stratified groups, supporting criterion validity for differentiating experience levels. [→ Friedrich Mirco 2018](#friedrich-mirco-2018)

## Evidence

### Friedrich Mirco 2018

Friedrich Mirco, Ober Julian, Haubruck Patrick, Bergdolt Christian, Bruckner Thomas, Kowalewski Karl-Friedrich, Kadmon Martina, Müller-Stich Beat-Peter, Tanner Michael Christopher, Nickel Felix. (2018). Pilot evaluation of an objective structured assessment of technical skills tool for chest tube insertion. GMS Journal for Medical Education 35(4), Doc48. [doi:10.3205/zma001194](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001194)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pilot trial with 41 participants (students=9; junior residents=12; senior residents=14; attending surgeons=6) performing chest tube insertions on a cadaveric porcine model, rated by two blinded remote video raters. Scores rose across experience levels, described as "significant differences between means of scores between four groups stratified by previous experience"; Kruskal-Wallis p=0.002 with Dunn-Bonferroni p0,3=0.005 and p1,3=0.049.

> "There were significant differences between means of scores between four groups stratified by previous experience in chest tube insertion (level 0: 22.1±3.2 vs. level 1: 26.8±2.8 vs. level 2: 35.4±2.2 vs. level 3: 41.0±2.0)"
