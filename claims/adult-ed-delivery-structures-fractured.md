# US federal adult education funding structures (AEFLA and Title I) are fragmented and lack purchasing leverage, making scale difficult for ed-tech entrepreneurs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r1`–`r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Government-run adult education programs deliver specialized services responsive to local needs rather than establishing overarching goals and success metrics to inform product design. [→ DeSchryver 2015](#deschryver-2015)
`q1 i?` AEFLA funds are disbursed in small amounts spread across local providers, and neither AEFLA competitions nor Workforce Investment Boards leverage purchasing power to acquire products at scale. [→ DeSchryver 2015 (2)](#deschryver-2015-2)

## Evidence

### DeSchryver 2015

DeSchryver, David A., with Deirdre Dlugoleski. (2015). Accelerating Change: How education technology developers can jump-start a new adult education market. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/29f39d7d-87f2-4e1e-920e-0c1ce86035cc

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

Policy analysis in the Introduction characterizing the delivery model: programs are "designed to deliver specialized services responsive to local needs" rather than setting system-level goals and success metrics. This is the authors' qualitative policy assessment, not an empirical test.

> "The government-run adult education programs are designed to deliver specialized services responsive to local needs, rather than establish overarching goals and success metrics to inform product design."

### DeSchryver 2015 (2)

DeSchryver, David A., with Deirdre Dlugoleski. (2015). Accelerating Change: How education technology developers can jump-start a new adult education market. Digital Promise. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/29f39d7d-87f2-4e1e-920e-0c1ce86035cc

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The AEFLA Program Structure section explains the market consequence of the funding flow: funds are "small and spread out across service providers," and state grantee competitions "typically approve localized services" rather than acquire products. Analysis is based on program documents and interviews, not outcome data.

> "First, once the funds are disbursed, they are small and spread out across service providers, making sales and marketing very difficult. Second, if any purchasing leverage exists, it would be with the state grantee that runs subsequent competitions."
