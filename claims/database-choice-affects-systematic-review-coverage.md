# Choice of bibliographic database affects the coverage of systematic reviews in student learning research

## Evidence

### John T. E. Richardson 2017

John T. E. Richardson. (2017). Student Learning in Higher Education: a Commentary. Educational Psychology Review, 29, 353–362. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9410-x

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary argues that Dinsmore's restriction to PsycINFO records leaves open the possibility that relevant publications were missed, and suggests forward and backward cross-citation searching as a remedy. This is an analytical argument about review method, not an empirical test.

> "PsycINFO does not actually contain the articles themselves, merely their titles, abstracts, keywords, and metadata, and this leaves open the possibility that some relevant publications might have been missed"

### John T. E. Richardson 2017 (2)

John T. E. Richardson. (2017). Student Learning in Higher Education: a Commentary. Educational Psychology Review, 29, 353–362. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9410-x

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary cites Richardson's (2015a) comparison of citation counts for Marton and Säljö (1976): 957 publications in Web of Science versus 3764 in Google Scholar, illustrating that Google Scholar indexes far more relevant material than conventional databases.

> "Richardson found that it had been cited in 957 publications listed in Web of Science but in 3764 publications listed in Google Scholar"
