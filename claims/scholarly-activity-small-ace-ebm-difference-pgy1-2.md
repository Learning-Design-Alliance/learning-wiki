## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Residents who engaged in scholarly activities without clinical research scored 0.22 points higher (95% CI: 0.02 to 0.41) on the ACE tool than residents without scholarly activity, after adjustment. [→ Someko Hidehiro 2026](#someko-hidehiro-2026)

## Evidence

### Someko Hidehiro 2026

Someko Hidehiro, Kataoka Yuki, Mizuno Atsushi, Nishizaki Yuji, Shimizu Taro, Tokuda Yasuharu, Shiojiri Toshiaki. (2026). Impact of scholarly activities on early postgraduate medical trainees: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09272-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional analysis of 1,152 Japanese junior residents using multiple regression adjusted for confounders, with multiple imputation. The adjusted ACE tool score difference for scholarly activity without clinical research was "0.22 points (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.02 to 0.41)". No standardized effect size was printed.

> "the adjusted difference in ACE tool scores between residents with and without scholarly activity (reference group) was 0.22 points (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.02 to 0.41) for residents who engaged in scholarly activities without clinical research"
