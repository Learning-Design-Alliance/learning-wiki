## Evidence

### Kleinrensink V. E. E. 2026

Kleinrensink V. E. E., Kranenburg L. W., Kleinrensink G. J. (2026) Taxing working memory during laparoscopic training: reducing the impact of perfectionistic concerns on skill learning. Surgical Endoscopy 40(6):4995–5002. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-026-12739-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple linear regression within the control condition of this randomized experimental study (n = 27), from the Perfectionism and retention section. Findings show Rigid Perfectionism was not associated with retention slopes for either outcome; the path-length association was marginal, p = 0.093.

> "Rigid Perfectionism was not associated with retention slopes for either path length (B = 11.30, 95% CI [-2.05, 24.65], t(25) = 1.74, p = 0.093, R2 = 0.108, β = 0.329) or completion time (B = 0.037, 95% CI [-0.260, 0.333], t(25) = 0.25, p = 0.801, R2 = 0.003, β = 0.051)."

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simple linear regression within the working-memory load condition of the same randomized experimental study (n = 28), from the Perfectionism and retention section. Findings show Rigid Perfectionism was likewise not associated with retention slopes for either outcome.

> "Rigid Perfectionism was likewise not associated with retention slopes for path length (B = -3.76, 95% CI [-21.40, 13.89], t(26) = -0.44, p = 0.665, R2 = 0.007, β = -0.085) or completion time (B = 0.138, 95% CI [-0.227, 0.504], t(26) = 0.78, p = 0.443, R2 = 0.023, β = 0.151)."
