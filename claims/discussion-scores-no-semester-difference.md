# Discussion scores improved across the semester in both classes with no significant difference between the taught-about-discussions and control classes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both semesters improved from first to second discussion (a significant within-subjects main effect), but the semester × discussion interaction and the main effect of semester were not significant. [→ Brank 2013](#brank-2013)

## Evidence

### Brank 2013

Brank, E. and Wylie, L. (2013). Let's discuss: Teaching students about discussions. Journal of the Scholarship of Teaching and Learning, Vol. 13, No. 3, August 2013, pp. 23–32. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1017045

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated measures GLM on instructor-plus-peer discussion scores (11-point maximum) for first and second discussion leadership; second-discussion means were Experimental M = 10.92, Control M = 10.87 versus first-discussion means of 10.08 and 9.67. No effect of semester, F(1, 74) = 2.20, p = .14.

> "there was a significant within-subjects main effect of discussion, F(1, 74) = 63.75, p <.01, η2 = .46, but no significant interaction between semester and discussion, F (1, 74) = 1.98, p = .16, η2 = .03"
