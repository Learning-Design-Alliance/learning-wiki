# An effort-based reward did not significantly reduce answer offloading or improve unaided performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The reward's association with lower odds of answer offloading was not significant (OR = 0.66, p = .139). [→ Sebastian Maier 2026](#sebastian-maier-2026)

## Evidence

### Sebastian Maier 2026

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Confirmatory item-level mixed-effects logistic regression (H3a) in the preregistered experiment, testing the effort-based reward main effect on answer offloading. The article reports the effect was "not significant" with OR = 0.66, 95% CI [0.31, 1.40], p = .139; no effect size beyond the OR is printed.

> "The reward was associated with lower odds of answer offloading, but this effect was not significant (𝑏=− 0.42, SE= 0.39,𝑂𝑅= 0.66, 95% CI[0.31, 1.40],𝑧=− 1.08,𝑝=. 139)"

### Sebastian Maier 2026 (2)

Sebastian Maier, Kai Schwabe, Manuel Schneider, and Stefan Feuerriegel. (2026). Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20143

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Confirmatory test of H3b on unaided final-test performance in the same experiment. The article reports participants "did not have higher odds of answering an unaided final test item correctly" with OR = 0.76, 95% CI [0.50, 1.18], p = .890; equivalence was not tested.

> "Participants who received the reward also did not have higher odds of answering an unaided final test item correctly (𝑏=− 0.27, SE= 0.22,𝑂𝑅= 0.76, 95% CI[0.50, 1.18],𝑧=− 1.23,𝑝=. 890)"
