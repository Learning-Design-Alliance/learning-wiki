## Evidence

### Peng 2020

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that meta-analyses of short-term intensive working memory and executive function training found effects on trained or similar cognitive tasks but "weak or rare" transfer to nontrained academic performance. No effect size is printed for the transfer finding.

> "meta-analyses have suggested that although such cognitive training has resulted in effects on performance of trained or similar cognitive tasks, evidence of transfer effects on nontrained academic performance has been weak or rare"

### Peng 2020 (2)

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

A meta-analysis of 328 intervention studies of direct academic skill instruction among school-age children (Stockard et al., 2018) found academic instruction improved academic performance and transferred to ability and IQ measures, though the article notes the authors did not describe those measures in detail.

> "In one meta-analysis of 328 intervention studies of direct academic skill instruction (including reading, math, language, spelling, and multiple or other academic subjects) among school-age children, academic instruction positively affected academic performance, and those effects transferred to performance on measures of ability and IQ"
