Evidence¶

Dockrell et al. 2015¶

Julie Dockrell, Chloë Marshall and Dominic Wyse. (2015). Talk for Writing: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581467

q2 · i? · review · r2

Narrative literature review appraising programme components against published research; the three supported elements are emphasis on audience and purpose, regular formative assessment, and regular shared, guided and independent writing.

"There is well -documented evidence from a range of experimental studies and meta- analyses that three of the elements included in the non- negotiables are effective."

Dockrell et al. 2015 (2)¶

Julie Dockrell, Chloë Marshall and Dominic Wyse. (2015). Talk for Writing: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581467

q2 · i? · causal · r1

The review reports that for the imitation phase, no studies were located supporting daily text repetition for writing; the review also found no evidence for conventionalised gestures for connectives or for washing lines. Absence of studies is not evidence of ineffectiveness, as the authors note.

"We found no evidence in the research literature that daily repetition of texts supports generic oral language skills or writing skills of school age children."
