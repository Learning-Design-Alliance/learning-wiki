# The integrity–work engagement association was significant only in different-gender manager-subordinate pairs, where problem-solving was also significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In different-gender pairs (47 managers, 266 subordinates), integrity (γ = 0.08, P = .006) and problem-solving (γ = 0.05, P = .043) were significantly associated with work engagement; in same-gender pairs (50 managers, 199 subordinates), the positive integrity relationship did not reach significance (γ = 0.03, P = .459). [→ Adachi H 2020](#adachi-h-2020)

## Evidence

### Adachi H 2020

Adachi H, Sekiya Y, Imamura K, Watanabe K, Kawakami N. (2020). The effects of training managers on management competencies to improve their management practices and work engagement of their subordinates: A single group pre‐ and post‐test study. J Occup Health. https://doi.org/10.1002/1348-9585.12085

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup multilevel analyses stratifying pairs by whether manager and subordinate genders matched, using adjusted Model 3. The effect held in the different-gender subgroup but not the same-gender subgroup, so the finding is limited to different-gender pairs.

> "In the same gender group (50 managers and 199 subordinates), although the relationship between subordinate work engagement and manager integrity was positive, it did not reach significance (γ = 0.03, P = .459, in Model 3). In the different gender group (47 managers and 266 subordinates), both integrity and managers’ problem‐solving were significantly associated with work engagement (γ = 0.08, P = .006; γ = 0.05, P = .043, respectively, in Model 3)."
