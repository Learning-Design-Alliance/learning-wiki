## Evidence

### Massalou Damien 2025

Massalou Damien, Doyen Jérôme, Almairac Fabien, Rohrlich Pierre-Simon, Fournier Jean-Paul, Vandersteen Clair, Sadaghianloo Nirvana. (2025). Can relaxation exercises improve students’ OSCE grades: a prospective study. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.77739

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Univariate correlational analysis in the prospective study of 199 students. The article reports "Spearman coefficient of -0.05, p = 0.5"; no statistically significant association; equivalence was not tested.

> "The final score of the exam was not correlated with the initial anxiety VAS (Spearman coefficient of -0.05, p = 0.5)."

### Massalou Damien 2025 (2)

Massalou Damien, Doyen Jérôme, Almairac Fabien, Rohrlich Pierre-Simon, Fournier Jean-Paul, Vandersteen Clair, Sadaghianloo Nirvana. (2025). Can relaxation exercises improve students’ OSCE grades: a prospective study. Canadian Medical Education Journal. https://doi.org/10.36834/cmej.77739

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Gender comparison of pre-exam VAS in the same study. The article reports "a median Visual Analog Scale (VAS) of 7 (on ten)" in women versus 5 in men, p = 0.001; no effect size printed.

> "Women presented with a higher degree of anxiety before the exam with a median Visual Analog Scale (VAS) of 7 (on ten) [IQR 1;10] in the women’s group versus a median score of 5 on 10 [IQR 1;10] in the men’s group, p = 0.001, Mann-Whitney U test)"
