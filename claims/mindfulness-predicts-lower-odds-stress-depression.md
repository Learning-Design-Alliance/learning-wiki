## Evidence

### Alzahrani AM 2020

Alzahrani AM, Hakami A, AlHadi A, Batais MA, Alrasheed AA, Almigbal TH. (2020). The interplay between mindfulness, depression, stress and academic performance in medical students: A Saudi perspective. PLoS ONE 15(4): e0231088. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231088

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple logistic regression on the same 289-student cross-sectional sample, with stress and depression as categorical outcomes; the analysis showed "a decrease in the chance of being stressed by 5%" per mindfulness unit (OR 0.95), adjusting for age, gender, year, GPA and the other condition. No standardised effect size is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "an increase of one unit in the mindfulness score corresponded to a decrease in the chance of being stressed by 5% (OR 0.95, CI (0.93–0.98), P < 0.002) and the probability of being depressed by 7% (OR 0.93, CI (0.90–0.96), P < 0.001)."
