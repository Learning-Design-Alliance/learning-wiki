# The pretesting effect persists after a one-week delay between study and final test

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` After a 1-week delay, bolded-and-tested items were recalled better (M = 55%) than bolded-only items from extended study (M = 45%). [→ Richland 2009](#richland-2009)

## Evidence

### Richland 2009

Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The Pretesting Effect: Do Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Learning? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 15(3), 243–257. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 4: 158 undergraduates tested individually, returning after 1 week for the final test. Bolding was manipulated within subjects; testing was between subjects. "Bolded and tested items in the test and study condition were recalled better," d = 0.45.

> "Bolded and tested items in the test and study condition were recalled better (M ⫽ 55%, SE ⫽ 2.0) than bolded-only items presented for longer study time in the extended study condition (M ⫽ 45%, SE ⫽ 3.0), t(156) ⫽ 2.8, p ⬍ .0025, d ⫽ 0.45."
