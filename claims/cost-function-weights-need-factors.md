# Cost function analysis indicates substantially higher spending is needed for equal opportunity: weights of 2.49 for first-generation and 2.63 for students older than 24

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` First-generation students and students older than 24 required the largest spending increases (149 percent and 163 percent more) to earn the statewide average milestones. [→ Levin 2022](#levin-2022)

## Evidence

### Levin 2022

Levin, J., Baker, B., Lee, J., Atchison, D., & Kelchen, R. (2022). An Examination of the Costs of Texas Community Colleges. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cost function analysis using an instrumental variables approach on the 50-college, 2014/15–2019/20 dataset. Table 4 prints weights of 2.49 (first-generation), 2.63 (older than 24), 1.31 (economically disadvantaged), 1.19 (English learner), and 0.84 (dual credit), with a base per-student cost of $4,536.86.

> "It would cost 149 percent more (or a weight of 2.49) for a first -generation college student to have the same opportunity to earn the statewide average level of success points milestones as an otherwise similar student who is not a first- generation college student"
