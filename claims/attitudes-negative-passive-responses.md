# Antibullying attitudes are negatively related to advocating avoidance and encouraging independent coping

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Teachers with stronger antibullying attitudes reported less frequently advocating avoidance to victims (r = -0.14) and encouraging independent coping (r = -0.25). [→ Dawes 2024](#dawes-2024)

## Evidence

### Dawes 2024

Dawes, M., Malamut, S. T., Guess, H., & Lohrbach, E. (2024). Teachers' Attitudes Toward Bullying and Intervention Responses: A Systematic and Meta-analytic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09951-5

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Separate three-level meta-analyses of retrospective reports for these passive responses, which put the burden on victims to cope alone. Both negative associations were significant; the review labels avoidance a small effect and independent coping a medium effect (Fig. 4).

> "Antibullying attitudes were negatively related to advocating avoidance to the victim (r = − 0.14, p < 0.001; small effect) and encouraging independent coping (r = − 0.25, p = 0.006; medium effect)"
