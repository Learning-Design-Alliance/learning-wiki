Evidence¶

Muthanna 2021¶

Muthanna, Samara; Da Silva Nascimento, Bruna; El-Asam, Aiman; Hammuda, Sara; Khattab, Nabil. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Sensitivity analysis repeating each meta-analysis with each study removed one by one. The article reports that "none of the studies changed the pooled effect sizes" and all results remained significant.

"The results show that when removed none of the studies changed the pooled effect sizes for the relationship between bullying and/or victimisation involvement and academic achievement (See Figure 5), for the relationship between bullying and/or victimisation involvement and cognitive motivational factors (See Figure 6), and for the mediation analysis (See Figure 7)."

Muthanna 2021 (2)¶

Muthanna, Samara; Da Silva Nascimento, Bruna; El-Asam, Aiman; Hammuda, Sara; Khattab, Nabil. (2021). How Can Bullying Victimisation Lead to Lower Academic Achievement? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Mediating Role of Cognitive-Motivational Factors. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. doi:10.3390/ijerph18052209

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Four publication-bias methods (Failsafe N, Kendall's Tau, Egger's test, Trim-and-Fill) applied per analysis. The '5k + 10' benchmark "was not reached for bullies only"; Kendall's Tau indicated bias for any bullying involvement on achievement, and academic engagement mediation studies also showed bias.

"The '5k + 10' benchmark using the Rosenthal's Failsafe N analysis was not reached for bullies only, indicating that the found effects are open for future disconfirmation."
