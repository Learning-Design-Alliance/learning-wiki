## Evidence

### Ninkova 2025

Ninkova, V., Hays, J., Lavi, N., Ali, A., Lopes da Silva Macedo, S., Davis, H. E., & Lew-Levy, S. (2025). Hunter-Gatherer Children at School: A View From the Global South. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241255614

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's social-barriers synthesis reports that "most school teachers come from dominant groups," usually do not speak local languages, and often view parents as obstacles rather than partners. It also reports children experiencing abuse from teachers, staff, and other students. Narrative synthesis; no effect sizes printed.

> "Most school teachers come from dominant groups, not uncommonly from other regions of the country"

### Ninkova 2025 (2)

Ninkova, V., Hays, J., Lavi, N., Ali, A., Lopes da Silva Macedo, S., Davis, H. E., & Lew-Levy, S. (2025). Hunter-Gatherer Children at School: A View From the Global South. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241255614

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's stigma synthesis reports children "stigmatized and discriminated against by teachers and peers for coming from the bush," including verbal and physical assault when linguistic barriers block comprehension. It states the mistreatment is often cited as a reason for leaving school. Narrative synthesis; no effect sizes.

> "Hunter-gatherer children are often stigmatized and discriminated against by teachers and peers for coming from "the bush""
