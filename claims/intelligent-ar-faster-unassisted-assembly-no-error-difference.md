## Evidence

### Giles Westerfield 2015

Giles Westerfield, Antonija Mitrovic & Mark Billinghurst. (2015). Intelligent Augmented Reality Training for Motherboard Assembly. Int J Artif Intell Educ 25:157–172. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0032-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the physical post-test, participants reassembled the five components without tutor help. The experimental group's mean completion time was 56.56 s (SD 11.31) versus 81.13 s (SD 21.11); participants "completed the physical task significantly faster than their peers (t(14) = 2.9, p<.0 2)".

> "There was no significant difference on the number of errors made, but the experimental group participants completed the physical task significantly faster than their peers (t(14) = 2.9, p<.0 2)."

### Giles Westerfield 2015 (2)

Giles Westerfield, Antonija Mitrovic & Mark Billinghurst. (2015). Intelligent Augmented Reality Training for Motherboard Assembly. Int J Artif Intell Educ 25:157–172. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-014-0032-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Error counts in the physical post-test were 0.50 (SD 0.93) for the experimental and 1.00 (SD 0.93) for the control group; the article reports "no significant difference on the number of errors made". Equivalence was not tested.

> "The errors generally fit into two categories: failing to match a name with the correct component, or incorrectly performing an installation procedure. There was no significant difference on the number of errors made"
