# The relationship between parental support and the outcomes is moderated by gender and age, with significant interaction effects in the multivariate analysis

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Greater general parental support related to less boredom in girls but not boys, and support–outcome relationships differed by age group for fun, physical activity, alcohol consumption and academic performance. [→ Moral-García J.E. 2020](#moral-garcia-je-2020)
`q3 i?` The multivariate analysis found significant effects in the factors and in the interactions among them, including gender × APAF1 (p < 0.001), age × APAF3 (p < 0.001 for alcohol) and age × APAF4 (p < 0.001 for boredom and alcohol). [→ Moral-García J.E. 2020 (2)](#moral-garcia-je-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Moral-García J.E. 2020

Moral-García J.E., Urchaga-Litago J.D., Ramos-Morcillo A.J., Maneiro R. (2020). Relationship of Parental Support on Healthy Habits, School Motivations and Academic Performance in Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030882

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction results from the multivariate analysis section of the 1100-adolescent cross-sectional study, illustrated in Figures 1–7. The article reports "when girls receive a greater general parental support, they experience less boredom, while in the case of boys, the boredom level is independent from the parental support".

> "It was observed that when girls receive a greater general parental support, they experience less boredom, while in the case of boys, the boredom level is independent from the parental support (Figure 1)."

### Moral-García J.E. 2020 (2)

Moral-García J.E., Urchaga-Litago J.D., Ramos-Morcillo A.J., Maneiro R. (2020). Relationship of Parental Support on Healthy Habits, School Motivations and Academic Performance in Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030882

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

MANOVA (Wilks' lambda, partial eta squared) of gender and age factors and the four APAF factors on the five dependent variables in the 1100-adolescent study. Table 2 prints significant interaction terms including A1 × B (p < 0.001), A3 × C (p < 0.001 for alcohol) and A4 × C (p < 0.001 for boredom and alcohol).

> "The multivariate analysis shows significant effects both in the different factors and in the interactions among them, as well as in many of the dependent variables."
