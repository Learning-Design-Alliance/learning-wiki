## Evidence

### van Rijsewijk 2016

van Rijsewijk, L., Dijkstra, J. K., Pattiselanno, K., Steglich, C., & Veenstra, R. (2016). Who Helps Whom? Investigating the Development of Adolescent Prosocial Relationships. Developmental Psychology, 52(6), 894-908. https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000106

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

RSiena meta-analysis: rejection ego effect b = 1.02 (odds = 2.77) and alter effect b = -0.78 (odds = 0.46); similarity effect b = 0.60 (odds = 1.81). High-rejected students received help from low- and high-rejected peers but did not give help to low-rejected peers.

> "Peer rejection was associated positively with receiving help over time (b ! 1.02, SE ! .42, odds ! 2.77), and negatively with giving help (b ! #.78, SE ! .27, odds ! .46)."
