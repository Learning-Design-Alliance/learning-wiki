# Self-esteem significantly mediates the association between psychological distress and grade repetition among Black adolescents

## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Self-esteem significantly mediated links between both perceived stress and depressive symptoms and grade repetition, with small standardized indirect effects (.029 and .030 for stress and depression, respectively). [→ Rose 2024](#rose-2024)

## Evidence

### Rose 2024

Rose, T., Joe, S., & Hancock, G. R. (2024). Mediators that Matter: Psychological Distress, Developmental Assets, and Educational Outcomes among Black Youth. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000876

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r3`

In the NSAL-A path analysis (n = 1,170), bootstrapped indirect effects through self-esteem on grade repetition were statistically significant for both perceived stress and depressive symptoms; the printed standardized estimates were .029 and .030. Self-esteem was not a significant mediator for grades, suspensions, or expulsions.

> "the bootstrap confidence intervals revealed statistically significant indirect mediated relations involving self-esteem and educational expectations (with estimated standardized effect sizes of .029 and .017, respectively)"
