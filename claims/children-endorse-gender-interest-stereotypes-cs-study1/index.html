Evidence¶

Master 2021¶

Master, A., Meltzoff, A. N., & Cheryan, S. (2021). Gender stereotypes about interests start early and cause gender disparities in computer science and engineering. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 118(48), e2100030118. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2100030118

q3 · i0 · associational · r1

Cross-sectional survey of 733 children in grades 3–7 in four Rhode Island schools. The article reports that children "significantly endorsed gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys in computer science, t(713) = 4.69, P < 0.001, and d = 0.18", with 36% believing girls are less interested versus 18% believing girls are more interested.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys in computer science and engineering are endorsed across grades 1–12 and across racial/ethnic groups in a diverse sample
  • The more girls endorse gender-interest stereotypes favoring boys, the lower their own interest in computer science and engineering, and interest stereotypes predict girls' interest more strongly than ability stereotypes
  • Gender-interest stereotypes create a gender gap in interest in a computer science activity: girls, but not boys, show lower interest when the stereotype is present
  • Experimentally labeling a novel activity with a gender-interest stereotype causes 8-year-old girls to report lower interest and choose it less often
  • Fixed mindsets predict stronger stereotyping and trait inferences, with experimental evidence of a causal role — related
