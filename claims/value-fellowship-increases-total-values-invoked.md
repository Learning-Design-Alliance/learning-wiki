# A year-long value-focused fellowship increased the total number of values scientists invoked when discussing ethical vignettes, with a large effect

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` After the fellowship, scientists invoked significantly more values (443 vs 308) when responding to ethical vignettes, a large effect. [→ Tyler Garcia 2024](#tyler-garcia-2024)

## Evidence

### Tyler Garcia 2024

Tyler Garcia, Bill Bridges, Caitlin Solis, Wyatt Jones, and James T. Laverty, Caleb Linville, Scott Tanona, Jonathan Herington. (2024). The Effect of Value-Focused Discussions on Scientists' Ethical Decision Making. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.06292](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.06292)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired pre/post analysis of the fellowship study: across the six ethical dilemmas, the paired t-test on total values invoked per dilemma gave a p-value of 0.007 and "a Cohen's d of 1.13 (large effect size)"; total invoked values rose from 308 to 443.

> "We found that the paired t-test for the total values gives us a p-value of 0.007 and a Cohen's d of 1.13 (large effect size). The difference between the post-interview values invoked and pre-interview values invoked are statistically significant and substantial."
