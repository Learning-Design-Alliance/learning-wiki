# Informants across professions reported enhanced self-efficacy and reduced stress after multi-professional PPH simulation training

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Informants expressed enhanced confidence (self-efficacy) connected to perceived increased competence in PPH management. [→ Egenberg Signe 2017](#egenberg-signe-2017)
`q2 i?` Informants reported reduced stress levels after training, attributed to enhanced competence and teamwork. [→ Egenberg Signe 2017 (2)](#egenberg-signe-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Egenberg Signe 2017

Egenberg Signe, Karlsen Bjørg, Massay Deodatus, Kimaro Happiness, Bru Lars Edvin. (2017). “No patient should die of PPH just for the lack of training!” Experiences from multi-professional simulation training on postpartum hemorrhage in northern Tanzania: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0957-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group content analysis of 42 informants at two hospitals; nurse midwives reported courage and confidence in performing bimanual compression, e.g. "After that training, I am courageous and confident."

> "All informants expressed feelings of enhanced confidence and reduced stress."

### Egenberg Signe 2017 (2)

Egenberg Signe, Karlsen Bjørg, Massay Deodatus, Kimaro Happiness, Bru Lars Edvin. (2017). “No patient should die of PPH just for the lack of training!” Experiences from multi-professional simulation training on postpartum hemorrhage in northern Tanzania: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0957-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group finding on the stress sub-category, emphasized especially at the regional referral hospital where informants described high stress and low confidence before training; one informant said she no longer felt stress on hearing of a PPH.

> "After being trained, their new competence enhanced motivation and reduced stress, enabling them to manage PPH timely and properly:"
