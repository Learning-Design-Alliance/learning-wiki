## Evidence

### Romine William L. 2016

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i0` · `design · r3`

Pre–post Rasch measures from the 2-wk eye and vision intervention (194 students measured at both time points) yielded Cohen's D values of 0.07, 0.14, and 0.40 for the science, healthcare, and vision care subscales respectively, increasing with curricular proximity.

> "In line with results from other studies utilizing multilevel assessment in cognitive contexts (Sadler et al., 2013, 2015), effect sizes for pre–post changes generally increased with proximity to the intervention (science: Cohen's D = 0.07; healthcare: Cohen's D = 0.14; vision care: Cohen's D = 0.40)."

### Romine William L. 2016 (2)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i2` · `design · r3`

Paired pre–post comparison on the vision care subscale showed a significant decline with Cohen's D = 0.40; the authors state this "indicates that students were slightly less inclined to want to become vision care professionals after the intervention."

> "A small but statistically significant decline was found for interest in vision care (tdf = 193 = 3.92, Cohen's D = 0.40)."

### Romine William L. 2016 (3)

Romine William L., Miller Michele E., Knese Shawn A., Folk William R. (2016). Multilevel Assessment of Middle School Students' Interest in the Health Sciences: Development and Validation of a New Measurement Tool. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-02-0034

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

The science and healthcare subscale changes were not statistically significant and their Cohen's D values (0.07 and 0.14) fall below 0.2; the article reports no significant gain on these distal and proximal measures.

> "The changes in interest in science and medical careers were nonsignificant at the 90% confidence level. Cohen's D values below 0.2 indicate that these changes also have little practical significance (Cohen, 1988)."
