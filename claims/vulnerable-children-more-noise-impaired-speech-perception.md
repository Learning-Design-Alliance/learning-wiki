# Children with language or attention disorders and second-language learners are more impaired by noise in speech perception than age-matched controls

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children with language or attention disorders are more impaired than normally developing children by noise in speech perception tasks. [→ Klatte M 2013](#klatte-m-2013)
`q2 i?` Children tested in their second language show a stronger noise effect than native children. [→ Klatte M 2013 (2)](#klatte-m-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Klatte M 2013

Klatte M, Bergström K and Lachmann T (2013) Does noise affect learning? A short review on noise effects on cognitive performance in children. Front. Psychol. 4:578. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00578

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes this finding, citing Geffner et al. and Ziegler et al., to the mediating role of linguistic competence and selective attention; the reviewed studies' designs are not described in the review.

> "children with language or attention disorders are still more impaired than normally developing children by noise in speech perception tasks (Geffner et al., 1996; Ziegler et al., 2005, 2009 )"

### Klatte M 2013 (2)

Klatte M, Bergström K and Lachmann T (2013) Does noise affect learning? A short review on noise effects on cognitive performance in children. Front. Psychol. 4:578. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00578

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Crandell and Smaldino, that "A stronger noise effect is also evident for children tested in their second language when compared to native children".

> "A stronger noise effect is also evident for children tested in their second language when compared to native children (Crandell and Smaldino, 1996 )."
