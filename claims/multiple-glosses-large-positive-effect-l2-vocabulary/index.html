Evidence¶

Vahideh Sadat Vahedi 2014¶

Vahideh Sadat Vahedi, Behzad Ghonsooly, and Reza Pishghadam. (2014). Vocabulary Glossing: A Meta-Analysis of the Relative Effectiveness of Different Gloss Types on L2 Vocabulary Acquisition. Teaching English with Technology, 16(1), 3-25. http://www.tewtjournal.org

q3 · i?

Meta-analysis of 34 primary studies comparing a treatment (text+visual) and control (text-only) group, using Hedges's g. The article reports "The overall weighted mean effect size of 34 weighted effect sizes (Hedge's g) was +0.839"; the 95% CI of 0.562 to 1.116 excludes zero. No standardized effect-size magnitude beyond g is printed for impact coding.

"The overall weighted mean effect size of 34 weighted effect sizes (Hedge's g) was +0.839 (Table 1)."

Vahideh Sadat Vahedi 2014 (2)¶

Vahideh Sadat Vahedi, Behzad Ghonsooly, and Reza Pishghadam. (2014). Vocabulary Glossing: A Meta-Analysis of the Relative Effectiveness of Different Gloss Types on L2 Vocabulary Acquisition. Teaching English with Technology, 16(1), 3-25. http://www.tewtjournal.org

q3 · i?

The meta-analysis reports the 95% confidence interval for the overall effect; because it "does not include the value zero", the difference between multiple and single gloss conditions is statistically significant. The article notes the multiple-gloss group outperformed the text-only control group on a vocabulary test.

"As reported above, a 95% confidence interval ranges from 0.562 to 1.116. This confidence interval range does not include the value zero, suggesting that multiple-gloss hypertexts (text + visual) did improve acquisition of L2 vocabulary items."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • A multiple-choice gloss format (L1 + L2) produced significantly higher vocabulary posttest scores than a single-gloss format in Nagata's Japanese study — related
  • In Yoshii's study, text + picture glosses beat text-only glosses only on a definition-supply test, with no L1 versus L2 gloss difference — related
  • In Lomicka's think-aloud study, no statistical differences emerged among no-gloss, single-gloss, and multiple-gloss groups despite apparent vocabulary gains from multiple annotations — reports the opposite
  • Earlier meta-analyses, as reported second-hand, find that glosses improve L2 vocabulary learning and that text-and-picture glosses outperform single-mode glosses — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In Chun and Plass's CyberBuch studies, the text + picture annotation group significantly outperformed text-definition and text + video groups on a vocabulary test — related
