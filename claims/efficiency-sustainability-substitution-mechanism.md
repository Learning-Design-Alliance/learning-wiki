`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical result from the article's qualitative value-critical analysis of contemporary scholarly literature and policy debates. The article argues that "sustainability claims become increasingly metric-dependent" and that this substitution is weakened when AI adoption is guided by explicit educational aims and ethical priorities such as human dignity, social justice, and epistemic integrity.

> "AI-driven educational reforms frequently operate through an efficiency–sustainability substitution mechanism, whereby "sustainability" is implicitly redefined as operational performance—such as scalability, optimization, and measurable outputs—rather than as a value-based educational project."
