## Evidence

### Maynard 2015

Maynard, B. R., Brendel, K. E., Bulanda, J. J., Thompson, A. M., & Pigott, T. D. (2015). Psychosocial Interventions for School Refusal Behavior with Primary and Secondary School Students: A Campbell Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. SREE Spring 2015 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562325

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis (Comprehensive Meta-Analysis 2.0) of four psychosocial intervention studies versus wait-list or alternative treatments, with pretest effect sizes subtracted from posttest effect sizes. The pooled attendance effect was "moderate and significant (g = 0.43 (95% CI [0.08, 0.78], p< .05)."

> "Effects on attendance were moderate and significant (g = 0.43 (95% CI [0.08, 0.78], p< .05)."

### Maynard 2018

Maynard, B. R., Heyne, D., Brendel, K. E., Bulanda, J. J., Thompson, A. M., & Pigott, T. D. (2018). Treatment for School Refusal Among Children and Adolescents: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Research on Social Work Practice, 28(1), 56-67. https://doi.org/10.1177/1049731515598619

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of six psychosocial treatment studies (RCTs and QEDs) of school-refusing youth, pooling Hedges' g for attendance at posttest. The pooled effect size was g = 0.54 (95% CI [0.22, 0.86], p = .00), a medium effect, with small heterogeneity (I2 = 43.32).

> "The mean effect size at posttest of the six psychosocial studies was g ¼ 0.54 (95% CI ¼ [0.22, 0.86], p ¼ .00), demonstrating a positive and significant effect."
