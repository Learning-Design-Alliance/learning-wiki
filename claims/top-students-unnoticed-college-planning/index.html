Evidence¶

Melissa Roderick 2008¶

Melissa Roderick, Jenny Nagaoka, Vanessa Coca, Eliza Moeller, with Karen Roddie, Jamiliyah Gilliam, and Desmond Patton. (2008). From High School to the Future: Potholes on the Road to College (Case Studies). Consortium on Chicago School Research at the University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-potholes-road-college

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative case study of Clara (weighted GPA 4.7, ACT 24), who with an exceptionally involved mother eventually found a good-fit somewhat selective college after they "passed by the school" and toured it — a choice the authors describe as arrived at through no small amount of luck.

"Her teachers recognized that she was a remark- able young woman, but she never spoke to a counselor and never seriously discussed her plans for the future with any adult at her school."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Thoughtful, well-supported students distinguish college fit from college match, treating match as one but not the only component of fit — related
  • One-on-one adult support at school, such as a coach or counselor, helped students prioritize and complete college applications — related
  • Becoming too focused on a single college option left a highly qualified student with no fallback when that option fell through, and she did not enroll in college — related
  • Even students with stellar qualifications and strong plans can fail to enroll after career uncertainty, incomplete financial aid paperwork, and no extended adult conversation about college combine to make choice feel too risky — related
  • School college-going climate predicts students' college enrollment steps and enrollment at colleges matching their qualifications — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Students with strong qualifications took themselves out of four-year college planning, choosing early two-year enrollment as a low-risk solution when searches felt overwhelming and costly — related
