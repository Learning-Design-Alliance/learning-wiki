# School growth status was changeable rather than stable: only 0.5% of schools stayed overperformers across all three years, while 40.5% stayed average

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All 27 possible growth-status transition patterns were observed; 40.5% of schools stayed Average (AAA), while only 0.5% stayed Overperformer (OOO) and 0.4% stayed Underperformer (UUU). [→ Hiroyuki Yamada 2024](#hiroyuki-yamada-2024)
`q2 i?` Schools shifted growth status from or to Average more easily than between extreme statuses, and it was hard to retain overperforming status over time. [→ Hiroyuki Yamada 2024](#hiroyuki-yamada-2024)

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal analysis of growth-status transitions for 4,623 schools with data at all three grade levels (Figure 4), extending the classification method to a third year; AAA was the most common pattern at 40.5%.

> "very few schools maintained overperforming status (0.5% as OOO) or underperforming status (0.4% as UUU). Even fewer schools demonstrated a shift in growth status from underperformer to overperformer, skipping average (0.1% as UOO and 0.2% as UUO)."
