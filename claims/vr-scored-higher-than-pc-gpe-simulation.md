# Preclinical medical students rated the VR version of a gynecologic pelvic exam simulation significantly higher than the PC version on all five Likert metrics regardless of order of use

## Evidence

### Plotzker Rosalyn E. 2025

Plotzker Rosalyn E., Harmon Derek J., Kanellitsas Tim, Klein Barbie A. (2025). Virtual reality or personal computer-based gynecologic pelvic exam simulation: medical student preferences. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06757-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Crossover study of 22 preclinical medical students comparing identical VR and PC GPE simulation software; Wilcoxon Rank Sum on 4-point Likert scores showed VR higher on all 5 metrics, "regardless of the order in which the modalities were used."

> "Compared to the PC version, participants scored the VR experience significantly higher for all 5 metrics (Table 1), regardless of the order in which the modalities were used."

### Plotzker Rosalyn E. 2025 (2)

Plotzker Rosalyn E., Harmon Derek J., Kanellitsas Tim, Klein Barbie A. (2025). Virtual reality or personal computer-based gynecologic pelvic exam simulation: medical student preferences. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06757-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Written survey results from the same crossover study report realism averages of "3.0 and 1.9, respectively (p < 0.001)" for VR and PC; only the test statistic is printed, with no effect size.

> "Average scores for realism for VR and PC were 3.0 and 1.9, respectively (p < 0.001)."

### Plotzker Rosalyn E. 2025 (3)

Plotzker Rosalyn E., Harmon Derek J., Kanellitsas Tim, Klein Barbie A. (2025). Virtual reality or personal computer-based gynecologic pelvic exam simulation: medical student preferences. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06757-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Written survey results from the 22-student crossover study print comfort averages of "3.5 and 2.7, respectively (p < 0.001)" and confidence averages of "3.6 and 2.5"; means are reported with the test statistic, no effect size.

> "Average scores for improving comfort with a GPE for VR and PC were 3.5 and 2.7, respectively (p < 0.001), while improving confidence were 3.6 and 2.5 for VR and PC, respectively, (p < 0.001)"
