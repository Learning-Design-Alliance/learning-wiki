# Depressive symptoms and academic achievement are negatively associated across adolescence, with a stronger overall association in boys than in girls

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The overall negative association between depressive symptoms and academic achievement is stronger in boys (r = -0.21) than in girls (-0.13). [→ López-López José A. 2021](#lopez-lopez-jose-a-2021)

## Evidence

### López-López José A. 2021

López-López José A., Kwong Alex S.F., Washbrook Liz, Tilling Kate, Fazel Mina S., Pearson Rebecca M. (2021). Depressive symptoms and academic achievement in UK adolescents: a cross-lagged analysis with genetic covariates. Journal of Affective Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.01.091

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Cross-lagged panel models with random intercepts fitted to four repeated measures of depressive symptoms (SMFQ) and academic achievement (National Pupil Database records) in the ALSPAC cohort, reported separately by sex. The unadjusted models showed "an overall negative association that was stronger in boys (R=-0.21, 95% CI -0.31 to -0.11) than in girls (-0.13, -0.31 to 0.05)"; the printed correlation of r = -0.21 in boys is a small-to-moderate effect size.

> "We found evidence of an overall negative association that was stronger in boys (R=-0.21, 95% CI -0.31 to -0.11) than in girls (-0.13, -0.31 to 0.05)."
