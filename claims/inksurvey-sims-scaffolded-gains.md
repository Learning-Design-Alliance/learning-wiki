# Coupling interactive simulations with InkSurvey-guided scaffolding raised average competence on six concepts from about 2.3 after unguided exploration to about 3.1 on a 4-point scale

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In two Chemical Engineering courses targeting six concepts, students' average competence rose from about 1.8 to about 2.3 after unguided sim exploration, and to about 3.1 after the instructor used InkSurvey to probe understanding and guide further exploration, without telling students the answers. [→ F.V. Kowalski 2013](#fv-kowalski-2013)

## Evidence

### F.V. Kowalski 2013

F.V. Kowalski, Gus Greivel, Todd Ruskell, Tracy Q. Gardner, S.E. Kowalski, Thomas J. Colling, J.V. Gutierrez Cuba, and Enrique Palou. (2013). Using InkSurvey with Pen-Enabled Mobile Devices for Real-Time Formative Assessment II. Indications of Effectiveness in Diverse Educational Environments. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Within-study comparison at three time points across six targeted concepts in two Chemical Engineering courses: before sim use, after students played with sims on their own (average rose from ∝ 1.8 to ∝ 2.3), and after InkSurvey-based scaffolded questioning (∝ 3.1). Means are descriptive; no test statistic is printed.

> "Without the instructor ever telling students the answers to the questions, but instead posing scaffolded questions based on the students' immediate issues and misconceptions and allowing students to further explore the sims as they answered these questions, students' understanding of these concepts increased to an average of ∝ 3.1 on the 4 point scale on these same topics."
