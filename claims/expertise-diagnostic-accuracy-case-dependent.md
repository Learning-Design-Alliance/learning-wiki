# The relation between medical expertise and diagnostic accuracy was case-dependent: positive for Case 4, negative for Case 1 (novices outperformed experts), and absent for Cases 2, 3, and 5

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For Case 4, diagnostic accuracy was significantly related to expertise (OR = 14.7); for Case 1, the relation was negative and significant, with novices achieving higher diagnostic accuracy than experts. [→ Cloude 2025](#cloude-2025)
`q2 i?` For Cases 2, 3, and 5, there were no significant relations between expertise and diagnostic accuracy (Case 3 OR = 0.11 was marginal, p = 0.0716, with a confidence interval crossing zero). [→ Cloude 2025 (2)](#cloude-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Cloude 2025

Cloude, E. B., Chapman, R., Azevedo, R., Castiglioni, A., LaRochelle, J., Hernandez, C., & Torre, D. (2025). Novice and expert self-regulated learning phase transitions in medical diagnosis: Implications for adaptive and intelligent systems. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09729-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Finding from the per-case diagnostic accuracy regression models: expertise significantly related to accuracy for Case 4 (Table 8 prints OR = 14.7, p < 0.0001) and negatively for Case 1, "showing that novices had higher diagnostic accuracy than experts". Two different groups on each side of the contrast.

> "For Case 4, we found this relation was supported; yet, for Case 1, we found a negative and significant relation between expertise and diagnostic accuracy, showing that novices had higher diagnostic accuracy than experts."

### Cloude 2025 (2)

Cloude, E. B., Chapman, R., Azevedo, R., Castiglioni, A., LaRochelle, J., Hernandez, C., & Torre, D. (2025). Novice and expert self-regulated learning phase transitions in medical diagnosis: Implications for adaptive and intelligent systems. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09729-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Null findings from the same regression models: no significant expertise–accuracy relations in Cases 2, 3, or 5; the Case 3 coefficient was described by the authors as marginally significant (OR = 0.11, p = 0.0716) with a confidence interval crossing zero. No effect size supports an inference of equivalence.

> "For Cases 2, 3, and 5, we did not find relations between expertise and diagnostic accuracy, which did not support H3."
