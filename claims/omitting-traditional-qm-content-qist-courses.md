# Educators recommend omitting traditional quantum mechanics content such as Hermitian operators, Hamiltonians, and the time-dependent Schrödinger equation from foundational interdisciplinary QIST courses

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some educators state that foundational interdisciplinary QIST courses can omit discussion of eigenvalues and eigenvectors of Hermitian operators, the Hamiltonian, and the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for accessibility. [→ Liam Doyle 2025](#liam-doyle-2025)

## Evidence

### Liam Doyle 2025

Liam Doyle, Fargol Seifollahi and Chandralekha Singh. (2025). Building Bridges in Quantum Information Science Education: Expert Insights to Guide Framework Development for Interdisciplinary Teaching and Evolution of Common Language. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00454-y](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00454-y)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In the RQ1 results on content that can be left out, Educator 8 describes teaching a gate-based course following Thomas Wong's book in which measurement was done only in the computational basis and the Hamiltonian was skipped, judged sufficient for quantum algorithms and communications.

> "So, for the sake of time and accessibility, I basically did not discuss eigenvalues and eigenvectors, because while they're necessary for certain physics problems, working off Thomas Wong's book [110], I mostly managed to basically avoid them."
