# Medical students show greater resistance to structured creativity tools and interdisciplinary teams than engineering students

> **Currency** · newest study 2014 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Medical students expressed a weak preference against working and learning in interdisciplinary groups, preferring to work with course colleagues. [→ Spoelstra Howard 2014](#spoelstra-howard-2014)
`q2 i?` Medical staff were more likely than engineering staff to state their curriculum did not address creativity and innovation, and rated patient-ethics and commercialisation content higher than engineering staff. [→ Spoelstra Howard 2014 (2)](#spoelstra-howard-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Spoelstra Howard 2014

Spoelstra Howard, Stoyanov Slavi, Burgoyne Louise, Bennett Deirdre, Sweeney Catherine, Drachsler Hendrik, Vanderperren Katrien, Van Huffel Sabine, McSweeney John, Shorten George, O'Flynn Siun, Cantillon-Murphy Padraig, O'Tuathaigh Colm. (2014). Convergence and translation: attitudes to inter-professional learning and teaching of creative problem-solving among medical and engineering students and staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-14

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive Likert results from the student survey (n = 633 respondents overall): students showed "a weak preference for not working and learning in interdisciplinary groups" (M = 2.89; SD = 0.90). Descriptive means only; no effect size.

> "Students expressed a weak preference for not working and learning in interdisciplinary groups (M = 2.89; SD = 0.90), preferring to work and learn with their course colleagues."

### Spoelstra Howard 2014 (2)

Spoelstra Howard, Stoyanov Slavi, Burgoyne Louise, Bennett Deirdre, Sweeney Catherine, Drachsler Hendrik, Vanderperren Katrien, Van Huffel Sabine, McSweeney John, Shorten George, O'Flynn Siun, Cantillon-Murphy Padraig, O'Tuathaigh Colm. (2014). Convergence and translation: attitudes to inter-professional learning and teaching of creative problem-solving among medical and engineering students and staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-14

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Staff comparison (159 staff respondents) via Mann-Whitney U tests: medical staff more often said their curriculum did not address creativity and innovation, e.g. "U = 1579, z = 4.53, p < 0.0001". No effect size printed.

> "Medical staff were more likely to state that their existing curriculum did not address creativity and innovation [U = 1579, z = 4.53, p < 0.0001]."
