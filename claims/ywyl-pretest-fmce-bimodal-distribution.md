# YWYL MOOC pretest FMCE scores average 39.9% with a bimodal distribution, including a top quintile unlike the typical introductory physics population

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` YWYL students earned an average pretest FMCE score of 39.9 ± 0.8% (median 27.7%) with a bimodal distribution; 30% scored above 50%, which the authors call uncharacteristic of introductory mechanics courses. [→ John M. Aiken 2013](#john-m-aiken-2013)
`q2 i?` The top quintile was more educated (70% college degree holders) and more physics-experienced than the general YWYL population, so is not representative of the typical introductory physics population. [→ John M. Aiken 2013 (2)](#john-m-aiken-2013-2)

## Evidence

### John M. Aiken 2013

John M. Aiken, Shih-Yin Lin, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz and Marcos D. Caballero. (2013). The Initial State of Students Taking an Introductory Physics MOOC. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.001](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.001)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

FMCE administered in the first week of the course; Fig. 1 shows the distribution of scores for N=1370 test takers, described as appearing bimodal. The mean is "39.9 ± 0.8%" with median 27.7%.

> "On the pre-test, YWYL students earned an aver- age score of 39.9 ± 0.8% (median 27.7%)."

### John M. Aiken 2013 (2)

John M. Aiken, Shih-Yin Lin, Scott S. Douglas, Edwin F. Greco, Brian D. Thoms, Michael F. Schatz and Marcos D. Caballero. (2013). The Initial State of Students Taking an Introductory Physics MOOC. [doi:10.1119/perc.2013.pr.001](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.pr.001)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey responses cross-tabulated against pretest FMCE performance for the top quintile, who earned scores above 74% with an average of 90.1% ± 7.8%. The authors conclude this subpopulation "is not representative of the typical introductory physics population."

> "Students appearing in the top quintile of the FMCE score distribution are more well-educated than the general population; 70% hold a college degree"
