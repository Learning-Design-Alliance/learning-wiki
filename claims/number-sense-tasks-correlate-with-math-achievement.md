# Number line estimation and numerical magnitude comparison correlate with mathematics achievement (r = .24 to .44), with number line estimation more strongly related in older children

Number line estimation correlates r = .44 with mathematics achievement (Schneider et al. 2018, k = 263), symbolic magnitude comparison .30 and nonsymbolic comparison .24 (Schneider et al. 2017). These are correlations compiled across meta-analyses: they describe how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not what causes it.

## Evidence

### Breit et al. 2025

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. *Learning and Individual Differences, 118*, 102621. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621)

`q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses` · `i3 · large effect, r = .24 to .44` · `n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants)` · `review · r3`

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. Number line estimation r = .44 (Schneider et al. 2018, k = 263, n = 10,576 children and adolescents, corrected for unreliability), with the correlation larger after age 9 (r = .49) than before age 6 (.30). Symbolic numerical magnitude comparison r = .30 (k = 89), nonsymbolic .24 (k = 195), overall .28 (k = 284, n = 17,201) (Schneider et al. 2017); the magnitude-comparison correlation was smaller in older samples.

## Discussion

The authors note that these correlations run against the expectation that numerical magnitude skills matter most early in talent development and that they may still help identify mathematics-specific strengths, being somewhat uniquely mathematical where intelligence and self-regulation are not.
