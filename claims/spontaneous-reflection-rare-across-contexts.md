## Evidence

### Wilcox 2019

Wilcox, B. R., & Corsiglia, G. (2019). A cross-context look at upper-division student difficulties with integration. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020136)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam-solution coding across all three contexts found only 20 of 329 students spontaneously reflected on their solutions. The authors note this is a lower bound since students may not write checks down explicitly, but interview results suggest this is less likely.

> "Overall, we found that very few of our students (N = 20 of the 329 students in any of the three contexts to complete the question) made explicit, spon- taneous attempts to reﬂect on their solution using either of these checks on exams."

### Wilcox 2019 (2)

Wilcox, B. R., & Corsiglia, G. (2019). A cross-context look at upper-division student difficulties with integration. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020136](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.020136)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Table III data combined across contexts shows 78 solutions contained errors detectable by a units check but only 3 students explicitly performed one. The authors conclude units checking "would likely be the most eﬀective reﬂective practice."

> "these results suggest that an ex- plicit check of units would likely be the most eﬀective re- ﬂective practice for students in terms of detecting errors, but that our students are rarely executing this (or other) checks spontaneously."
