Evidence¶

Andrew C. Butler & Shana K. Carpenter 2015¶

Andrew C. Butler & Shana K. Carpenter. (2015). Separating Myth from Reality in Education: Introduction to the Special Issue. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9344-0

q2 · i? · review · r2

The editorial reports, citing Finn and Tauber (2015), that fluency of processing has a "pervasive and misleading effect" on students' perceptions of learning, and that difficulty-increasing strategies "often lead to superior long-term learning."

"strategies and techniques that increase the perceived difficulty of learning and decrease the sense of fluency (e.g., self-testing and spaced practice) often lead to superior long-term learning"
