# The effect of a time constraint on scenario performance differs by experience level: large for junior residents (Cohen's d = 1.00) and small-to-medium for senior residents (Cohen's d = 0.34)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Junior-level residents improved from 59.9% to 70.9% of total possible points pre- to post-time constraint (Cohen's d = 1.00), while senior-level residents moved from 54.3% to 56.8% (Cohen's d = 0.34); neither difference was statistically significant. ## Evidence

### Collins Candace 2025

Collins Candace, Parr Madeline F. E., Lozy Tara, Chirico Amy. (2025). The effect of time constraints on resident performance in pediatric mock scenarios. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07865-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis of the same pre/post cohort study by resident experience level. Junior residents averaged "59.9% (± 13.5%)" pre and "70.9% (± 7.7%)" post (p = 0.113); seniors averaged "54.3% (± 3.9%)" pre and "56.8% (± 9.6%)" post (p = 0.516).

> "Overall, junior-level pediatrics residents completed an average of 59.9% (± 13.5%) of total possible points in the pre-time constraint group and 70.9% (± 7.7%) of total possible points in the post-time constraint group (p = 0.113). Senior-level residents completed an average of 54.3% (± 3.9%) of total possible points in the pre-time constraint group and 56.8% (± 9.6%) of total possible points in the post-time constraint group (p = 0.516)."
