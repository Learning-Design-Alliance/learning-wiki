# Self-study students produced more substantive comments, a higher proportion of substantive comments, and more co-constructive turns during discussion than lecture students, though interaction-episode counts did not differ

## Evidence

### Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park 2026

Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park (2026). Self-study followed by discussion enhances learning for high school students. Instructional Science (2026) 54:56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09801-7

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Dialogue analysis of audio- and video-recorded group discussions, coded by three independent raters (ICC 0.97 for episodes). The proportion of substantive comments was also "significantly higher in the self-study condition (94.15%) than in the lecture condition (72.56%), χ²(1) = 228.64, p < .001."

> "the number of substantive comments per discussion group was significantly higher in the self-study condition (M = 48.36, SD = 23.22) than in the lecture condition (M = 23.50, SD = 11.04), t(19) = 3.08, p = .006, d = 1.21."

### Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park 2026 (2)

Jaeseo Lim and Jooyong Park (2026). Self-study followed by discussion enhances learning for high school students. Instructional Science (2026) 54:56. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09801-7

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Coded transcripts also showed more substantive comments per interaction episode in the self-study condition, M = 12.78 vs M = 6.44, t(19) = 2.93, p = .009, d = 1.28.

> "Per interaction episode, students in the self-study condition had a significantly higher number of co-constructive turns ( M = 6.16, SD = 3.07) than students in the lecture condition (M = 2.46, SD = 0.85), t(19) = 3.65, p = .002, d = 1.59."
