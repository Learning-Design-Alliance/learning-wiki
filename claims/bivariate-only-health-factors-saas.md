## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Higher waterpipe use, higher physical activity, and higher BMI were each significantly associated with SAAS in bivariate analysis, but none remained significant in the final linear regression (p = 0.093, 0.184, and not entered/retained respectively). ## Evidence

### Hammoudi Halat Dalal 2023

Hammoudi Halat Dalal, Hallit Souheil, Younes Samar, AlFikany Mira, Khaled Sanaa, Krayem Maha, El Khatib Sami, Rahal Mohamad. (2023). Exploring the effects of health behaviors and mental health on students’ academic achievement: a cross-sectional study on lebanese university students. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-023-16184-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 1677 students: bivariate tables show significant associations for waterpipe smoking (p = 0.004), physical activity, and BMI with SAAS, but in the final regression waterpipe p = 0.093 and physical activity p = 0.184. The article states the waterpipe finding "was observed only in the bivariate analysis."

> "The current study found that university students who frequently consume a higher number of waterpipes per week had a lower SAAS score than their counterparts, although this was observed only in the bivariate analysis."
