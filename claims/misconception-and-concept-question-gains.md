## Evidence

### C. Efthimiou 2006

C. Efthimiou, R. Llewellyn, D. Maronde, T. Winningham. (2006). Physics in Films: An Assessment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609154](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609154)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Internal evaluation of class responses to a question targeting a common misconception, that room temperature drops in the presence of a ghost, presented as scientifically proven in popular media. After class discussion of physical explanations, the "majority of the original believers were more skeptical on the post-test."

> "On the pre-test, a significant number of students believed this myth. After possible physical explanations for the observed "ghost induced" temperature changes were discussed in class, the majority of the original believers were more skeptical on the post-test."

### C. Efthimiou 2006 (2)

C. Efthimiou, R. Llewellyn, D. Maronde, T. Winningham. (2006). Physics in Films: An Assessment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609154](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0609154)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Internal evaluation of class performance on a basic concept question about heat flow, with the correct answer being that heat flows from hotter to colder. The post-test score was nearly 80% after scattered pre-test answers.

> "Figure 6 demonstrates that most of the class has grasped the concept of heat flow, with a post-test score of nearly 80% while the pre-test answers were scattered."
