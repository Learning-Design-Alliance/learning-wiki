## Evidence

### Colin S. Wallace 2021

Colin S. Wallace, Edward E. Prather, John A. Milsom, Ken Johns, Srin Manne (2021). Students taught by a first-time instructor using active learning teaching strategies outperform students taught by a highly-regarded traditional instructor. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1080/0047231x.2021.12290517](https://doi.org/10.1080/0047231x.2021.12290517)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of two sections of the same introductory calculus-based physics course (traditional N = 258, reformed N = 217) on four common free-response exams, with items flagged as biased toward the reformed section excluded. The article reports the reformed section "had higher average scores on almost every question, with only two exceptions".

> "Even if we ignore all items flagged as biased toward the reformed section, the reformed section had higher average scores on almost every question, with only two exceptions (items 1 and 4 on exam 1)."

### Colin S. Wallace 2021 (2)

Colin S. Wallace, Edward E. Prather, John A. Milsom, Ken Johns, Srin Manne (2021). Students taught by a first-time instructor using active learning teaching strategies outperform students taught by a highly-regarded traditional instructor. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1080/0047231x.2021.12290517](https://doi.org/10.1080/0047231x.2021.12290517)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same section comparison at the individual-item level: the reformed class "outperformed the traditional class on all other items on all exams", with the final-exam item 4 difference of 18.6% ± 2.9% given as an example of large item-level differences.

> "The reformed class outperformed the traditional class on all other items on all exams, and in many cases the differences in scores were large (e.g., the difference on item 4 on the final exam was 18.6% ± 2.9%)."
