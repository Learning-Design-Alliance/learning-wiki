# SAT math score significantly predicts final exam performance and moderates the effect of delivery mode

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher-SAT students tended to perform better in the CB setting while lower-SAT students tended to perform better in F2F. [→ Krupa 2013](#krupa-2013)

## Evidence

### Krupa 2013

Krupa, E., Webel, C., & McManus, J. (2013). Evaluating the impact of computer-based and traditional learning environments on students' knowledge of algebra. In Martinez, M. & Castro Superfine, A (Eds.), Proceedings of the 35th annual meeting of the North American Chapter of the International Group for the Psychology of Mathematics Education. Chicago, IL: University of Illinois at Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED584492

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Level 2 ANCOVA on a subset (nCB=73, nF2F=50) controlling for SAT math, high school GPA, Algebra II grade, and university GPA. The "Mode*SAT math" interaction was significant, "(F= -2.31, p=.029)".

> "Mode*SAT math was a significant predictor of student achievement (F= -2.31, p=.029). These findings suggest that there is a significant relationship between SAT math, the mode of delivery, and the final exam"
