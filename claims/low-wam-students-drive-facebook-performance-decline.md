## Evidence

### James Wakefield & Jessica K. Frawley 2020

James Wakefield & Jessica K. Frawley. (2020). How does students’ general academic achievement moderate the implications of social networking on specific levels of learning performance? Computers & Education. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131519302477?via%3Dihub

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sub-sample OLS regressions splitting 505 respondents at the median WAM (low WAM n = 247). Low-WAM Facebook time coefficients were significant: "–0.164***" (decision making), "–0.183***" (cost volume profit), "–0.145**" (financial accounting).

> "Of particular concern is students who are generally lower academic achievers, our low WAM sub-sample, drive this result, indicating these students are the ones who are specifically negatively impacted by the disruptive nature of Facebook"
