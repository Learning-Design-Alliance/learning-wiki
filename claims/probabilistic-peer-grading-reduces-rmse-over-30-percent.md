# Probabilistic peer grading models that estimate grader bias and reliability reduce RMSE against ground-truth grades by 33% and 31% on two HCI course offerings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On the first HCI offering, probabilistic models reduced RMSE on ground-truth grade prediction from 7.95 to 5.30 (33%); on the second offering from 6.43 to 4.73 (31%). [→ Chris Piech 2013](#chris-piech-2013)

## Evidence

### Chris Piech 2013

Chris Piech, Jonathan Huang, Zhenghao Chen, Chuong Do, Andrew Ng, Daphne Koller. (2013). Tuned Models of Peer Assessment in MOOCs. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.2579

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Simulation-based evaluation on two Coursera HCI offerings (63,199 peer grades total), comparing probabilistic models against the median-of-four-peer-grades baseline on supergraded ground-truth submissions. The article reports RMSE reductions of "33% from 7.95 to 5.30" and "31% from 6.43 to 4.73"; for the second offering, grades within 10pp rose from 88% to 97%.

> "In our experiments we are able to reduce the RMS error on our prediction of the ground truth grade by 33% from 7.95 to 5.30. Similarly, on the second oﬀering of the course we were able to reduce error by 31% from 6.43 to 4.73."
