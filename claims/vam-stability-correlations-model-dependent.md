## Evidence

### Koedel 2015

Koedel, C., Mihaly, K., & Rockoff, J. E. (2015). Value-Added Modeling: A Review. Preprint, forthcoming at Economics of Education Review. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Value-Added+Modeling+A+Review+Koedel

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis of stability studies (Goldhaber, Walch and Gabele 2013; Koedel, Leatherman and Parsons 2012; McCaffrey et al. 2009) estimating year-to-year correlations from lagged-score models. The review reports the range "between 0.47 and 0.64" and concludes much of the variance in stability estimates is driven by modeling decisions.

> "Correlations of adjacent year value-added measures estimated from models without school or student fixed effects range between 0.47 and 0.64 across these studies."

### Koedel 2015 (2)

Koedel, C., Mihaly, K., & Rockoff, J. E. (2015). Value-Added Modeling: A Review. Preprint, forthcoming at Economics of Education Review. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Value-Added+Modeling+A+Review+Koedel

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis of stability under fixed-effects specifications. The review reports school-fixed-effects correlations of "0.18 to 0.33" and a student-fixed-effects correlation of 0.29 (McCaffrey et al. 2009), attributing reduced stability to narrower identifying variation and imprecision from indirect linkages.

> "Goldhaber, Walch and Gabele (2013) and Koedel, Leatherman and Parsons (2012) report correlations from models that include school fixed effects ranging from 0.18 to 0.33."
