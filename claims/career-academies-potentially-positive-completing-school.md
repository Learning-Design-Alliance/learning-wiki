# Career Academies have potentially positive effects on completing school: about 96% of academy students earned a diploma or GED within 8 years of expected graduation versus about 94% of comparison students (effect size 0.27, not statistically significant but substantively important).

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students enrolled in Career Academies were more likely to earn a high school diploma or GED within 8 years of expected graduation, with a printed effect size of 0.27 that was not statistically significant but was substantively important. [→ completing-1](#completing-1)

## Evidence

### completing-1

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2015, September). Dropout Prevention intervention report: Career Academies. Retrieved from https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/70

`q3 · i1` · `review · r3`

The WWC review reports the completing school domain finding from the Kemple & Willner (2008) 8-year follow-up of the randomized controlled trial, with 1,428 students in nine schools. The intervention group earned a diploma or GED at "about 96%" versus about 94% for the comparison group; the effect size of 0.27 (improvement index +11, p > .10) was not statistically significant but met the WWC substantively important threshold.

> "About 96% of the students enrolled in Career Academies earned a high school diploma or GED certificate within 8 years of their expected high school graduation date, compared to about 94% of students in the comparison group. The effect size of 0.27 was not statistically significant but was substantively important."
