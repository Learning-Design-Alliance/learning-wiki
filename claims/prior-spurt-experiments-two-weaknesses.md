## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that previous experiments on initial spurt were weakened by two defects: successive test periods that were too lengthy, and an insufficient number of subjects. [→ Chapman 1914](#chapman-1914)

## Evidence

### Chapman 1914

Chapman, J. C. (1914). A Study of Initial Spurt in the Case of Addition. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070019

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Theoretical argument reviewing prior work, including Thorndike's discussion and experiments using as few as three subjects (Lindley) and one subject (Arai). The author concludes skepticism about initial spurt was warranted given this evidence, and identifies "a lack of a sufficient number of subjects" as a key defect.

> "These two weaknesses are: 1. The successive time periods which test the spurt are too lengthy. 2. There is usually a lack of a sufficient number of subjects."
