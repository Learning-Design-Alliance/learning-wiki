## Evidence

### Smith et al. 2017

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Curriculum knowledge check on treatment received in the Mplus pilot analysis, using the 36-item ICKQ covering all four units. Main effects favored I Control for the EF, goal-setting, and emotion-regulation units.

> "Main effect tests for the ICKQ were significant for Unit 1 (z = 2.98, p  = .002, ES = .72), Unit 2 (z  = 1.80, p  = .036, ES = .41), and Unit 3 (z  = 2.92, p  = .002, ES = .55)."

### Smith et al. 2017 (2)

Stephen W. Smith, Ann P. Daunic, James Algina, Donna L. Pitts, Kristen L. Merrill, Michelle M. Cumming, and Courtney Allen. (2017). Self-Regulation for Students With Emotional and Behavioral Disorders: Preliminary Effects of the I Control Curriculum. Journal of Emotional and Behavioral Disorders. https://doi.org/10.1177/1063426616661702

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Unit 4 (social problem solving) knowledge in the same analysis showed an interaction rather than a main effect; simple effects were significant at low and moderate pretest values. The article attributes the lack of a main effect to incomplete Unit 4 delivery due to time constraints.

> "For Unit 4 knowledge, the test of the C × T interaction was significant (z = −2.58, p = .010), with the effect of I Control being larger when participants had lower covariate (pretest) scores."
