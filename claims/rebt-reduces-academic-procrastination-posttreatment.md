# A 12-session REBT group intervention significantly reduces academic procrastination in in-school adolescents at posttreatment compared with a placebo counseling control group

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` At posttreatment, in-school adolescents who received REBT had significantly lower academic procrastination than the placebo control group, with a printed Cohen d of 0.641. [→ Amoke CV 2025](#amoke-cv-2025)

## Evidence

### Amoke CV 2025

Amoke CV, Ede MO, Mawila-Chauke D, Obeagu IE. (2025). Disputing and challenging academic procrastination behaviors in students using REBT approach. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000041725

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA in a quasi-experimental pretest-posttest nonequivalent control group design with 129 Nigerian secondary-school adolescents (64 REBT, 65 control). The REBT group's posttreatment mean was 67.91 (SD 9.22) versus 73.26 (SD 7.35) for control, a "significant effect on participants’ academic procrastination" with d = 0.641.

> "At the posttreatment level (Time 2), the intervention had a significant effect on participants’ academic procrastination, F(1, 127) = 18.647, P = .006, d = 0.641."
