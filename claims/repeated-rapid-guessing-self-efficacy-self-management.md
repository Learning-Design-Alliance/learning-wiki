## Evidence

### James Soland 2020

James Soland, Steven Wise. (2020). Do students rapidly guess repeatedly over time? A longitudinal analysis of student test disengagement, background, and attitudes. NWEA Research presentation, 2020 National Council on Measurement in Education virtual conference. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/do-students-rapidly-guess-repeatedly-over-time-a-longitudinal-analysis-of-student-test-disengagement-background-and-attitudes/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Longitudinal LST analysis of six test administrations over three years linking rapid guessing behavior to psychological measures such as academic self-efficacy survey scores. The authors state the association is "strongly associated with students' academic self-efficacy and self-management scores." No coefficient is printed.

> "Further, we show that repeated rapid guessing is strongly associated with students' academic self-efficacy and self-management scores."

### James Soland 2019

James Soland, Megan Kuhfeld. (2019). Do students rapidly guess repeatedly over time? A longitudinal analysis of student test disengagement, background, and attitudes. Educational Assessment, 24(4), 327-342. https://doi.org/10.1080/10627197.2019.1645592

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Longitudinal study (six test administrations over three years) relating repeated rapid guessing to psychological and background measures. The article's abstract reports that repeated rapid guessing "is strongly associated with students’ academic self-efficacy and self-management scores." The association is correlational; the abstract states neither its direction nor a coefficient. The same study was presented by Soland and Wise at the 2020 NCME conference (previous entry).

> "Further, we show that repeated rapid guessing is strongly associated with students’ academic self-efficacy and self-management scores."
