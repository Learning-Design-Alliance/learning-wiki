# In an in-class implementation, student-led discussion of a contrastive paper set was community-centered, with students importing ideas from literary analysis such as writing for an audience

## Evidence

### Paul J. Camp 2012

Paul J. Camp. (2012). Contrastive sets and framing: A case study in scientific writing. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1306.4944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1306.4944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of the video-recorded, transcribed in-class discussion in Implementation One (11 students, Spring 2012) shows the flow was student-driven: "11 student prompts versus only 2 instructor prompts". The author characterizes it as community-centered with negotiated understandings.

> "With 11 student prompts versus only 2 instructor prompts to introduce new discussion topics, the implementation one is clearly community-centered, being driven largely by the students"

### Paul J. Camp 2012 (2)

Paul J. Camp. (2012). Contrastive sets and framing: A case study in scientific writing. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1306.4944](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1306.4944)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the Implementation One discussion transcript, students drew on prior knowledge from English courses; the author notes "the idea of writing for a specific audience is imported from English classes" and recurred throughout the discussion.

> "Here, the idea of writing for a specific audience is imported from English classes by two different students. This turned out to be a major organizing idea that was returned to multiple times over the course of the remaining discussion."
