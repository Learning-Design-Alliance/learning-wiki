## Evidence

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello, Dean Zollman and Manuel Rodriguez-Achach. (2003). The Vocabulary of Introductory Physics and Its Implications for Learning Physics. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.1119/1.1607802](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1607802)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with 14 students selected from the three survey categories, who wrote sentences with force and explained sentence meanings. The article reports “only those w ho identified the parameters associated with force were able to explain” the similarities and differences; only two of 14 related force to mass and/or acceleration.

> "Thus, all students were able to explain whet her the word “force” in the sentences has an everyday or physics meaning, but only those w ho identified the parameters associated with force were able to explain how the word “force” in the sentence was similar and different to its use in physics."

### Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz 2003 (2)

Salomon F. Itza-Ortiz, N. Sanjay Rebello, Dean Zollman and Manuel Rodriguez-Achach. (2003). The Vocabulary of Introductory Physics and Its Implications for Learning Physics. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.1119/1.1607802](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1607802)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same interview phase covered momentum and impulse: twelve students interpreted momentum in the physics context and six related it to mass and/or velocity, while for impulse “only one  of the students was able to explain the meaning of the term impulse as us ed in physics”; the others used the everyday meaning of instant action or spontaneity.

> "For the word “impulse” only one  of the students was able to explain the meaning of the term impulse as us ed in physics."
