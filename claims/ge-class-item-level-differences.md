# Item-level: Potsdam YOU-views are significantly lower on 30% of items, largest shifts are positive for Q29/Q17 and negative for Q18, and the highest/lowest items are the same in both datasets

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In 30% of items GE-CLASS YOU-question expert-agreement is significantly lower than E-CLASS (7% for EXPERT-questions); the same items score highest and lowest in both datasets. [→ Teichmann 2022](#teichmann-2022)
`q3 i?` The largest positive GE-CLASS YOU shifts are Q29 and Q17, followed by Q3 and Q2; the largest negative shift is Q18. [→ Teichmann 2022 (2)](#teichmann-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Teichmann 2022

Teichmann, E., Lewandowski, H. J., and Alemani, M. (2022). Investigating students' views of experimental physics in German laboratory classes. arXiv:2201.12145. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010135](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010135)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Item-by-item GE-CLASS analysis using two-point scoring with 95% confidence intervals from Fig. 2; Potsdam personal views are significantly lower on "30% of the survey's items" than the E-CLASS comparison. Fig. 3 shows the same four lowest and three highest items for both datasets.

> "in 30% of the survey’s items, the values of ER-agreement of YOU-questions in the GE-CLASS data are signiﬁcantly lower than those of the E-CLASS results. For the EXPERT-questions, this is the case for about 7% of the items."

### Teichmann 2022 (2)

Teichmann, E., Lewandowski, H. J., and Alemani, M. (2022). Investigating students' views of experimental physics in German laboratory classes. arXiv:2201.12145. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010135](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010135)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post item analysis of GE-CLASS data: largest positive shifts for Q29 and Q17, then Q3 and Q2; Fig. 3 shows seven items with statistically significant positive shifts and one (Q18, communicating results to peers) with a statistically significant negative shift.

> "we observe the largest positive shifts in the GE-CLASS for the YOU-questions Q29 ( ‘If I don’t have clear directions for analyzing data, I am not sure how to choose an appropriate analysis method’ ) and Q17"
