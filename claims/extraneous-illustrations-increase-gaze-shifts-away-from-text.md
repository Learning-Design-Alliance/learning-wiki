# Beginning readers make more gaze shifts away from text when reading a book with extraneous illustrations than a streamlined version

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Children looked away from the text more frequently per page in the Standard condition than in the Streamlined condition (Cohen's d = 0.83). [→ Eng 2020](#eng-2020)

## Evidence

### Eng 2020

Eng, C. M., Godwin, K. E., & Fisher, A. V. (2020). Keep it simple: streamlining book illustrations improves attention and comprehension in beginning readers. npj Science of Learning, 5:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-00073-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-subject eye-tracking experiment with 60 first- and second-graders reading a commercially available book in Standard and Streamlined halves; a linear mixed-effects model on gaze shifts away from text showed a main effect of book condition with the printed effect size d = 0.83, with children looking away "12.31 (SE = 1.94) more times per page in the Standard condition".

> "There was a main effect of book condition, F(1, 58) = 40.26; p < 0.0005; Cohen's d = 0.83."
