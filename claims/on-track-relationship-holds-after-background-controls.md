# The on-track–graduation relationship remains strong after accounting for student background characteristics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After accounting for elementary achievement, race/ethnicity, gender, economic status, and age at entry, on-track students with typical CPS backgrounds were 55 percentage points more likely to graduate in four years than similar off-track students in the same school. ## Evidence

### Allensworth 2005

Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2005). The On-Track Indicator as a Predictor of High School Graduation. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/track-indicator-predictor-high-school-graduation

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Statistical analyses of CPS cohorts controlling for background characteristics, reported in the section on whether on-track merely reflects background. The report states the relationship "remained very strong after accounting for differences between students" and that the indicator predicts equally well for all students and in all schools but one.

> "the students on-track at the end of freshman year were 55 percentage points more likely to graduate in four years than similar off-track students in the same school (81 percent compared to 26 percent)"
