# A 30-minute concreteness fading game with AR and tangible robots produces significant learning gains in vector addition for high-school students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teams' post-test scores on vector addition were significantly higher than pre-test scores after the gamified concreteness fading activity, with correct answers rising from 43% to 72% on average. [→ Sergei Volodin 2025](#sergei-volodin-2025)

## Evidence

### Sergei Volodin 2025

Sergei Volodin, Hala Khodr, Pierre Dillenbourg, Wafa Johal. (2025). Going Down the Abstraction Stream with Augmented Reality and Tangible Robots: The Case of Vector Instruction. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.14562](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.14562)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Experiment with 14 pairs of Year 10 and Year 12 students (30 participants) playing the three-stage game; pre-test mean was 2.7 (SD = 2) and post-test mean 6.4 (SD = 3). The Wilcoxon test showed scores were "significantly different (T = 10, p = 5· 10−3)". No effect size is printed.

> "Since the data does not seem normally distributed, we used a Wilcoxon test to compare pre- and post-test scores. The test reveals that the pre- and post- test scores are significantly different (T = 10, p = 5· 10−3). The results show an increase of 50% in correct answers between pre- and post-tests"
