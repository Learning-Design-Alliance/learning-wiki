## Evidence

### Babayiğit 2026

Babayiğit, S., & Trenkic, D. (2026). Vocabulary knowledge is key to understanding and addressing disparities in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 127–151. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70000

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Covariate-adjusted ANOVA of reading speed controlling parental education, digit backward span and matrix reasoning. The article reports "only vocabulary was significantly associated with reading speed", F(1, 166) = 17.52, p < .001, ηp2 = .10, described as a medium effect size.

> "Among the covariates, only vocabulary was significantly associated with reading speed, with a medium effect size, F(1, 166) = 17.52, p < .001, ηp2 = .10."

### Babayiğit 2026 (2)

Babayiğit, S., & Trenkic, D. (2026). Vocabulary knowledge is key to understanding and addressing disparities in higher education. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 127–151. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70000

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Covariate-adjusted ANOVA of time-restricted comprehension (Table S9, Model 1); controlling the covariates made the group effect nonsignificant while "Vocabulary remained the only covariate with a significant main effect", ηp2 = .15, described as a large effect size.

> "Vocabulary remained the only covariate with a significant main effect on reading comprehension performance, and a large effect size, F(1, 166) = 29.91, p < .001, ηp2 = .15"
