`q2 i?` Students in the word cloud condition reported higher scores on critical thinking than students analyzing the same text in linear form, though the difference was not statistically significant. [→ deNoyelles 2015](#denoyelles-2015)

## Evidence

### deNoyelles 2015

deNoyelles, A., & Reyes-Foster, B. (2015). Using Word Clouds in Online Discussions to Support Critical Thinking and Engagement. Online Learning, 19(4). https://olj.onlinelearningconsortium.org/index.php/olj/article/view/478

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental survey of 132 undergraduates in two sections of an anthropology course; one section analyzed speeches as word clouds, the other in linear form. The t-test comparison yielded F (1, 114) = 1.86, p = .176, a non-significant difference in self-reported critical thinking favoring the word cloud group.

> "A t-test revealed that students analyzing the text in word clouds reported higher scores on critical thinking than students analyzing the same text in a linear form, F (1, 114) = 1.86, p = .176."
