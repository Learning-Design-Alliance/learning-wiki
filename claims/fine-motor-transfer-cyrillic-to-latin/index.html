Evidence¶

Thibault Asselborn 2021¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental study in which 190 children from grades 1–4 copied a short text in both Cyrillic and Latin on a tablet, with Latin learning time equal to zero for all grades. The article reports that "the quality of the Latin writing increased as well" across grades, interpreted as transfer of fine motor control skills.

"While we found that the quality of Cyrillic writing increased from grades 1 – 4, due to increased practice, we also found that the quality of the Latin writing increased as well, despite the fact that all of the pupils had the same absence of experience in writing in Latin."

Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' interpretation (Discussion) of the co-evolution of features such as average handwriting velocity, where Cyrillic improves faster than Latin across grades; the authors conclude the transfer "is probably a partial or slow transfer". No effect size printed.

"Since there is an improvement in Latin across grades despite that the Latin alphabet is new for all grades, we may conclude there is some transfer of skill between scripts. However, since the improvement in Latin is slower than in Cyrillic, it is probably a partial or slow transfer."

Related Claims¶

  • Low-level dynamic handwriting features may be transferable to more languages, paving the way for a cross-lingual model for detecting handwriting difficulties — related
  • Some Cyrillic–Latin handwriting feature differences are constant across grades and reflect intrinsic differences between the alphabets, particularly pressure and tilt features — related
  • Transfer between scripts depends on the level of handwriting automation: Cyrillic–Latin differences in shakiness decreased with grade and were nearly non-existent by grade 4 — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Children's handwriting specificity transfers across scripts: 90% of Cyrillic–Latin feature rank correlations were significant and correlation levels increased with grade — related
  • For features such as Mean Velocity and Handwriting Density, Cyrillic improves faster than Latin, producing an increasing gap that indicates incomplete transfer — reports the opposite
  • Fine motor precision and manual dexterity are positively correlated with handwriting legibility in preschool children, and Functional Dexterity Test time is inversely correlated — related
