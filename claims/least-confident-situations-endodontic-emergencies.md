`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive radar-chart analysis (Figure 1) of mean confidence per communication item by year among the 227 respondents. Students reported the lowest confidence for angry, unpleasant, or threatening patients and patients with mental disabilities.

> "The types of patients for whom students felt least confident in their ability to manage endodontic emergencies were: angry, unpleasant, or threatening patients (40%, 33%, and 23% “not confident” in fourth, fifth, and sixth year, respectively); and patients with mental disabilities (22%, 18%, and 17% “not confident” in fourth, fifth, and sixth year, respectively) (Figure 1)."

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Radar-chart analysis of technical-skill items (Figure 4) by year of study. The lowest-confidence procedures were the inferior alveolar nerve block, high tuberosity maxillary nerve block technique, intrapulpal anaesthesia, and intraoral drainage, with sixth-year students more confident than lower grades.

> "The procedures for which students were least confident were the inferior alveolar nerve block, high tuberosity technique for maxillary nerve block, intrapulpal anaesthesia, and intraoral drainage (Figure 4). Overall, sixth‐year students were more confident in the procedures than students in lower grades."
