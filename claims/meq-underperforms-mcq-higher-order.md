## Evidence

### Hift Richard J 2014

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Palmer and Devitt's study of rigorously prepared exit examinations: over 50% of MCQ items versus 25% of MEQ items tested higher-level cognition, with more item-writing flaws in the MEQ paper and marking-scheme flaws in 60% of MEQs.

> "More than 50% of the multiple-choice items assessed higher level cognition, as opposed to just 25% of the MEQ items. The problem was compounded by a higher frequency of item-writing flaws in the MEQ paper, and flaws were found in the marking scheme in 60% of the MEQ's."

### Hift Richard J 2014 (2)

Hift Richard J. (2014). Should essays and other “open-ended”-type questions retain a place in written summative assessment in clinical medicine? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0249-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Feletti and Smith's finding that increasing the number of open-ended items to raise reliability displaces higher-order questions in favour of factual recall, causing the formats to converge.

> "Feletti and Smith have shown that as the number of items in the open-ended examination increases, questions probing high-order cognitive skills tend to be replaced by questions requiring factual recall alone"
