## Evidence

### Zand Vahid 2026

Zand Vahid, Bazi Ramin, Keshmiri Fatemeh. (2026). Game-based blended learning versus traditional lecture for evidence-based medicine (EBM) competencies in undergraduate medical students: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09628-3

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of 58 clerkship students cluster-assigned to puzzle-based blended (n=27) or interactive lecture (n=31), assessed with the ACE tool pre/post. ANCOVA controlling for pre-test, age, and sex showed "a large effect size (partial η² = 0.292)" favoring the game-based group.

> "after adjusting for these covariates, the intervention effect on total EBM competency scores remained statistically significant (F (1, 53) = 21.46, p < 0.001), with a large effect size (partial η² = 0.292)"
