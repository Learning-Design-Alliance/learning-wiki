# Elementary schools with clusters of three or more NBCTs demonstrate greater strength on 7 of 12 essential supports measures

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Elementary schools with NBCT clusters show significantly higher scores on teacher influence, collective responsibility, reflective dialogue, innovation, school commitment, teacher-teacher trust, and parent involvement, controlling for demographic differences. [→ Hart 2008](#hart-2008)
`q2 i1` High schools with NBCT clusters show stronger teacher-principal trust than non-cluster schools, but teachers report less teacher-parent interaction. [→ Hart 2008 (2)](#hart-2008-2)

## Evidence

### Hart 2008

Hart, H. M., Sporte, S. E., Ponisciak, S. M., Stevens, W. D., & Cambronne, A. (2008). Teacher and Principal Leadership in Chicago: Ongoing Analyses of Preparation Programs. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teacher-and-principal-leadership-chicago-ongoing-analyses-preparation-programs

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of CCSR 2007 teacher survey data comparing 44 elementary cluster schools to non-cluster schools on essential supports measures, controlling for teacher and school characteristics. Effect sizes in Table 5 range from .13 to .36 standard deviation units.

> "Elementary schools with clusters of three or more NBCTs demonstrated greater strength in 7 out of 12 essential supports measures, when compared to other schools, even taking into account underlying demographic and other differences."

### Hart 2008 (2)

Hart, H. M., Sporte, S. E., Ponisciak, S. M., Stevens, W. D., & Cambronne, A. (2008). Teacher and Principal Leadership in Chicago: Ongoing Analyses of Preparation Programs. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/teacher-and-principal-leadership-chicago-ongoing-analyses-preparation-programs

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of CCSR 2007 teacher survey data for 16 high schools with NBCT clusters; teacher-principal trust effect size .38 in Table 5. The chapter also reports teachers in cluster high schools reported less teacher-parent interaction (effect size -.13).

> "Among high schools, only teacher-principal trust was stronger for the cluster schools."
