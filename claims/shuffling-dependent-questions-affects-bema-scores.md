## Evidence

### Ene 2019

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article's summary statement of its comparison of fixed-order (WebAssign) versus shuffled one-question-per-page (local platform) presentations, using cleaned homogeneous subpopulations PRE and POST.

> "We found clear evidence that shuffling the order of the independent questions does not affect the scores of the BEMA test, while shuffling the order of the dependent questions does."

### Ene 2019 (2)

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Item-popularity comparison for question Q1 on the cleaned PRE sample, tested via normal approximation at the 0.001 level; the printed popularities are 0.652 versus 0.502, a 15% difference.

> "Its popularity for the quartile 1 at pre-instruction was 0.652 if presented as the first question but 0.502 if presented in a random position (a 15% difference)."

### Ene 2019 (3)

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of Q21 popularity between fixed-order and shuffled presentations on the cleaned PRE and POST samples; the effect held at pre-instruction only, so the finding is mixed across test takes.

> "Question 21, which refers to a bar magnet, received significantly less correct answers when it was presented separately from the other questions pertaining to magnetism at pre-instruction; no difference was found at post-instruction."
