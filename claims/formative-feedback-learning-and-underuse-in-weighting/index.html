Evidence¶

Paul 2025¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review states, citing Albano & Capuano, Caldwell & Gedeon, and Vu, that formative feedback helps graders develop reasoning, critical thinking, and writing skills. Narrative synthesis of cited studies; no effect size.

"Formative feedback helps graders develop their reasoning skills [92], foster critical thinking [93], and even improve their writing skills [24]."

Paul 2025 (2)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes to Gamage et al. the perception that "grades were viewed as less valuable than feedback", with feedback seen as the most valuable part. Perception finding; no effect size.

"Gamage et al. [95] mentioned that grades were viewed as less valuable than feedback, which was often seen as the most valuable part of the assignment."

Paul 2025 (3)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Yuan et al., that weighting by textual-feedback thoroughness (predicted via BERT) achieved "a mean squared error 6% lower than other baseline methods". MSE reduction printed; no standardized effect size.

"Yuan et al. [64] incorporated the thoroughness of textual feedback as a weighting factor in grade aggregation, achieving a mean squared error 6% lower than other baseline methods."
