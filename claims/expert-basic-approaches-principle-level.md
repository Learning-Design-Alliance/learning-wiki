## Evidence

### Chi et al. 1981

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Study Four: two experienced physicists stated basic approaches for 20 problems with surface configurations crossed with principles; agreement was nearly perfect, with only three apparent disagreements resolved as a work-versus-energy-conservation distinction.

> "In particular, experts gave "basic approaches'' defined by the major physics principle they would apply to the problem, and there was near total agreement between the experts regarding the principle they would apply."

### Chi et al. 1981 (2)

Michelene Chi, Paul Feltovich, Robert Glaser. (1981). Categorization and Representation of Physics Problems by Experts and Novices. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0502_2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same Study Four task: the two novices either made very global statements about how to proceed or attempted to solve the problem giving detailed equation sets, so their responses could not be analyzed like the experts' principle-level approaches.

> "Novicq were unable to formulate solution methods at intermediate levels of abstraction between "meta-level" prescriptions for how to proceed and highly specific equations."
