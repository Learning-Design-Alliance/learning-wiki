# Children's knower level of verbal number words is cross-sectionally associated with ANS acuity even after controlling for age and sex

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across preschoolers, children with more advanced verbal number knowledge (higher knower level) had better ANS acuity (lower w) at both Session 2 and Session 5, controlling for age and sex. [→ Shusterman Anna 2016](#shusterman-anna-2016)

## Evidence

### Shusterman Anna 2016

Shusterman Anna, Slusser Emily, Halberda Justin, Odic Darko. (2016). Acquisition of the Cardinal Principle Coincides with Improvement in Approximate Number System Acuity in Preschoolers. PLoS ONE 11(4): e0153072. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0153072

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional analyses of 42 children (S2) and 39 children (S5) in the six-month longitudinal study. Post-hoc tests showed subset-knowers differed from both CP-knower groups (p < .05 for all four comparisons), but CP-Mappers did not differ from CP-Non-Mappers; the authors call it "the first demonstration of a relationship between knower level and ANS acuity when controlling for age and sex."

> "A one-way ANCOVA, with knower level (one-knower, two-knower, three-knower, four-knower, CP-Mapper, CP-Non-Mapper) as the between subjects variable, w as the dependent variable, and age and sex as covariates, revealed a significant effect of knower level on w both at S2, F(5, 37) = 4.00, p < .01, and at S5, F(5, 37) = 4.43, p < .01."
