## Evidence

### Alexis Olsho 2021

Alexis Olsho, Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2021). Online test administration results in students selecting more responses to multiple-choice-multiple-response items. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2106.02137](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2106.02137)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The article reports, citing its companion PIQL validation work, that no significant differences in single-response item performance were found between administration methods; no effect size is printed, and equivalence was not tested.

> "We note there were no signiﬁcant differences in student performance on the PIQL's SR items when we moved from on-paper to online administration [6]; however, differences in performance on the PIQL's MCMR items suggest that administration method has an effect on how students interact with an assessment."
