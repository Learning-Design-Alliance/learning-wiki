# After the personalized quiz strategy, the experimental group's correctly answered questions shifted toward higher difficulty levels, most notably levels two and six

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Proportions of correctly answered questions across six difficulty levels differed significantly between pretest and posttest in the experimental group but not the control group. [→ Yi-Ting Huang 2018](#yi-ting-huang-2018)
`q3 i?` A posteriori comparisons showed correct answers at levels two and six significantly higher, and at levels one and four significantly lower, in the posttest than the pretest of the experimental group. [→ Yi-Ting Huang 2018 (2)](#yi-ting-huang-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Yi-Ting Huang 2018

Yi-Ting Huang, Meng Chang Chen, and Yeali S. Sun. (2018). Development and Evaluation of a Personalized Computer-aided Question Generation for English Learners to Improve Proficiency and Correct Mistakes. IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computing. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Chi-square test for homogeneity of proportions on the 28-item tests (six difficulty levels) in the same quasi-experiment, comparing pretest and posttest contingency tables per group (Table 4). The personalized test "affected the ability of the students in the experimental group" only.

> "The results of the experimental group ( X 2 (5)=16.24, p<0.01) show signifi- cantly different proportions between the pretest and post - test, while the control group ( X 2 (5)=7 .46, p>0.05) present- ed similar percentages among the six difficulty levels."

### Yi-Ting Huang 2018 (2)

Yi-Ting Huang, Meng Chang Chen, and Yeali S. Sun. (2018). Development and Evaluation of a Personalized Computer-aided Question Generation for English Learners to Improve Proficiency and Correct Mistakes. IEEE Transactions on Emerging Topics in Computing. Originally published 2014. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1808.09732)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

A posteriori follow-up comparisons on the experimental group's contingency table reported in the results text. Correct answering increased at the harder levels (two and six) and decreased at easier levels (one and four), suggesting more higher-difficulty questions were answered correctly after the personalized quiz strategy.

> "an a posteriori comparison revealed that the number of correctly answered questions with level two and level six in the post-test were statistically higher than those in the pretest, whereas the number of questions with level one and level four in the post-test were significantly lower than those in the pretest."
