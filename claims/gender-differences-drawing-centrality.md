# Female and male students’ IPLS drawings differ in element centrality with large effect sizes: female drawings centralize self and group members, male drawings centralize the instructor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r3` · `q2` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Female students’ drawings feature a central idea of explicitly identified self and lab group members collaborating through confusion or being stuck, while male students’ drawings centralize the instructor interacting with unidentified students, with large effect sizes (d = 2.44 for Group Members, d = 2.88 for Instructor). [→ W. Brian Lane 2025](#w-brian-lane-2025)

## Evidence

### W. Brian Lane 2025

W. Brian Lane, Charlotte Dries, Gabriella Khazal, and Tiffany Snow. (2025). Network analysis of students’ drawn representations of an introductory lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681)

`q2 · i3` · `qualitative · r3`

Subnetwork comparison of female (N = 44) and male (N = 28) students’ drawings. Table II prints large effect sizes for this contrast, including d = 2.44 for Group Members and d = 2.88 for Instructor, with all p values < .001.

> "Examining the elements in Table II with large effect sizes based on gender, we observe that female students’ drawings feature a central idea of their self and their lab group members (both explicitly identified) collaborating through points of confusion or being stuck. On the other hand, male students’ drawings feature a central idea of the instructor interacting with students (not explicitly identified)."
