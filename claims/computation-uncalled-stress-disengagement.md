# Computational activities added stress that students perceived as uncalled for, prompting some to disengage by copying others' solutions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students experienced computational activities as unnecessary stress because they already knew the relevant physics and computation felt like a hoop; one student's coping response was to disengage and copy along a "copy train." [→ Hamerski 2022](#hamerski-2022)
`q2 i?` A second student experienced "undue stress" from a tension between computation and her perceived physics knowledge, and coped by mentally separating physics from computation. [→ Hamerski 2022 (2)](#hamerski-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Hamerski 2022

Hamerski, P. C., McPadden, D., Caballero, M. D., & Irving, P. W. (2022). Students' perspectives on computational challenges in physics class. Physical Review Physics Education Research 18, 020109. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.18.020109

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Student interview excerpt from the case study's stress-or-frustration findings section. Circe describes computational activities as "unnecessary stress" and reports disengaging by copying within her group rather than confronting the difficulty.

> "I feel like it's just unnecessary stress, and I'm not about to put myself through that. So I just kind of sit there with the people, and we just talk and wait for one person to figure it out. Like I said, a copy train."

### Hamerski 2022 (2)

Hamerski, P. C., McPadden, D., Caballero, M. D., & Irving, P. W. (2022). Students' perspectives on computational challenges in physics class. Physical Review Physics Education Research 18, 020109. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.18.020109

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview excerpt from the same findings section. Ed reports "undue stress" arising when computation challenged her sense of knowing physics, and the authors note she coped by separating physics and computation mentally to preserve her self-view as competent.

> "I feel like [computation] causes me, sometimes, a lot of undue stress, which is like 'Oh, you don't know this and this and this.' So it's like, 'you do, you just think about it in a different way, but that's not a way that can be programmed on the platform.'"
