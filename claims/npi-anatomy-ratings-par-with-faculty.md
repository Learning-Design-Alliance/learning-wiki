# Near-peer instructors teaching anatomy dissection are rated by first-year students on par with professional teachers, and significantly higher in 2016

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 2016, NPIs received a significantly higher satisfaction score than professional preclinical teachers (4.75 ± 0.44 vs 3.80 ± 0.73, p < 0.0001). [→ Dickman N 2017](#dickman-n-2017)
`q2 i?` In 2014, NPIs' average score (4.10 ± 0.88) was somewhat lower, non-significantly, than the professional teachers' score (4.26 ± 0.69); in 2015 the two groups received almost identical scores. [→ Dickman N 2017 (2)](#dickman-n-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Dickman N 2017

Dickman N, Barash A, Reis S, Karasik D. (2017). Students as anatomy near-peer teachers: a double-edged sword for an ancient skill. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0996-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Program evaluation comparing sophomore near-peer instructors with professional preclinical teachers, rated by first-year students on a five-point Likert scale at the end of the Anatomy block. The article reports the 2016 contrast as "significantly higher score than the professional teachers" with p-value <0.0001.

> "in the 2016, the NPIs received significantly higher score than the professional teachers, 4.75 ± 0.44 and 3.80 ± 0.73, respectively (p-value <0.0001)"

### Dickman N 2017 (2)

Dickman N, Barash A, Reis S, Karasik D. (2017). Students as anatomy near-peer teachers: a double-edged sword for an ancient skill. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0996-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same evaluation in 2014: of 70 students, 41 feedback forms were returned (~58%); NPIs averaged 4.10 ± 0.88 versus 4.26 ± 0.69 for professional teachers, described as "somewhat lower (non-significant)". In 2015 both groups received almost identical scores (4.24 ± 0.69 and 4.25 ± 0.99).

> "the average score was 4.10 ± 0.88, somewhat lower (non-significant) than the general satisfaction score for the professional preclinical teachers"
