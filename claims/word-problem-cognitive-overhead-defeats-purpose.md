## Evidence

### Frank Quinn 2013

Frank Quinn. (2013). Fractions in Elementary Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the cognitive-overhead section, drawing on cognitive science findings about limited working memory and interference between mathematical analysis and thinking about physical significance. The author argues mathematical analysis and physical-significance thinking "strongly interfere with each other".

> "the cognitive overhead in traditional word problems makes it very diﬃcult for students to work them unless the mathematical core is trivial, or unless they learn to work them without engaging the physical context."

### Frank Quinn 2013 (2)

Frank Quinn. (2013). Fractions in Elementary Education. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.2235)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

Theoretical argument continuing the cognitive-overhead analysis: routine, repetitive problems lead students to infer operations without engaging context, including explicit keyword approaches. The author states these strategies "only work for mathematically-trivial problems and they do not open any doors".

> "the strategies students use to deal with this cognitive overhead often defeat the educational justiﬁcation for word problems. But, unlike professional modeling, these strategies only work for mathematically-trivial problems and they do not open any doors."
