# Growth mindsets associate with higher US mathematics scores but slightly lower Chinese mathematics scores (country × mindset interaction)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The country × mindset interaction significantly predicted mathematics performance, with fixed mindsets associated with higher scores among Chinese students and lower scores among US students. [→ Xin Sun 2021](#xin-sun-2021)
`q3 i0` For US students, fixed mindset groups scored lower with moderate-to-large effect sizes (d = −0.25 to −0.86), while for Chinese students fixed groups scored higher with small effect sizes (d = 0.13–0.31). [→ Xin Sun 2021 (2)](#xin-sun-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Xin Sun 2021

Xin Sun, Shaylene Nancekivell, Susan A. Gelman and Priti Shah. (2021). Growth mindset and academic outcomes: a comparison of US and Chinese students. npj Science of Learning 6:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00100-z

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression of PISA mathematics scores on country, mindset response, and their interaction; the omnibus "interaction also yielded significance", β = 34.16, indicating the mindset–achievement link differs across the two countries.

> "Importantly, their interaction also yielded significance, βcountry*mindset= 34.16, SEcountry*mindset= 2.28, t = 14.99, 95% CI = (29.69, 38.62), suggesting that mindset is associated with mathematics achievements differently across the two countries."

### Xin Sun 2021 (2)

Xin Sun, Shaylene Nancekivell, Susan A. Gelman and Priti Shah. (2021). Growth mindset and academic outcomes: a comparison of US and Chinese students. npj Science of Learning 6:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00100-z

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Six pairwise comparisons of PISA mathematics scores by mindset response per country (Table 2); for Chinese students fixed-mindset students scored higher with "small effect sizes (d = 0.13–0.31)".

> "For US students, those who held fixed mindsets had lower mathematics scores than those with relatively more growth mindsets (Mscore difference = −22.64 to −72.78) and this association yielded moderate to large effect sizes ( d = −0.25 to −0.86)."
