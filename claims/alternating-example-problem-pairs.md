# Alternating example-practice pairs produce faster and more accurate near-transfer solutions than blocked presentation of examples followed by practice

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants studying six interleaved example-practice pairs took less time and produced more accurate solutions on near-transfer posttest problems than participants receiving all examples then all practice problems. [→ Atkinson 2000](#atkinson-2000)

## Evidence

### Atkinson 2000

Atkinson, R. K., Derry, S. J., Renkl, A., & Wortham, D. (2000). Learning from Examples: Instructional Principles from the Worked Examples Research. Review of Educational Research, 70(2), 181-214. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543070002181

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Trafton and Reiser, an experiment using a LISP programming curriculum comparing alternating versus blocked sequencing of six example-practice pairs, with time to solution and accuracy on three near-transfer problems as measures.

> "participants in the alternating-example condition took less time and produced more accurate solutions on the transfer posttest than their counterparts in the blocked-example condition."
