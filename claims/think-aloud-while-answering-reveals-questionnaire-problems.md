# In the case study, students answering the questionnaire while thinking aloud showed misunderstanding of items, general rather than task-specific reference perspectives, middle-category 'neutral' answering, and probable social desirability

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Think-aloud questioning revealed that some items were misunderstood (e.g., the term 'concepts', abstract phrases like 'drawing conclusions'), some answers referred to general learning rather than the task, uncertain students chose the middle scale point as a 'neutral' answer, and social desirability may have affected ratings. [→ Gonny Schellings 2011](#gonny-schellings-2011)

## Evidence

### Gonny Schellings 2011

Gonny Schellings. (2011). Applying learning strategy questionnaires: problems and possibilities. Metacognition and Learning, 6, 91–109. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9069-5

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Case study in which four ninth-graders thought aloud while answering the questionnaire; the author summarizes the answering problems observed. Many questions were answered in a clear, task-specific way, and the author notes "we have presented almost all notable verbalizations."

> "sometimes students were not understanding the question, sometimes the students were 'rating' their general learning, and sometimes social desirability or uncertainty were probably playing a role."
