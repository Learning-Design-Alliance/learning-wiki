# Virtual reality simulation training improved reserve nurses' theoretical knowledge, emergency care capability, and total disaster preparedness, but not post-disaster management

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` In a quasi-experimental study of 120 reserve nurses, the virtual reality training group scored significantly higher than controls on theoretical knowledge, emergency care capability, and total disaster preparedness, with no significant difference in post-disaster management and slightly higher technical skills in the control group. [→ Delbene Lara 2026](#delbene-lara-2026)

`q2 i?` Postintervention DPET total score and the knowledge and skills regarding disaster subscales were significantly higher in the intervention group (p<0.001). [→ Zhang Dandan 2021](#zhang-dandan-2021)

## Evidence

### Delbene Lara 2026

Delbene Lara, Mezini Sara, Cacciabue Camilla, Watson Roger, Aleo Giuseppe, Di Nitto Marco, Zanini Milko, Catania Gianluca, Sasso Loredana, Bagnasco Annamaria. (2026). Effective Educational Programs for Disaster Preparedness and Management: A Rapid Evidence Assessment. International Nursing Review. https://doi.org/10.1111/inr.70193

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Prospective quasi-experimental study with control group (Zhang et al. 2021, China) of a 3-month virtual reality simulation training program incorporating COVID-19 cases in 120 reserve nurses (60 per group). The review reports "p < 0.001" for the main contrasts; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The intervention group showed significantly higher post‐intervention scores in theoretical knowledge, emergency care capability, and total disaster preparedness compared with the control group (p < 0.001). Disaster preparedness knowledge and skills subscales significantly improved (p < 0.001). No significant difference was found in post‐disaster management (p > 0.05). Technical skills scores were slightly higher in the control group (p < 0.01)."

### Zhang Dandan 2021

Zhang Dandan, Liao Hongwu, Jia Yitong, Yang Wenren, He Pingping, Wang Dongmei, Chen Yongjun, Yang Wei, Zhang Yin-Ping. (2021). Effect of virtual reality simulation training on the response capability of public health emergency reserve nurses in China: a quasiexperimental study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-048611

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasiexperimental study using the Chinese version of the Disaster Preparedness Evaluation Tool (45 items, Cronbach's α 0.865); postintervention total was 235.22±10.75 versus 218.88±21.34, t=5.295, p<0.001.

> "The scores of knowledge and skills regarding disaster in the intervention group were significantly higher than those in the control group (p<0.001, table 2), while the scores of postdisaster management showed no statistical significance between two groups (p>0.05, table 2)"
