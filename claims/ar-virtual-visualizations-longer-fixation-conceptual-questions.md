# During conceptual questions, learners fixate significantly longer on virtual AR visualizations than on non-virtual elements of an AR-enhanced quantum cryptography experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Dependent t-tests on normalized total fixation durations show learners focused significantly longer on virtual than non-virtual elements for questions 1 and 2 (p < α, also under Bonferroni-corrected αB = 0.0125), while for questions 3 and 4 the difference is not significant. ## Evidence

### David Dzsotjan 2024

David Dzsotjan, Atakan Coban, Christoph Hoyer, Stefan Küchemann, Jürgen Durst, Anna Donhauser, Jochen Kuhn. (2024). Quantum cryptography visualized: assessing visual attention on multiple representations with eye tracking in an AR-enhanced quantum cryptography student experiment. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.21975](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21975)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pilot study inferential analysis: dependent t-tests on eye-tracking fixation durations of N=38 students answering four QKD questions. The article reports "significantly longer on virtual than on non-virtual elements" for questions 1 and 2, holding under Bonferroni correction; no effect size is printed in the quoted text.

> "Taking α = 0.05, we can see that indeed, for Questions 1 and 2 the learners focus significantly longer on virtual than on non-virtual elements ( p < α) while for questions 3 and 4 this difference is not significant (p > α)."
