## Evidence

### Jessica Van Brummelen 2021

Jessica Van Brummelen, Tommy Heng, Viktoriya Tabunshchyk. (2021). Teaching Tech to Talk: K-12 Conversational Artificial Intelligence Literacy Curriculum and Development Tools. arXiv. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1609/aaai.v35i17.17844](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i17.17844)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Scenario-based assessment question about an Alexa skill trained with particular intent phrases, administered in the workshops. The article reports the even 50% split and that the most common reason for non-recognition was the phrase not exactly matching training data (40% of tagged answers).

> "With the given information, the system would have likely been able to learn the new phrase; however, responses were split evenly (50%) between if it would be recognized or not."

### Jessica Van Brummelen 2021 (2)

Jessica Van Brummelen, Tommy Heng, Viktoriya Tabunshchyk. (2021). Teaching Tech to Talk: K-12 Conversational Artificial Intelligence Literacy Curriculum and Development Tools. arXiv. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1609/aaai.v35i17.17844](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i17.17844)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Final-questionnaire question about implications of millions of people using students' final projects. The article reports "57% related to positive effects and 37% related to negative", and interprets negative effects as critical to understanding AI ethics.

> "Of the tagged quotes, 57% related to positive effects and 37% related to negative."
