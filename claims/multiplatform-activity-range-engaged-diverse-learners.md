# The range of multiplatform activity types and multiple representations of NGSS-aligned concepts engaged students with different learning preferences and abilities

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Educators reported that five different activity types presenting content in different modalities helped English learners, students with IEPs, and visual and kinesthetic learners. [→ Li 2022](#li-2022)

## Evidence

### Li 2022

Li, L., Hayakawa, M., Freese, J., Daniels, B., Weiser, G., Luttgen, K., Lor, M. C., Schneider, M., Huang, C.-W., & Jensen, E. (2022). Pivoting an in-person transmedia science program to a virtual program during a pandemic: Lessons learned. Children, Youth and Environments, 32(3), 53-81. http://www.jstor.org/action/showPublication?journalCode=chilyoutenvi

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Study 2 educator interview in the Virtual Implementation Successes section: one teacher noted it was "particularly great for them to have five different types of activities," each helpful for different students, so learners "can see the content in different ways, in different modalities."

> "the range of different activities (videos, digital games, hands-on activities, eBooks, and a digital notebook) and multiple representations of key NGSS-aligned science concepts woven throughout each of the activities engaged students with different learning preferences and abilities."
