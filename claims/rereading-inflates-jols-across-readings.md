# Rereading raises judgements of learning after each reading while multiple text reading raises them only after the second reading (significant interaction, ηp2 = 0.07)

## Evidence

### Peter Seban 2025

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Two-way mixed ANOVA on JOLs collected after each of three readings (experimental groups only), reported in the Results section: the time-point × approach interaction was significant with the article's printed "F(2, 244) = 9.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.07"; main effects were time-point η2 = 0.22 and approach ηp2 = 0.04.

> "there was a significant interaction between the time-points of measurement of JOLs and the reading approach that was utilized: F(2, 244) = 9.54, p < 0.001, ηp2 = 0.07."

### Peter Seban 2025 (2)

Peter Seban, Kamila Urban, Radovan Sikl. (2025). Comparing the effectiveness of multiple text reading and rereading on knowledge retention and metacognitive accuracy. Instructional Science, 53, 337–364. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09686-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Tukey HSD post-hoc tests of the mixed ANOVA: the RR group showed significant JOL increases between the first and second (p < 0.001) and second and third readings (p < 0.001), while the MTR group showed a significant increase only between the first and second reading (p = 0.034).

> "In short, rereading led to anticipation of higher scores after each reading, while multiple text reading resulted in higher JOLs only after the second reading – the third reading did not make leaners any more certain about their performance."
