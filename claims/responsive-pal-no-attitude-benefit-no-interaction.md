# A responsive PAL does not improve learner attitudes toward the agent, and competency and interaction type show no significant interaction

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was no main effect of PAL interaction type on attitudes; the hypothesis that a responsive PAL would positively affect attitudes was not supported. [→ Kim 2006](#kim-2006)

## Evidence

### Kim 2006

Kim, Y., Baylor, A. L., & PALS Group. (2006). Pedagogical Agents as Learning Companions: The Role of Agent Competency and Type of Interaction. Educational Technology Research and Development, 54(3), 223-243. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-006-8805-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null finding from the univariate analyses in the 2x2 experiment with 72 undergraduates; no effect size was printed for this contrast, so the finding reports non-detection rather than equivalence.

> "There was no main effect of PAL interaction type on attitudes. Hypothesis 4 that the responsive PAL would have a positive impact on student attitudes toward the PAL was not supported by the results."

### Kim 2006 (2)

Kim, Y., Baylor, A. L., & PALS Group. (2006). Pedagogical Agents as Learning Companions: The Role of Agent Competency and Type of Interaction. Educational Technology Research and Development, 54(3), 223-243. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-006-8805-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Omnibus MANCOVA with prior self-efficacy as covariate across four dependent variables; significant main effects for competency (Partial η2 = .28) and interaction type (Partial η2 = .15) but no significant interaction effect.

> "The overall MANCOVA yielded a significant main effect for competency, Wilks's Lambda = .72, F (4, 61) = 5.81, p < .001, Partial η2 = .28, and a significant main effect for interaction type, Wilks 's Lambda = .85, F (4, 61) = 2.61, p < .05, Partial η2 = .15. There was no overall significant interaction effect between competency and interaction type."
