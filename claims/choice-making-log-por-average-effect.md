# Re-analysis of choice-making meta-analysis estimates an average log-POR effect of -1.51 for allowing choice-making

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A random effects meta-analysis of 9 studies (27 participants) from Shogren et al. (2004) estimates the average effect of allowing choice-making at -1.51 on the log-POR scale, corresponding to a 64-87% reduction in prevalence odds. [→ Pustejovsky 2013](#pustejovsky-2013)

## Evidence

### Pustejovsky 2013

Pustejovsky, J. E. (2013). Operationally comparable effect sizes for quantifying changes in behavior, with application to meta-analysis of single-case studies. SREE Spring 2013 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED563364

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Application section: re-analysis of the Shogren et al. (2004) meta-analysis, in which 9 of 13 studies (27 participants) used the described recording methods; the random effects model yields an average log-POR of -1.51, CI [-2.01, -1.02].

> "In this preliminary analysis, the average effect of allowing choice-making is estimated to be -1.51 with a 95% confidence interval of [-2.01, -1.02]. This overall average effect corresponds to a reduction of between 64% and 87% in the prevalence odds from the no-choice baseline condition."
