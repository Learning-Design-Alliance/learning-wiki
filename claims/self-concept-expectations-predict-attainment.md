## Evidence

### Dempsey 2026

Dempsey, C., & McCoy, S. (2026). The role of academic self‐concepts, parent expectations and teacher–student interactions in socio‐economic gaps in educational attainment. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 422–442. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Structural equation model (Model 4) regressing age-20 attainment onto initial levels and changes in self-concept and expectations, covarying academic ability and SEN status; good fit (CFI = .982, RMSEA = .044). Standardized estimates appear in Table 3.

> "increases in parents' expectations and increases in children's academic self‐concept across the transition to secondary school were associated with higher levels of educational attainment."

### Dempsey 2026 (2)

Dempsey, C., & McCoy, S. (2026). The role of academic self‐concepts, parent expectations and teacher–student interactions in socio‐economic gaps in educational attainment. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 96, 422–442. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.70025

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group comparison within Model 4; constrained paths fit significantly worse, Δχ2(6) = 52.597, p = .0001. Non-EV counterparts: initial levels Std. Est. = .263, change scores Std. Est. = .173.

> "Both initial parental expectations and changes in expectations had a stronger role on educational attainment for EV children, initial levels Std. Est. = .297, z = .388, p < .0001, change scores Std. Est. = .206, z = .324, p < .0001, compared with their non‐EV counterparts"
