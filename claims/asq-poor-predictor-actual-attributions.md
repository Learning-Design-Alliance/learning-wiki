## Evidence

### Cutrona 1985

Cutrona, C. E., Russell, D., & Jones, R. D. (1985). Cross-Situational Consistency in Causal Attributions: Does Attributional Style Exist? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 47(5), 1043-1058. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.47.5.1043

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Longitudinal study of 85 women assessed during pregnancy and postpartum: ASQ scores from the first interview were correlated with life events, childcare stress, blues symptoms, and daily stress attribution measures. The average total-score correlation was r(83) = .190, a small effect size; internality showed the least consistency (average r = .125).

> "The correlations between the total ASQ score and total scores for the other attribution measures also indicated little evidence of cross-situational consistency in causal attributions [average r(83) = .190], with only two of the five composite scores found to be significantly correlated with the ASQ."
