## Evidence

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors' critique of the commented-on paper; they also note no factor analysis was done on data collected with the shorter version itself, and that statement order changes may alter responses so prior validity tests may not apply.

> "Douglas et al. also do not offer student interview results to support the validity of this shorter version or the categories that they have proposed."

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014 (2)

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

One of six methodological concerns raised; the authors state this "opens up the possibility of there being a self- selected bias in this population." Other concerns include undefined table statistics, inconsistent category naming, and unreported cross-loadings and communalities.

> "their analysis is based on a demographically narrow set of students that is 77% male, 85% non-minority students.  The sample also had a somewhat low response rate of 50%"
