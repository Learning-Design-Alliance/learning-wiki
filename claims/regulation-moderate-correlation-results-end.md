## Subclaims
`q2 i3` At the end of the academic year, regulation of metacognition correlated moderately with year-end results (r=0.504), a statistically stronger correlation than knowledge of metacognition (r=0.353). [→ Hong Wei Han 2015](#hong-wei-han-2015)

## Evidence

### Hong Wei Han 2015

Hong Wei Han, Vadivelu Jamunarani, Daniel Esther Gnanamalar Sarojini, Sim Joong Hiong. (2015). Thinking about thinking: changes in first-year medical students' metacognition and its relation to performance. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v20.27561

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlations at year end for 159 students, printed in Table 3 as 0.353 (knowledge), 0.504 (regulation) and 0.472 (total), all significant at the 0.01 level; regulation's r=0.504 is a moderate effect. The article states regulation's correlation was "statistically higher than the knowledge of metacognition".

> "At the end of the academic year, the correlation of the knowledge of metacognition (r=0.35), regulation of metacognition (r=0.50), and metacognitive skills (r=0.47) and the academic results revealed statistical significance at the 0.05 level"
