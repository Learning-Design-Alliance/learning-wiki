## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The perceived stress × perceived academic support interaction is significant (B = 0.196, p < .001); the stress–performance link is negative at both levels, and Table 5's slopes are more negative at high support (B = -0.820) than at low support (B = -0.368; difference B = -0.451, CI [-0.762, -0.187]), which is a stronger link at high support, not a buffer. The positive interaction term and the authors' buffering reading do not agree with their own slopes. [→ Ma Chenwei 2025](#ma-chenwei-2025)

## Evidence

### Ma Chenwei 2025

Ma Chenwei, Li Jiangyu. (2025). Self-transitioning or other-transitioning? The positive effects of LGBTQ + college students' positive coping and perceived academic support. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07204-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped moderation (2,000 resamples) in the same SEM of 359 Chinese LGBTQ+ college students. The interaction "B = 0.196, SE = 0.056, p <.001" was significant; the high–low difference was significant (B = -0.451, CI [-0.762, -0.187]), which the authors read as supporting Hypothesis 4. Table 5 gives the slope at high support as -0.820 and at low support as -0.368, so the negative link is stronger where support is high; the text also prints the low-support slope as positive (B = 0.368) against a negative confidence interval. The direction of moderation is therefore unresolved by this paper. Support was measured from teachers, peers, and parents.

> "The interaction term of perceived stress and perceived academic support was significant (B = 0.196, SE = 0.056, p <.001). In addition, From Table 5; Fig. 3 low perceived academic support (B = 0.368, SE = 0.110, 95% CI = [-0.595, -0.156]) was significant, and high (B = -0.820, SE = 0.111, 95% CI = [-1.055, -0.626) was also significant."
