## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Teachers shown work attributed to a lower-SES student gave significantly lower overall grades than those shown the same work attributed to a higher-SES student (η² = .02). [→ Doyle 2023](#doyle-2023)

## Evidence

### Doyle 2023

Doyle, L., Easterbrook, M. J., & Harris, P. R. (2023). Roles of socioeconomic status, ethnicity and teacher beliefs in academic grading. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 91–112. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12541

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

In the pre-registered 2×2 experiment (N = 416 in-service and trainee teachers), participants graded an identical year-6 writing sample attributed to a student varying by SES and ethnicity. The quote reports the significant SES main effect: "a grading difference of 4.4%" between higher and lower SES conditions.

> "teachers shown the work of a student from a lower SES background were significantly harsher in their grading compared to those who were shown the work of a student of higher SES (p < .01; η2 = .02; see Figure 1). This equates to a grading difference of 4.4%"
