# Gesture type affects reported mental effort, with lower effort for beat gestures in Experiment 1 and for deictic gestures in Experiment 2, but no effect on mental load

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 2, students in the deictic condition reported lower mental effort than the control condition (ηp2 = .07); mental load again showed no significant effect. [→ Maik Beege 2020](#maik-beege-2020)

## Evidence

### Maik Beege 2020

Maik Beege, Manuel Ninaus, Sascha Schneider, Steve Nebel, Julia Schlemmel, Jasmin Weidenmüller, Korbinian Moeller, Günter Daniel Rey. (2020). Investigating the effects of beat gestures and deictic gestures of a lecturer in educational videos. Psychology of learning with digital media, Chemnitz University of Technology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103955

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 mental load/effort MANOVA (N = 121), univariate mental-effort effect printed as ηp2 = .07. "students in the deictic condition reported lower ME than students in the control condition (Mdiff = 0.58; p = .01)"; mental load was again not significant.

> "Univariate ANOVAs revealed a significant effect of gesture instruction for ME; F(2, 118) = 4.59, p = .01, ηp2 = .07, but no significant effect for ML; F (2, 118) = 2.18, p = .12. Regarding ME, Sidak-corrected post-hoc analyses indicated that students in the deictic condition reported lower ME than students in the control condition (Mdiff = 0.58; p = .01)."
