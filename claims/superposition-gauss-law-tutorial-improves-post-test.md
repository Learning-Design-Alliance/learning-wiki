## Evidence

### Jing Li 2023

Jing Li, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Helping students apply superposition principle in problems involving spherical, cylindrical and planar charge distributions. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison across sections of a calculus-based introductory E&M course; tutorial sections worked through the tutorial in small groups while comparison sections saw the instructor work similar problems on the board. The t-test showed tutorial students "significantly outperformed students in the comparison group (p value < 0.001)". No effect size is printed.

> "the post-test students in the tutorial group significantly outperformed students in the comparison group (p value < 0.001)"

### Jing Li 2023 (2)

Jing Li, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Helping students apply superposition principle in problems involving spherical, cylindrical and planar charge distributions. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2308.02003)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre/post-test percentage scores reported in the supplementary results section for tutorial and comparison groups. The article reports the tutorial group "improved from an average of 27% to 81%" versus 24% to 35% for the comparison group; these are descriptive percentages with no effect size printed.

> "Students in the comparison group showed a slight improvement from 24% on the pre-test to 35% on the post-test on average, whereas for the tutorial group, students improved from an average of 27% to 81%."
