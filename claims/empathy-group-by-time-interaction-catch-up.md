## Evidence

### Huang Hsiang-Ping 2025

Huang Hsiang-Ping, Tien Yun, Lin Yung-Chang, Yu I-Chen, Chien Nai-Hui. (2025). Effects of empathy mapping and mini-simulation on second-year nursing students’ empathy and communication self-confidence: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06686-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the quasi-experimental study (n = 98), post-test empathy on the Chinese version of the Jefferson Scale of Empathy did not differ between groups (p = 0.737); the repeated measures ANOVA group and time effects were also nonsignificant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The posttest empathy levels measured at the end of the semester did not differ significantly between groups (111.11 ± 11.71 vs. 111.98 ± 113.68, respectively; p = 0.737)."

### Huang Hsiang-Ping 2025 (2)

Huang Hsiang-Ping, Tien Yun, Lin Yung-Chang, Yu I-Chen, Chien Nai-Hui. (2025). Effects of empathy mapping and mini-simulation on second-year nursing students’ empathy and communication self-confidence: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06686-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The repeated measures ANOVA for empathy reported a "significant group-by-time interaction" (F = 5.519, p = 0.021); per Fig. 1A the experimental group started lower and surpassed the control group by semester end.

> "However, there was a significant group-by-time interaction in the between-group effect (F = 5.519, p = 0.021), meaning that the differences in empathy levels between groups varied over time."

### Huang Hsiang-Ping 2025 (3)

Huang Hsiang-Ping, Tien Yun, Lin Yung-Chang, Yu I-Chen, Chien Nai-Hui. (2025). Effects of empathy mapping and mini-simulation on second-year nursing students’ empathy and communication self-confidence: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06686-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

At pretest, measured before any course content, control-group empathy was significantly higher than the experimental group's (p = 0.024). The authors attribute this to random group assignment and urge caution in interpreting results.

> "the mean empathy level was significantly higher in the control group than in the experimental group (111.91 ± 10.08 vs. 107.37 ± 9.56, respectively; p = 0.024)."
