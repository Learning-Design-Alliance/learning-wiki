# Junior middle school teachers are significantly more positive toward contextualized physics problems than senior high school teachers, while male and female teachers do not differ

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Junior middle school teachers score significantly higher than senior high school teachers on the Motivation and Interest sub-measure (U-test p = 0.0012) and lower on Difficulty Perception (p = 0.037). [→ Wei 2025](#wei-2025)
`q2 i?` Male and female teachers show no significant difference on either sub-measure (both p > 0.05). [→ Wei 2025 (2)](#wei-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Wei 2025

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Two U-tests on teacher sub-measure scores (Table 7: junior middle school N = 68, mean 3.07; senior high school N = 86, mean 2.77 for Motivation and Interest, p = 0.0012; Difficulty Perception p = 0.037). No effect size is printed.

> "The results indicate that junior middle school teachers are significantly more positive toward CPP than their senior high school counterparts."

### Wei 2025 (2)

Wei, Y., Peng, X., Zhong, Y., Pi, F., Zhai, Y., & Bao, L. (2025). Can Contextualized Physics Problems Enhance Student Motivation? Uncovering a Student-Teacher Perception Gap through a Large-Scale Survey. [doi:10.1103/2g1b-hmhq](https://doi.org/10.1103/2g1b-hmhq)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

U-tests comparing male (N = 105) and female (N = 49) teachers on both sub-measures (Table 8). Both comparisons are non-significant; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "Two U-tests were conducted for these sub-measures, with both p-values larger than 0.05 (Table 8). The findings indicate no significant difference between male and female teachers for either Motivation and Interest or Difficulty Perception."
