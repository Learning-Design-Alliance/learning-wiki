# Medical students trained with gamification achieve significantly higher OSCE skill scores than students in the routine in-person rotation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The gamification group's overall OSCE score (18.51 ± 0.74) was significantly higher than the routine group's (16.47 ± 0.74), P < 0.001. [→ Karimian Z 2025](#karimian-z-2025)

## Evidence

### Karimian Z 2025

Karimian Z, Momeni M, Zarifsanaiey N. (2025). Enhancing radiographic interpretation: effects of gamification on medical students' knowledge, skills, and satisfaction — a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07523-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Independent t-test on the 10-station OSCE (total 20 points) at course end: gamification group mean 18.51 ± 0.74 versus routine group 16.47 ± 0.74, with 82 students assessed.

> "Notably, the gamification training group achieved significantly higher scores than the routine training group (In-person rotation) (P < 0.001) (see Table 4)."

### Karimian Z 2025 (2)

Karimian Z, Momeni M, Zarifsanaiey N. (2025). Enhancing radiographic interpretation: effects of gamification on medical students' knowledge, skills, and satisfaction — a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07523-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-station analysis depicted in Fig. 3, each station scored 0–2; the article reports the gamification group "achieved mastery-level scores across all stations" while the routine group generally fell below mastery.

> "The comparison between the two groups at each station reveals that students in the gamification group achieved mastery-level scores across all stations, while the routine training group generally scored below the mastery threshold."
