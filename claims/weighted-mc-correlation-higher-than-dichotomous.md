# Weighted simulated multiple-choice scores correlate more highly with rubric-graded free-response performance than dichotomous simulated scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies (3 entries) · 2 causal `r1`–`r2` · `q2` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i3` In course A, correlations between rubric-graded free-response and simulated multiple-choice performance were r = 0.890 and r = 0.483 (dichotomous, Q1 and Q2) versus r = 0.928 and r = 0.945 (weighted, Q1 and Q2). [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i3` Weighted MC simulation correlations with rubric-graded FR performance were r = 0.925 (Q1) and r = 0.945 (Q2), higher than the dichotomous correlations. [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i3` Dichotomous MC simulation correlations with rubric-graded FR performance were r = 0.890 for Question 1 but dropped to r = 0.483 for Question 2. [→ Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#shih-yin-lin-and-chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Can Multiple-Choice Questions Simulate Free-Response Questions? Physics Education Research Conference proceedings (AIP Conference Proceedings). Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1603.07910](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1603.07910)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Correlation analysis (N=153) in course A between rubric-graded free-response performance and simulated multiple-choice performance, dichotomous and weighted, for both questions (Table 5). Weighted correlations reached r = 0.928 (Q1) and r = 0.945 (Q2); all p-values printed as 0.000.

> "Correlation coefficient (r) 0.890 0.483 0.928 0.945"

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Can Free-Response Questions Be Approximated by Multiple-Choice Equivalents? Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1609.00018](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1609.00018)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Correlation analysis (N=153) in course A between rubric-graded free-response scores and simulated weighted multiple-choice scores, reported in Table IX with p-values of 0.000. The article reports correlation coefficients r = 0.925 for Question 1 and r = 0.945 for Question 2, i.e., "0.925 and 0.945" for the weighted simulation.

> "the correlation coefficient between the rubric -graded FR performance and the weighted multiple-choice simulation is very high for both Questions (0.925 and 0.945 for Question 1 and Question 2, respectively.)"

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Can Free-Response Questions Be Approximated by Multiple-Choice Equivalents? Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1609.00018](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1609.00018)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Dichotomous simulation correlations in the same Table IX analysis (N=153): the coefficient for Question 1 is "similarly high" (r = 0.890 in Table IX) but drops to r = 0.483 for Question 2, which had several distracter choices each representing a different level of understanding.

> "When it comes to the correlation between the rubric -graded FR performance and the dichotomous multiple-choice simulation, the correlation coefficient for Question 1 is similarly high but the correlation coefficient for Question 2 drops to 0.483."
