Evidence¶

Sterpu Irene 2024¶

Sterpu Irene, Herling Lotta, Nordquist Jonas, Rotgans Jerome, Acharya Ganesh. (2024). Team-based learning (TBL) in clinical disciplines for undergraduate medical students—a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04975-x

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review's synthesis of the knowledge-acquisition outcome (reported in 38 of 49 studies) counts 23 studies in which TBL participants had "higher final exam grades, mean scores on knowledge tests, or board exam scores"; assessment methods varied across studies.

"Twenty-three studies reported that students who participated in TBL had either higher final exam grades, mean scores on knowledge tests, or board exam scores."

Sterpu Irene 2024 (2)¶

Sterpu Irene, Herling Lotta, Nordquist Jonas, Rotgans Jerome, Acharya Ganesh. (2024). Team-based learning (TBL) in clinical disciplines for undergraduate medical students—a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04975-x

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, citing Langer et al. and Krase et al., no difference in long-term knowledge retention versus non-TBL groups; the authors call this finding quite surprising given TBL's application exercises. No effect sizes are printed.

"While most of the studies showed that knowledge increased after undertaking a TBL module, Langer et al. [53] and Krase et al. [31] found that there was no difference in long-term knowledge retention."
