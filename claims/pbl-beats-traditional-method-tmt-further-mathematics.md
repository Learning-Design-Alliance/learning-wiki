## Evidence

### Fatade et al. 2013

Alfred Olufemi Fatade, David Mogari, Abayomi Adelaja Arigbabu. (2013). EFFECT OF PROBLEM-BASED LEARNING ON SENIOR SECONDARY SCHOOL STUDENTS’ ACHIEVEMENTS IN FURTHER MATHEMATICS. Acta Didactica Napocensia, Volume 6, Number 3. https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/2065-1430

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest non-equivalent control group study of 96 students (42 PBL, 54 TM) in intact classes. The post-test TMT mean was 43.79 (PBL) versus 34.96 (TM); the "mean difference of 8.83" after intervention was significant, while the pre-test difference of 2.60 was not.

> "The mean difference of 2.60 between the control and experimental classes in the pre-test TMT was however not significant (t=1.07, p=.286) whereas the mean difference of 8.83 between the experimental and control classes after the intervention was significant (t=-3.58, p=.001)."

### Fatade et al. 2013 (2)

Alfred Olufemi Fatade, David Mogari, Abayomi Adelaja Arigbabu. (2013). EFFECT OF PROBLEM-BASED LEARNING ON SENIOR SECONDARY SCHOOL STUDENTS’ ACHIEVEMENTS IN FURTHER MATHEMATICS. Acta Didactica Napocensia, Volume 6, Number 3. https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/2065-1430

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA on post-test TMT scores for the experimental and control classes reported in Table 5; between-groups sum of squares 1838.992 (df 1), within-groups 13482.997 (df 94), so F(1,94) = 12.82, p = .001, confirming the t-test result.

> "Further analysis of post-test achievement scores on TMT of students in both the experimental and control classes using one-way ANOVA as contained in Table 5 below showed that difference in means between the two classes was significant (F(1,95) = 12.82; p = .001)." [sic: Table 5 gives 94 within-groups degrees of freedom]
