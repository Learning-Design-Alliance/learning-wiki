## Evidence

### James-Brabham 2024

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Attendance analysis in the RCT: a one-way ANOVA found the number of completed sessions did not significantly vary among conditions (F(2, 246)=0.717, p=.489); the printed mean was 7.41 (SD=0.92) of 8 sessions.

> "Children had high attendance as they completed an average of 7.41 ( SD=0.92, range=3-8) out of 8 game sessions."

### James-Brabham 2024 (2)

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Experimenter-rated on-task engagement per session on a 5-point scale; a mixed ANOVA found a main effect of Session (F(6.68, 1642.27)=2.242, p[gg]=.031) but no significant Condition effect or Condition × Session interaction (ps≥.301), with Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc tests all non-significant.

> "Overall, children displayed high engagement when playing the games, with an average score of 4.5 out of 5 ( SD=0.51, range=2.29-5)."

### James-Brabham 2024 (3)

James-Brabham, E., Jay, T., & Sella, F. (2024). No evidence that playing a linear number board game improves numerical skills beyond teaching as usual: a randomized controlled trial in four- to five-year-old primary school children. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000857

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Post-test enjoyment ratings (two smiley-scale questions averaged) analyzed in a one-way ANOVA; the printed mean was 4.17 (SD=1.15) with no significant condition difference.

> "Children displayed high enjoyment with an average score of 4.17 ( SD=1.15, range=1-5), which did not significantly vary among conditions (F(2, 246)=0.055, p=.946)."
