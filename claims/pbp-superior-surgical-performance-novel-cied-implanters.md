## Evidence

### Mascheroni J 2024

Mascheroni J, Stockburger M, Patwala A, Mont L, Rao A, Retzlaff H, Garweg C, Gallagher AG, Verbelen T. (2024). Surgical skill simulation training to proficiency reduces procedural errors among novice cardiac device implanters: a randomized study. Europace. https://doi.org/10.1093/europace/euae229

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

International prospective randomized (1:1) double-blinded controlled study: 30 novice implanters (n = 15 per group) trained in PBP or SIM curricula and video-scored on porcine tissue using 27 validated intraoperative metrics. The study reports "61.2% fewer Critical Errors" and 60.7% fewer All Errors Combined for PBP.

> "In comparison to the SIM group, the PBP group completed, on average, 11.0% more procedural Steps and made 57.1% fewer Errors, 61.2% fewer Critical Errors, and 60.7% fewer All Errors Combined (total Errors + Critical Errors)."

### Mascheroni J 2024 (2)

Mascheroni J, Stockburger M, Patwala A, Mont L, Rao A, Retzlaff H, Garweg C, Gallagher AG, Verbelen T. (2024). Surgical skill simulation training to proficiency reduces procedural errors among novice cardiac device implanters: a randomized study. Europace. https://doi.org/10.1093/europace/euae229

Results section of the randomized trial reports that apart from non-critical Errors "all differences were statistically significant, and the effect size was large"; no numeric effect size appears in the quoted sentence.

> "Apart from Errors, all differences were statistically significant, and the effect size was large33 (see Table 1)."
