# Standardized sum scores greatly compress individual differences in simulated growth trajectories, while only MIRT scores capture the wide variation in true trajectories

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Sum scores produce highly compressed growth trajectories that poorly reflect true variability in starting point and growth, whereas MIRT scores properly capture the wide variation observed in true scores. [→ Kuhfeld 2020](#kuhfeld-2020)

## Evidence

### Kuhfeld 2020

Kuhfeld, Megan, and James Soland. (2020). Estimating Student Growth on Psychological and Social-emotional Constructs: A Comparison of Multiple Scoring Approaches. EdWorkingPaper No. 20-193. https://www.edworkingpapers.com/ai20-193

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Figure 2 of Simulation Study 1 plots estimated growth trajectories for a random sample of 100 simulees by scoring approach. The text states sum score trajectories are "highly compressed, poorly reflecting the true variability in starting point and growth observed with the true scores."

> "The degree of individual differences in growth trajectories is greatly underestimated when standardized sum scores are used. Instead of having score trajectories that span the y-axis, students’ sum scores are highly compressed, poorly reflecting the true variability in starting point and growth observed with the true scores."
