# Students and researchers gave systematically different types of explanatory comments about the visual argument, with students rejecting pictures as proofs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The two groups' comment categories differed significantly: researchers more often discussed how easy or difficult it would be to turn the argument into a proof, while students more often stated that a picture is not a proof. [→ Matthew Inglis 2008](#matthew-inglis-2008)

## Evidence

### Matthew Inglis 2008

Matthew Inglis, Juan Pablo Mejia-Ramos. (2008). The Effect of Authority on the Persuasiveness of Mathematical Arguments. https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802584513

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Qualitative coding of explanatory comments from roughly 20% of Experiment 1 participants, independently assigned by the two authors to pre-defined categories; the group difference in comment types was significant. No effect size printed.

> "The two groups had a different range, 2(3)=20.9, p<0.001, with the researchers more often discussing how easy or difficult it would be to turn the argument into a proof, and the students more often stating that a picture is not a proof."
