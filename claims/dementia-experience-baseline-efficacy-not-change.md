## Evidence

### FitzGerald J 2025

FitzGerald J, Regan B, Wells Y, Bhar S, Browne W. (2025). Improving clinicians' self‐efficacy in delivering a diagnosis of dementia: A before‐after study. Australasian Journal on Ageing. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajag.70078

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

A secondary Spearman correlation analysis of baseline data from the workshop study. The printed correlation effect size is rho = .44 (p = 0.01), a large correlation, between experience working with people with dementia and self-efficacy at T1; the article states "experience working with people with dementia was positively related to self-efficacy at T1".

> "experience working with people with dementia was positively related to self-efficacy at T1; rho = .44, p = 0.01."

### FitzGerald J 2025 (2)

FitzGerald J, Regan B, Wells Y, Bhar S, Browne W. (2025). Improving clinicians' self‐efficacy in delivering a diagnosis of dementia: A before‐after study. Australasian Journal on Ageing. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajag.70078

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A secondary non-parametric correlation analysis in the same study testing whether duration measures predicted self-efficacy change. The article reports all "Spearman's rho coefficients between change scores and duration measures were non-significant"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Years of experience in the profession and years working with people with dementia were not related to change in self-efficacy (all Spearman's rho coefficients between change scores and duration measures were non-significant)."
