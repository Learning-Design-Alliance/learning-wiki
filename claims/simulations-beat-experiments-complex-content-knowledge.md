# Secondary school students learn more complex marine ecology content from interactive computer simulations than from hands-on experiments, with a larger advantage for the more complex multidimensional topic

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the school study (90-min intervention, N=443), simulation students scored higher on content knowledge than experiment students (d Study I = − 0.19). [→ Krüger 2022](#kruger-2022)
`q3 i3` In the student-laboratory study (180-min intervention, N=367), simulation students scored substantially higher on content knowledge than experiment students (d Study II = − 1.05). [→ Krüger 2022 (2)](#kruger-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Krüger 2022

Krüger, J. T., Höffler, T. N., Wahl, M., Knickmeier, K., & Parchmann, I. (2022). Two comparative studies of computer simulations and experiments as learning tools in school and out-of-school education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09566-1

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison in Study I: 443 grade 10–13 students randomly assigned to experiments (n=221) or simulation (n=222) during a 90-min school intervention; post-test t-test with FDR correction showed higher knowledge for simulation, with printed effect size d Study I = − 0.19.

> "learning with the simulation compared to learning with experiments resulted in higher students’ content knowledge achievement (d Study I = − 0.19; d Study II = − 1.05)"

### Krüger 2022 (2)
