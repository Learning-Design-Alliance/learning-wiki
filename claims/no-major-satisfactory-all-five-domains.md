## Evidence

### Sensibaugh Cheryl A. 2017

Sensibaugh Cheryl A., Madrid Nathaniel J., Choi Hye-Jeong, Anderson William L., Osgood Marcy P. (2017). Undergraduate Performance in Solving Ill-Defined Biochemistry Problems. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(4):ar63. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0106

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Prevalence analysis of all 32 possible combinations of satisfactory domain scores (Table 5, N = 47) among graduating majors on the exit IPSA. The three dominant patterns were satisfactory scores in Evaluate and Reflect; in Integrate, Evaluate, and Reflect; or in Reflect only, together 41% of the sample.

> "Graduating biochemistry majors most commonly exhibited only three different patterns. Indeed, the average pattern (#15) occurred in 13% of cases."

### Sensibaugh Cheryl A. 2017 (2)

Sensibaugh Cheryl A., Madrid Nathaniel J., Choi Hye-Jeong, Anderson William L., Osgood Marcy P. (2017). Undergraduate Performance in Solving Ill-Defined Biochemistry Problems. CBE—Life Sciences Education 16(4):ar63. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.15-04-0106

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same prevalence analysis (Table 5) reports that 6% of the 47 graduates were unsatisfactory in all five domains, and no graduating biochemistry major achieved satisfactory performance across all domains. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size is printed.

> "Considering the other end of the performance spectrum, 6% of students exhibited unsatisfactory performance in all five domains (#1). No graduating biochemistry major was able to achieve satisfactory performance across all domains (#32)."
