# DL performance comprises a general dimension plus four tool-specific residual dimensions, with the specific dimensions defined by the software applications used

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A bifactor model with a global dimension and four specific dimensions fit better than a simple four-dimensional model without a general dimension. [→ Frank Reichert 2020](#frank-reichert-2020)
`q2 i?` The specific residual dimensions capture commonality due to students' familiarity with the assessment tools and contexts rather than mapping onto existing DL frameworks. [→ Frank Reichert 2020 (2)](#frank-reichert-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Frank Reichert 2020

Frank Reichert, Nancy W. Y. Law, Gary K. W. Wong, Jimmy de la Torre. (2020). Exploring the structure of digital literacy competence assessed with authentic software applications. Education Tech Research Dev, 68, 2991–3013. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09825-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory bifactor analysis of thirteen ILPA items in Results: the model combining a general dimension with four specific dimensions fit acceptably (RMSEA = .052, CFI = .978) and "performed better than a simple four-dimensional model without a general dimension".

> "The bifactor model had an acceptable to good model fit (RMSEA = .052, CFI = .978, SABIC = 15,963) and performed better than a simple four-dimensional model without a general dimension (RMSEA = .096, CFI = .909, SABIC = 16,334)."

### Frank Reichert 2020 (2)

Frank Reichert, Nancy W. Y. Law, Gary K. W. Wong, Jimmy de la Torre. (2020). Exploring the structure of digital literacy competence assessed with authentic software applications. Education Tech Research Dev, 68, 2991–3013. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09825-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the bifactor results: the specific "residual" dimensions "seem to be defined by the software applications that students used in the assessment", reflecting tool and context familiarity.

> "Instead, those dimensions seem to be defined by the software applications that students used in the assessment; they capture commonality among students' performance that seems to be due to their familiarity with the assessment tools and/or the context (RQ2)."
