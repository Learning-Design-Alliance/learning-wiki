## Evidence

### Paridon H 2026

Paridon H. (2026). Evidence-based teaching: The examination and use of psychological theories in medical education research using the example of cognitive load theory. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://journals.publisso.de/en/journals/jme/volume43/zma001805

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports experiments, including one on interpreting line plots comparing purely visual versus two-modality presentation, plus geometric-problem and electrical-engineering experiments; the review attributes these to cited studies and notes the audiovisual condition won. No effect size is printed for these experiments.

> "The results showed that the audiovisual presentation was superior to the purely visual one. Crucial for this finding was that both sources of information were connected and essential for the comprehensibility of the material."

### Paridon H 2026 (2)

Paridon H. (2026). Evidence-based teaching: The examination and use of psychological theories in medical education research using the example of cognitive load theory. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://journals.publisso.de/en/journals/jme/volume43/zma001805

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

A meta-analysis on the modality effect, reported by the review, concludes learners across materials and age groups performed better with graphics and spoken text than graphics with printed text; the effect size d=0.72 applies to the high element interactivity moderator.

> "With higher element interactivity (and thus complexity of the material), the modality effect is stronger (d=0.72). Furthermore, the modality effect is greater when the presentation duration is predetermined by the system compared to self-determined duration and with shorter compared to longer texts"
