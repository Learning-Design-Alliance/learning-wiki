# Co-registration changes certificate rates for roughly a third of courses in each direction, with effects governed by course engagement level

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` 32% of courses saw significant certificate-rate increases given co-registration while 35% saw decreases, and courses that benefited tended to benefit from co-registration with any other course. [→ Wintermute 2019](#wintermute-2019)
`q2 i?` Courses with below-average engagement benefit from co-registration because it raises expected engagement; high-engagement courses see co-registration lower expected engagement and certificate rates. [→ Wintermute 2019 (2)](#wintermute-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Wintermute 2019

Wintermute, E. H., Cisel, M., & Lindner, A. B. (2019). A survival model for course-course interactions in a Massive Open Online Course platform. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.04201](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.04201)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Heatmap analysis of 7321 co-registration cohorts from the FUN dataset (Fig. 3A): changes were homogeneous across possible co-registrations, so "courses that benefited from co-registration tended to benefit from co-registration with any other course."

> "32% of courses saw significant increases in certificate rate given co-registration while 35% saw decreases."

### Wintermute 2019 (2)

Wintermute, E. H., Cisel, M., & Lindner, A. B. (2019). A survival model for course-course interactions in a Massive Open Online Course platform. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1905.04201](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1905.04201)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model-derived analysis (Fig. 3B) comparing co-registration effects with course engagement values: the global effect of co-registration on double-log transformed certificate rates was largely governed by log user engagement.

> "In courses with below average user engagement, co-registration will generally increase expected engagement levels. In high-engagement courses, co-registration will generally decrease expected engagement and therefore certificate rates."
