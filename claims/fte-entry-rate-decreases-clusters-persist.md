`q2 i?` As an FTE task progresses, long thinking times become more frequent and larger, while short times continue to occur throughout, consistent with cluster-based retrieval of associated terms. [→ Beatty 2002](#beatty-2002)

## Evidence

### Beatty 2002

Beatty, I. D., Gerace, W. J., & Dufresne, R. J. (2002). Measuring and Modeling Physics Students' Conceptual Knowledge Structures Through Term Association Times. UMass Physics Education Research Group. [doi:10.1119/1.1482067](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1482067)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Global patterns evident in thinking-time-vs-index plots for subjects across both studies. The authors interpret that subjects exhaust readily accessible terms and must think harder later, and that entries cluster because a recalled term suggests connected terms; post-task interviews reportedly confirmed these interpretations.

> "As the task progresses, long thinking times become more frequent, and the long times that appear tend to have much larger values"
