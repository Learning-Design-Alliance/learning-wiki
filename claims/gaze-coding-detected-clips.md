# Qualitative coding of detected clips found students most often gazed at task materials (47.6%), overt help-seeking was nearly absent (1.6%), and the teacher was present in 49.2% of segments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 63 coded video segments (21 interaction events × before/during/after), at-task gaze dominated (30 of 63 segments), overt help-seeking appeared in only one segment, and teacher presence within the boundary occurred in 31 segments. [→ Hur 2026](#hur-2026)

## Evidence

### Hur 2026

Hur, P., Palaguachi, C., Machaka, N., Krist, C., Dyer, E. B., D'Angelo, C., & Bosch, N. (2026). A Framework for Considering Exploration, Interpretation, and Confirmation During Data Analysis: Computationally Assisted Analysis of Teacher–Group Interactions. Journal of Educational Data Mining, 18(1). https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/1030

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Small-scale qualitative coding of 63 randomly selected video segments (21 interaction events × before, during, after) by one researcher using a codebook of gaze, overt help-seeking, and teacher presence. Gaze results included "30 of 63 segments (47.6%) were labelled “at task” gaze"; help-seeking appeared in 1 of 63 segments (1.6%) and teacher presence in 31 segments (49.2%).

> "30 of 63 segments (47.6%) were labelled “at task” gaze, “at teacher” in 16 segments (25.4%), “at peer” in 10 segments (15.9%), “away - distracted” in four segments (6.3%), and ambiguous “other” gazes in three segments (4.8%)."
