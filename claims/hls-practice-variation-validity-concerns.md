## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Home language surveys are the primary identification tool in nearly all states, but question phrasing, content, and application vary substantially and carry documented validity concerns. [→ Linquanti 2013](#linquanti-2013)

## Evidence

### Linquanti 2013

Linquanti, R., & Cook, H. G. (2013). Toward a "Common Definition of English Learner": Guidance for States and State Assessment Consortia in Defining and Addressing Policy and Technical Issues and Options. Council of Chief State School Officers. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED565753

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The paper's review of state identification practices, drawing on Bailey & Kelly's (2012) comprehensive review of HLS practices, reports that HLSs are used in all but four states with substantial variation across states. The authors note surveys can "cast their net" widely or narrowly, affecting false positives and false negatives.

> "Home language surveys (HLS) are primarily used for this purpose in all but four states, but there is substantial variation in survey questions' phrasing, content, and application across states."
