# Supergroup accountability broadens which schools are held responsible for small subgroups but risks recreating averages that mask group differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Combining multiple small subgroups into a supergroup makes more schools accountable for those students than NCLB-style subgroup accountability did. [→ Daria Hall 2013](#daria-hall-2013)
`q2 i?` Supergroups risk recreating the problem subgroup accountability was meant to address: averages masking very different performance among different groups. [→ Daria Hall 2013 (2)](#daria-hall-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Daria Hall 2013

Daria Hall. (2013). A Step Forward or a Step Back? State Accountability in the Waiver Era. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543222

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The report's analysis of state plans (e.g., Arkansas's Targeted Achievement-Gap Group, Kentucky's Gap Group) states that "more schools are accountable for the performance of the smaller groups than they were before" under supergroup construction.

> "By combining multiple small groups into a bigger supergroup, more schools are accountable for the performance of the smaller groups than they were before — and more of the students in those groups are included in accountability as well."

### Daria Hall 2013 (2)

Daria Hall. (2013). A Step Forward or a Step Back? State Accountability in the Waiver Era. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543222

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report warns that combining groups raises the question of "averages masking very different performance among different groups," and notes Nevada and Wisconsin use supergroups only as a backup when a group has fewer than 10 students.

> "But there are big risks here, too. By putting groups together, it's important to ask whether we are recreating the problem that subgroup accountability was meant to address in the first place: averages masking very different performance among different groups."
