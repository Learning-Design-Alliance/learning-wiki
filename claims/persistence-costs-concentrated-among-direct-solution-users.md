## Evidence

### Grace Liu 2026

Grace Liu, Brian Christian, Tsvetomira Dumbalska, Michiel A. Bakker, Rachit Dubey. (2026). AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance. Published as a conference paper at COLM 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional analysis of the AI-assisted subset of Experiment 2, grouping participants by self-reported usage (direct answers 61%, hints 27%, no usage 12%). Pretest ANOVAs showed no group differences; test ANOVAs were significant for both measures.

> "However, a one-way ANOVA test revealed a significant difference between the groups for both test solve rate (F(3, 581) = 7.89, P< 0.001, η2 = 0.039) and test skip rate (F(3, 581) =2.80,P=0.039,η 2 =0.014)."

### Grace Liu 2026 (2)

Grace Liu, Brian Christian, Tsvetomira Dumbalska, Michiel A. Bakker, Rachit Dubey. (2026). AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance. Published as a conference paper at COLM 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise t-tests between usage groups in Experiment 2. Direct-solution users (mean 0.65) scored below controls (d = -0.36), below hint-users (d = -0.29), and below non-users (d = -0.66). The authors note these cross-sectional findings are not necessarily causal.

> "Participants who used the AI to get answers directly demonstrated lower subsequent task performance (mean 0.65, s.d. 0.38) compared to participants in the control group (mean 0.77, s.d. 0.32; t(464) =− 3.77, P< 0.001; Cohen's d=− 0.36, 95% CI [−0.54,-−0.17])"

### Grace Liu 2026 (3)

Grace Liu, Brian Christian, Tsvetomira Dumbalska, Michiel A. Bakker, Rachit Dubey. (2026). AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance. Published as a conference paper at COLM 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Same pairwise analysis: direct-solution users skipped more than controls (d = 0.20) and more than hint-users (d = -0.31 for hints vs direct). Self-reported usage was validated against chatlogs: 83.8% of self-reported non-users did not message the AI at all.

> "Furthermore, participants who used the AI to get answers directly had a higher skip rate (mean 0.13, s.d. 0.30) than participants in the control group (mean 0.07, s.d. 0.23; t(464) = 2.14, P= 0.033; Cohen's d= 0.20, 95% CI [0.02, 0.39 ])"

### Kuhn 2026

Kuhn, J., Gerjets, P., Trautwein, U., Greene, J. A., Malone, S., Vogt, P., & Fütterer, T. (2026). AI-Augmented Inquiry and Regulation in Hybrid Systems: A Control Allocation Architecture for Preserving Epistemic Agency in Hybrid Human-AI Cognition. https://osf.io/preprints

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports, citing Liu et al. (2026), a randomized controlled trial in which usage patterns diverged: "participants with AI access who used it primarily to obtain direct solutions (61%) showed declining independent performance relative to their own pretest." The groups did not differ at pretest. No effect size is printed in this article.

> "participants with AI access who used it primarily to obtain direct solutions (61%) showed declining independent performance relative to their own pretest, whereas those who used it for hints and clarification (27%) showed improved performance."
