## Evidence

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). Using Categorization of Problems as an Instructional Tool to Help Introductory Students Learn Physics. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.7.020110

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Results-section finding from the categorization study: surface-feature categories such as ramps and pulleys appeared among introductory students' categorizations but not among those of faculty or Ph.D. students. No effect size is printed.

> "There were some poor categories such as “ramps” and “pulleys” that were made by introductory physics students but not by physics faculty or Ph.D. students."

### Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh 2013 (2)

Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). Using Categorization of Problems as an Instructional Tool to Help Introductory Students Learn Physics. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.7.020110

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Results-section finding: some introductory students formed categories named for requested quantities such as speed, explaining only that the question asked for the speed; the article states these explanations were not adequate and that Ph.D. students were less likely to create such categories.

> "some introductory physics students created the categories such as speed and kinetic energy if the question explicitly asked them to calculate those physical quantities."
