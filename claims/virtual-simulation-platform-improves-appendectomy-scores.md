# Virtual simulation platform practice improves medical undergraduates' scores on a virtual laparoscopic appendectomy task

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who used the virtual simulation platform scored significantly higher on the virtual simulation of laparoscopic appendectomy than traditionally taught students. [→ Liu Jie 2025](#liu-jie-2025)

## Evidence

### Liu Jie 2025

Liu Jie, Hu Tingting, Xu Ke, Cheng Zihui, Bu Wenjie, Lin Aiqin. (2025). Does a virtual simulation teaching platform improve surgical training for medical undergraduate students? BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-025-03029-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison on the virtual laparoscopic appendectomy task, scored by two experienced surgical instructors on instrument-handling accuracy, efficiency, tissue damage, and overall performance. Experimental group scored "74.06 ± 8.34 points" versus 71.18 ± 6.52 (P = 0.031; t = 2.186). Both groups received the same traditional training for this task. No effect size printed.

> "Students in the experimental group also obtained significantly higher scores on the virtual simulation of laparoscopic appendectomy (74.06 ± 8.34 points) than did those in the control group (71.18 ± 6.52 points) (P = 0.031)."
