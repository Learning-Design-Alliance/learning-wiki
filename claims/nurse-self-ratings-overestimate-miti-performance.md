## Evidence

### Östlund Ann-Sofi 2015

Östlund Ann-Sofi, Kristofferzon Marja-Leena, Häggström Elisabeth, Wadensten Barbro. (2015). Primary care nurses’ performance in motivational interviewing: a quantitative descriptive study. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-015-0304-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of post-session self-ratings on a 5-point Likert scale with coded MITI global scores for the twelve nurses' sessions. The study found nurses were "showing great overestimation of their performance compared with their MITI scores on four dimensions"; no effect size is reported.

> "They self-rated high on all five dimensions, showing great overestimation of their performance compared with their MITI scores on four dimensions, whereas their self-ratings on Direction were consistent with their scores."

### Östlund Ann-Sofi 2015 (2)

Östlund Ann-Sofi, Kristofferzon Marja-Leena, Häggström Elisabeth, Wadensten Barbro. (2015). Primary care nurses’ performance in motivational interviewing: a quantitative descriptive study. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-015-0304-z

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of nurses' questionnaire self-ratings of behavior proportions with MITI behavior counts from their coded sessions. The study reports nurses "overestimated themselves most on use of Open questions" and underestimated complex reflections; no effect size is reported.

> "they reported using more Simple reflection than Complex, more Open questions than Closed and more MI adherent than Non-adherent behavior than was indicated by their coded sessions. In relation to their actual counts, they overestimated themselves most on use of Open questions and underestimated themselves most on use of Complex reflections."
