# Two versions of a retrieval-practice intervention left students with equal declarative metacognitive knowledge about retrieval practice, which declined over three weeks regardless of version

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Declarative metacognitive knowledge (a 1–7 agreement rating that retrieval practice beats rereading) did not differ between the two randomly assigned versions at T1 (d = 0.16) or T2; the only detectable effect was a decline over time (η 2 p = .045). It was first measured after the intervention and there was no untreated group, so no gain from either version is shown. [→ Lisi Wang 2023](#lisi-wang-2023)

## Evidence

### Lisi Wang 2023

Lisi Wang, Katherine Muenks, Veronica X. Yan. (2023). Interventions to Promote Retrieval Practice: Strategy Knowledge Predicts Intent, but Perceived Cost Predicts Usage. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000813

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Mixed ANOVA on declarative metacognitive knowledge (belief that retrieval practice beats rereading) across both sessions, in a randomised comparison of two intervention versions measured immediately (T1) and three weeks (T2) after the intervention; there was no pre-intervention measure and no untreated control, so the design compares versions and cannot show either raised knowledge. Both versions taught this content, and knowledge 'decreased over time, regardless of the intervention version'; at T1 the version difference was d = 0.16, not significant.

> "The only statistically detectable effect was the main effect of time point, F (1, 278) = 13.14, p < .001, η 2 p = .045. Participants' declarative metacognitive knowledge decreased over time, regardless of the intervention version they received"
