# Learners use navigational help less when a human tutor is accessible, contradicting the expected direction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Activations of the navigational help were significantly lower when an HT tutor was accessible (F=6.490, p=0.011), with no proactivity or interaction effect. [→ Bruno De Lièvre 2006](#bruno-de-lievre-2006)

## Evidence

### Bruno De Lièvre 2006

Bruno De Lièvre, Christian Depover & Pierre Dillenbourg. (2006). The relationship between tutoring mode and learners' use of help tools in distance education. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-005-6076-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on navigational help activations in the four-condition experiment. Means were 0.44 and 0.49 with CT access versus 0.35 and 0.21 with HT access; values were "signiﬁcantly lower where there is an HT tutor," contradicting the original hypothesis.

> "The analysis of variance (Table 15) conﬁrms that the values are signiﬁcantly lower where there is an HT tutor. This contradicts our original hypothesis."
