## Evidence

### Yeates Peter 2017

Yeates Peter, Woolf Katherine, Benbow Emyr, Davies Ben, Boohan Mairhead, Eva Kevin. (2017). A randomised trial of the influence of racial stereotype bias on examiners' scores, feedback and recollections in undergraduate clinical exams. BMC Medicine 15:179. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0943-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA in the randomised trial compared response times for stereotype vs neutral words among 159 completing examiners. Stereotype words were responded to faster (716 ms vs 769 ms, "F = 220.4, p < 0.001"). No effect size was printed.

> "Examiners' response times to stereotype words (mean = 716 ms (95% CI 702–731 ms)) were faster than their response times to neutral words (769 ms (753–786 ms), F = 220.4, p < 0.001)."

### Yeates Peter 2017 (2)

Yeates Peter, Woolf Katherine, Benbow Emyr, Davies Ben, Boohan Mairhead, Eva Kevin. (2017). A randomised trial of the influence of racial stereotype bias on examiners' scores, feedback and recollections in undergraduate clinical exams. BMC Medicine 15:179. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0943-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Between-group comparison in the same ANOVA found no difference between the stereotype-consistent group (750 ms) and stereotype-inconsistent group (735 ms), "p = 0.32", and the word type x group interaction was non-significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "No difference was observed, however, between groups in their response times: Group A, 750 ms (729–772) vs Group B, 735 ms (714–756 ms), p = 0.32, and the interaction of word type x group was also non-significant (p = 0.55)."
