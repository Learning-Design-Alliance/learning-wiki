## Evidence

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2021

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2021). Inequity exemplified by underrepresented minority students receiving lower grades and having higher rates of attrition across STEM disciplines. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2021.1900623](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2021.1900623)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis of institutional records; yearly mean GPA and STEM GPA plotted by racial/ethnic group in Fig. 9. The article reports URM degree-earners earn GPAs "roughly 0.3 grade points lower on average than non-URM students who earn a degree" in the first STEM cluster.

> "In particular, among chemistry, computer science, engineering, mathematics, and physics and astronomy majors, URM students who earn a degree have a GPA (Fig. 9a) and STEM GPA (Fig. 9b) roughly 0.3 grade points lower on average than non-URM students who earn a degree."

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2021 (2)

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2021). Inequity exemplified by underrepresented minority students receiving lower grades and having higher rates of attrition across STEM disciplines. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2021.1900623](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2021.1900623)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same institutional-data analysis, second STEM major cluster (Figs. 9c and 9d). The article reports URM students have "roughly 0.2-0.3 grade points lower" GPA than non-URM students among both degree-earners and droppers; no significance test is reported.

> "There is a similar trend among biological sciences, economics, geology, neuroscience, and statistics majors (Figs. 9c and 9d), with URM students having roughly 0.2-0.3 grade points lower than non-URM students, among those students who earn a degree in those majors and those who drop these majors."
