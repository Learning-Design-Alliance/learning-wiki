Evidence¶

Zou Ya 2026¶

Zou Ya, Xie Wenzhao, Huang Hui, Liu Dan, Zhou Qing, Yi Qifeng, Tong Xiaoliang, Mao Ping. (2026). The effects of case-based, problem-based, and team-based learning on nursing students’ problem-solving, self-directed learning, and communication skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08602-3

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r3

Subgroup meta-analysis by intervention duration for communication skills (Table 6). The ≤5-week subgroup is significant while the >5-week subgroup is not; the authors suggest short-term concentrated interventions establish stable behavioral patterns through repeated reinforcement.

"significant intervention effects were observed in the ≤ 5 five week group (SMD = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.37–1.71, I2 = 90%, P = 0.002) but not in the > 5 five week group (SMD = 2.87, 95% CI: -0.09-5.83, I2 = 99%, P = 0.06)"

Zou Ya 2026 (2)¶

Zou Ya, Xie Wenzhao, Huang Hui, Liu Dan, Zhou Qing, Yi Qifeng, Tong Xiaoliang, Mao Ping. (2026). The effects of case-based, problem-based, and team-based learning on nursing students’ problem-solving, self-directed learning, and communication skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08602-3

q3 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r3

Grade-level subgroup analysis for communication skills (Table 6): only second-year students improved significantly, with low heterogeneity ("I2 = 0%"). The authors attribute this to second-year students transitioning from theory to clinical practice.

"The results revealed that only the second-year students had significant improvement in their communication skills (SMD = 1.54, 95% CI: 1.27–1.80, I2 = 0%, P<0.001)"
