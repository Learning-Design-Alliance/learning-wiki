# Aggregated state emotions converge substantially but imperfectly with self-reported trait emotions, with averaged correlations ranging from r = 0.391 (shame) to r = 0.608 (boredom)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Across three waves, person-aggregated state emotions correlated substantially but well below perfectly with self-reported trait emotions for all six discrete emotions, with shame lowest (r = 0.391) and boredom highest (r = 0.608). [→ Keller 2025](#keller-2025)

## Evidence

### Keller 2025

Keller, M. M., Yanagida, T., Lüdtke, O., & Goetz, T. (2025). How Similar Are Students' Aggregated State Emotions to Their Self-Reported Trait Emotions? Results from a Measurement Burst Design Across Three School Years. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09995-1

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal measurement burst study of N = 161 students followed from grade 9 to 11; bivariate person-level correlations of aggregated states with traits per emotion and wave, Fisher-z averaged across waves. The study reports "for boredom r = 0.608" and shame r = 0.391, all correlations statistically significant at p = 0.05.

> "The across-waves averaged correlations between aggregated states and self-reported traits for enjoyment were r = 0.424, for anger r = 0.488, for pride r = 0.520, for anxiety r = 0.535, for shame r = 0.391, and for boredom r = 0.608."
