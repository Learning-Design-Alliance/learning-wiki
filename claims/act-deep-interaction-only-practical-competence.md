# Deep learning engagement shows little compensatory benefit by prior achievement: the ACT-by-DEEP interaction is significant and negative only for practical competence gains

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The ACT Composite by DEEP Learning interaction was non-significant for CAAP-CT, general education, and personal and social development, but significant and negative for practical competence gains. [→ Reason 2010](#reason-2010)
`q2 i?` Graphed predicted outcomes suggest students at all prior achievement levels receive roughly comparable benefits from deep learning activities. [→ Reason 2010 (2)](#reason-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Reason 2010

Reason, R. D., Cox, B. E., McIntosh, K., & Terenzini, P. T. (2010). Deep learning as an individual, conditional, and contextual influence on first-year student outcomes. Paper presented at the Annual Forum of the Association for Institutional Research, Chicago, IL. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED520486

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the HLM models for self-reported gains, the ACT x DEEP coefficient was -0.010 (p < .01) for practical competence and non-significant for the other three outcomes, a conditional effect in one measure only.

> "However, it was a significant, and negative, predictor of students’ reported gains in Practical Competence. That is, the positive influence of DEEP activities decreased as students’ ACT Composite scores increased."

### Reason 2010 (2)

Reason, R. D., Cox, B. E., McIntosh, K., & Terenzini, P. T. (2010). Deep learning as an individual, conditional, and contextual influence on first-year student outcomes. Paper presented at the Annual Forum of the Association for Institutional Research, Chicago, IL. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED520486

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figures 2 and 3 plot predicted outcomes by DEEP score for ACT Composite scores of 16 through 32; slopes are largely parallel, and for practical competence "the differences in slopes is subtle and of questionable practical significance."

> "Moreover, and more specifically addressing the conditional effects of deep processing, all three graphs suggest that students with all levels of prior achievement receive roughly comparable benefits from engagement in deep learning activities."
