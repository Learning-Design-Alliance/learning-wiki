# Hearing children significantly outscored the deaf group as a whole on Spatial Span Backward and Odd One Out

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The hearing group significantly outscored the deaf group on Spatial Span Backward and Odd One Out, but not on Spatial Span Forward. [→ Marshall C 2015](#marshall-c-2015)

## Evidence

### Marshall C 2015

Marshall C, Jones A, Denmark T, Mason K, Atkinson J, Botting N and Morgan G (2015) Deaf children's non-verbal working memory is impacted by their language experience. Front. Psychol. 6:527. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00527

`q3 · i?`

Independent samples t-tests compared the whole deaf group (N = 27) with hearing children (N = 28) on working memory tasks; groups did not differ on age or WASI matrix reasoning, so no covariates were applied. Hearing children scored higher on the two executive-loaded measures.

> "the hearing group signiﬁcantly outscored the deaf group on two measures: Spatial Span Backward, t(53) = 2.345, p = 0.023, and Odd One Out, t(53) = 2.650, p = 0.011. There were no group diﬀerences on the Spatial Span Forward task, t(53) = 1.231, p = 0.224."
