## Evidence

### Meng Jing 2025

Meng Jing, Wang Li, Jin Danni, Li Zihan, Tao Qingqing, Ying Lan. (2025). Situational simulation teaching can effectively enhance the clinical thinking ability of residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08248-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 121 second-year residents (intervention n = 57, control n = 64) rotating in emergency medicine, May-August 2025. Post-course total scores were 91.89 ± 3.00 versus 80.30 ± 4.07; the article reports "significantly higher total clinical thinking scores and scores in all dimensions" (P < 0.05).

> "After the course, the intervention group showed significantly higher total clinical thinking scores and scores in all dimensions compared to the control group, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05), as shown in Table 5."

### Meng Jing 2025 (2)

Meng Jing, Wang Li, Jin Danni, Li Zihan, Tao Qingqing, Ying Lan. (2025). Situational simulation teaching can effectively enhance the clinical thinking ability of residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08248-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group pre-post paired comparisons in the same RCT. Both groups improved significantly overall, but the control group showed "no significant difference between pre- and post-assessment" on the ethics, humanities, and professional behavior domain; no effect size is printed for this null.

> "However, in the dimension of ethics, humanities, and professional behavior, there was no significant difference between pre- and post-assessment in the control group (P > 0.05)."
