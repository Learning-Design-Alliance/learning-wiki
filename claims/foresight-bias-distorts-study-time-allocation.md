# The foresight bias has behavioral consequences: learners allocate similar study time to backward and forward pairs despite markedly lower recall for backward pairs

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the first presentation, study time did not differ between backward and forward pairs, although recall for backward pairs was markedly inferior. [→ Koriat 2006](#koriat-2006)
`q3 i?` With practice, participants shifted relatively more study time to the backward pairs. [→ Koriat 2006 (2)](#koriat-2006-2)

## Evidence

### Koriat 2006

Koriat, A., & Bjork, R. A. (2006). Illusions of competence during study can be remedied by manipulations that enhance learners' sensitivity to retrieval conditions at test. Memory & Cognition, 34(5), 959-972. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193244

`q3 · i?`

Experiment 5, 24 English-speaking undergraduates in a self-paced paired-associates task with no JOLs solicited; the pair type  measure ANOVA interaction was significant [F(2,46)  57.43, p  .0001], and "there was no difference between the amount of time spent studying the backward and the forward pairs".

> "A t test confirmed that less time was spent studying the backward pairs than studying the unrelated pairs [t(23)  6.08, p  .0001] but that there was no difference between the amount of time spent studying the backward and the forward pairs [t(23)  0.30, n.s.]."

### Koriat 2006 (2)

Koriat, A., & Bjork, R. A. (2006). Illusions of competence during study can be remedied by manipulations that enhance learners' sensitivity to retrieval conditions at test. Memory & Cognition, 34(5), 959-972. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03193244

`q3 · i?`

Experiment 5, first two presentations: the presentation  pair type interaction for backward versus forward pairs was significant [F(1,23)  8.71, p  .01], indicating "a shift in study time allocation" toward backward pairs with practice.

> "These results suggest a shift in study time allocation from the first to the second presentation in the direction of increased emphasis on the backward pairs,"
