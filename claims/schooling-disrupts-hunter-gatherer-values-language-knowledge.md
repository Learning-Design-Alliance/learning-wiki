## Evidence

### Ninkova 2025

Ninkova, V., Hays, J., Lavi, N., Ali, A., Lopes da Silva Macedo, S., Davis, H. E., & Lew-Levy, S. (2025). Hunter-Gatherer Children at School: A View From the Global South. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241255614

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's disruption-of-values synthesis reports that "hierarchy and competitiveness define children's daily lives in schools," that coercive obedience is central to schooling, and that games and adapted behaviour display assimilation of these new values, diminishing the value placed on personal autonomy. Narrative synthesis; no effect sizes.

> "In contrast to the cultural value of egalitarianism, hierarchy and competitiveness define children's daily lives in schools"

### Ninkova 2025 (2)

Ninkova, V., Hays, J., Lavi, N., Ali, A., Lopes da Silva Macedo, S., Davis, H. E., & Lew-Levy, S. (2025). Hunter-Gatherer Children at School: A View From the Global South. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543241255614

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes to Mafela (2009) that formal education plays a central role in San language erosion, "with only 10% of the San now speaking their mother tongues." It also reports parental concern that prolonged school separation limits traditional knowledge acquisition, citing Khwe and Hadza cases.

> "In a study of language shift among San speakers in Botswana, Mafela (2009) argues that formal education has a central role in the process of San language erosion, with only 10% of the San now speaking their mother tongues"
