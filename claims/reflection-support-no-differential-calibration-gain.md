# The level of reflection support (practice-only, grade comparison, or reflection prompts) did not differentially affect the improvement of secondary students' calibration accuracy over time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No statistically detectable interaction effects between intervention group and time on calibration accuracy; the hypothesis that support level would influence improvement was not supported. [→ Nederhand 2020](#nederhand-2020)

## Evidence

### Nederhand 2020

Nederhand, M. L., Tabbers, H. K., Jongerling, J., & Rikers, R. M. J. P. (2020). Reflection on exam grades to improve calibration of secondary school students: a longitudinal study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09233-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of the year-long quasi-experiment comparing Practice-only, Grade comparison, and Reflection conditions (classes randomly assigned within grade years). The article reports no statistically detectable group × time interactions on calibration accuracy, "all t’s<2".

> "Consequently, when adding the intervention groups to the model, there were no statistically detectable interaction effects between any of the groups and time, all t’s<2 (see Table 3). This means that our first hypothesis— the level of support would influence the improvement of calibration accuracy over time — was not supported."
