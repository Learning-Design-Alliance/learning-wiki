## Evidence

### Galuschka Katharina 2014

Galuschka Katharina, Ise Elena, Krick Kathrin, Schulte-Körne Gerd. (2014). Effectiveness of Treatment Approaches for Children and Adolescents with Reading Disabilities: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089900

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis of reading performance by severity of reading disability across randomized controlled trials. The Hedges g effect size for mild disability studies was g = 0.449 versus g = 0.228 for moderate and g = 0.305 for severe; the article states "this difference did not reach statistical significance (p = .188)".

> "The analyses revealed that intervention studies with mild reading disabled children and adolescents report a slightly higher mean effect size (g' = 0.449; 95% CI [0.239, 0.659]; I 2 = 0%) compared with studies that included moderately disabled (g' = 0.228; 95% CI [0.113, 0.342]; I 2 = 31%) or severe reading disabled (g' = 0.305; 95% CI [0.033, 0.576]; I 2 = 0%) study subjects."
