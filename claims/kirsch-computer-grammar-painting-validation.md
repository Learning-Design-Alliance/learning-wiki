# Kirsches' computer re-creations of paintings using artists' design grammars were validated by Diebenkorn

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Russell and Joan Kirsch prepared algorithms based on the design principles and creative strategies of Miro and Diebenkorn, had the computer create new works from those grammars, and the artist Diebenkorn confirmed several computer creations resembled his own recent works. ## Evidence

### McWhinnie 1991

McWhinnie, Harold J. (1991). Chaos Theory in the Arts and Design. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED368622

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The paper describes the Kirsches' computer analysis of paintings by Miro and Diebenkorn (Leonardo Vol 21, No 4, 1988), in which algorithms built on each artist's grammar of design composition generated new configurations; validation was the artist's own confirmation, not a controlled experiment.

> "They tested their results by having the artist Diebenkorn view the solutions. He confirmed that several of the computer's creations resembled recent works of his own."
