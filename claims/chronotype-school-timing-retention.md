# Chronotype predicts grade retention through its interaction with school timing (synchrony effect), not through a main chronotype effect: morning-attending late chronotypes with low math grades show higher retention odds than their evening peers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Grade retention is significantly affected by the interaction between chronotype and school timing (partial η2 = 0.016), but not by the main effect of chronotype (P = 0.081). [→ Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023](#guadalupe-rodriguez-ferrante-2023)
`q3 i1` A student with a 1h-later chronotype and 1-point-lower math grades shows OR = 1.656 (66%) higher retention odds in the morning timing, with ORs not different from 1 in any timing at average or higher math grades. [→ Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (2)](#guadalupe-rodriguez-ferrante-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Logistic regression on n = 407 students (grade retention as binary outcome): "grade retention is signi ﬁcantly affected by the synchrony effect and not by the chronotype effect", synchrony interaction partial η2 = 0.016 (small); the main chronotype effect had P = 0.081 with no printed effect size.

> "we found a signi ﬁcant interaction between chronotype and school timing (synchrony effect, F1405 = 7.260, P = 0.027, partial η2 = 0.016, 90% CI = 0.000–0.040). Consequently, grade retention is signi ﬁcantly affected by the synchrony effect and not by the chronotype effect"

### Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023 (2)

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Odds ratios from the retention model for two students with math grades of 5.15 differing by 1 h in chronotype: "1.656 higher odds (i.e. 66%)" in the morning (OR = 1.656, a small odds-ratio effect); MSFsc ORs were not significantly different from one at average or higher math grades.

> "the student with later chronotype shows 1.656 higher odds (i.e. 66%) of experiencing grade retention when attending the morning school timing (Table 2a). MSFsc OR are not signi ﬁcantly different from one in any school timing for students with average or 1- point-higher math grades"
