## Evidence

### Aldarsouni Fayez G. 2024

Aldarsouni Fayez G., Alkhulaiwi Hani, Alruwaite Hosam, Alsubaie Shara, Abdulkareem Faye, Alsubaie Norah. (2024). Advanced trauma life support training outcomes in Saudi Arabia: a four-year multicenter analysis of influential characteristics and factors (2019-2023). Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2024.394

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Specialty comparison in the retrospective cohort of physicians other than interns (Table 2, Figure 2). General surgeons passed at higher rates; univariate OR for GS success was 2.37 (P=.006), rising to 3.26 in multivariate analysis.

> "Notably, general surgeons had a significantly higher pass rate (27.2% passed vs. 13.6% failed, P=.001), indicating a strong association between general surgery practice and success in the ATLS course"

### Aldarsouni Fayez G. 2024 (2)

Aldarsouni Fayez G., Alkhulaiwi Hani, Alruwaite Hosam, Alsubaie Shara, Abdulkareem Faye, Alsubaie Norah. (2024). Advanced trauma life support training outcomes in Saudi Arabia: a four-year multicenter analysis of influential characteristics and factors (2019-2023). Annals of Saudi Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5144/0256-4947.2024.394

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Specialty comparison in the same cohort (Table 2). OMFS univariate OR for success was 0.18 (P=.0015); trauma-related specialties passed at 75.3% vs. 24.7% for non-trauma specialties (P=.000004).

> "Conversely, oral maxillofacial surgeons (OMFS) had a higher failure rate, with 10.9% of those who failed belonging to this specialty compared to only 2.1% who passed (P=.001)"
