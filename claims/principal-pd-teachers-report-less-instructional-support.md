# Teachers whose principals received the professional development reported receiving less frequent instructional support and feedback than teachers whose principals did not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teachers in treatment schools reported less frequent instructional support and feedback than teachers in control schools, an effect in the direction opposite to the program's intent. [→ Herrmann 2019](#herrmann-2019)

## Evidence

### Herrmann 2019

Herrmann, M., Clark, M., James-Burdumy, S., Tuttle, C., Kautz, T., Knechtel, V., Dotter, D., Smither Wulsin, C., & Deke, J. (2019). The Effects of a Principal Professional Development Program Focused on Instructional Leadership (Study Highlights). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/study-highlights-the-effects-of-a-principal-professional-development-program-focused

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

In the randomized comparison of the 100 elementary schools, teacher survey reports showed treatment-group teachers reported "less frequent instructional support and feedback than teachers whose principals did not receive the professional development." No effect size is printed.

> "In fact, teachers whose principals received the professional development reported receiving less frequent instructional support and feedback than teachers whose principals did not receive the professional development."
