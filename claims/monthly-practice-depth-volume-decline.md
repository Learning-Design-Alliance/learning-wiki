## Evidence

### García-Suárez 2019

García-Suárez, M., Méndez-Martínez, C., Martínez-Isasi, S., Gómez-Salgado, J., & Fernández-García, D. (2019). Basic Life Support Training Methods for Health Science Students: A Systematic Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16050768

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review's summary of Oermann et al. (n = 606 nursing students; control group with no practice, intervention group practising CPR 6 minutes a month with a voice advisory manikin), which evaluated random subgroups of each group at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. The review's table reports no differences in compressions:minute, hand placement or volume:minute, and "differences in depth and volume administered, with decreases in the control group"; its narrative says depth and volume "decreased significantly as the measurements were taken over time". A 12-month subgroup given a refresher showed no difference between groups. The review reports no effect sizes.

> "Among these groups, no differences were established in terms of the number of compressions, volume: minute, or hand placements, but there were in relation to depth and volume administered, which decreased significantly as the measurements were taken over time."
