# Monthly VAM practice did not prevent significant decline in compression depth and ventilated volume over twelve months

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Oermann et al., no differences appeared in compressions:minute, hand placement, or volume:minute across subgroups, but depth and volume administered decreased significantly over successive measurements. [→ García-Suárez 2019](#garcía-suárez-2019)

## Evidence

### García-Suárez 2019

García-Suárez, M., Méndez-Martínez, C., Martínez-Isasi, S., Gómez-Salgado, J., & Fernández-García, D. (2019). Basic Life Support Training Methods for Health Science Students: A Systematic Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16050768

`q2 · i?`

Review's summary of Oermann et al. (n = 606 nursing students), which evaluated random subgroups at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months: depth and volume administered "decreased significantly as the measurements were taken over time"; other measures showed no differences.

> "Among these groups, no differences were established in terms of the number of compressions, volume: minute, or hand placements, but there were in relation to depth and volume administered, which decreased significantly as the measurements were taken over time."
