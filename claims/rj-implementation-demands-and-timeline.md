## Evidence

### Fronius 2019

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of implementation literature states "RJ also requires staff time and buy-in, training, and resources" beyond what suspension imposes, and notes teachers often perform duties outside their job description, such as attending trainings and conducting circles.

> "RJ also requires staff time and buy-in, training, and resources that traditional sanctions such as suspension do not impose on the school."

### Fronius 2019 (2)

Fronius, Darling-Hammond, Persson, Guckenburg, Hurley, & Petrosino. (2019). Restorative Justice in U.S. Schools: An Updated Research Review. WestEd Justice & Prevention Research Center. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED595733

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports cited timelines: attitude shifts of "one to three years" and restorative climate shifts of "up to three to five years," assuming sustained financial support.

> "some researchers suggest that a shift in attitudes toward punishment may take one to three years (Karp & Breslin, 2001), and the deep shift to a restorative-oriented school climate might take up to three to five years (Evans & Lester, 2013)."
