Brian R. Flay 2014¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Student self-report social-emotional outcomes in the Chicago matched-pair cluster-randomized trial at grade 8. Effect sizes are as printed in the article.

"Significant effects were also demonstrated in the Chicago trial for a range of other outcomes, for example, improvement in affiliation with deviant peers (ES=-.45) or positive peers (ES=.46), aggressive problem solving (ES=-.76), empathy ES=.26) and altruism (ES=.21)"

Brian R. Flay 2014 (2)¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q2 · i1 · causal · r2

Internalizing outcomes in the Chicago trial, reported as significant reductions in depression and anxiety and improvements in life satisfaction. No effect size is printed for life satisfaction in the text.

"reductions in depression (ES=-.14) anxiety (ES=-.26) and improvements in life satisfaction (Lewis, DuBois, et al., 2013)"

Brian R. Flay 2014 (3)¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Student self-reported health behaviors in the Chicago trial. The article characterizes the healthy diet/exercise and BMI effects as marginal.

"Significant improvements were also demonstrated in the Chicago trial on student self-reported hygiene (ES=.48) and consumption of unhealthy food (ES=-.19), with marginal effects on healthy diet and exercise (ES=.21), and unhealthy BMI z-scores (ES=-.21)"

Related Claims¶

  • Major Positive Action findings from Hawai'i were replicated in Chicago, though often with smaller effect sizes attributed to disadvantage and lower implementation — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Positive Action mitigated the age-related decline in social-emotional and character development skills in both trials and a third small trial — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The Positive Action program produced more favorable change over time in peer self-esteem among low-income urban youth — related
  • The Positive Action program produced more favorable change over time in school self-esteem among low-income urban youth — related
  • Positive Action improved school quality and climate in both the Hawai'i and Chicago cluster-randomized trials — related
  • Positive Action reduced negative behaviors, including substance use, violence, bullying and disruptive behavior, in both trials — related
