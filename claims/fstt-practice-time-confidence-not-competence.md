## Evidence

### Nim C 2025

Nim C, Smith N, Starmer D, Wang S, Choi G, Alayed A, AlShareef J, Gnjatic A, Sloan K, Wong K, Funabashi M. (2025). Chiropractic students’ characteristics influencing confidence and competence in modulating spinal manipulation force–time characteristics of specific target forces: a secondary analysis of a cross-sectional study. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-025-00577-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable linear regression of confidence in 149 chiropractic students; the article reports time in the FSTT® lab "showed small increases in confidence levels". No effect sizes printed.

> "Age was not strongly associated with confidence, whereas, time spent in the FSTT® lab, showed small increases in confidence levels (Fig. 4, numerical variables)."

### Nim C 2025 (2)

Nim C, Smith N, Starmer D, Wang S, Choi G, Alayed A, AlShareef J, Gnjatic A, Sloan K, Wong K, Funabashi M. (2025). Chiropractic students’ characteristics influencing confidence and competence in modulating spinal manipulation force–time characteristics of specific target forces: a secondary analysis of a cross-sectional study. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-025-00577-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

From the discussion of the same cross-sectional study (mean 58 ± 14 lab hours in the prior 7 months): practice time increased confidence but "did not translate into higher competence". The authors suggest a possible ceiling effect of current FSTT® training methods.

> "while time spent in the FSTT® lab significantly influenced confidence, it did not translate into higher competence."
