## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a Finnish register cohort, birth length had a linear positive association with school grades at 16, explaining 0.08% of grade variation in boys and 0.15% in girls, more than birth weight or ponderal index. [→ Silventoinen Karri 2023](#silventoinen-karri-2023)

## Evidence

### Silventoinen Karri 2023

Silventoinen Karri, Luukkonen Juha, Myrskylä Mikko, Martikainen Pekka. (2023). Birth size, school performance and family social position: a study of 650,000 children. Pediatric Research. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-023-02757-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Population-averaged regression models on Finnish register data linking births 1987–1997 to grade-point average (range 4–10) at 16, unadjusted (Model 1). "BL explained a larger proportion of variation of school performance" than BW or BPI; the linear effect was β = 0.031 (boys) and 0.042 (girls).

> "BL explained a larger proportion of variation of school performance (0.08% in boys and 0.15% in girls) than BW (0.02% and 0.05%, respectively) or BPI (0.07% and 0.04%, respectively)."
