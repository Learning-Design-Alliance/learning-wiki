# Prior knowledge moderated the VF-process advantage over PF: the benefit of observing the process grew with prior knowledge, while prior knowledge did not affect post-test performance in the PF condition

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Condition interacted with prior knowledge on conceptual knowledge (F(2, 166) = 3.13, p = 0.047); the moderation coefficient was b 3 = 0.34, p = 0.011. [→ Christian Hartmann 2021](#christian-hartmann-2021)
`q3 i3` Within conditions, prior knowledge correlated strongly with conceptual post-test performance in VF-process (r = 0.53) but not in PF (r = 0.13, non-significant). [→ Christian Hartmann 2021 (2)](#christian-hartmann-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Christian Hartmann 2021

Christian Hartmann, Tamara van Gog, Nikol Rummel. (2021). Preparatory effects of problem solving versus studying examples prior to instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09528-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression moderation analysis (PROCESS Model 1) following up the significant condition × prior knowledge interaction, F(2, 166) = 3.13, p = 0.047, found "prior knowledge significantly moderated the effect on conceptual knowledge in the PF and the VF-process conditions, b 3 = 0.34, p = 0.011". No standardised effect size was printed for the moderation.

> "prior knowledge significantly moderated the effect on conceptual knowledge in the PF and the VF-process conditions, b 3 = 0.34, p = 0.011"

### Christian Hartmann 2021 (2)

Christian Hartmann, Tamara van Gog, Nikol Rummel. (2021). Preparatory effects of problem solving versus studying examples prior to instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09528-z

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up simple-correlation analyses within conditions: in VF-process prior knowledge correlated with the conceptual post-test at r = 0.53, p < 0.001 (a large printed effect size), whereas in PF the correlation was r = 0.13, p = 0.345 and not significant.

> "While prior knowledge did not affect the post-test performance in the PF condition, r(55) = 0.13, p = 0.345, students in the VF-process condition gained more from observing the process when they had higher prior knowledge, r(56) = 0.53, p < 0.001"
