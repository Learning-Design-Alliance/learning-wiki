# Full removal of SROs from CPS high schools was associated with a statistically significant reduction in high-level behavioral infractions at both the student and school levels

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in schools that fully removed SROs had a probability of a high-level infraction 2.2 percentage points lower than expected in 2022-23, a statistically significant difference-in-difference estimate. [→ Arneson 2024](#arneson-2024)
`q2 i?` Schools that fully removed SROs saw a typical increase of only 13 high-level infractions over the period, 42 fewer than expected, a statistically significant difference. [→ Arneson 2024 (2)](#arneson-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Arneson 2024

Arneson, A., Hinze-Pifer, R., Franklin, K., & Johnson, D.W. (2024). Removing police officers from Chicago schools: Trends and outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/removing-police-officers-from-Chicago-schools

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Difference-in-difference analysis of CPS administrative data comparing 2018-19 to 2022-23, controlling for observed student and school characteristics. The student-level estimate was "lower than that expectation by 2.2 percentage points" and statistically significant; the 1.3 percentage point estimate for partial removal was not.

> "the actual rate in 2022–23 for students in schools that fully removed SROs is lower than that expectation by 2.2 percentage points. This is the difference-in-difference estimate, and it is statistically significant."

### Arneson 2024 (2)

Arneson, A., Hinze-Pifer, R., Franklin, K., & Johnson, D.W. (2024). Removing police officers from Chicago schools: Trends and outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/removing-police-officers-from-Chicago-schools

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

School-level Poisson count analysis of the 72 high schools with two SROs at baseline. Schools retaining both SROs rose from 118 to 173 high-level infractions; full-removal schools rose "from 116 in 2018–19 to 129 in 2022–23", a significant difference-in-difference. Partial removal showed about 14 fewer than predicted, not significant.

> "the typical increase over time was just 13 (from 116 in 2018–19 to 129 in 2022–23) which is 42 high-level infractions lower than the expectation if there was no effect of SRO removal and is statistically significant."
