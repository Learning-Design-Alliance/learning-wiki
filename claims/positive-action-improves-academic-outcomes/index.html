Evidence¶

Brian R. Flay 2014¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

School-level standardized test score archival data in the Hawai'i matched-pair cluster-randomized trial, analyzed with growth-curve models. The article reports these significant effects for both reading and math on both test families.

"Growth-curve models demonstrated significant effects for both reading and math on both the HCPS (ESs of .65 and 1.1, respectively) and the Terra Nova (ES of .54 and .52, respectively)"

Brian R. Flay 2014 (2)¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q2 · i3 · causal · r2

School-level academic outcomes in the Chicago trial were weaker than in Hawai'i, but subgroup patterns emerged. Gender or ethnicity did not moderate the math effects; the reading subgroup effects listed are as printed.

"Although the overall effect (for grades 3-8 combined) on reading was not significant (ES=.22), there were marginal effects for boys (ES=.33), large and significant effects for cohort students transitioning between grades 7 and 8 (that is, the value-added score, ES=.83) and for African American students (ES=.50), especially African American boys (ES=1.50)"

Brian R. Flay 2014 (3)¶

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

q2 · i1 · causal · r2

Student-level academic measures in the Chicago trial at grade 8. The article reports these as significant program effects on self-report and teacher-rating outcomes.

"In Chicago, significant program effects were also observed for student self-reports of disaffection with learning (ES=-.19) and teacher ratings of student academic motivation (ES=.39) and performance (ES=.19)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Major Positive Action findings from Hawai'i were replicated in Chicago, though often with smaller effect sizes attributed to disadvantage and lower implementation — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Positive Action mitigated the age-related decline in social-emotional and character development skills in both trials and a third small trial — related
  • In the Hawaii randomized trial, Positive Action showed no statistically significant effects on state standardized test scores in reading and math — reports the opposite
  • Positive Action improved school quality and climate in both the Hawai'i and Chicago cluster-randomized trials — related
