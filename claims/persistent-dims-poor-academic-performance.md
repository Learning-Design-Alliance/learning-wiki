# Persistent sleep problems (DIMS at both ages 7–9 and 11–13) increase the risk of poor academic performance even after adjusting for mental health, gender, pubertal status and SES

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children with persistent DIMS had elevated odds of poor teacher-rated academic performance in the fully adjusted model (OR 1.87, 95% CI 1.19–2.95). [→ Stormark KM 2019](#stormark-km-2019)

## Evidence

### Stormark KM 2019

Stormark KM, Fosse HE, Pallesen S, Hysing M (2019) The association between sleep problems and academic performance in primary school-aged children: Findings from a Norwegian longitudinal population-based study. PLoS ONE 14(11): e0224139. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224139

`q3 · i?`

Logistic regression in the Bergen Child Study longitudinal cohort (N = 3453) comparing DIMS groups against the No DIMS reference for teacher-rated poor academic performance at T2. The fully adjusted model retained a significant association, with "OR 1.87; 95% CI 1.19–2.95".

> "Finally, persistent DIMS was a significant predictor for low academic performance, both in the crude (OR 3.09; 95% CI 2.03–4.70) as well as in the fully adjusted model (OR 1.87; 95% CI 1.19–2.95)."
