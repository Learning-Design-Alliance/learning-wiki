# Long-term retention after five months depends on an interaction between midterm format and students' entry scientific reasoning skills

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Five months after the course, retention related positively to reasoning ability for non-cumulative-midterm students but was unrelated to reasoning ability for cumulative-midterm students. [→ Bailey Elizabeth G. 2021](#bailey-elizabeth-g-2021)

## Evidence

### Bailey Elizabeth G. 2021

Bailey Elizabeth G., Greenall Rebeka F., Tullis Madeleine M., Williams Kurt R. (2021). The retention benefits of cumulative versus non-cumulative midterms in introductory biology may depend on students’ reasoning skills. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250143

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple linear regression targeting retention exam scores with midterm format, final exam score, LCTSR reasoning score, and a format-by-reasoning interaction; the interaction was retained in the best model (Table 2) and shown with raw data in Fig 4B.

> "For students who took non-cumulative exams (Fig 4B, green), there was a strong relationship between reasoning ability at the beginning of the semester and how much they retained five months after the final exam. However, there was no relationship between reasoning ability and retention for students who took cumulative exams (blue)."
