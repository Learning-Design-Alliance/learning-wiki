# Typically-developing children show the standard affective priming pattern, responding faster to arithmetic targets after positive than negative primes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the control group, response latencies were significantly shorter after positive than negative affective primes for all four arithmetic operations. [→ Rubinsten O 2010](#rubinsten-o-2010)

## Evidence

### Rubinsten O 2010

Rubinsten O, Tannock R. (2010). Mathematics anxiety in children with developmental dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions 6:46. http://www.behavioralandbrainfunctions.com/content/6/1/46

`q3 · i?`

Same priming experiment, control group planned comparisons. Positive primes facilitated targets for addition (F(1,10) = 47.1, p < 0.001), multiplication (F(1,10) = 54.23, p < 0.001), subtraction (F(1,10) = 799.9, p < 0.001) and division (F(1,10) = 905, p < 0.001), so "positive affective primes tend to act as affectively related to simple arithmetic problems".

> "a significantly shorter response latencies for positive as compared to negative affective primes was found in the control group (i.e., positive affective primes tend to act as affectively related to simple arithmetic problems) when targets were addition problems, F(1,10) = 47.1, p < 0.001"
