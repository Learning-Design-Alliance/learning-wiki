## Evidence

### Madden J. 2020

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

After completing the post-test, participants tried all three conditions and chose a favorite; 78% chose VR, 12% desktop, and 10% hands-on. Common reasons were that VR was "easier to visualize", "more realistic", "more immersive", and "the most accurate".

> "Consistent with previous work [3] participants overwhelmingly (Chi2 = 152, p < 0.00001) preferred the VR condition as their favorite learning method independent of their study condition (Fig 5)."

### J. H. Madden 2019

J. H. Madden, A. S. Won, J. P. Schuldt, B. Kim, S. Pandita, Y. Sun, T. J. Stone, and N. G. Holmes. (2019). Virtual Reality as a Teaching Tool for Moon Phases and Beyond. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preference survey administered after participants viewed all three conditions in the randomized study. The article reports 78% preferred VR (Fig. 3 shows 78.2% VR, 11.8% desktop, 10.0% hands-on); participants who preferred VR generally found that seeing the full picture helped build their mental model.

> "Despite these lack of differences between learning, we found that 78% of participants preferred the VR activity (Fig. 3) with no dependence on assigned condition."
