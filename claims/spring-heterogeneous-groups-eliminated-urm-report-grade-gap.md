## Evidence

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Spring-semester mixed-model results for lab report grades show a formation-by-race interaction: under random assignment URM students averaged "m = 33.22" versus "m = 33.82" for non-URM students. Only descriptive means were printed; no effect size.

> "The retention of the interaction term in the best-fitting model revealed that the lab report grades for URM students (m = 33.22) were lower than non-URM students (m = 33.82) in the randomly-assigned groups only."

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025 (2)

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same spring model, heterogeneous groups showed URM lab report grades of "m = 33.75" versus "m = 33.74" for non-URM students, which the authors describe as having "removed performance disparity". Descriptive means only; no effect size printed.

> "In the heterogenous groups, URM students' lab report grades were improved (m = 33.75) whereas non-URM students' lab report grades were unchanged (m = 33.74), which removed performance disparity."
