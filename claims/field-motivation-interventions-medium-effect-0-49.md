## Evidence

### Linnenbrink-Garcia 2026

Linnenbrink-Garcia, L., Wigfield, A., Cabrera, L., Mouzaoir, S., McKinney, D., Liu, P. P., Marchand, G., Schmidt, J. A., Harris, C. J., & Guthrie, J. (2026). The Case for Multifaceted, Integrative Motivation Interventions Embedded in Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10177-w

`q4 · i2` · `theoretical · r3`

Meta-analysis by Lazowski and Hulleman of 74 published and unpublished motivation-theory intervention studies in educational (non-laboratory) contexts, as reported by this review. It produced "a medium overall effect size (0.49)" across outcomes, with significant heterogeneity.

> "They found that these field-based motivation interventions were generally successful, producing on average a medium overall effect size (0.49) across a variety of outcomes tested."

### Linnenbrink-Garcia 2026 (2)

Linnenbrink-Garcia, L., Wigfield, A., Cabrera, L., Mouzaoir, S., McKinney, D., Liu, P. P., Marchand, G., Schmidt, J. A., Harris, C. J., & Guthrie, J. (2026). The Case for Multifaceted, Integrative Motivation Interventions Embedded in Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10177-w

`q4 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

In the same meta-analysis of field-based motivation interventions, no other moderators (theoretical framework, grade level, dependent variable type) explained the variation; the design-type contrast above was the only significant moderator reported.

> "the only significant moderator identified was the type of experimental design, with quasi-experimental designs yielding larger effects than randomized designs."

### Linnenbrink-Garcia 2026 (3)

Linnenbrink-Garcia, L., Wigfield, A., Cabrera, L., Mouzaoir, S., McKinney, D., Liu, P. P., Marchand, G., Schmidt, J. A., Harris, C. J., & Guthrie, J. (2026). The Case for Multifaceted, Integrative Motivation Interventions Embedded in Classrooms. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10177-w

`q4 · i2` · `theoretical · r3`

In the same meta-analysis, the "multiple theoretical frameworks" code applied to 23 of 92 effect sizes averaged an effect size of 0.41, a medium magnitude. The review authors note that very few of those studies were theoretically integrated in the way they advocate.

> "The average effect size for this sub-type of studies was 0.41, suggesting a similar effect size to other types of interventions tested."
