# Test strategies and item practice are not effective mechanisms for raising ACT scores; improvements are smaller the more class time goes to test preparation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` PLAN-to-ACT improvements are smaller the more time teachers spend on test preparation and the more they use test preparation materials. [→ Elaine Allensworth 2008](#elaine-allensworth-2008)

## Evidence

### Elaine Allensworth 2008

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey- and score-based analysis of eleventh graders and their teachers finds a negative association between class time spent on test preparation and PLAN-to-ACT improvement; the report finds no evidence that testing strategies raise scores.

> "improvements from the PLAN to the ACT are smaller the more time teachers spend on test preparation in their classes and the more they use test preparation materials."

### Elaine Allensworth 2008 (2)

Elaine Allensworth, Macarena Correa, Steve Ponisciak. (2008). From High School to the Future: ACT Preparation–Too Much, Too Late. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-act-preparation-too-much-too-late

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's summary of its practice-effect analyses: benefits exist for timed practice and English practice but are modest and fade after initial familiarity; no benefit was found for practicing test questions outside full timed tests.

> "While we find there is some benefit to timed test practice and practice on the English subject test, the effects of practice are modest and diminish once students have initial familiarity with the test."
