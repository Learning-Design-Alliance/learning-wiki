# Physiotherapy students who learned OSCE-based medical communication techniques scored significantly higher on total MC skills than both untrained students and practicing physiotherapists

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The learned group scored significantly higher than the unlearned group on total MC skills (median 28 vs 24, d = 2.325, p < 0.001). [→ Yanase Yukiko 2026](#yanase-yukiko-2026)
`q3 i3` The learned group also scored significantly higher than the PT group on total MC skills (median 28 vs 26, d = 1.264, p = 0.001). [→ Yanase Yukiko 2026](#yanase-yukiko-2026)

## Evidence

### Yanase Yukiko 2026

Yanase Yukiko, Hiragi Saki, Kawahara Yasuhiro. (2026). Effects of generic medical communication training on physiotherapy students’ ability to communicate in movement-based clinical scenarios: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08560-2

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional comparison of three groups (learned students n = 15, unlearned students n = 15, physiotherapists n = 15) using Kruskal–Wallis with Steel post-hoc tests on OSCE-based MC skill scores from video-recorded standardized-patient tasks. The learned group's median was 28 versus 24 and 26, with "effect size: d = 2.325" against unlearned students and d = 1.264 against physiotherapists.

> "The learned group (median: 28) scored significantly higher than both the unlearned group (median: 24, p < 0.001, effect size: d = 2.325, power: 1 − β = 0.821) and the PT group (median: 26, p = 0.001, effect size: d = 1.264, power: 1 − β = 0.9029)."
