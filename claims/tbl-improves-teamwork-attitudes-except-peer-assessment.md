# Students' self-rated attitudes toward team-based learning rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after a TBL course, except satisfaction with group member evaluation, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-rated attitudes toward group learning rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after TBL across dimensions including satisfaction with group work, learning quality, clinical reasoning, and professional development (p < 0.001). [→ Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025](#ngoc-lan-duong-thi-2025)
`q2 i?` Satisfaction with group member evaluation did not change significantly from pre- to post-TBL (p = 0.061) and received the lowest satisfaction scores among the five evaluated areas. [→ Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025 (2)](#ngoc-lan-duong-thi-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025

Ngoc Lan Duong Thi, Quynh Anh Nguyen Ngoc, Hoang Thi Phuong Thao. (2025). A pre – post quasi-experimental study of team-based learning effectiveness for Vietnamese nursing students. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323656

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post administration of the 19-item ATL instrument (Cronbach's alpha 0.947) to the same 186 students before and after the TBL course. The results section reports "increased scores in overall satisfaction with group work experience" and related dimensions, all p < 0.001; no effect size is printed.

> "Significant improvements were observed in students’ attitudes towards group learning after the TBL course, with increased scores in overall satisfaction with group work experience, group impact on learning quality, clinical reasoning ability, and professional development (p < 0.001)."

### Ngoc Lan Duong Thi 2025 (2)

Ngoc Lan Duong Thi, Quynh Anh Nguyen Ngoc, Hoang Thi Phuong Thao. (2025). A pre – post quasi-experimental study of team-based learning effectiveness for Vietnamese nursing students. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323656

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null result from the same ATL pre-post comparison in the results section: satisfaction with group member evaluation showed no significant change (p = 0.061). The discussion notes this area "received the lowest satisfaction scores among five evaluated areas"; equivalence was not tested.

> "However, no significant change was noted in satisfaction with group member evaluation (p = 0.061)."
