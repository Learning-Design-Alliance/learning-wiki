# Physics professors emphasize a 'physics approach' and non-content skills while non-physics professors emphasize discipline-relevant physics content as the value of physics for non-majors

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Physics professors framed physics' interdisciplinary value primarily through non-content skills constituting a 'physics approach' (model-building, quantitative reasoning, physical intuition), whereas non-physics professors emphasized specific physics content their disciplines build on. [→ Ian Descamps 2020](#ian-descamps-2020)

## Evidence

### Ian Descamps 2020

Ian Descamps, Thomas Moore, Benjamin Pollard. (2020). What is interdisciplinarity? Views from students and profe ssors in a non-major intro physics course. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2005.05360](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.05360)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of interviews with two physics professors who had recently taught General Physics found they emphasized "the idea of the "physics approach" to science" over content, framing skills like model-building as the foundational element of physics' relationship to other disciplines.

> "More so than content, the physics professors we spoke to emphasized the idea of the "physics approach" to science. They did bring up content as an important component, but this approach seemed to be the underlying, foundational element"

In the conclusions of this interview-based study, the authors report that professors had "nearly exact align - ment with the scientiﬁc skills they described as essential t o physics," naming problem solving, quantitative literacy, model-building, and confidence as shared outcomes.

> "Professors, in particular, had nearly exact align - ment with the scientiﬁc skills they described as essential t o physics."
