## Evidence

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Follower voltage graphing task administered at U1 (N = 57) and U2 (N = 157), paired or independent; Table III reports quantitatively correct rates of 61% (U1) and 25% (U2), with correct reasoning in 58% and 19%.

> "between 25% and 60% of students in a given course were able to produce a graph with the requisite quantitative features (i.e., Vout = Vin – 0.6 V when Vin > 0.6 V, and Vout = 0 V otherwise)."

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018 (2)

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same U1/U2 graphing task; the authors attribute these responses to "overgeneralizing the forward-biased voltage behavior of diodes to the transistor's BE junction, without properly attending to biasing constraints."

> "The most common incorrect response, given by approximately 20% of students, was to depict a linearly increasing output voltage offset from the input voltage by a constant, negative value (typically –0.6 V), as shown in Fig. 6B."

### Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer 2018 (3)

Kevin L. Van De Bogart and MacKenzie R. Stetzer. (2018). Investigating student understanding of bipolar junction transistor circuits. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1806.00404)

`q3 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same graphing task; students overgeneralized follower behavior, treating the circuit as a dc rather than a signal follower; the authors note even incorrect responses usually applied productive transistor ideas.

> "Approximately 10% of responses from U2 indicated that the output would be equal to the input, regardless of the value of Vin."
