## Evidence

### Hodgkiss 2018

Hodgkiss, A., Gilligan, K. A., Tolmie, A. K., Thomas, M. S. C., & Farran, E. K. (2018). Spatial cognition and science achievement: The contribution of intrinsic and extrinsic spatial skills from 7 to 11 years. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 88(4). https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12211

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discipline-level regressions in the same 123-child sample. Biology model: folding ∆F(1,119) = 12.77, p = .001, then scaling ∆F(1,118) = 5.13, p = .025; final model accounted for 47% of biology variance.

> "Mental folding was the only retained predictor of the physics scores. It was entered in step 3, and it accounted for an additional 4% of the variance in physics scores, ∆F(1,119) = 9.78, p = .002"

### Hodgkiss 2018 (2)

Hodgkiss, A., Gilligan, K. A., Tolmie, A. K., Thomas, M. S. C., & Farran, E. K. (2018). Spatial cognition and science achievement: The contribution of intrinsic and extrinsic spatial skills from 7 to 11 years. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 88(4). https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12211

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chemistry regression in the same sample: embedded figures (β = .141) and scaling (β = .147) had similarly sized coefficients; the final model accounted for 48% of chemistry variance.

> "The embedded figures task was retained as a significant spatial predictor of chemistry scores accounting for a further 3% of the variance in chemistry scores, ∆F(1,119) = 6.47, p = .012"
