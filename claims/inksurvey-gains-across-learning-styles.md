## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Contrary to the hypothesis that graphical ink input would favor strongly visual or kinesthetic learners, the data reflected strong learning gains for students of all learning styles. [→ F.V. Kowalski 2013](#fv-kowalski-2013)

## Evidence

### F.V. Kowalski 2013

F.V. Kowalski, Gus Greivel, Todd Ruskell, Tracy Q. Gardner, S.E. Kowalski, Thomas J. Colling, J.V. Gutierrez Cuba, and Enrique Palou. (2013). Using InkSurvey with Pen-Enabled Mobile Devices for Real-Time Formative Assessment II. Indications of Effectiveness in Diverse Educational Environments. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1308.3729)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

FK and SK hypothesized the graphical nature of InkSurvey input might appeal to visual or kinesthetic learners; their data instead showed strong learning gains across all learning styles. The article calls this a surprising result and prints no effect size.

> "However, their data reflects strong learning gains achieved when InkSurvey is used for students of all learning styles [8]."
