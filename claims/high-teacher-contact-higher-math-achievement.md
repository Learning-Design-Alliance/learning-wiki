## Subclaims
`q3 i?` First graders with high teacher–student contact (several times per week or almost every day) during closures had higher math achievement at end of Grade 1 than students under regular schooling (b = 0.16, p = 0.017). [→ Golle 2026](#golle-2026)

## Evidence

### Golle 2026

Golle, J., Lösche, P., Beißert, H., Wieland, A.-K., Rieber, R., Schult, J., Hasselhorn, M., Nagengast, B., & Trautwein, U. (2026). COVID-19-related school closures and students' achievement and motivation: Did teacher support make a difference. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00439-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multiple regression splitting the pandemic condition into three contact subconditions (low, medium, high) with Cohort 1 as reference; all pretest measures controlled. High contact meant several times per week or almost every day. The printed coefficient was b = 0.16 (ES in SDY), 95% CI [0.03, 0.30].

> "For math achievement, we surprisingly found that students with high teacher-student contact proﬁted from learning during the pandemic compared with stu- dents under regular schooling in terms of their math achievement, bmath ¼ 0:16; p ¼ 0:017."
