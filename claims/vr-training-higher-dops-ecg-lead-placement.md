# VR simulation training produces higher DOPS procedural skill scores than real-patient or mannequin training for ECG lead placement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Medical students trained with screen-based VR scored significantly higher on the DOPS test than students trained on real patients or a mannequin (VR 17.5 vs patient 15.2 and mannequin 15.3, P < 0.001). [→ Kermanian Fatemeh 2026](#kermanian-fatemeh-2026)

## Evidence

### Kermanian Fatemeh 2026

Kermanian Fatemeh, Safiyari Parham, Hassanzadeh Gholamreza, Mojtahedzadeh Rita, Mohammadi Aeen. (2026). Boosting electrocardiography leads location training through virtual reality simulation: effects on skill performance and learner satisfaction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09854-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

True experimental study randomizing 90 fourth-year medical students into three equal groups (n=30 each) for ECG lead placement training. ANOVA on DOPS scores showed a significant difference, with the VR group obtaining "the highest score on this test" at 17.5 out of twenty points.

> "The analysis of the DOPS test scores revealed a significant difference between the VR group and the other two groups (patient: 15.2 (1.89), mannequin: 15.3 (1.71) and VR: 17.5 (1.35)) (P value < 0.001)."
