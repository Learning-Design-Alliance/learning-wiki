# Physicists load physical meaning onto symbols and interpret equations differently from mathematicians, in a context-dependent way

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Physicists choose symbols to activate mental associations with physical quantities, and use physical meaning to decide how math should be interpreted, unlike mathematicians. [→ Edward F. Redish 2006](#edward-f-redish-2006)

## Evidence

### Edward F. Redish 2006

Edward F. Redish. (2006). Problem Solving and the Use of Math in Physics Courses. Invited talk, World View on Physics Education in 2005, Delhi. Originally published 2005. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608268](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0608268)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical/observational argument from the author's invited talk, illustrated with the A(x,y)=K(x^2+y^2) problem that physicists answer as Kr^2 while mathematicians insist on K(r^2+θ^2). The article states physicists "use their idea of what physical quantity a symbol represents to decide how the math should be interpreted."

> "In physics, our symbols are not arbitrary but tend to be chosen to activate a particular mental association with some physical quantity or measurement."
