# Learning from an animation is significantly more successful than learning from static pictures when identifying the specific motions of a gear mechanism's links

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In the main study, the Animation group significantly outperformed both the Picture group (d = 2.33) and the Four Pictures group (d = 2.41) on identification of motions. [→ Rolf Ploetzner 2021](#rolf-ploetzner-2021)

## Evidence

### Rolf Ploetzner 2021

Rolf Ploetzner, Sandra Berney, Mireille Bétrancourt. (2021). When learning from animations is more successful than learning from static pictures: learning the specifics of change. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Three-group randomized experiment with 88 undergraduate pre-service teachers; groups watched a single picture, four pictures, or an animation of a six-bar linkage. Post-hoc LSD comparisons on motion identification gave "d = 2.33" versus Picture and "d = 2.41" versus Four Pictures, both p < 0.01; the Picture and Four Pictures groups did not differ (LSD = 0.12, p = 0.606).

> "Concerning the identification of motions, post-hoc analyses further reveal that the group ‘Animation’ significantly differs from the group ‘Picture’ (Fisher's Least Significant Difference LSD = 2.03, p < 0.01, d = 2.33) as well as from the group ‘Four Pictures’ (LSD = 2.16, p < 0.01, d = 2.41)."
