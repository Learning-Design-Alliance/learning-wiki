## Evidence

### Chappell 2015

Chappell, S., Arnold, P., Nunnery, J., & Grant, M. (2015). An Examination of an Online Tutoring Program's Impact on Low-Achieving Middle School Students' Mathematics Achievement. Online Learning, 19(5). https://jolt.merlot.org/2015/vol11no2.htm

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental paired-samples t-test for 49 tutored Grade 6 students in a rural Virginia school, using 2013 and 2014 SOL scores as pretest and posttest. The article reports a "mean improvement of 22.88 points" with t = 5.99, p < .001, and a within-group effect size of d = +0.95.

> "Within-group differences from pre-interven tion to postintervention for School 1 showed Online Learning - Volume 19 Issue 5 - December 2015 43 significant improvement in scores for the group, with a mean improvement of 22.88 points ( t = 5.99, p < .001)."

### Chappell 2015 (2)

Chappell, S., Arnold, P., Nunnery, J., & Grant, M. (2015). An Examination of an Online Tutoring Program's Impact on Low-Achieving Middle School Students' Mathematics Achievement. Online Learning, 19(5). https://jolt.merlot.org/2015/vol11no2.htm

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t-test for 50 tutored Grade 7 and 8 students in a rural Kansas school, using program-administered pretests and posttests. The article reports a "mean gain of 26.16 points" with t = 10.11, p < .001, and a within-group effect size of d = +1.47.

> "students in School 2 did show significant improvement in achievement, with a mean gain of 26.16 points (t = 10.11, p < .001)."
