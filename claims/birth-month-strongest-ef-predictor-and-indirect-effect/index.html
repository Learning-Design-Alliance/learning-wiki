Evidence¶

Rémeau 2026¶

Rémeau, M., Schmitt, P., & Borst, G. (2026). Executive functions as mediators of early educational disparities by SES, gender and birth month. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00422-w

q2 · i? · associational · r2

SEM path estimates from the mediation model on the national sample show all three predictors affected EF scores, with birth month the largest ("β = -.37, p < .001"), followed by SES and gender. The model explained 61% of pre-academic variance.

"Family SES (β = .33, p < .001), gender (β = .10, p < .001) and month birth (β = -.22, p < .001) have an effect on pre-academic scores as well as on EF scores, with respectively β = .18, p < .001 for family SES, β = .12, p < .001 for gender and β = -.37, p < .001 for birth-month."

Rémeau 2026 (2)¶

Rémeau, M., Schmitt, P., & Borst, G. (2026). Executive functions as mediators of early educational disparities by SES, gender and birth month. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00422-w

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Contrast analysis of indirect effects in the same SEM. The birth-month indirect effect beat gender's and differed from SES's, while gender and SES did not significantly differ (β = .03, p = .24).

"the indirect effect of birth month was stronger than that of gender ( β = .27, p < .001), and the indirect effect of family SES also differed from that of month of birth ( β = .30, p < .001), indicating a stronger influence of month of birth on pre-academic abilities."
