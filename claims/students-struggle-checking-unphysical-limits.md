# Students check units more readily than limiting behavior when reflecting on reflection and transmission coefficients

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Three of four interviewed students spontaneously pointed out that a provided transmission coefficient T was not unitless, but only one student noticed the unphysical limits when taking extreme values of the incident angle. [→ Qing X. Ryan 2015](#qing-x-ryan-2015)

## Evidence

### Qing X. Ryan 2015

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews in which students were given incorrect but plausible expressions of R and T in a novel situation and asked to evaluate them without calculation. "Three out of the 4 students interviewed" caught the unitless issue; no effect size printed.

> "Three out of the 4 students interviewed were able to spontaneously point out that the T provided was not unitless (R1). Compared to unit analysis, students had more diﬃculty with checking limits."

### Qing X. Ryan 2015 (2)

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same interview study, limiting-behavior component (R2): students struggled to identify the independent variable (incident angle), with interviewer hints needed for 3 of 4 students. "Only one student noticed the unphysical limits"; no effect size printed.

> "Only one student noticed the unphysical limits. One student obtained T <0 and didn’t note that this is unphysical. Two students talked about taking the lower limit of the index of refraction to 0."
