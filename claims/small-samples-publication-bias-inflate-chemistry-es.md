## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors report that problem-based learning had the widest confidence interval despite a large overall effect, that inquiry-based learning's interval was wider than its effect size would suggest, and that a bias against non-significant findings likely leads meta-analysis to overestimate effect sizes. [→ Sugano 2020](#sugano-2020)

## Evidence

### Sugano 2020

Sugano, S. G. C., & Nabua, E. B. (2020). Meta-Analysis on the Effects of Teaching Methods on Academic Performance in Chemistry. International Journal of Instruction, 13(2), 881-894. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2020.13259a

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Authors' interpretation (discussion) of the forest plot (Figure 1): problem-based learning had "the widest range of interval and yet possessed a large over-all effect size", attributed to small sample sizes, and a negative correlation between sample size and effect sizes is cited (Levine et al, 2009).

> "Obtaining such result most likely stems from a bias against non-significant findings included, it likely results in meta-analysis overestimating effect sizes (Levine et al, 2009)."
