# Adding profession-role exchange to interprofessional community service learning markedly further increases medical and pharmacy students' positive attitudes toward physician-pharmacist collaboration compared with the control IPE activity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with the control IPE activity, the addition of profession-role exchange markedly further increased total SATP2C scores (p < 0.05), with gains of 23.4% (medical) and 19.2% (pharmacy) in the intervention group versus 17.7% and 13.3% in the control group. [→ Wang J 2020](#wang-j-2020)
`q2 i?` Profession-role exchange was associated with increased scores in the Responsibility and accountability and Shared authority subscales of the SATP2C versus control (p < 0.05). [→ Wang J 2020 (2)](#wang-j-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Wang J 2020

Wang J, Guo J, Wang Y, Yan D, Liu J, Zhang Y, Hu X. (2020). Use of profession-role exchange in an interprofessional student team-based community health service-learning experience. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02127-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre/post quasi-experiment with medical and pharmacy students completing the SATP2C before and after the community service IPE activity; the between-group repeated-measures comparison showed the exchange group's further increase, "p < 0.05". Percent gains are reported in the Results text.

> "Importantly, when compared with the control group, the addition of profession-role exchange in the intervention group markedly further increased the total SATP2C scores (p < 0.05)."

### Wang J 2020 (2)

Wang J, Guo J, Wang Y, Yan D, Liu J, Zhang Y, Hu X. (2020). Use of profession-role exchange in an interprofessional student team-based community health service-learning experience. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02127-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subscale analysis of the SATP2C factor structure in the same pre/post study; the article reports the exchange group's higher subscale scores versus control at "p < 0.05, vs. control group". No effect size is printed.

> "participating in the profession-role exchange was associated with the increased scores in “Responsibility and accountability” and “Shared authority” subscales on the SATP2C (p < 0.05, vs. control group)"
