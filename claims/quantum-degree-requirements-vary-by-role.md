## Evidence

### Ciaran Hughes 2022

Ciaran Hughes, Doug Finke, Dan-Adrian German, Celia Merzbacher, Patrick M. Vora, H. J. Lewandowski. (2022). Assessing the Needs of the Quantum Industry. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1109/te.2022.3153841](https://doi.org/10.1109/te.2022.3153841)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Distribution of preferred degrees per job role from the 57-company survey, shown in Fig. 4 and Table I. Masters are needed roughly at the same level for all jobs except highly specialised ones; technicians with associate degrees are expected to be needed increasingly as production grows.

> "Jobs requiring a PhD are the quantum speciﬁc roles that require important quantum skills, e.g., the error cor- rection scientist. If a PhD is needed for the highly speciﬁc quantum jobs, then this places doubt on how many new highly-focused quantum Masters programs are needed"

### Ciaran Hughes 2022 (2)

Ciaran Hughes, Doug Finke, Dan-Adrian German, Celia Merzbacher, Patrick M. Vora, H. J. Lewandowski. (2022). Assessing the Needs of the Quantum Industry. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1109/te.2022.3153841](https://doi.org/10.1109/te.2022.3153841)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors interpret from Table I that a degree may or may not provide skills, but skills and on-the-job experience are what is needed, and suggest universities partner with industries to provide internships.

> "It should be noted that there is considerably more con- sensus among which skills are important as compared to which degrees are important."
