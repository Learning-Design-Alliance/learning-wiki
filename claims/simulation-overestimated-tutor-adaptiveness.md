## Evidence

### Rumble 2026

Rumble, T., Zarrin, J., Lovell, P. G., Falconer, R. (2026). Learning Engagement Assistant (LEA): Cross-Course Scalability and Classroom Evaluation of an Agentic AI Tutoring System. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13370 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.13370](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.13370)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pilot survey Tutor Mode items from the eight-participant CMP511 study. Socratic questioning was endorsed by 7/8, but adaptation when uncertain drew only 3/8 agreement at median 4, which the authors call the most consequential divergence from simulation predictions.

> "The Socratic questioning approach was endorsed by 7/8 participants as helpful for deeper thinking, but only 3/8 agreed thatLEAadapted appropriately when they expressed uncertainty (median 4, Neutral)"

### Rumble 2026 (2)

Rumble, T., Zarrin, J., Lovell, P. G., Falconer, R. (2026). Learning Engagement Assistant (LEA): Cross-Course Scalability and Classroom Evaluation of an Agentic AI Tutoring System. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13370. [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.13370](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.13370)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Reflexive thematic analysis of open-text pilot responses identified Tutor Mode scaffolding as rigid under genuine confusion and motivational affirmations as performative for some postgraduate learners, with one participant copy-pasting LEA's own text to progress the dialogue.

> "Two participants independently reportedaffirmation fatigue: motivational affirmations felt "disconnected from the conversation flow," with one requesting their removal."
