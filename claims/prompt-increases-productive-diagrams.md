# Students prompted to draw a diagram were more likely to draw productive diagrams than the diagram-only and no-support groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` 96% of the PO group drew productive diagrams versus 60% of DO and 79% of NS; PO differed significantly from DO (p<0.001) and from NS (p=0.046, Fisher's exact test), while DO-NS was not significant (p=0.076). ## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2013

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2013). A good diagram is valuable despite the choice of a mathematical approach to problem solving. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.006

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Chi-square (and Fisher's exact test where expected cell frequencies were smaller than 10) comparisons of productive-diagram percentages in the quiz: PO 96%, DO 60%, NS 79%; p values PO-DO <0.001, PO-NS 0.046, DO-NS 0.076. Students given the empty-tube diagram "were somewhat less likely than students in the comparison group (NS) to draw productive diagrams".

> "It appears that students in PO are more likely than students in DO and NS to draw productive diagrams. Also, it appears that students in DO were somewhat less likely than students in the comparison group (NS) to draw productive diagrams."
