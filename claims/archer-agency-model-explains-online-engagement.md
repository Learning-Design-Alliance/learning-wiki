# Archer's model of agency helps explain how interviewed online students engaged in their learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students configured concerns pertaining to required tasks and produced courses of action and sustained practices on the basis of reflexive deliberations, matching the main features of Archer's model. [→ Peter Kahn 2017](#peter-kahn-2017)
`q2 i?` Task-related concerns dominated: concerns about carrying out required tasks appeared on 18 occasions and employment-utility concerns on 13 occasions in the interviews. [→ Peter Kahn 2017 (2)](#peter-kahn-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Peter Kahn 2017

Peter Kahn, Lucy Everington, Kathleen Kelm, Iain Reid, Francine Watkins. (2017). Understanding student engagement in online learning environments: the role of reflexivity. Education Tech Research Dev, 65, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-016-9484-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative analysis of interviews with eight online Masters students. The study concludes that concerns and actions were influenced by structural settings as students progressed challenging tasks alongside others.

> "The main features of Archer's model of agency are thus apparent in the analysis of these students' experiences."

### Peter Kahn 2017 (2)

Peter Kahn, Lucy Everington, Kathleen Kelm, Iain Reid, Francine Watkins. (2017). Understanding student engagement in online learning environments: the role of reflexivity. Education Tech Research Dev, 65, 203-218. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-016-9484-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview-based coding of concerns among the eight interviewed students; employment-related concerns are characteristic of autonomous reflexivity, and all interviewed students manifested at least some such concerns.

> "We identiﬁed concerns that were linked to how best to carry out a required task on 18 occa- sions, while concerns that pertained to the utility of their studies and tasks for employment were identiﬁed on 13 occasions."
