Evidence¶

Kucinkas 2025¶

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Evaluation-phase mixed ANOVA on processing time (seconds from response-option presentation to answer selection) with 60 sighted adults; "the Reversed group showed the largest increase in processing time for novel stimuli (+3.05 seconds)"; no effect size printed for this contrast, so impact is null.

"the Reversed group showed the largest increase in processing time for novel stimuli (+3.05 seconds), followed by the Traditional group (+2.53 seconds). In contrast, the Control group exhibited a smaller, though still statistically significant, increase (+0.83 seconds)."

Kucinkas 2025 (2)¶

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Main effect from the same processing-time ANOVA; "participants took significantly longer to process novel stimuli compared to familiar stimuli", with printed effect size η²ₚ = 0.633 (partial eta-squared = 0.633), a large effect. The Group main effect was not significant (η²ₚ = 0.066).

"A significant main effect of Stimulus Type was found, F(1,57) = 98.337; p < 0.001; η²ₚ = 0.633, indicating that participants took significantly longer to process novel stimuli compared to familiar stimuli."
