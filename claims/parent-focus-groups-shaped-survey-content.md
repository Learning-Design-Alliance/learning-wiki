# Parent focus groups showed parents could not report on specific classroom instructional interactions, so ambitious instruction items were developed for the teacher survey instead, and a new Teacher Care and Responsiveness towards Parents measure was created

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Focus groups with parents revealed that parents were not comfortable reporting on specific instructional interactions in the classroom, so ambitious instruction questions were developed for the teacher survey rather than the parent survey. [→ Ehrlich 2016](#ehrlich-2016)
`q2 i?` A measure called Teacher Care and Responsiveness towards Parents was created based on the focus groups, capturing whether teachers attend to the needs, struggles, and accomplishments of parents themselves. [→ Ehrlich 2016 (2)](#ehrlich-2016-2)

### Ehrlich 2016 (2)

Ehrlich, S.B., Pacchiano, D.M., Stein, A.G., & Luppescu, S. (2016). Essential organizational supports for early education: The development of a new survey tool to measure organizational conditions. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research and the Ounce of Prevention Fund. http://consortium.uchicago.edu/surveys

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Focus-group findings during parent survey content determination showed parents feel most comfortable with a program when teachers and staff attend to the needs, struggles, and accomplishments of the parents themselves, prompting creation of this new measure.

> "In another example, we created a measure called Teacher Care and Responsiveness towards Parents based on our focus groups."
