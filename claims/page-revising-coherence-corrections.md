# Page writers corrected significantly more coherence errors than Scroll writers, while surface and cohesion corrections did not differ

## Evidence

### Annie Piolat 1997

Annie Piolat, Jean-Yves Roussey and Olivier Thunin. (1997). Effects of screen presentation on text reading and revising. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies. https://doi.org/10.1006/ijhc.1997.0145

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 error-correction results: the article reports the number of coherence revisions was significantly higher with Page than with Scroll. The discussion gives the rates as "Page writers (50%)" versus "Scroll writers (31%)".

> "On the other hand, in line with Prediction 2, the number of coherence revisions was significantly higher with Page than with Scroll."

### Annie Piolat 1997 (2)

Annie Piolat, Jean-Yves Roussey and Olivier Thunin. (1997). Effects of screen presentation on text reading and revising. International Journal of Human-Computer Studies. https://doi.org/10.1006/ijhc.1997.0145

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 null result for lower-level revisions: surface and cohesion improvements did not differ significantly between Page and Scroll conditions; almost all surface errors were corrected under both.

> "Writers did not make significantly different surface or cohesion improvements under the Page and Scroll conditions."
