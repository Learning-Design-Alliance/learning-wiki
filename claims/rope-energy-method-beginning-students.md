# Energy conservation plus the identity a = (1/2)d(v²)/dx yields the falling rope's velocity and acceleration in a form suitable for beginning students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For a uniform rope of length l over a small frictionless pulley, energy conservation gives v = x(2g/l)^(1/2) and a = 2gx/l, derived analytically under a one-dimensional-motion approximation. [→ C´ elia A. de Sousa 2011](#c-elia-a-de-sousa-2011)

## Evidence

### C´ elia A. de Sousa 2011

C´ elia A. de Sousa. (2011). The Atwood's machine as a tool to introduce variable mass systems. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/47/2/169](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/47/2/169)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Analytical derivation in the falling-rope section: the centre of mass is located via sub-system masses mI = λ(l/2 − x) and mII = λ(l/2 + x), energy conservation gives the velocity, and combining v² with the identity a = (1/2)d(v²)/dx yields the acceleration a = 2gx/l. The article notes this mathematical procedure is one "students already know from math classes, but that do not usually apply in classes of introductory physics".

> "Combining the expression of v2 with equation (11) yields to the acceleration a = 2 g xl ."
