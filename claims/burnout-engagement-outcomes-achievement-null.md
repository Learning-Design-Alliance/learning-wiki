# Exhaustion relates positively to depression and negatively to life satisfaction; engagement relates positively to life satisfaction; neither relates to academic achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Exhaustion positively predicts depressive symptoms (β = 0.32) and engagement positively predicts life satisfaction (β = 0.12), but neither exhaustion nor engagement relates to academic achievement. [→ Teuber Ziwen 2021](#teuber-ziwen-2021)

## Evidence

### Teuber Ziwen 2021

Teuber Ziwen, Nussbeck Fridtjof W., Wild Elke. (2021). The Bright Side of Grit in Burnout-Prevention: Exploring Grit in the Context of Demands-Resources Model among Chinese High School Students. Child Psychiatry and Human Development, 52(3), 464–476. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-020-01031-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

SEM results in the N = 1527 sample: engagement related to life satisfaction (β = 0.12, p < 0.05) but not to achievement (β = 0.08, p = 0.14) or depression (β = 0.03, p = 0.48). The authors call the achievement null the most unexpected result, possibly due to self-reported, retrospectively reported exam scores.

> "Exhaustion and depressive symptoms were positively related (β = 0.32, p < 0.001), no significant association was found between exhaustion and academic achievement (β = 0.01, p = 0.82)."
