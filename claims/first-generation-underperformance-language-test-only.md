## Evidence

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cohort study, social background analysis on the first-year language skills test. The results report first-generation university students less often passed than non-first-generation students (44 vs 53 %), unadjusted OR 0.72 (95 % CI 0.56–0.93), largely explained after adjustment (adjusted OR 0.91, 95 % CI 0.69–1.19). No standardized effect size is printed.

> "First-generation university students less often passed the language skills examination than non-first-generation university students (44 vs 53 %; Table 3). This difference corresponds to an unadjusted OR of 0.72 (95 % CI 0.56–0.93)"

### Stegers-Jager K. M. 2016 (2)

Stegers-Jager K. M., Brommet F. N., Themmen A. P. N. (2016). Ethnic and social disparities in different types of examinations in undergraduate pre-clinical training. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-016-9676-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cohort study, social background analysis across theoretical knowledge tests. The results report no statistically significant differences between first-generation and non-first-generation university students on any theoretical knowledge test; the article similarly reports no significant differences on writing skills tests or OSCEs. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The pass/fail rates between first-generation university students and non-first-generation university students were not significantly different on any of the theoretical knowledge tests (Table 3)."
