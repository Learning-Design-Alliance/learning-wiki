# Computer game-based practice produces no statistically significant difference in basic mathematics achievement compared to classical homework practice

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fifth-grade students who practiced basic mathematical skills through a computer game program showed no statistically significant difference in posttest achievement compared to students who practiced through classical homework exercises. [→ Sayan 2015](#sayan-2015)

## Evidence

### Sayan 2015

Sayan, H. (2015). The effects of computer games on the achievement of basic mathematical skills. Educational Research and Reviews, 10(22), 2846-2853.

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest study with 44 fifth-grade students in Istanbul, assigned to experimental (computer game practice, n=22) and control (classical homework, n=22) groups. The posttest comparison favored the experiment group by 1.82 points but was "not statistically meaningful (p=0.448)".

> "The last comparison is between the posttests of the average of the two groups. There is a difference in favor of the experiment group. The difference is 1.82 points, and it is not statistically meaningful (p=0.448)"
