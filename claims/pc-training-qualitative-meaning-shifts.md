# After specialist-led palliative care training, hospital professionals' meanings shifted across five themes, from disagreement, hard communication, perceived uselessness of end-of-life care, perceived impossible integration, and emotional difficulty, towards synergy, collaboration, competence, feasible integration and emotional mindfulness

`q2 i?` Focus group analysis before and after training identified five overarching themes whose meanings shifted, e.g. from 'Disagreement' to 'Synergy' in relationships between I- and II-levels, and from 'Perception of EoL care as useless' to 'Becoming competent EoL care clinicians'. ## Evidence

### Artioli Giovanna 2019

Artioli Giovanna, Bedini Gabriele, Bertocchi Elisabetta, Ghirotto Luca, Cavuto Silvio, Costantini Massimo, Tanzi Silvia. (2019). Palliative care training addressed to hospital healthcare professionals by palliative care specialists: a mixed-method evaluation. BMC Palliative Care. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12904-019-0476-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Framework-method qualitative analysis of separate pre- and post-training focus groups for physicians and other roles across the three wards, supervised by an external qualitative expert; meaning shifts were compared across data collections.

> "The analysis of the FGs before and after training led us to identify five overarching themes (Table 3): (1) Relationships among I- and II-levels, (2) Communication with patients and their families, (3) Clinicians' competences in EoL care, (4) Integration among I- and II-levels, and (5) Self consideration of their emotions."
