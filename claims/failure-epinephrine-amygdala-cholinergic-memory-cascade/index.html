Conceptual review attributing this mechanism to Cahill and McGaugh's pharmacological work and its reversal: "When epinephrine is high but pharmacologically blocked from binding to receptors in the amygdala, learning does not improve". No education-context data reported.

"Cahill and McGaugh (1998) demonstrated that epinephrine improves memory by increasing activation of the amygdala. In turn, the amygdala increases activation of cholinergic neurons, which improves memory formation"
