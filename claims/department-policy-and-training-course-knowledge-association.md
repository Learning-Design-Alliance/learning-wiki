## Evidence

### Hadi Yasser H. 2025

Hadi Yasser H., Aljuaid Mahmoud H., Alotaibi Nourah A., Altalhi Fatimah K., Ali Hussam M., Alrefaei Mosaab G., Abu Aqil Abdulrahman I., Fagiry Moram A., Osman Hamid, Mahmoud Mustafa. (2025). Investigating computed tomography radiographers’ expertise in responding to severe contrast media reactions: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07040-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square analysis of prior education and experience against consistent knowledge (Table 6) in the survey of 241 radiographers found "a 26.3% rate of strong knowledge, compared to just 2.3% in departments without such rules (p < 0.001)".

> "Departments that have a specialized policy for managing IVCM showed significantly superior outcomes. Among their workforce, there was a 26.3% rate of strong knowledge, compared to just 2.3% in departments without such rules (p < 0.001)."

### Hadi Yasser H. 2025 (2)

Hadi Yasser H., Aljuaid Mahmoud H., Alotaibi Nourah A., Altalhi Fatimah K., Ali Hussam M., Alrefaei Mosaab G., Abu Aqil Abdulrahman I., Fagiry Moram A., Osman Hamid, Mahmoud Mustafa. (2025). Investigating computed tomography radiographers’ expertise in responding to severe contrast media reactions: a cross-sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07040-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same Table 6 analysis, training-course participants (mean 26.07) scored higher than orientation-only (24.74) or never-trained (23.67) staff, but the article calls the finding of marginal significance only, p = 0.050.

> "those who participated in the training course had somewhat greater levels of expertise in IVCM compared to those who received department orientation or had no training, with a marginal level of statistical significance"
