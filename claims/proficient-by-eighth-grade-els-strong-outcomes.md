# Students who began as ELs and demonstrated proficiency by eighth grade had academic outcomes similar to or higher than students never classified as ELs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Eighty percent of students who began as ELs demonstrated proficiency on the ACCESS test by the end of eighth grade. [→ de la Torre 2019](#de-la-torre-2019)
`q2 i?` Compared to never-EL students, this group had higher attendance, higher math NWEA-MAP scores, and higher course grades, with comparable reading scores and Freshman OnTrack rates. [→ de la Torre 2019 (2)](#de-la-torre-2019-2)

## Evidence

### de la Torre 2019

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive proficiency outcome for the three continuously-enrolled CPS cohorts. The report notes these students have been categorized in public statistics as non-ELs once proficient, so analyzing them as a distinct subgroup adds nuance. No effect size is printed.

> "Eighty percent of students who began as ELs demonstrated proficiency on the ACCESS test by the end of eighth grade."

### de la Torre 2019 (2)

de la Torre, M., Blanchard, A., Allensworth, E.M., & Freire, S. (2019). English Learners in CPS: A new perspective. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Descriptive multi-outcome comparison (attendance, math and reading NWEA-MAP, course grades, Freshman OnTrack) for the proficient-by-eighth-grade subgroup versus never-EL students. The report interprets the pattern as suggesting appropriate supports. No effect size is printed.

> "In all outcomes, the academic performance of students who began as ELs and demonstrated proficiency by eighth grade was similar to or higher than students who were never classified as ELs, which suggests that students who fall into this category were offered supports appropriate for their academic needs."
