## Evidence

### Engerer C 2019

Engerer C, Berberat PO, Dinkel A, Rudolph B, Sattel H, Wuensch A. (2019). Specific feedback makes medical students better communicators. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1470-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Ancillary analysis comparing baseline-matched subgroups (17 low vs. 17 high performers in CST-behav) using Mann-Whitney-U-tests on blinded-rater global ratings; low performers gained roughly two scale points, high performers did not change; the article prints no effect size for this subgroup result.

> "In the Intervention Group (CST-behav) the low performers improved significantly from Mpre = 0.53 SD = 0.51 to Mpost = 2.59 SD = 0.94; p < 0.001, whereas high performer remained stable: Mpre = 2.59 SD = 0.71, Mpost = 2.59 SD = 0.94; p = 0.99."

### Engerer C 2019 (2)

Engerer C, Berberat PO, Dinkel A, Rudolph B, Sattel H, Wuensch A. (2019). Specific feedback makes medical students better communicators. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1470-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Post hoc ancillary comparison across trial groups; the article reports the control group's low-performer increase was significantly lower than the intervention group's, and control high performers declined slightly, shown in Fig. 3.

> "In the Control Group (CST-exp) we observed a slight improvement of low performers. However, this increase was significantly lower than that of the Intervention Group (CST-behav). Finally, high performers in the Control Group (CST-exp) even declined slightly (Fig. 3)."
