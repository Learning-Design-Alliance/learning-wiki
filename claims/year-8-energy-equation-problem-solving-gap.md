# Year 8 students solved the E=hf photon-energy problem (77%) far more often than the E=mc2 mass-loss problem (43%)

## Evidence

### Kaur 2024

Kaur, T., Kersting, M., Blair, D., Adams, K., Treagust, D., Santoso, J., Popkova, A., Boublil, S., Zadnik, M., Ju, L., Wood, D., Horne, E., & McGoran, D. (2024). Developing and implementing an Einsteinian science curriculum from Years 3 to 10 – Part A: Concepts, rationale and learning outcomes. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ad66a7](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ad66a7)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-test mathematics questions for one Year 8 class (22 students) taught the "From E = mc2 to renewable energy" module; the E=hf question asked students to calculate the energy of an ultraviolet photon.

> "In another question, students were asked to calculate the energy in joules of an ultraviolet photon of frequency 10 16 Hertz using E = hf, where Planck’s constant h = 7 x 10 -34JHz-1. After the program, 77% were able to solve it correctly."

### Kaur 2024 (2)

Kaur, T., Kersting, M., Blair, D., Adams, K., Treagust, D., Santoso, J., Popkova, A., Boublil, S., Zadnik, M., Ju, L., Wood, D., Horne, E., & McGoran, D. (2024). Developing and implementing an Einsteinian science curriculum from Years 3 to 10 – Part A: Concepts, rationale and learning outcomes. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ad66a7](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ad66a7)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

The same Year 8 post-test included an E=mc2 problem giving the sun's power output; the article reports "Only 43% of students solved the problem after the program", indicating this concept was harder than the E=hf calculation.

> "Students were given the sun ’s power output and asked to calculate the mass lost by the sun using the equation E=mc 2.  Only 43% of students solved the problem after the program."
