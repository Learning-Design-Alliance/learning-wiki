# Post-test (delayed) task-based feedback raised perceived competence more than immediate feedback and more than performance-based feedback

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Perceived competence was significantly higher in the delayed feedback groups (PR and PE) than in the immediate feedback groups (IR and IE), F = 16.90, p < .001, partial η² = .19. [→ Reiser 2026](#reiser-2026)
`q3 i2` Against performance-based feedback, the delayed groups scored higher on perceived competence (Pearson's r = .30 and .25, both p < .001); the immediate groups did not differ significantly. [→ Reiser 2026 (2)](#reiser-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Reiser 2026

Reiser, S., Schick, K., Pittroff, S. I. D., Janssen, L., Berberat, P. O., Gartmeier, M., & Bauer, J. (2026). Integrating formative and summative feedback in online situational judgement tests: effects of feedback design on medical students’ motivational and cognitive learning factors. Medical Education Online, 31(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2026.2639198

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up one-way ANOVA among the four task-based groups in the RCT. The Table 5 row for "Perceived competencec" prints F = 16.90, p < .001, partial η² = .19, with post hoc means showing the delayed groups (PR 4.689, PE 4.482) above the immediate groups (IR 3.531, IE 3.754).

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Planned contrasts against the Summative Test Profile group. Table 4 prints for contrast 3 (PR vs. TP) "1.01 5.14 261 <.001*** .30" and for contrast 4 (PE vs. TP) "0.80 4.14 261 <.001*** .25", giving Pearson's r = .30 and r = .25.
