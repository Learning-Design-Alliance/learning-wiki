## Evidence

### Sengupta 2016

Sengupta, P., & Clark, D. (2016). Playing Modeling Games in the Science Classroom: The Case for Disciplinary Integration. Educational Technology, 56(3), 16–22. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1607.05094)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The authors' ViMAP research program (Farris & Sengupta, 2014; Sengupta, Farris & Wright, 2012) shows that elementary students can begin to understand "motion as a process of continuous change" via agent-based modeling plus graphing; no quantitative effect sizes are printed.

> "Our ViMAP research showed that even elementary grade students can begin to understand motion as a process of continuous change by (a) modeling the behavior of the agent that produces a dot-trace representation using agent-based programming commands and (b) graphing the motion of the agent in a manner that makes explicit the pattern of change over time"
