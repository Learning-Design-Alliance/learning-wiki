# Explainability's effect on trust in AI-enabled systems is mixed: it can increase trust and reliance but also raises cognitive load, slows performance, and sometimes does not improve trust

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Several reviewed studies found explainability positively increased user trust and reliance, particularly in complex visual tasks with heatmap-based explanations. [→ Abramson SL 2026](#abramson-sl-2026)
`q2 i?` Other reviewed studies found explanations, particularly multiple types simultaneously, increased cognitive load, slowed user performance, or did not improve trust even when present. [→ Abramson SL 2026 (2)](#abramson-sl-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Abramson SL 2026

Abramson SL, Sora-Cardenas J, Nandakumar P, Bruce JE, Gopinath N, Zangari LG and Feijóo-García PG (2026). Trust and interaction design in AI-enabled systems: a systematic literature review. Front. Psychol. 17:1932655. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1932655

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The review reports, citing Maehigashi et al. (2024a, b), that heatmap-based explanations aided comprehension and increased trust in complex visual tasks; the review narrates these studies without printing effect sizes.

> "Several studies found that explainability positively increased user trust and reliance, particularly in complex visual tasks where heatmap-based explanations aided user comprehension of AI reasoning"

### Abramson SL 2026 (2)

Abramson SL, Sora-Cardenas J, Nandakumar P, Bruce JE, Gopinath N, Zangari LG and Feijóo-García PG (2026). Trust and interaction design in AI-enabled systems: a systematic literature review. Front. Psychol. 17:1932655. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1932655

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review attributes countervailing findings to the same cited studies: explanations "increased cognitive load", slowed performance, and in some cases did not improve trust. No effect sizes are printed for these outcomes.

> "other studies reported that providing explanations, particularly multiple types of explanations simultaneously, increased cognitive load ( Maehigashietal., 2024a ), slowed user performance (Maehigashietal.,2024b ),andinsomecasesdidnotimprovetrust evenwhenexplanationswerepresent"
