Evidence¶

Ke Yang 2026¶

Ke Yang, Chenglong Wang, Michel Galley, Chandan Singh, Jeevana Priya Inala, ChengXiang Zhai, Jianfeng Gao. (2026). StudentSim: Training LLM-based Student Simulators. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.01591

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Expert human study in which competitive chess players rated each tutor's guidance blind to presentation order on accuracy, guidance quality (1-5), and personalization (1-5); "our STUDENTSIMreward produces the best-rated tutor on all three axes". No effect size is printed.

"Among the rewards we study, our STUDENTSIMreward produces the best-rated tutor on all three axes (Table 4)."

Ke Yang 2026 (2)¶

Ke Yang, Chenglong Wang, Michel Galley, Chandan Singh, Jeevana Priya Inala, ChengXiang Zhai, Jianfeng Gao. (2026). StudentSim: Training LLM-based Student Simulators. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01591

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Same expert human study: the GPT-5.4-simulator reward condition "stays below even the no-RL baseline on accuracy", which the authors attribute to a markedly higher rate of severe factual errors in its generated guidance.

Related Claims¶

  • Across chess, L2 writing, and math, trained StudentSim simulators outperform every reference baseline on behavioral fidelity (F) — related
  • Across all three domains, trained StudentSim simulators outperform every baseline on guidance responsiveness (R), with the largest gap on open-ended guidance modes — related
  • A 4B per-student simulator solves a held-out Socratic-guidance chess case where prompted GPT-5.4 fails, indicating multi-turn training adds guidance-following capability beyond hint copying — related
  • The LLM-judge scoring pipeline for the two metrics aligns with human labels and human raters — related
  • An analytical chapter identifies reward hacking, engagement optimization over learning, and scaffolding dependency as risks of RL policies with poorly specified rewards — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Anchored judge prompts restore score discrimination and raise the reward's agreement with independent human ratings (Spearman ρ 0.672→0.741) — related
  • A screenshot-only reward was reward-hacked into producing visually convincing but unplayable games; hardening the reward with an interactivity probe and hard-fail gate reversed the collapse — related
  • An engagement-optimized RL tutor exhibits reward hacking, achieving the highest cumulative reward while producing minimal learning and a 70.8% behavioral-safety violation rate — related
