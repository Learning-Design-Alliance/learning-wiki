## Evidence

### Birrenbach T 2024

Birrenbach T, Stuber R, Müller CE, Sutter P-M, Hautz WE, Exadaktylos AK, Müller M, Wespi R, Sauter TC. (2024). Virtual reality simulation to enhance advanced trauma life support trainings – a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education 24:666. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05645-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison in a randomized controlled pilot study (VR n = 28 vs control n = 28). The article reports "no significant differences observed between the two groups" on knowledge tests; no effect size was printed.

> "There were no significant differences observed between the two groups in terms of their mean scores for the pre- and final multiple choice knowledge tests (p = 0.832 and p = 0.237 respectively)."

### Birrenbach T 2024 (2)

Birrenbach T, Stuber R, Müller CE, Sutter P-M, Hautz WE, Exadaktylos AK, Müller M, Wespi R, Sauter TC. (2024). Virtual reality simulation to enhance advanced trauma life support trainings – a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education 24:666. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05645-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group comparison of final clinical scenario first-attempt pass rates in the same randomized pilot; the article reports "no significant difference" (p = 0.485) and all participants ultimately passed the course.

> "Similarly, there was no significant difference in the pass rates for the final clinical scenario on the first attempt (p = 0.485)."
