## Evidence

### Wodarz M.N. 2025

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

District-level analysis of Math 8 minus Math 3 score differences across all states (Results, Fig 8). The article reports the histogram is asymmetric with heavier negative values and that "more than 82%" of districts show a decrease; the printed count is a proportion of districts, not an effect size.

> "The bar graph on the right in panel (c) shows that in a vast majority (more than 82%) of all the districts, the percentage of students performing well in math decreases from 3rd to 8th grade."

### Wodarz M.N. 2025 (2)

Wodarz M.N., Komarova N.L., Ma T. (2025). The functional form of the association between K-12 student performance and household income in U.S. school districts. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329296

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same district-level analysis for ELA (Results, Fig 8(a),(c)). The article describes a "roughly symmetric one-peak distribution" of ELA score differences, with decreases only slightly more frequent than increases.

> "We can see a roughly symmetric one-peak distribution of such score differences. This is confirmed by the bar chart in panel (c), left, where the number of districts with a decrease in performance is only slightly higher than the number of districts with an increase in performance in ELA."
