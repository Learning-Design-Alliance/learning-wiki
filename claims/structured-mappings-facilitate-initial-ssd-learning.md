## Evidence

### Kucinkas 2025

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way between-subjects ANOVA on Attempt 1 accuracy in a randomized experiment with 60 sighted adults in Traditional, Reversed, or Control mapping groups; mapping condition significantly influenced performance on the first attempt, with printed effect size η²ₚ = 0.310 (partial eta-squared = 0.310), a large effect.

> "The analysis showed a significant main effect of Condition (F(2, 57) = 12.81, p < 0.001, η²ₚ = 0.310; ω² = .282), indicating that mapping condition significantly influenced performance on the first attempt."

### Kucinkas 2025 (2)

Kucinkas, A., Retsa, C., Meijer, P. B. L., Wallace, M. T., Gori, M. & Murray, M. M. (2025). Learning visual to auditory sensory substitution reveals flexibility in image to sound mapping. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00385-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive group means (mean ± SEM) from the same learning-phase analysis; the Traditional group reached "90.00 ± 1.85%" accuracy, the Reversed group 84.17 ± 2.65%, and the Control group 72.08 ± 3.02%.

> "The Traditional group achieved 90.00 ± 1.85%, the Reversed group 84.17 ± 2.65%, and the Control group 72.08 ± 3.02% accuracy."
