## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The ability to recognize others’ emotions, measured with the GERT-S performance task, significantly predicted OSCE performances in both cohorts with small effect sizes (Year 3: β = 0.15; Year 5: β = 0.13). [→ Carrard Valerie 2026](#carrard-valerie-2026)

## Evidence

### Carrard Valerie 2026

Carrard Valerie, Bourquin Céline, Félix Sylvie, Viret Francine, Takeuchi Yusuke L., Berney Alexandre. (2026). Simulated-patient evaluations during formative interviews predict exam performance better than self-reported empathy and the ability to recognize others’ emotions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09934-w

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Independent SEM for the Year 5 cohort (N = 399) showed the GERT-S emotion recognition task significantly predicted OSCE performance ("ability to recognize others’ emotions: β = 0.13, p=.013"), a small effect. The authors say this aligns with scarce prior literature.

> "In the Year 5 cohort, all predictors yielded small significant effect sizes (self-reported empathy: β = 0.15, p=.039; ability to recognize others’ emotions: β = 0.13, p=.013; simulated-patient evaluations: β = 0.21, p<.001)."
