Sandiford 1922¶

Sandiford, P., & R. P. (1922). New Publications: reviews of Baldwin's mental growth study, Root's A Socio-Psychological Study of 53 Supernormal Children, and Ballard's Group Tests of Intelligence. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/journal/edu

q2 · i? · review · r?

Sandiford's review of Baldwin's mental growth work reports this as Baldwin's finding on the constancy of the IQ; the review states Baldwin "shows conclusively that it is fairly constant though subject to fluctuations."

"He shows conclusively that it is fairly constant though subject to fluctuations so far as the measurements made are reliable."

"He has also pointed out that the results of repeated examinations exhibit a definite practice effect."

Related Claims¶

  • IQ is only approximately constant across successive examinations, with first-to-fifth IQ correlation of +0.82 and prediction error of 4 to 7 points — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • IQs obtained by dividing mental by chronological age remain fairly constant up to the age of 14, which supports treating yearly increments of intelligence as equal absolute units up to about age 14. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The reviewer judges Goddard's book an optimistic, clear popular account that exaggerates intelligence's influence and omits technical questions such as IQ validity — related
  • Cephalic index computed from calliper head measurements shows fluctuations in both children, with parental indices of 78.8 and 81.8 — related
  • Large IQ differences in this sample were mostly attributable to identifiable causes such as failures of previously passed tests, chance success, effort fluctuation, or lack of experience — related
  • The question of the constancy of IQ is not settled; further studies with regular intervals and repeated tests of the same cases over a number of years are needed — related
