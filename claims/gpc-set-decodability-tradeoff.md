## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Computational analysis shows a trade-off: larger GPC sets raise the proportion of theoretically decodable words, smaller sets raise uniquely decodable words. [→ Ungerer 2026](#ungerer-2026)

## Evidence

### Ungerer 2026

Ungerer, T., Rastle, K., & Armstrong, B. C. (2026). Bringing the Reading Sciences Into the Classroom: Insights for Phonics Instruction. Perspectives on Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916261438004

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A computational algorithm (Rognan, Ungerer, et al., 2026) generated all possible pronunciations of words from naturalistic child texts given different programs' GPCs; it found larger sets raise theoretical decodability while "programs that teach fewer GPCs increase the proportion of words that can be uniquely decoded."

> "They found that programs that teach more, including inconsistent, GPCs increase the proportion of words that can be theoretically decoded."
