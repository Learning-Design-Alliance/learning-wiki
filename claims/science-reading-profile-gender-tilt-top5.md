# Male top-performing math students show strongly science-oriented science–reading profiles while female students' profiles are more balanced

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Science–reading profile distributions of male and female top performers show a mean nonoverlap of 42%, which the article classifies as a large effect. [→ Keller 2021](#keller-2021)

## Evidence

### Keller 2021

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic synthesis of country-specific nonoverlap statistics for the science–reading profile. The distributions "showed a mean nonoverlap of 42%, representing a large effect" per the article's benchmarks; no standardized effect size was printed.

### Keller 2021 (2)

Keller, L., Preckel, F., Eccles, J. S., & Brunner, M. (2021). Top-Performing Math Students in 82 Countries: An Integrative Data Analysis of Gender Differences in Achievement, Achievement Profiles, and Achievement Motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000685

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analytic integration of profile score differences. Male top performers' science exceeded reading "by, on average, 32.20 points"; female students' difference was 2.08 points in favor of science, and 76% of males versus 48% of females showed a science tilt.

> "mathematically top-performing male students performed better in science than in reading, showing a profile score difference of, on average, 32.20 points in favor of science"
