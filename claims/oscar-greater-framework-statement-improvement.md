# OSCAR framework-based training produced greater improvement in trainees' agreement with framework-related statements 1–2 than regular framework training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intervention-group clerks improved more than regular-group clerks in familiarity and confidence with the framework (framework-1: 111%±13% vs 27%±5%, p<0.001; framework-2: 77%±9% vs 48%±2%, p<0.05). [→ Chiang Dung-Hung 2022](#chiang-dung-hung-2022)

## Evidence

### Chiang Dung-Hung 2022

Chiang Dung-Hung, Chen Chung-Ting, Wang Tse-Yao, Yang Ying-Ying, Huang Chia-Chang, Li Tzu-Hao, Huang Shiau-Shian, Kao Shou-Yen, Chen Chen-Huan, Hou Ming-Chih, Sheu Wayne Huey-Herng. (2022). BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-055953

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized comparison of 48 regular and 48 intervention clerks in a prospective case-controlled study; end-of-rotation self-assessments showed greater framework-statement improvement in the intervention group, with "framework-1 statement: 27%±5% vs 111±13%, p<0.001". No effect size printed.

> "The degree of improvement in trainees' agreement with the framework-related statement 1–2 was greater in the intervention group than that in the regular group ((regular vs intervention group: framework-1 statement: 27%±5% vs 111±13%, p<0.001); framework-2 statement: 48%±2% vs 77±9%, p<0.05, figure 2B)."
