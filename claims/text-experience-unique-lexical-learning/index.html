Evidence¶

Nation 2017¶

Nation, K. (2017). Nurturing a lexical legacy: reading experience is critical for the development of word reading skill. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0004-7

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Nagy, Herman and Anderson (ref 44), that "the majority of new vocabulary is learned via reading, not listening" once children are able to read. This is a second-hand descriptive claim with no effect size printed.

"Once children are able to read, the majority of new vocabulary is learned via reading, not listening"

Nation 2017 (2)¶

Nation, K. (2017). Nurturing a lexical legacy: reading experience is critical for the development of word reading skill. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0004-7

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Montag, Jones and Smith (ref 45), that "Even text written for young children is more lexically diverse than speech", with syntax differences also noted (ref 46). The review offers this as consistent with text affording unique learning opportunities; no effect size is printed.

"Even text written for young children is more lexically diverse than speech"
