## Evidence

### Amosa Isiaka Gambari 2014

Amosa Isiaka Gambari, Hamdalat Taiwo Yusuf, Sherifat Adepeju Balogun. (2014). Effectiveness of Powerpoint Presentation On Students' Cognitive Achievement in Technical Drawing. Malaysian Online Journal of Educational Technology, Volume 3, Issue 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1085923

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest non-equivalent group study of 100 JSS 1 students in two Abuja schools, analyzed with ANCOVA controlling for pretest. The article reports "F(1, 91) = 11.039, p = 0.001" for the treatment main effect favoring PPT over chalkboard.

> "Table 3 shows that F(1, 91) = 11.039, p = 0.001 for the main effect (treatment) was significant, indicating that the method of instruction produced a significant effect on the achievement scores of students when covariate effect (pre -test) was controlled."

### Amosa Isiaka Gambari 2014 (2)

Amosa Isiaka Gambari, Hamdalat Taiwo Yusuf, Sherifat Adepeju Balogun. (2014). Effectiveness of Powerpoint Presentation On Students' Cognitive Achievement in Technical Drawing. Malaysian Online Journal of Educational Technology, Volume 3, Issue 4. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1085923

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive mean gain comparison from the same quasi-experiment: pretest-posttest gains were 53.60 for chalkboard versus 50.22 for PPT, even though PPT had the higher posttest mean. No significance test is reported for the gain difference.

> "Table 3 shows Chalkboard group (CB) had higher mean gain score of 53.60; followed by PowerPoint Presentation (PPT) with mean gain scores of 50.22."
