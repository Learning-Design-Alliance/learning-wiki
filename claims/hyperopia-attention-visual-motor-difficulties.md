## Evidence

### Kulp MT 2026

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the VIP-HIP study finding "significant difficulties with sustained visual attention and visual-motor integration" in uncorrected hyperopic children versus age-typical peers, and states these difficulties were associated with reduced near visual function. No effect size is printed.

> "The VIP-HIP study found significant difficulties with sustained visual attention and visual-motor integration in uncorrected hyperopic children as compared to children with age-typical refractive error."

### Kulp MT 2026 (2)

Kulp MT, Ciner EB, Candy TR, Ying G-s, Hall C, Quinn G, Moore B. (2026). Impact of uncorrected hyperopia on visual function and academic performance in preschool and school-age children. Optometry and Vision Science. https://doi.org/10.1002/ovs2.70018

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review attributes to Roch-Levecq et al. a comparison of 35 uncorrected hyperopic/astigmatic children with 35 age-matched emmetropic controls showing significantly lower cognitive and visual-motor integration scores; after correction, visual-motor integration differences were no longer statistically significant.

> "Roch-Levecq et al. found significantly lower scores on the Wechsler Preschool and Primary Scale of intelligence-revised and visual-motor integration for 35 children with uncorrected bilateral hyperopia and/or astigmatism compared to 35 age-matched emmetropic controls (<=2 D hyperopia and <=1 D astigmatism)."
