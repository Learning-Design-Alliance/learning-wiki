# Exposure to a video model improves trainee teachers’ skill in using questioning for retrieval by 0.80 SD relative to restudying the evidence summary

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Trainee teachers randomly allocated to watch a video model of evidence-based questioning for retrieval improved their simulator skill score by 0.80 SD relative to an active restudy control. [→ Sam Sims 2026](#sam-sims-2026)

## Evidence

### Sam Sims 2026

Sam Sims, Harry Fletcher-Wood, Thomas Godfrey-Faussett, Peps Mccrea, Stefanie Meliss. (2026). Modelling evidence-based practice in initial teacher training: effects on teachers’ skills, knowledge and self-efficacy. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-026-09779-2

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Pre-registered OLS regression (Model 1) on 89 randomised trainee teachers in a Mursion classroom simulator, comparing the two model arms against the restudy active control on a standardised 18-point skill measure; the coefficient is interpretable as Cohen’s d, with a printed effect size of d = 0.80 (95% CI = [0.39, 1.20]), as the article reports “exposure to the video model improved teachers’ use of questioning for retrieval by 0.80 SD”.

> "The results show that exposure to the video model improved teachers’ use of questioning for retrieval by 0.80 SD, relative to restudy (95% CI = [0.39, 1.20]). This difference is statistically significant at conventional levels ( p < 0.001)."
