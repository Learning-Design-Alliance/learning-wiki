### Trevor I. Smith 2019

Trevor I. Smith, Suzanne W. Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Andrew Boudreaux, Philip Eaton, Paul J. Kelly, Kyle J. Louis, Mitchell A. Nussenbaum, and Louis J. Remy. (2019). Developing a reasoning inventory for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1901.03351)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' interpretation based on informal feedback: the comb's charge going from 0 C to -5 C means the net charge is larger (response E), but some claim it is smaller "because negative numbers are smaller than zero." The authors disagree, noting charge-type labels are arbitrary. No systematic evidence is presented.

> "Informal feedback from research participants and our colleagues suggests that some of the issues with Q15 may come from different interpretations of the term “net charge.”"
