# The spaced-practice knowledge benefit reflects deeper learning or transfer rather than memorization of repeated items

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Because quiz and final-assessment items were never repeated, the benefit is attributed to deeper learning or transfer to different contexts. [→ Kang 2023](#kang-2023)

## Evidence

### Kang 2023

Kang, S. H. K., Eglington, L. G., Schuetze, B. A., Lu, X., Hinterstoisser, T. M., & Huaco, J. (2023). Using Cognitive Science and Technology to Enhance Financial Education: The Effect of Spaced Retrieval Practice. Journal of Financial Counseling and Planning, 34(1), 20–31. https://doi.org/10.1891/JFCP-2021-0032

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion of the field experiment's knowledge results: final-assessment items were a distinct set tapping the same credit concepts, so the spaced-practice advantage "reflect deeper learning or transfer" rather than memorization of previously seen answers.

> "the items on the quizzes and on the final assessment were never repeated (though they tapped a common set of concepts related to credit), and so the benefit observed was not due to improved memorization of specific answers to previously encountered questions but rather reflect deeper learning or transfer"
