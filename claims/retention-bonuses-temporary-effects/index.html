Evidence¶

Podolsky 2025¶

Podolsky, Anne, and Linda Darling-Hammond. (2025). Think Again: Do the Returns to Teacher Experience Fizzle Out? Washington, D.C.: Thomas B. Fordham Institute. https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/research/think-again-do-returns-teacher-experience-fizzle-out

q2 · i? · review · r2

The brief reports, citing Glazerman et al.'s multisite randomized experiment, that bonus effects on retention ended when payments ended; equivalence after the program was observed, not formally tested.

"a 2013 study of the Talent Transfer Initiative, which paid a $20,000 bonus over two years to highly effective teachers to teach in schools with low average test scores, found increased retention rates during the 2-year period that teachers received the bonus. However, after the program ended, there was no difference between the retention rates of bonus recipients and other teachers."

Podolsky 2025 (2)¶

Podolsky, Anne, and Linda Darling-Hammond. (2025). Think Again: Do the Returns to Teacher Experience Fizzle Out? Washington, D.C.: Thomas B. Fordham Institute. https://fordhaminstitute.org/national/research/think-again-do-returns-teacher-experience-fizzle-out

q2 · i? · review · r2

The brief reports, citing Liu, Johnson, and Peske (2004), that teachers self-reported working conditions outweighed the bonus; a self-report finding with no effect size printed.

"Massachusetts teachers who received a $20,000 signing bonus that was paid out over 4 years reported that working conditions played a larger role in their mobility decisions than financial incentives."
