# Textbook derivations typically fail to indicate which parts of the Lorentz derivation rest on which postulate

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Standard textbook presentations of the Lorentz transformation are often confusing or misleading because they do not separate the roles of the relativity principle and the constancy of the speed of light. [→ Alfred Ziegler 2007](#alfred-ziegler-2007)

## Evidence

### Alfred Ziegler 2007

Alfred Ziegler. (2007). The role of the two postulates of special relativity. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.0988 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.0988](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.0988)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's introductory statement of the problem, based on the author's survey of representative textbook derivations in Section VII, which reviews twenty-two texts and classifies them by where the postulates enter.

> "In particular texts often fail to point out clearly which parts of the derivation rest on which of the two postulates on which the derivation is commonly based (relativity principle and constancy of the speed of light)"
