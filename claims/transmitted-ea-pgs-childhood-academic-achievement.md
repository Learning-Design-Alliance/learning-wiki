# The transmitted EA polygenic score is significantly associated with childhood academic achievement (β = 0.24), but the non-transmitted EA PGS is not, showing no genetic nurturing for achievement around the end of primary school

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The transmitted EA PGS significantly predicted childhood academic achievement, while the non-transmitted EA PGS did not, contrary to the hypothesis of genetic nurturing for achievement. [→ de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020](#de-zeeuw-eveline-l-2020)
`q2 i?` The non-transmitted EA PGS showed no significant association with childhood academic achievement or ADHD symptoms, indicating no genetic nurturing by parental EA. [→ de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020 (2)](#de-zeeuw-eveline-l-2020-2)

## Evidence

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020

de Zeeuw Eveline L., Hottenga Jouke-Jan, Ouwens Klaasjan G., Dolan Conor V., Ehli Erik A., Davies Gareth E., Boomsma Dorret I., van Bergen Elsje. (2020). Intergenerational Transmission of Education and ADHD: Effects of Parental Genotypes. Behavior Genetics, Volume 50, Issue 4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-020-09992-w

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Regression of children's academic achievement on the transmitted EA PGS in the trio sample: "significantly associated with academic achievement (β = 0.24, p < 0.001)". The same model shows the transmitted EA PGS also predicted ADHD symptoms at home and at school (β = − 0.13 each).

> "In childhood, the transmitted EA PGS was significantly associated with academic achievement (β = 0.24, p < 0.001), ADHD symptoms at home (β = − 0.13, p < 0.001) and ADHD symptoms at school (β = − 0.13, p < 0.001) (Table 1—model 1)."

### de Zeeuw Eveline L. 2020 (2)

de Zeeuw Eveline L., Hottenga Jouke-Jan, Ouwens Klaasjan G., Dolan Conor V., Ehli Erik A., Davies Gareth E., Boomsma Dorret I., van Bergen Elsje. (2020). Intergenerational Transmission of Education and ADHD: Effects of Parental Genotypes. Behavior Genetics, Volume 50, Issue 4. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-020-09992-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the same childhood regressions, the non-transmitted EA PGS "did not have a significant effect"; the authors interpret this as "no genetic nurturing on offspring’s academic achievement and ADHD symptoms elicited by parental EA". No effect size for the null is quoted here.

> "The non-transmitted EA PGS did not have a significant effect (see Fig. 2b). This suggests that there was no genetic nurturing on offspring’s academic achievement and ADHD symptoms elicited by parental EA."
