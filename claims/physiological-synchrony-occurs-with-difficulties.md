# Session-level physiological synchrony occurred in the two groups that struggled with the collaborative exam, while the two groups that found the task easy showed no synchrony.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Two of four groups showed significant session-level physiological synchrony ( assessed via Monte Carlo shuffling of PC-index correlations ), and both struggled with the exam; the two non-synchronised groups expressed that the task was easy. [→ Jonna Malmberg 2019](#jonna-malmberg-2019)

## Evidence

### Jonna Malmberg 2019

Jonna Malmberg, Eetu Haataja, Tapio Seppänen, Sanna Järvelä. (2019). Are we together or not? The temporal interplay of monitoring, physiological arousal and physiological synchrony during a collaborative exam. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 14:467–490. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09311-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Case-based qualitative analysis of four groups' exam sessions; groups 2 and 4 showed significant synchrony in between-pair PC-index comparisons ( e.g. group 2 left–middle SSI = 0.311, p < 0.0001) and "both of these groups struggled" with the task.

> "What characterizes the two groups that had significant physiological synchrony for the whole session is that both of these groups struggled in terms of how to determine the refractive index of light for water and how to report the results."

### Jonna Malmberg 2019 (2)

Jonna Malmberg, Eetu Haataja, Tapio Seppänen, Sanna Järvelä. (2019). Are we together or not? The temporal interplay of monitoring, physiological arousal and physiological synchrony during a collaborative exam. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 14:467–490. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-019-09311-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The complementary case analysis found groups 1 and 3 showed no session-level synchrony and "both of thesegroups expressed that the task was easy"; per-pair SSIs for these groups were non-significant ( e.g. group 1 left–middle SSI = −0.034, p = 0.6635).

> "What characterizes the two groups that had no significant physiological synchrony for the whole session is that both of thesegroups expressed that the task was easy."
