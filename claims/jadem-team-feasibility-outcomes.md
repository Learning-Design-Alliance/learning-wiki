## Evidence

### Reumerman M. 2021

Reumerman M., Tichelaar J., Richir M.C., van Agtmael M.A. (2021). Medical students as adverse drug event managers, learning about side effects while improving their reporting in clinical practice. Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Archives of Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-021-02060-y

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

One-year prospective observational evaluation of the J-ADEM team in a tertiary academic hospital, reporting screening and submission counts plus patient questionnaire results: 61 of 62 patients (98%) found reporting relevant and all 62 felt taken seriously. No effect size applies; descriptive feasibility data.

> "From Augustus 2018 through Augustus 2019, 136 patients were screened for medication-related problems, and 65 high-quality ADRs reports, of which 50 ADRs (77%) were classified as severe reactions, were submitted to the Netherlands Pharmacovigilance Center Lareb by the J-ADEM team."

### Reumerman M. 2021 (2)

Reumerman M., Tichelaar J., Richir M.C., van Agtmael M.A. (2021). Medical students as adverse drug event managers, learning about side effects while improving their reporting in clinical practice. Naunyn-Schmiedeberg's Archives of Pharmacology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00210-021-02060-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Short physician survey of attending physicians who did not report ADRs the J-ADEM team detected; 56 of 65 physicians responded, providing 146 reasons (average 2.6 per physician). Reasons were categorized against known determinants, with knowledge/attitude factors and professional excuses dominant. Descriptive percentages only.

> ""Factors associated with ADR-related knowledge and attitudes" (n = 73; 50.0%) and "Excuses made by professionals" (n = 72; 49.3%) were the reasons mentioned the most often"
