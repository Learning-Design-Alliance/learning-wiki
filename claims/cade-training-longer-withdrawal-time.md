## Evidence

### Orzeszko Z 2025

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Intraprocedural quality-indicator comparison in the retrospective cohort of 6000 patients: the AI-trained group averaged 9 min withdrawal time versus 8 min in the conventionally trained group, a mean difference the authors link to AI-driven habits of detailed colon evaluation. No standardized effect size was printed.

> "WT was longer in the AI-trained group (mean difference 0.8 min; 95% CI: 0.6 to 1.0 min)."

### Orzeszko Z 2025 (2)

Orzeszko Z, Gach T, Necka S, Ochwat K, Major P, Szura M. (2025). The implementation of computer-aided detection in an initial endoscopy training improves the quality measures of trainees' future colonoscopies: a retrospective cohort study. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-025-11890-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Logistic regression on the same cohort found AI training predicted high-quality withdrawal time ("OR 1.88, 95% CI: 1.69–2.09", p < 0.05), but WT data quality was deemed low due to a 38.2% missing-data rate requiring multiple imputation.

> "Logistic regression confirmed that AI-trained endoscopists were more likely to meet high-quality standards in terms of ADR, SDR, and WT (OR 1.27, 95% CI: 1.14–1.42, p < 0.05; OR 1.93, 95% CI: 1.10–3.37, p < 0.05; OR 1.88, 95% CI: 1.69–2.09, p < 0.05, respectively), with no impact on CIR (Table 4)."
