Evidence¶

Veloo 2016¶

Veloo, A., Md-Ali, R., & Chairany, S. (2016). Using Cooperative Teams-Game-Tournament in 11 Religious School to Improve Mathematics Understanding and Communication. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 13(2), 97-123. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134733

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Mixed between-within subjects ANOVA in a randomized pretest-posttest control group study of 64 Grade 11 students (32 experimental, 32 control) at Madrasah Aliyah, Riau. The article reports "significant interaction between group type and time periods" with Wilks Lambda = .72, F(2,61) = 11.80, p < .05, partial eta squared = 0.28.

"There was significant interaction between group type and time periods, Wilks Lambda = .72, F (2, 61) = 11.80, p < .05, partial eta squared ( λ) = 0.28)."

Veloo 2016 (2)¶

Veloo, A., Md-Ali, R., & Chairany, S. (2016). Using Cooperative Teams-Game-Tournament in 11 Religious School to Improve Mathematics Understanding and Communication. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 13(2), 97-123. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134733

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Between-subject effect test from the same mixed ANOVA on mathematics understanding across the three time periods. The article reports the group main effect as significant with F(1,62) = 11.24, p < .05, partial eta squared = 0.15, described as a big effect size per Cohen (1988).

"The main effect for experimental group and control group was significant, F (1, 62) = 11.24, p < .05, partial eta squared (λ) = 0.15), suggesting a difference in the effectiveness of the cooperative TGT on mathematics understanding."

Veloo 2016 (3)¶

Veloo, A., Md-Ali, R., & Chairany, S. (2016). Using Cooperative Teams-Game-Tournament in 11 Religious School to Improve Mathematics Understanding and Communication. Malaysian Journal of Learning and Instruction, 13(2), 97-123. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1134733

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive means from Table 3 for the post-test 1 mathematics understanding scores: experimental group M = 14.53 (SD = 2.03) versus control group M = 11.09 (SD = 1.91), a printed difference of 3.44 attributed by the authors to the TGT effect.

"Figure 2 shows that the post 1 mathematics understanding score for the experimental group (14.53) is higher than the control group (11.09)."
