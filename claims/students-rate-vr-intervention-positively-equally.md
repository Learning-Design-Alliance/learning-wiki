# Students rated the VR electrostatics intervention positively and equally across treatment groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Average ratings for helpfulness, enjoyability, and likelihood to recommend were positive, near +1 on a -2 to +2 scale, with no significant differences between trained and untrained groups after post-hoc correction. [→ C. D. Porter 2020](#c-d-porter-2020)

## Evidence

### C. D. Porter 2020

C. D. Porter, J. R. H. Smith, E. M. Stagar, A. Simmons, M. Nieberding, C. M. Orban, J. Brown, A. Ayers. (2020). Using Virtual Reality in Electrostatics Instruction: The Impact of Training. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020119)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Student feedback survey on a 5-point scale from -2 to +2 administered after all electrostatics questions in the randomized study, covering helpfulness, enjoyability, and likelihood to recommend. The slight difference in likelihood to recommend was "not signiﬁcant after a post-hoc (Bonferroni) correction"; no effect size printed.

> "In all cases, average scores were positive, very close to +1 (“Helpful”, “Enjoyable”, “Likely”). Figure 9 shows that both groups (trained and untrained) found the VR instruction equally helpful and equally enjoyable."
