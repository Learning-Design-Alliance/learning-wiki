### Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li 2026

Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li. (2026). Investigating students’ gender expression and its relation to sense of belonging in introductory physics courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Semi-structured interview study of 26 introductory physics students. When asked why others might perceive their gender differently, "16 of the 26 interviewees reasoned that others might not know them well on a personal level," citing limited personal knowledge as the source of discrepancies.

> "When asked why others might perceive their gender expression differently than they did, 16 of the 26 interviewees reasoned that others might not know them well on a personal level."

### Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li 2026 (2)

Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li. (2026). Investigating students’ gender expression and its relation to sense of belonging in introductory physics courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

In the same interview study, a second theme emerged: "some students (8/26) also attributed appraisal discrepancies to the feeling that others may have differing definitions" of gender terms, reflecting hesitance to assume others hold non-stereotypical views.

> "In addition to not feeling known by others, some students (8/26) also attributed appraisal discrepancies to the feeling that others may have differing definitions or perceptions of masculinity, femininity, and androgyny than themselves."

### Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li 2026 (3)

Noah Leibnitz and Yangqiuting Li. (2026). Investigating students’ gender expression and its relation to sense of belonging in introductory physics courses. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.17966)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Interview analysis of the same 26 students found that "both factors were particularly pronounced in their physics classes, often more so than in other academic environments," which students attributed to class demographics and pedagogical structure.

> "Notably, some interviewees commented that both factors were particularly pronounced in their physics classes, often more so than in other academic environments."
