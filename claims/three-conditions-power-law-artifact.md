# The power law artifact occurs only when three conditions co-occur: arithmetic averaging, a nonlinear model, and individual differences

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The artifact arises from the combined contributions of arithmetic averaging of data generated from a nonlinear model in the presence of individual differences; if the model is linear, arithmetic averaging does not produce the artifact. [→ Myung et al. 2000](#myung-et-al-2000)

## Evidence

### Myung et al. 2000

In Jae Myung, Cheongtag Kim, and Mark A. Pitt. (2000). Toward an explanation of the power law artifact: Insights from response surface analysis. Memory & Cognition, 28(5), 832-840. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03198418

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Authors' summary of the response surface analysis and simulation results, stating the three necessary ingredients for the artifact. They add that "If the model is linear, arithmetic averaging will not produce the artifact, even in the presence of individual differences."

> "To summarize, we have shown that the power law artifact can occur whenever arithmetic averaging is employed to aggregate data that are generated from a nonlinear model in the presence of individual differences. These are three necessary ingredients for the artifact."
