## Subclaims
`q3 i3` For 51 subjects, hard opposites correlated .40 with information and .19 with vocabulary; easy opposites correlated .37 with information and .16 with vocabulary; information and vocabulary correlated .45. [→ Irving King and Hugo Gold 1911](#irving-king-and-hugo-gold-1911)

## Evidence

### Irving King and Hugo Gold 1911

Irving King and Hugo Gold. (1911). A Tentative Standardization of Certain "Opposites Tests". University of Iowa. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=A+Tentative+Standardization+of+Certain+Opposites+Tests

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Supplementary Pearson correlations from Whipple vocabulary and information blanks (100 test words each) given to 51 of the original subjects. The printed effect sizes are Pearson coefficients: hard opposites with Information 40 (i.e., r = .40), the largest printed, and the authors note "the highest with the range of information"; no close check was made on overestimated knowledge.
