# Using a single question per EBM topic–cognitive level combination resulted in low reliability coefficients for the overall test and its subscales

## Evidence

### Buljan Ivan 2021

Buljan Ivan, Marušić Matko, Tokalić Ružica, Viđak Marin, Peričić Tina Poklepović, Hren Darko, Marušić Ana. (2021). Cognitive levels in testing knowledge in evidence-based medicine: a cross sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02449-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Psychometric analysis of the SPLIT test in the overall sample (N = 203): overall Cronbach's alpha was 0.37 (0.32 first-year, 0.29 third-year), with negative coefficients for Evaluation and Synthesis subscales, "which indicated poor reliability for the subscale results in that population."

> "In some cases, due to the low correlations between the items and the small number of items, the reliability coefficients were negative, which indicated poor reliability for the subscale results in that population."

### Buljan Ivan 2021 (2)

Buljan Ivan, Marušić Matko, Tokalić Ružica, Viđak Marin, Peričić Tina Poklepović, Hren Darko, Marušić Ana. (2021). Cognitive levels in testing knowledge in evidence-based medicine: a cross sectional study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02449-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Reliability analysis by subgroup shows overall test alpha of 0.73 in the expert sample (n = 14), with higher coefficients in the experts' own domains (Internal validity 0.40, Sources of evidence 0.38) and low or negative in non-expert domains such as Clinical importance.

> "The reliability coefficient for the overall test was the highest for the expert population, where coefficients were high across cognitive levels and for EBM topics related to their expertise (e.g., Internal validity and Sources of evidence), and low in domains where they were not experts (e.g., Clinical importance)."
