Evidence¶

Mazzocco 2013¶

Mazzocco, M. M. M., Myers, G. F., Lewis, K. E., Hanich, L. B., & Murphy, M. M. (2013). Limited knowledge of fraction representations differentiates middle school students with mathematics learning disability (dyscalculia) vs. low mathematics achievement. Journal of experimental child psychology, 115(2), 371-387. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2013.01.005

q3 · i3 · associational · r2

A 2 (Item Type) × 5 (Grade) × 3 (MLD Status) repeated-measures ANOVA tested the premise that one-half comparisons are easier. The Item Type main effect was significant with η2p = .333, "with more accurate performance on items that included representations of one-half" than non-half items.

"the predicted main effect emerged for Item Type, Wilks’ Lambda F(1, 119) = 59.41, p < .0001, η2p = .333, with more accurate performance on items that included representations of one-half vs. items that did not (correct responses = 89.2 vs. 83.4%, respectively)."

Mazzocco 2013 (2)¶

Mazzocco, M. M. M., Myers, G. F., Lewis, K. E., Hanich, L. B., & Murphy, M. M. (2013). Limited knowledge of fraction representations differentiates middle school students with mathematics learning disability (dyscalculia) vs. low mathematics achievement. Journal of experimental child psychology, 115(2), 371-387. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2013.01.005

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Post hoc comparisons of the significant three-way interaction (Wilks’ Lambda F(8, 232) = 3.45, p = .001, η2p = .106) showed the one-half advantage appears by Grade 4 for TA and Grade 5 for LA, but "for the MLD group the one-half advantage does not emerge until Grades 7 and 8".

"for the MLD group the one-half advantage does not emerge until Grades 7 and 8 (ps < .005), and is quite absent prior to then (ps range from .134 to .689)."
