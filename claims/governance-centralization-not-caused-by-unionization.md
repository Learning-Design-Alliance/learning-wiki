# Increasing explicitness and centralization of university governance is driven by fiscal stringency and other underlying factors, not by unionization

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r2` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The tendency toward more explicit, centralized governance occurs with and without collective bargaining and reflects fiscal and cultural factors rather than trade unionism. [→ Bloustein 1973](#bloustein-1973)

## Evidence

### Bloustein 1973

Bloustein, Edward J. (1973). Collective Bargaining and University Governance. Speech presented at the annual meeting of the Association of American Colleges. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED074928

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author's interpretive argument from his experience at two institutions, one without and one with a bargaining unit: boards scrutinized budgets at Bennington without a union and at Rutgers with one, so the tendency, he argues, is "not a product of trade unionism".

> "The fiscal stringencies of our time have led academic governing boards to look more closely at the budgets and the operations of educational institutions and, for the first time, to ask, "what is happening here?""
