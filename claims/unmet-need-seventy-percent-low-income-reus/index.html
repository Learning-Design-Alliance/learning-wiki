Evidence¶

Haycock 2010¶

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Ed Trust analysis of NPSAS:04 and NPSAS:08 data (Figure 12) shows bottom-quintile mean income fell from $17,576 to $16,685 while the share of income needed for remaining costs rose from 64% to 70%.

"students in public research-extensive universities who come from low-income fami-lies must find a way to finance an average of more than $10,000 per year in remaining college costs—an amount equivalent to a whopping 70 percent of the annual income of a typical family in this quintile, up from 64 percent four years ago"

Haycock 2010 (2)¶

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The 2007 unmet-need analysis by income quintile (Figure 13) reports top-quintile grant aid of $2,025 and EFC of $37,424 against a $21,961 price of attendance, yielding the $17,488 overmet need.

"families earning more than $115,400 actually have a combined average of $17,488 in overmet need, meaning that grant aid, together with the "Expected Family Contribution" (calculated by the federal government), total to more than the cost of attending a public research-extensive university"
