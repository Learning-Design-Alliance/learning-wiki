## Evidence

### Zwolak 2015

Zwolak, J. P. and Manogue, C. A. (2015). Assessing student reasoning in upper-division electricity and magnetism at Oregon State University. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Question-by-question comparison of OSU (N = 37) and CU (N = 103) CUE post-test averages (Fig. 2). Both questions test setting up partial-differential-equation solutions; the authors link the gaps to OSU teaching separation of variables mainly in quantum contexts and finding "positive transfers of skills across context and content to be rare".

> "In particular, OSU students’ scores diﬀer by over 50% on question Q1 regarding ﬁnding the potential V (or ﬁeld E) inside an insulating sphere and by almost 40% on question Q15 regarding selecting boundary conditions to solve for the potential V (r,θ ) on a charged spherical surface"

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Per-question comparison from the Figure 2 analysis of CUE results at OSU versus CU; the article reports score differences of "over 50%" on Q1 and "almost 40%" on Q15, both questions testing setup of partial-differential-equation problems. No effect size or test statistic is printed.
