## Evidence

### Haavisto 2025

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the within-subjects classroom experiment, post-test retrieval with materials available showed no significant modality difference; the article notes "the minimal effect size indicates no practical difference in retrieval between modalities (R2mar = .003), with a slight trend favoring videos."

> "Post-test transfer tasks showed no significant differences. Interestingly, videos performed no worse than texts in retrieval from materials, contrary to Hypothesis 2, which posited illustrated texts would facilitate easier location of relevant information."

### Haavisto 2025 (2)

Haavisto, M., Lepola, J., & Jaakkola, T. (2025). The “simple” view of learning from illustrated texts and videos. Learning and Instruction, 100, 102200. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102200

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Open-ended transfer questions in the post-test, answered with materials available but without direct answers in the materials, showed "no significant differences" between the video and illustrated text conditions in the mixed-effects analysis.

> "Post-test transfer tasks showed no significant differences. Interestingly, videos performed no worse than texts in retrieval from materials, contrary to Hypothesis 2, which posited illustrated texts would facilitate easier location of relevant information."
