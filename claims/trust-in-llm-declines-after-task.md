# Self-rated trust in LLM responses declined significantly from pre-test to post-test after learners completed a problem-solving task with the LLM, in one group with no comparison group

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In one group with no comparison group, self-reported trust in LLM responses showed no significant change from the initial measurement to after guidance but declined significantly by the post-experience measurement (Mean 4.36 to 3.49). [→ Harsh Kumar 2024](#harsh-kumar-2024)

## Evidence

### Harsh Kumar 2024

Harsh Kumar, Ilya Musabirov, Mohi Reza, Jiakai Shi, Xinyuan Wang, Joseph Jay Williams, Anastasia Kuzminykh, and Michael Liut. 2024. Impact of Guidance and Interaction Strategies for LLM Use on Learner Performance and Perception. Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. 8, CSCW2, Article 499 (November 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3687038

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same Prolific experiment (N=356); trust measured at three interaction stages. The authors report "a significant decline in final post-experience trust to LLM measure" and suggest participants experienced untrustworthy advice from the LLM.

> "we observed significant differences in reported trust in LLM responses (𝜒 2Friedman(2) = 80.31, 𝑛pairs = 356, 𝑝 < 0.001). While the growth between initial trust ( Mean = 4.36, Me = 4, SD = 1.36), immediately after getting guidance (Mean = 4.35, Me = 5, SD = 1.89) is not significant, there is a significant decline in final post-experience trust to LLM measure (Mean = 3.49, Me = 3, SD = 1.80)"
