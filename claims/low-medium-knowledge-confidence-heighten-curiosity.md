## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In an online blurred-picture experiment, both Not Sure and No responses on Knowledge Confidence positively predicted curiosity ratings compared with Yes responses, contrary to the information-gap prediction that curiosity peaks only at intermediate confidence. [→ Xiaoyun Chen 2024](#xiaoyun-chen-2024)

## Evidence

### Xiaoyun Chen 2024

Xiaoyun Chen, Katherine E. Twomey, Miranda Hayes, Gert Westermann. (2024). The limits of curiosity? New evidence for the roles of metacognitive abilities and curiosity in learning. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09407-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear mixed-effects model of 6000 trials from 100 adults rating 60 blurred images, with participant and stimulus as random effects. The article reports "Both Not Sure (β = 0.12, SE = 0.04, p =.001) and No ( β = 0.11, SE = 0.05, p =.041) responses positively predicted" curiosity relative to Yes; full-null comparison χ2 = 20.30, df = 3, p <.001, R2 = 0.40.
