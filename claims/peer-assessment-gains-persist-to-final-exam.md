## Evidence

### Sun DL 2015

Sun DL, Harris N, Walther G, Baiocchi M (2015) Peer Assessment Enhances Student Learning: The Results of a Matched Randomized Crossover Experiment in a College Statistics Class. PLoS ONE 10(12): e0143177. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0143177

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Final exam analysis in the same crossover RCT, 320 students analyzed, with blinded instructor grading of free-response questions. The article reports "d = .122, t(319) = 3.03, p = .001" for peer-assessment units.

> "Students participating in peer assessment also did better on the corresponding questions on the final exam (d = .122, t(319) = 3.03, p = .001), which suggests that the benefits of peer assessment persist over time."
