## Evidence

### Carr Sandra E. 2016

Carr Sandra E., Brand Gabrielle, Wei Li, Wright Helen, Nicol Pam, Metcalfe Helene, Saunders Julie, Payne John, Seubert Liza, Foley Laurie. (2016). “Helping someone with a skill sharpens it in your own mind”: a mixed method study exploring health professions students experiences of Peer Assisted Learning (PAL). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0566-8

`q2 · i3` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon signed rank test comparing medicine peer learners' pre/post confidence in paediatric examination skills; the article reports the effect “can be considered “large”, r = 0.84” with p = 0.001 (two-tailed).

> "PAL had significantly greater relative influence on clinical examination skills amongst paediatric (medicine) students after peer tutoring, T = 131.00, z = -3.345 (corrected for ties), N – Ties = 16, p = 0.001 (two-tailed), and this effect can be considered “large”, r = 0.84"
