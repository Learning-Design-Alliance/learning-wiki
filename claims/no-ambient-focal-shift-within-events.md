## Evidence

### Lee Madison Mason 2025

Lee Madison Mason, Vatral Caleb, Cohn Clayton, Davalos Eduardo, Jessee Mary Ann, Biswas Gautam, Levin Daniel T. (2025). Eye movements as predictors of student experiences during nursing simulation learning events. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-025-00640-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Events were divided into three equal parts (beginning, middle, end) and gaze metrics compared with data clustered by participant; descriptive statistics appear in Table 3. Equivalence was not tested, only non-significance.

> "However, our results showed no significant differences in mean fixation duration, saccade amplitude, or pupil diameter between the different event phases, indicating consistency in gaze behavior throughout participant-defined events."

### Lee Madison Mason 2025 (2)

Lee Madison Mason, Vatral Caleb, Cohn Clayton, Davalos Eduardo, Jessee Mary Ann, Biswas Gautam, Levin Daniel T. (2025). Eye movements as predictors of student experiences during nursing simulation learning events. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-025-00640-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Test of the ambient-to-focal processing pattern reported by Eisenberg and Zacks (2016) in these first-person clinical stimuli; the pattern did not replicate. The authors attribute the divergence to conceptual rather than perceptual event boundaries in clinical footage.

> "We found no significant difference between the first five seconds and second five seconds of an event (fixation duration: b = − 33.28, t(611) = − 1.34, p = 0.182; saccade amplitude: b = -0.464, t(617) = − 1.70, p = 0.09; pupil diameter: b = − 0.00, t(611) = − 0.123, p = 0.902)."
