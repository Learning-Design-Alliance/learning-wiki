## Evidence

### Pers Michał 2026

Pers Michał, Kupis Robert, Kononowicz Andrzej A., Cebula Grzegorz, Szopa Magdalena. (2026). Linear virtual patients versus student role-plays in teaching patient-centeredness: a mixed-method study. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.6a0d.902f

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized mixed-methods study of 122 third-year medical students allocated by coin toss to linear virtual patients or role-play; Mann–Whitney U tests on PPOS scores were non-significant in both rounds, with "small effects for all comparisons (|Δ| < 0.30)".

> "No statistically significant differences were observed between the experimental and control groups in the total score of the Patient–Practitioner Orientation Scale (PPOS) or its subscales (see Table 2)."

### Pers Michał 2026 (2)

Pers Michał, Kupis Robert, Kononowicz Andrzej A., Cebula Grzegorz, Szopa Magdalena. (2026). Linear virtual patients versus student role-plays in teaching patient-centeredness: a mixed-method study. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.6a0d.902f

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Schuirmann TOST procedure with margin Δ = 0.5 on PPOS scores from 109 post-intervention questionnaires; all 90% CIs fell within equivalence bounds, indicating "statistical equivalence between the two teaching methods both before and after the intervention".

> "For all PPOS dimensions, the 90% confidence intervals (CIs) for the mean differences between the experimental and control groups were entirely contained within the predefined equivalence bounds of −0.5 to +0.5."
