Femke van der Wilt 2019¶

Femke van der Wilt, Chiel van der Veen, Claudia van Kruistum, Bert van Oers (2019). Why Do Children Become Rejected by Their Peers? A Review of Studies into the Relationship Between Oral Communicative Competence and Sociometric Status in Childhood. Educational Psychology Review (2019) 31:699–724. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09479-z

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review reports, from van der Wilt et al. (2016) (N = 54 children aged 3.83–6.25, Nijmegen Test for Pragmatics, correlation analyses, cross-sectional design), that competence "was associated with peer acceptance (although only for boys), but not with peer rejection." No effect size is printed.

Related Claims¶

  • Rejected and neglected children show lower oral communicative competence than average children, while popular and controversial children do not differ from average
  • Children's oral communicative competence is significantly related to their sociometric status in childhood — related
  • Children with poorer oral communicative competence experience higher levels of peer rejection after controlling for gender, age, and SES — related
  • Rejected and disliked children communicate less responsively and less coherently than popular or liked peers — related
  • Gender moderates the link between educational processes and children's social–emotional competence, with stronger associations for girls than boys — related
  • The relation between oral communicative competence and sociometric status is expected to be interactional and transactional rather than unidirectional — related
