## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 83 randomised comparisons, swapping other teaching for video significantly improved learning with a small pooled effect (g = 0.28), with about half of true effects helpful and 19% potentially unhelpful. [→ Noetel 2021](#noetel-2021)

## Evidence

### Noetel 2021

Noetel, M., Griffith, S., Delaney, O., Sanders, T., Parker, P., del Pozo Cruz, B., & Lonsdale, C. (2021). Video Improves Learning in Higher Education: A Systematic Review. Review of Educational Research, 91(2), 204–236. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654321990713

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Multilevel random-effects meta-analysis of 83 randomised comparisons (pooled n = 166 effect sizes) swapping video for existing teaching. The review reports "a significant positive effect on student learning (g = 0.28, 95% CI [0.14, 0.42])" with substantial unexplained heterogeneity (I²₂ = 0.32, I²₃ = 0.55).

> "Overall, replacing other teaching with video had a significant positive effect on student learning (g = 0.28, 95% CI [0.14, 0.42], n = 166, k = 83)."
