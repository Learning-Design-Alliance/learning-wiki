## Evidence

### Bridges 1920

Bridges, J. W. (1920). The correlation between college grades and the Alpha intelligence tests. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 11(7), 361-369. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071462

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlations computed separately for 106 Arts, 87 Engineering and 70 Agriculture students in the 436-case sample (Table IV prints r = .54 Agriculture, r = .27 Arts, r = .22 Engineering); subtests such as Test 4 gave zero correlation for Engineering students.

> "the examination as a whole gives a much higher correlation with academic standing in the college of Agriculture than in either of the other colleges."

### Bridges 1920 (2)

Bridges, J. W. (1920). The correlation between college grades and the Alpha intelligence tests. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 11(7), 361-369. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0071462

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A second analysis obtained point-hour ratios for all senior students in the colleges of Arts, Agriculture, Engineering and Education and recomputed correlations with Alpha and each subtest; several values shifted, with the tests 2, 4 and 6 findings confirmed. No effect size is printed for the corroboration itself.

> "They vary in some respects from those at first obtained; but the findings regarding tests 2, 4 and 6 are corroborated."
