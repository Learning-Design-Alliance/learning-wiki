Evidence¶

K. Paige Harden 2020¶

K. Paige Harden, Benjamin W. Domingue, Daniel W. Belsky, Jason D. Boardman, Robert Crosnoe, Margherita Malanchini, Michel Nivard, Elliot M. Tucker-Drob and Kathleen Mullan Harris. (2020). Genetic associations with mathematics tracking and persistence in secondary school. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-020-0060-2

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Poisson interaction model (Supplementary Table 4) of advancing steps on education-PGS by school status, reported in Results. The interaction was similar when including 9th-grade tracking as a covariate (b = −0.282). Figure 4b shows high-PGS students "persisted about equally" regardless of school status, while average-PGS students in low-SES schools completed 1.6 advancing steps.

"There was a signi ﬁcant and negative interaction on mathematics persistence, such that low-PGS students were less likely to drop out of math if attending high-status schools as compared to low- status schools ( b = −0.304, SE = 0.074, 95% CI = [−0.443, −0.147];"
