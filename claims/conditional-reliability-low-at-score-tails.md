# Reliability of state test scores is highest near cut-scores or the mean and much lower for very high- or low-performing students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Conditional reliability follows a downward curve as scores move away from the average or cut-scores, so reliability for very high or very low scores can be much worse. [→ May 2009](#may-2009)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Psychometric discussion in the reliability section, citing Lord and Novick and Hambleton and Swaminathan. The paper notes conditional reliabilities are rarely published in technical manuals, so researchers should consult the state assessment office about whether the test is unusually hard or easy for the target population.

> "For most assessments, reliability is maximized near the average score on norm-referenced tests or near performance cut-scores on criterion referenced tests, with a downward curve as scores move away from the average or cut-scores"
