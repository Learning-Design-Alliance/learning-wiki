# Medical students' perceived usefulness of online learning is positively correlated with their familiarity with online learning modes, with a medium familiarity difference between PU groups

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students' PU correlated significantly and positively with familiarity with all six online learning modes, and students who found online learning useful were more familiar with it (Cohen's d=0.53). [→ Wang Cixiao 2020](#wang-cixiao-2020)

## Evidence

### Wang Cixiao 2020

Wang Cixiao, Xie A’na, Wang Weimin, Wu Hongbin. (2020). Association between medical students’ prior experiences and perceptions of formal online education developed in response to COVID-19: a cross-sectional study in China. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-041886

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional survey of 99,559 Chinese undergraduate medical students across 90 medical schools, analysed with Pearson correlations and an independent samples t-test comparing familiarity across PU subgroups. The article reports "mean difference=1.58, p<0.01, Cohen’s d=0.53".

> "in terms of students’ familiarity with online learning under different levels of PU, there was a significant difference between the ‘useful’ and ‘not-so-useful’ subgroups (mean difference=1.58, p<0.01, Cohen’s d=0.53). Students who agreed that online modes were useful were more familiar with online learning (mean=6.79, 95% CI 6.76 to 6.82), whereas those students who perceived online learning as not so useful were less familiar with online modes (mean=5.21, 95% CI 5.18 to 5.23)."
