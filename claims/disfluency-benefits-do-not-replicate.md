# The learning benefits of perceptual disfluency reported by Diemand-Yauman et al. (2011) do not replicate in any general way

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across six studies in the special issue, the dramatic benefits of disfluency on educational outcomes reported by Diemand-Yauman et al. (2011) failed to replicate in any general way. [→ Bjork 2016](#bjork-2016)

## Evidence

### Bjork 2016

Bjork, R. A., & Yue, C. L. (2016). Commentary: Is disfluency desirable? Metacognition Learning, 11, 133–137. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-016-9156-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The authors' synthesis of the six special-issue studies, which collectively examined disfluency effects on metacognitive judgments, reading time, recall, and comprehension across varied materials and media. The commentary reports that the original findings "do not replicate, at least not in any general way."

> "The conclusion from the impressive body of research reported in this special issue seems inescapable: The remarkable and intriguing benefits of disfluency reported by Diemand-Yauman et al. do not replicate, at least not in any general way."
