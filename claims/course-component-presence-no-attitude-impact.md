## Evidence

### Adam S. Trotter 2019

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Baseline regression on 749 responses with each course component included as a binary independent variable (-1 = not used, +1 = used). Components included videos, e-books, homework, e-polling, textbook, office hours, online forums, Skynet labs, other telescope labs, and non-telescope labs; none significantly impacted attitudes.

> "We found that the merepresence of any of these individual course components (included in the lin- ear regression analysis as binary independent vari- ables where -1 = not used in course and +1 = used in course) did not signiﬁcantly impact the students’ at- titudes about science."

### Adam S. Trotter 2019 (2)

Adam S. Trotter, Daniel E. Reichart, Aaron P. LaCluyzé & Rachel Freed. (2019). Factors Contributing to Attitudinal Gains in Introductory Astronomy Courses. Robotic Telescopes, Student Research and Education (RTSRE) Proceedings, Vol. 1, No. 1. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.32374/rtsre.2019.014](https://doi.org/10.32374/rtsre.2019.014)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subset regressions for each remaining component on the 749-respondent sample, adding that component's Helpfulness Index as an independent variable. For components other than in-class lectures and Skynet-based labs, neither presence nor rated helpfulness impacted the Attitude Index; equivalence was not tested.

> "For the other course components, neither the existence of the component nor how helpful the students found it to be impacted the Attitude Index."
