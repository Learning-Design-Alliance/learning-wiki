# A superordinate structuring term such as “bouquet” increases use of the factorization algorithm in isomorphic multiplication problems

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 5th graders, problems without a structuring term were less often solved by factorization (44%) than identical problems mentioning a bouquet (68%), because the term evokes world semantics of grouping flowers into one set. [→ Hippolyte Gros 2020](#hippolyte-gros-2020)

## Evidence

### Hippolyte Gros 2020

Hippolyte Gros, Jean-Pierre Thibaut, Emmanuel Sander. (2020). Semantic Congruence in Arithmetic: A New Conceptual Model for Word Problem Solving. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2019.1691004

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The article reports, citing Coquin-Viennot and Moreau (2003), a study of 3rd and 5th graders using factorization and expansion algorithms on florist problems. Problems without the structuring term were solved by factorization by "44% among 5th graders"; no effect size is printed.

> "were less often solved using factorization (44% among 5th graders) than problems identical in every aspect except for the presence of a superordinate structuring term such as “a bouquet”"

### Hippolyte Gros 2020 (2)

Hippolyte Gros, Jean-Pierre Thibaut, Emmanuel Sander. (2020). Semantic Congruence in Arithmetic: A New Conceptual Model for Word Problem Solving. Educational Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2019.1691004

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article reports, citing Coquin-Viennot and Moreau (2003), that the bouquet-worded version of the same problem was solved by factorization by "68% among 5th graders". The percentages are descriptive; no statistical test or effect size is printed.

> "“For a prize-giving, the florist prepares for each of the 14 candidates a bouquet made up of 5 roses and 7 tulips.” (68% among 5th graders)."
