## Evidence

### Lura Forcum and William Harms 2005

Lura Forcum and William Harms. (2005). New report shows that high-quality induction can help keep teachers on the job. University of Chicago press release announcing Keeping New Teachers: A First Look at the Influence of Induction in the Chicago Public Schools. https://consortium.uchicago.edu

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Survey-based analysis of new CPS teachers in the 2004-05 school year comparing strong versus weak mentoring levels on plans to remain. The study reports a 25 percentage point difference for elementary novices, described as "25 percentage points more likely to plan to remain in the same school."

> "Novice elementary school teachers who received strong levels of mentoring were 25 percentage points more likely to plan to remain in the same school compared to teachers who received weak levels of mentoring. The relationship was similar for high school teachers, although not statistically significant."
