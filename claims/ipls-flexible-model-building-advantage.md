## Evidence

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Rubric-coded written responses on part (b) of the end-of-semester fluids task, comparing IPLS (N = 61) and standard-course (N = 34) life science students: "46% of IPLS students (N = 28) combined two models together to solve problem 1b, only a single non-IPLS student did".

> "While 46% of IPLS students (N = 28) combined two models together to solve problem 1b, only a single non-IPLS student did, a difference which is highly significant (p = 5 x 10-6, Fisher's exact test)."

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (2)

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Wilcoxon comparison of total rubric scores on part (b) of the fluids task: "higher overall rubric scores (D(median) = 2, p << 0.0001)".

> "Overall, as shown in Fig. 6a, IPLS students were much more successful in solving problem 1b, as indicated by higher overall rubric scores (D(median) = 2, p << 0.0001)."

### Benjamin D. Geller 2022 (3)

Benjamin D. Geller, Maya Tipton, Brandon Daniel-Morales, Nikhil Tignor, Calvin White, and Catherine H. Crouch (2022). Assessing the impact of Introductory Physics for the Life Sciences on students' ability to build complex models. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010131)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r?`

Effect-size reporting for the same written-task comparison on fluids part (b): "an effect size (Cliff's delta) of 0.70 for the total scores and 0.74 for the modeling decision scores".

> "This difference corresponds to an effect size (Cliff's delta) of 0.70 for the total scores and 0.74 for the modeling decision scores."
