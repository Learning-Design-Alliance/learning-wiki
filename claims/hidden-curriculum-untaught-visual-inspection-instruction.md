## Evidence

### Fong Raymond 2026

Fong Raymond, Kwan Connie C. Y. (2026). Accuracy of Visual Inspection of Thickened Liquids: Implications for Multidisciplinary Dysphagia Management and Clinical Nutrition. Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics. https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.70294

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Student survey items on teaching of visual inspection during clinical practicums (n = 50 students). The study found "84.0% (42/50)" were asked to inspect visually while "50% (25/50)" were never taught how, which the authors call a potential hidden-curriculum problem in clinical education. No effect size is printed.

> "From the results, 84.0% (42/50) of students reported being explicitly asked by their clinical educators to visually inspect liquid thickness, but 50% (25/50) reported they were never taught how to perform this inspection during the practicum."

### Fong Raymond 2026 (2)

Fong Raymond, Kwan Connie C. Y. (2026). Accuracy of Visual Inspection of Thickened Liquids: Implications for Multidisciplinary Dysphagia Management and Clinical Nutrition. Journal of Human Nutrition and Dietetics. https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.70294

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Self-rating survey item compared against actual total accuracy score in the same student cohort. Despite low overall accuracy, 70% of students rated visual inspection as moderately accurate, and perceived accuracy was uncorrelated with actual score (r = −0.076), indicating poor metacognitive insight.

> "When asked to rate the perceived accuracy on a scale of 0–5, 70% of students rated it as moderately accurate (3 out of 5 stars). A Spearman correlation revealed a lack of correlation between a student's perceived accuracy of the method and their actual total score (r = −0.076)."
