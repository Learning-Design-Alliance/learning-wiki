## Evidence

### Balakrishnan Athira 2021

Balakrishnan Athira, Nair Sreedharan, Kunhikatta Vijayanarayana, Rashid Muhammed, Unnikrishnan M. K., Jagannatha P. S., Chandran Viji P., Khera Kanav, Thunga Girish. (2021). Effectiveness of blended learning in pharmacy education: An experimental study using clinical research modules. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256814

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Feedback survey among the 92 BL students after module completion; percentages as printed in the Results section. The authors note these findings align with other studies reporting positive student perceptions of BL in pharmacy education.

> "Students of BL were asked their preference for learning strategies and found that 86.5%of students prefer BL, 2.7% and 9.5% preferred DT and WEL, respectively."

### Balakrishnan Athira 2021 (2)

Balakrishnan Athira, Nair Sreedharan, Kunhikatta Vijayanarayana, Rashid Muhammed, Unnikrishnan M. K., Jagannatha P. S., Chandran Viji P., Khera Kanav, Thunga Girish. (2021). Effectiveness of blended learning in pharmacy education: An experimental study using clinical research modules. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256814

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Mann-Whitney tests on the 12-question feedback form comparing BL and WEL students' ratings of the shared website (Table 5); all six attitude items had p>0.05, e.g. design of modules p = 0.252.

> "It was found that there was no statistically significant difference(p>0.05) in opinions regarding design of module, clarity of explanation, comprehensive coverage of subject matter, and relevancy of hyperlinks between the WEL and BL groups who used the website for learning."
