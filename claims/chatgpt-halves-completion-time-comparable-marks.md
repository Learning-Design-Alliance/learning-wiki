## Evidence

### Hapnes Toba 2024

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled experiment mid-test: nine students without ChatGPT versus eight with ChatGPT on four Python tasks, completion time and marks compared via unpaired t-test. The ChatGPT group's time was "around half than that of students without ChatGPT" (p<0.001); overall marks were 77.75 vs 77.80.

> "students with ChatGPT generally had shorter completion time (around half than that of students without ChatGPT) and the difference is statistically significant according to a two-tailed unpaired t-test with a 95% confidence rate (p<0.001)"

### Hapnes Toba 2024 (2)

Hapnes Toba, Oscar Karnalim, Meliana Christianti Johan, Terutoshi Tada, Yenni Merlin Djajalaksana, and Tristan Vivaldy. (2024). Inappropriate Benefits and Identification of ChatGPT Misuse in Programming Tests: A Controlled Experiment. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-51979-6_54)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Final-test comparison in the same controlled experiment (four tasks covering all course concepts): ChatGPT group averaged 46.67 minutes versus 104.19, "two times faster than those without ChatGPT (p<0.001)", with overall marks 73.00 versus 69.63 and all mark differences insignificant.

> "Students with ChatGPT completed the tasks two times faster than those without ChatGPT (p<0.001) but they had comparable programming performance."
