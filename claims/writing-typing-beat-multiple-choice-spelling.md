## Subclaims
`q3 i?` On the post-test and delayed post-test, pupils who practiced by handwriting or typewriting achieved higher spelling scores than pupils who practiced by multiple choice. [→ van Hove 2017](#van-hove-2017)

## Evidence

### van Hove 2017

van Hove, S., vanderhoven, E., & Cornillie, F. (2017). The tablet for Second Language Vocabulary Learning: Keyboard, Stylus or Multiple Choice. Comunicar, 50, XXV. https://doi.org/10.3916/C50-2017-05

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two (practicing modality) by three (time) repeated-measures ANOVA on spelling scores found a main effect of practicing modality (F(2,180)=9.99, p=.000) and a time × modality interaction (F(3.08, 277.24)=4.57, p=.004). Post-hoc comparisons showed handwriting (76.51%) and typewriting (81.04%) conditions outperformed multiple choice (67.43%) on the post-test.

> "learning new French vocabulary by handwriting or typewriting led to higher spelling scores based on post-hoc pairwise comparisons on the post-test (p≤.005) and on the delayed posttest (p≤.015) in comparison with completing multiple-choice exercises during the intervention"
