## Evidence

### Otis 1918

Otis, A. S. (1918). An absolute point scale for the group measurements of intelligence. Part I. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 9(5), 239-261. https://books.google.com/books/about/the_journal_of_educational_psychology.html

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Reliability analysis of the main data from 121 pupils tested in duplicate forms of ten tests. Probable errors were derived from median differences between corresponding scores on Test I and Test II, and reliability coefficients from the difference formula; the author reports being "quite surprised to find the Spelling Test to be so much in the lead in this rating" and attributes it partly to test length.

> "The reliability coefficients thus found for each test are shown in the third column of Table III. We were quite surprised to find the Spelling Test to be so much in the lead in this rating. However, the Spelling, Narrative Completion, and Synonym Tests had 50 elements while the other tests had only 25 or less."
