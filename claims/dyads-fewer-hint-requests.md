## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Hausmann 2006

Hausmann, R. G. M., van de Sande, B., & VanLehn, K. (2006). Are Self-explaining and Coached Problem Solving More Effective When Done by Pairs of Students Than Alone? Pittsburgh Science of Learning Center and the Learning Research and Development Center, University of Pittsburgh. https://www.andes.pitt.edu/

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized individuals-versus-dyads comparison, individuals requested "M = 2.26, SD = 1.52" overall hints per correct entry versus .99 for dyads, F(1, 23) = 7.20, p = .01, d = 1.13 (Fig. 2, left side).

> "the individuals (M = 2.26, SD = 1.52) requested more overall hints from the Andes tutoring system than the dyads  (M = .99, SD = .82). The differential use of the help system  was statistically reliable, with a large effect size (see the left side of Fig. 2) ,  F(1, 23) = 7.20, p = .01, d = 1.13."

### Hausmann 2006 (2)

Hausmann, R. G. M., van de Sande, B., & VanLehn, K. (2006). Are Self-explaining and Coached Problem Solving More Effective When Done by Pairs of Students Than Alone? Pittsburgh Science of Learning Center and the Learning Research and Development Center, University of Pittsburgh. https://www.andes.pitt.edu/

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Bottom-out hint requests per correct entry were higher for individuals ("M = .34, SD = .40") than dyads (M = .10), F(1, 23) = 4.71, p = .04, d = .91 (Fig. 2, right side). The authors take this as evidence dyads needed less help from the tutoring system.

> "The individuals ( M = .34, SD = .40) requested more bottom -out hints per correct entry than the dyads (M = .10, SD = .14). This difference was statistically reliable, with a large effect size (see the right side of Fig. 2),  F(1, 23) = 4.71, p = .04, d = .91."
