# Sleep quality index correlates negatively with Mediterranean diet adherence, self-efficacy and average academic grade

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Higher sleep quality index scores (poorer sleep) correlate with lower Mediterranean diet adherence (r = −0.28), lower self-efficacy (r = −0.28) and lower average academic grade (r = −0.18). [→ Fernández-Medina 2020](#fernandez-medina-2020)

## Evidence

### Fernández-Medina 2020

Fernández-Medina, I. M., Ruíz-Fernández, M. D., Hernández-Padilla, J. M., Granero-Molina, J., Fernández-Sola, C., Jiménez-Lasserrotte, M. d. M., Lirola, M.-J., Cortés-Rodríguez, A. E., & López-Rodríguez, M. M. (2020). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet and Self-efficacy as Mediators in the Mediation of Sleep Quality and Grades in Nursing Students. Nutrients, 12(11), 3265. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12113265

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis in a cross-sectional survey of 334 nursing students. The article reports significant negative correlations of the sleep quality index with diet adherence (r = −0.28), self-efficacy (r = −0.28) and academic record (r = −0.18).

> "The correlations are significant and negative between the sleep quality index and adherence to the Mediterranean diet (r = −0.28; p ˂ 0.05), self-efficacy (r = −0.28; p ˂ 0.01) and the average score of the academic record (r = −0.18; p ˂ 0.01)."
