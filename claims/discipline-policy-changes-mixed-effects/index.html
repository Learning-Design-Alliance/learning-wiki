Evidence¶

Sartain 2014¶

Sartain, L., Allensworth, E., & Porter, S. (2014). Suspending Chicago's Students: Differences in Discipline across Schools. UChicago Consortium. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/suspending-chicagos-students-differences-discipline-practices-across-schools

q2 · i? · associational · r?

Observational analysis of district policy changes in Chicago. The report found shortening suspension length was "associated with improved attendance but worse school climate", with effects concentrated in high-suspending schools.

"Shortening the length of suspensions, which mostly affected schools with the highest suspension rates, was associated with improved attendance but worse school climate."

Sartain 2014 (2)¶

Sartain, L., Allensworth, E., & Porter, S. (2014). Suspending Chicago's Students: Differences in Discipline across Schools. UChicago Consortium. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/suspending-chicagos-students-differences-discipline-practices-across-schools

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Observational analysis of restorative justice practices alongside suspensions. The report found a more positive climate association in lower-suspending schools, suggesting implementation is harder where disciplinary problems are greatest.

"Restorative practices that accompany suspensions have a more positive association with climate in schools with lower suspension rates, suggesting that policy solutions are particularly difficult to implement effectively in schools with the most disciplinary problems."
