# Age correlates with detection performance: hits and d′ increase and false alarms decrease with age

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Percentage of hits and d′ were positively correlated with age, and percentage of false alarms was negatively correlated with age. [→ Thillay A 2015](#thillay-a-2015)

## Evidence

### Thillay A 2015

Thillay A, Roux S, Gissot V, Carteau-Martin I, Knight RT, Bonnet-Brilhault F and Bidet-Caulet A (2015) Sustained attention and prediction: distinct brain maturation trajectories during adolescence. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 9:519. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00519

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analyses across the 36 participants related behavioral sustained-attention measures to age. The study reports "r = 0.502, b = 0.119, p = 0.002" for d′ with age and "r =−0.401, b =−0.072, p = 0.015" for false alarms, supporting continued maturation of sustained attention after age 12.

> "The % of hits to the randTs and d’ were positively correlated with age ( r = 0.393, b = 0.869, p = 0.018, or r = 0.502, b = 0.119, p = 0.002; respectively). The % of FAs was negatively correlated with age ( r =−0.401, b =−0.072, p = 0.015)."
