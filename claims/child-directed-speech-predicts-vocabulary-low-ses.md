# Amount of child-directed speech at 19 months predicts expressive vocabulary size at 24 months in low-SES Spanish-learning infants

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Children who heard more child-directed speech at 19 months had larger expressive vocabularies at 24 months (r = 0.57), even controlling for infant vocalizations (r = 0.51). [→ Weisleder Adriana 2013](#weisleder-adriana-2013)

## Evidence

### Weisleder Adriana 2013

Weisleder Adriana, Fernald Anne. (2013). Talking to children matters: Early language experience strengthens processing and builds vocabulary. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613488145

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of 29 low-SES Spanish-learning infants using all-day LENA home recordings at 19 months and parent-reported Spanish MCDI vocabulary at 24 months. The relation "remained robust (r = 0.51, p < 0.01)" after controlling for infant vocalizations at 19 months.

> "Those children who heard more child-directed speech at 19 months had larger vocabularies at 24 months (r = 0.57, p < 0.01)"
