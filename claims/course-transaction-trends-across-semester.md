# Course add and drop activity concentrates early in enrollment phases, with drops outnumbering adds from mid-to-late Phase 2 onward and basket size never substantially decreasing

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Approximately 52.4% of spring semester add-drop activities occur during Phase 1 (the first 5 weeks), while in fall this proportion is 40.0% spanning the first 10 weeks. [→ Borchers 2025](#borchers-2025)
`q2 i?` Drop activities consistently outnumber add activities from mid to late Phase 2 onward, yet average basket size shows no substantial decrease across the enrollment period. [→ Borchers 2025 (2)](#borchers-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Borchers 2025

Borchers, C., Xu, Y., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). Workload Overload? Late Enrollment Leads to Course Dropout. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 1. https://github.com/CAHLR/enrollment-procrastination-edm

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive time-series analysis of average weekly waitlist, enrollment, drop, and swap activity across spring and fall semesters in the UC Berkeley transaction data (RQ1). Percentages of add-drop activity per phase are as printed; patterns were visually consistent across years one through four.

> "approximately 52.4% of spring semester add-drop activities occur during Phase 1, covering roughly the first 25% (first 5 weeks) of enrollment phases, while in fall, this proportion is 40.0%, spanning the initial 50% (first 10 weeks)."

### Borchers 2025 (2)

Borchers, C., Xu, Y., & Pardos, Z. A. (2025). Workload Overload? Late Enrollment Leads to Course Dropout. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 1. https://github.com/CAHLR/enrollment-procrastination-edm

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of daily student basket size (number of enrolled courses) across the semester, shown in Figure 3. The article reports the rise in dropping actions does not counterbalance enrollment actions, with basket size stabilizing during the add-drop period.

> "despite drop activities surpassing add activities at mid-to-late Phase 2, we observe no substantial decreases in average basket size throughout the entire enrollment period."
