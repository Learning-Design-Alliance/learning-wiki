# Schools serving the greatest percentages of Black students have higher percentages of novice teachers than schools serving the fewest (15% vs 10% nationally)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Nationally, schools serving the greatest percentages of Black students average 15% novice teachers versus 10% in schools serving the fewest, and in 1 in 4 states the gap is at least twofold. [→ Sarah Mehrotra 2021](#sarah-mehrotra-2021)

## Evidence

### Sarah Mehrotra 2021

Sarah Mehrotra, Ivy S. Morgan, Allison Socol. (2021). Getting Black Students Better Access to Non-Novice Teachers. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED617544

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of the U.S. Department of Education's 2017-18 Civil Rights Data Collection comparing schools in the highest and lowest quartiles for Black student enrollment. The report finds "15% of teachers are novice teachers" in the highest-quartile schools versus 10% in the lowest, with Maryland at 1 in 5 versus 1 in 10.

> "On average, in schools serving the greatest percentages of Black students, 15% of teachers are novice teachers. In schools serving the smallest percentages of Black students, 10% of the teachers are novice teachers."
