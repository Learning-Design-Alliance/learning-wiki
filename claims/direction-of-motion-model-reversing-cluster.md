## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using a generalized force/acceleration-in-the-direction-of-motion model on the Reversing Direction cluster classifies many more students into a common model than the F ∝ v model, and this generalization adds no significant information for the Force Sled or Force Graphs clusters. [→ Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann 2008](#trevor-i-smith-and-michael-c-wittmann-2008)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann 2008

Trevor I. Smith and Michael C. Wittmann. (2008). Applying a resources framework to analysis of the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1838 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0711.1838)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Data-driven classification research by one author (cited as ref. 28, an unpublished masters thesis) comparing model classifications across clusters. The direction-of-motion model helps only where objects move in more than one direction; no effect size is printed.

> "Research conducted by one author (T.I.S.) suggests that considering the direction-of-motion model for the Reversing Direction cluster allows many more students to be classified into a common model than the F ∝ v model.[28] Incorporating the direction-of-motion model into the results from the Force Sled or Force Graphs clusters, however, did not add any signiﬁcant information."
