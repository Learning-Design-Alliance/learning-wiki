## Evidence

### Connolly 2018

Connolly, M. R., Lee, Y.-G., & Savoy, J. N. (2018). The Effects of Doctoral Teaching Development on Early-Career STEM Scholars' College Teaching Self-efficacy. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-02-0039

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

OLS interaction analysis (Table 3A; women × TD engagement coefficients 0.029, p < 0.01 for course planning and teaching methods). The comparison names women and men as the two sides, and "these gaps became significantly smaller as women became more engaged in TD activities."

> "While women without TD experience were significantly less confident in course planning and teaching methods than men without TD experience, these gaps became significantly smaller as women became more engaged in TD activities."

### Connolly 2018 (2)

Connolly, M. R., Lee, Y.-G., & Savoy, J. N. (2018). The Effects of Doctoral Teaching Development on Early-Career STEM Scholars' College Teaching Self-efficacy. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.17-02-0039

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Predictive-margin analysis (Figure 2, based on Table 3A models) identifying engagement thresholds at which the gender difference becomes non-significant; a non-significant result with no printed effect size.

> "Women's confidence in course planning and teaching methods was not significantly different from that of men when women spent approximately more than 60–70 hours and 30 hours, respectively, on TD activities during their doctoral programs."
