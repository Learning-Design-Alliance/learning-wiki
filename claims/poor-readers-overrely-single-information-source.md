# Poor beginning readers overrely on a single information source rather than integrating all available cues

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Remedial readers with a deficit in one source (e.g., phonics) shift away from that source and overrely on their strength, which initially works but becomes a weakness as texts grow unfamiliar. [→ Walker 1989](#walker-1989)

## Evidence

### Walker 1989

Walker, B. J. (1989). The Interactive Model of Reading: Deciding How Disability Occurs. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED315726

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Allington (1984b), that poor beginning readers rely on one available source of information rather than integrating cues. The paper illustrates with case examples Andy (reader-based overreliance) and Kris (text-based overreliance).

> ""Poor beginning readers...seem to rely on one available source of information rather than integrating all available cues" (Allington, 1984b, p. 847)."
