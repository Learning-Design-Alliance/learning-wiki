# Raising selection thresholds under the NCM did not disadvantage applicants from low SES backgrounds, with no significant SES difference between models

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There was no statistically significant difference in SES between applicants selected by the NCM and the CM, and the minimum SES level was the same across decision groups. [→ Shulruf Boaz 2020](#shulruf-boaz-2020)

## Evidence

### Shulruf Boaz 2020

Shulruf Boaz, O'Sullivan Anthony, Velan Gary. (2020). Selecting top candidates for medical school selection interviews- a non-compensatory approach. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02031-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

SES comparison across model decision groups in the six-cohort applicant data, with SES estimated from postcode-based SEIFA deciles. Minimum SES was the same across groups ("SES ≥1") and mean SES for the NCM was "slightly lower" (7.74 vs. 7.97, n.s.); no effect size is printed for the non-significant test.

> "there was no difference in the minimum SES level across the same groups (SES ≥1) and the mean SES for the NCM was slightly lower compared to the CM (7.74 vs. 7.97,n.s.)"
