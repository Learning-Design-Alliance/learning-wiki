# Teachers' developmental stage of teaching skills significantly predicts student mathematics achievement, with higher stages performing better

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students of stage-4 teachers achieved more than students of teachers in the first three stages, and stage-1 teachers' students achieved least, supporting the developmental ordering of stages. [→ Antoniou 2011](#antoniou-2011)

## Evidence

### Antoniou 2011

Antoniou, P., Kyriakides, L., & Creemers, B. (2011). Investigating the Effectiveness of a Dynamic Integrated Approach to Teacher Professional Development. ceps Journal, 1(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1130737

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel model 4 entered stage dummies (stage 3 as reference) for observed quality of teaching. The article reports the stage variable had "a considerably large and statistically significant effect on student achievement"; model 4 explained 35.8% of variance with a significant chi-square change (p<0.001).

> "the students of teachers at stage 1 have the lowest achievement, whereas students of teachers at level 4 have higher achievement than students of the first three levels"
