## Evidence

### Skipper 2015

Skipper, Y. (2015). An evaluation of an intervention to change first-year psychology students' theory of intelligence. Psychology Teaching Review, 21(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146563

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on pre-post difference scores in the randomised study of 80 first-year students, using a scenario measure of behavioural intentions. The intervention group "became significantly more likely to choose a complex task F(1,69)=4.27, p=.043".

> "Other ANOVAs showed that students in the intervention group became significantly more likely to choose a complex task F(1,69)=4.27, p=.043."

### Skipper 2015 (2)

Skipper, Y. (2015). An evaluation of an intervention to change first-year psychology students' theory of intelligence. Psychology Teaching Review, 21(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146563

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on goal orientation difference scores from the same randomised study, measured with Grant and Dweck's 12-item scale. Students "came to hold more learning than performance related goals F(1,60)=6.74, p=.012".

> "Students also came to hold more learning than performance related goals F(1,60)=6.74, p=.012."

### Skipper 2015 (3)

Skipper, Y. (2015). An evaluation of an intervention to change first-year psychology students' theory of intelligence. Psychology Teaching Review, 21(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146563

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on the simple-task intention measure in the same study. The article reports "the effect was not significant, but means tended in the hypothesised direction F(1,69)=3.37, p=.071".

> "In terms of choosing a simple task, the effect was not significant, but means tended in the hypothesised direction F(1,69)=3.37, p=.071."
