# District pre-K enrollment was not associated with kindergarten-entry achievement levels or with districts' achievement trends over time

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In hierarchical linear models controlling for district poverty, the percentage of students enrolled in district pre-K programs was associated with neither the average 2010 RIT score nor change in scores over time; high-enrollment districts showed similar declines as districts without pre-K. [→ Kuhfeld 2019](#kuhfeld-2019)

## Evidence

### Kuhfeld 2019

Kuhfeld, Megan, Soland, James, Pitts, Christine, & Burchinal, Margaret. (2019). Trends in Children's Academic Skills at School Entry: 2010 to 2017. EdWorkingPaper No. 19-137, Annenberg Institute at Brown University. http://www.edworkingpapers.com/ai19-137

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Three-level HLM of kindergarten cohorts nested in schools within districts (reading: 2,229,390 students in 10,509 schools in 3,414 districts; math: 2,339,900 in 10,690 schools in 3,437 districts), with lagged district pre-K enrollment from CCD data and a cohort-by-enrollment interaction. Districts with high pre-K enrollment showed similar overall drops over time as districts that did not offer pre-K.

> "While district poverty level is significantly associated with average district RIT score in 2010, the percentage of students enrolled in district pre-K programs was not associated with either the average RIT score in 2010 or change in scores over time."
