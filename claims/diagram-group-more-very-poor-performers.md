## Subclaims

`q3 i?` On both problems, the percentage of Group 2 students scoring less than 5 out of 10 was significantly larger than in Groups 1 and 3, while intermediate-score percentages were comparable. [→ Alex Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016](#alex-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q3 i?` The percentage of poorly performing students (score 4 or less out of 10) was significantly larger in the DO group than in the PO and NS groups on both problems (Fisher's exact p values 0.033 and 0.001 for problem 1; 0.002 and <0.001 for problem 2). [→ Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018](#alexandru-maries-and-chandralekha-singh-2018)

## Evidence

### Alex Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Alex Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Should Students be Provided Diagrams or Asked to Draw Them While Solving Introductory Physics Problems?. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1601.04357](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1601.04357)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Score-distribution analysis (Table 4) across the three intervention groups on both problems, reporting percentages and counts of students above 8, at 5-7, and below 5 out of 10. The article reports the low-score percentage in Group 2 as "significantly larger than those in Groups 1 and 3"; no effect size is printed.

> "Table 4 shows that the percentage of students who performed poorly on this problem (obtained a score less than 5 out of 10) from Group 2 is significantly larger than those in Groups 1 and 3 but percentages with an intermediate score are comparable."

### Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh 2018

Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Case of two electrostatics problems: Can providing a diagram adversely impact introductory physics students' problem solving performance? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 010114. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010114

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Study 1 distributional analysis shown in Fig. 3: poor (4 or less), average (5-7), and good (8 or more) score bands per group; Fisher's exact tests gave the printed p values, while intermediate-score percentages were comparable (all p larger than 0.5).

> "the percentage of students who performed poorly from the DO group is significantly larger than those in the PO and NS groups on both problems"
