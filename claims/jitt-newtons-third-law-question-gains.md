# On Newton's Third Law questions specifically, Just-in-Time Teaching students gained 50.8%±4.1% while traditional lecture students gained only 6.6%±5.2%

## Evidence

### S. P. Formica 2010

S. P. Formica, J. L. Easley, and M. C. Spraker. (2010). Transforming common-sense beliefs into Newtonian thinking through Just-In-Time Teaching. Phys. Rev. ST Phys. Educ. Res. 6(2), 020106. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.6.020106

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Sub-analysis of the same 222-student, five-semester FCI pretest/post-test comparison, restricted to the Newton's Third Law questions. The traditional lecture classes "only saw a gain of 6.6%±5.2%" against 50.8%±4.1% for JiTT; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "When analyzing only those gains pertaining to the Newton's Third Law questions, the results again favor the Just-in-Time teaching method with a gain of 50.8%±4.1% while the traditional lecture classes only saw a gain of 6.6%±5.2%."
