# Automated encouragement messages sent shortly before exams do not raise university students’ end-of-semester exam grades, and the effect does not differ between first and second exams

## Subclaims
`q4 i0` Receiving an encouragement message did not increase students’ first-exam grades; the printed Cohen’s d of 0.011 is negligible. [→ Keller Tamás 2021](#keller-tamas-2021)
`q4 i?` There was no carry-over effect on exam grades: the treatment effect was similar at first and second exams. [→ Keller Tamás 2021 (2)](#keller-tamas-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Keller Tamás 2021

Keller Tamás, Szakál Péter. (2021). Not just words! Effects of a light-touch randomized encouragement intervention on students’ exam grades, self-efficacy, motivation, and test anxiety. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256960

`q4 · i0` · `causal · r3`

Preregistered randomized field experiment with crossover design at the University of Szeged (N = 15,539 analyzed; 28,156 exam observations), ITT analysis of registry grades. The null is confirmed: “did not gain higher exam grades at their first exam (β1 = 0.017; p = 0.418)”, with a printed Cohen’s d effect size of 0.011.

> "The first row of Column 1 shows that students who received the encouragement message did not gain higher exam grades at their first exam (β1 = 0.017; p = 0.418)."

### Keller Tamás 2021 (2)

Keller Tamás, Szakál Péter. (2021). Not just words! Effects of a light-touch randomized encouragement intervention on students’ exam grades, self-efficacy, motivation, and test anxiety. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0256960

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Same crossover field experiment. The article reports “no carry-over effect (β3 = -0.040; p = 0.208)”, so encouragement before the first exam showed no enduring effect on grades at the second exam; the joint linear effect of β1 and β3 was -0.023.

> "The results show no carry-over effect (β3 = -0.040; p = 0.208). Thus, the treatment effect was similar at students’ first and second exams."
