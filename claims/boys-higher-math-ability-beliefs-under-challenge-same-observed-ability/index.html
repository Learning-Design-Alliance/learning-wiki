Evidence¶

Perez-Felkner L 2017¶

Perez-Felkner L, Nix S and Thomas K (2017) Gendered Pathways: How Mathematics Ability Beliefs Shape Secondary and Postsecondary Course and Degree Field Choices. Front. Psychol. 8:386. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00386

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Two-sample t-tests (allowing for unequal variances) compared girls' and boys' ability beliefs among students at the 10th through 90th percentiles of 10th grade mathematics test scores in the ELS analytic sample. Gender differences were "highly signiﬁcant (p < 0.001)" and "the gender gaps are largest among the least and most talented mathematics students."

"Girls rate their ability in diﬃcult mathematics systematically lower than boys: the gen der diﬀerence is 0.29 standard deviations for those at the 50th percentile of observed ability, 0.34 standard deviations fo r those at the 70th percentile"
