# The synchronised EYE-ECG2 video produced a moderate but nonsignificant tendency toward better ECG interpretation performance than training as usual

## Evidence

### Scherff Aline D. 2025

Scherff Aline D., Kääb Stefan, Fischer Martin R., Berndt Markus. (2025). EYE-ECG2: Addressing medical student feedback in an RCT with eye-tracking videos featuring cued retrospective reporting and modified learning sequences for ECG interpretation skills training. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://journals.publisso.de/en/journals/jme/volume42/zma001771

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the RCT comparing INT (n=47) against TAU (n=47), Welch t-tests on pre-post difference scores found no statistically significant advantage for the synchronised EYE-ECG2 video; the article reports a "nonsignificant tendency towards better performance using the video". No effect size was printed.

> "the three pre-post difference scores were BS ΔM=1.60%±2.03%, RS ΔM=2.42%±2.31% and CS ΔM=0.80%±1.91%, conferring a nonsignificant tendency towards better performance using the video (red vs. blue lines in figure 4 (Fig. 4); BS Welch-t(91.67)=0.79, p=.43; BS Welch-t(90.07)=1.05, p=.30; CS Welch-t(91.70)=1.15, p=.67)."
