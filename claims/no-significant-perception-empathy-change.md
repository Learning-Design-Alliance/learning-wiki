## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Self-reported CSES-Perception median scores did not change significantly from pre-test to post-test in any study group. [→ Donnelly S 2019](#donnelly-s-2019)

## Evidence

### Donnelly S 2019

Donnelly S, Dean S, Razavy S, Levett-Jones T. (2019). Measuring the impact of an interdisciplinary learning project on nursing, architecture and landscape design students’ empathy. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215795

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of CSES-Perception scores pooled across gender in the pre-test post-test study found "no statistically significant difference" within each group; descriptively landscape and architecture increased slightly while nursing decreased slightly.

> "The results obtained from the analysis indicated no statistically significant difference in the CSES-Perception median scores across the two measurement sessions in each study group."

### Donnelly S 2019 (2)

Donnelly S, Dean S, Razavy S, Levett-Jones T. (2019). Measuring the impact of an interdisciplinary learning project on nursing, architecture and landscape design students’ empathy. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215795

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Kruskal-Wallis H tests comparing the three study groups on CSES-Perception in the female group at both sessions; between-group differences remained significant at both time points.

> "The distribution of CSES-Perception scores were statistically significantly different between study groups for both the pre-test measurement session (χ2(2) = 9.530, p = 0.009) and the post-test measurement session (χ2(2) = 8.039, p = 0.018) only in the female group."
