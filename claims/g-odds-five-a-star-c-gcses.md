# A standard deviation increase in age-11 g raises the odds of achieving five or more GCSEs at grades A*–C by 7.32

## Evidence

### Deary and Smith 2007

Ian J. Deary, Steve Strand, Pauline Smith, Cres Fernandes. (2007). Intelligence and educational achievement. Intelligence 35, 13–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2006.02.001

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Logistic regression of the five-or-more A*–C criterion (39,193 meeting it, 30,646 not) on g scores in the matched dataset; the printed effect size is an odds ratio = 7.32 per SD of g, with predicted probabilities of 58%, 91% and 16% at the mean, +1 SD and −1 SD.

> "The result was that a standard deviation increase in g was associated with an increased odds of 7.32 (p < 0.001, 95% CI = 7.10 to 7.54)."

### Deary and Smith 2007 (2)

Ian J. Deary, Steve Strand, Pauline Smith, Cres Fernandes. (2007). Intelligence and educational achievement. Intelligence 35, 13–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2006.02.001

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate logistic regression with g (odds ratio = 8.09), residual verbal factor (OR = 1.43), sex (OR = 1.96) and age (OR = 1.014) predicting the criterion; ROC analysis of predicted values gave AUC 0.873 versus 0.859 for g alone.

> "The area under the curve was 0.873 (p < 0.001, 95% CI = 0.870 to 0.875), only a little greater than when g was used alone, but significantly so, because the 95% confidence intervals of the two models do not overlap."
