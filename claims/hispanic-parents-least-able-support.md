# Hispanic parents feel least able to support their children's high school work and report the least school support

## Evidence

Changing Standards, Changing Relationships: Building Family-School Relationships to Promote Achievement in High Schools. `q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Parent interviews (n=73; 36 Hispanic, 28 African-American). Hispanic parents averaged six years of education versus 13 for African-Americans; 92% of Mexican-origin parents felt unable to help with homework. Hispanic parents also reported less school support (Figure 7).

> "Only 22 percent of the Hispanic parents of freshman we interviewed (versus 96 percent of the African-American parents) stated that they feel able to help their child with school work"

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of Consortium teacher surveys across 51 high schools and 280 elementary schools, controlling for student achievement and teacher characteristics (Figure 8). Majority Hispanic high schools showed the lowest communication, while majority Hispanic elementary schools showed higher-than-average communication and trust.

> "teachers in majority His-panic high schools report the lowest lev-els of communication and academic support for parents"
