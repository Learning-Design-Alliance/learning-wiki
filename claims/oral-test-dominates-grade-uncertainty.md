## Evidence

### G.L. Lippi 2014

G.L. Lippi. (2014). A priori analysis: an application to the estimate of the uncertainty in course grades. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Decomposition of the final-grade uncertainty into per-category contributions (Table III, Discussion section). The participation component is "entirely negligible (by two orders of magnitude)" despite its large a priori uncertainty, because of its many repetitions and small weight.

> "We immediately recognize that the inﬂuence on the ﬁnal uncertainty coming from the participation grade (p) is entirely negligible (by two orders of magnitude), in spite of its intrinsic variability and of the large a priori uncertainty we have consequently assigned to it."

### G.L. Lippi 2014 (2)

G.L. Lippi. (2014). A priori analysis: an application to the estimate of the uncertainty in course grades. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Discussion-section clarification of the same decomposition: weighting determines each category's share of the grade, but "the conﬁdence interval is determined exclusively by the oral test".

> "Each grade category contributes, proportionally to its weight, to the course grade, but the conﬁdence interval is determined exclusively by the oral test, all other forms of grading providing a much more “accurate” evaluation."

### G.L. Lippi 2014 (3)

G.L. Lippi. (2014). A priori analysis: an application to the estimate of the uncertainty in course grades. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/35/4/045012)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Concluding sentence of the per-category decomposition: only the oral test's uncertainty matters for the final grade, "owind to the larger size of σo, the single event (Mo = 1), and especially its dominant weight ( w0 = 0.4, table I)".

> "We thus conclude that only the uncertainty on the latter matters in the determination of the uncertainty on the ﬁnal grade, owind to the larger size of σo, the single event (Mo = 1), and especially its dominant weight ( w0 = 0.4, table I)."
