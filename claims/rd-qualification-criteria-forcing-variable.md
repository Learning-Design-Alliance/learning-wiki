# A study qualifies as an RD design only if assignment uses an ordinal forcing variable with at least four unique values on each side of the cutoff and no confounding factor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` RD qualification requires a forcing variable that is ordinal with sufficient unique values (at least four below and four above the cutoff) and no factor confounded with it. [→ Schochet 2010](#schochet-2010)

## Evidence

### Schochet 2010

Schochet, P., Cook, T., Deke, J., Imbens, G., Lockwood, J.R., Porter, J., Smith, J. (2010). Standards for Regression Discontinuity Designs. Retrieved from What Works Clearinghouse website: http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/pdf/wwc_rd.pdf.

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The panel's definitional standard for RD studies states the forcing variable "should never be based on cardinal (non-ordinal) categories (like gender or race)" and that data must include "at least four unique values of the forcing variable below the cutoff and four unique values above the cutoff."

> "The analyzed data must also include at least four unique values of the forcing variable below the cutoff and four unique values above the cutoff."
