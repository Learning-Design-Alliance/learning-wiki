## Evidence

### Fallah-Karimi Samaneh 2025

Fallah-Karimi Samaneh, Ghaljaie Fereshteh, Khalilzadeh-Farsangi Zahra. (2025). Comparison of the effects of role-playing, small group discussion, and traditional teaching methods on nursing students’ self-efficacy. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07996-w

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest–posttest study at Zahedan University of Medical Sciences; the role-playing group (n = 28) completed six 60-minute scenario sessions enacting nurse, parent, child and observer roles. The article reports "a 55.7% increase in self-efficacy scores" and "(p < 0.001, d = 1.55)".

> "Role-playing, by providing a safe environment for practicing clinical skills, had the greatest effect on nursing students’ self-efficacy (p < 0.001, d = 1.55)."
