# Undergraduates taught Solar System moon content in an immersive fulldome planetarium retained a moderate gain (ES = 0.371) at delayed post-test, while non-immersive and no-visualization groups retained almost none

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Students who saw immersive fulldome planetarium presentations showed a moderate Post-Pre effect size of 0.371, whereas the no-visualization and flat-screen groups had Post-Pre gains close to zero. [→ Yu 2016](#yu-2016)

## Evidence

### Yu 2016

Yu, K. C., Sahami, K., Denn, G., Sahami, V., & Sessions, L. C. (2016). Immersive Planetarium Visualizations for Teaching Solar System Moon Concepts to Undergraduates. Journal of Astronomy & Earth Sciences Education, 3(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123383

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental study of 781 undergraduates in 17 classes across three treatment groups (no visuals, flat-screen visuals, fulldome planetarium), with identical instructors, lecture outline, and visuals. Aggregated longitudinal gains show only the planetarium group (GIII) retained a net Post-Pre gain, with "a moderate effect size 𝐸𝑆=0.371".

> "However, the Post-Cont differences were all losses, and the Post-Pre gains were close to zero except for students in GIII who had a moderate effect size 𝐸𝑆=0.371."
