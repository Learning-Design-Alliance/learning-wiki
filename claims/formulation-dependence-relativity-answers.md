# First-year college science students' answers on relativity of motion depend strongly on the question's formulation, dropping from 88% Galilean on the abstract formulation to 74% on a merry-go-round formulation and 3% on the Sun-Earth formulation

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 2115 first-year college science students, 88.1% validated the abstract formulation of relativity of motion, 74% validated the merry-go-round formulation, and only 3.0% gave Galilean answers to the Sun-Earth open question. [→ Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle 2019](#estelle-blanquet-and-eric-picholle-2019)

## Evidence

### Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle 2019

Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle. (2019). Emergence of conformist thinking under strong paradigmatic pressure: the case of (Galilean) relativity. Physical Review Physics Education Research (preprint dated December 5, 2019). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Questionnaire study of 2115 French first-year college science students run over 4 years via a call from the French Physical Society; the abstract MCQ formulation yielded "a strong majority (88%) of correct (Galilean) answers" (Table III: 88.1% Galilean, 11.9% Copernican, 0 don't-know).

> "The first, more abstract formulation induced a strong majority (88%) of correct (Galilean) answers (Table III)."

### Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle 2019 (2)

Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle. (2019). Emergence of conformist thinking under strong paradigmatic pressure: the case of (Galilean) relativity. Physical Review Physics Education Research (preprint dated December 5, 2019). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same SFP questionnaire, merry-go-round formulation (presumably weak paradigmatic pressure): "14% less student (74%) manage to also validate Formulation 2" (Table IV: 74% Galilean, 26% Copernican).

> "The concrete application of this principle of relativity nevertheless already appears a bit more difficult, even within a presumably weak-pressure context, since 14% less student (74%) manage to also validate Formulation 2 (Table IV)."

### Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle 2019 (3)

Estelle Blanquet and Éric Picholle. (2019). Emergence of conformist thinking under strong paradigmatic pressure: the case of (Galilean) relativity. Physical Review Physics Education Research (preprint dated December 5, 2019). [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1912.04110)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Open Formulation 3 on the relative motion of Earth and Sun: only 3.0% Galilean answers overall (Table V), so "an overwhelming 97%" of the 2115 students failed to apply the principle they had validated earlier in the same questionnaire.

> "But the key result of this first study is that an overwhelming 97% of this large sample of college-level students appear unable to apply correctly the same idea they were more than 88% to validate earlier in the same questionnaire"
