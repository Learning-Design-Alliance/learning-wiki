## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Girls had higher self-reported social competences than boys (main effect of gender, B = -0.10, p < .001). [→ Rudmann 2023](#rudmann-2023)

## Evidence

### Rudmann 2023

Rudmann, O., Batruch, A., Visintin, E. P., Sommet, N., Bressoux, P., Darnon, C., ... Butera, F. (2023). Cooperative Learning Reduces the Gender Gap in Perceived Social Competences. A Large-Scale Nationwide Longitudinal Experiment. Journal of Educational Psychology (accepted for publication). https://osf.io/qarxk/?view_only=e772332576a5421cbf61b3092e567ac9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Longitudinal multilevel model (observations nested in students, classes, schools) on the ProFAN sample of 10,163 vocational high-school students; Model 1 tested the gender main effect on the Social Competence Teen Survey score, showing "girls had higher self-reported social competences than boys".

> "Our first hypothesis was supported, B = -0.10, SE = 0.02, p < .001 (see Model 1). As expected, girls had higher self-reported social competences than boys."
