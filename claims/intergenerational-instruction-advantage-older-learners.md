## Evidence

### Zi-Wei Liang 2025

Zi-Wei Liang, Zhi-Jun Zhan, Ying-Chen Liu, Hong-Zhou Xu & Jing Yu. (2025). Interpersonal neural synchronization underlies interactive concept learning in older adults. npj Science of Learning 10:75. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00368-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Study 2 compared 31 older learners with younger instructors and 32 with older instructors using the passive format from Study 1. The behavioral dyad effect was significant (t(61) = 3.64, p < 0.001, d = 0.93); "the intergenerational learning group exhibited higher INS than the intrageneratio nal learning group", with permutation confirmation (ps < 0.05), and frontopolar INS correlated with performance in the intergenerational group (r = 0.56, p = 0.01) but not the intragenerational group.

> "An independent samples t-test revealed a signi ﬁcant main effect of instructor–learner dyad on INS in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC; CH8; t(61) =3 . 8 0 ,p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.97) and frontopolar area (CH19; t(61) =3 . 2 4 ,p = 0.002, Cohen’s d = 0.83; Fig. 4b, d) within 0.228–0.484 Hz frequency band."

### Zi-Wei Liang 2025 (2)

Zi-Wei Liang, Zhi-Jun Zhan, Ying-Chen Liu, Hong-Zhou Xu & Jing Yu. (2025). Interpersonal neural synchronization underlies interactive concept learning in older adults. npj Science of Learning 10:75. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00368-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Granger causality analysis (HERMES toolbox) on intergenerational dyads in Study 2 found "learner-to-instructor inﬂuence was signiﬁcantly stronger than the reverse direction" (d = −0.72), indicating learner-driven coupling; intragenerational dyads showed no significant directional difference (d = 0.07).

> "Results showed that learner-to-instructor inﬂuence was signiﬁcantly stronger than the reverse direction (t(30) = −4.00, p < 0.001, Cohen’s d = −0.72; Fig. 6)."
