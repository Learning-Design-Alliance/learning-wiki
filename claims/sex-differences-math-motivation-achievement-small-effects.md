# Boys showed higher mean intrinsic motivation and early-grade achievement than girls, with small effect sizes, and girls' motivation declined from grade 1 to 2

## Subclaims
`q3 i0` In repeated-measures ANOVAs, boys showed higher intrinsic motivation than girls at all ages and better achievement in grades 1 and 2, but the sex-by-time interactions accounted for a very small percentage of variance (η2 = .005 and .004). [→ Garon‐Carrier 2016](#garoncarrier-2016)

## Evidence

### Garon‐Carrier 2016

Garon‐Carrier, G., Boivin, M., Guay, F., Kovas, Y., Dionne, G., Lemelin, J. P., ... & Tremblay, R. E. (2016). Intrinsic Motivation and Achievement in Mathematics in Elementary School: A Longitudinal Investigation of their Association. Child Development, 87(1), 165-175. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12458

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

A 3 (time) × 2 (sex) repeated-measures ANOVA found a significant sex-by-time interaction for motivation, F(1.98, 2385.46) = 5.66, p < .01, η2 = .005; the article states these sex differences "account for a small percentage of the variance." A parallel ANOVA for achievement gave η2 = .004.

> "Boys showed a significant higher level of intrinsic motivation than girls at all ages (ps < .01). Girls’ motivation significantly decreased from grade 1 to grade 2, but not from grade 2 to 4 (p >.05)."
