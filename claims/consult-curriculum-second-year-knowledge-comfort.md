# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, second-year students' knowledge of the consultation process and comfort requesting consults rose from pre-test to post-test after a 5 Cs model didactic

## Evidence

### Carter Keme MD 2015

Carter Keme MD, Golden Andrew BS, Martin Shannon MD, Donlan Sarah MD, Hock Sara MD, Babcock Christine MD, Farnan Jeanne MD, Arora Vineet MD. (2015). Results from the First Year of Implementation of CONSULT: Consultation with Novel Methods and Simulation for UME Longitudinal Training. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.25520

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post survey study of second-year students around a 50-minute consultation didactic (25 completed the pre-survey, 26 the post-survey). Students were able to "identify significantly more correct components of the 5 Cs model (4.04 vs. 4.81, p<0.001)"; understanding of formal and curbside consultation definitions also increased significantly.

> "students were able to identify significantly more correct components of the 5 Cs model (4.04 vs. 4.81, p<0.001). The number of students understanding the definitions of formal and curbside consultations also significantly increased (24% vs. 100%, p<0.001; 16% vs. 100%, p<0.001; respectively)."

### Carter Keme MD 2015 (2)

Carter Keme MD, Golden Andrew BS, Martin Shannon MD, Donlan Sarah MD, Hock Sara MD, Babcock Christine MD, Farnan Jeanne MD, Arora Vineet MD. (2015). Results from the First Year of Implementation of CONSULT: Consultation with Novel Methods and Simulation for UME Longitudinal Training. Western Journal of Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2015.9.25520

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same second-year pre/post survey, which asked students to rate comfort requesting a consultation. After the curriculum, more students "indicated they would be comfortable in requesting consults (0% vs. 69%, p<0.001)" and fewer felt they would need guidance (60% vs. 31%, p<0.001).

> "After the curriculum, more students indicated they would be comfortable in requesting consults (0% vs. 69%, p<0.001), while fewer felt they would need guidance while calling a consultation (60% vs. 31%, p<0.001)."
