# CSC coaching significantly reduced teachers' need for change in instructional practices (IS discrepancy scores, d = .88)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` IS Rating Scale total discrepancy scores were significantly smaller at postintervention than baseline, a large effect. [→ Dudek et al. 2019](#dudek-et-al-2019)

## Evidence

### Dudek et al. 2019

Christopher M. Dudek, MEd, Linda A. Reddy, PhD, Adam Lekwa, PhD, Anh N. Hua, PhD, and Gregory A. Fabiano, PhD. (2019). Improving Universal Classroom Practices Through Teacher Formative Assessment and Coaching. Assessment for Effective Intervention. https://doi.org/10.1177/1534508418772919

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t test comparing baseline and postintervention IS discrepancy scores for the 32 waitlist teachers, observed by independent observers. The article reports "t(31) = 4.97, p <.001, d = .88", a large effect.

> "For IS discrepancy scores, paired samples t tests revealed that teachers had significantly smaller discrepancy scores at postintervention assessment (M = 12.81, SD = 9.82) than at baseline assessment (M = 20.15, SD = 11.81), t(31) = 4.97, p <.001, d = .88."
