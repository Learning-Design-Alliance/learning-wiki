# In the laboratory experiment, next-day recognition was higher for congruent new-word meanings than for incongruent items, while day-1 performance showed the opposite pattern

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among day-1-correct items, congruent new-words were recognized more often than incongruent ones (62.4% vs. 38.9%, χ2(1) = 78.32, p < 0.0001), despite incongruent trials being answered more accurately on day 1. [→ Vavra 2023](#vavra-2023)

## Evidence

### Vavra 2023

Vavra, P., Sokolovič, L., Porcu, E., Ripollés, P., Rodriguez-Fornells, A., & Noesselt, T. (2023). Entering into a self-regulated learning mode prevents detrimental effects of feedback removal on memory. npj Science of Learning, 8(1). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00150-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GLMM on recognition memory among items answered correctly on day 1 in the laboratory recognition group (N = 29). The pattern held for raw recognition rates independent of day-1 accuracy (χ2(1) = 9.18, p < 0.002).

> "Participants ( N = 29) were, on average, more likely to remember the congruent new-words (congruent: 62.4% vs. incongruent: 38.9%, main effect of congruence: χ2(1) = 78.32 p < 0.0001) after 24 h"
