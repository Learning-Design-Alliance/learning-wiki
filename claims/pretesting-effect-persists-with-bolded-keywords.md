# The pretesting effect persists when attention is directed via bolded keywords in both conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2009 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` When keywords were bolded in both conditions, tested and bolded items were recalled better (M = 82%) than bolded-only items in extended study (M = 64%). [→ Richland 2009](#richland-2009)

## Evidence

### Richland 2009

Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The Pretesting Effect: Do Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Learning? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 15(3), 243–257. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3: 64 undergraduates read the passage with one keyword bolded per testable sentence in both conditions. "tested and bolded items in the test and study condition were recalled significantly more often on the final test," d = 0.84.

> "tested and bolded items in the test and study condition were recalled significantly more often on the final test (M ⫽ 82%, SE ⫽ 3.8) than were bolded-only items in the extended study condition (M ⫽ 64%, SE ⫽ 4.0), t(62) ⫽ 3.3, p ⬍ .002, d ⫽ 0.84"
