# Presenting the '+' sign 150 ms before operands speeds up addition problem solving in expert older adults, replicating the effect found in young adults

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In older adults with high arithmetic skills, solution times were faster when the '+' sign preceded operands by 150 ms than when sign and operands appeared together. [→ Thevenot 2018](#thevenot-2018)

## Evidence

### Thevenot 2018

Thevenot, C., Dewi, J., Bagnoud, J., Wolfer, P., Fayol, M., & Castel, C. (2018). The use of automated procedures by older adults with high arithmetic skills during addition problem solving. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED

`q3 · i3`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on solution times in the sign priming experiment with 18 older and 26 young adults. "Problems were solved faster when the sign appeared before the operands (811 ms) than when it appeared at the same time (832 ms)", a 21 ms overall SOA effect, ηp2 = .34.

> "There was also a main effect of SOA, F(1, 42) = 21.74, MSE = 90542.79, ηp2 = .34, p < .001. Problems were solved faster when the sign appeared before the operands (811 ms) than when it appeared at the same time (832 ms)."
