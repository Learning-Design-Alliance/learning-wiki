## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants undergoing HOST skills training scored better on needle driving, needle positioning and suture placement, and obtained higher GEARS scores, than controls. [→ Sridhar Ashwin N. 2017](#sridhar-ashwin-n-2017)

## Evidence

### Sridhar Ashwin N. 2017

Sridhar Ashwin N., Briggs Tim P., Kelly John D., Nathan Senthil. (2017). Training in Robotic Surgery—an Overview. Current Urology Reports. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11934-017-0710-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports a multi-institutional randomized controlled trial by Chowriappa et al. evaluating HOST training for robotic urethrovesical anastomosis; the HOST group "obtained significantly higher scores (14.4 vs 11.9; P 0.012)" on GEARS. No standardized effect sizes are printed.

> "They found that participants who underwent the HOST™ skills training had better scores in the domains of needle driving (3.0 vs 2.3; P = 0.042), needle positioning (3.0 vs 2.4; P = 0.033) and suture placement (3.4 vs 2.6; P = 0.014)."
