# In Experiment 1, participants in both groups were significantly biased towards excessive reminder use relative to the optimal strategy

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` One-sample tests showed reminder bias significantly above zero in both the feedback and no-feedback groups, replicating the pro-reminder bias found in prior optimal offloading studies. [→ Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert 2026](#ceri-ngai-and-sam-j-gilbert-2026)

## Evidence

### Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert 2026

Ceri Ngai and Sam J. Gilbert (2026). Metacognitive training facilitates optimal cognitive offloading. Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-026-00714-0

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r?`

One-sample t-tests against zero reminder bias in Experiment 1 (N = 164). "both groups chose to set reminders on more trials than was optimal" (feedback d = .367; no-feedback d = .702), consistent with prior studies cited in the introduction.

> "Two-tailed one-sample t-tests showed that participants in both the feedback group (t(81) = 3.22, p = .002, d = .367) and in the no-feedback group (t(81) = 6.35, p < .001, d = .702) were significantly biased towards reminders, showing that both groups chose to set reminders on more trials than was optimal."
