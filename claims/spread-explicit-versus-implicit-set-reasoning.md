# Students apply set-like reasoning to spread when it is presented explicitly (SMDS) even before instruction, but are initially less set-like when spread must be conceptualized implicitly (DMSS), converging after instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students treated spread with set-like reasoning when confronted with it explicitly before instruction, but were initially less set-like using implicit notions of spread; after instruction they consistently applied spread concepts in both contexts. ## Evidence

### H. J. Lewandowski 2018

H. J. Lewandowski, Robert Hobbs, Jacob T. Stanley, Dimitri R. Dounas-Frazer, and Benjamin Pollard (2018). Student reasoning about measurement uncertainty in an introductory lab course. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.056)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the SMDS/DMSS pre-post contrast, drawing on Buffler et al.'s observation that SMDS "presented the issue of spread explicitly" while in DMSS it "needed to be conceptualized and applied". This is an interpretive reading of the data, not a tested contrast; no effect size is printed.

> "our data suggest that students treat spread using set-like reasoning when confronted with it explicitly, even before instruction, but they are initially less set-like in their ability to use notions of spread implic- itly. However, after instruction in our traditional course, students consistently apply concepts related to spread"
