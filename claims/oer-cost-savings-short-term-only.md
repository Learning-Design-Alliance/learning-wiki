## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The article argues that adopting OER during the emergency shift to digital learning saves students money and can help close the achievement gap between lower and higher income students, but that as textbook prices plateau and the price difference shrinks, the affordability-attributable impact of OER will decrease in parallel. ## Evidence

### David A. Wiley 2021

David A. Wiley. (2021). Open educational resources: undertheorized research and untapped potential. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09907-w

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Authors' argument in the Application section: because the cost of textbooks has plateaued for the first time in decades and publishers respond to OER price pressure, the price difference between OER and traditionally copyrighted resources is likely to continue to decrease. This is an interpretive prediction, not a tested result.

> "If the access hypothesis holds, the impact of OER on student outcomes attributable to affordability will decrease in parallel. In other words, adopting OER may not be a long-term strategy for saving students significant amounts of money or closing the achievement gap between lower income and higher income students."
