Evidence¶

Ritchey et al. 2017¶

Kristen D. Ritchey, Kimberly Palombo, Rebecca D. Silverman, and Deborah L. Speece. (2017). Effects of an Informational Text Reading Comprehension Intervention for Fifth-Grade Students. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(2). https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948716682689

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same randomized trial, ANOVA/ANCOVA on distal norm-referenced and fluency measures. "no significant group differences" on all three; printed effect sizes were −0.037 (WIAT), 0.155 (TOSREC), and a small negative g = −0.105 for Passage Reading Fluency favoring control.

"There were also no significant group differences on TOSREC, WIAT Reading Comprehension, or Passage Reading Fluency. For WIAT Reading Comprehension, the effect size was −0.037, and for TOSREC, the effect size was 0.155."

Related Claims¶

  • The CCT intervention produced statistically significant main effects favoring CCT on reading comprehension (ES = 0.14), TOSREC (ES = 0.28), and taught vocabulary (ES = 0.43), but not on SWE or ORF — reports the opposite
  • Initial reading fluency was not a consistent predictor of response to the comprehension intervention — related
  • The CCT treatment effect on comprehension was statistically significant only for students with pretest ORF up to 0.20 SD above the sample mean (64.0% of the sample), and not for higher-fluency students — related
  • Some students showed strong standardized-score response while others remained below 90 on WIAT Reading Comprehension — related
  • K–2 children receiving conceptually coherent texts performed similarly to children receiving leveled texts on standardized reading comprehension outcomes — related
