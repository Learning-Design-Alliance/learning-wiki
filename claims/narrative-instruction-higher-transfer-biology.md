# Narrative instruction produces higher transfer performance than expository instruction in undergraduate biology learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Undergraduate students learning biology with narrative materials showed significantly higher transfer performance than those with expository materials (d = 0.46, BF = 44.22). [→ Samuel Tobler 2025](#samuel-tobler-2025)

## Evidence

### Samuel Tobler 2025

Samuel Tobler, Tanmay Sinha, Katja Köhler, Manu Kapur. (2025). Teaching biology with narratives: examining the impact of affective and cognitive variables on undergraduate student learning. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09723-w

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA in a randomized controlled classroom experiment with 90 second-semester undergraduate natural science students (42 narrative, 48 expository) found a medium effect size favoring narrative instruction on transfer, with "d = 0.46, BF = 44.22". No differences were found for recall or understanding.

> "We found significant learning material-related differences with a medium effect size for students’ performance in the transfer task in favor of the narrative group (F(1,86) = 4.674, p <.05, d = 0.46, BF = 44.22)"
