Evidence¶

Johnson 2023¶

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental analysis (2SLS-IV difference-in-difference and regression kink designs) of longitudinal administrative data for California public school students, exploiting the staggered LCFF rollout. Figure 9 shows math impacts of roughly 0.94–0.98 grade-level equivalence, significant at the .001 level.

"the results indicate that a $1,000 increase in per-pupil spending experienced in 3 consecutive years led to roughly a full grade-level increase in math achievement in grades 3 –8, relative to what the average student achieved prior to the funding increases."

Johnson 2023 (2)¶

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Same quasi-experimental spending analysis, reading outcome (Figure 10); printed estimates run roughly 0.94 to 1.04 grade-level equivalence across grades 3–8, each significant at the .001 level per the figure note.

"the estimated LCFF-induced effects of a $1,000 increase in per-pupil spending experienced in 3 consecutive years also resulted in a full grade-level increase in reading in elementary school and middle school grades."
