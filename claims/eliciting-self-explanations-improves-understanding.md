# Eliciting Self Explanations Improves Understanding

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · none recorded yet

When learners are prompted to explain to themselves why a step, statement, or example is correct, they integrate new material with prior knowledge and show deeper understanding than learners who only read or rehearse the material.

## Discussion

**Mechanism.** Self-explanation prompts are usually understood through generative-learning logic: producing an explanation forces the learner to connect new information to what they already know, exposing gaps and repairing flawed mental models. This places self-explanation close to [Activation](activation-improves-learning.md) and [Analogical reasoning](analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) as integration-supporting techniques.

**Costs and boundary conditions.** Generating explanations consumes working-memory resources, so for novices or very high-element-interactivity material the prompt itself can add load rather than support integration [~M] — the same trade-off that governs [Chunking](chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) and [Cognitive load theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) more broadly. When material is already well understood, explaining it can become redundant and yield little or no benefit, echoing the expertise-reversal pattern described in [Expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]. Prompt design matters: open-ended "explain this" prompts can produce shallow restatements, while prompts that target principles or underlying reasons are more likely to generate productive explanations [~M].

**Design implications.** Practitioners should (a) attach explanation prompts to specific steps or statements rather than asking for global summaries, (b) scaffold early prompts with sentence starters or principle cues and fade them as competence grows, and (c) avoid prompting explanation of material learners can already recite fluently — at that point the prompt is redundant effort with no integration payoff [~M]. Self-explanation pairs naturally with [worked examples](../elements/demonstration.md) and with [case-based learning](case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md), where prompts can direct attention to why a case or solution step unfolds as it does.

**Open questions.** The durability of self-explanation effects over delayed tests, their interaction with learner expertise, and the relative value of self-generated versus instructional explanations remain active areas of investigation.
