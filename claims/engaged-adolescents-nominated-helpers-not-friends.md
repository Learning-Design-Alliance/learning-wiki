# Behaviorally and emotionally engaged adolescents are more often nominated as helpers, but academically engaged adolescents receive fewer friendship nominations

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Academically engaged individuals received fewer friendship nominations (negative alter effects of engagement in friend networks). [→ Shin 2023 (2)](#shin-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Shin 2023

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel SAOM results from the 20-classroom sample: help-network indegree popularity was positive, so highly engaged adolescents were more often sought as helpers, with "behavioral (η = 0.19, SD = 0.04, p > 0.99)" and "emotional (η = 0.10, SD = 0.03, p > 0.99)" effects.

> "The positive indegree popularity effects in help networks were reflected in the finding that individuals who received many help nominations (gave help) displayed higher behavioral (η = 0.19, SD = 0.04, p > 0.99) and emotional (η = 0.10, SD = 0.03, p > 0.99) engagement."

### Shin 2023 (2)

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same study's friend networks showed negative alter effects of behavioral (η = −0.11, p < 0.01) and emotional (η = −0.14, p < 0.01) engagement, and the discussion states that "such academically engaged individuals received fewer friendship nominations from peers".

> "Interestingly, such academically engaged individuals received fewer friendship nominations from peers."
