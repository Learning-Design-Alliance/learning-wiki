# Student engagement with the Science Power Notebooks and eBooks dropped significantly after the transition to distance learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Notebook engagement dropped significantly in distance learning, with students struggling to navigate the platform on laptops instead of tablets. [→ Li 2022](#li-2022)
`q2 i?` eBook engagement diminished after transitioning to distance learning due to logistical challenges accessing and navigating the eBook platform. [→ Li 2022 (2)](#li-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Li 2022

Li, L., Hayakawa, M., Freese, J., Daniels, B., Weiser, G., Luttgen, K., Lor, M. C., Schneider, M., Huang, C.-W., & Jensen, E. (2022). Pivoting an in-person transmedia science program to a virtual program during a pandemic: Lessons learned. Children, Youth and Environments, 32(3), 53-81. http://www.jstor.org/action/showPublication?journalCode=chilyoutenvi

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 teacher interviews: students needed significant support to navigate the notebook platform, had trouble fitting work onto the digital notepad, and often did not complete asynchronous assignments; one teacher explained "The students lost their interest because it’s not the same as on the tablet."

> "Teachers reported that while students enjoyed using the notebooks when they were in the classroom before COVID-19, engagement dropped significantly in the distance learning environment."

### Li 2022 (2)

Li, L., Hayakawa, M., Freese, J., Daniels, B., Weiser, G., Luttgen, K., Lor, M. C., Schneider, M., Huang, C.-W., & Jensen, E. (2022). Pivoting an in-person transmedia science program to a virtual program during a pandemic: Lessons learned. Children, Youth and Environments, 32(3), 53-81. http://www.jstor.org/action/showPublication?journalCode=chilyoutenvi

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Study 1 teacher interviews in the eBooks subsection: most teachers assigned eBooks individually, and low engagement was attributed to platform access difficulties; several teachers noted the read-aloud feature remained "critical for engaging students who struggled to read."

> "Teachers reported that students’ engagement in eBooks diminished after transitioning to distance learning. It was logistically challenging to access and navigate the eBook platform, which contributed to the low engagement levels."
