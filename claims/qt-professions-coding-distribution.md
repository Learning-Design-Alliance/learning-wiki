## Evidence

### Franziska Greinert 2024

Franziska Greinert, Malte S. Ubben, Ismet N. Dogan, Dagmar Hilfert-Rüppell, Rainer Müller. (2024). Advancing quantum technology workforce: industry insights into qualification and training needs. arXiv. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00294-2](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00294-2)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Quantitative coding of professions discussed in the interviews (Table 2); 288 codes were given, with one coding per category per interview counted, yielding 196 codes; the most frequent category is software engineers including data scientists and algorithm designers.

> "Overall, almost half of all roles coded belong to engineering (46%), less than every fifth to physics (17%) and only 11% address business roles."

### Franziska Greinert 2024 (2)

Franziska Greinert, Malte S. Ubben, Ismet N. Dogan, Dagmar Hilfert-Rüppell, Rainer Müller. (2024). Advancing quantum technology workforce: industry insights into qualification and training needs. arXiv. [doi:10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00294-2](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00294-2)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same interview coding analysis: about half of all codes (48% or 95 out of 196) fall on the 13 start-up interviews, indicating start-ups speak more about different roles than other companies.

> "For these start-ups, the average number of codes per document is significantly higher, at approximately 7, compared to 4 to 5 in other company types."
