# Audiovisual presentation yields significantly higher sentence recognition than auditory-alone presentation for most children with cochlear implants

## Evidence

### Lorin Lachs 2001

Lorin Lachs, David B. Pisoni, and Karen Iler Kirk. (2001). Use of Audiovisual Information in Speech Perception by Prelingually Deaf Children with Cochlear Implants. Ear Hear. 2001 June ; 22(3): 236–251. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/

`q3 · i?`

Live-voice Common Phrases Test administered under auditory-alone, visual-alone, and audiovisual conditions to 27 prelingually deafened children with cochlear implants. The article reports that "the audiovisual score was significantly higher than the auditory-alone score, t(26) = 5.48, p < 0.001" for most children. No effect size was printed.

> "However, for most of the children (N = 23), the audiovisual score was significantly higher than the auditory-alone score, t(26) = 5.48, p < 0.001."
