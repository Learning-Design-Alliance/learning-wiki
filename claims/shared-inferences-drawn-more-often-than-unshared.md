# Collaborative and individual inferences are less likely to be drawn than shared inferences during collaborative problem solving

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Inferences from shared information were drawn more frequently than inferences requiring unshared (collaborative or individual) information. [→ Deiglmayr 2011](#deiglmayr-2011)
`q3 i?` The planned contrast between collaborative and individual inferences did not reach significance in this experiment, so Hypothesis 2a was not supported here. [→ Deiglmayr 2011](#deiglmayr-2011)

## Evidence

### Deiglmayr 2011

Deiglmayr, A., & Spada, H. (2011). Training for fostering knowledge co-construction from collaborative inference-drawing. Learning and Instruction, 21(3), 441-451. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2010.06.004

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Testing-phase two-way mixed ANOVA with inference type as within-dyads factor in 36 dyads: a significant main effect of inference type ("F(2, 64) = 9.55, p < .01, partial η2 = .23"), and the planned contrast showed "collaborative and individual inferences together were less likely to be drawn than shared inferences" (partial η2 = .41). The collaborative-vs-individual contrast was "F(1, 32) = 1.13, ns".

> "they did confirm that collaborative and individual inferences together were less likely to be drawn than shared inferences , F(1, 32) = 22.37 , p < .01 , partial η2 = .41"
