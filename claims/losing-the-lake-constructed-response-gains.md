## Evidence

### E. Michael Nussbaum 2015

E. Michael Nussbaum, Marissa C. Owens, Gale M. Sinatra, Abeera P. Rehmat, Jacqueline R. Cordova, Sajjad Ahmad, Fred C. Harris, Jr., Sergiu M. Dascalu. (2015). Losing the Lake: Simulations to Promote Gains in Student Knowledge and Interest about Climate Change. https://doi.org/10.12973/ijese.2015.277a

`q4 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Analysis of the second constructed-response item in the randomized trial using a gamma log-link GLM; a significant Group by Time interaction (Wald χ2 = 5.40, p = .02) favored the experimental group, with large within-group gains ("d = 1.57").

> "For the experimental group, scores significantly increased from pretest to posttest (p < .001, d = 1.57) and from pretest to delayed posttest (p < .001, d = 2.05), and were significantly different from the control group at posttest ( p = .006, d = 0.64)"

### E. Michael Nussbaum 2015 (2)

E. Michael Nussbaum, Marissa C. Owens, Gale M. Sinatra, Abeera P. Rehmat, Jacqueline R. Cordova, Sajjad Ahmad, Fred C. Harris, Jr., Sergiu M. Dascalu. (2015). Losing the Lake: Simulations to Promote Gains in Student Knowledge and Interest about Climate Change. https://doi.org/10.12973/ijese.2015.277a

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result on the first constructed-response item (scored 0-3, inter-rater reliability 86%) in the same randomized trial; the overall mean score was 1.17 and the interaction test was not significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "For the first constructed response question (“Why is it important for the Las Vegas Valley specifically to conserve water?”), there were no significant effects (e.g., for the interaction, Wald χ 2 = 0.70, p = .40)."
