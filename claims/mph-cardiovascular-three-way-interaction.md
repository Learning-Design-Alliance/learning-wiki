## Evidence

### Sambeth A 2026

Sambeth A, Toth M, Blokland A (2026). Methylphenidate (Ritalin) does not improve exam performance in an experimental setting. Psychopharmacology 243(3):593–600. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-025-06864-1

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

GLM repeated measures ANOVA of heart rate measured before drug administration and 3 h later on both days; the article reports η2 = 0.20, a large printed effect size, and attributes the pattern mainly to a lower reduction in heart rate on the MPH days.

> "There was a three-way interaction (Group*Day*Treat) for heart rate, indicating that MPH had a different effect in the treatment groups on the two days (F(2,68) = 8.51, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.20)"

### Sambeth A 2026 (2)

Sambeth A, Toth M, Blokland A (2026). Methylphenidate (Ritalin) does not improve exam performance in an experimental setting. Psychopharmacology 243(3):593–600. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-025-06864-1

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same repeated measures design for systolic and diastolic blood pressure, with printed η2 = 0.23 and η2 = 0.15; the authors interpret this as the expected cardiovascular effect of MPH on the day the drug was given.

> "On day 1, the systolic blood pressure did not decrease in the MPH -PLAC group and on day 2 the blood pressure only increased in the PLAC-MPH group (F(2,69) = 9.92, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.23). The diastolic blood pressure increased only in the MPH -PLAC group on day 1 and increased only in the PLAC-MPH group on day 2 (F(2,70) = 6.43, p < 0.003, η2 = 0.15)"
