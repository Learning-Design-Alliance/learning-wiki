# With reduced construct overlap and a no-feedback control, feedback did not significantly affect challenge (Experiments 3 and 4), and negative feedback raised expected effort required only in Experiment 3

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the replication experiments with different challenge/threat questionnaires, feedback condition did not significantly affect challenge in either experiment. [→ Tamara van Gog 2024](#tamara-van-gog-2024)
`q3 i2` Threat differed significantly across conditions in both experiments, and negative feedback raised expected effort required relative to positive and no feedback only in Experiment 3. [→ Tamara van Gog 2024 (2)](#tamara-van-gog-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Tamara van Gog 2024

Tamara van Gog, Eva Janssen, Florence Lucas, Maaike Taheij (2024). A Motivational Perspective on (Anticipated) Mental Effort Investment: The Biopsychosocial Model of Challenge and Threat. Educational Psychology Review (2024) 36:54. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09861-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs across three conditions (no, predominantly positive, predominantly negative feedback; N = 167 and N = 211) found no significant effect on challenge. The authors suggest the earlier challenge findings may have resulted from self-efficacy overlap in the challenge scale.

> "we did not find a significant effect of feedback condition on challenge, either in Experiment 3, F(2, 164) = 2.41, p = 0.093, or in Experiment 4, F(2, 208) = 1.78, p = 0.172."

### Tamara van Gog 2024 (2)

Tamara van Gog, Eva Janssen, Florence Lucas, Maaike Taheij (2024). A Motivational Perspective on (Anticipated) Mental Effort Investment: The Biopsychosocial Model of Challenge and Threat. Educational Psychology Review (2024) 36:54. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09861-6

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVAs on expected effort required across the three feedback conditions found a significant effect in Experiment 3 (η²p = 0.06) but not in Experiment 4; post-hoc tests showed the negative feedback condition expected more effort than positive (p = 0.007) and no feedback (p = 0.039) conditions.

> "we did find a significant effect of condition in Experiment 3, F(2, 164) = 5.25, p = 0.006, /u1D7022p  = 0.06, but not in Experiment 4, F(2, 208) = 0.86, p = 0.425."
