# Active learning reduces achievement gaps in examination scores between students from minoritized groups and their peers by 33% on average across STEM courses

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` On average, the standardized exam-score gap between MGS and non-MGS students is −0.62 SDs under traditional lecturing and −0.42 SDs under active learning, a 33% reduction. [→ Theobald Elli J. et al 2020](#theobald-elli-j-et-al-2020)

## Evidence

### Theobald Elli J. et al 2020

Theobald Elli J. et al. (2020). Active learning narrows achievement gaps for underrepresented students in undergraduate science, technology, engineering, and math. Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1916903117

`q4 · i?`

Individual-participant-data meta-analysis using hierarchical Bayesian regression of standardized exam scores from 15 studies (9,238 students, 51 classrooms). The article reports "a 33% reduction in achievement gaps on examinations in the STEM disciplines" under active learning; no standardized effect size is printed for the gap change.

> "the standardized achievement gap between MGS and non-MGS students on identical or formally equivalent examinations was −0.62 SDs in courses based on traditional lecturing (95% CI: −0.69 to −0.55). In courses that included active learning, this gap was −0.42 SDs (95% CI: −0.48 to −0.35)"
