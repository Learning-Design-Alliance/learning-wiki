# Students hold generally positive attitudes toward educational chatbots and agree the chatbots they used improved their knowledge and understanding

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Survey respondents generally held positive attitudes toward chatbots in education and agreed the chatbots improved their knowledge and understanding, while disagreeing that chatbots produced better results than they could produce on their own. ## Evidence

### Stephen Colbran 2026

Stephen Colbran, Meena Jha and Caitlin Schiavone. (2026). Understanding student perspectives on generative AI chatbots: a human-centred mixed-methods study in higher education. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00605-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

One-sample t-tests in the RQ1 results of the survey study (n = 121 cohort) showed respondents "agreed that the chatbots they used improved their knowledge and understanding ( p < .001, n = 120)" and felt more effective as learners (p = .003). Respondents disagreed that chatbots generated better results than they could produce on their own (p < .001).

> "Respondents thought that the chatbots they used, made them more effective as a learner (p = .003, n = 120) and agreed that the chatbots they used improved their knowledge and understanding ( p < .001, n = 120)."
