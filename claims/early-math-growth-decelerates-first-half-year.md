# Mathematics growth in Grades 1-3 is nonlinear with deceleration, so more growth occurs in the first half of the school year, and average growth rates decline across grade levels

## Evidence

### Hiroyuki Yamada 2024

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three-level hierarchical growth model of i-Ready Diagnostic scores (fall, winter, spring) for 225,152 students in Grades 1-3, fit by grade with a quadratic term. Table 1 prints linear estimates of 1.28, 1.02, and 0.91 and quadratic estimates of -0.11, -0.06, -0.04 for Grades 1-3, showing "a slight deceleration for all grade levels".

> "student growth was nonlinear with a slight deceleration for all grade levels; thus, more growth would be expected in the first half of the school year. On average, both the weekly linear growth rate and the deceleration rate decreased as the grade level increased."
