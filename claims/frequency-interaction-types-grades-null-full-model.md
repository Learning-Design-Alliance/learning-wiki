# In frequency-based models, Argue, Discuss, Evaluate, and Socialize show significant positive associations with grades, but no interaction type is significant in the full model

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Argue and Discuss were significant in the cognitive model, Argue and Evaluate in the high-cognitive model, and Socialize in the off-task model; none of the variables reached significance in the full model. [→ Mohammed Saqr 2023](#mohammed-saqr-2023)

## Evidence

### Mohammed Saqr 2023

Mohammed Saqr, Sonsoles López-Pernas. (2023). The temporal dynamics of online problem-based learning: Why and when sequence matters. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09385-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Four multilevel regression models (RQ3) used interaction-type frequencies to predict grades, with course as random effect; the cognitive model showed "Argue... and Discuss... both with a positive association with grades", and the high-cognitive model was significant for Argue and Evaluate.

> "The variables that were statistically significant were Argue (beta = 0.36, 95% CI [8.54e-03, 0.72], t(190) = 2.02, p = 0.045) and Discuss (beta = 0.22, 95% CI [0.03, 0.42],  t(190) = 2.27, p = 0.024), both with a positive association with grades."
