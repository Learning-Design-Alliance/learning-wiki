# Students taught with the TEFA-T model rated their own metacognitive skills higher than a conventionally taught class

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Independent t-test showed a statistically significant difference in self-reported metacognitive skills (a 5-point Likert scale adapted from Schraw & Dennison) between the TEFA-T class and the conventional class (significance value less than 0.05). [→ Maksum 2022](#maksum-2022)
`q2 i?` TEFA-T students rated themselves higher than the conventional class on every indicator of the self-report metacognition scale (knowledge, beliefs, planning, monitoring, evaluation). [→ Maksum 2022 (2)](#maksum-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Maksum 2022

Maksum, H., Yuvenda, D. & Purwanto, W. (2022). Improvement of metacognitive and critical thinking skills through development of the a 'Teaching Factory Based on Troubleshooting' (TEFA-T) Model in automotive vocational learning. Journal of Turkish Science Education, 19(3), 1015-1036. https://doi.org/10.36681/tused.2022.161

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 32 experimental and 32 control students (intact classes) after 8 learning meetings; metacognitive skills were measured with a 72-item, 5-point Likert self-report scale adapted from Schraw & Dennison (1994), not a performance task. The article reports only the significance value, with no effect size, and concludes the effect on metacognition is "quite large when compared to the conventional model".

> "The independent t-test result showed that the significance value of the metacognitive skills between the control and experimental classes was less than 0.05."

### Maksum 2022 (2)

Maksum, H., Yuvenda, D. & Purwanto, W. (2022). Improvement of metacognitive and critical thinking skills through development of the a 'Teaching Factory Based on Troubleshooting' (TEFA-T) Model in automotive vocational learning. Journal of Turkish Science Education, 19(3), 1015-1036. https://doi.org/10.36681/tused.2022.161

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-indicator comparison of the self-report Likert metacognition scale, plotted in Figure 4 (evaluation, monitoring, planning, metacognitive beliefs, metacognitive knowledge) favouring the TEFA-T class; the conclusion reports percentage gains of 29.58% to 31.88% across indicators, which are descriptive gains, not effect sizes.

> "Figure 4 shows that after ten weeks of implementing the TEFA-T model, students that used it had better metacognitive skills for all indicators compared to the conventional approach."
