# Fresh Start pupils made more progress in literacy than control pupils after 22 weeks, with an overall effect size of +0.24

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` In a two-arm randomised controlled trial across 10 secondary schools, Year 7 pupils receiving Fresh Start for 22 weeks made more progress in literacy than control pupils, with an overall gain-score effect size of +0.24, equivalent to approximately 3 months of additional progress in reading age. [→ Stephen Gorard 2015](#stephen-gorard-2015)

## Evidence

### Stephen Gorard 2015

Stephen Gorard, Nadia Siddiqui, Beng Huat See. (2015). Fresh Start Evaluation Report and Executive Summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581126

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Two-arm school-led randomised controlled trial with 433 Year 7 pupils in 10 secondary schools, analysed by intention to treat using gain scores on GL Assessment's New Group Reading Test A and B. The report states "The overall effect size was +0.24", equivalent to about 3 months of additional reading progress.

> "The FS pupils in the intervention group made more progress in literacy than the control group after 22 weeks. The overall effect size was +0.24 in terms of the gain between the scores in the test before the intervention"
