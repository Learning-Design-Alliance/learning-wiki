# Exploratory analysis found lower-funded schools (below $14,000 per pupil) grew graduation rates faster (1.4%) than higher-funded schools (0.75%), suggesting compensatory funding does not explain the negative spending trend

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In an exploratory check, schools with average funding below $14,000 per pupil had an average graduation-rate growth of 1.4% versus 0.75% for schools above $14,000, contrary to a compensatory-funding explanation. [→ Adam Raabe 2024](#adam-raabe-2024)

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Exploratory analysis in the Discussion section testing whether lower-performing schools receiving extra funding would show higher growth; the article reports the investigation "seemed to suggest that this is not the case" and no statistical test is printed.

> "Schools that received an average funding of less than $14,000 per pupil had an average growth rate of 1.4%, while schools that received an average funding of more than $14,000 per pupil only had an average growth rate of 0.75%."
