# Introductory students answered a conceptual momentum question at roughly half the correct rate of an isomorphic quantitative problem, but performed equally on both when the quantitative question came first

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The percentage correct on a conceptual boat-momentum question was roughly half that on a corresponding quantitative problem, but a separate group given both questions in sequence performed equally well on both. [→ Singh 2013](#singh-2013)

## Evidence

### Singh 2013

Singh, C., and Marshman, E. (2013). Analogous Patterns of Student Reasoning Difficulties in Introductory Physics and Upper-Level Quantum Mechanics. 2013 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2013.inv.010

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Three groups of introductory students received multiple-choice questions: 138 students given a quantitative problem asking for the expression (Ft), 215 given a similar conceptual comparison question, and 289 given both in sequence. Interviews suggest students who solved the quantitative problem used their expression (Ft) to answer the conceptual question.

> "The percentage of students providing the correct response for the conceptual question was roughly half of the percentage of students who correctly answered the quantitative problem. When a third group of 289 students (different from the first two) was given both questions with the quantitative question followed by the conceptual question, they performed equally well on both."
