## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Jones Gareth G. 2018

Jones Gareth G., Logishetty K., Clarke S., Collins R., Jaere M., Harris S., Cobb J. P. (2018). Do patient-specific instruments (PSI) for UKA allow non-expert surgeons to achieve the same saw cut accuracy as expert surgeons? Archives of Orthopaedic and Trauma Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00402-018-3031-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Sawbone experiment comparing 4 high-volume UKA surgeons and 34 trainees with no prior UKA experience performing tibial saw cuts with conventional Oxford Phase III instruments; the results section reports the experts' mean error was "significantly lower than the trainees’" in axial and sagittal planes, and Table 1 reports compound error 11.6° (4.0°) for trainees versus 7.7° (2.3°) for experts, p = 0.029.

> "For both groups using conventional instruments, the experts’ mean error was significantly lower than the trainees’ in both the axial and sagittal planes, but interestingly not in the coronal plane"
