# Universal screening data from a small sample of full-implementation schools show fall-to-spring benchmark gains in reading and most math grades, largest in kindergarten, but behavior data show losses and the data are inconclusive

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average fall-to-spring gains in the percentage of students at benchmark were positive for reading (largest in kindergarten and first grade) and for math except third grade, with the largest math gain in kindergarten. [→ Kristin Reedy 2015](#kristin-reedy-2015)
`q2 i?` Behavior benchmark data for grades 6–8 show a loss of progress over the school year, and the small number of reporting schools makes the Building-Level Status Form data inconclusive. [→ Kristin Reedy 2015 (2)](#kristin-reedy-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Kristin Reedy 2015

Kristin Reedy, Ed.D., and Natalie Lacireno-Paquet, Ph.D. (2015). Implementation and Outcomes of Kansas Multi-Tier System of Supports: Final Evaluation Report—2014. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED559729

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Building-Level Status Form data from full-implementation schools serving grades K–5 (Tables 4–5); e.g., kindergarten reading averaged a 9.4 percentage-point gain and kindergarten math 18.5, while third-grade math averaged -9.2. The report states these data cannot be considered causal.

> "Universal screening assessment data from a sample of schools shows gains on average for reading and math, with the exception of third grade math. The largest gains were evidenced in Kindergarten."

### Kristin Reedy 2015 (2)

Kristin Reedy, Ed.D., and Natalie Lacireno-Paquet, Ph.D. (2015). Implementation and Outcomes of Kansas Multi-Tier System of Supports: Final Evaluation Report—2014. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED559729

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Behavior data tracked as office discipline referrals for grades 6–8 from five schools (Table 6), with average changes of -7.6, -9.8, and -7.92 percentage points. The report calls the low number of reporting schools inconclusive.

> "The behavior data show a loss of progress over the course of the school year. It is important to note that Building-Level Status Form data are from a small number of schools and cannot be considered causal."
