# L2 oral language proficiency predicts reading comprehension more strongly than decoding skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Oral language proficiency accounts for substantial variance in L2 reading comprehension, while decoding skills play only a minor role in its development. [→ Khatib 2011](#khatib-2011)

## Evidence

### Khatib 2011

Khatib, M., & Fat'hi, J. (2011). The Effect of Automatization of the Phonological Component on the Reading Comprehension of ESP Students. International Education Studies, 4(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v4n4p192

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article reports, citing Droop and Verhoeven (2003), that bilingual Turkish-Dutch children comparable in word recognition performed more poorly in reading comprehension than monolingual peers, attributed to lower syntactic ability and oral fluency. No effect size is printed.

> "both for native speakers and for L2 speakers, decoding skills played only a minor role in the development of reading comprehension, and according to the authors, decoding and reading comprehension appear to develop as independent skills from third grade on"
