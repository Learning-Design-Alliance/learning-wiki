Evidence¶

Eveline L. de Zeeuw 2019¶

Eveline L. de Zeeuw, Kees-Jan Kan, Catharina E. M. van Beijsterveldt, Hamdi Mbarek, Jouke-Jan Hottenga, Gareth E. Davies, Michael C. Neale, Conor V. Dolan and Dorret I. Boomsma. (2019). The moderating role of SES on genetic differences in educational achievement in the Netherlands. npj Science of Learning 4:13. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0052-2

q2 · i? · associational · r3

Classical univariate twin model on the total sample: MZ and DZ twin correlations of 0.79 and 0.40, heritability of 72%, and a non-significant common environmental component after controlling for SES (Δ−2LL = 0.0, p = 0.963).

"In the total sample twin correlations were 0.79 (MZ) and 0.40 (DZ) and individual differences in EA were highly heritable (72%). The common environmental variance (8%) was attributable to SES effects as shown by the non-signi ﬁcant contribution of the C variance component after controlling for SES ( Δ−2LL = 0.0, Δdf = 1, p = 0.963)."

Related Claims¶

  • Twin-specific shared environments account for a substantial portion of CTD shared-environment estimates for educational attainment and cannot be interpreted as between-family environmental differences — related
  • Genetic and environmental variance components of educational achievement decrease with increasing parental SES — related
  • Adjusting published twin-study ACE estimates for assortative mating raises mean heritability by about 17 points and lowers shared environmental estimates by about 16 points — related
  • The best-fitting NTFD model estimates heritability of educational attainment at 51% and total shared environmental influence at 26%, disaggregated into twin-specific (16%) and sibling-shared (10%) components — related
  • Under phenotypic assortment NTFD models, parental education shows no environmental effect on offspring educational attainment once genetic influences are accounted for — related
  • A Classical Twin Design model of educational attainment in the TwinLife sample estimates shared environmental influence at 43% and additive genetic influence at 34% — related
  • Average educational achievement at age 12 increases with parental SES in the Netherlands — related
  • Environmental factors such as social support modulate classic individual-difference findings, including delay of gratification and heritability estimates — related
  • School quality does not moderate genetic and shared-environmental influences on educational performance once moderation by school SES and parental SES is considered — related
  • Twin and adoption studies suggest that 50–80% of IQ variation is due to genetic differences, with the range reflecting population heritability — related
  • GPAΔ is strikingly highly heritable (85%), exceeding the heritability of achievement itself (67%) — related
  • Educational achievement is highly heritable across school years and subjects, with twin heritability around 60% and SNP heritability around 30% — related
  • Genetic factors account for a substantial share of variation in educational outcomes and related behavioral traits — related
  • Age-to-age change in achievement is largely explained by non-shared environmental factors, while genetic and shared environmental influences are stable — related
  • Heritability estimates are population-specific parameters, and the heritability of cognitive skills rises gradually through childhood and adolescence — related
  • High heritability does not imply that environmental interventions to modify a trait are doomed to failure — related
  • A large share of the genetic influence on secondary-school attainment is due to genes that did not influence primary-school performance — related
  • Genetic factors account for most of the longitudinal stability of educational achievement (70% in the common pathway model; 63–79% of bivariate phenotypic correlations) — related
