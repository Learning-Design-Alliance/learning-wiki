## Subclaims
`q1 i?` As individuals repeatedly cycle through knowledge acquisition, they develop a default, context-independent rewarding value of these processes, explaining why curiosity and interest appear as both state and trait. [→ Kou Murayama 2019](#kou-murayama-2019)

## Evidence

### Kou Murayama 2019

Kou Murayama, Lily FitzGibbon & Michiko Sakaki. (2019). Process Account of Curiosity and Interest: A Reward-Learning Perspective. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09499-9

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical account in the review: trait constructs (sensation seeking, need for cognition, novelty seeking, vocational interests) are interpreted as generalized reward values explained by the generalization process in reward learning, consistent with a social-cognitive view of personality development.

> "As individuals go through the cycle of knowledge acquisition repeatedly, they develop a default rewarding value of these different processes, forming a context independent, personality-like curiosity or interest."
