# ROOTS participation reduced the achievement gap between at-risk and non-at-risk students within intervention classrooms on the TEMA

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` ROOTS intervention students' gains exceeded those of non-ROOTS students in the same intervention classrooms by 3.5 TEMA standard-score points (t = 2.33, df = 27, p = .0273), but not on the EN-CBM (t = −1.45, p = .1579). [→ Clarke 2016](#clarke-2016)
`q3 i?` In control classrooms, students selected for small-group instruction made significantly smaller EN-CBM gains (−14.3) than non-selected peers (t = −2.55, df = 27, p = .0169), consistent with a widening gap absent intervention. [→ Clarke 2016 (2)](#clarke-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Clarke 2016

Clarke, B., Doabler, C. T., Smolkowski, K., Baker, S. K., Fien, H., & Strand Cary, M. (2016). Examining the Efficacy of a Tier 2 Kindergarten Mathematics Intervention. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 49(2), 152–165. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219414538514

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second mixed time-by-condition analysis including the complete sample with ROOTS participation as a moderator, testing gains of ROOTS students versus non-participating peers within intervention classrooms. The differential gain was significant on the TEMA only.

> "The gains made by ROOTS intervention students exceeded gains by students who did not receive ROOTS by 3.5 on the TEMA standard score (t = 2.33, df = 27, p = .0273) but not on the EN-CBM (t = −1.45, df = 27, p = .1579)."

### Clarke 2016 (2)

Clarke, B., Doabler, C. T., Smolkowski, K., Baker, S. K., Fien, H., & Strand Cary, M. (2016). Examining the Efficacy of a Tier 2 Kindergarten Mathematics Intervention. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 49(2), 152–165. https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219414538514

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Unhypothesized secondary analysis within control classrooms comparing selected versus non-selected students on EN-CBM gains. The authors interpret it as consistent with the intervention keeping the gap from widening; the discussion links the widening control gap to the Matthew effect.

> "students selected for small-group instruction in control classrooms made statistically significantly smaller gains on the EN-CBM (−14.3) than students not selected in control classrooms (t = −2.55, df = 27, p = .0169)"
