# Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Early Childhood Express Math produces statistically significant positive effects on preschool children's math outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`–`q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In the Klein et al. (2008) RCT, differences between the intervention group and the comparison group on the Child Math Assessment were statistically significant, with an effect size of 0.52. [→ U.S. Department of Education 2013](#us-department-of-education-2013)
`q2 i2` In the PCER Consortium (2008) RCT, the domain average effect size for math across three measures was 0.47, statistically significant, with the Building Blocks Shape Composition effect size of 0.91. [→ U.S. Department of Education 2013 (2)](#us-department-of-education-2013-2)

## Evidence

### U.S. Department of Education 2013

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2013). Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Early Childhood Express Math. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/381

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 40 classrooms (278 children analyzed) in Head Start and state-funded preschools in California and New York. The CMA effect size was 0.52 with intervention mean 0.55 (0.13) versus comparison 0.47 (0.14), p-value 0.00, improvement index +20. The WWC characterizes this as "a positive effect".

> "Klein et al. (2008) analyzed the effectiveness of Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Early Childhood Express Math on math using the Child Math Assessment (CMA). The authors reported that differences between the Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Early Childhood Express Math group and the comparison group were statistically significant."

### U.S. Department of Education 2013 (2)

U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, What Works Clearinghouse. (2013). Pre-K Mathematics with DLM Early Childhood Express Math. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/381

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial meeting WWC standards with reservations (high child attrition, nonrandom teacher replacement), 297 children analyzed. Math was assessed with the WJ-III Applied Problems subtest, the CMA-A, and the Building Blocks Shape Composition task; domain average effect size 0.47, improvement index +18. The Building Blocks Shape Composition effect size was 0.91 and statistically significant.

> "The WWC characterizes the average effect size across all findings as a statistically significant positive effect."
