# Socially-related factors trigger mixed-valence physiological activation; task-related factors trigger activation without emotional expressions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In mixed-valence segments, activation was triggered significantly more often through socially-related factors than pure task-related factors; in neutral segments, activation was triggered significantly more through task-related factors. [→ Törmänen 2021](#tormanen-2021)
`q2 i?` The overall relationship between trigger type and valence was significant (χ2 (3) = 21.355, V = 0.415, f = 124, p = 0.000). [→ Törmänen 2021 (2)](#tormanen-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Törmänen 2021

Törmänen, T., Järvenoja, H., & Mänty, K. (2021). Exploring groups’ affective states during collaborative learning: what triggers activating affect on a group level? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10037-0

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative content analysis of transcribed activating segments identified task-related (f = 68) and socially-related (f = 56) triggers; adjusted residuals for neutral segments were "z = 3.6" for task-related and "z = –3.6" for socially-related factors.

> "Physiological activation in neutral segments was triggered significantly more through pure task-related factors ( z = 3.6, f = 27, p < 0.001), than socially-related factors (z = –3.6, f = 6, p < 0.001)."

### Törmänen 2021 (2)

Törmänen, T., Järvenoja, H., & Mänty, K. (2021). Exploring groups’ affective states during collaborative learning: what triggers activating affect on a group level? Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-021-10037-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square test of independence relating trigger type (task vs. social) to observed valence of activating segments, reported as "χ2 (3) = 21.355, V = 0.415" with p = 0.000.

> "The relationship between these variables was significant (χ2 (3) = 21.355, V = 0.415, f = 124, p = 0.000)."
