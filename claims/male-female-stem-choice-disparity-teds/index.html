Evidence¶

Ferdinand 2024¶

Ferdinand, R., Malanchini, M. & Rimfeld, K. (2024). Mathematics interest, self-efficacy, and anxiety predict STEM career choice in emerging adulthood. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00275-1

q3 · i2 · associational · r3

Pearson's chi-square test on the N = 2254 subsample with degree/apprenticeship choices. The article prints OR = 2.63, an effect size; 21.9% of females vs 42.5% of males chose STEM careers.

"The p roportion of STEM career choices was found to differ signi ﬁcantly by sex, with males being more than 2.5 times as likely to make a STEM career choice than females, (X2(1, N = 2254) = 105, p < 0.001, OR = 2.63)"

Related Claims¶

  • Maths anxiety predicted STEM career choice in males but not females, although the sex difference itself was not statistically significant — related
  • Maths interest in adolescence is a significant independent predictor of STEM career choice in emerging adulthood, for both males and females, including after adjusting for maths achievement and SES — related
  • Maths self-efficacy predicted STEM career choice in females but not males, though the association was explained by maths achievement after adjustment — related
  • Female participants reported significantly higher maths anxiety and general anxiety, while males reported higher maths self-efficacy and maths interest — related
  • Male secondary students report significantly higher STEM career interest than female students across AI, computer science, and engineering at baseline — related
  • Girls show less confidence and interest in math and science from early adolescence, and a strong math/science self-concept predicts course choice and performance for both genders — related
