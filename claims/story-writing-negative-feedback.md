## Evidence

### Kontur 2014

Kontur, F. J. (2014). Writing Science Fiction Stories to Motivate Analysis of Journal Articles. mailto:frederick.kontur@usafa.edu. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1403.2435](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1403.2435)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-semester survey in the fall 2012 offering; the article reports response themes and representative comments in Table II rather than coded counts. Comments described the activity as a waste of time and out of place in physics.

> "student comments on an end-of-semester survey about the project were overwhelmingly negative. Table II gives a summary of the feedback."

### Kontur 2014 (2)

Kontur, F. J. (2014). Writing Science Fiction Stories to Motivate Analysis of Journal Articles. mailto:frederick.kontur@usafa.edu. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1403.2435](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1403.2435)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Second iteration in spring 2013 after the author increased rubric emphasis on science understanding and gave pre-discussion talks about the activity's purpose. Negative feedback persisted and two students did not submit a final draft.

> "the feedback at the end of spring 2013 was substantially the same as in fall 2012, and 2 of the students did not even bother turning in a final draft of the story."
