# VR, desktop simulation, and hands-on activities produced no significant differences in immediate post-test learning about Moon phases

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Post-test scores did not differ significantly across the VR, desktop, and hands-on conditions, though all conditions improved substantially from pre-test. [→ Madden J. 2020](#madden-j-2020)

`q3 i?` Average post-test performance was about 58% in all three conditions with no significant differences between VR, hands-on, and desktop conditions. [→ J. H. Madden 2019](#j-h-madden-2019)

## Evidence

### Madden J. 2020

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Between-subjects experiment with 172 undergraduates randomly assigned to VR, desktop, or hands-on Moon-phase conditions. The article reports "no significant differences between the post-test scores across the three conditions"; an ANCOVA controlling for pre-score also showed no differences (F(2,168) = 1.07, p = 0.344).

> "Student performance significantly increased from pre- to post-test by 25.3% on average (average score of 59.1%), with no significant differences between the post-test scores across the three conditions: F(2,169) = 0.815, p = 0.444"

### J. H. Madden 2019

J. H. Madden, A. S. Won, J. P. Schuldt, B. Kim, S. Pandita, Y. Sun, T. J. Stone, and N. G. Holmes. (2019). Virtual Reality as a Teaching Tool for Moon Phases and Beyond. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.madden)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-condition comparison of post-test scores in a randomized pre-post study of 172 Cornell students assigned to VR, hands-on, or desktop Moon phases activities. The article reports "no signiﬁcant differences between conditions" with F (2, 169) = 0 .86, p = 0.57; no effect size is printed.

> "The average score on the post-test was about 58% in all three conditions, with no signiﬁcant differences between conditions (F (2, 169) = 0 .86, p = 0.57)."
