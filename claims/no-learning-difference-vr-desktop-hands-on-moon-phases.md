## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-test scores did not differ significantly across the VR, desktop, and hands-on conditions, though all conditions improved substantially from pre-test. [→ Madden J. 2020](#madden-j-2020)

## Evidence

### Madden J. 2020

Madden J., Pandita S., Schuldt J. P., Kim B., S. Won A., Holmes N. G. (2020). Ready student one: Exploring the predictors of student learning in virtual reality. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229788

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Between-subjects experiment with 172 undergraduates randomly assigned to VR, desktop, or hands-on Moon-phase conditions. The article reports "no significant differences between the post-test scores across the three conditions"; an ANCOVA controlling for pre-score also showed no differences (F(2,168) = 1.07, p = 0.344).

> "Student performance significantly increased from pre- to post-test by 25.3% on average (average score of 59.1%), with no significant differences between the post-test scores across the three conditions: F(2,169) = 0.815, p = 0.444"
