## Evidence

### A. Gasparini 2026

A. Gasparini, F. Stern, M. Delaval, A. Müller. (2026). Using smartphones and tablets as experimental tools in the physics classroom: Effects on learning and motivation. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.10959](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.10959)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Main study quasi-experiment (N = 111, eight class groups, four teachers) in a regular Geneva high-school mechanics course; paired t-tests on the 19-item concept test showed significant pre/post gains with the printed large effect sizes (d > 0.8), e.g. total d = 1.0, TG d = 0.9, CG d = 1.1 (Tab. 6). The article also reports "the number of the most frequent incorrect answers fell from 60% in the pre-test to 25% in the post-test".

> "The results of the paired t-tests revealed significant gains for learning, with large effects sizes (d > 0.8) for all but one groups"
