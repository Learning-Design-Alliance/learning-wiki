# Students in labs designed to teach critical thinking score about 0.52 points higher at post-test controlling for pre-score

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Controlling for pre-scores, lab treatment has a statistically significant impact on post-scores, with CTLabs students scoring 0.52 points higher (β1 = 0.52 ± 0.15, p < 0.001). [→ Cole Walsh 2019](#cole-walsh-2019)

## Evidence

### Cole Walsh 2019

Cole Walsh, Katherine N. Quinn, C. Wieman, and N.G. Holmes. (2019). Quantifying critical thinking: Development and validation of the Physics Lab Inventory of Critical thinking (PLIC). [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.010135](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.15.010135)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Linear model predicting post-score from lab treatment and pre-score, fit to 273 FY students in critical-thinking labs and 1285 in other FY labs. The coefficient for CTLabs was 0.52 ± 0.15 with p < 0.001; pre-score coefficient 0.25 ± 0.05.

> "We see that, control- ling for pre-scores, lab treatment has a statistically signif- icant impact on post-scores; students trained in CTLabs perform 0.52 points higher, on average, at post-test com- pared to their counterparts trained in other labs with the same pre-score."
