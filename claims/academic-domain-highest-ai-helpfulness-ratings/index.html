Nourian 2026¶

Nourian, L.; Callis, A.; Patterson, S.; Miao, J.; Heard, J.; Tigwell, G. W. (2026). Understanding How International Students in the U.S. Are Using Conversational AI to Support Cross-Cultural Adaptation. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15127

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Descriptive analysis of 5-point Likert ratings per challenge domain in the survey of 60 international students. "Academic challenges had the highest scores across all three metrics"; the article adds that socio-cultural showed the lowest satisfaction mean and psychological the lowest usefulness mean.

"Academic challenges had the highest scores across all three metrics (e.g., Usefulness: Mean=4.27, SD=0.49, Satisfaction: Mean=3.88, SD=0.67, and Intent to Continue: Mean=4.38, SD=0.49)."

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Summary of the survey's three global helpfulness questions (Table 2: dealing with challenges Mean=4.00, growing 3.79, adapting 3.67). "international students had a positive view of conversational AI's helpfulness" with a consistent median of 4; descriptive statistics only, no effect size.

"Overall, international students had a positive view of conversational AI's helpfulness, as shown in Table 2, presented by the high percentage of positive responses (Agree/Strongly Agree) and a consistent median score of 4 (Agree) for all three of the global questions about helpfulness."
