## Evidence

### Gartland Nicola 2022

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's critical appraisal of the five papers measuring standardised test scores, GPA, or school performance indices rather than executive function. The review recommends also examining indirect effects via executive function given the strength of evidence linking working memory and academic performance.

> "The problem with these studies is that they are few in number, and all are of lower quality: they use estimated pollution variables rather than taking objective measures within the school setting, most just look at one test score, none model longitudinal effects, and two of the studies were ecological in design"

### Gartland Nicola 2022 (2)

Gartland Nicola, Aljofi Halah E., Dienes Kimberly, Munford Luke Aaron, Theakston Anna L., van Tongeren Martie. (2022). The Effects of Traffic Air Pollution in and around Schools on Executive Function and Academic Performance in Children: A Rapid Review. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020749

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The Grineski et al. Texas ecological study (n = 1888) used multi-level modelling showing school-level diesel PM risk predicted individual GPA even when controlling for school-level covariates (e.g., free meals eligibility, student–teacher ratio) and individual-level variables. The printed coefficient is unstandardised; no standardised effect size is given.

> "an inter-quartile range increase in on-road diesel PM risk was associated with a 0.16 reduction in GPA equivalent to 4%"
