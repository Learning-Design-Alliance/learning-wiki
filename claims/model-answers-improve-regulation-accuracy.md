# Model answers improve both monitoring-based and comprehension-based regulation accuracy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Model answers had a small significant main effect on monitoring-based regulation accuracy (partial η2 = .04) and on comprehension-based regulation accuracy (partial η2 = .02). [→ Hepner 2024](#hepner-2024)

## Evidence

### Hepner 2024

Hepner, S. L., Oudman, S., Carlson, T. E., van de Pol, J., & van Gog, T. (2024). Improving (meta)comprehension: Feedback and self-assessment. Learning and Instruction, 92, 101922. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101922

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the 258-student randomized experiment, mixed models on regulation accuracy (correlations between restudy decisions and, respectively, judgments of learning and actual test performance) showed "partial η2 = .04" for monitoring-based and "partial η2 = .02" for comprehension-based effects of model answers.

> "There was a small, significant main effect of model answers on monitoring-based regulation accuracy, F (2,252) = 5.14, p = .007, partial η2 = .04, and on comprehension-based regulation accuracy, F (2,252) = 3.04, p = .05, partial η2 = .02."
