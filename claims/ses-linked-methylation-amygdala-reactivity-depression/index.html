Evidence¶

Martha J. Farah 2017¶

Martha J. Farah. (2017). Socioeconomic status and the brain: prospects for neuroscience-informed policy. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-018-0023-2

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The Perspective reports, citing a longitudinal multimethod study of stress-related methylation of the serotonin transporter gene in adolescents, that "teenagers with lower SES showed a greater increase in methylation than their higher SES peers". No effect size is printed.

"Across two time-points, teenagers with lower SES showed a greater increase in methylation than their higher SES peers and this was associated with greater amygdala reactivity and more depression symptoms."
