# Reducing SBA options from five to three maintains exam reliability and fail rates while increasing mean percentage marks by 1-3%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean percentage mark increased by 1% with a four-option model and by 3% with a three-option model (p < 0.001 for all paired comparisons). [→ Kilgour 2016](#kilgour-2016)
`q2 i?` Alpha reliability remained constant across five-, four- and three-option models (0.81 average for all three). [→ Kilgour 2016](#kilgour-2016)
`q2 i?` Fail rates remained constant regardless of the number of options per question. [→ Kilgour 2016](#kilgour-2016)

## Evidence

### Kilgour 2016

Kilgour, J. M., & Saadia, T. (2016). An investigation into the optimal number of distractors in single-best answer exams. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 21(3), 571-585. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-015-9652-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Simulation study using ITEMAN frequency data to eliminate least functional distractors, with random redistribution of affected responses, modelled in SPSS. Paired samples t tests showed statistically significant difficulty changes (p < 0.001). Reliability and fail rates were unaffected.

> "These results reveal that the mean percentage mark for each paper would have increased by 1% following a change from the five-option paper to the four-option version, and would have increased by a total of 3% if the three-option model had been used."
