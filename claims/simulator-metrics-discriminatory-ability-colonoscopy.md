## Evidence

### Preisler Louise MD 2015

Preisler Louise MD, Svendsen Morten Bo Søndergaard MScEng, Nerup Nikolaj MD, Svendsen Lars Bo MD, DMSc, Konge Lars MD, PhD. (2015). Simulation-Based Training for Colonoscopy: Establishing Criteria for Competency. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000000440

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Metric screening for the virtual-reality simulator test using two-way mixed ANOVA with cases as repeated measures and experience as grouping factor. Only metrics with discriminatory ability entered the aggregated score; loop formation, patient discomfort, and hazardous tension to the bowel did not discriminate.

> "The following metrics had discriminatory ability: percentage of colonic mucosa visualized, percentage of time with clear view, intubation of terminal ileum, and time to reach cecum"

### Preisler Louise MD 2015 (2)

Preisler Louise MD, Svendsen Morten Bo Søndergaard MScEng, Nerup Nikolaj MD, Svendsen Lars Bo MD, DMSc, Konge Lars MD, PhD. (2015). Simulation-Based Training for Colonoscopy: Establishing Criteria for Competency. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000000440

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Metric screening for the phantom-model test using the purpose-built rating tool, with items scored 0 to 2 points on core technical skills. All tested metrics discriminated between novices and experienced endoscopists and entered the aggregated score.

> "Handling of scope, safe scope advancement, force used, efficiency of withdrawal, and ability to loop in rectum all added to the aggregated score. There were significant differences between the 2 groups and all metrics showed discriminatory ability."
