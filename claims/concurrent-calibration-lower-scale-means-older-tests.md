# Concurrent calibration yielded consistently lower and less fluctuating scale score means than separate calibration with chain or simultaneous equating, especially for older tests

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Scale score means from concurrent calibration (CR) were relatively less fluctuated and consistently lower than those from the separate-calibration equating methods (CH and SM), with the difference largest for tests prior to 2002F. [→ Jungnam Kim 2017](#jungnam-kim-2017)

## Evidence

### Jungnam Kim 2017

Jungnam Kim, Furong Gao, & Dong-In Kim. (2017). Robust IRT Scaling: Considerations in constructing item bank from tests across years. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, San Antonio, TX. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/robust-irt-scaling-considerations-in-constructing-item-bank-from-tests-across-years/

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Results section comparison of mean scale scores across 45 administrations (Figure 4; Tables 2-3). For example, 1992F summed-scoring means were 513.72 (CH_S), 514.37 (SM_S), and 424.92 (CR_S), showing the CR means were "consistently lower."

> "With the CR method, the score means across years were relatively less fluctuated and consistently lower, more so with the older tests, than those from the separate calibration methods."
