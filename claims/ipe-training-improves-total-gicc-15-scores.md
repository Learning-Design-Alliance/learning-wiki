# Medical students' total interpersonal communication competence scores on the GICC-15 rose from pre-test to post-test after a 3-hour interprofessional communication training, in one single-group pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Third-year medical students' total GICC-15 score increased from 55.60±6.94 before training to 58.89±7.34 after training, a statistically significant change (p=0.000). [→ Kim Seung Jae 2019](#kim-seung-jae-2019)

## Evidence

### Kim Seung Jae 2019

Kim Seung Jae, Kwon Oh Deog, Kim Kyae Hyung, Lee Ji Eun, Lee Seung-Hee, Shin Jwa-Seop, Park Sang Min. (2019). Investigating the effects of interprofessional communication education for medical students. Korean Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3946/kjme.2019.125

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post paired comparison in a single-session interprofessional training; paired t-tests compared GICC-15 totals before and after practice, with 144 of 149 students analyzed. The reported change was "statistically significant (p=0.000)" in the authors' words. No effect size was printed.

> "The total mean score of the students before practice was 55.60±6.94 points (mean±SD) out of a potential 75, and the total mean score after practice was 58.89±7.34, showing that students' overall communication competency was higher after practice."
