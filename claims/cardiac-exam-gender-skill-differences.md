# Female students had a significantly lower error frequency than male students in cardiac examination, driven by inspection and palpation, while percussion was more error-prone in females

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Average error frequency per student in cardiac examination was lower in females than males (p=0.041); inspection and palpation were more error-prone in males (both p=0.054, marginal) and percussion more error-prone in females (p=0.008). [→ Li Y 2014](#li-y-2014)
`q3 i?` In pulmonary examination the gender difference was not statistically significant (p=0.074), and skill-level differences were not significant (p=0.736). [→ Li Y 2014 (2)](#li-y-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Li Y 2014

Li Y, Li N, He Q, He S, Bae RS, Lv Z, Lv Y, Shi B. (2014). Performance of Physical Examination Skills in Medical Students during Diagnostic Medicine Course in a University Hospital of Northwest China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109294

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subgroup analysis of the cardiac examination assessments by gender and PE skill (inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation). Percussion in cardiac examination was "more error-prone in female students (p = 0.008, Table 4)"; overall skill distribution differed by gender (p=0.009).

> "The average error frequency per student in females was less than that in males in cardiac examination (p = 0.041, Table 3)."

### Li Y 2014 (2)

Li Y, Li N, He Q, He S, Bae RS, Lv Z, Lv Y, Shi B. (2014). Performance of Physical Examination Skills in Medical Students during Diagnostic Medicine Course in a University Hospital of Northwest China. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0109294

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the same subgroup analysis, pulmonary examination showed no statistically significant gender difference overall (p=0.074) or in its component skills (p=0.736); percussion was the most error-prone skill in both genders. Equivalence was not tested.

> "The error frequency in pulmonary examination in male students was higher than that in female although the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.074, Table 3)."
