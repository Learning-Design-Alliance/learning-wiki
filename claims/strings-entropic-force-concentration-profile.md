# In a strings-and-particles channel task, most graduate students correctly predicted reduced string conformations and a dipped concentration profile via an entropic trade-off

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Seven of eight graduate students correctly decided the number of string conformations decreases when circle particles enter the strings region, most explaining this via volume exclusion. [→ Nathan Crossette 2021](#nathan-crossette-2021)
`q2 i?` Five students drew the correct concentration profile with a dip in the center region, reflecting a trade-off between entropy gained by the balls and entropy lost by the strings. [→ Nathan Crossette 2021 (2)](#nathan-crossette-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Nathan Crossette 2021

Nathan Crossette, Michael Vignal, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2021). Investigating graduate student reasoning on a conceptual entropy questionnaire. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020119](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020119)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Part E of the Strings question, in which particles enter a channel region containing strings attached to the walls; responses summarized in Table IV. The article reports 'seven students (correctly) decided it would decrease,' six attributing this to particles excluding volume from the strings.

> "When considering what happens to the number of conformations of the strings, seven students (correctly) decided it would decrease when the circles and strings interact."

> "On the whole, ﬁve students (not including Alex) decided the concentration proﬁle would have a dip, which we con- sidered the correct answer, similar to Beth’s third sketch i n Fig. 5."
