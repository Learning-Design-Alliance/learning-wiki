# The hypothetico-deductive approach yields slightly better delayed diagnostic accuracy than small-group self-explanation when learning clinical cases

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Second-year medical students who learned seven cases through hypothetico-deduction in small groups diagnosed new cardiovascular cases one week later more accurately than students who learned through self-explanation, with a small effect. [→ Al Rumayyan Ahmed 2018](#al-rumayyan-ahmed-2018)

## Evidence

### Al Rumayyan Ahmed 2018

Al Rumayyan Ahmed, Ahmed Nasr, Al Subait Reem, Al Ghamdi Ghassan, Mohammed Mahzari Moeber, Awad Mohamed Tarig, Rotgans Jerome I., Donmez Mustafa, Mamede Silvia, Schmidt Henk G. (2018). Teaching clinical reasoning through hypothetico-deduction is (slightly) better than self-explanation in tutorial groups: An experimental study. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-018-0409-x

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

A randomized experiment with 88 second-year medical students (49 female, 39 male) working in groups of approximately six on seven clinical vignettes, then tested on eight new cases one week later. Diagnostic accuracy on the four criterion cardiovascular cases was higher in the hypothetico-deduction condition, "F(1, 86) = 4.20, p = 0.04", Cohen’s d = 0.38.

> "Students in the hypothetico-deduction condition performed better than those in the self-explanation condition, F(1, 86) = 4.20, p = 0.04. The effect size was small (Cohen’s d = 0.38) [18]."
