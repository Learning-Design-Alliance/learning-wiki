# The lab session corrected student misconceptions about optical phase and optical versus electrical spectra, with positive feedback over four years

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Across four years (2012–2016, about forty students), the sessions corrected misconceptions, including the idea that the electrical spectrum is a simple enlargement of the optical spectrum. [→ K Hammani 2019](#k-hammani-2019)

## Evidence

### K Hammani 2019

K Hammani, J. Fatome, and C Finot. (2019). Applications of sinusoidal phase modulation in temporal optics to highlight some properties of the Fourier transform. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab1a54](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab1a54)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' evaluative conclusion from running the session 2012–2016 with about forty students: feedback was very positive from both students and supervisors, and the sessions helped students understand the complementarity of different signal analyses. This is the authors' interpretation, not a measured outcome.

> "The sessions provided a real dialogue around the importance of the optical phase, telecommunications and the concept of optical and electrical spectra, correcting a number of misconceptions that students had in mind."

### K Hammani 2019 (2)

K Hammani, J. Fatome, and C Finot. (2019). Applications of sinusoidal phase modulation in temporal optics to highlight some properties of the Fourier transform. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab1a54](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab1a54)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' stated observation that live experimental displays on the instruments complemented numerical simulation tools; the session also enabled discussion of current academic research topics, including why self-phase modulation causes spectral broadening.

> "We have found that the practical examples displayed directly on the measuring devices can be a very valuable complement to the numerical simulation tool by reducing the potential passivity of some students."
