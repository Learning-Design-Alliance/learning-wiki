# The learning benefit of the worked-example textbook was greater for students with lower pretest proportional reasoning skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A significant negative treatment × pretest proportional reasoning interaction indicated treatment students with poorer pretest proportional reasoning benefited more, gaining more probabilistic reasoning at posttest. [→ Begolli 2021](#begolli-2021)

## Evidence

### Begolli 2021

Begolli, K. N., Dai, T., McGinn, K. M., & Booth, J. L. (2021). Could probability be out of proportion? Self-explanation and example-based practice help students with lower proportional reasoning skills learn probability. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-021-09550-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression interaction from the same cluster-randomized classroom experiment (Model 4, N = 220). The printed coefficient was "bTreatment x PR Pretest = − 0.34, SE = 0.15, t = − 2.34, p = 0.044", plotted in Fig. 4 with 95% confidence interval bars.

> "the significant interaction between treatment and pretest Proportional Reasoning scores (bTreatment x PR Pretest = − 0.34, SE = 0.15, t = − 2.34, p = 0.044) had a small negative influence on posttest Probabilistic Reasoning scores, indicating that the treatment participants with poorer pretest Proportional Reasoning scores actually benefited more from the treatment"
