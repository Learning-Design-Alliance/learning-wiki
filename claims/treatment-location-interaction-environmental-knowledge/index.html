Evidence¶

Ajitoni 2015¶

Ajitoni, S. O., & Gbadamosi, T. V. (2015). Community-Based Instructional Strategies, School Location, and Primary School Pupils' Environmental Knowledge. JISTE Vol. 19, No. 2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1177058

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Two-way ANCOVA interaction of treatment by school location on environmental knowledge in the quasi-experiment; the printed effect size is η² = 0.084 and hypothesis 3 was rejected.

"Table 5 shows a significant 2 -way interaction effect of treatment and school location on pupils' environmental knowledge: F (2, 251) = 11.57, p= 0.00;𝜂² = 0.084). Hence, hypothesis 3 is rejected."

Ajitoni 2015 (2)¶

Ajitoni, S. O., & Gbadamosi, T. V. (2015). Community-Based Instructional Strategies, School Location, and Primary School Pupils' Environmental Knowledge. JISTE Vol. 19, No. 2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1177058

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Estimated marginal means (Table 5) show educational trips favoured urban pupils (mean 14.62 vs 12.21) while service learning favoured peri-urban pupils (13.17 vs 12.08); the article describes the pattern as disordinal. No effect size is printed for this directional pattern.

"Figure 1 shows a disordinal interaction effect of treatment and school location on environmental knowledge in which the effect of the treatment on environmental knowledge of the students does not follow the same trend across the school location."
