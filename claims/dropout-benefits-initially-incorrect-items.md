# The dropout schedule was particularly effective for initially incorrect items, an advantage not attributable to initial ISI length or item difficulty in Experiment 2

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Initially incorrect items were significantly more likely to be correct at final test in the 23-drop group than in either conventional group. [→ Pyc 2007](#pyc-2007)
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, the 23-drop group also showed an advantage for initially incorrect items over both conventional groups. [→ Pyc 2007 (2)](#pyc-2007-2)

## Evidence

### Pyc 2007

Pyc, M. A., & Rawson, K. A. (2007). Examining the efficiency of schedules of distributed retrieval practice. Memory & Cognition, 35(8), 1917-1927. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193420

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 conditional analyses (Table 2), t(41) = 2.86, p = .007 versus 5-5-5 and t(41) = 2.31, p = .026 versus 23-23-23. Since 23-drop and 23-23-23 shared the same initial ISI and similar numbers of incorrect items, the authors attribute the advantage to additional practice, with 4.7 initially incorrect items receiving four or more trials on average.

> "Initially incorrect items were significantly more likely to be correct at final test in the 23-drop group than in either the 5-5-5 group or the 23-23-23 group"

### Pyc 2007 (2)

Pyc, M. A., & Rawson, K. A. (2007). Examining the efficiency of schedules of distributed retrieval practice. Memory & Cognition, 35(8), 1917-1927. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193420

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 conditional analyses (Table 1), t(63) = 1.90, p = .067 versus 5-5-5 and t(61) = 2.02, p = .048 versus 1-5-9; 5.1 initially incorrect items received four or more test-restudy trials on average.

> "Indeed, this was the case for the 23-drop group, with initially incorrect items more likely to be correct at final test than in the 5-5-5 and 1-5-9 groups"
