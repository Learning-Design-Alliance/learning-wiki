# The path from psychological quality to professional recognition is stronger in applied disciplines (Social Sciences, Engineering) than in theoretical disciplines (Humanities, Natural Sciences)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The PQ→PR path is not invariant across disciplines (Δχ² = 10.403, p = 0.015), with applied disciplines showing stronger coefficients (Engineering β = 0.621; Social Sciences β = 0.577) than theoretical ones (Humanities β = 0.384; Natural Sciences β = 0.372). [→ Tian Qi 2026](#tian-qi-2026)
`q2 i?` Pairwise Wald tests show the PQ→PR effect is significantly stronger in Social Sciences than in Humanities (p = 0.033) and Natural Sciences (p = 0.003). [→ Tian Qi 2026 (2)](#tian-qi-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Tian Qi 2026

Tian Qi, Huang Yinpin, Xiong Jun. (2026). Psychological quality and entrepreneurial competence: the mediating role of professional recognition and moderating effect of academic discipline among Chinese university students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-03954-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM across four disciplinary groups (n = 741) after establishing full metric invariance (ΔCFI = −0.002; ΔRMSEA = 0.000): "Applied disciplines (Engineering Sciences β = 0.621; Social Sciences β = 0.577) are stronger than those of Theoretical disciplines"; the path-specific invariance test gave Δχ² = 10.403, p = 0.015.

> "Applied disciplines (Engineering Sciences β = 0.621; Social Sciences β = 0.577) are stronger than those of Theoretical disciplines (Humanities β = 0.384; Natural Sciences β = 0.372)."

### Tian Qi 2026 (2)

Tian Qi, Huang Yinpin, Xiong Jun. (2026). Psychological quality and entrepreneurial competence: the mediating role of professional recognition and moderating effect of academic discipline among Chinese university students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-03954-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pairwise Wald comparisons within the multi-group analysis (n = 741): "the effect of PQ on PR was significantly stronger in Social Sciences as compared to Humanities (p = 0.033) and Natural Sciences (p = 0.003)"; the Engineering vs. Natural Sciences contrast was not significant (p = 0.052).

> "Pairwise Wald tests confirmed that the effect of PQ on PR was significantly stronger in Social Sciences as compared to Humanities (p = 0.033) and Natural Sciences (p = 0.003)."
