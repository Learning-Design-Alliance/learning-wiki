Evidence¶

Montero Perez 2014¶

Montero Perez, M., Peters, E., Clarebout, G., & Desmet, P. (2014). Effects of Captioning on Video Comprehension and Incidental Vocabulary Learning. Language Learning & Technology 18(1), 118–141. http://llt.msu.edu/issues/february2014/monteroperezetal.pdf

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Post hoc Bonferroni comparisons following a significant MANCOVA (Wilks's lambda, F(12, 301.91) = 4.63, p < .001, ηp² = .14) on four vocabulary tests with 122 participants. The article reports "captioned groups significantly outperformed the control group on form recognition and clip association."

"The results of the post hoc analysis (Bonferroni correction) partially confirmed our hypotheses: captioned groups significantly outperformed the control group on form recognition and clip association. However, no differences between the captioning groups were found."

Montero Perez 2014 (2)¶

Montero Perez, M., Peters, E., Clarebout, G., & Desmet, P. (2014). Effects of Captioning on Video Comprehension and Incidental Vocabulary Learning. Language Learning & Technology 18(1), 118–141. http://llt.msu.edu/issues/february2014/monteroperezetal.pdf

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Between-subjects effects from the same MANCOVA (Table 11) show the captioning effect decreased as the vocabulary component increased in difficulty: form recognition ηp² = .27, clip association ηp² = .16, meaning recognition ηp² = .11.

"The effect size of type of captioning was largest for form recognition (ηp² = .27) and clip association (ηp² = .16) and smaller for meaning recognition (ηp² = .11)."
