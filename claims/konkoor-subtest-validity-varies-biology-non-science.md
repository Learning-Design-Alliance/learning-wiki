## Evidence

### Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin 2012

Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin, Nedjat Saharnaz, Mohammadi Aeen, Rad Elaheh Malakan, Majdzadeh Reza, Monajemi Farshid, Jamali Ehsan, Yazdani Shahryar. (2012). The validity of Iran’s national university entrance examination (Konkoor) for predicting medical students’ academic performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-60

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlation and regression analyses across the three outcome measures in the national cohort; the authors note biology maintained a strong relationship with all three outcomes while mathematics and physics were weakest with msGPA.

> "In the present study, the science sub-scores – especially biology – showed the largest predictive validity for all outcome variables."

### Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin 2012 (2)

Farrokhi-Khajeh-Pasha Yasin, Nedjat Saharnaz, Mohammadi Aeen, Rad Elaheh Malakan, Majdzadeh Reza, Monajemi Farshid, Jamali Ehsan, Yazdani Shahryar. (2012). The validity of Iran’s national university entrance examination (Konkoor) for predicting medical students’ academic performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-60

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stepwise regression models C2 and C3 on the national cohort (msGPAs for 1822 students); in model C4 only biology remained among science sub-scores, and no science sub-score remained once hsGPAs were added (C5).

> "In contrast to the CPIE, Konkoor non-science sub-scores were better predictors of msGPAs (R2= 0.133) than science sub-scores (R2= 0.097)."
