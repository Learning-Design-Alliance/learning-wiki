# Students' template-scored problem-solving skills improved over the term, with overall scores rising from 45% to 55%

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (4 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Overall problem-solving scores, scored against the template on homework from weeks 1, 4, and 9, increased steadily from 45% to 55% (p < 0.001). [→ Burkholder 2021](#burkholder-2021)
`q2 i?` Planning scores rose from 55% to 82% and plan-following scores from 60% to 75% (both p < 0.001). [→ Burkholder 2021 (2)](#burkholder-2021-2)
`q2 i?` Problem-representation scores decreased at the last time point, from 70% to about 48% (p < 0.001), as visual representations became less detailed. [→ Burkholder 2021 (3)](#burkholder-2021-3)
`q2 i?` Students' z-score ranking in the class improved by 0.26 standard deviations from week 1 to week 9 (p = 0.054), which the authors read as the lowest-performing students improving more relative to the class. [→ Burkholder 2021 (4)](#burkholder-2021-4)

## Evidence

### Burkholder 2021

Burkholder, E., Salehi, S., Sackeyfio, S., Mohamed-Hinds, N., & Wieman, C. (2021). An equitable and effective approach to introductory mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Template-based scoring of students' solutions to three homework problems from weeks 1, 4, and 9 of the course. The article reports "students' overall problem-solving scores increased steadily from 45% to 55% during the course (p < 0.001)".

> "We found that students' overall problem-solving scores increased steadily from 45% to 55% during the course (p < 0.001)."

### Burkholder 2021 (2)

Burkholder, E., Salehi, S., Sackeyfio, S., Mohamed-Hinds, N., & Wieman, C. (2021). An equitable and effective approach to introductory mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same template-based homework scoring: "students showed substantial increases in their ability to plan their solution (55% to 82%, p < 0.001) and their ability to follow their proposed plans (60% to 75%, p < 0.001)".

> "Indeed, students showed substantial increases in their ability to plan their solution (55% to 82%, p < 0.001) and their ability to follow their proposed plans (60% to 75%, p < 0.001)."

### Burkholder 2021 (3)

Burkholder, E., Salehi, S., Sackeyfio, S., Mohamed-Hinds, N., & Wieman, C. (2021). An equitable and effective approach to introductory mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504)

> "Interestingly, there was a decrease in their problem-representation at the last time point (From 70% to about 48%, p < 0.001), as students' visual representation of the problem became less detailed."

### Burkholder 2021 (4)

Burkholder, E., Salehi, S., Sackeyfio, S., Mohamed-Hinds, N., & Wieman, C. (2021). An equitable and effective approach to introductory mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2111.12504)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same homework scores converted to within-class z-scores each week showed "an increase of 0.26 standard deviations from Week 1 to Week 9 (p = 0.054)"; the article prints no standardized effect size for this marginal result.

> "We found an increase of 0.26 standard deviations from Week 1 to Week 9 (p = 0.054), meaning that on average, a student's z-score (ranking in the class) improved by 0.26 over the course of the term."
