## Evidence

### Pavlo D. Antonenko 2007

Pavlo D. Antonenko, John Jackman, Piyamart Kumsaikaew, Rahul R. Marathe, Dale S. Niederhauser, C. A. Ogilvie, and Sarah M. Ryan. (2007). Understanding Student Pathways in Context-rich Problems. Originally published 2006. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0701284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0701284)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Scatterplot analysis (Figure 5) and Table 3 classify teams as expert-like or novice-like by whether qualitative analysis preceded viewing 80% of resources. The expert-like fraction "doubles from 26% in problem 1" to about half, then decreases to 39% in problem 5.

> "This fraction doubles from 26% in problem 1, to approximately half the teams for problems 3 to 4, and slightly decreases to 39% in problem 5 (see Table 3)."

### Pavlo D. Antonenko 2007 (2)

Pavlo D. Antonenko, John Jackman, Piyamart Kumsaikaew, Rahul R. Marathe, Dale S. Niederhauser, C. A. Ogilvie, and Sarah M. Ryan. (2007). Understanding Student Pathways in Context-rich Problems. Originally published 2006. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0701284](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0701284)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Table 3 reports mean performance alongside team classification for the five problems. The article states the correct-answer fraction "did not significantly differ between the two types of teams"; no effect size is printed.

> "The fraction of groups who entered a correct answer is also shown in this  table: this fraction did not significantly differ between the two types of teams."
