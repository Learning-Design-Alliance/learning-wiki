# Non-layered AI feedback produced significantly higher post-test performance than layered AI feedback after controlling for pre-test

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students receiving non-layered elaborated AI feedback scored significantly higher on the post-test than students receiving layered feedback (adjusted means 11.24 vs 10.40). [→ Jie Cao 2026](#jie-cao-2026)

## Evidence

### Jie Cao 2026

Jie Cao, Chloe Qianhui Zhao, Christian Schunn, Elizabeth A. McLaughlin, Jionghao Lin, Kenneth R. Koedinger. (2026). Assessing the Impact and Underlying Pathways of Sequenced AI Feedback on Student Learning. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07469

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized experiment (layered n=100, non-layered n=99) in an online higher-education lesson on the multimedia principle; ANCOVA on post-test with pre-test as covariate. The contrast was significant, t(196) = 2.32, p = .02, and the model accounted for 34% of variance (R2 = .34).

> "After controlling for initial performance, students in the non-layered feed- backgroupachievedsignificantlyhigherpost-testscores(M ad j = 11.24,SE= 0.25) than those in the layered feedback group (Mad j = 10.40,SE= 0.25)."
