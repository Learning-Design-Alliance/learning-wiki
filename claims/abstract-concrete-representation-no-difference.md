# Abstract and concrete animation representations do not differ significantly in middle school students' electricity achievement under any of the five text-representation conditions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Five independent t-tests found no significant abstract-versus-concrete differences for written, signaled written, spoken, written+spoken, or signaled written+spoken text conditions. [→ Kutbay 2020](#kutbay-2020)

## Evidence

### Kutbay 2020

Kutbay, E. & Akpınar, Y. (2020). Investigating modality, redundancy and signaling principles with abstract and concrete representation. International Journal of Education in Mathematics, Science and Technology (IJEMST), 8(2), 131-145. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1255527

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Five independent-sample t-tests in the quasi-experiment compared abstract with concrete animation under each text condition; none was significant (e.g., written text "t (152) = .45, p = .650"). Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "there is not a significant difference between abstract or concrete representation of animations with (a) written text  (t (152) = .45, p = .650), (b) signaled written text ( t (170) = 1.06, p = .288), (c) spoken text (t (167) = .13, p = .894)"
