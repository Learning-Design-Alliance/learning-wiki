## Evidence

### Walck-Shannon 2019

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Step 3 of the final-exam hierarchical regression (n = 310) added preparation-by-re-quizzing interactions; step improved fit (F(2, 304) = 6.251, p = 0.002, ΔR2 = 0.020) with a "negative interaction with re-quizzing and chemistry exam score" (interaction βstd = −0.138 in Table 2).

> "Additionally, we found a negative interaction with re-quizzing and chemistry exam score; that is, delayed re-quizzing was most helpful for students who previously struggled in a STEM course (General Chemistry I)."

### Walck-Shannon 2019 (2)

Walck-Shannon, E. M., Cahill, M. J., McDaniel, M. A., & Frey, R. F. (2019). Participation in Voluntary Re-quizzing Is Predictive of Increased Performance on Cumulative Assessments in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 18(2). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.18-08-0163

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model-based illustration from the same final-exam regression: a "student who scored in the 25th percentile" was predicted at 76.5% without and 82.0% with full re-quizzing, while a 75th-percentile student showed almost no difference (90.1% vs 90.5%).

> "on average, a student who scored in the 25th percentile for both the chemistry final exam (75/120) and biology pretest (14/38) would earn 76.5% on the biology final if they did not participate in re-quizzing but would earn 82.0% on the biology final if they re-quizzed at every opportunity."
