## Evidence

### Gibbs 2019

Gibbs, L., Nursey, J., Cook, J., Ireton, G., Alkemade, N., Roberts, M., Gallagher, H. C., Bryant, R., Block, K., Molyneaux, R., & Forbes, D. (2019). Delayed Disaster Impacts on Academic Performance of Primary School Children. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13200

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chi-square tests compared categorical demographics across affectedness regions in the matched sample. A parallel significant difference was found for fathers (40.2% high, 42.4% medium, 56.2% low), and living with both parents was significantly lower in the high impact region (p = .004).

> "the proportion of mothers who had a minimum level of education being Year 12 or equivalent was significantly lower (p < .001) in the high impact (51.8%) and medium impact (56.0%) regions compared with the low impact region (64.4%)."
