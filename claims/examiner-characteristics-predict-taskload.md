## Evidence

### Dziadzko Mikhail 2024

Dziadzko Mikhail, Varvinskiy Andrey, Di Loreto Rodolphe, Scipioni Hugues, Ateleanu Bazil, Klimek Markus, Berger-Estilita Joana. (2024). Examiner workload comparison: three structured oral examination formats for the European diploma in anaesthesiology and intensive care. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2364990

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Results-section analysis of workload against examiner demographics in the 2023 EDAIC cohort. The article reports male examiners scored "62 ± 13 vs 57 ± 15 (females)" with p = 0.0115; no effect of years of professional experience was found.

> "The mean NASA TLX score was significantly higher in male examiners 62 ± 13 vs 57 ± 15 (females), p = 0.0115;"

### Dziadzko Mikhail 2024 (2)

Dziadzko Mikhail, Varvinskiy Andrey, Di Loreto Rodolphe, Scipioni Hugues, Ateleanu Bazil, Klimek Markus, Berger-Estilita Joana. (2024). Examiner workload comparison: three structured oral examination formats for the European diploma in anaesthesiology and intensive care. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2364990

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Results-section analysis of self-reported preparation time (1 h to >3 h categories) against NASA-TLX score. The article reports a "4.7 points of NASA TLX increase" per hour of preparation, statistically significant (p < 0.0001).

> "A higher task load was associated with the time needed for the examination preparation: the increment of 1 hour spent for preparation is associated with 4.7 points of NASA TLX increase, statistically significant (p < 0.0001)."

### Dziadzko Mikhail 2024 (3)

Dziadzko Mikhail, Varvinskiy Andrey, Di Loreto Rodolphe, Scipioni Hugues, Ateleanu Bazil, Klimek Markus, Berger-Estilita Joana. (2024). Examiner workload comparison: three structured oral examination formats for the European diploma in anaesthesiology and intensive care. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2024.2364990

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Results-section comparison of examiners holding additional specialties or subspecialties versus those who do not. The article reports means of "59 ± 14 vs 66 ± 13" with p = 0.0101; examining for another European diploma showed no difference.

> "Holding more than one specialty or subspecialty, other than general anaesthesiology, is associated with lower taskload: 59 ± 14 vs 66 ± 13, p = 0.0101."
