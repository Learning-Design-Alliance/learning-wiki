## Evidence

### Connor J. Richardson 2021

Connor J. Richardson, Trevor I. Smith, and Paul J. Walter. (2021). Replicating analyses of item response curves using data from the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020127)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Dot product effect size (DES) comparison of the RSW American data set with the IDW Japanese data set (1,531 students), reported in Table II and Fig. 7. The authors report "The median DES value is 2.7 for the RSW/IDWJ comparison" and 29 of 47 items with DES above 2.

> "The median DES value is 2.7 for the RSW/IDWJ comparison (compared to 0.6 for RSW/IDWA), and the highest value is 13.1"

### Connor J. Richardson 2021 (2)

Connor J. Richardson, Trevor I. Smith, and Paul J. Walter. (2021). Replicating analyses of item response curves using data from the Force and Motion Conceptual Evaluation. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020127)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' conclusion about the nature of the American-Japanese response differences, based on their IRC dot product and DES analyses across the three data sets.

> "The majority of these diﬀerences are related to speciﬁc choices of incorrect answers rather than diﬀerences in numbers of correct answers."
