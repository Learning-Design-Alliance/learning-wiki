Stevenson 2017¶

Stevenson, C. E. (2017). Role of Working Memory and Strategy-Use in Feedback Effects on children's Progression in Analogy Solving: an Explanatory Item Response Theory Account. Int J Artif Intell Educ 27:393–418. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-016-0129-5

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Model M3 of the explanatory IRT analysis, comparing the two latent strategy groups with age and WM controlled. The article prints z = −4.95, p < .001 with .33 lower odds; no standardized effect size is reported, so impact is null.

"Non-analogical reasoners had greater learning gains, i.e. improvement from pretest to posttest, than analogical reasoners in the MTF condition (z = −4.95, p < .001, .33 lower odds)."

Related Claims¶

  • Latent class analysis of pretest strategy-use identifies two subgroups of children, analogical and non-analogical reasoners, of roughly equal size — related
  • Working memory moderates feedback effects: higher working memory children gain more from tutoring feedback and no feedback, but gains in the multiple-try condition are unrelated to working memory — related
  • Interactive tutoring feedback leads to greater progression in children's analogy solving than multiple-try feedback, which is more effective than no feedback — related
