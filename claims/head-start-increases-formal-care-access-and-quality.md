# Random assignment to Head Start increases children's access to formal care and the quality of formal care relative to control

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Compared to control children, Head Start-randomized children show much higher odds of being in formal care at higher quality levels rather than in parent care. [→ Friedman-Krauss 2013](#friedman-krauss-2013)

## Evidence

### Friedman-Krauss 2013

Friedman-Krauss, A. H., Connors, M. C., & Morris, P. A. (2013). Estimating impacts of treatment random assignment on classroom quality in the Head Start Impact Study: The problem of missing data. SREE Spring 2013 Conference. https://www.sree.org/conferences/2013s/

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Multinomial logit model with center-group fixed effects on nine mutually exclusive groups defined by quality score and type of missingness, with parent care as reference. Table 2 reports odds ratios from 2.31 to 37.99 (all p<.001; Wald chi-square 937.40). The article reports "increases in access to formal care and increases in quality of formal care".

> "the odds for children randomized to Head Start to be in formal care with a score on the ECERS between 5 and 6 instead of parent care is 28 times as high as the same odds for children randomized to the control group."
