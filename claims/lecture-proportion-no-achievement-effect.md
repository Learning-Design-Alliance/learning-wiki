# Reducing the face-to-face lecture proportion from 1/2 to 1/3 of class time did not affect student achievement in flipped physics classes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two flipped classes with lecture proportions of 1/3 (Class 1, N = 160) and 1/2 (Class 2, N = 176) showed no statistically significant differences in midterm or final exam score distributions. [→ Heeseung Zoe 2014](#heeseung-zoe-2014)

## Evidence

### Heeseung Zoe 2014

Heeseung Zoe, Kyujin Kwak, Yoonjoo Cho, Yun-Young Seo, Jin-suk Choi, Jin-Hyouk Im, Soogyun Beum and Hai-Woong Lee. (2014). The role of face-to-face lecturing in flipped classrooms. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1404.3566)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Semester 2013 comparison of two redesigned classes taught by one instructor with lecture proportions 1/3 and 1/2. F-tests (p = 0.514 midterm, 0.8758 final) supported equal variances; T-tests found no significant differences between the classes.

> "According to T-test under the equal variance assumption, the p-values are 0.6747 for the midterm exam and 0.09728 for the ﬁnal exam. Hence, it is hard to say that the overall statistical distributions for the midterm and ﬁnal exams are diﬀerent."
