# Digital test administration caused data-collection difficulties in the pilot, while paper-based tests were administered without problems

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All schools that encountered data-collection difficulties had been assigned digital tests, and their inability to complete testing on schedule was directly related to that mode. [→ Miller 2015](#miller-2015)
`q2 i?` Schools using pencil and paper tests reported no difficulties administering tests. [→ Miller 2015 (2)](#miller-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Miller 2015

Miller, S., Gildea, A., Sloan, S., & Thurston, A. (2015). Physically Active Lessons: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581164

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Trial data-collection account from the impact evaluation. Digital (InCAS) testing was time consuming, limited by computer availability, and hit technical issues such as log-on failures and laptop battery problems, delaying two schools' data collection to September/October 2014.

> "All three of the schools that encountered difficulties in completing the data collection had been assigned to complete the digital tests and this was directly related to their inability to complete the tests in June or July."

### Miller 2015 (2)

Miller, S., Gildea, A., Sloan, S., & Thurston, A. (2015). Physically Active Lessons: Evaluation report and Executive summary. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED581164

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Reported contrast within the same data-collection account: the three schools completing paper-based Progress in English and Progress in Maths tests under exam conditions "reported no difficulties administering tests".

> "The schools that used pencil and paper tests reported no difficulties administering tests."
