# High-frequency intermittent expert feedback yields smoother global procedural performance than low-frequency feedback after repetitive mannequin training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After five practice repetitions, the high-frequency feedback group achieved better global procedural performance (IPPI global rating) than the low-frequency feedback group (p = .004), with no difference at baseline. [→ Bosse Hans Martin 2015](#bosse-hans-martin-2015)

## Evidence

### Bosse Hans Martin 2015

Bosse Hans Martin, Mohr Jonathan, Buss Beate, Krautter Markus, Weyrich Peter, Herzog Wolfgang, Jünger Jana, Nikendei Christoph. (2015). The benefit of repetitive skills training and frequency of expert feedback in the early acquisition of procedural skills. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0286-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized prospective study of 47 medical students trained to insert a nasogastric tube in a mannequin, with blinded expert video ratings on the IPPI global rating. The article reports "the HFF group achieving better scores than the LFF group (p = .004)" at T2; no effect size is printed.

> "At T2, both groups scored higher in their global procedural performance compared to T1 (HFF group p < .001; LFF group p = .002). In addition, after the final, sixth repetition (T2), the HFF group achieving better scores than the LFF group (p = .004; see Table 4)."
