# EOC does not significantly moderate the relationship between teachers' contingent reward behavior and residents' self-efficacy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The reward-by-EOC interaction on self-efficacy is not significant (β = 0.00, p > 0.1), so the rewarded behavior of teachers does not change residents' self-efficacy regardless of EOC level. [→ Deng Guangwei 2019](#deng-guangwei-2019)

## Evidence

### Deng Guangwei 2019

Deng Guangwei, Zhao Di, Lio Jonathan, Chen Xinyu, Ma Xiaopeng, Liang Liang, Feng Chenpeng. (2019). Strategic elements of residency training in China: transactional leadership, self-efficacy, and employee-orientation culture. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1792-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderated regression on the same 247-resident sample testing the reward-by-EOC interaction on self-efficacy. The article reports a non-significant interaction (β = 0.00, p > 0.1); equivalence was not tested, and no effect size is printed.

> "The moderating effect of the EOC on the rewarded behavior of clinical teachers with TLS and self-efficacy of residents is not significant (β = 0.00, p>0.1). This indicates that, regardless of the high or low EOC level, the rewarded behavior of teachers with TLS does not change the self-efficacy of residents."
