## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The review reports that Freedman's randomized study, Kruglak, Bradley, Dubravcic, and Saunders and Dickinson found lab enrollment did not improve content-knowledge test outcomes relative to controls. [→ DeFeo 2020](#defeo-2020)

## Evidence

### DeFeo 2020

DeFeo, D. J., Bibler, A., & Gerken, S. (2020). The Effect of a Paired Lab on Course Completion and Grades in Nonmajors Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education, 19(3). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.20-03-0041

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Narrative review section attributing this finding to Saunders and Dickinson (1979); the review also cites Freedman (1997) finding no grade effect though improved content-test performance and attitudes in a 270-student randomized study.

> "And when Saunders and Dickinson (1979) randomly assigned first-year biology students to lecture-only, lecture–recitation, and lecture–lab groups, they found no significant difference on content knowledge tests between the recitation and lab groups; however, both scored better than the lecture-only group."
