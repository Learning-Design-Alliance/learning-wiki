# Pairs managed mutual awareness through spatial and temporal affordances and developed attention-getting protocols finer-grained than turn-taking

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants used three conventions to manage awareness: moving materials to the partner's side, contributing temporally proximal to the partner's contribution, and getting attention before contributing. [→ Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006](#nathan-dwyer-daniel-d-suthers-2006)
`q2 i?` Participants changed attentional focus at finer granularity than individual contributions, interrupting even word production, which existing turn-taking models do not account for. [→ Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006 (2)](#nathan-dwyer-daniel-d-suthers-2006-2)

## Evidence

### Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers. (2006). Consistent practices in artifact-mediated collaboration. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 1: 481–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-006-9001-1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the video analysis, awareness management was bidirectional: "Participants took responsibility both for maintaining their awareness of their partner ’s contributions and making their partner aware of their own contributions," via movable materials, temporal proximity and attention-getting.

> "Participants took responsibility both for maintaining their awareness of their partner ’s contributions and making their partner aware of their own contributions."

### Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers 2006 (2)

Nathan Dwyer & Daniel D. Suthers. (2006). Consistent practices in artifact-mediated collaboration. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 1: 481–511. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-006-9001-1

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Attention protocols (tapping, repositioning materials, self-interruption) let participants shift focus mid-contribution; the authors state "existing turn-taking models of interaction do not adequately account for this data."

> "The commonality amongst all these interactions is that the participants are able to change their attentional focus at a finer granularity than individual contributions, interrupting even the production of words."
