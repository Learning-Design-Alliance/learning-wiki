# Cognitive and metacognitive prompts did not increase self-reported online strategy use, the quality of strategies in learners' notes, or learning outcomes in hypermedia learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Prompted and non-prompted learners did not differ in self-reported cognitive or metacognitive online strategy use, in the quality of cognitive or metacognitive strategies in their notes, in time on relevant pages, or in recall, comprehension, and transfer outcomes. [→ Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert 2020](#nadja-gentner-tina-seufert-2020)
`q3 i?` A MANCOVA with prior knowledge as covariate showed no systematic differences between groups on the three learning outcome measures. [→ Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert 2020 (2)](#nadja-gentner-tina-seufert-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert 2020

Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert. (2020). The Double-Edged Interactions of Prompts and Self-efficacy. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09227-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-subjects experiment with N = 70 first-semester psychology students learning with hypermedia; independent t-tests compared the prompt and no-prompt groups on self-reported online strategies, note quality, and navigation. The study reports "no statistically significant differences between both groups ’ self-reported cognitive online learning strategies"; equivalence was not tested.

> "In contrast to our expectations, analyses indicated that no statistically significant differences between both groups ’ self-reported cognitive online learning strategies existed, t(63.5) = -1.34, p = .09 (one-tailed)."

### Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert 2020 (2)

Nadja Gentner & Tina Seufert. (2020). The Double-Edged Interactions of Prompts and Self-efficacy. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09227-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANCOVA on the post-test (recall, comprehension, transfer) with prior knowledge as covariate found "no systematic differences regarding learning outcomes" between the prompt and no-prompt groups; no effect size was printed for this null result.

> "Following Pillai’st r a c e the analysis indicated no systematic differences regarding learning outcomes (recall, comprehension, transfer) between the groups V = 0.003, F(3,65) = 0.063, ns."
