## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The feedback argument against cheating applies to homework more than exams and depends on actual teacher practice, since not all instructors use graded assignments to provide or act on feedback. [→ Mathieu Bouville 2009](#mathieu-bouville-2009)

## Evidence

### Mathieu Bouville 2009

Mathieu Bouville. (2009). Why is cheating wrong?. [doi:10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11217-009-9148-0)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument (section 3.1) assessing the argument, attributed to Passow et al., that academic dishonesty undermines measures of student learning. The article argues the argument's applicability "depends deeply on the actual practice of the teachers" and that some teachers never alter courses based on student feedback. Philosophical analysis, no data.

> "it is not a universal truth that teachers use graded assignments for feedback to the students."
