Exploratory analysis of practitioner effectiveness ratings (Q9): "practitioners were curiously more likely to rate the evidence as “positive”" under condition c than bc; the authors attribute this to condition bc containing one negative-effect study, suggesting a vote-counting variation attending to any negative results.

"practitioners were curiously more likely to rate the evidence as “positive” (the highest rating on the 5-point scale) when the summary effect was large ( d¼ 0:2) and non-significant (experimental condition c) than they were when the summary effect was large and significant (experimental condition bc)."
