# Adolescents select friends and helpers similar to themselves in behavioral academic engagement; emotional-engagement similarity plays a weaker role

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Similarity selection effects on behavioral academic engagement were positive for both friend and help networks. [→ Shin 2023](#shin-2023)

## Evidence

### Shin 2023

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel SAOM selection results for the same 20-classroom multiplex network sample. Similar behavioral academic engagement predicted both friend and helper nominations, with "friends ( η = 0.33, SD = 0.13, p > 0.99)" and "helpers ( η = 0.36, SD = 0.17, p = 0.98)".

> "We found evidence for the similarity selection effect being positive for the behavioral dimension of academic engagement for both friends ( η = 0.33, SD = 0.13, p > 0.99) and helpers ( η = 0.36, SD = 0.17, p = 0.98)."

### Shin 2023 (2)

Huiyoung Shin. (2023). The co-evolution of friend and help relationships and their different relationship formation and social influence. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-43346-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same SAOM analysis found only weak evidence for emotional-engagement similarity in friend selection (η = 0.20, p = 0.93, below the stated ≥ 0.975 threshold); the corresponding helper effect (η = 0.05, p = 0.39) was not significant.

> "For the emotional dimension of academic engagement, we found weak evidence for the similarity selection effect for friends (η = 0.20, SD = 0.13, p = 0.93)."
