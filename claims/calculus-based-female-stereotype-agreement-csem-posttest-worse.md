# At the end of the year-long calculus-based sequence, female students who agreed with the gender stereotype performed significantly worse on the CSEM post-test (8% difference)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Calculus-based female students who agreed with the stereotype scored significantly worse (8% difference) than those who disagreed on the CSEM post-test, despite no significant pretest difference. [→ Alexandru Maries 2018](#alexandru-maries-2018)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2018

Alexandru Maries, Nafis I. Karim, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Is agreeing with a gender stereotype correlated with the performance of female students in introductory physics? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 020119. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.020119

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of CSEM performance of calculus-based female students grouped by stereotype agreement; a statistically significant 8% difference emerged on the post-test even though "there was no statistically significant difference between women who agreed or disagreed with the stereotype at the beginning of the course".

> "However, on the CSEM post-test, there was a statistically significant difference (a difference of 8%) between the calculus-based female students who agreed and the female students in the same course who disagreed with the stereotype."
