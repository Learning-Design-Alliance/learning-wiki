## Evidence

### Hartman 2016

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Documentary analysis of K-12 math standards. The 1989 NCTM standards recommended “decreased attention” to “memorizing rules and algorithms” and “paper and pencil fraction computation”; over 40 states adopted NCTM-type standards between 1990 and 2002.

> "Recommended for “decreased attention” were “memorizing rules and algorithms,” “finding exact forms of answers,” “manipulating symbols,” “relying on outside authority (teacher or answer key),” “rote practice,” and “paper and pencil fraction computation” (NCTM 1989)."

### Hartman 2016 (2)

Hartman, JudithAnn R., and Eric A. Nelson. (2016). Automaticity in Computation and Student Success in Introductory Physical Science Courses. Originally published 2015. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1608.05006)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The authors’ synthesis of the Virginia, Iowa, and NAEP LTT data: computation skills “did indeed fall precipitously” but did so “only after textbooks and/or state standards required teachers to de-emphasize math automaticity.”

> "there is evidence that student computation skills needed for scientific calculations did indeed fall precipitously, but they did so only after textbooks and/or state standards required teachers to de-emphasize math automaticity."
