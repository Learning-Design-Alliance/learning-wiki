## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Alpha scores correlated 0.438 with first-semester grades for the class of 1923 and 0.498 for the class of 1924. [→ Stone 1920](#stone-1920)
`q2 i2` The correlation with second-semester grades was lower, 0.333, for the class of 1923. [→ Stone 1920](#stone-1920)

## Evidence

### Stone 1920

Stone, Charles Leonard. (1920). The significance of alpha in colleges. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+significance+of+alpha+in+colleges%22+Stone

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of Alpha scores against freshman grades at Dartmouth for two entering classes. The printed correlation coefficients are r = 0.438 ± 0.022 (first semester, class of 1923), r = 0.333 ± 0.026 (second semester), and r = 0.498 ± 0.021 (first semester, class of 1924).

> "In the class of 1923 the correlation with first semester grades was 0.438 + 0.022; with second semester grades 0.333 ± 0.026; in the class of 1924 the first semester correlation with total scholarship was 0.498 ± 0.021."

### Stone 1920 (2)

Stone, Charles Leonard. (1920). The significance of alpha in colleges. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+significance+of+alpha+in+colleges%22+Stone

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Author interpretation regarding the class of 1924, for whom administration influence, a college-paper article, and campus tradition were used to encourage serious test-taking. The author writes it seems safe to assume "this effort was in significant degree responsible for the higher scores."

> "It seems safe to assume that this effort was in significant degree responsible for the higher scores, and very possibly for higher correlations, as compared with the class of 1923."
