## Evidence

### Doyle 2023

Doyle, L., Easterbrook, M. J., & Harris, P. R. (2023). Roles of socioeconomic status, ethnicity and teacher beliefs in academic grading. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 91–112. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12541

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

In the 2×2 experiment, descriptive ratings of White British and Black Caribbean students were similar across outcomes; the quote reports the non-significant ethnicity main effect and SES × ethnicity interaction on grade. Equivalence was not tested.

> "However, there was no significant main effect of ethnicity on overall grade (p = .755). The two‐way interaction between SES and ethnicity was also non‐significant (p = .216)."

### Doyle 2023 (2)

Doyle, L., Easterbrook, M. J., & Harris, P. R. (2023). Roles of socioeconomic status, ethnicity and teacher beliefs in academic grading. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93, 91–112. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12541

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The objective-errors outcome (0–20 estimated errors on the same writing sample) showed no significant effects in the same experiment, suggesting biased judgements occurred despite objective clues indicating equal performance.

> "There were no significant main effects of SES or ethnicity on teacher judgements of objective errors (ps > .4; Figure 4), nor was there a two‐way interaction (p = .260)."
