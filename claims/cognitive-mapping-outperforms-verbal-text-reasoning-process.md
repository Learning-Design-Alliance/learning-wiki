# Medical students using a computer-based cognitive-mapping approach outperform those using a verbal-text approach in reasoning-process performance when learning with clinical cases

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Learners in the cognitive-mapping group outperformed those in the verbal-text group in the reasoning process for the last case (mean difference = 0.24, d = 0.82 overall). [→ Wu Bian 2016](#wu-bian-2016)

## Evidence

### Wu Bian 2016

Wu Bian, Wang Minhong, Grotzer Tina A., Liu Jun, Johnson Janice M. (2016). Visualizing complex processes using a cognitive-mapping tool to support the learning of clinical reasoning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0734-x

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experiment with 52 students from two medical schools, schools randomly assigned to conditions rather than individual students. Independent t-tests on last-case learning products: reasoning process mean difference 0.24 (t = 3.43, df = 49, p = .005); overall "mean difference = 0.16, t = 2.89, df = 49, p < 0.05" with Cohen's d = 0.82.

> "learners in the cognitive-mapping group were found to outperform those in the verbal-text group in the reasoning process, but not in the construction of knowledge underlying the reasoning process."
