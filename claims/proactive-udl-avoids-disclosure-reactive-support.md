## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Proactively making the learning environment accessible from the beginning supports students with disabilities without requiring disclosure, unlike reactive supports for individual needs. [→ Amanda Lannan 2019](#amanda-lannan-2019)

## Evidence

### Amanda Lannan 2019

Amanda Lannan, Erin Scanlon, & Jacquelyn J. Chini. (2019). Resources for Supporting Students with and without Disabilities in Your Physics Courses. The Physics Teacher. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1907.07301](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1907.07301)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument advanced by the authors to motivate UDL use in physics courses; the article offers this as a reasoned position rather than a tested result, noting disclosure "may make students vulnerable to stigmatization" (see CL-003).

> "One way to provide support for students with disabilities without requiring disclosure is to proactively make the learning environment accessible to and supportive of all learners from the beginning instead of reactively providing supports for individual students’ needs."
