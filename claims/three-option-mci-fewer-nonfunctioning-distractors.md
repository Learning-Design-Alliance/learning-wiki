## Evidence

### Chen Dandan 2023

Chen Dandan, Harman Ann E., Sun Huaping, Ye Tianpeng, Gaiser Robert R. (2023). A comparison of 3- and 4-option multiple-choice items for medical subspecialty in-training examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04277-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Distractor analysis of the 450 distractors in each 4-option form and 300 in each 3-option form. For the ITE-PA the comparable drops were from 62.7% to 46.0% (traditional) and 44.9% to 27.7% (sliding scale); no effect sizes were printed.

> "For the ITE-CCM, the percentage of NFDs dropped from 51.3% to 37.0% using the traditional "frequency and/or discrimination" method, and from 36.0% to 21.7% using the sliding scale "frequency and/or discrimination" method."

### Chen Dandan 2023 (2)

Chen Dandan, Harman Ann E., Sun Huaping, Ye Tianpeng, Gaiser Robert R. (2023). A comparison of 3- and 4-option multiple-choice items for medical subspecialty in-training examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-023-04277-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Counts of items with zero non-functioning distractors under the traditional method, from the distractor analysis of the matched 150-item forms. Under the sliding scale method, items without NFDs rose from 18.0% to 58.7% (ITE-CCM) and 12.7% to 52.0% (ITE-PA).

> "Using the traditional method, the number of items without NFDs increased from 12 to 58 (8.0% to 38.7%) for the ITE-CCM and 6 to 45 (4.0% to 30.0%) for the ITE-PA after converting the MCIs from 4- to 3-option format (Table 3)."
