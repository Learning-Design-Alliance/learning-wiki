Evidence¶

Janet Mannheimer Zydney 2025¶

Janet Mannheimer Zydney, Erin Rumpke, Gi Woong Choi, Lauren Angelone. (2025). Understanding interactions between scientists and elementary school students in a citizen science project. Instructional Science (2025) 53:949–971. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09710-1

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Betweenness centrality analysis (Table 3) identified S14 as the key bridge between expert scientists and students; the authors note this was surprising since Rangers typically take this role.

"S14 played a key role in bridging connections between the expert scientists and the students (Betweenness = 10)."

Janet Mannheimer Zydney 2025 (2)¶

Janet Mannheimer Zydney, Erin Rumpke, Gi Woong Choi, Lauren Angelone. (2025). Understanding interactions between scientists and elementary school students in a citizen science project. Instructional Science (2025) 53:949–971. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09710-1

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Qualitative contextualization of the SNA findings shows bridging students asked simple questions (e.g., about location or identification) and made declarative assertions that invited discussion.

"These students seemed to do this through asking questions and making assertions which generated discussion."

Related Claims¶

  • Participation in non-scientific discussion was weakly negatively related to participation in scientific discussion (r = 0.14, p < 0.05)
  • Non-scientific discussion was much denser than scientific discussion, with all students participating non-scientifically but only 12 scientifically
  • Expert scientists fulfilled a crucial role in generating scientific discussion, with one expert holding the highest degree centrality in the scientific network — related
  • Higher overall participation was not associated with scientific engagement, but co-contribution with expert scientists was — related
