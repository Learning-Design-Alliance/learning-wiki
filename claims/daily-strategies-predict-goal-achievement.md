## Evidence

### Theobald 2025

Theobald, M. (2025). Study longer or study effectively? Better study strategies can compensate for less study time and predict goal achievement and lower negative affect. British Journal of Educational Psychology, 95, 405–420. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12725

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-person hierarchical linear models (Model 2, Table 2) on k = 3411 daily observations from 231 students show all four strategy variables predicted goal achievement, and study time remained significant after strategies were entered, indicating both explain variance in daily goal achievement.

> "Second, better planning (ß = .04), better monitoring (ß = .10), better concentration (ß = .06) and less procrastination (ß = −.31) were associated with higher goal achievement (see Table 2, Model 2). Perceived study time remained a significant predictor of goal achievement after study strategies were included in the model."
