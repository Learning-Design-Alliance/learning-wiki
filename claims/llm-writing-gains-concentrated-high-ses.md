## Evidence

### Renzhe Yu 2024

Renzhe Yu, Zhen Xu, Sky CH-Wang, Richard Arum. (2024). Whose ChatGPT? Unveiling Real-World Educational Inequalities Introduced by Large Language Models. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Three-way interaction regressions (linguistic group x SES x phase) on the same submission-level sample, with results in Table S3 and Figure 4. The article reports the SES gap as descriptive ranges across comparisons, with some estimates more statistically significant than others.

> "Students from low-income families on average experienced -0.005 to 0.051 standard deviation (0.025 on average) less writing improvement than their higher-income peers across various comparisons"

### Renzhe Yu 2024 (2)

Renzhe Yu, Zhen Xu, Sky CH-Wang, Richard Arum. (2024). Whose ChatGPT? Unveiling Real-World Educational Inequalities Introduced by Large Language Models. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.22282)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same three-way interaction analysis by parental education. The article states the gaps persisted across both post-LLM phases, though some estimates were more statistically significant than others.

> "this average gap was -0.029 to 0.069 standard deviation (0.015 on average) for students whose parents attended college compared to first-generation college students. These gaps existed in Phase 1 and persisted in Phase 2."
