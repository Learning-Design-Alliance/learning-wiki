# First graders assigned to Dynamic RTI scored significantly higher on spring reading outcomes than students assigned to Typical RTI, with a moderate effect size of .36

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Immediate assignment to intervention (Dynamic RTI) produced statistically significantly higher end-of-first-grade reading scores than waiting in Tier 1 for 8 weeks (Typical RTI), with an effect size of .36. [→ Al Otaiba 2015](#al-otaiba-2015)

## Evidence

### Al Otaiba 2015

Al Otaiba, S., Connor, C. M., Folsom, J. S., Wanzek, J., Greulich, L., Schatschneider, C., & Wagner, R. K. (2015). To Wait in Tier 1 or Intervene Immediately: A Randomized Experiment Examining First Grade Response to Intervention (RTI) in Reading. SREE Spring 2015 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562485

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Intent-to-treat analysis using hierarchical linear modeling in a randomized experiment with approximately 500 first graders in seven schools, with children nested in classrooms. The unconditional model showed an ICC of .250, and the Dynamic RTI group outperformed Typical RTI with "an effect size of .36".

> "Results of the analyses (see Table 1) revealed that students in the Dynamic RTI group had statistically significantly higher Spring Reading scores than did students in Typical RTI, with an effect size of .36"
