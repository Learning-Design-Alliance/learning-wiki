Evidence¶

Wolbers 2018¶

Wolbers, K., Dostal, H., Graham, S., Branum-Martin, L., Kilpatrick, J., & Saulsburry, R. (2018). Strategic and Interactive Writing Instruction: An Efficacy Study in Grades 3-5. Journal of Educational and Developmental Psychology, 8(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jedp.v8n1p99

q2 · i2 · causal · r2

Multilevel regression of five personal narrative language measures (WER I-III, words per t-unit, % complete sentences) scored with the SAWL. The article prints effect sizes in the range 0.46 to 1.20, including 1.20 for WER II (t = 2.90, p = .01), 1.09 for WER III (p = .03), 1.13 for words per t-unit (t = 2.84, p = .02) and 1.09 for % complete sentences (p = .02); WER I's 0.46 was not statistically significant (t = 1.18, p = .26). The printed range supports impact 2.

"For the five personal narrative written language outcomes, the treatment effects were moderate to large (0.46 to 1.20)."

Wolbers 2018 (2)¶

Wolbers, K., Dostal, H., Graham, S., Branum-Martin, L., Kilpatrick, J., & Saulsburry, R. (2018). Strategic and Interactive Writing Instruction: An Efficacy Study in Grades 3-5. Journal of Educational and Developmental Psychology, 8(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jedp.v8n1p99

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Multilevel regression of five persuasive language measures. The authors suspect "non-significance of persuasive writing language variables is an indication of small sample size" since effect sizes were comparable to personal narrative outcomes.

"For the five persuasive written language outcomes, treatment effects were also substantial (0.38 to 1.06), although not all were statistically significant at the 0.05 level."
