# Carelessness detected by the BKFC model is negatively and significantly associated with post-test scores, controlling for final knowledge level

> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among middle school students using a decimal learning game, higher BKFC-detected carelessness predicted lower post-test scores after controlling for final knowledge level. [→ Jiayi Zhang 2025](#jiayi-zhang-2025)

## Evidence

### Jiayi Zhang 2025

Jiayi Zhang, Ryan S. Baker, Namrata Srivastava, Jaclyn Ocumpaugh, Caitlin Mills, Bruce M. McLaren. (2025). Carelessness Detection using Performance Factor Analysis: A New Operationalization with Unexpectedly Different Relationship to Learning. arXiv. [doi:10.1109/cste64638.2025.11092064](https://doi.org/10.1109/cste64638.2025.11092064)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression analysis predicting post-test scores from average carelessness and final knowledge level for 181 students; the BKFC model's carelessness coefficient was negative and significant (b = −74.88, t(157) = −2.31, p = .02), with the model explaining "47% of the variance in post-test scores."

> "carelessness, as detected using the BKT-based contextual slip model or the ML contextual slip model, was positively and significantly associated with post-test scores, whereas carelessness detected using the BKFC model was negatively and significantly associated with post-test scores."
