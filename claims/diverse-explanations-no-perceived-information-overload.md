# Students reported similar perceived information quantity across conditions, indicating diverse explanations did not produce perceived information overload

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Perceived amount of information did not differ significantly across explanation groups, with mean ratings around 3.3–3.4, suggesting diverse explanations did not increase perceived cognitive load relative to generic explanations. [→ Seth Bernstein 2026](#seth-bernstein-2026)

## Evidence

### Seth Bernstein 2026

SIGCSE Virtual '26. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28882 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.28882](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.28882)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Likert-scale information-quantity ratings (1 = Not Enough Information to 5 = Too Much Information) aggregated across snippets and analyzed with Kruskal-Wallis H tests; the article reports mean ratings around 3.3–3.4 and "no significant difference in perceived information quantity across groups."

> "A Kruskal-Wallis H test also showed no significant difference in perceived information quantity across groups,𝐻( 7)= 3.57, 𝑝=. 828, showing that students generally rated the information load similarly regardless of condition."
