## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The belief that conspicuous talent succeeds by its own strength is unsound: success requires great physical energy and strength of will, qualities often present only slightly in exceptionally talented individuals, so the talent may be wasted. [→ William Stern 1910](#william-stern-1910)

## Evidence

### William Stern 1910

William Stern. (1910). The Supernormal Child. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://archive.org/search?query=Journal+of+Educational+Psychology

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the individual phase of the problem. Stern rejects the commonplace that talent succeeds unaided, noting we know only talents that attained success and there is no record of potential greatness that failed of fruition. The article presents this as argument, not tested evidence.

> "Success requires, above all, great physical energy and strength of will, and these qualities may often be found in but a slight degree with exceptional talent, so that there is always danger that the talent may be wasted."
