## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After training, residents taught with multidirectional stitching technology completed sutures significantly faster than residents taught the traditional method (P = 0.001). [→ Yu Zhao 2020](#yu-zhao-2020)

## Evidence

### Yu Zhao 2020

Yu Zhao, Qiong Chen, Jia-Ning Hu, Qi Shen, Lu Xia, Lin-Zhi Yan, Yi Wang, Xiu-Jie Zhu, Wen-Ju Li, Yue Hu, Qiong Zhang. (2020). Application of multidirectional stitching technology in a laparoscopic suturing instructional program: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02146-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of forty junior residents (twenty per group) trained on laparoscopic simulators. Post-training, the experimental group averaged "7.9 ± 1.04 min" versus the control group's 11.6 ± 1.19 min; only the P value is printed, no effect size.

> "There were significant differences between the experimental group (7.9 ± 1.04 min) and the control group (11.6 ± 1.19 min) in terms of suture time (P = 0.001)."
