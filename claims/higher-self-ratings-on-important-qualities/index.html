Evidence¶

Knight 1921¶

Knight, F. B., & Franzen, R. H. (1921). Pitfalls in rating schemes. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073817

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Fifty college students first rated the qualities by importance from A to F, then rated themselves 0 to 20 on each quality. The article reports the graded averages and states the tendency falls in line with the rank-order correlations between importance and amount possessed.

"It is significant to find that these 50 reporters gave themselves an average of 14.75 points on A qualities 13.5 points on B qualities 11.1 points on E qualities 7.0 points on F qualities"

Related Claims¶

  • Raters correlate the importance of a trait with how much of it they credit themselves, an average correlation of +.52 read as a self-defense mechanism — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Self-overrating persists with a simplified grading method and on a behavioral self-analysis test, where raters claim far more 'Always' and far fewer 'Sometimes' for themselves than for others
  • Self-raters show very little association between their introspective self-order of interests and the order attributed to them by peers, indicating a tendency to overrate themselves
  • Juniors judge their fellow students as falling short of the ideal order of interests, with a negative correlation between the objective and ideal orders
