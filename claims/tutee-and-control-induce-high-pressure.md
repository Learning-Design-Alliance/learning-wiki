# The Tutee and Control roles induce significantly higher perceived pressure than the Peer and Challenger roles

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Perceived pressure differed significantly across roles (η²p = 0.20), with Tutee and Control higher than Peer and Challenger. [→ Zhengtao Xu 2026](#zhengtao-xu-2026)

## Evidence

### Zhengtao Xu 2026

Zhengtao Xu, Junti Zhang, Anthony Tang, and Yi-Chieh Lee. (2026). Who You Explain To Matters: Learning by Explaining to Conversational Agents with Different Pedagogical Roles. Proceedings of the 2026 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '26). https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790298

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on the IMI Pressure/Tension subscale (α = .919) in the N=96 study. Post-hoc tests confirmed the Tutee group induced significantly more pressure than both Peer and Challenger groups, and Control also exceeded both.

> "An ANOVA revealed a significant main effect for perceived pressure (𝐹( 3, 92)= 7.82,𝑝<. 001), with a large effect size (𝜂2𝑝 = 0.20). This indicates that the agent's role was a strong determinant of the learners' felt pressure"
