Evidence¶

Hanselman 2017¶

Hanselman, P., Rozek, C. S., Grigg, J., & Borman, G. D. (2017). New evidence on self-affirmation effects and theorized sources of heterogeneity from large-scale replications. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(3), 405-424. http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000141

q3 · i? · causal · r3

This is the cohort 2 randomized trial of self-affirmation among 7th grade students in one Midwestern school district, analyzed with multilevel models controlling for pre-treatment characteristics. The article reports that "Estimates for all outcomes were negative, but none were statistically different from zero" for the targeted group.

"Estimates for all outcomes were negative, but none were statistically different from zero (Table 2). The GPA effect in grade 7 was approximately zero (d=-0.002), while the effect in grade 8 was nominally negative (d=-0.072)."

Related Claims¶

  • Cohort 1 self-affirmation students received significantly higher grades in 8th grade (d=0.152), supporting persistent benefits from the prior study. — related
  • A brief values-affirmation writing exercise raised African American seventh-graders' grades in two small field experiments, but a large same-district replication found no effect — related
  • The article found no evidence of greater benefits for potentially threatened students or differential effectiveness by prior academic performance in cohort 2. — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Fidelity to self-affirmation treatment was high in both cohorts, with 98.0% of treatment students providing at least one self-affirming response. — related
  • The null effects in cohort 2 were significantly different from the persistent benefits observed in cohort 1 in the same setting. — related
  • In a four-wave MOOC intervention study, statistically significant first-wave findings failed to replicate in subsequent preregistered waves — related
