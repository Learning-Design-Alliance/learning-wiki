# SPARK's reading achievement benefit was larger for lower-achieving students (4.4 RIT points, g = .36), with a significant SPARK-by-baseline interaction

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Curtis J Jones 2016

Curtis J Jones, Michael Christian, Andrew Rice. (2016). The Results of a Randomized Control Trial Evaluation of the SPARK Literacy Program. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567484

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction terms exploring differential impact were added to the grade-level MAP models; when significant, models split the sample in half by baseline literacy and achievement scores. The printed statistic is t = 2.17, p < .05; no effect size is printed for the interaction itself.

> "The pooled interaction term of SPARK participation and baseline PALS scores/MAP scores was also significant ( t = 2.17, p < .05) suggesting that SPARK differentially impacted MAP scores for students with different starting literacy and achievement levels."

### Curtis J Jones 2016 (2)

Curtis J Jones, Michael Christian, Andrew Rice. (2016). The Results of a Randomized Control Trial Evaluation of the SPARK Literacy Program. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567484

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Split-sample models for students in the lower half of baseline achievement estimated the SPARK effect on MAP Reading, reported in Table 7 as 4.4 RIT points with "an effect size (Hedge's g) of .36".

> "SPARK had a much larger impact on the reading achievement of lower-achieving students. The unstandardized effect of SPARK on these students was 4.4 RIT score points on the MAP (Table 7). This corresponds to an effect size (Hedge's g) of .36."
