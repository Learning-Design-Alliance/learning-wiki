# Experimental induction of expertise produced only a small-to-medium, non-significant difference on the learning test (d = 0.31)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` The manipulation check showed experts scored numerically but not significantly higher than novices on the learning test (d = 0.31). [→ Rey 2011](#rey-2011)

## Evidence

### Rey 2011

Günter Daniel Rey, Florian Buchwald. (2011). The Expertise Reversal Effect: Cognitive Load and Motivational Explanations. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022243

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Manipulation check (t test on the 10-item learning test, α = .68) reported in the Induced Expertise section of Results. The article adds "The effect size (Cohen's d) was 0.31" and states the induction was only partially successful; both groups lacked prior experience (84.6% each).

> "Although not statistically significant, t(102) ⫽ 1.61, p ⫽ .06 (one-sided), the experts’ score on the learning test (M ⫽ 6.19, SD ⫽ 2.34) measured immediately after inducing expertise was numerically higher than the novices’ (M ⫽ 5.44, SD ⫽ 2.43)."
