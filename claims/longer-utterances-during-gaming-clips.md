# Students produce significantly longer think-aloud utterances during gaming-the-system periods than during non-gaming periods

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Think-aloud utterances were significantly longer in gaming clips than non-gaming clips, for both total words and non-stop words. [→ Jiayi Zhang 2026](#jiayi-zhang-2026)

## Evidence

### Jiayi Zhang 2026

Jiayi Zhang, Conrad Borchers, Canwen Wang, Vishal Kumar, Leah Teffera, Bruce M. McLaren, and Ryan S. Baker. (2026). Understanding Gaming the System by Analyzing Self-Regulated Learning in Think-Aloud Protocols. LAK 2026. [doi:10.1145/3785022.3785025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785025)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Generalized linear mixed model (Poisson, random student intercept) on 117 clips from ten students using StoichTutor: gaming clips averaged 105 words vs 74 in non-gaming clips (IRR = 1.18, p<.001); non-stop words IRR = 1.21, p<.001. "students used significantly more words in their think-aloud utterances during periods when gaming was observed".

> "students used significantly more words in their think-aloud utterances during periods when gaming was observed, as indicated by incidence rate ratios (IRRs) greater than 1 when comparing utterance length using all words (IRR = 1.18, 𝑝<. 001)"
