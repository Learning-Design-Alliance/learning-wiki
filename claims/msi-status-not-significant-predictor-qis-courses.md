## Evidence

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, and Bethany Wilcox. (2024). Disparities in access to US quantum information education. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010131)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Negative binomial regression across 456 institutions found URM-serving status not statistically significant for QIS course counts "once other factors are controlled for", unlike the disparity suggested by the earlier 2020 study the authors build on.

> "We find no statistically significant correlation between status as a URM-serving institution and number of QIS courses offered, once other factors are controlled for."

### Josephine C. Meyer 2024 (2)

Josephine C. Meyer, Gina Passante, and Bethany Wilcox. (2024). Disparities in access to US quantum information education. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010131)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Cross-correlation analysis of the regression's independent variables (Appendix C, reported in the Discussion) found a "strong correlation ( r = 0.62)" between MSI status and Pell grant share, which the authors read as income-mediated racial equity concern.

> "strong correlation ( r = 0.62) between the independent variables Is_MSI and Pell_Grant warrants concern about racial equality and equity in access to QIS educational programs even if the effect is primarily mediated through income rather than race"
