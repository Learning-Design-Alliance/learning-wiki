Evidence¶

Alharbi 2024¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis across the included pre/post studies. The review reports "an increase ranging from 20 to 50%" in post- over pre-intervention scores; no pooled effect size was computed.

"Furthermore, there was an improvement in the knowledge and skill acquisition as the review identified a consistent increase across studies, with post-intervention scores showing an increase ranging from 20 to 50% compared to pre-intervention scores."

Alharbi 2024 (2)¶

Alharbi, A., Nurfianti, A., Mullen, R. F., McClure, J. D., & Miller, W. H. (2024). The effectiveness of simulation-based learning (SBL) on students’ knowledge and skills in nursing programs: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education, 24. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06080-z

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Quasi-experimental pre/post study of 89 fourth-year nursing students in Turkey using medium-fidelity CPR simulation. The review reports the printed means and the "45.9% improvement in CPR skills"; the study table reports p < 0.001 for the skills gain.

"Additionally, the mean CPR skills score improved from 22.29 ± 5.07 pre-test to 32.51 ± 1.80 post-test, resulting in a 45.9% improvement in CPR skills."
