# After training, the simulated patient and relative express more medical and emotional words while residents use fewer medical and social words

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The simulated patient used significantly more medical (RR=1.55; P<0.001) and emotional (RR=2.76; P<0.001) terms, and the simulated relative more medical words (RR=1.37; P=0.005), after training. [→ Merckaert I 2013](#merckaert-i-2013)
`q3 i?` Trained residents showed a significant decrease in medical (RR=0.81; P<0.001) and social (RR=0.84; P=0.040) words and a downward trend in emotional words (RR=0.79; P=0.088). [→ Merckaert I 2013 (2)](#merckaert-i-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Merckaert I 2013

Merckaert I, Liénard A, Libert Y, Bragard I, Delvaux N, Etienne A-M, Marchal S, Meunier J, Reynaert C, Slachmuylder J-L, Razavi D. (2013). Is it possible to improve the breaking bad news skills of residents when a relative is present? A randomised study. British Journal of Cancer. https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2013.615

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

GEE analysis of LaComm dictionary-based word counts (medical, emotional, social) in the randomised study (n=95). The relative's emotional-word increase was marginal only (RR=2.04; P=0.075).

> "For the simulated patient, regression analysis showed a significant increase in the use of medical (RR=1.55; P<0.001) and emotional (2.76; P<0.001) terms in the second assessment session compared with baseline for the training group compared with the control group."

### Merckaert I 2013 (2)

Merckaert I, Liénard A, Libert Y, Bragard I, Delvaux N, Etienne A-M, Marchal S, Meunier J, Reynaert C, Slachmuylder J-L, Razavi D. (2013). Is it possible to improve the breaking bad news skills of residents when a relative is present? A randomised study. British Journal of Cancer. https://doi.org/10.1038/bjc.2013.615

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same LaComm/GEE word-count analysis for residents. The emotional-word decrease did not reach significance (P=0.088).

> "regression analyses showed a significant decrease in the count of medical (RR=0.81; P<0.001) and social (RR=0.84; P=0.040) words used, and a downward trend in the count of emotional words used (RR=0.79; P=0.088) for the training group compared with the control group."
