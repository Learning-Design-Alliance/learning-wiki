# Third-grade pupils using the audio-and-image (AI) mode attained significantly higher music theory post-test scores than pupils using the text-and-image (TI) mode

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pupils learning music theory via audio and image significantly outperformed pupils learning via text and image on the post-test (p = 0.000). [→ Aldalalah and Fong 2010](#aldalalah-and-fong-2010)

## Evidence

### Aldalalah and Fong 2010

Osamah (Mohammad Ameen) Ahmad Aldalalah & Soon Fook Fong. (2010). Effects of Modality and Redundancy Principles on the Learning and Attitude of a Computer-Based Music Theory Lesson among Jordanian Primary Pupils. International Education Studies, 3(3). https://www.ccsenet.org/ies

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA post hoc (LSD) pairwise comparison of post-test scores among three treatment groups of third-grade pupils, with pre-test scores as covariate. The AI group (Mean = 25.17, SD = 3.35) significantly outperformed the TI group (Mean = 16.82, SD = 3.77), "p = 0.000".

> "There were significant differences between pupils’ learning in AI group and pupils’ learning in TI group (p = 0.000), and there were significant differences between pupils’ learning in AI group and pupils’ learning in AIT group (p = 0.000)."
