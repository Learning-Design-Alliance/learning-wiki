# Evidence for integrated EF–mathematics interventions is growing but mixed

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some integrated interventions improved EF and numeracy, but a proof-of-principle study found integrated training improved mathematics no more than numeracy-only training. [→ Scerif 2023](#scerif-2023)

## Evidence

### Scerif 2023

Scerif, G., Blakey, E., Gattas, S., Hawes, Z., Howard, S., Merkley, R., O'Connor, R., & Simms, V. (2023). Making the Executive 'Function' for the Foundations of Mathematics: the Need for Explicit Theories of Change for Early Interventions. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09824-3

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Prager et al. (2023), a small-scale proof-of-principle study contrasting EF-only, numeracy-only, and integrated conditions; integrated training "improved mathematics, but not to a significantly greater degree than the numeracy condition only".

> "Training in EF alone improved EF, but not general mathematics skills, whereas integrated EF and mathematics training improved mathematics, but not to a significantly greater degree than the numeracy condition only."
