# Mental practice produced no statistically significant greater improvement than textbook study on the Task Specific Evaluation of Mastoidectomy among senior residents

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a randomized trial of 12 PGY-5 residents, the mental practice group's post-intervention Task Specific Evaluation score was higher than the textbook group's but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.736), and the group-by-test interaction was non-significant (p = 0.922). ## Evidence

### Conlin Anne 2016

Conlin Anne, Lea Jane, Bance Manohar, Chadha Neil, Kilty Shaun, Kozak Frederick, Savage Julian, Sidhu Ravindar, Chen Joseph, Westerberg Brian D. (2016). Mental practice in postgraduate training: a randomized controlled trial in mastoidectomy skills. Journal of Otolaryngology - Head & Neck Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40463-016-0162-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial in which 12 senior OHNS residents performed baseline and 48-h post-intervention temporal bone dissections, video-recorded and blindly rated by two otologists. The article reports the mental practice group "had higher post-intervention total score than the Textbook Study group but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.736)"; no effect size is printed.

> "On total Task-Specific Evaluation for Mastoidectomy score (primary outcome), subjects in the Mental Practice group had higher post-intervention total score than the Textbook Study group but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.736)."
