# Creating products as part of problem-driven learning does not change its effect on motivation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q4`

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Implementations with and without product creation showed no statistically significant difference in motivation effects (F < 0.01, p = 0.970). [→ Wijnia 2024](#wijnia-2024)

## Evidence

### Wijnia 2024

Wijnia, L., Noordzij, G., Arends, L. R. A., Rikers, R. M. J. P., & Loyens, S. M. M. (2024). The Effects of Problem-Based, Project-Based, and Case-Based Learning on Students' Motivation: a Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09864-3

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

CHE-model and three-level moderator analysis of product creation (e.g., website, report, presentation), which is integral to PjBL but sometimes incorporated into PBL. Table 3 reports d = 0.471 (no product) versus d = 0.481 (product), F < 0.01, p = 0.970 — a null result; equivalence was not tested.

> "Our analyses revealed that there were no differences between implementations with or without product creation on motivation."
