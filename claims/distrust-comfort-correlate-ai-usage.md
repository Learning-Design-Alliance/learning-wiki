## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Distrust in AI correlated negatively with usage (ρ = –0.38, p < 0.01): students lacking trust used the tool significantly less. [→ Sajja 2026](#sajja-2026)
`q2 i2` Comfort with the AI correlated positively with usage (ρ = 0.31, p < 0.05), as did perceived homework helpfulness (ρ = 0.30, p < 0.05). [→ Sajja 2026 (2)](#sajja-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Sajja 2026

Sajja, R., Sermet, Y., Fodale, B., & Demir, I. (2026). Evaluating AI-Powered Learning Assistants in Engineering Higher Education: Student Engagement, Ethical Challenges, and Policy Implications. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Spearman correlations between post-survey variables and system usage (Table 6, N = 44), top five correlations considered. The printed effect size is ρ = –0.38, a large correlation.

> "The strongest association was a negative correlation between distrust in AI and system usage (ρ = –0.38, p < 0.01), indicating that students who lacked trust in the AI assistant were significantly less likely to use it."

### Sajja 2026 (2)

Sajja, R., Sermet, Y., Fodale, B., & Demir, I. (2026). Evaluating AI-Powered Learning Assistants in Engineering Higher Education: Student Engagement, Ethical Challenges, and Policy Implications. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5)

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Same correlational analysis (Table 6, N = 44); homework helpfulness ρ = 0.30 (p < 0.05). Perceived convenience (ρ = 0.22) and grade helpfulness (ρ = 0.14) were positive but not significant.

> "comfort level with the AI (relative to human assistance) was positively associated with usage (ρ = 0.31, p < 0.05)"
