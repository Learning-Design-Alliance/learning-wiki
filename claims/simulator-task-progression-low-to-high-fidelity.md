# Students in simulator training encounter a progression from simplified low-fidelity tasks to more complex higher-fidelity tasks as a deliberate instructional logic.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The instructional sequence moves students from a simplified simulation (boxes on a flat platform) toward a more complex task (grappling logs on uneven ground) with higher fidelity. [→ Giulia Messina Dahlberg · Susanne Gustavsson 2025](#giulia-messina-dahlberg-susanne-gustavsson-2025)

## Evidence

### Giulia Messina Dahlberg · Susanne Gustavsson 2025

Giulia Messina Dahlberg · Susanne Gustavsson. (2025). Keeping worlds apart to put them back together: VET teachers' instructional patterns in simulation-based training. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09714-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of Excerpt 1, a video-recorded interactional sequence in the simulator-centre where a student moves boxes with a forwarder simulator. The authors report that "an easier task leads to the next one that is more complex in terms of a higher fidelity".

> "The fact that the current task is a preparation for another one that includes the moving of logs (rather than boxes) on the ground (rather than a flat platform) shows a logic wherein an easier task leads to the next one that is more complex in terms of a higher fidelity"
