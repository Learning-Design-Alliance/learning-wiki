Evidence¶

Bernstein Brian O. 2021¶

Bernstein Brian O., Lubinski David, Benbow Camilla P. (2021). Academic Acceleration in Gifted Youth and Fruitless Concerns Regarding Psychological Well-Being: A 35-Year Longitudinal Study. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000500

q3 · i1 · associational · r3

Constructive replication surveying 478 elite STEM graduate students identified in 1992 at age 25 and followed up at age 50 with the same instruments. The printed effect size r = .11 (p = .02) for the acceleration composite is called small but significant and positive; Table 4 shows only two of ten correlations reach the small threshold.

"Overall, the correlation between psychological well-being and age of high school graduation was r(408) = .08, p = .09; the correlation between psychological well-being and the acceleration composite was r(452) = .11 p = .02."

Bernstein Brian O. 2021 (2)¶

Bernstein Brian O., Lubinski David, Benbow Camilla P. (2021). Academic Acceleration in Gifted Youth and Fruitless Concerns Regarding Psychological Well-Being: A 35-Year Longitudinal Study. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000500

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Structural equation models for Study 2 controlling for latent SES, with good-to-adequate fit (Model 3 CFI = .969; Model 4 CFI = .928). The printed standardized path coefficients were .13 (p = .015) in Model 3 and .10 (p = .065) in Model 4, described as small and mirroring Study 1's null-to-trivial pattern; no standardized effect size is printed, so impact is unquantified.

"In each model, the coefficient of interest (from the indicator of academic acceleration to psychological well-being) was small: .13 (p = .015) in Model 3 and .10 (p = .065) in Model 4."
