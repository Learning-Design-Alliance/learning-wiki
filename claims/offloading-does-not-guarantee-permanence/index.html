Evidence¶

Chen 2026¶

Chen, O., Allen, R., Waterman, A., & Sweller, J. (2026). The Relationship Between Cognitive Offloading and the Transient Information Effect. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10132-9

q2 · i? · review · r2

The article states that "increasing information permanence, that is, making it more available and accessible by externalising to the environment, can aid cognitive performance but does not necessarily always result in representational permanence." This qualifies the general assumption in Cognitive Load Theory.

Related Claims¶

  • Substitutive offloaders show severe performance decline when external stores become unavailable, while duplicative offloaders maintain accuracy — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Offloading information to external stores is associated with lower memory performance, moderated by awareness of a later test — related
  • Cognitive offloading to AI during early learning can stifle development of stable knowledge and skills — related
  • Individuals systematically overuse reminders relative to the optimal offloading level, and reminder bias correlates with confidence and metacognitive bias — related
  • Both cognitive offloading and the transient information effect reduce extraneous cognitive load by making information more accessible or less transient — related
  • The transient information effect states that learning from transient presentations leads to inferior learning compared to permanent presentations — related
