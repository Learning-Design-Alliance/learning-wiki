Evidence¶

Amponsah 2026¶

Amponsah, K. D., Adu-Boahen, J., Commey-Mintah, P., Kumassah, E. K., Ayittey, R. F., Nketsiah, J. (2026). Perceptions And Acceptance of Artificial Intelligence in Science Education Programmes: Voices of Pre-Service Science Teachers. Education Quarterly Reviews, 9(3), 51-65. https://doi.org/10.31014/aior.1993.09.03.724

q2 · i? · associational · r2

One-sample t-tests on performance expectancy items in the survey of 380 pre-service science teachers; Table 2 reports means from 3.80 to 4.13, all above the midpoint of 3.0, with p < .001 for every item.

"One-sample t-test results revealed that all these mean scores were significantly higher than the neutral midpoint, as indicated by statistically significant t-values (p < .001), suggesting that teachers' positive perceptions of AI are not due to chance but represent a meaningful trend in the sample."

Amponsah 2026 (2)¶

Amponsah, K. D., Adu-Boahen, J., Commey-Mintah, P., Kumassah, E. K., Ayittey, R. F., Nketsiah, J. (2026). Perceptions And Acceptance of Artificial Intelligence in Science Education Programmes: Voices of Pre-Service Science Teachers. Education Quarterly Reviews, 9(3), 51-65. https://doi.org/10.31014/aior.1993.09.03.724

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Table 3 reports effort expectancy means from 3.52 to 3.90, all significantly above the midpoint (p < .001). The article notes "some reservations about the effort involved" on the lowest-scoring item.

"The results for effort expectancy indicate that teachers generally perceive AI applications as easy to use and integrate into science teaching."
