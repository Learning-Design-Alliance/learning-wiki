# Children with CKD in the lowest global SES quartile are less likely to show average-or-above parent-rated numeracy and literacy performance than those in the highest quartile

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Compared with the highest SES quartile, children in the lowest quartile had lower adjusted odds of average-or-above performance in numeracy (aOR 0.39) and literacy (aOR 0.32). [→ Khalid Rabia 2022](#khalid-rabia-2022)

## Evidence

### Khalid Rabia 2022

Khalid Rabia, Van Zwieten Anita, Kim Siah, Didsbury Madeleine, Francis Anna, Mctaggart Steven, Walker Amanda, Mackie Fiona E., Prestidge Chanel, Teixeira-Pinto Armando, Barton Belinda, Lorenzo Jennifer, Lah Suncica, Howard Kirsten, Nassar Natasha, Au Eric, Tong Allison, Blazek Katrina, Craig Jonathan C., Wong Germaine. (2022). Association between socioeconomic status and academic performance in children and adolescents with chronic kidney disease. Pediatric Nephrology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00467-022-05515-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional multivariable logistic regression analysis of KCAD baseline data from 377 children with CKD, adjusted for child age, CKD stage, ethnicity, gender, caregiver age, marital status, and location. The lowest quartile showed "0.39 (0.18–0.86) for numeracy, and 0.88 (0.41–1.85), 0.77 (0.35–1.66), and 0.32 (0.14–0.72) for literacy" relative to the highest quartile.

> "With reference to the highest global SES quartile, adjusted ORs (95%CI) for average or above average performance by SES quartile in descending order were 1.24 (0.60–2.54), 0.76 (0.37–1.58), and 0.39 (0.18–0.86) for numeracy, and 0.88 (0.41–1.85), 0.77 (0.35–1.66), and 0.32 (0.14–0.72) for literacy."
