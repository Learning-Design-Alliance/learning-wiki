## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After the 5-day curriculum, the fraction of interns with at least 'average' self-perceived procedural confidence rose from 32.3 to 85.7% (p < .001), and self-perceived competence from 30.6 to 81.3% (p < .001). [→ Dversdal Renee K. 2018](#dversdal-renee-k-2018)

## Evidence

### Dversdal Renee K. 2018

Dversdal Renee K., Gold Jeffrey A., Richards Matthew H., Chiovaro Joseph C., Iossi Katherine A., Mansoor André M., Hunter Alan J., Desai Sima S. (2018). A 5-day intensive curriculum for interns utilizing simulation and active-learning techniques: addressing domains important across internal medicine practice. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-018-4011-4

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre/post paired survey analysis of pooled 2014 (N = 33) and 2015 (N = 32) intern cohorts using student paired t-tests on 1–5 Likert self-ratings. The article reports "increased from 32.3 to 85.7% (p < .001)" for confidence and 30.6 vs 81.3% for competence.

> "the fraction of interns who had at least “average” self-perceived confidence across the procedures increased from 32.3 to 85.7% (p < .001). Similar results were observed with self-perceived competence (30.6 vs 81.3%; p < .001)."
