### LM Dana 2023

LM Dana, Benjamin Pollard, and Sara Mueller. (2023). More than technical support: the professional contexts of physics instructional labs. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020127)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Thematic analysis of interviews under the "Only one" code, where a subset of interviewees described being the only person at their institution associated with instructional labs. One faculty interviewee reported doing all listed responsibilities because "there was basically no one else doing it." The authors link this to the wide range of equipment shown in Figure 1.

> "As a general trend, we found that lab instructors tend to “wear many hats” that require a range of overlap- ping skills and expertise."

### LM Dana 2023 (2)

LM Dana, Benjamin Pollard, and Sara Mueller. (2023). More than technical support: the professional contexts of physics instructional labs. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020127](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020127)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Authors' interpretive claim from the thematic analysis: lab instructor roles fill gaps between canonical roles such as pedagogy, technical expertise, or logistics. The article states "elements of perpetual shifting and a sense of ambiguity characterize the experiences of many lab instructors and support staﬀ." The authors note they do not fully embrace the anthropological connotations of liminality.

> "In that way, we suggest that instructional lab staﬀ occupy a liminal space, in the sense of being in between the jobs of their colleagues."
