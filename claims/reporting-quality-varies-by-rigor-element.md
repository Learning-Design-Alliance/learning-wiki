# Reporting quality was highest for data collection (84.2% high ratings) and data analysis (80.7%), and lowest for mixed methods study design (47.4%), aims and purposes (52.6%), and elements of writing (54.4%)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 57 mixed methods articles, high ratings were most frequent for data collection and data analysis and least frequent for study design labeling, aims and purposes, and elements of writing. ### Matthew T. McCrudden · Macy Bowman · Gala Sofia Campos Oaxaca 2025

Matthew T. McCrudden · Macy Bowman · Gala Sofia Campos Oaxaca. (2025). Reporting of Methodological Rigor in Empirical Mixed Methods Research in Educational Psychology. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10090-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of 57 articles coded with a six-element rubric adapted from Harrison et al. (2020) by two independent raters (94.7% pre-reconciliation agreement). The review reports "data collection (84.2%; n = 48/57) and data analysis (80.7%; n = 46/57)" received the most high ratings.

> "The two characteristics with the greatest quantity of high ratings were data collection (84.2%; n = 48/57) and data analysis (80.7%; n = 46/57). The articles also tended to receive high ratings for integration (71.9%; n = 41/57)."
