## Evidence

### Gill CJ 2014

Gill CJ, MacLeod WB, Phiri-Mazala G, Guerina NG, Mirochnick M, Knapp AB, Hamer DH. (2014). Can traditional birth attendants be trained to accurately identify septic infants, initiate antibiotics, and refer in a rural African setting? Global Health, Science and Practice. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-14-00045

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Prospective follow-up of 1,889 live-born neonates delivered by the 60 intervention TBAs: 208 (11.0%) were referred, and "176/208 [84.6%] completed their referral to the RHC"; amoxicillin was administered 188 times, split roughly evenly between week 1 and weeks 2–4.

> "The majority of neonates (176/208 [84.6%]) completed their referral to the RHC. TBAs administered amoxicillin 188 times, 99 times during week 1 (52.7% of total administrations) and 89 times (47.3% of total administrations) during weeks 2–4."

### Gill CJ 2014 (2)

Gill CJ, MacLeod WB, Phiri-Mazala G, Guerina NG, Mirochnick M, Knapp AB, Hamer DH. (2014). Can traditional birth attendants be trained to accurately identify septic infants, initiate antibiotics, and refer in a rural African setting? Global Health, Science and Practice. https://doi.org/10.9745/GHSP-D-14-00045

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Concordance analysis in the same cohort: "171/208 (82.2%)" of referred neonates received amoxicillin and "171/183 (93.4%)" of amoxicillin recipients were referred, indicating the trained behaviors of referring and dosing were tightly coupled.

> "Among referred neonates, 171/208 (82.2%) received amoxicillin; among neonates given amoxicillin, 171/183 (93.4%) were referred (referral status unknown for 5 subjects)."
