## Evidence

### Costar 2020

Costar, D. M., & Hall, K. K. (2020). Improving Team Performance and Patient Safety on the Job Through Team Training and Performance Support Tools: A Systematic Review. Journal of Patient Safety. https://doi.org/10.1097/PTS.0000000000000746

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of 20 included studies, most using quasiexperimental premeasure/postmeasure designs (75%), reports transfer-level evidence: "10 studies in the review provide evidence of improved team KSAs on the job a minimum of 30 days after team training initiatives."

> "Consistent with Kirkpatrick’s transfer level criteria, 10 studies in the review provide evidence of improved team KSAs on the job a minimum of 30 days after team training initiatives, 4 demonstrate improvement on clinical processes, and 3 support an increase in patient safety."

### Costar 2020 (2)

Costar, D. M., & Hall, K. K. (2020). Improving Team Performance and Patient Safety on the Job Through Team Training and Performance Support Tools: A Systematic Review. Journal of Patient Safety. https://doi.org/10.1097/PTS.0000000000000746

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports two studies measuring sustained knowledge: CRM knowledge rose from "61%–66%, P = 0.026" at 30 days, and 14 of 21 TeamSTEPPS Knowledge Test items remained significantly improved at 90 days. Only test statistics are printed; no effect sizes.

> "Levy et al15 reported that overall knowledge of CRM had significantly increased at 30-day follow-up (61%–66%, P = 0.026), whereas Lisbon et al16 documented longer maintenance of knowledge, as significant improvements were noted on 14 of 21 TeamSTEPPS Knowledge Test items 90 days after training (P < 0.05)."

### Costar 2020 (3)

Costar, D. M., & Hall, K. K. (2020). Improving Team Performance and Patient Safety on the Job Through Team Training and Performance Support Tools: A Systematic Review. Journal of Patient Safety. https://doi.org/10.1097/PTS.0000000000000746

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review narrates a TeamSTEPPS study in surgical and pediatric intensive care units where teamwork perceptions improved on all 6 dimensions at 30 days and "remained significant on 5 of the 6 dimensions at a 12-month follow-up (P < 0.01)." One other study found no significant improvement over baseline.

> "Mayer et al19 reported that perceptions of teamwork significantly improved on the 6 dimensions measured 30 days after the training (P < 0.01) and remained significant on 5 of the 6 dimensions at a 12-month follow-up (P < 0.01)."
