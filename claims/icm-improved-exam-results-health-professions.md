## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Tolks D 2016

Tolks D, Schäfer C, Raupach T, Kruse L, Sarikas A, Gerhardt-Szép S, Kllauer G, Lemos M, Fischer MR, Eichner B, Sostmann K, Hege I. (2016). An Introduction to the Inverted/Flipped Classroom Model in Education and Advanced Training in Medicine and in the Healthcare Professions. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001045](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001045)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review report of the University of North Carolina School of Pharmacy redesign, in which facts were transmitted online and classroom activities included clickers, pair-and-share and quizzes. The review reports "significantly improved exam results" versus the previous semester, 93.1% satisfaction and higher attendance; no effect size is printed.

> "The students (n=150) showed significantly improved exam results in comparison with the previous, traditionally instructed semester. Furthermore, greater course concept satisfaction (93.1%) and higher attendance rates were observed"

### Tolks D 2016 (2)

Tolks D, Schäfer C, Raupach T, Kruse L, Sarikas A, Gerhardt-Szép S, Kllauer G, Lemos M, Fischer MR, Eichner B, Sostmann K, Hege I. (2016). An Introduction to the Inverted/Flipped Classroom Model in Education and Advanced Training in Medicine and in the Healthcare Professions. GMS Journal for Medical Education. [doi:10.3205/zma001045](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001045)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Review report of Prober and Heath's biochemistry implementation, comparing learning results with the previous semester. The review reports significantly improved results, very high satisfaction, and "a 30% to 80% higher attendance rate"; no effect size is printed.

> "Prober and Heath implemented the Inverted Classroom Model in a biochemistry curriculum and also showed significantly improved learning results in comparison with the previous semester. They too observed very high satisfaction levels and a 30% to 80% higher attendance rate"
