Evidence¶

Sten CW 2024¶

Sten CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Single-group pretest–posttest study of 32 resident psychiatrists using an IPS intervention with an 11-item multiple-choice knowledge test on PTSD symptomatology, clinical management, and communication skills. The review reports "a significant improvement on the knowledge test"; no effect size value is printed.

"This study reported a significant improvement on the knowledge test after the VR training intervention."

Sten CW 2024 (2)¶

Sten CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Controlled before-and-after study of 79 medical students in psychiatric clinical training; the experimental group received an IPS intervention alongside traditional learning. The review reports "significantly higher posttest scores in the experimental group" with a moderate effect size.

"The outcome was evaluated with a knowledge test administered before and after the intervention with significantly higher posttest scores in the experimental group than in the control group, with a moderate effects size observed between the groups."

Sten CW 2024 (3)¶

Sten CW, Söderström K, Stensrud B, Nylund IB, Siqveland J. (2024). The effectiveness of virtual reality training on knowledge, skills and attitudes of health care professionals and students in assessing and treating mental health disorders: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05423-0

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Prospective cohort study of 299 undergraduate nursing students using a VP game design; outcome was accuracy of diagnoses of depression and schizophrenia case vignettes. The review reports "no statistically significant effect"; equivalence was not tested.

"The study found no statistically significant effect of VR training on diagnostic accuracy between the simulation and the non-simulation cohort."

  • A single-group pretest–posttest study found no significant effect of VSP training on attitudes toward individuals with substance abuse disorders but a significant increase in perceived competence — related
  • VR training interventions can promote knowledge and skills acquisition in health care professionals and students for assessing and treating mental health disorders, though the evidence base is uncertain — a broader claim this one bears on
  • An RCT using self-reported combined knowledge and skills found a significant within-group pretest-to-posttest increase with a large effect size, but reported no between-group comparison — related
  • The RCTs showed some concern or high overall risk of bias and the non-randomized studies moderate to serious risk, making the evidence base uncertain — related
