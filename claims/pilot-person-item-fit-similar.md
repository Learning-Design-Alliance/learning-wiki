# Person and item fit statistics were similar across pilot and comparison groups, with items showing overfitting in both

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Mean observed-minus-expected proportions and outfit statistics were close to expected values and very similar between groups for persons and items. [→ J. Patrick Meyer 2023](#j-patrick-meyer-2023)

## Evidence

### J. Patrick Meyer 2023

J. Patrick Meyer, Ann Hu, Sylvia Li. (2023). Content Proximity Spring 2022 Pilot Study Research Brief. Psychometric Solutions, NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/content-proximity-spring-2022-pilot-study-research-report/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Person-fit analysis of observed versus expected proportion correct (Table 2.6) showed means "very similar for each group and all are close to zero"; person outfit means were near the expected value of one in both subjects.

> "The mean values are very similar for each group and all are close to zero. The standard deviations are also quite similar."

### J. Patrick Meyer 2023 (2)

J. Patrick Meyer, Ann Hu, Sylvia Li. (2023). Content Proximity Spring 2022 Pilot Study Research Brief. Psychometric Solutions, NWEA. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/content-proximity-spring-2022-pilot-study-research-report/

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-fit analysis (Tables 2.8-2.9) found outfit statistics "all below one on average," indicating overfitting, with similar item outfit between groups; pilot math showed larger standard deviations in observed-minus-expected differences.

> "As seen with person outfit, the fit statistics in Table 2.9 were all below one on average. Items are also showing overfitting. Item outfit statistics for the two groups are quite similar."
