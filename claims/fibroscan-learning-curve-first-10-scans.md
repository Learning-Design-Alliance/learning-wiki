## Evidence

### Armstrong M J 2013

Armstrong M J, Corbett C, Hodson J, Marwah N, Parker R, Houlihan D D, Rowe I A, Hazlehurst J M, Brown R, Hübscher S G, Mutimer D. (2013). Operator training requirements and diagnostic accuracy of Fibroscan in routine clinical practice. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2012-131640

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Binary logistic regression of all 2311 retrospective clinical LSEs, with scan number log10 transformed to model the learning curve. The model printed "OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.39 to 1.78; p<0.001" for a 10-fold increase in scans performed; no standardised effect size is printed.

> "The model shows that a 10-fold increase in the number of LSEs that an operator has performed significantly improves their odds of obtaining a valid LSE (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.39 to 1.78; p<0.001)."

### Armstrong M J 2013 (2)

Armstrong M J, Corbett C, Hodson J, Marwah N, Parker R, Houlihan D D, Rowe I A, Hazlehurst J M, Brown R, Hübscher S G, Mutimer D. (2013). Operator training requirements and diagnostic accuracy of Fibroscan in routine clinical practice. Postgraduate Medical Journal. https://doi.org/10.1136/postgradmedj-2012-131640

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same statistical learning-curve model (figure 2) shows descriptive validity rates: "only 46% of the initial clinical LSEs" were valid, rising to 57% by 10 LSEs, 64.7% by 50 and 67.7% by 100; the model forecasts approximately 2500 LSE for 80% validity.

> "Figure 2 shows that only 46% of the initial clinical LSEs performed by an operator were valid, whereas the validity rate rises to 57% by 10 LSEs."
