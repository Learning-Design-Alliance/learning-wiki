## Evidence

### Kaluschke Maximilian 2023

Kaluschke Maximilian, Yin Myat Su, Haddawy Peter, Suebnukarn Siriwan, Zachmann Gabriel. (2023). The effect of 3D stereopsis and hand-tool alignment on learning effectiveness and skill transfer of a VR-based simulator for dental training. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291389

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized controlled study of 40 dental students, the pooled "mono" groups improved real plastic-teeth error ratings by −0.472 (p = 0.082) while the "stereo" groups did not improve (difference −0.0263, p = 0.4695); the between-group learning-gain difference of 0.446 was not statistically significant (p = 0.348).

> "In contrast, the “mono” group’s post-test error ratings (M = 2.08, SD = 1.26) improved compared to the pre-test error ratings (M = 2.56, SD = 1.04). This large difference of −0.472 have a statistical significance of p = 0.082 (t(17) = 1.45)."

### Kaluschke Maximilian 2023 (2)

Kaluschke Maximilian, Yin Myat Su, Haddawy Peter, Suebnukarn Siriwan, Zachmann Gabriel. (2023). The effect of 3D stereopsis and hand-tool alignment on learning effectiveness and skill transfer of a VR-based simulator for dental training. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0291389

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same study's in-simulator analysis, both rendering conditions improved simulator error ratings significantly (mono: 2.45 to 1.79, t(17) = 7.95, p < 0.0001; stereo: 2.01 to 1.77, t(18) = 2.70, p < 0.0001), but the between-condition learning-gain difference was not significant (p = 0.249).

> "The simulator learning gain was larger for the monoscopic 3D condition (M = −0.66, SD = 0.90), similarly to the influence on the real-world learning gain. The training gains in the stereoscopic condition were −0.24 on average (SD = 0.99)."
