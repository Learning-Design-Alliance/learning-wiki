## Evidence

### Winch 1913

Winch, W. H. (1913). Experimental researches on learning to spell. Part I. *Journal of Educational Psychology, 4*(9), 525–537. [doi:10.1037/h0075675](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075675)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled experiment with 53 Standard II boys in a London elementary school. Two equal-difficulty word lists were learned by different methods over four 10-minute practice periods across four days, then tested by dictation after a three-day interval. The difference of 1.8 words between the "combined simultaneous method with directed attention" and the "predominantly visual" method was 5 times its probable error.

> "After learning, the results for Set A average 17.6, and for Set B average 15.8. The correlation-coefficient between the results for Set A and Set B is +.90. The difference between the averages is 1.8 and the 'probable error' of that difference is .36. Thus the difference between the averages is 5 times its 'probable error'"
