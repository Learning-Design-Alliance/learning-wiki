Evidence¶

Dillon H. Murphy 2023¶

Dillon H. Murphy, Jeri L. Little, Elizabeth L. Bjork. (2023). The Value of Using Tests in Education as Tools for Learning—Not Just for Assessment. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09808-3

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

The commentary reports, citing Anderson et al. (1994) and educational demonstrations (Chan, 2009; Little et al., 2011, 2012), that "cued-recall tests ... can sometimes impair their ability to later answer questions involving related information." No effect size is printed.

"cued-recall tests, where students are given cues to recall information from memory, can sometimes impair their ability to later answer questions involving related information"

Related Claims¶

  • Practicing retrieval of some portions of an educational text can enhance retention of related nontested portions (retrieval-induced facilitation), but not when relational encoding is disrupted — related
  • Retrieval practice effects on mediator-cued final tests have been positive, but Coppens et al. (2016) concluded the true effect may be only about 0.10 to 0.20 — related
  • Taking initial multiple-choice tests without feedback can lead students to later produce the incorrect lure answers they selected, even when an overall retrieval practice benefit occurs — related
  • Providing feedback after initial multiple-choice tests cut lure intrusions on final short-answer tests roughly in half, with immediate and delayed feedback equally effective — related
  • Multiple-choice testing increases the later production of multiple-choice lures as answers on cued recall tests, even under warning against guessing — related
  • Feedback on a multiple-choice test sharply reduces lure intrusions on a later cued recall test, with immediate and delayed schedules equally effective — related
  • Incorrect information learned from a multiple-choice test is applied and reasoned with on conceptually related transfer questions — related
