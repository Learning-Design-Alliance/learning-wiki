## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For high nonscience students on misconception items, gains of students whose teachers had only SMK were not significantly different from those of students whose teachers lacked SMK; gains were significantly larger only when teachers had both SMK and KOSM. [→ Sadler 2013](#sadler-2013)

## Evidence

### Sadler 2013

Sadler, P. M., Sonnert, G., Coyle, H. P., Cook-Smith, N., & Miller, J. L. (2013). The Influence of Teachers' Knowledge on Student Learning in Middle School Physical Science Classrooms. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831213477680

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Item-level hierarchical logistic regression (181 teachers, 9,556 students) comparing teacher-knowledge categories on the 12 misconception items. The Results report that "Only when teachers had both SMK and KOSM were student gains significantly larger."
