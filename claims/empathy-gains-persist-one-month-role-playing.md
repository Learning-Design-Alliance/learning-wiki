# The empathy-score gains of operating room nursing students after role-playing training persist at 1 month, with no significant change between the immediate and 1-month posttests

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Total mean empathy scores at 1 month after the intervention remained significantly higher in the experimental group than in the control group (P< 0.001). [→ Larti N 2018](#larti-n-2018)
`q3 i?` Within the experimental group, there was no significant difference between scores immediately after and at 1 month after the intervention (P= 0.94). [→ Larti N 2018 (2)](#larti-n-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Larti N 2018

Larti N, Ashouri E, Aarabi A. (2018). The effects of an empathy role-playing program for operating room nursing students in Iran. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2018.15.29

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up comparison in the same 77-student quasi-experimental study; experimental mean at 1 month was 122.11 vs control 105.02 (Table 3, t= 6.66, P< 0.001). No effect size printed.

> "Regarding the durability of the empathy scores, the results of the present study indicated that the total mean empathy score and its dimensions at 1 month after the intervention were significantly higher in the experimental group than in the control group."

### Larti N 2018 (2)

Larti N, Ashouri E, Aarabi A. (2018). The effects of an empathy role-playing program for operating room nursing students in Iran. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2018.15.29

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Bonferroni-corrected paired comparison within the experimental group across time points (Table 4); the paired interval immediately after vs 1 month after showed P= 0.94 for the total score, so equivalence was not tested.

> "however, there was no significant difference between the mean scores immediately after and at 1 month after the intervention (P> 0.05)"
