# Solution behavior accuracy does not decrease across item positions, contradicting decreasing effort models of unmotivated test taking

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students with RTE below .90, the percentage of rapid guesses increased across item positions (from under 5% to roughly 35%), but the accuracy of solution behaviors did not decrease, so effort declined only in the form of more rapid guesses rather than generically diminishing effort. [→ Wise 2015](#wise-2015)

## Evidence

### Wise 2015

Wise, S. L., & Kingsbury, G. G. (2015). Modeling Student Test-Taking Motivation in the Context of an Adaptive Achievement Test. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, Chicago. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Modeling+Student+Test-Taking+Motivation+in+the+Context+of+an+Adaptive+Achievement+Test

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-position analysis of test events from the 5,567 students whose RTE was less than .90 (Figure 2). The article reports rapid guesses rose "from less than 5% at the beginning of the test to roughly 35% near the end" while solution behavior accuracy stayed near .50, supporting a bifurcated rather than decreasing-effort view.

> "Collectively, Figure 2 indicates that while the percentage of rapid guesses increased during the test event, the accuracy of solution behaviors did not decrease."
