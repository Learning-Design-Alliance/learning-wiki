## Evidence

### Xin Sun 2023

Xin Sun, Owen Norton and Shaylene E. Nancekivell. (2023). Beware the myth: learning styles affect parents', children's, and teachers' thinking about children's academic potential. npj Science of Learning 8:46. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00190-x

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Pre-registered Hypothesis 2B analysis of non-core subjects in Experiment 3. "participants rated the hands-on learner to score higher than the visual learner in Art" (d = 0.35), Gym (d = 1.42), and Music (d = 1.14); the Learning Style × Subject interaction was significant, d = –0.48.

> "participants rated the hands-on learner to score higher than the visual learner in Art (( M(SD)Visual= 8.61(1.55), (M(SD)Hands-on= 9.09(1.10), t = 3.47, p < 0.001, d = 0.35)"

### Xin Sun 2023 (2)

Xin Sun, Owen Norton and Shaylene E. Nancekivell. (2023). Beware the myth: learning styles affect parents', children's, and teachers' thinking about children's academic potential. npj Science of Learning 8:46. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00190-x

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Pre-registered Hypothesis 3 replicated Experiment 2 with a works-harder comparison. "The main effect of the question type was signi ﬁcant", odds ratio = 0.44: participants picked the visual learner as smarter and the hands-on learner as working harder; the question × group interaction was not significant (p = 0.990).

> "The main effect of the question type was signi ﬁcant, b = –0.83, 95% CI = [–1.04, –0.612], z = –7.59, p < 0.001, odds ratio = 0.44."
