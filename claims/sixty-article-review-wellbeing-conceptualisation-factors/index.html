Evidence¶

Bagnall 2025¶

Bagnall, C. L., Jindal-Snape, D., Banwell, E., Panayiotou, M., Mason, C., & Qualter, P. (2025). Emotional Wellbeing in the Context of Primary-Secondary School Transitions: A Concept Analysis Paper. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09990-6

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Systematic literature review following the EPPI-Centre method, 60 articles included. Thematic Analysis identified three implicit themes: presence or absence of psychological symptoms, feelings and affective experience, and quality of life.

"In terms of the conceptualisation of emotional wellbeing, 50 articles did not explicitly conceptualise wellbeing, nor emotional wellbeing."

Bagnall 2025 (2)¶

Bagnall, C. L., Jindal-Snape, D., Banwell, E., Panayiotou, M., Mason, C., & Qualter, P. (2025). Emotional Wellbeing in the Context of Primary-Secondary School Transitions: A Concept Analysis Paper. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09990-6

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Same 60-article review. The seven factors printed with article counts include self-concept (n=7), depressive symptoms/low mood (n=7), anxiety symptoms (n=5), resilience/mental toughness (n=3), coping (n=5), attitudes towards school (n=9), and loneliness (n=2).

"The review also identified seven factors associated with emotional wellbeing across 38 articles, which provides further support for Park et al. (2023) emotional wellbeing psychometrically defined concepts"

Related Claims¶

  • Children (59 across 10 focus groups) conceptualise transitions-context emotional wellbeing around adapting to the unknown, fitting in, and signs of positive wellbeing — related
  • A UK-wide expert panel (n=30) conceptualised transitions-context emotional wellbeing as affective state, tangible signs of adjustment, and mechanisms to maintain a stable affective state — related
  • No single measure exists that assesses children's emotional wellbeing in the context of primary-secondary school transitions; existing measure designs are inadequate — related
  • Most primary-secondary transitions research articles neither clearly define nor operationalise transitions, and nine conceptualisations appear across the 96-article literature — related
  • There is a need for a standardised, validated wellbeing instrument suitable for longitudinal use to evaluate primary-secondary transitions interventions — related
