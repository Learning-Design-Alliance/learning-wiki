Related Claims¶

  • Undergraduate students hold mixed views on whether using GenAI for coursework constitutes plagiarism, ranging from not misconduct to serious misconduct — related
  • PGR students rejected GenAI generation of research ideas, original arguments, or whole papers as violating authorship norms, while accepting surface-level support — related
  • Ethical acceptability of GenAI use varied with disciplinary norms: assistance was more acceptable for supporting or procedural tasks than for core intellectual tasks — a broader claim this one bears on
  • AI-assisted ideation was conditionally acceptable when researchers retained decision-making authority, but problematic when it displaced conceptual labour — related
  • In interviews with 25 engineering students at one Swedish university, students described GenAI as acceptable when it stimulates reflection and directly copying its outputs as cheating, and one used its fallibility as a prompt to double-check — related
  • Student perceptions of AI use in academic work are neutral and range from viewing AI as valid learning assistance to viewing it as cheating, depending on task type, transparency, and assessment design — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Whether student GenAI use constitutes academic misconduct depends fundamentally on the prior question of the university's purpose — related
  • Evaluators view GenAI use in story writing as broadly acceptable but reject unacknowledged use and demand compensation for source creators — related
  • Students and educators disagree on the acceptability of unacknowledged generative AI use across academic writing subtasks — related
