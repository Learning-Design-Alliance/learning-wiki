# Iterative revision produced a 20-item PIQL whose final version meets reliability and item-quality thresholds (Cronbach's α = 0.80 on v2.2)

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` After several revisions, the 20-item PIQL v2.2 has a broad range of difficulty values, all items with discrimination above 0.3, and Cronbach's α = 0.80, meeting thresholds for measuring groups and individuals. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2020](#trevor-i-smith-2020)

`q2 i?` Across versions, more items fall in the desired difficulty range (0.2–0.8) and discrimination values rise, with all 20 v2.2 items above the desired threshold of 0.3. [→ White Brahmia 2021](#white-brahmia-2021)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux. (2020). Toward a valid instrument for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classical test theory analysis of approximately 1000 students per version across six PIQL versions at a large public research university. The final 20-item instrument shows broad difficulty with only one item above 0.8 and "all items having discrimination values above 0.3"; α rose from 0.67 to 0.80.

> "Taken together, these revisions have resulted in a 20-item instrument with a broad range of difﬁculty values (only one of which is above the desired upper limit of 0.8), and all items having discrimination values above 0.3."

### White Brahmia 2021

White Brahmia, S. W., Olsho, A., Smith, T. I., Boudreaux, A., Eaton, P., and Zimmerman, C. (2021). The Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy: A tool for assessing mathematical reasoning in introductory physics. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

CTT analysis of item difficulty and discrimination across the six PIQL versions, shown in Fig. 6 histograms, indicates successive improvement, with all 20 v2.2 items exceeding the desired discrimination threshold and more items falling within the desired difficulty range in each successive version.

> "Additionally, the discrimination values get progressively higher, with all 20 items in v2.2 being higher than the desired threshold (red dashed line)."

### White Brahmia 2021 (2)

White Brahmia, S. W., Olsho, A., Smith, T. I., Boudreaux, A., Eaton, P., and Zimmerman, C. (2021). The Physics Inventory of Quantitative Literacy: A tool for assessing mathematical reasoning in introductory physics. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020129)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Reliability measured by Cronbach's alpha across the iterative instrument versions reached 0.80 on v2.2, the article's printed value, meeting its stated threshold of at least 0.8 for measuring individual students.

> "Additionally, Cronbach’s α went from 0.67 on the protoPIQL to 0.80 on v2.2."
