# Iterative revision produced a 20-item PIQL whose final version meets reliability and item-quality thresholds (Cronbach's α = 0.80 on v2.2)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After several revisions, the 20-item PIQL v2.2 has a broad range of difficulty values, all items with discrimination above 0.3, and Cronbach's α = 0.80, meeting thresholds for measuring groups and individuals. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2020](#trevor-i-smith-2020)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2020

Trevor I. Smith, Philip Eaton, Suzanne White Brahmia, Alexis Olsho, Charlotte Zimmerman, Andrew Boudreaux. (2020). Toward a valid instrument for measuring physics quantitative literacy. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.smith_t)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Classical test theory analysis of approximately 1000 students per version across six PIQL versions at a large public research university. The final 20-item instrument shows broad difficulty with only one item above 0.8 and "all items having discrimination values above 0.3"; α rose from 0.67 to 0.80.

> "Taken together, these revisions have resulted in a 20-item instrument with a broad range of difﬁculty values (only one of which is above the desired upper limit of 0.8), and all items having discrimination values above 0.3."
