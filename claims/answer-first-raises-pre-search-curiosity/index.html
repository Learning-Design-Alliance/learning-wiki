Evidence¶

Ju Ae Kim 2026¶

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Baseline randomization check via independent samples t-test in the randomized experiment: the answer-first manipulation "proved successful in triggering curiosity", with a printed effect size of d = −.50 favoring thinking-before-googling on pre-search curiosity ratings.

"Participants who tried to answer first before resorting to the search engine (the thinking-before-googling condition: M = 5.05, SD =.95) reported significantly higher pre-search curiosity than their counterparts who only read keywords before using the internet (the googling-only condition: M = 4.50, SD = 1.23), t(102) = −2.54, p =.012, d = −.50."

Ju Ae Kim 2026 (2)¶

Ju Ae Kim, Jiwon Kim, Sung-il Kim, Mimi Bong. (2026). How mindful and mindless online searching affects curiosity and information recall. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10570-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Repeated measures ANCOVA on curiosity in the same experiment: the pre-search group difference (ηp² = .07) vanished post-search (F(1,100) = 1.32, p = .253, ηp² = .01), consistent with resolved knowledge gaps.

"Note that the between-group difference in curiosity was significant after the experimental treatment, F (1100) = 6.98, p =.010, ηp 2 =.07, but this differ -ence was no longer evident after participants had engaged in the search task, F(1100) = 1.32, p =.253, ηp 2 =.01."
