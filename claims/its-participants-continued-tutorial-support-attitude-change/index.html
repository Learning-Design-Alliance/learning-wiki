Evidence¶

Copus 2016¶

Copus, C., & McKinney, B. (2016). Early Integration of Tutorial Support in Beginning Algebra. Journal of Developmental Education, 40(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1152251

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Program participation data reported in the discussion section: students were given the option to continue meeting with their tutoring group after the four-week intervention, and 95.3% did so.

"Most students (95.3%) who participated in the program continued with tutorial support throughout the semester."

Copus 2016 (2)¶

Copus, C., & McKinney, B. (2016). Early Integration of Tutorial Support in Beginning Algebra. Journal of Developmental Education, 40(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1152251

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Qualitative anecdotal observation reported by the authors in the discussion, attributed to development of social networks and self-actualization; no instrument or systematic measure is reported.

"More importantly, however, was the noticeable positive change in attitude regarding the learning process, even among those students who did not pass the course."
