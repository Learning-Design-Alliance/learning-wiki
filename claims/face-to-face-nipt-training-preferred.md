## Evidence

### Oxenford K 2017

Oxenford K, Daley R, Lewis C, Hill M, Chitty LS. (2017). Development and evaluation of training resources to prepare health professionals for counselling pregnant women about non-invasive prenatal testing for Down syndrome: a mixed methods study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1315-7

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-training questionnaire (Q2) of 374 attendees plus thematic analysis of 35 interviews; the article reports that 357 (94%) agreed face-to-face training is essential and interviews confirmed the group format was valued for discussion. Descriptive percentages only, no effect size.

> "The vast majority of the midwives (n = 357, 94%) either agreed or strongly agreed that face-to-face training is essential to teach health professionals about NIPT (Additional file 2: Table 2) and the interview findings confirmed that the group format was valued as it allowed discussion."

### Oxenford K 2017 (2)

Oxenford K, Daley R, Lewis C, Hill M, Chitty LS. (2017). Development and evaluation of training resources to prepare health professionals for counselling pregnant women about non-invasive prenatal testing for Down syndrome: a mixed methods study. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1315-7

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-training questionnaire item on e-learning; 287 (82%) respondents viewed an e-learning module favourably. The article separately reports that in interviews "the majority thought it should only be used as an adjunct to face-to-face training". Descriptive percentages only.

> "The majority of respondents (n = 287, 82%) also felt that an e-learning module would be a good way to educate health professionals about NIPT, with only 18% (n = 63) of respondents stating they did not think it would be useful."
