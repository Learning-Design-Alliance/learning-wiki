# Final-year medical students' Quick Risk Test performance rose from a median of 50% to about 90% correct after a 90-minute training session, in one single-group pre-test–post-test study

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a 90-minute training session, students' median percentage correct rose from 50% to 90% (92.3% after the full course segment), with 82% of participants improving their performance. [→ Jenny Mirjam Annina 2018](#jenny-mirjam-annina-2018)

## Evidence

### Jenny Mirjam Annina 2018

Jenny Mirjam Annina, Keller Niklas, Gigerenzer Gerd. (2018). Assessing minimal medical statistical literacy using the Quick Risk Test: a prospective observational study in Germany. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-020847

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Single-group pretest–posttest with 104 final-year students (61.5% of 169 completing both tests); the article reports "82% of participants improved their performance" and 81.7% performed better after training, analysed with χ2 tests.

> "After the 90 min session (and an unrelated task of another 90 min), their performance improved to a median of 92.3% (IQR=83.2%–94.2%) correct answers per question (χ2=300, df=1, p<2e-16)."
