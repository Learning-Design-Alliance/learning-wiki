# When correcting pupil work, the teacher revoices rather than dismisses pupils' solutions, challenging them toward more sophisticated ones

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The teacher transforms pupils' partially inappropriate text (e.g. 'poor past' to 'her life before she married Macbeth') rather than dismissing it. [→ Christian Greiffenhagen 2011](#christian-greiffenhagen-2011)
`q2 i?` Suggestions such as breaking a monologue into smaller pieces challenge pupils toward more sophisticated, more imaginative work. [→ Christian Greiffenhagen 2011 (2)](#christian-greiffenhagen-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Christian Greiffenhagen 2011

Christian Greiffenhagen. (2011). Making Rounds: the routine work of the teacher during collaborative learning with computers. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (forthcoming). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9134-8

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of Fragment 9, where pupils subsequently wrote almost word-by-word the teacher's suggested caption-box and thought-bubble text; the teacher marked their writing as "funny" and "amusing" but outside the assignment, then offered alternatives preserving aspects of it.

> "the teacher does not completely dismiss the pupils' current solutions, but rather transforms or 'revoices' (cf., O'Conner and Michaels, 1993) them"

### Christian Greiffenhagen 2011 (2)

Christian Greiffenhagen. (2011). Making Rounds: the routine work of the teacher during collaborative learning with computers. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning (forthcoming). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-011-9134-8

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of Fragment 8, where the teacher suggested pupils "break it down into smaller pieces" and have characters "moving around" across scenes; the suggestion required pupils to do more than their current single-frame solution.

> "it is not so much that the teacher tells the pupils to do it her way, but that she challenges the pupils to come up with a more sophisticated solution"
