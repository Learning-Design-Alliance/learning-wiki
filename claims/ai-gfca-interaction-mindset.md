## Evidence

### Darmawansah 2025

Darmawansah, D., Hwang, G.-J., Lin, C.-J., & Febiyani, F. (2025). An artificial intelligence-supported GFCA learning model to enhance L2 students' role-play performance, English speaking and interaction mindset. Education Tech Research Dev (2025) 73:1451–1479. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10453-6

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r1`

Mann–Whitney test on the peer-interaction subscale of the 15-item interaction-mindset post-survey (α = 0.69) in the quasi-experiment; peer interaction favored AI-GFCA with "r = 0.32".

> "In terms of peer interaction, students in the AI-GFCA group (Mdn = 5) had better peer interaction than students in the AI-C group (Mdn = 4.5) with U = 158.00, z = − 2.176, p = 0.030, and r = 0.32."

### Darmawansah 2025 (2)

Darmawansah, D., Hwang, G.-J., Lin, C.-J., & Febiyani, F. (2025). An artificial intelligence-supported GFCA learning model to enhance L2 students' role-play performance, English speaking and interaction mindset. Education Tech Research Dev (2025) 73:1451–1479. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10453-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null Mann–Whitney result for the reception-of-peer-feedback subscale; no statistically significant group difference was detected and equivalence was not tested.

> "in terms of reception of peer feedback, students in the AI-GFCA group (Mdn = 4.33) had a relatively equal level of receiving peer feedback with students in the AI-C group (Mdn = 4.33), with U = 135.50, z = − 1.596."

The article's prose gives U = 135.50 for this subscale, but its Table 7 reports U = 185.00; both give a non-significant difference, which is not evidence that the groups are equal.
