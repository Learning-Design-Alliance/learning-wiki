# Students' self-reported supervision level for managing an acute situation rose from median 3 to 4 after the simulation course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students' self-reported supervision level for managing an acute situation shifted from reactive supervision toward distant supervision (median 3 to 4 on a 5-point scale), a statistically significant change with a large effect size. [→ Moore Amber Jennifer Sara 2025](#moore-amber-jennifer-sara-2025)

## Evidence

### Moore Amber Jennifer Sara 2025

Moore Amber Jennifer Sara, Dhas Kallpana, Karunaratne Dilroshini, Dasigan Krishna, Brennan-Davies Ailie, Sahni Ankita, Patrick James, Rasool Lisa Saleh, Hridi Syeda, Carter Sef, Patel Shiras, Fakes Lianne, Kelada Mary, Shohayeb Ammar, Baptista Ana, Bartholomew Brent. (2025). Exploring the role of high-fidelity simulation for pre-foundation assistantship students to improve preparedness for practice. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08477-w

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post questionnaire analysis (N = 247) of self-reported supervision level for managing an acute situation, analysed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The article reports "Standardised effect size was large (0.769)" and a 1-point median improvement.

> "Students perceived they needed more remote supervision after the course (Mdn = 4.00, distant supervision; IQR1 = 3.00, IQR3 = 4.00) compared to before the course (Mdn = 3.00, reactive supervision; IQR1 = 3.00, IQR3 = 3.00) using the Wilcoxon Signed Ranks Test (W = 402.50, z = 12.08, p < 0.001)"
