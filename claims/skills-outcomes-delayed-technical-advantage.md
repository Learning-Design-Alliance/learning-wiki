## Evidence

### Amechghal A 2026

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review synthesis across skills outcomes: clinical reasoning (P=0.576), clinical ability (P=0.721), medical management (P>0.1), and overall performance (P=0.36) showed no significant differences between roles.

> "Overall, skills-related outcomes most often showed no statistically significant between-role differences, with active participant advantages limited to non-technical skills-related affective outcomes [20], delayed technical skills [19], and role-continuity effects on clinical competency [28]."

### Amechghal A 2026 (2)

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Review reports, citing de Moraes et al. [19] (N=44 quasi-experimental, palliative extubation HFS) and Zulkosky et al. [28] (N=159, pediatric critical care HFS); only P-values printed, no effect size.

> "2 findings favored active participants: higher technical skills at 14-day follow-up (P=0.047) [19] and better performance among learners maintaining the active participant role across repeated scenarios (P=0.003) [28]."
