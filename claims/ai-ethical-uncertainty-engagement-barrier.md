## Evidence

### Sajja 2026

Sajja, R., Sermet, Y., Fodale, B., & Demir, I. (2026). Evaluating AI-Powered Learning Assistants in Engineering Higher Education: Student Engagement, Ethical Challenges, and Policy Implications. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-survey item on misconduct worry (Figure 6, N = 45) in the two engineering courses. The article calls this concern "a significant deterrent to full engagement with AI tools"; no effect size printed.

> "a majority of students (58%) reported worry about being accused of academic misconduct when using AI, compared to 31% who disagreed, and 11% who were neutral."

### Sajja 2026 (2)

Sajja, R., Sermet, Y., Fodale, B., & Demir, I. (2026). Evaluating AI-Powered Learning Assistants in Engineering Higher Education: Student Engagement, Ethical Challenges, and Policy Implications. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39237-5)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-survey apprehension item (Figure 6, N = 45); the article describes a polarized response suggesting an emotional or psychological barrier. Descriptive percentages only.

> "49% agreed they felt apprehensive, while 24% disagreed, and 27% reported neutral feelings."
