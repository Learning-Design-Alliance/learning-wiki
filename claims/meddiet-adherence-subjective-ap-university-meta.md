## Evidence

### Peral-Martínez F. 2026

Peral-Martínez F., Bizzozero-Peroni B., Díaz-Goñi V., Olivo-Martins-de-Passos T., Peraile-Huerta A.M., Prieto-Ayuso A., Jiménez-López E., Martínez-Vizcaíno V., Mesas A.E. (2026). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet and Academic Performance in University Students: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Nutrients. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18172866

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Exploratory random-effects meta-analysis of 3 cross-sectional studies using subjective AP measures (academic self-concept via AF-5, perceived academic progress). The pooled estimate was "SMD = 0.42; 95% CI: 0.14 to 0.71" with I2 = 0.0% (p = 0.778); the prediction interval (−0.20 to 1.05) crossed the null.

> "Higher MedDiet adherence was also significantly associated with better subjective AP (SMD = 0.42; 95% CI: 0.14 to 0.71; p = 0.004), based on 3 studies comprising 1272 university students"
