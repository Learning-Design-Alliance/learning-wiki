The Educational Attainment of Chicago Public Schools Students: 2018 2018¶

The Educational Attainment of Chicago Public Schools Students: 2018. (2018). UChicago Consortium on School Research / To&Through Project. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-educationa-attainment-of-chicago-public-schools-students-2018

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive district-data analysis by the UChicago Consortium and To&Through Project using the Bachelor's Degree Attainment Index (DAI), which "estimates how likely current CPS ninth-graders are to obtain a college degree within 10 years of beginning high school."

"The current analysis finds that the percent of CPS ninth-graders projected to earn a bachelor's degree within six years of high school graduation has nearly doubled since the 2006 rate of 11 percent to 20 percent in 2018."

Related Claims¶

  • Projected bachelor's degree attainment is far lower for Black and Latino young men (10 and 14 percent) than the CPS district average of 20 percent — related
  • The 2017 CPS Bachelor's Degree Attainment Index is estimated at 19 percent, up from 11 percent in 2006, with the Direct Bachelor's DAI at 17 percent — related
  • Bachelor's DAI varies sharply by race/ethnicity and gender: Black and Latino young men are projected at 10 and 13 percent versus a 19 percent district average — related
  • Four-year college graduation rates remained flat at about 49 percent for direct four-year enrollees, with much lower rates for two-year (8 percent) and delayed enrollees (4 percent) — related
  • CPS's Bachelor's Degree Attainment Index rose 7 percentage points, from 11 percent for 2006 ninth-graders to 18 percent for 2016 ninth-graders — related
  • The estimated percentage of CPS ninth-graders taking a direct path to a bachelor's degree nearly doubled between 2006 and 2016, from 9 percent to 16 percent — related
  • If current rates hold, only 20 percent of current CPS ninth-graders will complete a bachelor's degree within 10 years (2019 BDAI), with 18 percent via a direct pathway — related
  • College aspirations doubled between 1980 and 2002 while readiness and completion gaps by race, ethnicity, and income persisted or widened — related
