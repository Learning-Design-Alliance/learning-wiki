## Evidence

### Vosniadou 2024

Vosniadou, S., Bodner, E., Stephenson, H., Jeffries, D., Lawson, M. J., Darmawan, I. G. N., Kang, S., Graham, L., & Dignath, C. (2024). The promotion of self-regulated learning in the classroom: a theoretical framework and an observation study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09374-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Across the 1,126 coded direct promotion instances, the article reports "The most common SRL capability promoted was cognitive (56.1%)," with few resource management and just two affective instances; most promotions were domain general and direct verbal.

> "The most common SRL capability promoted was cognitive (56.1%), followed by meta- cognitive (22%) and motivational (14.2%). There were a few instances of resource man- agement promotion and just two instances of the promotion of affective capabilities."

### Vosniadou 2024 (2)

Vosniadou, S., Bodner, E., Stephenson, H., Jeffries, D., Lawson, M. J., Darmawan, I. G. N., Kang, S., Graham, L., & Dignath, C. (2024). The promotion of self-regulated learning in the classroom: a theoretical framework and an observation study. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-024-09374-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Benefit-of-use coding of direct strategy promotion instances showed most lacked any mention of benefit; explanations centered on subject content, and transfer mentions were very few.

> "In three-quarters of the instances of direct strategy promotion there was no mention of benefit of use. When teachers did provide explanations, they mostly focused on explaining the subject content being taught rather than the benefit of the strategy, and very few mentioned how the strategy being promoted could transfer to different situations."
