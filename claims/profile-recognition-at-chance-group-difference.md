## Evidence

### Vaccaro Jr. 2024

Vaccaro Jr., M., Friday, M., and Zaghi, A. (2024). Evaluating the capability of large language models to personalize science texts for diverse middle-school-age learners. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2408.05204](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2408.05204)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Profile evaluation phase: participants chose between their true profile and an opposite profile generated by GPT-4 from their training-session choices. The article reports 14 of 23 selected the true profile, "not significantly different (p = .405) from the expected value of the binomial distribution".

> "Of the 23 participants enrolled in this study, 14 selected their true profile in the profile evaluation phase of this study (Table 4)."

### Vaccaro Jr. 2024 (2)

Vaccaro Jr., M., Friday, M., and Zaghi, A. (2024). Evaluating the capability of large language models to personalize science texts for diverse middle-school-age learners. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2408.05204](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2408.05204)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of true-profile selection proportions between the experimental (9 of 12) and control (5 of 11) groups in the profile evaluation phase, tested with a proportion test reporting "Z = 1.450, one-sided p = .074, h = 0.615"; the article calls h = 0.615 a moderate effect size, so no standardized-magnitude coding is asserted here beyond that label.

> "we do find a statistically significant difference at the .10-level in the proportion of participants who correctly selected their true profile between the experimental and control groups (Z = 1.450, one-sided p = .074, h = 0.615)."
