# Phase 4 low-guidance procedure was truncated for children who already demonstrated unconfounded designs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Phase 4, the second testing opportunity for a variable was skipped when a child's first experimental design for that variable was unconfounded, to reduce redundancy and disengagement. [→ Matlen 2013](#matlen-2013)

## Evidence

### Matlen 2013

Matlen, B.J., Klahr, D. (2013). Sequential effects of high and low instructional guidance on children's acquisition of experimentation skills: Is it all in the timing?. Instructional Science, 41, 621–634. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-012-9248-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Procedural note from the study: in the low-guidance condition's Phase 4, redundancy was minimized by skipping a second test of a variable when the child's first design was unconfounded. The article notes all HL children and five LL children exhibited this pattern.

> "if a child’s first experimental design for a specific variable was unconfounded, then the second opportunity to test this variable was skipped—because the child had already mastered CVS."
