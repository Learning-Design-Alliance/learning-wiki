Evidence¶

Amechghal A 2026¶

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

q3 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review synthesis of Level 1 outcomes in 8 studies: state anxiety fell in both roles, learning flow showed no difference (P=0.291), perceptions showed "97% agreement; P=0.37", and most satisfaction studies found no between-role difference.

"State anxiety decreased significantly in both roles after SBE (P<0.01), with no statistically significant between-role differences at pretest (P=0.432) or posttest (P=0.204)."

Amechghal A 2026 (2)¶

Amechghal A, Naciri A, Takhdat K, Loubbairi S, Mellouki A, Lahlou L, Nassik H. (2026). The impact of the learner role in healthcare simulation-based education on learning outcomes: a systematic review. J Educ Eval Health Prof. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2026.23.24

q3 · i? · review · r3

Review reports, citing Rogers et al. (prospective cohort, pediatric residents, N=56), that active participants showed higher negative arousal (F(1,24)=33.8, P<0.001 uniprofessional; t(32)=4.96, P<0.001 multiprofessional) and positive arousal; no effect size printed.

"emotional arousal was the only Level 1 outcome favoring active participants over observers, with higher negative and positive arousal in both uniprofessional and multiprofessional contexts (all P<0.001) [25]."
