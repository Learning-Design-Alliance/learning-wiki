# Naturalistic studies show a triphasic retention curve including a flat permastore phase from about 6 to 25–30 years after learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Bahrick and colleagues' naturalistic studies of knowledge learned in formal education consistently found a triphasic curve: Ebbinghaus-like decline up to about six years, a flat 'permastore' phase from about 6 to 25–30 years, and further age-related loss thereafter. [→ Custers 2010](#custers-2010)

## Evidence

### Custers 2010

Eugène J. F. M. Custers. (2010). Long-term retention of basic science knowledge: a review study. Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-008-9101-y

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review attributes the triphasic curve and permastore concept to Bahrick's naturalistic studies, citing supporting studies by Conway et al. and Ellis et al. (about one-third retention of factual knowledge after 7–14 years). The review reports these second-hand; no effect size printed.

> "The second phase lasts from approximately 6 until approximately 25–30 years after learning; as the forgetting curve in this phase is flat, Bahrick and colleagues dubbed it ''permastore,'' because knowledge is apparently permanently retrievable in this phase."
