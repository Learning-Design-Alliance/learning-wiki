## Evidence

### Jackson L 2018

Jackson L, O'Connor A, Paneque M, Curtisova V, Lunt PW, Pourova RK, Macek M Jr, Stefansdottir V, Turchetti D, Campos M, Henneman L, Godino L, Skirton H, Cornel MC. (2018). The Gen-Equip Project: evaluation and impact of genetics e-learning resources for primary care in six European languages. Genetics in Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41436-018-0132-3

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Phase 1 pre/post analysis separating skills and knowledge question categories. The article prints "Average increase in skills and knowledge was 46.8% and 34.7%, respectively"; these are descriptive percentage changes, no effect size is printed.

> "The questions on the tests were split into two categories, those that were inquiring about clinical skills and those that related purely to knowledge. Average increase in skills and knowledge was 46.8% and 34.7%, respectively."

### Jackson L 2018 (2)

Jackson L, O'Connor A, Paneque M, Curtisova V, Lunt PW, Pourova RK, Macek M Jr, Stefansdottir V, Turchetti D, Campos M, Henneman L, Godino L, Skirton H, Cornel MC. (2018). The Gen-Equip Project: evaluation and impact of genetics e-learning resources for primary care in six European languages. Genetics in Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41436-018-0132-3

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Phase 1 skills-question analysis: pregnancy module 3 skills questions were answered extremely well at pretest (skills change 5.40%, p = 0.255 in Table 3), which the authors interpret as a knowledge deficit. No effect size printed.

> "When considering the skills-based questions, all modules generated a significant increase in the posttest scores apart from, rather unexpectedly, the pregnancy module 3. Despite the overall low pretest score in this module, the skills questions were answered extremely well, demonstrating that the issue with this topic is a severe knowledge deficit."
