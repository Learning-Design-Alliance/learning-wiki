# The counting and cardinality coefficient is significantly larger than the coefficients for patterning, geometry, and measurement and data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` F-tests comparing coefficient equality found counting and cardinality significantly larger than patterning (F = 25.92), geometry (F = 24.93), and measurement and data (F = 32.25), while patterning, geometry, and measurement and data did not differ from one another. [→ Nguyen 2016](#nguyen-2016)

## Evidence

### Nguyen 2016

Nguyen, T., Watts, T. W., Duncan, G. J., Clements, D. H., Sarama, J. S., Wolfe, C., & Spitler, M. E. (2016). Which Preschool Mathematics Competencies Are Most Predictive of Fifth Grade Achievement? Early Childhood Research Quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2016.02.003

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Post-regression F-tests in the same 781-child regression models compare the standardized coefficients across the four preschool competency domains; only the counting and cardinality coefficient stands out as significantly larger.

> "the coefficient for counting and cardinality was significantly larger than the coefficient produced by patterning (F = 25.92, p < 0.001), geometry (F=24.93, p < 0.001), and measurement and data (F = 32.25, p < 0.001). The coefficients produced by patterning, geometry, and measurement and data were not significantly different from one another."
