## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Bayes factors provided substantial support for gender similarities across most voxels; 0.8% of voxels showed substantial or strong evidence of gender differences, and no cortical regions supported greater neural maturity in boys. [→ Kersey 2019](#kersey-2019)

## Evidence

### Kersey 2019

Kersey, A. J., Csumitta, K. D., & Cantlon, J. F. (2019). Gender similarities in the brain during mathematics development. npj Science of Learning 4:19. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0057-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Whole-brain Bayes Factor analysis with a Cauchy prior width of 0.707, complementing the frequentist analyses of the same 104-child fMRI dataset. The article reports that "Less than 1% of voxels (0.8%)" supported gender differences and 23.6% of voxels supported neither hypothesis.

> "Less than 1% of voxels (0.8%) showed substantial or strong (B10 > 10) evidence of gender differences (Fig. 1b; 23.6% voxels did not substantially support either similarities or differences in the Bayesian analysis)."
