## Evidence

### Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley 2012

Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley. (2012). Building and Sustaining Talent: Creating Conditions in High-Poverty Schools That Support Effective Teaching and Learning. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543216

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of the U.S. Department of Education’s 2007-08 Schools and Staffing Survey, a nationally representative teacher survey, finds “teacher satisfaction is more influenced by the culture of the school than by the demographics of the students in the school.”

> "However, further analysis of data from that same national survey finds that teacher satisfaction is more influenced by the culture of the school than by the demographics of the students in the school."

### Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley 2012 (2)

Sarah Almy and Melissa Tooley. (2012). Building and Sustaining Talent: Creating Conditions in High-Poverty Schools That Support Effective Teaching and Learning. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED543216

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The report cites a large quantitative Massachusetts study in which “school working conditions explained away the relationship between teacher attrition and student demographic characteristics”; teachers in positive environments were more satisfied and their students made more progress.

> "a large quantitative study done in Massachusetts found that school working conditions explained away the relationship between teacher attrition and student demographic characteristics."
