# MCQ format is unsatisfactory for diagnosis because fixed options combine concepts and permit erroneous inferences

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` MCQ items can combine several independent concepts and lack exhaustive options, so a chosen option can support totally wrong inferences about a student's concepts. [→ Mittal 2011](#mittal-2011)

## Evidence

### Mittal 2011

Mittal, A. K. (2011). Developing a diagnostic e-module for testing conceptual understanding of Symmetry and Gauss's Law. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1106.0441](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1106.0441)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Theoretical argument in the design section, illustrated with a vectors MCQ combining electric field, electric flux and charge: a student holding an unlisted combination of beliefs may choose randomly, so "the options are also not exhaustive" and inference fails. This is an argued, not measured, result.

> "Quite often a question does not admit sufficiently purposive distracters. The requirement of three or more distracters usually leads to combining more than one concept."
