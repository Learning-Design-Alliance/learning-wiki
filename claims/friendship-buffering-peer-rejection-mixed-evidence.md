# Findings are mixed on whether friendship buffers the negative effects of peer rejection for children with ADHD

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some studies found having at least one good friend buffers the negative effects of peer rejection on maladjustment and victimization, but an ADHD-sample study found only peer rejection, not friendship, contributed to later depression. [→ Mikami 2015](#mikami-2015)

## Evidence

### Mikami 2015

Mikami, A. Y., & Normand, S. (2015). The Importance of Social Contextual Factors in Peer Relationships of Children with ADHD. Curr Dev Disord Rep. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40474-014-0036-0

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports conflicting findings: some studies of children with externalizing behavior or ADHD found friendship buffers rejection effects, while other ADHD-sample work found "only peer rejection, and not friendship, contributed to negative outcomes such as depression".

> "other work involving an ADHD sample found that only peer rejection, and not friendship, contributed to negative outcomes such as depression 6 and 8 years later"
