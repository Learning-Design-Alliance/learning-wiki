# A meta-analysis of FCI and FMCE data found no correlation of institutional SAT scores, pre-test scores, or institution type with normalized gains, though pre-test scores correlate with institution type.

## Evidence

### Madsen 2016

Madsen, McKagan, and Sayre. (2016). Best Practices for Administering Concept Inventories. American Association of Physics Teachers, College Park, MD. [doi:10.1119/1.5011826](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5011826)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Von Korff et al.'s meta-analysis of FCI and FMCE data from 72 papers, about 600 classes and about 45000 students, found no correlation of institutional average SAT or class pre-test score with class average normalized gains, implying prior preparation does not influence normalized gains.

> "They found that neither institutional average SAT scores nor class average pre-test score correlated with class average normalized gains."

### Madsen 2016 (2)

Madsen, McKagan, and Sayre. (2016). Best Practices for Administering Concept Inventories. American Association of Physics Teachers, College Park, MD. [doi:10.1119/1.5011826](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5011826)

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The same meta-analysis found institution type uncorrelated with normalized gains but correlated with pre-test scores, suggesting prior preparation differs by institution type without influencing gains, so normalized gains may be compared across institution types.

> "They also did not find a correlation between institution type (whether it is an Associates-, Bachelors-, Masters-, or Doctoral-granting) and normalized gains, but they did find a correlation between pre-test scores and institution type."
