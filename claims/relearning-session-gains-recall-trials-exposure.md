# First-pass recall accuracy rises while trials-to-criterion and exposure duration fall across the three weekly relearning sessions

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` First-pass recall accuracy in the relearn condition increased across the three weekly practice sessions, from M = .24 in Session 1 to M = .68 in Session 2. [→ Philip A. Higham 2020](#philip-a-higham-2020)
`q2 i3` Mean exposure duration per question decreased across sessions, from M = 37.05 s in Session 1 to M = 27.22 s in Session 2. [→ Philip A. Higham 2020 (2)](#philip-a-higham-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Philip A. Higham 2020

Philip A. Higham, Bettina Zengel, Laura Bartlett, and Julie A. Hadwin (2020). The Benefits of Successive Relearning on Multiple Learning Outcomes. https://osf.io/nx4va/?view_only=049ee04aab4f420e9fa0091b3b69b9d0

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

One-way repeated-measures ANOVA with session as the independent variable tested first-pass recall in the relearn condition across the three weekly practice sessions. Accuracy was "lowest in the first session" and "greatly increased in the second session (M = .68; SD = .16)".

> "recall accuracy was lowest in the first session (M = .24; SD = .14), greatly increased in the second session (M = .68; SD = .16), F(1, 211) = 1860.66, p < .001, ηp2 = .898"

### Philip A. Higham 2020 (2)

Philip A. Higham, Bettina Zengel, Laura Bartlett, and Julie A. Hadwin (2020). The Benefits of Successive Relearning on Multiple Learning Outcomes. https://osf.io/nx4va/?view_only=049ee04aab4f420e9fa0091b3b69b9d0

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on mean exposure duration in the relearn condition across sessions. Exposure "was highest in the first session (M = 37.05; SD = 4.43), greatly reduced in the second session (M = 27.22; SD = 4.70)".

> "exposure duration was highest in the first session (M = 37.05; SD = 4.43), greatly reduced in the second session (M = 27.22; SD = 4.70), F(1, 211) = 2003.33, p < .001, ηp2 = .905"
