## Evidence

### Tanja Bross 2024

Tanja Bross, Ulrike Elisabeth Nett, & Martin Daumiller. (2024). Interrelations Among Achievement Goals and Achievement Emotions: A Meta-Analytic Examination. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09931-9

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

The meta-analytic results confirm that work-avoidance goals are "particularly related to negative activity emotions," supporting the assumption that work avoidance carries unfavorable emotional correlates at the activity-emotion level.

> "The results for work-avoidance goals confirmed the assumption that engaging in work avoidance is particularly related to negative activity emotions."

### Tanja Bross 2024 (2)

Tanja Bross, Ulrike Elisabeth Nett, & Martin Daumiller. (2024). Interrelations Among Achievement Goals and Achievement Emotions: A Meta-Analytic Examination. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09931-9

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Primary studies reviewed in the article report work-avoidance goals correlating positively with anxiety, anger, shame and boredom and negatively with enjoyment, with ranges such as "boredom (r = [0.18; .40])" and "enjoyment (r = [-.41; -.16])".

> "these goals showed small to large strength positive associations with the negative emotions anxiety (r = [.19; .20], anger ( r = [.28; .31]), shame ( r = .12) and boredom (r = [0.18; .40]) and small to large negative associations with the positive emotion enjoyment (r = [-.41; -.16])"
