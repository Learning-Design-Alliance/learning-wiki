## Evidence

### Ryan 2017

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews (N=4) presented an incorrect R and T expression for a novel material. Students struggled to identify the independent variable and take limits; only one noticed the unphysical limits.

> "Three out of the 4 students interviewed were able to spontaneously point out that the T provided was not unitless (R1)."

### Ryan 2017 (2)

Ryan, Q. X., Wilcox, B. R., & Pollock, S. J. (2017). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.020126)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Exam question (N=30) asking students to describe situations with zero reflection or a 180-degree phase shift using given Fresnel equations. Solutions revealed difficulty recognizing physical limits.

> "Almost a quarter (20%, 6 out of 30) of the solutions wrote down unphysical or wrong limits such as: the index of refraction becomes negative, zero; velocity of the wave in the media is much larger than the speed of light, α has to be ﬁnite, α*β=1 etc."
