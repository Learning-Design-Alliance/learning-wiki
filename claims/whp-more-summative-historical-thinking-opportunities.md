# World History Project adoption increases learning opportunities for historical thinking in summative activities relative to business-as-usual curricula

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` WHP adopters assigned summative activities with higher historical thinking rubric scores than comparison teachers (Hedges' g = .66). [→ Iwatani 2023](#iwatani-2023)

## Evidence

### Iwatani 2023

Iwatani, E., Hardy, A., Means, B., Daal, S., & Wei, X. (2023). Evaluation of World History Project. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/199

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 119 WHP and 149 comparison curricular activity samples, triple-scored by blinded trained scorers on 4-point historical thinking rubrics. The report states the summative-overall difference favored "World History Project" with "Hedges' g = .66".

> "We found statistically significant impact estimates on learning opportunities for historical thinking skills, favoring the use of World History Project in: summative activities overall ( M WHP = 1.77, M Comp = 1.29, β = .56, SE = .24, p = .018, Hedges' g = .66)"
