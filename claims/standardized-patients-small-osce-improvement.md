# Training on standardized patients yields a small but statistically significant improvement in OSCE scores compared with traditional seminars

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Medical students randomized to standardized-patient training scored slightly higher on a 3-station OSCE (61.2 ± 3 vs 60.3 ± 3.5; P = 0.017, Cohen d: −0.279) than students in traditional seminars. [→ Herbstreit Frank 2017](#herbstreit-frank-2017)

## Evidence

### Herbstreit Frank 2017

Herbstreit Frank, Merse Stefanie, Schnell Rainer, Noack Marcel, Dirkmann Daniel, Besuch Anna, Peters Jürgen. (2017). Impact of standardized patients on the training of medical students to manage emergencies. Medicine 96(5):e5933. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000005933

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 242 fourth-year medical students allocated to standardized-patient training (n = 129) or traditional seminars (n = 113). The OSCE comparison showed a small printed effect size, Cohen d: −0.279, with P = 0.017 favoring the standardized-patient group on a 66-point maximum.

> "students taught with traditional seminars scored an average of 60.3 points ± 3.5 (±SD), whereas students of the standardized patient cohort scored 61.2 points ± 3 (t = −2.140 [Student t test; 1-tailed, unequal variances], d.f. = 221, difference = −0.913; P = 0.017, Cohen d: −0.279"
