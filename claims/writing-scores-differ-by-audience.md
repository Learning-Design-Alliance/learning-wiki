# Writing scores differed significantly by audience, with younger-student letters rated highest and teacher-directed letters lowest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Total writing scores differed significantly across audiences (d = 0.76), with all three audience groups differing significantly from each other; younger students > peers > teacher. [→ Kingir 2013](#kingir-2013)
`q3 i2` Conceptual-understanding items of the writing scale also differed by audience: younger-student and peer writers demonstrated significantly better understanding in writing than teacher-directed writers (d = 0.56). [→ Kingir 2013 (2)](#kingir-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Kingir 2013

Kingir, S. (2013). Using Non-traditional Writing as a Tool in Learning Chemistry. Eurasia Journal of Mathematics, Science & Technology Education, 9(2), 101-114. https://doi.org/10.12973/eurasia.2013.922a

`q3 · i2`

ANOVA on total writing scores (17-item scale) across the three audience groups; younger-student writers (M = 23.34) "significantly outperformed those writing to peers (M = 21.46, SD = 5.29) and the teacher (M = 18.73, SD = 5.16)".

> "The results showed that there was a significant mean difference in  total writing scores with respect to the audience, F(2, 335) = 23.99, p < 0.05, d = 0.76.  Bonferroni follow-up tests showed that all three groups differed significantly from each other."

### Kingir 2013 (2)

Kingir, S. (2013). Using Non-traditional Writing as a Tool in Learning Chemistry. Eurasia Journal of Mathematics, Science & Technology Education, 9(2), 101-114. https://doi.org/10.12973/eurasia.2013.922a

`q3 · i2`

ANOVA on the six conceptual-understanding items of the writing scale showed "significant mean differences in students’ conceptual understanding scores on writing with respect to the audience"; younger (M = 8.45) and peer (M = 7.83) writers beat teacher-directed writers (M = 7.07).

> "The results revealed significant mean differences in students’ conceptual understanding scores on writing with respect to the audience, F(2, 335) = 58.31, p < 0.001, d = 0.56."
