# Self-scoring improves primary students' monitoring accuracy on math problem-solving tasks to near-accurate levels

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The whole sample and the low- and high-performing subsets made more accurate monitoring judgments after self-scoring than before, on both tasks, becoming close to accurate. [→ Sophie Oudman 2021](#sophie-oudman-2021)

## Evidence

### Sophie Oudman 2021

Sophie Oudman, Janneke van de Pol, Tamara van Gog. (2021). Effects of self-scoring their math problem solutions on primary school students' monitoring and regulation. Metacognition and Learning (2022) 17:213–239. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-021-09281-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multilevel regression at the before/after level for the whole sample and both skill subsets; all self-scoring effects on monitoring were significant (e.g., multiplication whole sample B = −1.08, p < 0.001). The article states "Monitoring became close to accurate after self-scoring"; the increase averaged about one problem out of six.

> "the whole sample of students and the subsets of low- and high-performing students made on average more accurate monitoring judgments after self-scoring, compared to before self-scoring, both on the multiplication and division tasks."
