## Evidence

### Jones Gareth G. 2018

Jones Gareth G., Logishetty K., Clarke S., Collins R., Jaere M., Harris S., Cobb J. P. (2018). Do patient-specific instruments (PSI) for UKA allow non-expert surgeons to achieve the same saw cut accuracy as expert surgeons? Archives of Orthopaedic and Trauma Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00402-018-3031-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within-group comparison in the sawbone experiment: 34 trainees each performed the planned cut once with conventional instruments and once with PSI, with order counterbalanced. Table 2 reports trainee compound error falling from 11.6° (4.0°) to 5.5° (3.4°), p < 0.0005, with coronal p = 0.001, sagittal p < 0.0005, axial p = 0.002.

> "Compared to conventional instruments, PSI significantly reduced the mean error for trainees in all three planes (Table 2)."
