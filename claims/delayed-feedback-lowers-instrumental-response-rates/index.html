Evidence¶

Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay 2025¶

Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay. (2025). Delayed rewards weaken human goal directed actions. npj Science of Learning 10:36. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00325-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Laboratory experiment with N = 53 undergraduates performing the stockbroker task; Welch t-test on final (Block 2) response rates in the immediate versus delayed context found higher rates in the immediate condition, "t(52) = 9.25,p <0 . 0 0 1". Only the test statistic is printed; no effect size is given.

"For Experiment 1, participants demonstrated signiﬁcantly higher response rates in the immediate condition compared to the delayed condition, [t(52) = 9.25,p <0 . 0 0 1 ,9 5 %CI[1.65, 2.57]]"

Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay 2025 (2)¶

Omar David Perez & Gonzalo P. Urcelay. (2025). Delayed rewards weaken human goal directed actions. npj Science of Learning 10:36. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00325-2

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiments 2 (online, N = 39 undergraduates) and 3 (preregistered online, N = 198 Prolific participants) each showed higher response rates with immediate feedback: "t(38) = 3.41,p = 0.002" and "t(197) = 4.71,p < 0.001" respectively. No effect sizes are printed.

"Similarly, in Experiment 2, response rates were higher in the immediate condition compared to the delayed condition, [t(38) = 3.41,p = 0.002, 95%CI[2.64, 10.36]]. This pat-tern was also observed in Experiment 3, with signiﬁcantly higher response rates in the immediate condition [ t(197) = 4.71,p < 0.001, 95%CI[1.28, 3.13]]"
