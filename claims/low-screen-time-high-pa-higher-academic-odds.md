## Evidence

### Ishii Kaori 2020

Ishii Kaori, Aoyagi Kenryu, Shibata Ai, Javad Koohsari Mohammad, Carver Alison, Oka Koichiro. (2020). Joint Associations of Leisure Screen Time and Physical Activity with Academic Performance in a Sample of Japanese Children. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph17030757

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Same longitudinal logistic regression analysis of 261 Japanese children, adjusted for demographic characteristics. The low screen time/high physical activity group showed an odds ratio of OR = 2.75 (95% CI: 1.17–6.43, p < 0.05), i.e., "2.75 (95% CI: 1.17–6.43, p < 0.05) times greater odds" versus the reference group.

> "participants in the low screen time/high physical activity group had 2.75 (95% CI: 1.17–6.43, p < 0.05) times greater odds of having high academic performance compared with those in the high screen time/low physical activity group."
