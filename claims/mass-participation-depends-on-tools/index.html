Evidence¶

Baker 2024¶

Baker, M., Reimann, P. (2024). CSCL: a learning and collaboration science?. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-024-09433-4

q1 · i? · theoretical · r2

The editorial attributes this finding, quoting the paper's reported contrast, to Yang (2024)'s study of a mass-collaboration MOOC environment; the review reports it in summary, not from the primary data.

"It was found that the form of mass participation depended on the tools used — indirect crowd speech in the Danmaku environment versus direct communication and collaboration within threads of the discussion board."

Related Claims¶

  • Interaction in danmaku and discussion boards spanned A3C levels from attendance through cooperation, with collaboration rare and limited to knowledge discussions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Danmaku questions elicit rapid co-answering and co-confusion, while discussion-board questions often go unanswered or support multi-round elaboration — related
  • Danmaku hosts mass participation as a continuous collective flow while the discussion board divides learners into threaded small groups — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Danmaku interaction is predominantly socio-emotional while discussion-board interaction tilts cognitive, and the tools share only four content categories — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Danmaku primarily carries tacit knowledge sharing via personal experience while the discussion board carries explicit mathematical knowledge — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Danmaku learners engage in parasocial greetings and compliments to the instructor and seek co-learning presence, phenomena absent on the discussion board — related
  • All discussion-board contributors sent significantly more text per person than all danmaku contributors in every shared category — related
