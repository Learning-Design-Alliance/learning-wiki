## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors report that the OPAs between participant pathologists (85–95%) were comparable to published light-microscope values (SP263 96.7% OPA at ≥25%; 22C3 92.7% OPA at ≥50%), implying similar results when scoring glass slides or digital images. ## Evidence

### Jasani Bharat 2020

Jasani Bharat, Bänfer Gudrun, Fish Rebecca, Waelput Wim, Sucaet Yves, Barker Craig, Whiteley Jessica L., Walker Jill, Hovelinck Rudy, Diezko Rolf. (2020). Evaluation of an online training tool for scoring programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) diagnostic tests for lung cancer. Diagnostic Pathology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13000-020-00953-9

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' comparison in the Discussion between this training study's OPA range and OPA values reported in other publications; no head-to-head comparison of glass versus digital training was performed in this study.

> "The OPAs (85–95%) between different participant pathologists were comparable to those observed in other publications achieved via light microscope evaluation (SP263 96.7% OPA at the ≥25% cut-off and 22C3 92.7% OPA at the ≥50% cut-off) [6, 7]; these findings imply similar results when scoring stained tissue from either glass slides or via the digital images."
