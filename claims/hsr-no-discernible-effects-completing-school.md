# High School Redirection shows no discernible effects on completing school (diploma or GED receipt)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Stockton, 40% of intervention youth versus 32% of control youth had completed high school or earned a GED by the end of year three, a difference that was not statistically significant. [→ High School Redirection 2007](#high-school-redirection-2007)

## Evidence

### High School Redirection 2007

High School Redirection. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: High School Redirection. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc

`q3 · i?` · `review · r?`

Across the three randomized trials, no study found a statistically significant or substantively important effect on the combined diploma-or-GED outcome; the report states "No study found statistically significant or substantively important effects in this domain," supporting the no-discernible-effects rating.

> "The Stockton study found that, by the end of the third follow-up year, High School Redirection youth were more likely than control group youth to have completed high school or earned a GED certificate—40% versus 32%—a difference that was not statistically significant."
