## Evidence

### Tor Ole B. Odden and John Burk 2020

Tor Ole B. Odden and John Burk. (2020). Computational Essays in the Physics Classroom. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/1.5145471](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5145471)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Follow-up interviews with the 17 pilot students. One student favorably compared computational essays to standard computational assignments, saying the open format pushed them harder because there was no fixed endpoint. The article reports students "expressed appreciation for the creative freedom and non-linear nature of the project."

> "In follow-up interviews, students expressed appreciation for the creative freedom and non-linear nature of the project."

### Tor Ole B. Odden and John Burk 2020 (2)

Tor Ole B. Odden and John Burk. (2020). Computational Essays in the Physics Classroom. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1119/1.5145471](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.5145471)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The same follow-up interviews surfaced challenges: "it could be difficult to choose a topic and investigation question," difficulties with variables carried between code blocks, and a desire for more time. The authors plan to add time, notebook training, and suggested topics in future iterations.

> "However, students also reported that this openness was challenging in that it could be difficult to choose a topic and investigation question. Some students also had difficulties with the non-linear nature of notebooks, where stored variables could be carried over between different blocks of code, and many wished for more time to build out their projects."
