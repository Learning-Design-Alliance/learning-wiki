## Evidence

### Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026

Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Feedback That Clicks: Introductory Physics Students’ Valued Features in AI Feedback Generated From Self-Crafted and Engineered Prompts. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/v3pj-8491](https://doi.org/10.1103/v3pj-8491)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Emergent coding of students’ justifications for their preferred feedback type, coded binary by features in a code sheet; the analysis found "students most frequently valued features associated with the Evaluation theme (69.4%)".

> "Across 1160 responses, students most frequently valued features associated with the Evaluation theme (69.4%), followed by Content (55.2%), Presentation (47.8%), and Depth (44.2%)."

### Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello 2026 (2)

Amogh Sirnoorkar and N. Sanjay Rebello. (2026). Feedback That Clicks: Introductory Physics Students’ Valued Features in AI Feedback Generated From Self-Crafted and Engineered Prompts. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1103/v3pj-8491](https://doi.org/10.1103/v3pj-8491)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Feature-level coding of the same 1160 justifications; the article adds that "no single feature was consistently valued across all three feedback types."

> "Within Evaluation, Critique and Guidance were the most valued features. Within Content, Conceptual and Schema were rated most highly. For Presentation, Conciseness was emphasized most, while for Depth, the Detail feature was most frequently valued."
