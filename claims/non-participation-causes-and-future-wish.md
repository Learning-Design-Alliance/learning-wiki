## Evidence

### Abdelhai R 2012

Abdelhai R, Yassin S, Ahmad MF, Fors UGH. (2012). An e-learning reproductive health module to support improved student learning and interaction: a prospective interventional study at a medical school in Egypt. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-11

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Third-questionnaire survey of 106 students who did not use the e-learning course (multiple answers allowed): commuting from outside Cairo and extensive course material each accounted for 38.7% of responses, missed first deadline 32.1%, no internet access 21.7%, and only 12.3% considered e-learning a waste of time.

> "The main reasons claimed were mainly due to commuting daily from governorates outside Cairo and the extensive amount of required course material to study (38.7% of responses each - n = 41/106)."

### Abdelhai R 2012 (2)

Abdelhai R, Yassin S, Ahmad MF, Fors UGH. (2012). An e-learning reproductive health module to support improved student learning and interaction: a prospective interventional study at a medical school in Egypt. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-12-11

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same non-participation questionnaire, 65 of 106 students (nearly 62%) expressed a wish to participate in the e-learning module in the future; non-participants in focus groups also wished to access the course before year end via cyber cafés for exam revision.

> "However, nearly 62% (n = 65/106) expressed their wish to participate in the e-learning module in the future [Table 5]."
