## Evidence

### Leopold D.R. 2024

Leopold D.R., Kim H., Carlson K.W., Rowe M.A., Groff B.R., Major M.P., Willcutt E.G., Cutting L.E., Banich M.T. (2024). Stimulus shapes strategy: Effects of stimulus characteristics and individual differences in academic achievement on the neural mechanisms engaged during the N-back task. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101372

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational whole-brain fMRI analysis in 126 youth using Neurosynth executive-control masks, controlling for age and gender. The study reports that "greater math achievement was associated with increased activation within middle frontal regions" across all stimulus types. No effect size is printed for this association.

> "With regards to regions within the executive control mask, greater math achievement was associated with increased activation within middle frontal regions (center panel of Fig. 5) across all stimulus types (i.e., amodal; bottom row; S8)."

### Leopold D.R. 2024 (2)

Leopold D.R., Kim H., Carlson K.W., Rowe M.A., Groff B.R., Major M.P., Willcutt E.G., Cutting L.E., Banich M.T. (2024). Stimulus shapes strategy: Effects of stimulus characteristics and individual differences in academic achievement on the neural mechanisms engaged during the N-back task. Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101372

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' discussion of their correlational fMRI results states that "higher reading achievement was associated with greater activation of the left middle frontal gyrus" for letters and symbols. Results section reports the large lateral middle frontal cluster was only marginally significant amodally and nonsignificant for numbers. No effect size printed.

> "Rather higher reading achievement was associated with greater activation of the left middle frontal gyrus for the 2>0-back contrast for both letters and symbols."
