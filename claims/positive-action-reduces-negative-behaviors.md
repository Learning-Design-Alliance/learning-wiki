# Positive Action reduced negative behaviors, including substance use, violence, bullying and disruptive behavior, in both trials

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Substance use and extreme violence were reduced in PA schools compared with control schools in both trials (substance use ESs -1.05 in Hawai'i and -.35/-.27 in Chicago; violence ESs -1.39 and -.26/-.54). [→ Brian R. Flay 2014](#brian-r-flay-2014)
`q3 i2` Chicago students in PA schools also showed reduced bullying (ESs -.26 and -.39) and disruptive behaviors (ESs -.23 and -.50), and Hawai'i students reduced early onset of sexual behavior (ES=-1.10). [→ Brian R. Flay 2014 (2)](#brian-r-flay-2014-2)
`q3 i2` Archival school-level data from both trials showed reduced disciplinary suspensions (ESs -.87 and -.27). [→ Brian R. Flay 2014 (3)](#brian-r-flay-2014-3)

## Evidence

### Brian R. Flay 2014

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Student self-report outcomes first assessed at grade 5 in both matched-pair cluster-randomized trials. The article reports these negative behaviors were all reduced in PA schools compared with control schools in both trials.

> "For substance use, the effect sizes were -1.05 at grade 5 in Hawai'i and -.35 and -.27 at grades 5 and 8, respectively in Chicago. For extreme violence, the effect sizes were -1.39 at grade 5 in Hawai'i and -.26 and -.54 at grades 5 and 8"

### Brian R. Flay 2014 (2)

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Additional self-report behavioral outcomes in the two trials. The Chicago Table 1 lists bullying ES -0.59 at grade 5 and -0.39 at grade 8, and disruptive behaviors ES -0.47 at grade 5 (ns) and -0.50 at grade 8.

> "Hawai'i students also demonstrated reduced early onset of sexual behavior (ES=-1.10). Chicago students also demonstrated reduced bullying (ES=-.26 and -.39 at grades 5 and 8, respectively) and disruptive behaviors (ES=-.23 and -.50 at grades 5 and 8, respectively)"

### Brian R. Flay 2014 (3)

Brian R. Flay, D.Phil. (2014). Replication of effects of the Positive Action program in randomized trials in Hawai'i and Chicago schools. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT01025674

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

School-level archival disciplinary data collected from a year or more before to a year or more after each trial. The article reports suspension effect sizes of -.87 (Hawai'i) and -.27 (Chicago).

> "Archival data from both Hawai'i (Snyder et al., 2010) and Chicago (Lewis, Schure, et al., 2013) demonstrated program effects on disciplinary suspensions, with ESs of -.87 and -.27"
