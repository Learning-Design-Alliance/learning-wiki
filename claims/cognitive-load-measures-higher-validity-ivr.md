## Evidence

### Lee 2026

Lee, J. Y., Moon, J., Paas, F., Cho, M., Darejeh, A., Holmer, S., & Ayres, P. (2026). Validity of Eye-Tracking Measures in Immersive Virtual Reality: Using Cognitive Theories of Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10186-9

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Mann-Whitney U comparison in the review's Results (Table 5) of validity scores for 12 cognitive-load measures versus 79 other measures. The review reports "a small-to-moderate effect size (U = 674.00, p = .018, r = .25)" with median 3 (IQR 0.5) versus 2 (IQR 1.5).

> "Cognitive load measures demonstrated significantly higher overall validity, with a small-to-moderate effect size (U = 674.00, p = .018, r = .25), suggesting practical relevance for prioritizing cognitive load measures in IVR research."

### Lee 2026 (2)

Lee, J. Y., Moon, J., Paas, F., Cho, M., Darejeh, A., Holmer, S., & Ayres, P. (2026). Validity of Eye-Tracking Measures in Immersive Virtual Reality: Using Cognitive Theories of Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10186-9

`q2 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Component-wise Mann-Whitney comparisons in Results (Table 5), with effect sizes r = .31 and r = .21 as printed. The review notes these variables "typically included performance outcomes and learner expertise levels," reinforcing the association between cognitive load and key learning indicators in IVR.

> "Among the four validity components, relations with other variables (U = 676.00, p = .003, r = .31) and consequences ( U = 618.00, p = .050, r = .21) showed significantly higher scores, also with a small-to-moderate effect size."
