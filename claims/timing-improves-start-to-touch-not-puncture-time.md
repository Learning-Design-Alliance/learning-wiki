## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Start-to-touch time decreased significantly over the three days for both in-plane (p = 0.01) and out-of-plane (p < 0.01) techniques, while touch-to-puncture time showed no significant change (in-plane p = 0.14; out-of-plane p = 0.4). [→ Anschuetz Lukas 2025](#anschuetz-lukas-2025)

## Evidence

### Anschuetz Lukas 2025

Anschuetz Lukas, Tschopp Samuel, Trippel Mafalda, Huwendiek Sören, Trepp Roman, Caversaccio Marco, von Werdt Moritz, Borner Urs. (2025). A Novel Training Model to Improve Diagnostic Accuracy of Cervical Fine‐Needle Aspiration. Head & Neck. https://doi.org/10.1002/hed.28039

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Video-verified timing of each US-FNA trial for 15 residents over three training days; Table 2 reports in-plane start-to-touch falling from 3.8 (2.8) on day 1 to 1.9 (1.3) on day 3, p = 0.01, but in-plane touch-to-puncture p = 0.14 and total time p = 0.1. Only descriptive means and p-values are printed.

> "The timing of each US‐FNA trial was measured and verified using video recordings. The time from the start to the probe touching the model, the time from the probe touching the model and successful puncture, and the total time were analyzed for each day as detailed in Table 2."
