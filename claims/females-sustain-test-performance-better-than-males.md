## Evidence

### Balart 2019

Balart, P. & Oosterveen, M. (2019). Females show more sustained performance during test-taking than males. Nature Communications, 10, 3798. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11691-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Baseline analysis of PISA 2009 item-level data using LOWESS visualization for Ireland; the article reports that in Ireland, "females were better able to sustain their performance throughout the test than male". Cross-country OLS estimates show significance in 56 of 74 countries.

> "The key take-away of Fig. 1, in the context of our research, is that performance decline is weaker for female students. That is, the figure shows that in Ireland, females were better able to sustain their performance throughout the test than male."

### Balart 2019 (2)

Balart, P. & Oosterveen, M. (2019). Females show more sustained performance during test-taking than males. Nature Communications, 10, 3798. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11691-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Country-level OLS estimates by topic (Fig. 3) show female students better at sustaining performance in math and science, with point estimates positive in 68 countries and statistically significant at the 5% level in 41; the article reports "there was not a single country" where males significantly out-sustained females.

> "Despite male students having an initial advantage in the math and science domains, there was not a single country in which they were significantly better able to sustain their performance during the test."
