## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of matched responses classified into four response patterns (Fig. 3): incorrect-to-correct, correct-to-correct, incorrect-to-incorrect, and correct-to-incorrect. On average "82.4% of students gave con-sistent responses," with both tests post-instruction and no intervening instruction.

> "Averaged across all items, 82.4% of students gave con-sistent responses to the item in PHYS I and II by re-sponding correct for both or incorrect for both."

### Bethany R. Wilcox 2020 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox, Steven J. Pollock, and Daniel R. Bolton. (2020). Retention of conceptual learning after an interactive introductory physics course. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.010140)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Per-item consistency across the FMCE showed relatively little variation: the most consistent item was Q15 (94.7%), then Q33 (92.4%), and the least was Q17 (72.5%), a difficult item about the force on an object moving at constant velocity.

> "the question with the highest consistency of responses was Q15 (94.7% consistent), followed by Q33 (92.4% consistent), while the question with the lowest consistency was Q17 (72.5% consistent)."
