## Evidence

### W. Brian Lane 2025

W. Brian Lane, Charlotte Dries, Gabriella Khazal, and Tiffany Snow. (2025). Network analysis of students’ drawn representations of an introductory lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Racial subnetwork comparison (Asian N = 14, Hispanic N = 14, White N = 44) highlighting elements with at least two large effect sizes (d ≥ 0.8 per the article’s threshold) and the statistically highest betweenness; Table III reports all p values < .001.

> "We find that Asian students tended to depict “Furniture,” “Positive Feelings,” and generic “Students” most centrally among these subnetworks. Hispanic students tended to depict “Learning,” “Lab Equipment,” and “Questions” most centrally among these subnetworks."

### W. Brian Lane 2025 (2)

W. Brian Lane, Charlotte Dries, Gabriella Khazal, and Tiffany Snow. (2025). Network analysis of students’ drawn representations of an introductory lab. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.02681)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Same racial subnetwork comparison, examining elements with at least two large effect sizes (d ≥ 0.8 per the article’s threshold) and the statistically lowest betweenness value among the Asian, Hispanic, and White subnetworks.

> "Asian students depicted “Group Members” and “Self” the least centrally among these subnetworks, Hispanic students depicted “Furniture,” “Computer,” and “Instructor” the least centrally, and White students depicted “Graphs” the least centrally."
