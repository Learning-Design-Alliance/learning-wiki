# First-year students' engagement in deep learning activities is positively related to their self-reported gains in general education, practical competence, and personal and social development

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students' DEEP Learning scale scores were significantly and positively related to self-reported gains in general education, practical competence, and personal and social development, net of controls. [→ Reason 2010](#reason-2010)

## Evidence

### Reason 2010

Reason, R. D., Cox, B. E., McIntosh, K., & Terenzini, P. T. (2010). Deep learning as an individual, conditional, and contextual influence on first-year student outcomes. Paper presented at the Annual Forum of the Association for Institutional Research, Chicago, IL. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED520486

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel (HLM) analysis of 5,905 first-year students at 33 institutions; level-1 DEEP coefficients were positive and significant (p < .01) for all three self-reported gain scales in Tables 3, "positively related to students’ self-reported gains" even with demographic and ACT controls.

> "Like Nelson Laird and colleagues (2008), we found the DEEP Learning scales to be positively related to students’ self-reported gains during the first year of college"
