## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Across-cohort observational statement by the authors comparing the 2008 (n=25), 2009 (n=13) and 2010 (n=20) experimental classes on the infinite square well quiz, attributing the trend to refinement of the concept tests from instructor and student feedback. No effect size is printed.

> "We have also observed continuous improvement in the experimental groups’ performance from 2008 to 2010 as we refined the concept tests based upon the feedback obtained."

### Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Guangtian Zhu. (2016). Improving Students’ Understanding of Quantum Mechanics by Using Peer Instruction Tools. 2010 PERC Proceedings. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05461)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Score-distribution data shown in Figure 3 for the four groups; the accompanying text states higher percentages of students obtained scores of "9 or 10 points" in later cohorts. A related text sentence notes 30% of the 2010 experimental group obtained the full score of 10 points.

> "Figure 3 shows that as we refined the concept tests from 2008 to 2010, higher percentages of students obtained high scores (9 or 10 points)."
