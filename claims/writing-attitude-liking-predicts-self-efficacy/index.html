Evidence¶

Kim M. Mitchell 2023¶

Kim M. Mitchell, Sharon Zumbrunn, Danielle N. Berry, Lisa Demczuk. (2023). Writing Self-Efficacy in Postsecondary Students: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09798-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's synthesis of the four studies (8%) measuring writing attitude, operationalized variously as liking of writing, attitudes about leisure writing, or affect toward writing, reports a consistent ordered pattern across liking, ambivalence, and disliking.

"Participants who liked writing or had higher positive writing affects reported the highest writing self-efficacy in all studies. Self-efficacy levels were the lowest in students who reported disliking writing, and medium in students who expressed ambivalence about writing"
