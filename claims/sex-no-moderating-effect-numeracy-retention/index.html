Evidence¶

Gbenga-Akanmu & Jegede 2019¶

Gbenga-Akanmu, T. O., & Jegede, P. O. (2019). Influence of Meta-cognitive Training and Cognitive Styles on Numeracy Achievement of Nigerian Pupils’ in Computer-mediated Classrooms. World Journal of Education, 9(1). https://doi.org/10.5430/wje.v9n1p221

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Retention test administered two weeks after post-test to both groups in the quasi-experiment; the article reports no significant moderating effect of sex on retention. Table 3 prints Sex F = 0.22, p = 0.6484; no effect size is printed for sex. The table has sex only as a covariate main effect, with no sex-by-training interaction term, so moderation was not directly tested, and a non-significant result is not evidence of no difference. (The text's "Sex ... (F = 2.16; p>0.05)" is the table's Group row value.)

"The result of ANCOVA showed that there was no moderating effect of sex on retention of numeracy performance of the two catego ries of learners, thus, the hypothesis was not rejected."
