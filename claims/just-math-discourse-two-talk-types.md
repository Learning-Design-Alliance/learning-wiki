# Just Math discourse falls into two categories: brief narrow-and-serious math check-ins, and expansive-and-silly off-topic comments

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` During Just Math, student talk takes one of two forms: brief narrow and serious math-focused check-ins, or expansive and silly off-topic comments made while working. [→ Steven F. Wolf 2014](#steven-f-wolf-2014)

## Evidence

### Steven F. Wolf 2014

Steven F. Wolf, Leanne Doughty, Paul W. Irving, Eleanor C. Sayre and Marcos D. Caballero. (2014). Just Math: A New Epistemic Frame. [doi:10.1119/perc.2014.pr.065](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2014.pr.065)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Discourse analysis of candidate episodes from the homework help session video. The authors also note that "the rate of talk decreases when students are engaged in Just Math." The off-topic talk type was not reported in Scherr and Hammer's Worksheet e-frame.

> "Episodes where student talk included narrow and serious math-focused check-ins (see Episode 1) and/or expansive and silly comments (see Episode 2) were identiﬁed as Just Math."
