Evidence¶

Wechsler 2024¶

Wechsler, M. E., Patrick, S. K., Thompson, C., & Levin, S. (2024). On the path to leadership: California's administrator induction programs. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/636.224

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Qualitative case studies of six highly rated induction programs, drawing on interviews, focus groups, observations, and documents, report participants' improved reflection and leadership skills. These are self-reported perceptions, not measured outcomes.

"One, newly credentialed administrators' leadership knowledge and skills improved because of their work with their coaches or through other professional development activities. A commonly cited improvement was in administrators' ability to reflect on their decisions in a purposeful, critical, and nuanced way."

Wechsler 2024 (2)¶

Wechsler, M. E., Patrick, S. K., Thompson, C., & Levin, S. (2024). On the path to leadership: California's administrator induction programs. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/636.224

q2 · i? · qualitative · r?

Case study focus groups and interviews report that induction experiences gave participants tools, language, and confidence to apply for principal positions and a framework for systematic leadership thinking. Qualitative self-report data.

"Three, participants reported that induction advanced their professional growth and their careers through networking and support. For example, their experiences in induction gave them the tools, language, and confidence to apply for principal positions and a framework to think about their leadership in a more systematic way so they could articulate their vision and goals."
