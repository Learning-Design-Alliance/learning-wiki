# Course participants scored higher on social awareness than controls at the end of the course, though the pre-post interaction was not significant

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Participants and controls did not differ at the start, but participants scored higher on social awareness at the end of the course (F(1,33) = 5.46, p = 0.026, η²p = 0.14). [→ Schutte et al. 2018](#schutte-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Schutte et al. 2018

Ingrid Schutte, Elanor Kamans, Marca Wolfensberger and Wiel Veugelers. (2018). Effects of an International Undergraduate Honors Course on Awareness of Global Justice. Educ. Sci. 2018, 8, 82. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci8020082

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pre-posttest with control group using the Social Awareness Scale from the Shared Futures Survey (SA: N participants = 19, N control = 16). A simple effect showed "a trend of an increase among participants F(1,35) = 2.77, p = 0.10, N2p = 0.08". The printed endpoint effect size is η²p = 0.14 (printed as N2p = 0.14), a large partial eta-squared.

> "Further, although participants did not differ from the control group at the beginning of the course, F(1,33) = 1.90, p = 0.18, N2p = 0.06, they scored higher on social awareness at the end of the course, F(1,33) = 5.46, p = 0.026, N2p = 0.14."

### Schutte et al. 2018 (2)

Ingrid Schutte, Elanor Kamans, Marca Wolfensberger and Wiel Veugelers. (2018). Effects of an International Undergraduate Honors Course on Awareness of Global Justice. Educ. Sci. 2018, 8, 82. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci8020082

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The same factorial repeated-measures analysis of the SFS social awareness subscale found no significant group × pre-post interaction; the increase trend within participants was untested against controls.

> "However, no signiﬁcant difference in the increase of social awareness between the participants and control group was found. The analysis did not show the anticipated interaction F < 1."
