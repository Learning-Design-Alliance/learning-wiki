## Evidence

### Sinha 2014

Sinha, T., Jermann, P., Li, N., & Dillenbourg, P. (2014). Your click decides your fate: Inferring Information Processing and Attrition Behavior from MOOC Video Clickstream Interactions. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same survival models on summarized behavioral clickstream action vectors and IPI: students whose rewatching behavior changed from low to high were "33% less likely to dropout (Hazard ratio: 0.6734, p <0.001)".

> "If students' rewatching behavior changes from low to high, they are 33% less likely to dropout (Hazard ratio: 0.6734, p <0.001)"

### Sinha 2014 (2)

Sinha, T., Jermann, P., Li, N., & Dillenbourg, P. (2014). Your click decides your fate: Inferring Information Processing and Attrition Behavior from MOOC Video Clickstream Interactions. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same survival models: as students watched a greater proportion of the video lecture they were "37% less likely to dropout of the MOOC (Hazard ratio: 0.6334, p <0.001)", which the article reads as indicative of continued interest.

> "As students start watching more proportion of the video lecture, they are 37% less likely to dropout of the MOOC (Hazard ratio: 0.6334, p <0.001)."
