# Teacher characteristics from the will-skill-tool model — data literacy, availability of data technologies, and positive beliefs — predict teachers' pedagogical use of digital data, with data literacy the strongest predictor

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Teacher data literacy (skill), availability of data technologies (tool), and positive beliefs about technologies (will) each significantly and positively predict digital data use, with skill the strongest predictor. [→ Michos 2025](#michos-2025)
`q3 i?` The full multilevel model explained 52% of the variance in teachers' digital data use, and 11% of variance was attributable to between-school differences. [→ Michos 2025](#michos-2025)

## Evidence

### Michos 2025

Michos, K., Schmitz, M.-L., & Petko, D. (2025). Learning Analytics in Schools: Is Digital Data Use Influenced by Teacher-Level or School-Level Factors? Journal of Learning Analytics, 12(3), 87–101. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2025.8567

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multilevel regression (teachers nested in 112 schools) on survey data from N = 2,247 Swiss upper secondary teachers. "Model 1" with all predictors "explained 52% of the variance in the use of digital data by teachers"; the null model showed an ICC of 0.11 with significant between-school variance. All three WST coefficients were significant at p < .001.

> "The variables that mostly influence the use of digital data by teachers were all related to the WST model, namely positive beliefs toward digital technologies (will; B = 0.13, p < 0.001), teacher competency with digital data (skill ; B = 0.55, p < 0.001), and availability of data technologies (tool ; B = 0.40, p < 0.001)"
