`q2 i?` ANCOVA showed significant treatment effects for inconsistent-language problems (WPG2) and multi-operation problems (WPG3) among fifth graders. [→ Vula et al. 2017](#vula-et-al-2017)

## Evidence

### Vula et al. 2017

Eda Vula, Rrezarta Avdyli, Valbona Berisha, Blerim Saqipi, Shpetim Elezi. (2017). The impact of metacognitive strategies and self-regulating processes of solving math word problems. International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education. https://doi.org/10.26822/iejee.2017131886

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA summary (Table 5) of fifth-grade post-test scores by problem group: Treatment F values were 20.548*** for total score, 4.539* for WPG1 (one operation), 12.23** for WPG2 (inconsistent language), and 6.28* for WPG3 (two or more operations).

> "From the data given in table 5, it is observed that learners are more likely to benefit from metacognitive instruction and self -regulating strategies in solving more complex word problems than in simple problems."

### Vula et al. 2017 (2)

Eda Vula, Rrezarta Avdyli, Valbona Berisha, Blerim Saqipi, Shpetim Elezi. (2017). The impact of metacognitive strategies and self-regulating processes of solving math word problems. International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education. https://doi.org/10.26822/iejee.2017131886

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' discussion of the intervention results: learners of both grades proved more successful after the intervention on problems whose linguistic information was not consistent with the arithmetical operations.

> "The findings that derive from this study confirm that the learners of both group, of the two grades, grade three and five, proved to be more successful after the intervention in solving problems with inconsistent language, that is the problems in which linguistic information was not consistent with the arithmetica l operations."
