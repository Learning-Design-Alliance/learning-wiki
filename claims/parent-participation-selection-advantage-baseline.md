### McCormick 2016

McCormick, M. P., Cappella, E., O'Connor, E. E., Hill, J., & McClowry, S. G. (2016). Do Effects of Social-Emotional Learning Programs Vary by Level of Parent Participation? Evidence from a Randomized Trial. SREE Spring 2016 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567204

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis with independent samples t-tests comparing the high and low parent dosage groups at baseline (Table 1; N = 84 high dosage, N = 131 low dosage) found high dosage parents "older, more likely to be married, more educated, more likely to be working full-time."

> "There were differences between the high and low dosage parent participation groups at baseline (see Table 1). Specifically, high dosage parents were older, more likely to be married, more educated, more likely to be working full-time, and more likely to have children with high baseline skills."
