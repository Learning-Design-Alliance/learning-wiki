## Evidence

### Jennifer Richards 2014

Jennifer Richards, Andrew Elby, and Ayush Gupta. (2014). Incorporating Disciplinary Practices Into Characterizations of Progress in Responsive Teaching. Manuscript in the review process. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Qualitative case-study analysis of the March 2011 episode of the same key-drop lesson. The authors describe Mr. S attending to "their causal stories of how and why their predictions would occur", e.g., repeatedly revoicing that gravity pulls things down.

> "he primarily attended and responded to a different form of scientific knowledge with respect to students’ ideas – their causal stories of how and why their predictions would occur."

### Jennifer Richards 2014 (2)

Jennifer Richards, Andrew Elby, and Ayush Gupta. (2014). Incorporating Disciplinary Practices Into Characterizations of Progress in Responsive Teaching. Manuscript in the review process. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1502.04420)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Transcript-based analysis of the Chavez exchange in the March 2011 episode: Mr. S asked "Why will the keys go fast too?" and offered the counterpoint that the keys might "just be there", pressing beyond factor identification toward a causal story.

> "This question, followed by Mr. S’s counterpoint that the keys might “just be there” once they’re released (line 39), indicates that Mr. S was interested in more than the identification of speed as a causal factor."
