## Evidence

### Varano 2023

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative interviews with an independent sample of 20 exhibition visitors (10 sighted, 10 BVI) and 2 BVI professional astronomers at "Inspiring Stars", Rome, January 2022. All reported no familiar features and "the intelligibility of the code after its declaration".

> "All users referred not to have found any familiar feature in the representation and reported the intelligibility of the code after its declaration, suggesting that the representation was not misleading"

### Varano 2023 (2)

Varano, S., and Zanella, A. (2023). Design and evaluation of a multi-sensory representation of scientific data. Frontiers. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1082249)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Same final-evaluation interview study: all 22 users "could not find any “analogy with experience”", which the authors read as evidence that the iterative design removed features triggering misconceptions about the represented physics.

> "All the 22 interviewed users of the final exhibit reported that they could not find any “analogy with experience”, meaning they did not recognize any of the represented features as familiar."
