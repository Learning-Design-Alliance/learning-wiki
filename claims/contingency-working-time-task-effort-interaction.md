# Low contingent support increased task effort when independent working time was short; with long independent working time task effort decreased under both contingencies but less so with high contingency

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The three-way interaction of occasion, contingency, and independent working time on students' task effort was significant (small effect size). [→ van de Pol 2015](#van-de-pol-2015)
`q3 i?` With short independent working time, low contingency increased task effort; with long independent working time, high contingency showed a smaller decrease in task effort than low contingency. [→ van de Pol 2015 (2)](#van-de-pol-2015-2)

## Evidence

### van de Pol 2015

van de Pol, J., Volman, M., Oort, F., & Beishuizen, J. (2015). The effects of scaffolding in the classroom: support contingency and student independent working time in relation to student achievement, task effort and appreciation of support. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9351-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel analysis of task effort questionnaire scores (Cronbach's a = .92) in the experimental classroom study; the article reports the three-way interaction "was signiﬁcant (small effect size of R 2 = .04)". Task effort overall decreased from premeasurement to postmeasurement.

> "the three-way interaction of occasion, contingency, and independent working time on students’ task effort was signiﬁcant (small effect size of R 2 = .04; Cohen 1992)"

### van de Pol 2015 (2)

van de Pol, J., Volman, M., Oort, F., & Beishuizen, J. (2015). The effects of scaffolding in the classroom: support contingency and student independent working time in relation to student achievement, task effort and appreciation of support. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-015-9351-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Printed direction of the interaction in Results: "When the independent working time was short, low levels of contingency resulted in an increase in task effort"; with long working time both levels decreased effort, less with high contingency.

> "When the independent working time was short, low levels of contingency resulted in an increase in task effort. When the independent working time was long, both high levels and low levels of contingency resulted in a decrease of task effort."
