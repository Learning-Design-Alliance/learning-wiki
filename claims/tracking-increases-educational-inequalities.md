The article synthesizes prior empirical research on school tracking, citing meta-analytic and comparative studies. It states that "such tracking practices increase educational inequalities" because lower tracks provide fewer learning opportunities and disadvantaged students are disproportionately placed there.

> "robust empirical evidence now suggests that such tracking practices increase educational inequalities as less-demanding tracks and courses often provide less opportunities to learn, with disadvantaged students more often assigned to these lower-level tracks"
