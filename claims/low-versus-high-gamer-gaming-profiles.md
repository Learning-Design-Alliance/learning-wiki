## Evidence

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of the proportion of gamed turn-pairs for low and high gamers (median split of 286 Andes students), considering only the five gaming opportunities. The article reports guessing as the only gaming turn pair low gamers abuse more than high gamers (Table 3 and Fig. 8).

> "low gamers were significantly more likely than high gamers to game by guessing (46% vs. 13.2%; F(1, 283) = 126, p < .01)"

### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010 (2)

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same proportion-of-gaming analysis comparing high and low gamers. The article also reports high gamers significantly higher on lack of planning (18.5% vs. 8.9%) and bottom-out hint copying (6.5% vs. 1.7%), all with F(1, 283) statistics at p < .01.

> "high gamers had a significantly higher proportion of skipped high-level hints (61.6% vs. 43.4%; F(1, 283) = 64, p < .01)"
