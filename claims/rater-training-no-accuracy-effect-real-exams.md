# Frame-of-reference rater training has no measurable effect on individual rating accuracy in a real-life standardized physical examination assessment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomised controlled trial of real-life curricular assessments, trained raters were not more accurate than untrained raters in matching the observers' median grade. [→ Weitz G 2014](#weitz-g-2014)

## Evidence

### Weitz G 2014

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Generalised estimating equations analysis of 242 third-year students' real-live assessments by 21 randomised raters, with accuracy defined as concordance with the median of three video-based observers. The study found no effect on accuracy: "No effect of the training on rating accuracy was detectable (β=-0.09 ±0.20, P=0.64)."

> "In the generalised estimating equations model, the trained raters were more stringent than those without the training (β=-0.94 ±0.36, P=0.01). No effect of the training on rating accuracy was detectable (β=-0.09 ±0.20, P=0.64)."

### Weitz G 2014 (2)

Weitz G, Vinzentius C, Twesten C, Lehnert H, Bonnemeier H, König IR. (2014). Effects of a rater training on rating accuracy in a physical examination skills assessment. GMS Zeitschrift für Medizinische Ausbildung. [doi:10.3205/zma000933](https://doi.org/10.3205/zma000933)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article's summary of the randomised trial outcome: "Rater training did not have a measurable influence on accuracy", while stringency shifted significantly toward the observers' grading.

> "Rater training did not have a measurable influence on accuracy. However, trained raters rated significantly more stringently than untrained raters, and their overall stringency was closer to the stringency of the observers."
