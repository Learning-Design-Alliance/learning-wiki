## Evidence

### W. H. Winch 1910

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlation computed from individual cases in the School I experiment, where problem tests and rule-sum tests were worked side by side under the same school conditions; the Pearson formula showed "the positive correla- tion to amount to +.69."

> "It is fairly obvious that positive correlation exists, but it is also obvious that no very definite value can be obtained from the grouped averages. An application of the Pearson formula to the individual ca.ses shows the positive correla- tion to amount to +.69."

### W. H. Winch 1910 (2)

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlation in School O.R., computed only for the practised half of the class from four reasoning tests and ten numerical exercises, which the author notes is theoretically less satisfactory because conditions differed.

> "The correlation is obviously a high one, and its exact de- termination from the individual cases by means of the Pear- son formula of correlation gives +.85."

### W. H. Winch 1910 (3)

W. H. Winch. (1910). Accuracy in school children. Does improvement in numerical accuracy 'transfer'? The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Winch+accuracy+school+children

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlation in School B, based on side-by-side tests under the same school conditions; the grouped table showed a considerable positive relation before individual computation.

> "it was thought advisable to work it out from the 43 individual cases by the Pearson product—moment formula. It reaches the high figure of + .79."
