# CSCL scripts have a small significant positive effect on domain learning compared to unstructured collaborative learning

## Subclaims

`q3 i1` Learning with CSCL scripts yields a small significant positive summary effect on domain learning (Hedges' g = 0.24). [→ Radkowitsch 2020](#radkowitsch-2020)
`q3 i1` The positive effect holds for both recall tests (g = 0.27) and application tests (g = 0.21), but not for mixed tests (g = 0.09, non-significant). [→ Radkowitsch 2020 (2)](#radkowitsch-2020-2)

`q3 i1` Collaborative learning with a CSCL script produces a statistically significant small positive average effect on domain-specific knowledge relative to collaborative learning without a script. [→ Freydis Vogel 2016](#freydis-vogel-2016)

## Evidence

### Radkowitsch 2020

Radkowitsch, A., Vogel, F., & Fischer, F. (2020). Good for learning, bad for motivation? A meta-analysis on the effects of computer-supported collaboration scripts. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 15, 5–47. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-020-09316-4

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 124 domain-learning effects (54 independent) from experimental comparisons of CSCL-scripted versus unguided collaborative learning; "a small significant positive summary effect, with Hedges' g of 0.24". Heterogeneity was significant (Q = 101.77, df = 53, p < .001) but low by the authors' own criteria (I2 = 46.23% per Table 2; 25–49% counts as low).

> "In total, we examined 124 effects of CSCL scripts on domain learning, of which 54 were independent. Overall, the meta-analysis resulted in a small significant positive summary effect, with Hedges' g of 0.24."

### Radkowitsch 2020 (2)

Radkowitsch, A., Vogel, F., & Fischer, F. (2020). Good for learning, bad for motivation? A meta-analysis on the effects of computer-supported collaboration scripts. International Journal of Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning, 15, 5–47. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-020-09316-4

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis distinguishing domain-learning measure types: significant effects for "recall tests (Hedges' g = 0.27)" and "application tests (Hedges'g = 0.21)", with mixed tests non-significant at g = 0.09.

> "CSCL scripts showed small significant positive effects on both recall tests (Hedges' g = 0.27) and application tests (Hedges'g = 0.21). Studies reporting mixed tests for domain learning yielded a positive but non-significant summary effect of Hedges' g = 0.09"

### Freydis Vogel 2016

Freydis Vogel, Christof Wecker, Ingo Kollar, Frank Fischer. (2016). Socio-Cognitive Scaffolding with Computer-Supported Collaboration Scripts: a Meta-Analysis. Educ Psychol Rev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-016-9361-7

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 33 effect sizes from 21 articles comparing CSCL-script conditions to unstructured CSCL. The synthesis found a statistically significant positive, although small, average effect; Table 1 reports d = 0.20 with a statistically significant test (z = 2.08, p = .019).

> "As hypothesized, a statistically significant positive, although small, average effect was found (see Table 1). Post hoc power analysis revealed that the statistical power of this test was 87 %."
