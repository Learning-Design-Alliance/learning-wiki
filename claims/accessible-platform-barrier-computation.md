## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Choosing a teaching platform is difficult because there is no universal physics coding language, platforms have differing pros and cons, and departmental opinions diverge on what to use. [→ Ashleigh Leary 2019](#ashleigh-leary-2019)

## Evidence

### Ashleigh Leary 2019

Ashleigh Leary, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2019). The difficulties associated with integrating computation into undergraduate physics. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview findings on platform choice: one interviewee described departmental disagreement between MATLAB users and astronomers moving toward Python, with some suggesting keeping computation a small part of the course.

> "Choosing a platform is a difficult task due to the fact that there is not a universal physics coding language that every professor uses regularly. There are pros and cons to each platform that have to be looked at, and each person has their own opinions on what the best thing to learn is."

### Ashleigh Leary 2019 (2)

Ashleigh Leary, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2019). The difficulties associated with integrating computation into undergraduate physics. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview evidence on affordability and access: a teacher needed "a class set of laptops" with VPython installed, and funding took two years to obtain, illustrating the access constraint the article says is more common among high school and community college instructors.

> "So when I first started...I wanted to have ac- cess to VPython for all the students. And what I needed was a class set of laptops. I needed VPython installed on all of them, and it took ... It actually took two years to get the funding. To get the laptops."
