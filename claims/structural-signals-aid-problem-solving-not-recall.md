# Structural signals show no consistent recall benefits but can aid deeper understanding measured by problem-solving tests

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Structural signals (previews, headings, logical connectives) produced significant effects on problem-solving tests but no significant differences on recall and recognition tests. [→ Mary H. Sawyer 1991](#mary-h-sawyer-1991)
`q2 i?` Signal effects on comprehension are passage dependent rather than predictable across texts. [→ Mary H. Sawyer 1991 (2)](#mary-h-sawyer-1991-2)

## Evidence

### Mary H. Sawyer 1991

Mary H. Sawyer. (1991). A Review of Research in Revising Instructional Text. Journal of Reading Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1080/10862969109547744

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Loman and Mayer's (1983) study of 10th-grade good and poor comprehenders, that signaled passages aided problem-solving performance while recall and recognition tests showed no significant differences.

> "they found significant effects for signaled passages (combining previews, headings, and logical connectives) with both groups in a problem-solving test. Recall and recognition tests found no significant differences between the signaled and unsignaled passages"

### Mary H. Sawyer 1991 (2)

Mary H. Sawyer. (1991). A Review of Research in Revising Instructional Text. Journal of Reading Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1080/10862969109547744

`q2 · i?`

The review reports, citing Spyridakis and Standal (1987), that across eight versions of four technical passages, signals neither inhibited nor predictably increased comprehension; previews and logical connectives had significant effects in only two of the four passages.

> "none of the signals (headings, previews, and logical connectives) inhibited comprehension in the different passages, neither did they predictably increase it. AH results were instead passage dependent"
