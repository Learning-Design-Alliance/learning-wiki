## Evidence

### Christopher J. Fischer 2021

Christopher J. Fischer, Jennifer Delgado, Sarah LeGresley. (2021). Interplay between physics self-efficacy, calculus transfer ability, and gender. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2110.07364)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre/post calculus transfer assessment in General Physics II; Table 1 shows women's normalized change of 0.25 on question 2 versus men's 0.17, and 0.30 versus 0.26 on question 3. The article notes "a larger percentage of the cohort of women did answer question 1 on the pre-test correctly".

> "As shown in Table 1, the normalized changes in the percentage of students answering the transfer questions (question 2 and question 3) were larger for women than for men."
