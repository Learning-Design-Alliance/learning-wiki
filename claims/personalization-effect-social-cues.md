# The personalization effect improves learning, moderated by prior knowledge, content type, pacing, culture, and language

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Sascha Schneider 2021

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports the personalization effect has been proven by a large number of studies (citing Ginns et al. 2013 for an overview) and attributes the mechanism to self-schema activation, familiarity, interest, and relevance. Second-hand narrative report; no effect sizes printed.

> "The effect can be explained by the activation of self-schemata (i.e., one type of a social schema), increased familiarity, as well as a rise in interest and relevance for learners (e.g., Reichelt et al.2014, Schneider et al. 2015a)."

### Sascha Schneider 2021 (2)

Sascha Schneider, Maik Beege, Steve Nebel, Lenka Schnaubert, Günter Daniel Rey. (2021). The Cognitive-Affective-Social Theory of Learning in digital Environments (CASTLE). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09626-5

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Moderator reported second-hand by the review, citing Stiller and Jedlicka (2010). The article describes this as one of several moderators visible through empirical findings of various studies; no statistics are printed.

> "learners with low prior knowledge benefit more from personalized materials than learners with a high level of prior knowledge (e.g., Stiller and Jedlicka 2010)"
