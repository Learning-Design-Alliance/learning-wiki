# Teacher-child conflict does not predict subsequent increases in children's inattention and impulsivity, contradicting a bidirectional transaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After controlling for stability and within-time covariation, conflict with teachers did not predict later increases in inattention and impulsive behaviors across kindergarten or into first grade. [→ Portilla Ximena A. 2014](#portilla-ximena-a-2014)

## Evidence

### Portilla Ximena A. 2014

Portilla Ximena A., Ballard Parissa J., Adler Nancy E., Boyce W. Thomas, Obradović Jelena. (2014). An Integrative View of School Functioning: Transactions between Self-Regulation, School Engagement, and Teacher-Child Relationship Quality. Child development. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.12259

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

In the Discussion the authors report that the reverse pathway from conflict to later inattention/impulsivity was not significant in the path models, contrary to their hypothesis and to prior transactional findings they cite (Arnold et al., 1998; Doumen et al., 2008; Leflot et al., 2011).

> "In contrast, conflict with teachers did not predict increases in inattention and impulsive behaviors across time. This ran counter to our original hypothesis that we would observe bidirectional transactions between teacher-child relationship quality and children's behaviors, as has been found in other work"
