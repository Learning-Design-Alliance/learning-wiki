Evidence¶

Stephanie Simmons Zuilkowski & Benjamin Piper 2014¶

Stephanie Simmons Zuilkowski & Benjamin Piper. (2014). Improving Reading Outcomes in Kenya: First-Year Effects of the PRIMR Initiative. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562806

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Difference-in-differences analysis of the PRIMR RCT (2312 students in 117 schools at midterm), fit by OLS with covariates and standard errors accounting for nesting of students in schools. The article reports "statistically significant impacts on oral reading fluency" in the named grade and school-type groups.

"Using the DID estimator, we found statistically significant impacts on oral reading fluency in grade 1 formal and nonformal schools and grade 2 nonformal schools for both English and Kiswahili"

Stephanie Simmons Zuilkowski & Benjamin Piper 2014 (2)¶

Stephanie Simmons Zuilkowski & Benjamin Piper. (2014). Improving Reading Outcomes in Kenya: First-Year Effects of the PRIMR Initiative. SREE Spring 2014 Conference Abstract. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED562806

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation of the pattern of DID fluency estimates across grade, school type, and language in the same RCT analysis; the article offers longer Kiswahili words as "one possible explanation for the disparity between English and Kiswahili fluency impacts."

"The magnitude of the impact was largest for grade 1 students in nonformal schools and was larger in English than in Kiswahili."

Related Claims¶

  • Previous non-longitudinal analyses of the PRIMR dataset reported statistically significant impacts on English and Kiswahili outcomes, with impacts ranging from .3 to .5 standard deviations. — related
