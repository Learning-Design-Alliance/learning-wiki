# Argument structure training improved identification of atypical full-fledged arguments at posttest, but not at the 4-week follow-up

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At posttest, the training group identified atypical, full-fledged arguments more accurately than the control group. [→ Sarah von der Mühlen 2019](#sarah-von-der-muhlen-2019)
`q3 i?` No training effects on atypical argument identification were found at follow-up. [→ Sarah von der Mühlen 2019 (2)](#sarah-von-der-muhlen-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Sarah von der Mühlen 2019

Sarah von der Mühlen, Tobias Richter, Sebastian Schmid, Kirsten Berthold. (2019). How to improve argumentation comprehension in university students: experimental test of a training approach. Instructional Science 47:215–237. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9471-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Model 2 of the nested regression on posttest accuracy (N = 50), controlling for pretest accuracy and study performance. The experimental group reached M = .68 versus M = .53 in the control condition for atypical full-fledged arguments.

> "Model 2 revealed a main effect of training condition for the identification of atypical, full-fledged arguments (B = 0.08, SE = 0.03, p < .01, ΔR2 = .09, one-tailed), with participants in the experimental condition receiving higher posttest accuracies (M = .68, SE = .04), as compared to those in the control condition (M = .53, SE = .04)."

### Sarah von der Mühlen 2019 (2)

Sarah von der Mühlen, Tobias Richter, Sebastian Schmid, Kirsten Berthold. (2019). How to improve argumentation comprehension in university students: experimental test of a training approach. Instructional Science 47:215–237. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-018-9471-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Follow-up test 4 weeks after the posttest in the same experiment showed no significant training effects on identifying atypical full-fledged arguments; equivalence was not tested.

> "Unexpectedly, however, no such effects were found in the follow-up, p > .05."
