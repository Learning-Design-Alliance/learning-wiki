## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Thematic analysis identified themes of higher confidence, faster adaptation, immediate feedback, realistic advanced scenarios and less supervision needed for VR-trained radiographers, with the reverse pattern reported by traditionally trained peers. ## Evidence

### Karimi Hossein 2025

Karimi Hossein, Clarke Samantha, Watson Emily. (2025). Comparing Clinical Preparedness of Newly Qualified Diagnostic Radiographers Trained With Immersive Virtual Reality vs. Traditional Simulation: A Mixed‐Methods Study. Journal of Medical Radiation Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1002/jmrs.882

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke six-phase, NVivo 14) of semi-structured interviews and focus groups with radiographers and heads of department from five hospitals. Table 4 lists themes including "Higher Confidence (VR)", "Immediate Feedback (VR)", "Less Supervision Needed (VR)" and their traditional-training counterparts.

> "VR‐trained radiographers expressed higher confidence in their clinical abilities, noting that the immersive nature of the training helped them adapt more quickly to real‐world settings."
