# School characteristics were associated with participation: district-run schools, higher proportions of eligible students, and strong family engagement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Charter schools had 4 percentage points lower participation than district schools; schools with a high proportion of eligible students had 2 percentage points higher participation; schools with strong family engagement (Involved Families) had 3 percentage points higher participation. [→ de la Torre 2023](#de-la-torre-2023)

### de la Torre 2023

de la Torre, M. (2023). Exploring Chicago Connected: Participation patterns. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/exploring-chicago-connected

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel model comparisons of schools serving similar students (Table 2, from Table A.1 models). The report also reports +2 ppt for high vs. low proportion of eligible students and +3 ppt for high vs. low Involved Families (5Essentials); all relationships presented in the table were statistically significant.

### de la Torre 2023 (2)

de la Torre, M. (2023). Exploring Chicago Connected: Participation patterns. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/exploring-chicago-connected

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

School-level descriptive analysis (Figure 7, 649 schools). The report notes notable variation in participation among schools even among schools serving students with similar characteristics, partly because schools serving higher-hardship communities had higher rates.

> "the schools represented on the far right of Figure 7 had, on average, a population of students from community areas with a hardship index of 70, and the participation rate among students in these schools varied from 29% to 63%."
