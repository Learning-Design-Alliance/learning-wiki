## Evidence

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Blinded expert OSATS ratings (6 categories, 7-point scale) of 70 video-recorded realistic resections. No statistically significant global difference among the three groups; equivalence was not tested. No standardized effect size printed.

> "Mean global OSATS ratings were not statistically significant among the VOA (4.63 [95% CI, 4.06 to 5.20] points), instructor (4.40 [95% CI, 3.88-4.91] points), and control (3.86 [95% CI, 3.44 to 4.27] points) groups"

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022 (2)

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

OSATS subscore analyses from the same blinded ratings of 70 realistic resections: VOA beat control on the overall subscore (1.04 points, P = .02) and instructor beat control on instrument handling (1.18 points, P = .01). Both arms also beat control on respect for tissue and economy of movement. No standardized effect size printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Compared with the control group, expert instruction significantly enhanced instrument handling (mean difference, 1.18 [95% CI, 0.22 to 2.14] points; P = .01) and VOA resulted in significantly higher OSATS overall subscore (mean difference, 1.04 [95% CI, 0.13 to 1.96] points; P = .02)"

### Fazlollahi AM et al 2022 (3)

Fazlollahi AM et al. (2022). Effect of Artificial Intelligence Tutoring vs Expert Instruction on Learning Simulated Surgical Skills Among Medical Students: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.49008

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis across the 70 randomized students linking completion of VOA's instrument movement competency step with blinded OSATS economy-of-movement ratings, "Pearson r = 0.25, P = .03" — a small-to-moderate printed correlation.

> "Completing VOA's instrument movement competency correlated significantly with higher economy of movement (Pearson r = 0.25, P = .03), suggesting successful acquisition of the relevant competency."
