# Everyday language routinely and productively crosses ontological categories

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · theoretical `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Matter predicates are commonly and comprehensibly applied to non-material entities such as time, technology, and ceasefires in everyday speech. [→ Ayush Gupta 2008](#ayush-gupta-2008)

## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2008

Ayush Gupta, David Hammer, Edward F. Redish. (2008). The Case for Dynamic Models of Learners' Ontologies in Physics. Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/hlns20

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors' linguistic analysis of everyday expressions, drawing on Lakoff and Johnson's work on ontological metaphors, shows that matter predicates are applied to non-material entities without being noticed as anomalous. They note that "in everyday language it does not seem nonsensical" to do so, and that such crossing helps rather than hinders understanding.

> "in everyday language it does not seem nonsensical for us to apply matter predicates (infuse, litter, broken, and quantification-in-molecular-units) to non-material entities (technology, ceasefire, time)."
