# Eyetracking process measures do not predict posttest performance, so they do not explain the labeling benefit

> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fixation duration on labeled target areas, looks between text and table, and total fixation time on the lesson each showed no effect on posttest scores. [→ Clinton 2016](#clinton-2016)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2016

Clinton, V., Morsanyi, K., Alibali, M. W., & Nathan, M. J. (2016). Learning about Probability from Text and Tables: Do Color Coding and Labeling through an Interactive-user Interface Help? Applied Cognitive Psychology, 30(3), 440-453. https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3223

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Series of general linear models predicting posttest score from eyetracking process variables (fixation duration on labeled areas, looks between text and table, total fixation time), each adjusted for pretest and need for cognition; none of the process variables was significant.

> "There was no effect of fixation duration on target areas for labeling and posttest scores, b = -.02, χ2(1, N = 98) = .27, t = -.52, p = .61."
