# Because iCPR performance is highly repeatable over a week, performance changes after training can be attributed to true skill decay rather than inconsistent performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The demonstrated repeatability of iCPR over one week means previously reported post-training performance changes reflect decay, resolving whether such changes could instead be natural variation. [→ Almeida Debora 2020](#almeida-debora-2020)

## Evidence

### Almeida Debora 2020

Almeida Debora, Clark Carol, Jones Michael, McConnell Phillip, Williams Jonathan. (2020). Consistency and variability in human performance during simulate infant CPR: a reliability study. Scandinavian Journal of Trauma, Resuscitation and Emergency Medicine. [doi:10.1186/s13049-020-00785-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-020-00785-y)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Conclusion drawn from the full within-day and between-day reliability analysis of 27 healthcare students performing repeated 2-min simulated iCPR trials on an instrumented manikin at two time points; the study frames this as the first exploration of iCPR performance consistency.

> "iCPR performance was highly repeatable and consistent, and this was maintained over a week. These results provide an opportunity to further explore iCPR skill acquisition and decay."
