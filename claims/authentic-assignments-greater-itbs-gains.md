# Chicago classrooms with high-quality authentic intellectual assignments showed greater one-year ITBS gains than classrooms with low-quality assignments

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 associational `r2`, 1 theoretical `r2` · `q2`–`q3`

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Students in classrooms with high-quality assignments recorded learning gains 20 percent greater than the national average on the ITBS, while students in low-quality-assignment classrooms gained 25 percent less than the national average in reading and 22 percent less in mathematics. [→ Fred M. Newmann 2001](#fred-m-newmann-2001)

`q3 i?` In classrooms with authentic assignments, one-year student gains on the ITBS were 20 percent greater than the national average in both reading and mathematics, while less demanding classrooms showed gains 22 to 25 percent below the national average. [→ Smylie 2006](#smylie-2006)

## Evidence

### Fred M. Newmann 2001

Fred M. Newmann, Anthony S. Bryk, Jenny K. Nagaoka. (2001). Authentic Intellectual Work and Standardized Tests: Conflict or Coexistence? Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/authentic-intellectual-work-and-standardized-tests-conflict-or-coexistence

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational analysis linking scored classroom assignments to ITBS gains in Chicago elementary classrooms, controlling for prior achievement, race, gender, and socioeconomic status. High-quality-assignment classrooms showed gains "20 percent greater than the national average." No standardized effect size is printed for this comparison.

> "In Chicago classrooms with high-quality assignments, students' record learning gains were 20 percent greater than the national average."

### Smylie 2006

Smylie, Mark A., and Stacy A. Wenzel. (2006). Promoting Instructional Improvement: A Strategic Human Resource Management Perspective. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/promoting-instructional-improvement-strategic-human-resource-management-perspective

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Newmann, Bryk, and Nagaoka analyzed about 2,000 assignments and standardized test scores from up to 2,100 students per year across 12 to 18 under-resourced Chicago elementary schools over three years. They found "one-year student gains on the ITBS were 20 percent greater than the national average" in authentic-assignment classrooms.

> "Controlling for student race, socioeconomic status, gender, and prior achievement, this study found that in classrooms with authentic assignments, one-year student gains on the ITBS were 20 percent greater than the national average in both reading and mathematics."
