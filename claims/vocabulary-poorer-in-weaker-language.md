# Vocabulary is poorer in the weaker language, with roughly one-third the word types of the stronger language

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` At comparable sample points, each child produced about one-third the number of word types in the Romance language compared to Swedish. [→ Bernardini 2004](#bernardini-2004)

## Evidence

### Bernardini 2004

Bernardini, P., & Schlyter, S. (2004). Growing syntactic structure and code-mixing in the weaker language: The Ivy Hypothesis. Bilingualism: Language and Cognition, 7(1), 49-69. https://doi.org/10.1017/S1366728904001270

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Vocabulary comparison at selected sample points where children spoke relatively more in their weaker language (MLU 1.7-2.0, about 200 utterances, about 20 minutes of dialogue). The study found "each child produced in his Romance language about a third of the number of word types he produced in Swedish."

> "each child used 100–150 word types in Swedish, whereas this was 46 for Lukas in Italian, 31 for Paul in French and 47 for L´eo in French. This means that each child produced in his Romance language about a third of the number of word types he produced in Swedish."
