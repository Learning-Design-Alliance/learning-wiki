Evidence¶

Johnson 2023¶

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental analysis of LCFF exposure on high school graduation, using concentration-grant funding variation. The article reports the estimate is strongest for Black students but not statistically distinguishable from other subgroups.

"For all student groups, a $1,000 increase in the average per-pupil spending experienced throughout one's high school years (grades 9–12) increased the likelihood of graduating from high school by 8.2 percentage points, on average."

Johnson 2023 (2)¶

Johnson, R. C. (2023). School funding effectiveness: Evidence from California's Local Control Funding Formula. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/529.194

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Same LCFF spending analysis for high school college-readiness outcomes, attributed to $1,000 per-pupil increases experienced across grades 9–11. Percentages are printed as reported; no standardised effect size is printed.

"a 9.8 percentage-point increase in the likelihood of meeting college readiness standards in math and a 14.7 percentage-point increase in the likelihood of meeting college readiness standards in reading."
