## Evidence

### Amirthanayagam Anumithra 2026

Amirthanayagam Anumithra, Zecca Massimiliano, Chitrakar Anisha, Chavda Vijay, Moss Esther L. (2026). Looks like a surgeon? Impact of anthropometry on the kinematics of surgical novices: counterbalanced cross-over design study of minimally invasive surgery. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-026-04077-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Sensitivity analysis within the linear mixed-effects models of 54 surgical novices' kinematic data, testing modality-by-starting-modality interaction. The analysis found "Starting modality and task order were not associated with angular velocity or jerk" with the printed p values and carry-over estimate; no standardised effect size was printed.

> "Starting modality and task order were not associated with angular velocity or jerk (all p > 0.4); however, for jerk the LAP-RA difference was larger in participants who started with LAP (0.18 m/s³; 95% CI 0.03 to 0.33), consistent with a learning effect."
