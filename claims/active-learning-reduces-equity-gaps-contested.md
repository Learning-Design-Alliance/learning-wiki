## Evidence

### Sedlacek 2025

Sedlacek, Q., Villa, A. M., III, Friend, M., Pérez, G., Dozier, S., Haeger, H., Lomelí, K., & Mejia, J. A. (2025). How Representation Matters: Conceptualizing Systemic Racism to Develop Diversity Research Agendas for Undergraduate STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review, 37:49. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10021-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Freeman et al. (2014) and Theobald et al. (2020), that active learning classrooms "tended to achieve better outcomes relative to control-group classrooms" and that interventions "appear to reduce racial and socioeconomic equity gaps." The review attributes these findings to the cited meta-analyses.

> "A meta-analysis by Freeman et al. (2014) suggests that college STEM classrooms which implemented active learning strategies tended to achieve better outcomes relative to control-group classrooms across numerous studies, and a similar meta-analysis by Theobald et al. (2020) found evidence that active learning interventions appear to reduce racial and socioeconomic equity gaps in student outcomes (Theobald et al., 2020)."

### Sedlacek 2025 (2)

Sedlacek, Q., Villa, A. M., III, Friend, M., Pérez, G., Dozier, S., Haeger, H., Lomelí, K., & Mejia, J. A. (2025). How Representation Matters: Conceptualizing Systemic Racism to Develop Diversity Research Agendas for Undergraduate STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review, 37:49. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10021-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports an observational study cited as Stains et al. (2018) covering over 2,000 lessons in over 700 college STEM courses, in which "55% of lessons still relied heavily on didactic lecturing" and over 37% contained no student interaction beyond individual question-answering.

> "A recent observational study of over 2,000 lessons in over 700 college STEM courses found that 55% of lessons still relied heavily on didactic lecturing (Stains et al., 2018), with over 37% of observed lessons containing no student interaction whatsoever beyond individual students answering or asking questions."
