## Evidence

### Wörner 2022

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Classical test theory item analysis on the posttest data of 318 students; item Q2 "had a discrimination of –.03" while all other items ranged from .18 (Q1) to .45 (Q5). The article states this negative discrimination "was another argument to remove item Q2 from the CLCI"; the value is an item statistic, not an effect size.

> "We calculated the item discrimination for all original 16 items, resulting in values between .18 (Q1) and .45 (Q5), except for item Q2, which had a discrimination of –.03 (see Fig. 5)."

### Wörner 2022 (2)

Wörner, S., Becker, S., Küchemann, S., Scheiter, K., & Kuhn, J. (2022). Development and validation of the Converging Lenses Concept Inventory for middle school physics education. Receipt Date: 19.05.2022. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.020131)

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Distractor analysis of answer-option selection frequencies across pre-, post-, and follow-up tests; "only 2-3% of the students selected distractor B in all three measurement points", and more students chose incorrect option A than correct option C at every point. The article concludes "This indicates item Q2 is not a good functioning item in this test"; no effect size is printed.

> "only 2-3% of the students selected distractor B in all three measurement points, which is the smallest percentage of students who selected an answer option in the whole test."
