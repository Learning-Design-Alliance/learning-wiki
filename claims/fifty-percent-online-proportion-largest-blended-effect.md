# Online teaching proportion significantly moderates effects, with 50% online composition yielding the largest effect (SMD = 0.792) and both extremes the smallest

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Effect sizes differ remarkably across online proportions (p = 0.000), with 50% online (0.792) largest, 30-49% (0.525) and 51-69% (0.468) moderate, and both below 30% (0.346) and 70-80% (0.313) smallest. [→ Li 2022](#li-2022)

## Evidence

### Li 2022

Li, B., Yu, Q., & Yang, F. (2022). The effect of blended instruction on student performance: A meta-analysis of 106 empirical studies from China and abroad. Best Evidence in Chinese Education, 10(2):1395-1403. https://doi.org/10.15354/bece.22.ar018

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis of the meta-analysis classifying online teaching proportions into five groups. The between-group test (p = 0.000 < 0.05) shows "remarkably different effects", with "Blended instruction with 50% online teaching yields the most significant positive effect on student learning performance."

> "SMDs of proportions of 50%, 30% - 49%, 51% - 69%, lower than 30%, and 70% - 80% are 0.792, 0.525, 0.468, 0.346, and 0.313 respectively"

### Li 2022 (2)

Li, B., Yu, Q., & Yang, F. (2022). The effect of blended instruction on student performance: A meta-analysis of 106 empirical studies from China and abroad. Best Evidence in Chinese Education, 10(2):1395-1403. https://doi.org/10.15354/bece.22.ar018

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The same moderator analysis notes a boundary pattern: both extremes of online proportion show the weakest effects, while all five groups remain positive (above 0.3).

> "It is worth noticing that both the lowest proportion (lower than 30%) and the highest one (70% - 80%) produce the least significant effects"
