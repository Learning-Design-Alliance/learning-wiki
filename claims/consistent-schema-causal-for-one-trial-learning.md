# A consistent activated schema is causally necessary for rapid one-trial learning: new pairs are learned and retained only in the consistent-schema context

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Normal rats trained concurrently in two arenas acquired and retained 24 hours later new one-trial pairs only when encoding occurred in the consistent-schema context, not the inconsistent one. [→ Tse et al. 2007](#tse-et-al-2007)

## Evidence

### Tse et al. 2007

Dorothy Tse, Rosamund F. Langston, Masaki Kakeyama, Ingrid Bethus, Patrick A. Spooner, Emma R. Wood, Menno P. Witter, Richard G. M. Morris. (2007). Schemas and Memory Consolidation. Science. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135935

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 4 trained normal rats (n = 9) concurrently on a consistent schema (flavors 1 to 6 always at locations 1 to 6) and an inconsistent schema whose flavor-location mapping changed every two sessions. New pairs trained in two-trial sessions were probed 24 hours later; performance was "consistently good to the cued new location" only for the consistent schema (Group × Location F = 13.92, df = 1.64/26.30, P < 0.001); approach latencies were equivalent across contexts (20.9 ± 1.9 s vs 20.0 ± 2.5 s), indicating comparable motivation.

> "Performance 24 hours after exposure to the two new cue flavors and their locations when the animals would be encoding information using a consistent activated schema (PTs 3 and 5) was consistently good to the cued new location"
