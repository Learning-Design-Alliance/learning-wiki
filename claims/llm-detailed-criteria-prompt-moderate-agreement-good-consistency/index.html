Evidence¶

Da-Wei Zhang 2024¶

Da-Wei Zhang, Melissa Boey, Yan Yu Tan & Alexis Hoh Sheng Jia. (2024). Evaluating large language models for criterion-based grading from agreement to consistency. npj Science of Learning 9:79. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00291-1

q2 · i? · design · r2

Quantitative ICC analysis of 31 IELTS essays graded by ChatGPT 3.5 under three incremental prompts versus official examiner ratings; Table 1 reports Prompt 3 interrater agreement of 0.61 [0.02, 0.85] and consistency of 0.77 [0.57, 0.88]. The article reports "Only prompt 3 showed signiﬁcant interrater agreement but at a moderate level."

"Only prompt 3 showed signiﬁcant interrater agreement but at a moderate level. Regarding int errater consistency, all prompts showed signiﬁcance, but again, it was Prompt 3 that achieved a good level."

Related Claims¶

  • More advanced LLMs (ChatGPT 4.0, Claude 3.0 Haiku) did not improve criterion-based grading over ChatGPT 3.5 with the same prompt — related
  • A teacher without IELTS-specific training showed poorer grading consistency than the best LLM prompt condition — related
  • ChatGPT 3.5 grading with the best prompt was significantly lower than official IELTS examiner grades, indicating systematic bias in absolute grading — related
  • The best prompt showed excellent test-retest reliability (intrarater consistency ICC = 0.89) when rerun in ChatGPT 3.5 — related
  • Prompting type shows no significant effect on rated text quality (transfer, validity of conclusions, integration), with low interrater reliability for two criteria — related
