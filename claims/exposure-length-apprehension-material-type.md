## Evidence

### Whipple 1910

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Apprehension test in which collocations of 10 objects were exposed without instrumental aid for 6 sec to three adult observers; the article reports the very small increase compared with the 0.08-sec range-of-attention tests.

> "with an exposure of 6 sec, the average number of objects named was 6.03, while, with an exposure only 1-75 as long, between 4 and 5 objects could be named."

### Whipple 1910 (2)

Whipple, G. M. (1910). The effect of practise upon the range of visual attention and of visual apprehension. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://books.google.com/books?q=Whipple+effect+of+practise+range+of+visual+attention+apprehension

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same apprehension tests, four-line stanzas of poetry were exposed for 3 sec; the article attributes the materially increased range for this sense-material to grouping and combination into meaningful units.

> "nearly 12 words were read in poetry as against the 4 to 6 limit in ordinary tachistoscopy."
