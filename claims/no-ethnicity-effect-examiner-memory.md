# Examiners' memories of performances showed no stereotype bias: recognition rates for real stereotype-inconsistent and invented stereotype-consistent statements did not differ between groups recalling an Asian vs a white student

## Evidence

### Yeates Peter 2017

Yeates Peter, Woolf Katherine, Benbow Emyr, Davies Ben, Boohan Mairhead, Eva Kevin. (2017). A randomised trial of the influence of racial stereotype bias on examiners' scores, feedback and recollections in undergraduate clinical exams. BMC Medicine 15:179. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0943-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Recognition-based memory test of 40 quotes from the mixed performance, analysed with independent-group univariate ANOVAs; real statements were endorsed more than invented overall (72.5% vs 32.7%, F = 871.3, p < 0.001). No effect size was printed for the group contrast.

> "no between-group difference occurred in recognition rates of such statements: Group A (recalling an Asian student on the mixed performance) 75% (72–79%); Group B (recalling a white student on the mixed performance) 78% (75–82%), F = 1.63, p = 0.20."

### Yeates Peter 2017 (2)

Yeates Peter, Woolf Katherine, Benbow Emyr, Davies Ben, Boohan Mairhead, Eva Kevin. (2017). A randomised trial of the influence of racial stereotype bias on examiners' scores, feedback and recollections in undergraduate clinical exams. BMC Medicine 15:179. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0943-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Second univariate ANOVA on invented stereotype-consistent statements found no difference between the group that had seen the Asian actor (29%) and the white actor (32%). No effect size was printed.

> "No between-group difference occurred in these statements: Group A (recalling an Asian student on the mixed performance) 29% (24–33%); Group B (recalling a white student on the mixed performance) 32% (27–36%), F = 0.84, p = 0.36."
