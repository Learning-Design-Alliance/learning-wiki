Evidence¶

Núñez-Regueiro 2024¶

Núñez-Regueiro, F. (2024). Cubic Relations of Autonomous and Controlled Motivation to Achievement: A Cross-National Validation of Self-Determination Theory Using Response Surface Analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 36 (3). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09905-x

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Structural invariance testing of the 'Cubic effect of x' polynomial family across 14 TIMSS 2019 countries (including Australia, England, Finland, France, Singapore, Sweden). The constrained single-group solution "explained 10.7% of the variance in achievement" with excellent fit (CFI = 0.997, RMSEA = 0.011).

"This model explained 10.7% of the variance in achievement, or an 8.1% increase relative to the additive model (i.e., R2= .099)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Autonomous and controlled motivation correlate positively and strongly across countries (r = .544), and each relates positively to mathematics achievement (r = .248 and r = .080) — related
  • Across 37 countries, polynomial models with nonlinear or interactive effects of autonomous and controlled motivation on mathematics achievement always fit better than linear additive-only models — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In 12 countries, a level-dependent incongruence polynomial with free cubic effect of control explained 7.8% of achievement variance (a 30.9% increase over the additive model) — related
  • In 8 countries, a non-parallel asymmetric congruence/incongruence polynomial explained a modest 4.1% of achievement variance yet a 41% increase over the additive model — related
  • The cubic RSA findings were cross-validated on replication data from TIMSS 2015 (N = 169,269) and an SDT college-student dataset (N = 270) using the Academic Motivation Scale — related
  • Higher grades occur when high autonomous motivation coincides with low controlled motivation (incongruence), not congruence — related
  • Academic self-concept relates most strongly to achievement, with achievement goals, achievement motivation, autonomous motivation, and effort beliefs each adding unique variance — related
