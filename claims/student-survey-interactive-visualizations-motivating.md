## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In anonymous end-of-course surveys, over 70% of students found the inclusion of interactive visualizations motivating and helpful for understanding core concepts, and a similar proportion reused them for post-lecture revision. [→ Dou Du 2023](#dou-du-2023)

## Evidence

### Dou Du 2023

Dou Du, Taylor J. Baird, Sara Bonella, Giovanni Pizzi. (2023). OSSCAR, an open platform for collaborative development of computational tools for education in science. arXiv. Originally published 2022. [doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2022.108546](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2022.108546)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Anonymous surveys conducted at the end of one of the EPFL courses using OSSCAR notebooks. The article reports that "for the majority of students (over 70%)" the visualizations were motivating and aided understanding, and that the same proportion also used them after lectures while revising. No effect size or test statistic is printed.

> "The results indicate that for the majority of students (over 70%) the inclusion of interactive visualizations during the classes was both motivating, and helped them to better understand the core course concepts by being actively engaged in the learning process."
