# Latent profile analysis identifies three therapist subgroups differing in relative teletherapy versus in-person skill: developing teletherapists, teletherapy experts and teletherapy-challenged therapists

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Developing teletherapists (54.26%, n = 121) showed small skill differences (total d = 0.36), suggesting roughly equivalent competencies across settings. [→ Lin 2025](#lin-2025)
`q3 i2` Teletherapy experts (34.98%, n = 78) showed higher observer-rated skills in teletherapy than in-person therapy (d = 0.51). [→ Lin 2025 (2)](#lin-2025-2)
`q3 i3` Teletherapy-challenged therapists (10.76%, n = 24) showed much lower skills in teletherapy (d = 1.36). [→ Lin 2025 (3)](#lin-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Lin 2025

Lin, T., Aafjes‐van Doorn, K., Heckman, T. G., Antebi‐Lerman, E., & Anderson, T. (2025). Are Therapists Less Skilful in Teletherapy Than In‐Person Therapy Scenarios? A Latent Profile Analysis of Facilitative Interpersonal Skills. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70047

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Latent profile analysis of eight FIS difference scores in the 223-participant sample; the three-class model was selected on AIC, BIC and SSA-BIC fit. The article reports a total-skill difference of "d = 0.36" for this class, with item effects from 0.19 to 0.40.

> "The first class, labelled developing teletherapists, accounted for 54.26% (n = 121) of the sample."

### Lin 2025 (2)

Lin, T., Aafjes‐van Doorn, K., Heckman, T. G., Antebi‐Lerman, E., & Anderson, T. (2025). Are Therapists Less Skilful in Teletherapy Than In‐Person Therapy Scenarios? A Latent Profile Analysis of Facilitative Interpersonal Skills. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70047

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Second latent class from the same LPA; this subgroup scored higher in teletherapy on all eight FIS subskills, especially alliance rupture repair responsiveness (d = 0.67), hope (d = 0.58), warmth (d = 0.57) and alliance bond capacity (d = 0.53).

> "This group had higher levels of observer‐rated FIS in teletherapy scenarios than in in‐person therapy scenarios with a medium effect size (d = 0.51; Table 3)."

### Lin 2025 (3)

Lin, T., Aafjes‐van Doorn, K., Heckman, T. G., Antebi‐Lerman, E., & Anderson, T. (2025). Are Therapists Less Skilful in Teletherapy Than In‐Person Therapy Scenarios? A Latent Profile Analysis of Facilitative Interpersonal Skills. Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy. https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.70047

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Third latent class from the same LPA; the largest skill differences were in verbal fluency (d = 1.56), alliance rupture repair responsiveness (d = 1.56), hope (d = 1.24) and alliance bond capacity (d = 1.23).

> "Therapists in this class showed significantly lower levels of observer‐rated FIS when addressing challenging situations in teletherapy compared with in‐person therapy, with a large effect size (d = 1.36; Table 3)."
