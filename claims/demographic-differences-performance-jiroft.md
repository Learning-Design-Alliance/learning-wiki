# Academic performance and the predictor constructs differ significantly across demographic subgroups, with higher scores among students aged 23–27, medical-field students, and non-working students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean academic performance differed significantly by age (p = 0.02), field of study (p = 0.03), educational level (p = 0.04), and employment status (p = 0.03), with higher means among 23–27-year-olds, medical/professional-doctorate students, and non-working students. [→ Bordbar Shima 2025](#bordbar-shima-2025)

## Evidence

### Bordbar Shima 2025

Bordbar Shima, Mirzaei Saeid, Bahmaei Jamshid, Atashbahar Omolbanin, Yusefi Ali Reza. (2025). Predicting students' academic performance based on academic identity, academic excitement, and academic enthusiasm: evidence from a cross-sectional study in a developing country. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07374-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

T-tests and ANOVA on the 290-student sample (Table 6); the article reports these descriptive subgroup means and significant p-values for age, field, level, and employment status, without a standardised effect size for this contrast.

> "the mean score for academic performance was higher among students aged 23–27 years (137.46 ± 6.75 out of 240), those in the medical field, including professional doctorate students (136.77 ± 6.63 out of 240), and non-working students (136.85 ± 6.52 out of 240)"

### Bordbar Shima 2025 (2)

Bordbar Shima, Mirzaei Saeid, Bahmaei Jamshid, Atashbahar Omolbanin, Yusefi Ali Reza. (2025). Predicting students' academic performance based on academic identity, academic excitement, and academic enthusiasm: evidence from a cross-sectional study in a developing country. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07374-6

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

ANOVA and t-tests on the same sample; the article reports significant demographic differences for identity, positive excitement (age p = 0.03, gender p = 0.02, field p = 0.04), negative excitement (age p = 0.04, marital status p = 0.02), and enthusiasm (including residence p = 0.04).

> "the mean score for academic identity varied significantly according to age (p = 0.02), gender (p = 0.04), field of study (p = 0.03), and educational level (p = 0.02)"
