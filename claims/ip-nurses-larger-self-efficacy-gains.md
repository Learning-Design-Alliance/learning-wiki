# Interprofessionally trained nurses and midwives show significantly larger self-efficacy gains than uniprofessionally trained nurses

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Nurses/midwives trained interprofessionally improved by 20% versus 12% for the UP group (t=3.4; p<0.001). [→ Watters Colm 2015](#watters-colm-2015)
`q3 i?` The IP advantage held on both factors: communication/teamwork (p<0.05) and leadership/management (p<0.001). [→ Watters Colm 2015 (2)](#watters-colm-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Watters Colm 2015

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison 1: pre/post quasi-experimental analysis of nurses and midwives trained alone (UP; n=64) or interprofessionally with FY1/2 doctors (IP; n=66). The quote reports "The UP group improved overall by 12% (SD 14) and the IP group by 20% (SD 11)". No standardised effect size printed.

> "The UP group improved overall by 12% (SD 14) and the IP group by 20% (SD 11). An independent samples t test for equality of means showed that this difference was significant (t=3.4; df 128; p<0.001; 95% CI 11.98 to 3.22)."

### Watters Colm 2015 (2)

Watters Colm, Reedy Gabriel, Ross Alastair, Morgan Nicola J, Handslip Rhodri, Jaye Peter. (2015). Does interprofessional simulation increase self-efficacy: a comparative study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2014-005472

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Factor-level analysis for the same nurse/midwife comparison (n=115) reported in table 1: communication/teamwork shift 15.5% IP vs 10.1% UP (p<0.05); leadership/management shift 26.6% IP vs 15.8% UP (p<0.001).

> "the significant effect of IP training for nurses overall (comparison 1) is reflected in significantly better improvement on communication (p<0.05) and leadership (p<0.001) items."
