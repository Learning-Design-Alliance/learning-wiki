## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Saccadic amplitudes were significantly shorter for experts than for semi-experts and naïve participants, the opposite of Hypothesis 6. [→ Bertram Raymond 2013](#bertram-raymond-2013)

## Evidence

### Bertram Raymond 2013

Bertram Raymond, Helle Laura, Kaakinen Johanna K., Svedström Erkki. (2013). The Effect of Expertise on Eye Movement Behaviour in Medical Image Perception. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066169

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mixed-effects modelling of 31,998 saccades: experts averaged 2.31 degrees versus 2.64 (semi-experts) and 2.86 (naïve), a significant effect (p<.05). An Expertise × Video interaction showed semi-experts used longer saccades in ABN videos while experts did not.

> "Instead of larger saccadic amplitudes for experts, amplitudes were significantly shorter for experts than for other groups (E: 2.31; SE: 2.64; N: 2.86; p<.05)."
