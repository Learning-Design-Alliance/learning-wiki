Evidence¶

Al-Jarrah 2018¶

Al-Jarrah, T. M., Mansor, N., Ab Rashid, R., Bashir, I., & Al-Jarrah, J. M. (2018). EFL Students' Attitude Toward Using Metacognitive Strategies in Writing. English Language Teaching, 11(10). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v11n10p162

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Post-intervention interview findings from the same 10-student qualitative study: the article reports "mixed reactions" on difficult strategies, with difficulties spread across planning, monitoring and evaluating stages, and a request for more training time. No effect size is printed.

"In addition, the findings revealed mixed reactions from student s on the strategies they found to be difficult to learn and apply. Some students perceived difficulties in the pl anning stage, whereas some perceived difficulties in monitoring stage and s evaluating stage. Participants report ed that they need more time to be trained using metacognitive strategies."
