# Metacognitive strategy use correlates with comprehension non-monotonically across grades, which the model attributes to unconscious self-regulation in experienced learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Correlations between reported metacognitive strategy use and text comprehension increased to grade 9 but decreased to a non-significant r = .15 in grade 11, a pattern the authors attribute to unconscious self-regulation among experienced high-grade students. [→ Joachim Wirth 2020](#joachim-wirth-2020)

## Evidence

### Joachim Wirth 2020

Joachim Wirth, Ferdinand Stebner, Melanie Trypke, Corinna Schuster, & Detlev Leutner. (2020). An Interactive Layers Model of Self-Regulated Learning and Cognitive Load. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09568-4

`q2 · i1` · `review · r2`

Narrative review attributing this to Leopold and Leutner (2002): students of different grades read an expository science text, reported strategy use, and were tested on comprehension. The review reports the metacognitive correlations "followed a non-monotone trend" with a small final correlation of r = .15 (n.s.) in grade 11.

> "correlations of the use of metacognitive learning strategies and text comprehension were surprising since they followed a non-monotone trend, increasing from r = −.05 (grade 5) and r = .23 (grade 7) to r = .31 (grade 9) before decreasing to r = .15 (n.s., grade 11)"
