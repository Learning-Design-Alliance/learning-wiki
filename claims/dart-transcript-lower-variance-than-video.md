## Subclaims
`q2 i?` CV% for every DART variable and ratio was lower for the transcript of Video A than for Video A or Video B rated from video. [→ Baliga Kaushik 2022](#baliga-kaushik-2022)

## Evidence

### Baliga Kaushik 2022

Baliga Kaushik, Coggins Andrew, Warburton Sandra, Mathias Divya, Yamada Nicole K., Fuerch Janene H., Halamek Louis P. (2022). Pilot study of the DART tool - an objective healthcare simulation debriefing assessment instrument. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-022-03697-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of CV% across the three rating data sets in the pilot study. Transcript raters could reread sentences while video raters watched in real time without rewinding; the authors caution that "transcript scores may underestimate the true variation in scoring, and videos A and B may better represent the real-world use of DART".

> "we found the CV% for each variable in the transcript (IQ = 21.0%, IS = 29.1%, TR = 31.3%, IQ:IS = 32.2%, TR:[IQ + IS] = 21.1%) lower than the same CV% values for Video A (IQ = 28.3%, IS = 33.0%, TR = 38.8%, IQ:IS = 42.6%, TR:[IQ + IS] = 32.0%) and Video B (IQ = 34.1%, IS = 56.3%, TR = 34.2%, IQ:IS = 47.5%, TR:[IQ + IS] = 31.0%)"
