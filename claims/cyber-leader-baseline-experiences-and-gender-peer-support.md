## Evidence

### Cross et al. 2015

Donna Cross, Leanne Lester, Amy Barnes, Patricia Cardoso and Kate Hadwen. (2015). If it’s about me, why do it without me? Genuine student engagement in school cyberbullying education. International Journal of Emotional Education, 7(1), 35-51. www.um.edu.mt/cres/ijee. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1085606

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre-training questionnaire responses from 157 cyber leaders aged 14 (61%) and 15 (39%) from 19 schools, measured with an eleven-item victimisation scale (alpha=.86). The article reports the descriptive percentages in Table I; no effect size is printed.

> "Most of the cyber leaders had not experienced any cyberbullying in the past term (Table I). For those who did, the most common experiences were being sent nasty or threatening messages while chatting on the Internet (18%)"

### Cross et al. 2015 (2)

Donna Cross, Leanne Lester, Amy Barnes, Patricia Cardoso and Kate Hadwen. (2015). If it’s about me, why do it without me? Genuine student engagement in school cyberbullying education. International Journal of Emotional Education, 7(1), 35-51. www.um.edu.mt/cres/ijee. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1085606

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Secondary analysis of the pre-training questionnaire among the 157 responding cyber leaders, using the eleven-item peer support scale (alpha=0.819). The article reports the Mann-Whitney statistic z=-3.70, p<.001; no effect size is printed.

> "Using a Mann-Whitney non-parametric test, females reported significantly higher peer support than males (z=-3.70, p<.001)."
