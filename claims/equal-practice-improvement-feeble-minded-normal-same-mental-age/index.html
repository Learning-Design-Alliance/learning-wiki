Evidence¶

Woodrow 1915¶

Woodrow, H. (1915). Practice and transference in normal and feeble-minded children. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Practice+and+Transference+in+Normal+and+Feeble-Minded+Children%22

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Controlled four-group experiment (feeble-minded practice n=20, feeble-minded control n=17, normal practice n=16, normal control n=13) with thirteen days of practice on a form sorting test. "For each group, the absolute improvement in average ability is 54, or about 45%." Average percentage improvement was 49 for feeble-minded and 46 for normal children.

"feeble-minded children were found to show the same amount of improvement and to improve in accordance with essentially the same practice curve as normal children of the same mental age and the same initial ability"
