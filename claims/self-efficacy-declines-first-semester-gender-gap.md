## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Both female and male students became less confident in their abilities after one term, and males reported higher self-efficacy than females both entering CEGEP and after the first semester. [→ Dickie 2006](#dickie-2006)

## Evidence

### Dickie 2006

Dickie, L. O., Dedic, H., Rosenfield, S., Rosenfield, E., & Simon, R. A. (2006). Success and Persistence in Science: The Influence of Classroom Climate. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0610243)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of 398 cohort students before and after the first semester using a 6-item self-efficacy scale. The article reports "significant differences in self-efficacy beliefs between females and males" at both time points (HS: F(1,398) = 42.6; CEGEP: F(1,398) = 19.9, both p < .001).

> "Similarly, there were significant differences in self-efficacy beliefs between females and males, both at the beginning of CEGEP (when they were reporting on their experiences in high school) and after the first semester of CEGEP"
