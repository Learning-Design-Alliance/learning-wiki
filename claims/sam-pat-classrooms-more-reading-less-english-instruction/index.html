Evidence¶

Condelli 2010¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners (NCEE 2011-4003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Classroom observations using the study's observation protocol (980 intervals in Sam and Pat classes, 1,034 in control classes). "more time on reading development instruction than control classrooms (66 percent vs. 19 percent of observed time intervals)" was significant at the 0.05 level on a 2-tailed t-test.

"The Sam and Pat classrooms spent more time on reading development instruction than control classrooms (66 percent vs. 19 percent of observed time intervals, respectively), and the difference was statistically significant (Figure ES.1)."

Condelli 2010 (2)¶

Condelli, L., Cronen, S., Bos, J., Tseng, F., and Altuna, J. (2010). The Impact of a Reading Intervention for Low-Literate Adult ESL Learners (NCEE 2011-4003). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Same observation data as the reading-instruction contrast. "control classrooms spent more time on English language acquisition instruction than did Sam and Pat classrooms (68 percent vs. 27 percent of observed time intervals)"; control classes also spent more time on functional reading, writing and math (18 percent vs. 5 percent).

"Conversely, the control classrooms spent more time on English language acquisition instruction than did Sam and Pat classrooms (68 percent vs. 27 percent of observed time intervals, respectively), and this difference was statistically significant."
