# Training in metacognitive regulation combined with a cognitive learning strategy transfers to better metacognitive regulation of mental effort (far transfer), with small effect sizes

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After training, students in the two training groups were better able to metacognitively regulate their mental effort than students in the control group, indicating far transfer from a cognitive learning strategy to a resource management strategy. [→ Wirth 2025](#wirth-2025)

## Evidence

### Wirth 2025

Wirth, J., Weber-Reuter, X.-L., Schuster, C., Fleischer, J., Leutner, D., & Stebner, F. (2025). Far Transfer of Metacognitive Regulation: From Cognitive Learning Strategy Use to Mental Effort Regulation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09983-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Cluster-randomized experimental field study in six German secondary schools; 5th/6th graders cluster-randomized to two training groups and a control group; analyses based on 368 students attending both pretest and posttest. The abstract reports that "students in the training groups were better able to metacognitively regulate their mental effort than students in the control group." The full Results section was not available in the supplied text, so this evidence rests on the article's own summary of its results; no effect size value is printed in the available text.

> "Results (based on data from 368 students due to pandemic conditions) indicated far metacognitive regulation transfer: After training, students in the training groups were better able to metacognitively regulate their mental effort than students in the control group."
