# Training in metacognitive regulation combined with a cognitive learning strategy transfers to better metacognitive regulation of mental effort (far transfer), with small effect sizes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After training, students in the two training groups were better able to metacognitively regulate their mental effort than students in the control group, indicating far transfer from a cognitive learning strategy to a resource management strategy. [→ Wirth 2025](#wirth-2025)

## Evidence

### Wirth 2025

Wirth, J., Weber-Reuter, X.-L., Schuster, C., Fleischer, J., Leutner, D., & Stebner, F. (2025). Far Transfer of Metacognitive Regulation: From Cognitive Learning Strategy Use to Mental Effort Regulation. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09983-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Field study in six German secondary schools (37 classes of 5th/6th graders, N = 777). Twenty-two classes were randomly assigned to one of two training groups (direct and indirect training, or direct training only); the 15 control classes were not randomized but recruited from a school development program and assigned to the control group, so the training-versus-control contrast is quasi-experimental. Because of pandemic absences, analyses rest on the 368 students who attended both pretest and posttest (141, 124 and 103). A linear contrast showed the two training groups improved their metacognitive regulation of mental effort more than the control group (F(1,365) = 4.15, one-tailed p = .021; Group x Time partial eta squared = .02), while the two training groups did not differ from each other; the authors describe the effect sizes as small.

> "Results (based on data from 368 students due to pandemic conditions) indicated far metacognitive regulation transfer: After training, students in the training groups were better able to metacognitively regulate their mental effort than students in the control group."
