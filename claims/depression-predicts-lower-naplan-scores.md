## Subclaims
`q2 i0` Self-reported depression negatively and significantly predicted Numeracy (β* = −0.0446, p = .0012) and Reading (β* = −0.0328, p = .0281) scores, with small standardised effects. [→ Cárdenas 2022](#cardenas-2022)

## Evidence

### Cárdenas 2022

Cárdenas, D., Lattimore, F., Steinberg, D., & Reynolds, K. J. (2022). Youth well-being predicts later academic success. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05780-0

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal ML analysis (Table 1): Numeracy β* = −0.0438 to −0.0475 (p = 1.5 × 10–5 to .0012); Reading β* = −0.0328 to −0.0425 (p = .0006 to .0281), all significant across the three models, with prior NAPLAN and 40 confounders controlled. The printed standardised coefficients (e.g., β* = −0.0446) are small (below d = 0.2).

> "When examining each of the subjective well-being measures independently, self-reported depression negatively and significantly predicted lower Numeracy and Reading NAPLAN scores 7–8 months later."

### Cárdenas 2022 (2)

Cárdenas, D., Lattimore, F., Steinberg, D., & Reynolds, K. J. (2022). Youth well-being predicts later academic success. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05780-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The article's own quantification of the depression effect against the average grade-7-to-9 score differences (42.52 Numeracy; 34.80 Reading), derived from the two-stage model estimates; these are descriptive translations of the printed β* values, not a separately printed effect size.

> "In terms of NAPLAN scores, reducing self-reported depression by one standard deviation would increase Numeracy score by 3 points or ~ 7% (3/42.52) and Reading score by 2.5 points, or ~ 7% (2.5/34.80)."
