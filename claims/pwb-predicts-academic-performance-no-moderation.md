## Evidence

### Sanyaolu Olutosin 2026

Sanyaolu Olutosin, Reeves-Doyle Brandy, Olaniyan Afolakemi C., Max Tarenina, Asala Adetoun, Osime Esther. (2026). Relationships Between Physical Activity, Sleep, Psychological Well-Being, and Academic Performance Among Native American College Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23040491

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderation analysis (ordinal logistic regression) on the ACHA-NCHA secondary sample controlling for age, academic year, and biological sex; the sleep-PWB model was significant (χ2(7) = 147.97, p < 0.001, Nagelkerke R2 = 0.088).

> "Higher PWB was associated with increased odds of reporting higher academic performance (B = 0.031, SE = 0.006, Wald = 25.98, p < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.019–0.043)."

### Sanyaolu Olutosin 2026 (2)

Sanyaolu Olutosin, Reeves-Doyle Brandy, Olaniyan Afolakemi C., Max Tarenina, Asala Adetoun, Osime Esther. (2026). Relationships Between Physical Activity, Sleep, Psychological Well-Being, and Academic Performance Among Native American College Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23040491

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Interaction terms in the two moderation models: sleep duration*PWB (p = 0.462) and PA*PWB (B = 0.003, p = 0.722) were both non-significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "However, the interaction between sleep duration and PWB was not statistically significant (B = −0.007, SE = 0.010, Wald = 0.54, p = 0.462, 95% CI: −0.026–0.012). This indicates that PWB did not moderate the relationship between sleep duration and academic performance."
