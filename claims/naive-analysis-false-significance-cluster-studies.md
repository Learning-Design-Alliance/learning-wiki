# Ignoring cluster dependence in analysis (naive analysis) inflates significance, making new teaching methods appear better than they are

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A naive analysis that applies standard methods without cluster adjustment can make confidence intervals and p values too small, leading to falsely significant findings in educational research. [→ Dreyhaupt Jens 2017](#dreyhaupt-jens-2017)

## Evidence

### Dreyhaupt Jens 2017

Dreyhaupt Jens, Mayer Benjamin, Keis Oliver, Öchsner Wolfgang, Muche Rainer. (2017). Cluster-randomized Studies in Educational Research: Principles and Methodological Aspects. GMS Journal for Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.3205/zma001103

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Methodological claim from the statistical analysis section, supported by citations to Bland and Kerry and to Donner and Klar. The article warns that without cluster adjustment, standard tests such as the two-sample t-test yield over-small confidence intervals and p values, so studies are "falsely reported as significant and thus new teaching methods appearing to be better than they actually are."

> "A so-called “naive analysis” (cluster adjustment is not performed; standard methods are applied, such as a two-sample t-test) can result in the estimated confidence intervals and p values being too small"
