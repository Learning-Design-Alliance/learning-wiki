## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Item-level response times revealed an examinee whose response strategy switched abruptly from solution behavior to consistent rapid guessing around item 50 of an 80-item test. [→ Wise 2005](#wise-2005)

## Evidence

### Wise 2005

Wise, S. L., & Kong, X. (2005). Response Time Effort: A New Measure of Examinee Motivation in Computer-Based Tests. Paper presented at the annual meeting of the National Council on Measurement in Education, Montreal. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED490203. Published version, Applied Measurement in Education: [doi:10.1207/s15324818ame1802_2](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15324818ame1802_2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level response time analysis (Figure 4) of a single examinee with RTE .53, graphing response times across the 80 ILT items. Response times were relatively long for the first 50 items, then "abruptly changed to consistent rapid-guessing levels", suggesting the examinee gave up about two thirds of the way through.

> "Around item 50, however, the response times abruptly changed to consistent rapid-guessing levels (with response times of one to two seconds for nearly all of the remaining items)."
