## Evidence

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post questionnaire comparison on the question 'What is the space-time?'. Before the activity 46% answered correctly and 34% said it is 2-dimensional; after, "over 90% of the participants states that the space-time has 4 dimensions". Percentages are descriptive; no test statistic is printed.

> "After the activity, the percentage of correct answers increases: over 90% of the participants states that the space-time has 4 dimensions, while only the 6% still confuses the concept of space-time with the fourth dimension."

### Postiglione A and De Angelis I 2021 (2)

Postiglione A and De Angelis I. (2021). Students' understanding of gravity using the rubber sheet analogy: an Italian experience. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/abd1c4)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-questionnaire item on what can deform space-time (Figure 4). The printed distribution is "the 60% of the participants states that the cause of the deformation can be any mass", 34% big mass, 3% downward force; descriptive percentages only.

> "Immediately after the activity, the 60% of the participants states that the cause of the deformation can be any mass, the 34% only refers to a big mass while the 3% cites a force that points downward."
