## Evidence

### Angela D. Bender 2017

Angela D. Bender, Hannah L. Filmer, Claire K. Naughtin and Paul E. Dux. (2017). Dynamic, continuous multitasking training leads to task-speciﬁc improvements but does not transfer across action selection tasks. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-017-0015-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed-design baseline assessment of the combined sample (39 analyzed participants) showed a dual-task accuracy cost of "mean multitasking cost = 15.58%" with printed effect size ηp² = .846 (printed as η2 P = .846), confirming the adaptive thresholding produced comparable ~80% component-task accuracy.

> "At pre-training, simultaneous performance of the shape discrimination task and the visuospatial tracking task resulted in large multitasking costs (main effect of single-task vs. multitask: mean multitasking cost = 15.58%; F(1,37) = 202.77, p ≤ .001, η2 P = .846)"
