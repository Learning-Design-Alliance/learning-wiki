# Engagement modes correlated with post-learning passing: the NB mode passed less on modules 1, 2, and 4, and on module 10 the BN and BB modes passed less than the NN mode

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On modules 1, 2, and 4, the NB engagement mode (Normal pre-learning problem solver, Brief learner) was significantly correlated with lower post-learning passing percentages. [→ Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie 2019](#zhongzhou-chen-and-matthew-guthrie-2019)
`q2 i?` On module 10, both the BN and BB engagement modes had significantly lower passing percentages than the NN mode. [→ Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie 2019 (2)](#zhongzhou-chen-and-matthew-guthrie-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie 2019

Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie. (2019). Measuring the Effectiveness of Learning Resources Via Student Interaction with Online Learning Modules. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1903.08003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1903.08003)

`q2 · i?`

Fisher's exact tests (Table 4, row 2) correlating the four engagement modes with passing on post-learning attempts found that "On modules 1, 2, and 4, the “NB” modes are significantly correlated with lower AAL passing percentages", with BB modes removed for low student numbers (modes with fewer than 5 students were neglected).

> "On modules 1, 2, and 4, the “NB” modes are significantly correlated with lower AAL passing percentages , whereas the “BB” modes are removed because of low student number."

### Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie 2019 (2)

Zhongzhou Chen and Matthew Guthrie. (2019). Measuring the Effectiveness of Learning Resources Via Student Interaction with Online Learning Modules. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1903.08003](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1903.08003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The same Fisher's exact test analysis (Table 4 row 2, Figure 10) shows that "On module 10, both “BN” and “BB” modes have significantly lower passing percentage than the “NN” mode", indicating Brief pre-learning problem solvers were more likely to fail after learning regardless of their learning effort.

> "On module 10, both “BN” and “BB” modes have significantly lower passing percentage than the “NN” mode."
