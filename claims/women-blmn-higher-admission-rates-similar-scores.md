## Evidence

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Probability-of-admission analysis split by gender and by race (Figs. 6–7) in the 2537-applicant dataset. The study finds "women are admitted at higher rates than men of equal score" and B/L/M/N applicants at higher or equal rates than white or Asian applicants at similar scores.

> "Interestingly, we ﬁnd that for most physics GRE scores, women are admitted at higher rates than men of equal score are. Likewise, we ﬁnd that applicants identifying as Black, Latinx, Multi-racial, or Native are admitted at higher or the same rates at applicants identifying as white or Asian for similar physics GRE scores."

### Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero 2020 (2)

Nicholas T. Young and Marcos D. Caballero. (2020). The Physics GRE does not help applicants “stand out”. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2008.10712)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the demographic admission-rate results in the Discussion. Because the five programs were seeking diversity, the article states its data cannot separate "standing out" from highlighting, so results should be interpreted with caution.

> "How- ever, as the ﬁve programs studied here were interested in in- creasing their diversity, our data does not allow us to disentan- gle "standing out" from highlighting."
