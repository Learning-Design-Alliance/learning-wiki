## Evidence

### Barbosa D 2026

Barbosa D, Marcel MC, Kirrane D, Avillez M, Gidelew AA, Mitchell CS, Beldavs V, Ariotti C, Dombrowski S, Kofler P. (2026). A First Africa-Wide Amateur Radio Contact Experiment with the International Space Station. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.16545](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.16545)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

A five-point Likert feedback survey administered to all event participants; 73 people completed both pre- and post-event questionnaires. The article reports that proportions selecting "Agree" and "Strongly Agree" "increased substantially after the event" across all measured domains, with no inferential statistics printed.

> "the proportions of participants selecting "Agree" and "Strongly Agree" increased substantially after the event, while "Disagree" and "Strongly Disagree" responses decreased or disappeared almost entirely."

### Barbosa D 2026 (2)

Barbosa D, Marcel MC, Kirrane D, Avillez M, Gidelew AA, Mitchell CS, Beldavs V, Ariotti C, Dombrowski S, Kofler P. (2026). A First Africa-Wide Amateur Radio Contact Experiment with the International Space Station. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2607.16545](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.16545)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Pre- and post-event questionnaires on a five-point Likert scale were completed by 73 participants. The article notes baseline interest was already high, and "responses shifted consistently toward the positive end of the scale following the ARISS contact"; no effect sizes or significance tests are printed.

> "A total of 73 participants completed both the pre- and post-event questionnaires. Before the ARISS contact, participants already demonstrated a high level of interest in astronomy and space science"
