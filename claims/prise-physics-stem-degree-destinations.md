## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Destination analysis comparing 14 follow-up respondents' degree subjects against UK national statistics for A-Level physics students (n=31,845) using binomial tests with Clopper–Pearson intervals.

> "The 7 out of 14 PRiSE students going on to study a physics degree is considerably higher than the national rate of 9.7% (p = 1 × 10−4 in a binomial test)."

### Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt 2021 (2)

Martin O. Archer and Jennifer DeWitt. (2021). “Thanks for helping me ﬁnd my enthusiasm for physics!” The lasting impacts ‘research in schools’ projects can have on students, teachers, and schools. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2104.04431)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same destination comparison: other-STEM uptake was consistent with national statistics, but the increased physics uptake made the overall STEM proportion significantly greater than national figures.

> "the overall STEM-degree proportion (13 out of 14) also being signiﬁcantly greater than found nationally ( 59.3%, p = 0.012)"
