## Evidence

### Counihan H 2012

Counihan H, Harvey SA, Sekeseke-Chinyama M, Hamainza B, Banda R, Malambo T, Masaninga F, Bell D. (2012). Community Health Workers Use Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) Safely and Accurately: Results of a Longitudinal Study in Zambia. The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene. https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.2012.11-0800

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Assessment of the 10-photograph RDT reading card, which included two invalid results per wave. The article reports invalid-result interpretation "improved from 90.2% correct at 3 months to 96.7% at 6 months and 96.5% at 12 months."

> "Interpretation of invalid test results improved from 90.2% correct at 3 months to 96.7% at 6 months and 96.5% at 12 months."

### Counihan H 2012 (2)

Counihan H, Harvey SA, Sekeseke-Chinyama M, Hamainza B, Banda R, Malambo T, Masaninga F, Bell D. (2012). Community Health Workers Use Malaria Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs) Safely and Accurately: Results of a Longitudinal Study in Zambia. The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene. https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.2012.11-0800

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Stata pretest equality-of-proportions comparisons between ambiguous invalid results (no line versus test line only). Table 5 gives P = 0.02 at 3 and 6 months and P = 0.31 at 12 months.

> "a significantly higher proportion of participants at 3 months correctly interpreted the RDT with no line than the RDT with a test line only. This difference remained significant, though smaller, at 6 months, but was no longer significant at 12 months."
