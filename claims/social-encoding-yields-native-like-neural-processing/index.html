Evidence¶

Ana Zappa 2025¶

Ana Zappa, Mel Slater & Antoni Rodriguez-Fornells. (2025). Social interaction shapes and boosts second language learning: virtual reality can show us how. npj Science of Learning 10:90. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00381-8

q2 · i? · review · r?

An fMRI study reviewed by the article (Jeong et al., 2010, its reference 89) compared retrieval-related brain activity for L2 words encoded via social-situation videos versus textual translation; social-video learning engaged the right supramarginal gyrus "similarly to what is observed during L1 processing". The article reports no behavioral result for this comparison.

"words learned by viewing videos depicting social situations acti-vated the right supramarginal gyrus during retrieval, similarly to what is observed during L1 processing, whereas those learned via textual transla-tions activated the left frontal middle gyrus"

Ana Zappa 2025 (2)¶

Ana Zappa, Mel Slater & Antoni Rodriguez-Fornells. (2025). Social interaction shapes and boosts second language learning: virtual reality can show us how. npj Science of Learning 10:90. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00381-8

q2 · i? · review · r3

A second fMRI study reviewed by the article (Fig. 1A) compared encoding in social versus non-social video contexts; the social condition "led to improved behavioral performance" and greater activation in social-affective and perception-action regions. The article reports no effect size, and notes both studies used passive viewing, limiting conclusions about embodiment and interaction.

"when focusing on neural processes during L2 word encoding in social vs. non-social situations as depicted by videos, the former also led to improved behavioral performance and greater activation of brain regions previously associated with social, affective, and perception-action processes during learning"
