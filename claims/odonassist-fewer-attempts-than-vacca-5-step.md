# Novice learners require fewer attempts to master OdonAssist than the Vacca 5-step vacuum technique in simulation training

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants required fewer attempts to achieve three consecutive error-free assisted vaginal births with OdonAssist (median 4, IQR 3-5) than with the Vacca 5-step vacuum technique (median 6, IQR 4-8). [→ Cuerva Marcos Javier 2026](#cuerva-marcos-javier-2026)

## Evidence

### Cuerva Marcos Javier 2026

Cuerva Marcos Javier, Durá Candela, Aramendia Andrés, Cortes Marta, Lopez Francisco, Bartha José Luis. (2026). Vacuum 5-step technique versus OdonAssist: Which is easier to learn for professionals without experience in assisted vaginal births? A simulation study. International Journal of Gynecology & Obstetrics. https://doi.org/10.1002/ijgo.70836

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized crossover simulation study of 12 final-year medical students, each trained in both techniques; Wilcoxon signed-rank test showed "medians of 6 (IQR 4–8) and 4 (IQR 3–5), respectively" with effect size r = 0.72.

> "training in vacuum AVB using the Vacca 5-step technique required a significantly greater number of simulated procedures than training with OdonAssist, with medians of 6 (IQR 4–8) and 4 (IQR 3–5), respectively (Z = −2.51, P = 0.012)"
