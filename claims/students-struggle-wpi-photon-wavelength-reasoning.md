## Evidence

### Ryan Sayer 2017

Ryan Sayer, Alexandru Maries, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Quantum interactive learning tutorial on the double-slit experiment to improve student understanding of quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 010123. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.010123

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Semistructured think-aloud interviews with upper-level undergraduate and graduate students after traditional instruction. The article reports that "none of the students tried to use WPI reasoning" in this context.

> "in our interviews, none of the students tried to use WPI reasoning to answer questions about interference in the DSE setup with the monochromatic lamp"

### Ryan Sayer 2017 (2)

Ryan Sayer, Alexandru Maries, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Quantum interactive learning tutorial on the double-slit experiment to improve student understanding of quantum mechanics. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 010123. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.010123

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

From individual interviews where students predicted the screen pattern when photon wavelength was significantly smaller than slit separation. Students disregarded photon wavelength entirely, focusing only on lamp intensity.

> "Many students claimed that the wavelength of the scattered photons is not important, and that only the intensity of the lamp matters."
