## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Students' epistemologies about experimental physics: Validating the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics. arXiv:1512.06869. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null-condition proxy at CU: students in one middle-division classical mechanics course not concurrently in a lab course (N = 28) scored 17.6±1.6 pre and 17.6±1.5 post; the difference was "not statistically significant". Ten concurrently enrolled students showed a non-significant decrease (22.2 to 21.6 points).

> "The diﬀerence between these scores is not statistically signiﬁcant, suggesting that these students’ epistemologies did not shift over the course of a semester in which they were not enrolled in a laboratory course."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2015 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2015). Students' epistemologies about experimental physics: Validating the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey for Experimental Physics. arXiv:1512.06869. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.010123)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Semester-stability proxy across 16 courses at 11 institutions; the 5 significant cases stemmed from a single anomalous semester, with effect sizes ranging from small (two courses, d = 0.2) to large (three courses, d > 0.5).

> "During the 5 semesters of data collection, we collected matched pre-post data for two or more semesters of 16 diﬀerent courses at 11 institutions. Of these, only 5 courses showed statistically signiﬁcant diﬀerences between the average pre-instruction scores between semesters."
