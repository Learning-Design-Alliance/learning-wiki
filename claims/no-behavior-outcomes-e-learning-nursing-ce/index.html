Effects of E-Learning in a Continuing Education Context on Nursing Care: Systematic Review of Systematic Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed-Studies Reviews. q3 · i? · review · r3

Discussion of the Kirkpatrick level 3 synthesis across the 22 included SRs. The review states "We found no SRs that reported outcomes regarding the translation of the content of e-learning interventions into nurses' practice and behavior", while cautioning this is not evidence of no effect.

"We found no SRs that reported outcomes regarding the translation of the content of e-learning interventions into nurses' practice and behavior (ie, evaluation level 3). This finding does not mean that e-learning had no outcomes on practice."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • E-learning interventions for registered nurses in continuing education are evaluated almost exclusively at Kirkpatrick levels 1 and 2, with no reported outcomes on practice change and almost none on patient outcomes
  • Evidence on transfer to clinical behavior and patient outcomes is scarce: only 2 studies reached Kirkpatrick level 3 and 1 reached level 4
  • E-learning improves nurses' knowledge in continuing education on many topics, but effects are mixed and classroom learning was superior for drug dose calculations — related
  • Nurses generally report satisfaction with e-learning in continuing education, though dissatisfaction arises from technical difficulties and preference for face-to-face formats — related
  • E-learning improves nurses' skills in continuing education, but effects are mixed, with worse defibrillation performance under long-distance learning and lower first-attempt cannulation success with computer-based simulation — related
  • No included SDL studies measured Kirkpatrick Level 4 (patient/system) outcomes — related
  • No included comparative study assessed patient-based clinical outcomes, patient acceptability of care, or staff resource time — related
  • Outcome evaluation of research education programs concentrated at lower levels of the modified Kirkpatrick typology, with few objective or organisational outcomes — related
