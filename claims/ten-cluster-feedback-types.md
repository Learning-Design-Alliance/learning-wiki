# K-means clustering of factor scores identifies 10 distinct types of teacher feedback content, mostly scaffolding-oriented

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Clustering factor scores produced 10 interpretable clusters representing distinct feedback types, with the largest cluster emphasizing examples and explanations. [→ Seiyon M. Lee 2025](#seiyon-m-lee-2025)
`q2 i?` The majority of identified feedback types centered on scaffolding or facilitative strategies such as hinting, rhetorical questions, and worked examples. [→ Seiyon M. Lee 2025 (2)](#seiyon-m-lee-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Seiyon M. Lee 2025

Seiyon M. Lee, Sami Baral, Hongming Chip Li, Li Cheng, Shan Zhang, Carly S. Thorp, Jennifer St. John, Tamisha Thompson, Neil Heffernan, Anthony F. Botelho. (2025). Developing Feedback Taxonomy for Math: A Synergy of Perspectives through Data Mining Methods. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 2. https://osf.io/puhyr/?view_only=514d8534e9be41d9a57ad82e9efb3249

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

K-means clustering on EFA factor scores, with cluster count selected via the silhouette method and manual interpretability checks. The largest cluster (Cluster 9, 26 observations) was defined by Example Provision and Explanation factors; Cluster 10 (16 observations) combined Positive Affirmation with low Specificity. Observation counts are as printed.

> "As shown in Figure 4, we identified 10 clusters, each representing a distinct type of feedback content."

### Seiyon M. Lee 2025 (2)

Seiyon M. Lee, Sami Baral, Hongming Chip Li, Li Cheng, Shan Zhang, Carly S. Thorp, Jennifer St. John, Tamisha Thompson, Neil Heffernan, Anthony F. Botelho. (2025). Developing Feedback Taxonomy for Math: A Synergy of Perspectives through Data Mining Methods. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 17, No 2. https://osf.io/puhyr/?view_only=514d8534e9be41d9a57ad82e9efb3249

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

The authors' characterization of the taxonomy in the RQ3 results section: most identified feedback types serve a facilitative, scaffolding role that prompts students to rethink problem-solving processes. No effect size is reported; this is a descriptive finding.

> "The majority of the feedback centered on various forms of scaffolding, or strategies oriented towards playing a facilitative role, such as "Hinting," "Rhetorical Question," "Show Your Work," and "Worked Example.""
