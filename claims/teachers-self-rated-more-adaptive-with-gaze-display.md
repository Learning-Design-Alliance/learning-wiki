## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Korteland 2024

Korteland, R.-J., Kok, E., Hulshof, C., & van Gog, T. (2024). Teaching through their eyes: effects on optometry teachers’ adaptivity and students’ learning when teachers see students’ gaze. Advances in Health Sciences Education, 29(5), 1735–1748. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-024-10325-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive analysis of teachers' 0–10 self-ratings of adaptive support: gaze-display condition M = 7.33 (SD = 0.89, n = 9) vs control M = 6.71 (SD = 0.76, n = 7). "The other five teachers who taught in both conditions rated their adaptivity 1 or 2 points higher in the eye-tracking condition." No inferential statistics were computed due to the low number of teachers.

> "Note that only one teacher reported a higher level of adaptivity in the control condition, and one teacher rated their adaptivity equal in both conditions. The other five teachers who taught in both conditions rated their adaptivity 1 or 2 points higher in the eye-tracking condition."
