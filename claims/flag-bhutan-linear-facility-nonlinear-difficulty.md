### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

Partially correct response analysis of the Flag of Bhutan MCMR item: the nonlinear diagonal (d) and unknown-function backbone (c) were omitted by 55% and 43% of students respectively, while "Only 24% of students do not choose (a)" the linear perimeter choice.

> "These were not chosen by 55% and 43% of students respectively. Only 24% of students do not choose (a)."

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020 (2)

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Tetrachoric correlation analysis of response patterns on the Flag of Bhutan item found "students considered (c) and (d) together" 66% of the time; the authors interpret this as early signs of goes-like reasoning that do not improve over instruction.

> "Using tetrachoric correlation analysis, we found that students considered (c) and (d) together (either choosing both or declining to choose both) 66% of the time."
