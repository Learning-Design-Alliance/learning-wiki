# Cognitive-motivational techniques that increase both focus support and reward are hypothesized to be most effective for learning

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Techniques fostering both focus support and reward (e.g., intrinsic integration, multimedia, adaptivity, elaborative feedback, guided inquiry) are postulated to be more effective than techniques fostering either factor alone. [→ Eitel 2025](#eitel-2025)

## Evidence

### Eitel 2025

Eitel, A., Krebs, M.-C., Schöne, C. (2025). Introducing the FoRe-Squares Model: Focus Support and Reward as Key Predictors of Effective Technology-Augmented Instruction. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10045-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

This is the model's third hypothesis, a theoretical postulation rather than a tested result: because they link guidance and reward at a fine-grained level, cognitive-motivational techniques (with intrinsic integration as the epitome, where playing a game is training the target skill) are claimed most likely to lead to processing in the effective upper-right square. The review states they "should be prioritized when only a few techniques can be applied."

> "Techniques that foster both focus support and reward are therefore more effective for learning outcomes than techniques to foster either focus support or reward alone."
