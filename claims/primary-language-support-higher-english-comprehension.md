# Primary-language (Spanish) oral instructions yielded significantly higher English reading comprehension than English-only instructions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Grade 1 ELs receiving Spanish oral instructions achieved significantly higher English reading comprehension scores than the English-only instruction group, with both groups growing in English word reading. [→ ev-4](#ev-4)

## Evidence

### ev-4

Taboada Barber, A., Urbano Aguilar, S., Lopez Rodriguez, A., and Pan, Y. (2026, September). Performing Is Not Teaching: What the Evidence Says About Reading Fluency Practices for Emergent Bilinguals. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/316

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

Rodriguez et al. (2012) compared two computer-based Lexia intervention groups over eight weeks; participants in both groups experienced significant growth in English word reading regardless of instruction language, and the Spanish-support group "achieved significantly higher scores in English reading comprehension". No effect size is printed.

> "the group receiving primary language support (Spanish oral instructions) achieved significantly higher scores in English reading comprehension compared to the English oral instruction group"
