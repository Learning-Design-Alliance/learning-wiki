## Subclaims
`q2 i3` Students reporting C, D, or F grades had significantly higher general anxiety than A or B students (Cohen's f = 0.43, a large effect). [→ England Benjamin J. 2017](#england-benjamin-j-2017)
`q2 i2` Biology majors intending to leave the major had higher general anxiety than those continuing (Hedges' g = 0.50). [→ England Benjamin J. 2017 (2)](#england-benjamin-j-2017-2)

## Evidence

### England Benjamin J. 2017

England Benjamin J., Brigati Jennifer R., Schussler Elisabeth E. (2017). Student anxiety in introductory biology classrooms: Perceptions about active learning and persistence in the major. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182506

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

One-way ANOVA of general class anxiety by self-reported letter grade (A: 2.61 ± 0.13 through F: 4.97 ± 0.79) in the survey sample; the printed effect size is Cohen's f = 0.43, a large effect.

> "Those who reported earning a C, D, or F had significantly higher general anxiety than those reporting earning an A or B (p < 0.001, Fig 5A). Cohen's f was calculated as 0.43, a large effect."

### England Benjamin J. 2017 (2)

England Benjamin J., Brigati Jennifer R., Schussler Elisabeth E. (2017). Student anxiety in introductory biology classrooms: Perceptions about active learning and persistence in the major. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0182506

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Two-tailed t-test comparing general anxiety of 129 biology majors by persistence intention (29 leaving vs 100 remaining); the printed effect size is Hedges' g = 0.50, a medium effect.

> "Those who indicated they were not continuing in the major had significantly higher general class anxiety (p = 0.017, Fig 5B). The mean score for those leaving the major was 3.62 ± 0.27; the mean score for those remaining in the major was 2.90 ± 0.14. Effect size (Hedges' g) was calculated as 0.50, indicating a medium effect."
