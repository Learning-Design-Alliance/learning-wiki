## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Removing items 5, 11, 25, and 28 from the test administration reduced the sum of positive distances from the ideal line from 1.69 to 0.85. [→ Bakman 2012](#bakman-2012)

## Evidence

### Bakman 2012

Bakman, Y. (2012). Identifying Unfair Items in Educational Measurement. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1205.3380](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1205.3380)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Numerical result from the real-test example (55 examinees, 40 items). The article explains that unfair items distort students' scores and hence the regression coefficients of fair items, so 'the elimination of unfair items brings the fair item points closer to the ideal line'.

> "Before the unfair items deletion the sum of positive distances from the ideal line was 1.69, after the deletion it became 0.85, which is half of the previous value."
