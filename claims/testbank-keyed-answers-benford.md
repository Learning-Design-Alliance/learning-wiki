## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The keyed responses of the Knight testbank conform acceptably to Benford's Law (MAD = 0.011; SSD = 15), so over 50% of numerical answers are expected to have leading digits 1, 2, or 3. [→ Slepkov AD 2015](#slepkov-ad-2015)

## Evidence

### Slepkov AD 2015

Slepkov AD, Ironside KB, DiBattista D. (2015). Benford's Law: Textbook Exercises and Multiple-Choice Testbanks. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0117972

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of leading digits of 1485 keyed numerical multiple-choice answers supplied with the Knight textbook, using the same rejection protocol as the textbook data. The article reports the printed MAD and SSD conformity values.

> "The keyed-responses (i.e. correct answers) conform acceptably to Benford's Law, yielding a MAD value of 0.011 and an SSD value of 15."
