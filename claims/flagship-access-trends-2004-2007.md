# Flagship minority access improved slightly from 2004 to 2007 while low-income (Pell) enrollment declined

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The flagships' minority-student access ratio increased from 0.44 to 0.46 between 2004 and 2007, though it remains below the 0.50 level of 1992. [→ Haycock 2010](#haycock-2010)
`q2 i?` Pell recipient enrollment at flagships decreased by more than 7,000 students, dropping low-income representation from 22 percent to 20 percent and the low-income access ratio from 0.54 to 0.52. [→ Haycock 2010 (2)](#haycock-2010-2)

## Evidence

### Haycock 2010

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Ed Trust analysis of IPEDS 2004 and 2007 data: minority freshmen rose from 12.1% to 13.4% while minority high school graduates rose from 27.4% to 29.2%, yielding the 0.44-to-0.46 access ratio.

> "the flagships' combined performance on our minority-student access ratio increased by two percentage points, from 0.44 to 0.46, though the trend is downward from 1992, when the ratio was 0.50"

### Haycock 2010 (2)

Haycock, K., Lynch, M., & Engle, J. (2010). Opportunity Adrift: Our Flagship Universities Are Straying From Their Public Mission. The Education Trust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED507851

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The low-income access trend sidebar reports the flagship Pell share falling from 22.1% to 20.4% while the all-colleges share fell from 40.8% to 39.1%, moving the access ratio "from 0.54 to 0.52."

> "This decline of more than 7,000 Pell recipients decreased the representation of low-income students on the flagship campuses from 22 percent to 20 percent."
