# Students with lower baseline performance (below the 50th percentile) showed significantly greater math achievement gains with ASSISTments than matched virtual peers

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Among the 1,626 students below the 50th baseline percentile, treatment students outperformed comparison students by 1.20 points (p = .020), an effect size of 0.10. [→ Feng 2025](#feng-2025)

## Evidence

### Feng 2025

Feng, M., Li, L., Brezack, N., & Schneider, M. (2025). Scaling teachers' professional development for ASSISTments companion report: Implementation analysis of the ASSISTments intervention. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED677784

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis of the quasi-experimental study's 1,626 low-performing students against matched virtual comparison peers. "This difference is statistically significant (p = .020)" with printed effect size 0.10 (small).

> "On average, students with lower levels of performance in the treatment group scored 214.38 compared to 213.18 for the matched comparison students, a difference of 1.20 points. This difference is statistically significant (p = .020) and corresponds to an effect size of 0.10"
