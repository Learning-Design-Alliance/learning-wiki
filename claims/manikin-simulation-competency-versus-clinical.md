## Evidence

### González-Sanz A 2026

González-Sanz A, López-García JC, Sutil-Rodríguez E, Juárez-Vela R, Santos-Sánchez JÁ, Navas-Echazarreta N, Martínez-Sabater A, Sancho-Sanchez C. (2026). Simulation-Based Training for Nursing Students to Improve Patient Safety: Systematic Review. JMIR Nursing. https://doi.org/10.2196/87898

`q2 · i2` · `review · r2`

Quasi-experimental study (n=193) comparing traditional clinical practice, manikin-based high-fidelity simulation, and screen-based virtual simulation; no significant cognitive differences between groups. The review reports the printed effect size=0.52 for communication.

> "students in the manikin-based simulation group achieved significantly higher scores than those in the traditional clinical group in several competency domains measured by the Creighton Competency Evaluation Instrument, including communication (effect size=0.52; P=.04), although no significant differences were observed in the Patient Safety domain."
