### Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn 2010

Kasia Muldner · Winslow Burleson · Brett Van de Sande · Kurt VanLehn (2010). An analysis of students’ gaming behaviors in an intelligent tutoring system: predictors and impacts. User Model User-Adap Inter. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-010-9086-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis of Andes log data collapsing across problems, reporting average percentages for each tutor–student turn-pair type (Table 2). Guessing and lack of planning were next most frequently gamed (5.4% and 5.2%); copying bottom-out hints was rare. On average, 22.5% of turn pairs were gamed.

> "students most frequently took advantage of the opportunity to game when the tutor presented a high-level hint: on average, 18.4% of all student actions corresponded to gaming on these hints; when given such a hint, students gamed on it 58.6% of the time"
