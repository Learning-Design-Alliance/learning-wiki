# Attempted recall during vocabulary study showed no demonstrable superiority over mere reading and re-reading in adult learners

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among 28 adults learning four 20-pair vocabularies, the recall method showed no demonstrable superiority over repetition; face-value results slightly favored re-reading. [→ Edward L. Thorndike 1911](#edward-l-thorndike-1911)

## Evidence

### Edward L. Thorndike 1911

Edward L. Thorndike. (1911). Repetition versus recall in memorizing vocabularies; The effect of continuous exercise and of rest upon difficult mental multiplication. Journal of Educational Psychology, Communications and Discussions. https://archive.org/search?query=Journal+of+Educational+Psychology+Thorndike

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A small controlled experiment in which 28 adult students learned four vocabularies of twenty pairs, two by attentive re-reading and two by reading with attempted recall; the test came immediately after learning with pair order changed. The author reports "no demonstrable superiority in the method involving recall from within".

> "There was no demonstrable superiority in the method involving recall from within so far as possible. Indeed, the results, at their face value, slightly favored the mere reading and re-reading."

### Edward L. Thorndike 1911 (2)

Edward L. Thorndike. (1911). Repetition versus recall in memorizing vocabularies; The effect of continuous exercise and of rest upon difficult mental multiplication. Journal of Educational Psychology, Communications and Discussions. https://archive.org/search?query=Journal+of+Educational+Psychology+Thorndike

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Subgroup analysis of the vocabulary experiment: among the eleven individuals with 17 or fewer correct on the first vocabulary, "the mere read- ing and re-reading required 3 per cent. less time and produced 14 per cent. more words correct" than the recall method.

> "For the eleven individuals whose records in the first vocabulary were 17 correct or less, the mere read- ing and re-reading required 3 per cent. less time and produced 14 per cent. more words correct"
