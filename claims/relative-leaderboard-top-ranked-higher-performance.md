## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Top-ranked learners on a relative leaderboard performed significantly better than middle- and bottom-ranked learners in post-test performance, supporting H3b. [→ Bai 2021](#bai-2021)

## Evidence

### Bai 2021

Bai, Shurui, Khe Foon Hew, Michael Sailer, and Chengyuan Jia. 2021. "From top to bottom: how positions on different types of leaderboard may affect fully online student learning performance, intrinsic motivation, and course engagement." Computers & Education 173: 104297. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104297.

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVA on post-test grades in the 26-student relative leaderboard quasi-experiment was significant, F (2, 23) = 8.71, p = .002; top third M = 91.07 (SD = 7.28), middle M = 76.43 (SD = 10.91), bottom M = 68.75 (SD = 13.56). The "Scheffe post-hoc test suggested that the top-ranked students achieved significantly higher learning performance".

> "A further Scheffe post-hoc test suggested that the top-ranked students achieved significantly higher learning performance than the middle third (p = .032) and bottom-ranked ones (p = .002) did."
