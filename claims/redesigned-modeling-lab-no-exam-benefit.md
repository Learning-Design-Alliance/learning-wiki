# A redesigned lab course emphasizing modeling and data analysis showed no added benefit on lecture-course exam performance: ratio scores did not differ significantly between lab and non-lab students

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who took the redesigned modeling-focused lab and students who did not showed no statistically significant difference in their ratio of lab-related to non-lab-related final exam scores (p = .464). [→ Carl Wieman and N.G. Holmes 2015](#carl-wieman-and-ng-holmes-2015)

## Evidence

### Carl Wieman and N.G. Holmes 2015

Carl Wieman and N.G. Holmes. (2015). Measuring the Impact of Introductory Physics Labs on Learning. [doi:10.1119/1.4931717](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4931717)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 443 final-exam students in an electricity and magnetism course, 126 of whom took the optional redesigned lab. The ratio analysis "showed" "no significant difference between the two groups on their ratio scores", with a 95% CI of [-0.03, 0.07] and no effect size printed.

> "There was no significant difference between the two groups on their ratio scores (average lab-item score over non-lab-item score, see Table 1):, 95% confidence interval of the difference is [-0.03, 0.07]."
