# The association between group dynamics course participation and GPA is substantially smaller for Jewish majority students than for Arab minority students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i0` negligible
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q2 i0` Course participation was associated with higher GPA among Jewish students, but the effect (ηp² = .003) was negligible and substantially smaller than among Arab students (ηp² = .07), with no overlap in CIs. [→ Kahalon 2023](#kahalon-2023)

`q2 i?` A two-way ANCOVA revealed a significant interaction between ethnic group and course participation, such that the association between participation and GPA was stronger for Arabs than for Jewish students. [→ Kahalon 2023](#kahalon-2023)

## Evidence

### Kahalon 2023

Kahalon, R., Shnabel, N., Sharvit, K., Halabi, S., & Wright, S. C. (2023). High-Quality Contact With Fellow Majority Group Students Is Associated With Better Academic Performance of Minority Group Students. Personality & Social Psychology Bulletin, 49(12), 1723–1736. https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672221115943

`q2 · i0`

Same ANCOVA design applied to the Jewish sample (n = 2,179) in Study 1's archival analysis. The article reports "Participation in the group dynamics course was associated with higher GPA, F(1, 2176) = 5.66, p = .017, ηp2 = .003" — a negligible effect size — and notes no CI overlap between the two groups' estimates.

> "Participation in the group dynamics course was associated with higher GPA, F(1, 2176) = 5.66, p = .017, ηp2 = .003, 90% CI[<.01, .01]. There was no overlap in the CIs obtained for the Arab and Jewish samples, pointing to an overall difference between the two populations."
