# In a self-referential framework, LLMs, particularly GPT-4.1, tend to outperform a single operational human rater at identifying relative weaknesses (negative feedback) across several Language and Content aspects

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` GPT-4.1 outperforms both other LLMs and the operational rater on negative feedback for Intelligibility, Complexity, Fluency, Comprehensibility, and Logicality, and attains the highest average F0.5 for negative feedback. [→ Stefano Bannò 2026](#stefano-banno-2026)

## Evidence

### Stefano Bannò 2026

Stefano Bannò, Kate Knill, Mark Gales. (2026). Towards Self-Referential Analytic Assessment: A Profile-Based Approach to L2 Writing Evaluation with LLMs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04298

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Zero-shot LLM scoring experiment on ICNALE GRA essays (N=140) with Rasch-calibrated fair average reference scores; negative feedback F0.5 results in Table 4 show GPT-4.1 "notably outperforms both the other models and OR" on the named aspects, with the highest average F0.5 (33.71).

> "GPT-4.1 notably outperforms both the other models and OR onIntelligibility,Complexity,Fluency,Comprehensibility, andLogicality, indicating a stronger alignment with the reference signal in identifying aspects that deviate negatively compared to the overall average score."
