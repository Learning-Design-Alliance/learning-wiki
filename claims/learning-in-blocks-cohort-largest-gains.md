## Evidence

### Nicy Scaria 2026

Nicy Scaria, Silvester John Joseph Kennedy, and Deepak Subramani. (2026). Learning in Blocks: A Multi Agent Debate Assisted Personalized Adaptive Learning Framework for Language Learning. Preprint, to appear in International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Education (LNAI). [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_27](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_27)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

8-week randomized three-cohort study with 180 CEFR A2 learners (N=60 per cohort); paired t-tests on Week 2 to Week 8 CEFR-aligned scores showed "large to very large effect sizes (Cohen's d= 0.98-1.97)" for the Learning in Blocks cohort.

> "Paired t-tests confirmed statistically significant pre-post gains for Cohort 3 across all dimensions (allp < .001), with large to very large effect sizes (Cohen's d= 0.98-1.97)."

### Nicy Scaria 2026 (2)

Nicy Scaria, Silvester John Joseph Kennedy, and Deepak Subramani. (2026). Learning in Blocks: A Multi Agent Debate Assisted Personalized Adaptive Learning Framework for Language Learning. Preprint, to appear in International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in Education (LNAI). [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_27](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29755-6_27)

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Between-cohort ANOVAs on gain scores for Grammar, Vocabulary, and IC were significant (all p < .001); "Cohort 3 significantly outperformed both 2 and 1 across all three dimensions" while Cohort 2 vs 1 was not significant.

> "Comparisons showed that Cohort 3 significantly outperformed both 2 and 1 across all three dimensions (allp < .001,d= 0.67-1.99), while differences between Cohort 2 and 1werenotsignificant(allp > .05)"
