Comment on “How perception of learning environment predicts male and female students’ grades and motivational outcomes in algebra-based introductory physics courses.” Department of Physics, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.018001](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.018001)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical argument about variable choice in SEMs: the comment states this holds regardless of whether including the mediator improves overall predictive accuracy, and illustrates it with the Fig. 4 model of the original paper.

> "Omitting a variable that mediates gender effects can lead to spurious coefficients for direct gender effects on downstream results"

### M. B. Weissman 2023 (2)

M. B. Weissman. (2023). Comment on “How perception of learning environment predicts male and female students’ grades and motivational outcomes in algebra-based introductory physics courses.” Department of Physics, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.018001](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.018001)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The comment applies the omission critique to two follow-up papers (refs 10, 11) that omit Interest and Self efficacy, the two most gender-asymmetric traits, and notes press coverage reported the results with purely causal policy recommendations.

> "It is possible that if interest were not excluded from the analysis the newer results would also show little evidence of a gender effect via routes other than interest, grades and math SATs, as in this study(3)."
