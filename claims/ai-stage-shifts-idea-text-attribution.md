# AI support shifts perceived attribution of ideas and text toward the AI, most strongly for AI-generated drafts, and an AI draft based on the writer's own outline contributed more ideas than expected (27%).

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` An AI-generated draft based on participants' own outline produced more ideas attributed to AI than expected (27%). [→ draft-ideas](#draft-ideas)

## Evidence

### draft-ideas

Katy Ilonka Gero, Tao Long, Carly Schnitzler, and Paramveer S. Dhillon. 2026. From Planning to Revision: How AI Writing Support at Different Stages Alters Ownership. In Designing Interactive Systems Conference (DIS '26), June 13–17, 2026, Singapore, Singapore. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3800645.3813003

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same experiment's RQ2 results, the ai draft condition's idea attribution (27%) exceeded expectations, indicating "the drafting stage is not necessarily one of simply “filling in” an outline, but a stage in which ideas are developed." The 27% figure is as printed.

> "Additionally, an AI-generated draft based on participants’ own outline producedmoreideas than expected (27%); one might imagine that an outline could contain all the ideas necessary to write an essay but we found this was not necessarily the case."
