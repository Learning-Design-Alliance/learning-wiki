Evidence¶

Corona and Vindel 2012¶

José Luis Jiménez Corona, Isabel Mª Ferrándiz Vindel. (2012). The Use Of Analogies As A Methodology In The School Of Medicine. Contemporary Issues In Education Research – Special Edition 2012 Volume 5, Number 5. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1073140

q3 · i? · causal · r1

Experimental cross-sectional study of 66 second-year Surgery I students taught a wound-healing unit in four four-hour sessions; the experimental group used analogies in small groups of 6. The t test showed no significant differences between the two groups, though results were in favor of the experimental group.

"Despite the results obtained, in favor of the experimental group, the statistical analysis shows that, with the significance levels found and the confidence interval of 95%, there are no significant differences between the two groups: t(64) = - ,56, p>0,05."

Corona and Vindel 2012 (2)¶

José Luis Jiménez Corona, Isabel Mª Ferrándiz Vindel. (2012). The Use Of Analogies As A Methodology In The School Of Medicine. Contemporary Issues In Education Research – Special Edition 2012 Volume 5, Number 5. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1073140

q3 · i? · causal · r1

The same cross-sectional study's final evaluation used clinical cases with multiple choice questions in Moodle, on the same day, time and place for both groups. No student scored at either extreme of the exam range.

"None of the 66 students distributed in both study groups got 50 correct answers, a grade equivalent to 10, or failed the exam (with 30 correct answers)."
