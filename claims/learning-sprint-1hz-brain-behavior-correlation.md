### Fang Wang 2025

Fang Wang, Elizabeth Y. Toomarian, Radhika S. Gosavi, Blair Kaneshiro, Anthony M. Norcia & Bruce D. McCandliss. (2025). Steady-state EEG captures how elementary classroom instruction drives plasticity for novel visual words. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00371-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Brain-behavior correlation analysis in the same EEG sample (N = 28), relating 1 Hz oddball amplitudes for the learned-words contrast to grade-scaled reading assessments. The authors report "Children with smaller response amplitudes to new vocabulary learning had better reading performance (faster RAN and better word decoding)".

> "For the contrast of LFWLearned-LFWUnlearned, we found signiﬁcant correla- tions between EEG amplitudes at 1 Hz and both word decoding ability (measured by Woodcock–Johnson letter word identiﬁcation, WJ-LWID, R2 = .27, p < 0.01) and rapid naming ability for color (RANcolor, R2 =. 3 , pFDR < 0.01, Fig. 3)."

### Fang Wang 2025 (2)

Fang Wang, Elizabeth Y. Toomarian, Radhika S. Gosavi, Blair Kaneshiro, Anthony M. Norcia & Bruce D. McCandliss. (2025). Steady-state EEG captures how elementary classroom instruction drives plasticity for novel visual words. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00371-w

Null brain-behavior results in the same sample: correlations were non-significant at 4 Hz for the learned-words contrast and for both corpus-based word-pseudoword contrasts. The authors note this replicates their earlier finding of little to no association between responses to frequent words versus pseudowords and reading skill.
