## Evidence

### L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane 1912

L. E. Frailey and C. M. Crane. (1912). Correlation of Excellence in Different School Subjects Based on a Study of School Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Educational_Psychology

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational analysis of school grades for 32 pupils at Urbana High School, ranked into four groups in algebra, civics, American history, geometry, Latin and English. The authors report that "there is a correlation of excellence in the various subjects of study," with perfect group correlation illustrated by pupils 4, 5, and 8 in Group I and 27 and 32 in Group IV.

> "Our data, therefore, suggest (1) that there is a correlation of excellence in the various subjects of study; (2) that the most perfect correlation exists among the brightest and among the most stupid children; (3) that the least perfect correlation exists among the pupils of medium ability;"
