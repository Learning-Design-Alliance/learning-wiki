# Students in the RPS introductory physics course persist in engineering at higher one-year rates than lecture-course students, significantly so in spring

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` One-year engineering persistence was higher for RPS students than lecture students in both semesters, significantly in spring (p=0.037) and marginally in fall (p=0.071). [→ Nathan Davis 2024](#nathan-davis-2024)

## Evidence

### Nathan Davis 2024

Nathan Davis, Eric Burkholder. (2024). Real-World Problem-Solving Class is Correlated with Higher Student Persistence in Engineering. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2405.03822)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Observational comparison of institutional major records one year after an introductory calculus-based physics course, N=782 students across two semesters. RPS persistence was 74% (fall) and 90% (spring) versus 64% and 78% for lecture; the spring contrast was "significantly higher" at p = 0.037, fall marginal at p = 0.071.

> "We found that the percentage of students who remained engineering majors one year after completing the RPS course was significantly higher than the lecture-based courses in the Spring semester (p = 0.037; Table 3)"
