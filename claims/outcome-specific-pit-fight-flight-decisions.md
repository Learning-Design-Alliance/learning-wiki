# Threat-conditioned cues selectively bias fight-or-flight action choice toward the response effective against the signaled monster (outcome-specific PIT)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Participants selected fight more than flight when exposed to the CS signaling the attackable monster and flight more than fight for the flight-monster CS, demonstrating an outcome-specific PIT effect in an aversive task. [→ Andreas B. Eder & Vanessa Mitschke 2025](#andreas-b-eder-vanessa-mitschke-2025)

## Evidence

### Andreas B. Eder & Vanessa Mitschke 2025

Andreas B. Eder & Vanessa Mitschke. (2025). Pavlovian to instrumental transfer of control over fight or flight decisions. npj Science of Learning 10:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-025-00331-4

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Experiment 1 (preregistered online task, final N=86 recruited via Prolific) used a 2x2 repeated-measures ANOVA on cumulative response counts with CS+AB and response type as within-subjects factors; the interaction was significant with a large printed effect size, partial eta squared ηp2 = 0.598 (also written ηp² = 0.598). Fight responses (M=119.7, SE=4.95) exceeded flight (M=33.1, SE=4.48) during CS+A, and the reverse pattern held for CS+B.

> "However, the hypothesized interaction effect was signi ﬁcant, F(1, 85) = 126.19, p < 0.001, ηp 2 = 0.598, demonstrating an outcome-speciﬁc PIT effect."
