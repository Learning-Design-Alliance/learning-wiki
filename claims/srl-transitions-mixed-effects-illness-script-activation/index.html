Evidence¶

Cloude 2025¶

Cloude, E. B., Chapman, R., Azevedo, R., Castiglioni, A., LaRochelle, J., Hernandez, C., & Torre, D. (2025). Novice and expert self-regulated learning phase transitions in medical diagnosis: Implications for adaptive and intelligent systems. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09729-4

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Ordinal logistic regression models predicting rubric-scored illness script activation per case (five separate models; predictors were phase frequencies, transitions, complexity, entropy). The authors summarize that transitions were sometimes "beneficial (Case 2, 4)" and other times "detrimental" (Cases 1, 3, 5).

"Interestingly, there were mixed results for the frequency and diversity of SRL phase transitions, where sometimes it was beneficial (Case 2, 4), but other times, detrimental to illness script activation to initiate more frequent and diverse SRL phases (Case 1, 3, 5)."

Cloude 2025 (2)¶

Cloude, E. B., Chapman, R., Azevedo, R., Castiglioni, A., LaRochelle, J., Hernandez, C., & Torre, D. (2025). Novice and expert self-regulated learning phase transitions in medical diagnosis: Implications for adaptive and intelligent systems. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09729-4

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Expertise predictor in the same per-case regression models showed no significant relations with illness script activation (ps > 0.05); a non-significant result with no printed effect size, so equivalence was not tested.

"Across all cases, there were no significant relations between illness script activation and expertise (ps > 0.05). This result did not support H3, where we expected experts to perform better than novices."
