# Higher-expertise students allocate more attention to correct options and less to popular incorrect options, but strong distractors retain attention at all expertise levels

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` A significant expertise × option type interaction (F(12, 4734) = 8.1, d = 0.27) shows attention shifts toward correct options with expertise, while most popular incorrect options still receive more attention than other incorrect options even at the highest expertise. [→ P Klein 2020](#p-klein-2020)

## Evidence

### P Klein 2020

P Klein, A Lichtenberger, S Küchemann, S Becker, M Kekule, J Viiri, C Baadte, A Vaterlaus and J Kuhn. (2020). Visual attention while solving the test of understanding graphs in kinematics: An eye-tracking analysis. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab5f51](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab5f51)

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA (4×5) with option category (correct, most popular, popular, unpopular) as within-subject variable and expertise percentile as between-subject variable. The printed effect size is d = 0.27 for the interaction.

> "Furthermore, a signiﬁcant interaction eﬀect with small eﬀect size was conﬁrmed, F (12, 4734) = 8 .1, p < 10−15, d = 0 .27. The interaction between students’ expertise level and option type is most pronounced for the highest expertise levels."
