Evidence¶

Nelson 2012¶

Nelson, L. P., McMahan, S. K., & Torres, T. (2012). The Impact of a Junior High School Community Intervention Project: Moving Beyond the Testing Juggernaut and Into a Community of Creative Learners. School Community Journal, 22(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ974689

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive school-level attendance and dropout figures reported in the Results section for the intervention school across the three measurement years; the percentages are descriptive and no significance test is reported for them.

"Dropout rates did improve from 2.9% to .2% after year one, and slipped back to .6% after the intervention project ended. Similarly, attendance rates increased slightly after year one from 94.6% to 96.6% and decreased to 93.9% by the end of the project."

Related Claims¶

  • Career Academies show no discernible effects on staying in school: about 10% of academy students dropped out before the end of grade 12 versus about 12% of comparison students (effect size 0.14, not statistically significant or substantively important). — related
  • Career Academies have potentially positive effects on completing school: about 96% of academy students earned a diploma or GED within 8 years of expected graduation versus about 94% of comparison students (effect size 0.27, not statistically significant but substantively important). — related
