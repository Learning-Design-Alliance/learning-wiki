# Stepwise MCQs, which delay answer options by 4 s, enhance vocabulary retention significantly compared to standard MCQs in three of four experiments, measured days after practice.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiments 1, 3a, and 3b, stepwise MCQs enhanced retention significantly compared to standard MCQs on a posttest several days after practice; the effect did not hold in one of the four experiments. [→ van den Broek 2023](#van-den-broek-2023)

## Evidence

### van den Broek 2023

van den Broek, G., Gerritsen, S. L., Oomen, I. T. J., Velthoven, E., van Boxtel, F. H. J., Kester, L., & van Gog, T. (2023). Optimizing multiple-choice questions for retrieval practice: Delayed display of answer alternatives enhances vocabulary learning. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000810

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multi-experiment study (classroom N=75; online N=45, N=77, N=79) comparing standard MCQs with stepwise MCQs in which the word was "shown for 4 s before the translations appeared". Three of four experiments showed a significant retention advantage for stepwise MCQs; no effect size is printed in the available text.

> "In three of the four experiments (Experiments 1, 3a, and 3b), stepwise MCQs enhanced retention significantly compared to standard MCQs, measured by a posttest several days after practice."
