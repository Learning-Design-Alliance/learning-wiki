## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Simulation-group students scored significantly higher than control students on mini-CEX physical examination (p = .014), clinical reasoning and diagnosis (p = .042), and overall clinical competency (p = .016) in real MSK outpatient encounters. ## Evidence

### Yamauchi Kazuyo 2021

Yamauchi Kazuyo, Hagiwara Yoko, Iwakura Nahoko, Kubo Saori, Sato Azusa, Ohtsuru Tadahiko, Okazaki Ken, Okubo Yumiko. (2021). Using peer role-playing to improve students’ clinical skills for musculoskeletal physical examinations. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02742-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of 90 fifth-year female medical students (simulation N = 64, control N = 26) on an orthopaedic clerkship; mini-CEX ratings during real first-visit MSK outpatient encounters compared with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The printed results: "physical examination: p < .05, p = .014, clinical reasoning: p < .05, p = .042, overall: p < .05, p = .016".

> "The mini-CEX scores of physical examination, clinical reasoning and diagnosis, and overall clinical competency were significantly higher in the simulation group than in the control group (physical examination: p < .05, p = .014, clinical reasoning: p < .05, p = .042, overall: p < .05, p = .016)."
