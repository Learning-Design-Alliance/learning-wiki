# Gender differences appear in academic achievement and several cognitive parameters but not in inhibition/interference or social skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Gender significantly influenced academic achievement (p=0.002), working memory (p=0.003; p=0.013), processing speed (p=0.001) and cognitive flexibility (p=0.042), with no significant differences in social skills (p=0.960) or inhibition/interference (p=0.925). ## Evidence

### Cancela J 2019

Cancela J, Burgo H, Sande E. (2019). Physical fitness and executive functions in adolescents: cross-sectional associations with academic achievement. J. Phys. Ther. Sci. 31: 556–562. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpts/31/7/31_17-478/_article

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive analysis with Student's t-test comparing males (n=336) and females (n=374) in the cross-sectional sample. The study reports "gender had a significant influence on AA (p=0.002)" and null differences for social skills and inhibition/interference.

> "gender had a significant influence on AA (p=0.002), working memory (p=0.003; p=0.013), processing speed (p=0.001) and cognitive flexibility (p=0.042), no significant differences being observed in the social skills (p=0.960) or in inhibition/interference (p=0.925)."
