Related Claims¶

  • Peer tutoring benefits extend to the tutors themselves, including confidence, autonomy, leadership, teamwork, and empowerment — related
  • Peer tutoring in clinical simulation is associated with improved technical skills such as laparoscopic tasks, lumbar puncture, and cardiovascular examination — related
  • Reviewed peer-tutoring simulation studies mostly reached only Kirkpatrick Level 2, with no assessment of behavior change or clinical-practice results — related
  • Skills outcomes mostly showed no between-role differences, with active participant advantages limited to delayed technical skills, non-technical affective outcomes, and role-continuity effects — related
  • Favorable learner perception of laparoscopic simulation should not be equated with competence, clinical readiness or transfer of learning — related
  • Most included studies carried a high risk of bias or some concerns, particularly for confounding and outcome measurement, limiting certainty — related
  • Most learner-facing studies of technology-enhanced simulation in laparoscopic training report favorable learner responses, mainly confidence, satisfaction, perceived safety and self-efficacy — related
  • Included studies reported improved comfort, confidence and empathy, but used no standardised outcome measures — related
