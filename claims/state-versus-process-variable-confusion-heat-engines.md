# Upper-division students show lingering confusion about state variables versus process variables, treating heat and work as if they were state functions

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students interchanged d−Q and Q, and one student expressed insufficient understanding of why Q is not written as ΔQ given its position in the first-law equation. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2015](#trevor-i-smith-2015)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2015

Trevor I. Smith, Warren M. Christensen, Donald B. Mountcastle, and John R. Thompson. (2015). Identifying Student Diﬃculties with Entropy, Heat Engines, and the Carnot Cycle. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020116)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Videotaped tutorial observations at School 1 in which students used imprecise notation for heat (dQ rather than d−Q) and questioned the absence of a Δ symbol for heat; the authors interpret this as an upper-division manifestation of previously documented state-versus-process variable difficulties. No effect size is printed.

> "This diﬃculty is another manifestation of PER ﬁndings that many primarily introductory or intermediate-level students reason about work and heat transfer as if they were state functions [16, 17]."
