## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interviewed students perceived generating problems as engaging and real-world relevant, while flagging lack of familiarity with open-ended assumptions and uncertainty about whether the task fosters more learning than well-defined problems. [→ Javier Pulgar 2021](#javier-pulgar-2021)

## Evidence

### Javier Pulgar 2021

Javier Pulgar, Alexis Spina, Carlos Ríos. (2021). Investigating how students collaborate to generate physics problems through structured tasks. arXiv. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010120](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.010120)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Post-session interviews with 4 students (one per group), 15-20 minutes each, transcribed and analyzed for perceived benefits, challenges, and collaboration. Student S3 reported generating problems "allowed her to use her mind more so than algebra-based problems"; S2 doubted whether the activity fosters higher-level learning compared to a good worked example.
