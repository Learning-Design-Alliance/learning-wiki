## Evidence

### Jun-ichiro Yasuda 2025

Jun-ichiro Yasuda, Michael M. Hull, Naohiro Mae, Kentaro Kojima. (2025). Chained computerized adaptive testing for the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/x2rq-z8lm](https://doi.org/10.1103/x2rq-z8lm)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Simulation histograms (Fig. 4) of per-item administration likelihood across nine L = 5 tests in the Linear model (0 <= theta <= 1). Four high-discrimination items would appear on every test a student takes, while, with constraints imposed, "nearly half of the items with low discrimination were never administered."

> "We can see that some of th e items with high discrimination parameters (Q.13, Q25, Q26, and Q30) have a nearly 1 00% probability of being administered."

### Jun-ichiro Yasuda 2025 (2)

Jun-ichiro Yasuda, Michael M. Hull, Naohiro Mae, Kentaro Kojima. (2025). Chained computerized adaptive testing for the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1103/x2rq-z8lm](https://doi.org/10.1103/x2rq-z8lm)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of the FCI item bank by content subgroup shows limited difficulty variability where subgroups are small; for example, the Second law subgroup's difficulty parameters are 0.471 (Q.21), -0.125 (Q.22), and 0.567 (Q.26), which the authors say may be insufficient to estimate abilities accurately and precisely.

> "Since three of the content areas (KN, SL, and TL) con tain ﬁve or fewer items, the range of diﬃculty parameters is limited."
