## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After students completed both e-learning modules, including the virtual dental clinic, use of complex medical jargon in OSCE role-play (examiners acting as simulated patients) decreased and straightforward communication increased (both p < 0.001); with no control group, the change cannot be attributed to either module. ## Evidence

### S. Y. Math 2026

S. Y. Math, O. S. Sultan, M. F. B. Z. Anuar, and C. Pacheco‐Pereira, "Interactive E‐Learning Module: Enhancing Panoramic Radiograph Interpretation Skills of Dental Students." Journal of Dental Education 90, no. 1 (2026): 136–146. https://doi.org/10.1002/jdd.13923

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

OSCE pre-post comparison in the 45-student cohort, with examiners acting as simulated patients and no control group; students completed both e-learning modules: complex jargon use fell from 26 (57.8%) to 7 (15.5%) and straightforward language rose from 19 (42.2%) to 38 (84.4%). Only p-values printed; communication skills scores on DPR 1 also improved (p < 0.01).

> "Specifically, the use of complex medical jargon decreased, and straightforward communication increased (both p < 0.001) (Table 2)."
