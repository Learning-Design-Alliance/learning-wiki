# Cadaveric simulation research for surgical trainees comprises 51 studies of 2002 trainees across 69 interventions, with 67 per cent methodologically weak (MERSQI below 10·7)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The review included 51 studies involving 2002 surgical trainees across 69 cadaveric training interventions, and 67 per cent had MERSQI scores below 10·7, indicating weak methodology. [→ James 2019](#james-2019)

## Evidence

### James 2019

James, H. K., Chapman, A. W., Pattison, G. T. R., Griffin, D. R. and Fisher, J. D. (2019). Systematic review of the current status of cadaveric simulation for surgical training. The British Journal of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1002/bjs.11325

`q3 · i?`

Systematic review (PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO-registered) searching databases to January 2019. The review reports "51 studies were included, involving 2002 surgical trainees across 69 cadaveric training interventions", with 22 subjective, 5 knowledge and 23 objective-behaviour studies.

> "A total of 51 studies were included, involving 2002 surgical trainees across 69 cadaveric training interventions. Of these, 22 assessed the impact of the cadaveric training intervention using only subjective measures, five measured impact by change in learner knowledge, and 23 used objective tools to assess change in learner behaviour after training."
