# When Tier 3 inadequate responders are compared with higher-responding peers, treatment groups show large negative effects, indicating they often fail to catch up

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In studies comparing Tier 3 inadequate responders with students who responded adequately to Tier 2, treatment groups scored below controls on reading measures (e.g., DIBELS ORF d = −1.74 in Vaughn et al., 2009). [→ Austin 2017](#austin-2017)

## Evidence

### Austin 2017

Austin, C. R., Vaughn, S., & McClelland, A. M. (2017). Intensive Reading Interventions for Inadequate Responders in Grades K–3: A Synthesis. Learning Disability Quarterly, 40(4), 191–210. https://doi.org/10.1177/0731948717714446

`q3 · i3` · `review · r2`

Study in Table 5 of the synthesis: grade 2 inadequate responders received a small-group (2–4 students) Tier 3 intervention targeting phonics, vocabulary, fluency, passage reading, and comprehension for 50 min, 5×/week for about 26 weeks; the comparison was adequate responders receiving typical school instruction. All printed effects favored control.

> "Treatment < Control: WRMT-R Word ID: d = −1.06; WRMT-R Word Attack: d = −0.11; WRMT-R Passage Comprehension: d = −1.19; DIBELS ORF: d = −1.74; PPVT-III: d = −0.83"
