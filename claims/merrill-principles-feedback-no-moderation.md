# Merrill's instructional design principles and feedback features did not significantly moderate the effects of ICT-supported writing interventions

## Evidence

### López 2026

López, P., Fidalgo, R., Álvarez-Moreno, C., Díaz-Tejedor, R., van den Bergh, H., & Rijlaarsdam, G. (2026). Compositional Analysis of Writing Instructional Practices Performed with Digital Tools: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10153-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

One-by-one meta-regression moderator analyses of Merrill's five principles across the 53-effect corpus; none was significant, and the application principle could not be estimated for lack of variation.

> "None of the five binary coded principles exhibited statistically significant moderation (See Supplementary Material 6. Table A1); the application principle could not be estimated due to a lack of variation."

### López 2026 (2)

López, P., Fidalgo, R., Álvarez-Moreno, C., Díaz-Tejedor, R., van den Bergh, H., & Rijlaarsdam, G. (2026). Compositional Analysis of Writing Instructional Practices Performed with Digital Tools: A Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10153-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analyses of the six coded feedback types (Hattie & Timperley and Shute classifications) across the corpus; all p-values were at least .417, so no feedback feature moderated effects.

> "None of the feedback indicators showed statistically significant moderation (all p ≥ .417; See Supplementary Material 6. Table A3)."
