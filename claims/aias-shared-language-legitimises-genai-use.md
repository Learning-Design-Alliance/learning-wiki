# Academic staff experienced the AIAS as a shared language that legitimised GenAI use in teaching and assessment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Faculty across both institutions described the AIAS as lending legitimacy to bringing GenAI into teaching and assessment, providing bounded permission and reassurance to experiment. [→ Perkins 2026](#perkins-2026)

## Evidence

### Perkins 2026

Perkins, M., Postma, D., Roe, J., Sisay, S., & Holdcroft, C. (2026). 'A bit of chaos and madness': The AI Assessment Scale and the work of assessment reform. Preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26729

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative focus-group study with 30 academic staff at two universities (Vietnam and UK), analysed by hybrid thematic analysis. Staff reported the scale gave reassurance, with one noting it was "a framework that legitimised these emerging practices"; some staff, however, credited changing practice to GenAI generally rather than the scale.

> "Across both institutions, faculty engagement with GenAI was characterised by curiosity and experimentation, with faculty describing the AIAS as a framework that legitimised these emerging practices in higher education."
