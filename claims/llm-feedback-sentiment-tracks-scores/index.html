Evidence¶

Jerin George Mathew 2026¶

Jerin George Mathew, Sumayya Taher, Anindita Kundu, Denilson Barbosa. (2026). LLMs Do Not Grade Essays Like Humans. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23714 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2603.23714

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Aspect-based sentiment analysis (DeBERTa-v3 ABSA, confidence above 0.9) of trait-specific praise and criticism in LLM feedback, aggregated by assigned score for DREsS and ASAP Task 1 (Figures 11 and 12). Positive mentions frequently exceeded 80-100% for high-scoring essays on DREsS.

"essays receiving low scores are typically associated with a high proportion of negative mentions across traits, while essays receiving higher scores contain increasingly positive feedback."

Related Claims¶

  • Different LLMs rely on different subsets of rubric traits when assigning essay scores — related
  • LLM feedback for students marked by race, language, disability, gender, or motivation shows positive feedback bias and feedback withholding bias — more praise and less substantive critique than for unmarked counterparts — related
  • Motivation prompts shift feedback tone: unmotivated students receive enthusiastic, collaborative encouragement while motivated students receive direct, higher-level critique — related
  • Gender prompts shift feedback stance: female students receive affective, first-person, empathy-oriented feedback while male students receive objective, task-oriented critique — related
  • LLMs overrate short underdeveloped essays and over-penalize minor language errors in well-developed essays — related
  • Both fine-tuned models show regression toward the mean in score-wise bias, and error rises monotonically above score 3.0, with high scores hardest to predict — related
  • Solution sentiment showed no meaningful individual relationship with LLM accuracy despite a small statistically significant collective trend — related
