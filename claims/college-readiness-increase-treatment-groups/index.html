Evidence¶

Dye 2013¶

Dye, J., Cheatham, T., Rowell, G. H., Barlow, A. T., & Carlton, R. (2013). The Impact of Modeling Instruction within the Inverted Curriculum on Student Achievement in Science. Electronic Journal of Science Education, 17(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1188400

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Analysis of the same 808 students comparing the percentage meeting the science college readiness benchmark on PLAN (score 21) versus ACT (score 24) by instructional context; percentages are descriptive with no effect size or significance test reported.

"Treatment 1 (inverting the curriculum alone) demonstrated an increase in readiness of 17.8% and Treatment 2 (modeling instruction within the inverted curr iculum) demonstrated an increase of 20.8% , each more than seven times the increase in the baseline group ."

Related Claims¶

  • Students close to college ready in science on the PLAN reached college ready on the ACT at 46% and 52% in the treatment groups versus one-third in the baseline group — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Students off-target in science on the PLAN moved to college ready on the ACT at 18% and 21% in the treatment groups versus 8% in the traditional group — related
  • PLAN-to-ACT science score gains were larger under both non-traditional contexts (4.1 and 4.3 points) than under the traditional context (2.8 points) — related
  • Students taught with modeling instruction within the inverted curriculum scored highest on the ACT science subscore (M=24.3) compared with the inverted-curriculum-alone (M=23.7) and traditional (M=23.1) contexts — related
