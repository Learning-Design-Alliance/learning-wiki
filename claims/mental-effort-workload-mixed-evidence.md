# Evidence on the mental effort–workload relation is mixed: AIC prefers quadratic, BIC prefers linear

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the reanalysis of Aldekhyl et al. (2018) data, AIC preferred a quadratic and BIC a linear model for the mental effort–workload relation; the quadratic term was statistically significant at α = 0.05 but the evidence was not strong. [→ Jimmie Leppink & Patricia Pérez-Fuster 2019](#jimmie-leppink-patricia-perez-fuster-2019)

## Evidence

### Jimmie Leppink & Patricia Pérez-Fuster 2019

Jimmie Leppink & Patricia Pérez-Fuster. (2019). Mental Effort, Workload, Time on Task, and Certainty: Beyond Linear Models. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-09460-2

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Reanalysis of repeated-measurement data (4 ultrasound scenarios per participant, 29 participants) from Aldekhyl et al. (2018); the criteria disagreed, and the quadratic term was significant: "F1, 106.565 = 4.142, p = 0.044, B = 0.576".

> "The only study for which AIC and BIC indicate a different preference is Aldekhyl et al. (2018): AIC prefers Model 2 (quadratic: H 2) while BIC prefers Model 1 (linear: H 1). The difference in R2M between these two models is about 0.006 and the difference in df is 1. In Model 2, we find for the quadratic term: F1, 106.565 = 4.142, p = 0.044, B = 0.576, and 95% CI = [0.021; 1.131]."
