## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Guzel 2016

Guzel, S. & Aydin, S. (2016). The effect of second life on speaking achievement. Global Journal of Foreign Language Teaching. 6(4), 236-245. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED573975

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired-sample t-tests on control group interview scores (Kanatlar 2005 scale) showed the total mean rose from 58.91 to 72.83; the article reports "significance value for total scores in pre-test and post-test was calculated as .00".

> "total mean score for pre-test was 58.91 while post-test mean score was 72.83"

### Guzel 2016 (2)

Guzel, S. & Aydin, S. (2016). The effect of second life on speaking achievement. Global Journal of Foreign Language Teaching. 6(4), 236-245. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED573975

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired comparisons for the control group found significant pre-post differences in grammar, intelligibility, fluency and task achievement (Sig. .00); vocabulary was marginal at .05, which the authors treat as non-significant.

> "all areas except for vocabulary section, the significance values were calculated as .00. Significance values for grammar, intelligibility, fluency, and task achievement were computed as .00 in value. However, significance value for vocabulary was found .05"
