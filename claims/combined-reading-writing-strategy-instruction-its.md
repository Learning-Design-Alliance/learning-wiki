# Combining reading comprehension and writing strategy instruction in an ITS is significantly more effective than either strategy alone

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For source-based writing, ITS strategy instruction combining reading comprehension and writing strategies produced significantly better outcomes on both content quality and writing quality than either strategy alone. [→ Passonneau 2017](#passonneau-2017)

## Evidence

### Passonneau 2017

Passonneau, R. J., McNamara, D., Muresan, S., & Perin, D. (2017). Preface: Special Issue on Multidisciplinary Approaches to AI and Education for Reading and Writing. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-017-0158-8

`q2 · i?` · `review · r1`

The editors report, citing Weston-Sementelli et al., a comparison of reading-comprehension-only, writing-only, and combined strategy instruction for source-based writing; "the combination is found to be significantly more effective than either strategy alone". No effect size is printed.

> "Both content quality and writing quality are evaluated, and the combination is found to be significantly more effective than either strategy alone."
