## Evidence

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2011

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2011). How numbers help students solve physics problems. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1112.3229)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview analysis of the Tortoise and Hare symbolic problem: five students produced the incorrect result a = v²/(2L). When questioned, two said v was the Tortoise's velocity and three said the Hare's; the two displayed a dynamic interpretation, treating symbols in the final answer as general variables.

> "There were ﬁve students in the sample who made this error. Those ﬁve students were questioned in an attempt to determine if they had a consistent interpretation of the symbol. Two students said that the symbol represented the velocity of the Tortoise, and three said that"

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Unpacking Symbolic Equations in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Speak-aloud interview study; the author reports the most common Tortoise and Hare error, in which v is given as the velocity of the Tortoise but used as if it were the Hare's velocity at the finish line, made by five students.

> "The most common error was to use the equation v2f = v2i + 2a∆x, to get the incorrect result a = v2/(2L). This is incorrect because the symbol v is given as the velocity of the Tortoise, but is used as if it were the velocity of the Hare when it reaches the finish line. There were five students who were observed to make this error."

### Eugene T. Torigoe 2015 (2)

Eugene T. Torigoe. (2015). Unpacking Symbolic Equations in Introductory Physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1508.00535)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Follow-up questioning of the five students who made the a = v2/(2L) error: two of the five said v represented the Tortoise's velocity yet switched interpretations within a minute, showing no firm connection between symbol and physical system.

> "These two students did not seem to hold any firm connection between the symbol v and any particular property of the physical system. They treated the equation in an overly general way."
