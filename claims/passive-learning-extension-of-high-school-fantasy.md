## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Physics is learned by practice more than studying: productive learning time involves actively working problems, focused attention, and hands-on laboratory engagement rather than passive reading or borrowing notes. [→ Michael Courtney 2006](#michael-courtney-2006)

## Evidence

### Michael Courtney 2006

Michael Courtney, Norm Althausen, and Amy Courtney. (2006). Five Frequently Fatal Freshmen Physics Fantasies. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0605152](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0605152)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

Theoretical argument asserting that active engagement, not passive study, produces Physics learning. The article offers no data; the claim rests on the authors' teaching experience and the analogy that cramming mirrors the miracle finish on a shorter time scale.

> "Physics is learned by practice more than studying. The most productive learning time is spent with the pencil moving, with the thoughts fully focused on the classroom discussion, or with the mind and hands fully engaged in the laboratory."
