# Students rating tutors as excellent gave significantly higher scores on all five learning approaches than students rating tutors as sufficient

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Excellent-ranked tutors received significantly higher learning-approach scores than sufficient-ranked tutors across all five categories (all p < 0.001). [→ Barnawi Ahlam 2024](#barnawi-ahlam-2024)

## Evidence

### Barnawi Ahlam 2024

Barnawi Ahlam, Sonbol Ahmed M., Al-Shawwa Lana, Abulaban Alwalla, Asiri Khalil, Bagasi Abdulaziz, Alafari Reem, Alamoudi Aliaa Amr. (2024). Employing students' evaluations and tutors' perceptions to evaluate a faculty development program on problem-based learning at the Faculty of Medicine, King Abdulaziz University. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05662-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Independent and Welch's t-tests comparing learning-approach scores between the 150 sufficient-ranked and 134 excellent-ranked tutor ratings. The article prints means and p-values but no effect sizes.

> "significantly higher excellent scores than sufficient scores were reported for the constructive/active learning (excellent: 3.87 ± 0.7 vs. sufficient: 2.95 ± 0.9), self-directed learning (3.93 ± 0.7 vs. 3.12 ± 1.0), contextual learning (3.80 ± 0.7 vs. 2.95 ± 0.9), collaborative learning (3.59 ± 0.9 vs. 2.66 ± 1.0), and intra-personal behavior (3.56 ± 0.8 vs. 2.47 ± 1.0) approaches."
