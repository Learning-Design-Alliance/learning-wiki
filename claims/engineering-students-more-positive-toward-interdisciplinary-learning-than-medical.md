# Engineering students are significantly more positive than medical students toward interdisciplinary learning and teams

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Engineering students were significantly more likely than medical students to agree that interdisciplinary learning is indispensable for their professional development and that learning in interdisciplinary teams is preferred over learning with students from their own group. `q2 i?` Engineering students were significantly more likely than medical students to favour working in interdisciplinary groups and team formations aimed at fostering innovation and creativity. [→ Spoelstra Howard 2014](#spoelstra-howard-2014)

## Evidence

### Spoelstra Howard 2014

Spoelstra Howard, Stoyanov Slavi, Burgoyne Louise, Bennett Deirdre, Sweeney Catherine, Drachsler Hendrik, Vanderperren Katrien, Van Huffel Sabine, McSweeney John, Shorten George, O'Flynn Siun, Cantillon-Murphy Padraig, O'Tuathaigh Colm. (2014). Convergence and translation: attitudes to inter-professional learning and teaching of creative problem-solving among medical and engineering students and staff. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-14

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey comparison of 633 student respondents in medicine and engineering at two European universities using Mann-Whitney U tests on Likert responses. Engineering students agreed more with interdisciplinary learning statements, e.g. "U = 42714, z =4.19, p < 0.0001". No effect sizes were printed.

> "Compared to medical students, engineering students were significantly more likely to agree or strongly agree with the following statements: 'Interdisciplinary learning is indispensable for my professional development' [U = 42714, z =4.19, p < 0.0001]; 'Learning in interdisciplinary teams is to be preferred over learning with students from my own group' [U = 43284, z =3.98, p < 0.0001]"
