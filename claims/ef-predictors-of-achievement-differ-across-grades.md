# The set of executive-function and intelligence predictors of academic achievement differs across Grades 2, 4, and 6, with EF contributions appearing cumulative as students get older

## Evidence

### Sofia Magalhães 2020

Sofia Magalhães, Luísa Carneiro, Teresa Limpo & Marisa Filipe (2020). Executive functions predict literacy and mathematics achievements: The unique contribution of cognitive flexibility in grades 2, 4, and 6. Child Neuropsychology, 26(7), 934-952. https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2020.1740188

`q2 · i?`

Cross-sectional regression results across three grade cohorts (243, 284, and 203 students) show shifting predictor sets: "In Grade 2, ﬂuid intelligence and inhibitory control predicted literacy achievements, while ﬂuid intelligence, attention, and inhibitory control predicted mathematics outcomes." Grade 4 added working memory and planning; Grade 6 added attention for literacy.
