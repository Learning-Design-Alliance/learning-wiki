## Evidence

### Postiglione A. 2023

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Synchronous Zoom sessions with school class groups, alternating Kahoot! with explanations and comments. Participation counts are as printed; the engagement statement is the authors' interpretation, not a tested result.

> "It is worth noting that in this case the use of Kahoot! allowed us to involve the whole class, making even the most shy students actively participate. In fact, out of 173 participants, 152 students took part in Kahoot!."

### Postiglione A. 2023 (2)

Postiglione A., De Angelis I., Di Blasi M. (2023). AstroGarden of Roma Tre University: from presence to online tour. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2301.03842)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Authors' summary in the Discussion and conclusions of the online modality across all their audiences; this is their interpretation of engagement, not a measured outcome.

> "we managed to guarantee a great interaction with the audience also thanks to the use of Kahoot!, which kept the attention high and allowed us to actively involve the great majority of participants, even when it came to high school or university students, who were already worn out by distance learning."
