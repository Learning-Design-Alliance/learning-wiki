## Evidence

### Aura‐Tormos Juan Ignacio 2025

Aura‐Tormos Juan Ignacio, Vello‐Ribes M. Angeles, Frechina‐Borras Neus, Casaña‐Ruiz M. Dolores, Catalá‐pizarro Montserrat. (2025). Evaluating Haptic Virtual Reality Simulator for Pulpotomy Training in Primary Teeth: A Pilot Study on Dental Student Pespectives. European Journal of Dental Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/eje.70013

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive Likert-survey results from a randomised controlled pilot trial comparing HVRS and LAB groups (n = 12 each), reported as proportions of agree/strongly agree responses in Figure 2; the study found "predominantly positive responses in the HVRS group, with over 80% of participants".

> "items SP2, SP3 and SP4, which addressed visual realism, tissue differentiation and tactile feedback, received predominantly positive responses in the HVRS group, with over 80% of participants selecting ‘agree’ or ‘strongly agree’."

### Aura‐Tormos Juan Ignacio 2025 (2)

Aura‐Tormos Juan Ignacio, Vello‐Ribes M. Angeles, Frechina‐Borras Neus, Casaña‐Ruiz M. Dolores, Catalá‐pizarro Montserrat. (2025). Evaluating Haptic Virtual Reality Simulator for Pulpotomy Training in Primary Teeth: A Pilot Study on Dental Student Pespectives. European Journal of Dental Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/eje.70013

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive comparison of SP6 (fine motor skills) responses between HVRS and LAB groups in the same pilot survey; the HVRS group showed "minimal response variability" with nearly all positive responses versus wider, partly negative LAB-group distributions.

> "Item SP6 showed minimal response variability in the HVRS group, with nearly all participants responding positively. In contrast, responses in the LAB group were more widely distributed, including a substantial proportion of negative ratings."
