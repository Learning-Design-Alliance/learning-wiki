## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For grades IV-VI (peak r = 0.68) and VII-VIII (peak r = 0.49), arithmetic correlations with the intelligence tests are highest at the second verbalness step and drop when tests become more verbal than arithmetic generally is. [→ Arthur I. Gates 1923](#arthur-i-gates-1923)

## Evidence

### Arthur I. Gates 1923

Arthur I. Gates. (1923). The correlations of achievement in school subjects with intelligence tests and other variables (Continued). The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1923-02675-001

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis across the four verbalness sections (Tables III and IV) shows arithmetic correlations peaking at the somewhat-verbal step: 0.68 for grades IV-VI and 0.49 for VII-VIII. The author states "the second step on the verbal scale gives the highest correlation with arithmetic."

> "In both groups of subjects, the second step on the verbal scale gives the highest correlation with arithmetic. Most arithmetical problems are judged to be of about that degree of verbalness."
