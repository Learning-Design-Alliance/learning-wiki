## Evidence

### Beck Amanda P 2025

Beck Amanda P. (2025). Assessment of first-year medical student perceptions in the development of self-directed learning skills in a single medical school course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06890-9

`q3 · i3` · `design · r2`

Paired t-tests on the 89 paired respondents for each of the five rubric items; the article reports significant increases for Items 1, 2, 3 and 5, e.g. "a statistically significant increase in the self-assessed score for competency" for assessing credibility of information sources, with a printed correlation of r = 0.386 for that item.

> "For Item 2, the skill of “Assess the credibility of information sources,” there was a positive correlation (r = 0.386) between the midpoint and endpoint scores and a statistically significant increase in the self-assessed score for competency from the midpoint (M = 3.19) to the endpoint of the CRE course (M = 3.60), (t(88) = 4.991, p < 0.001)"

### Beck Amanda P 2025 (2)

Beck Amanda P. (2025). Assessment of first-year medical student perceptions in the development of self-directed learning skills in a single medical school course. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06890-9

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Paired t-test for Item 4 (critically think) on the same 89 paired respondents; the article reports no statistically significant increase, with means rising descriptively from 3.33 to 3.48. No effect size is printed for this null result.

> "The results did not show a statistically significant increase in self-assessed competency in this skill from the midpoint (M = 3.33) to the endpoint of the CRE course (M = 3.48), (t(88) = 1.864, p > 0.05)"
