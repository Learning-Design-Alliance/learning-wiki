# When assigned as ungraded self-study tools in large courses, only about 60% or less of students reported using the tutorials, and significant paired-problem improvements appeared for only some tutorials

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Only approximately 60% or less of students in both course types reported working through each tutorial. [→ DeVore 2019](#devore-2019)
`q2 i?` Two of three tutorials produced statistically significant paired-problem improvement in the algebra-based group and one in the calculus-based group; only angular momentum was significant in both, with a Hake gain of 0.369 in calculus-based classes. [→ DeVore 2019 (2)](#devore-2019-2)

## Evidence

### DeVore 2019

DeVore, S., Marshman, E., & Singh, C. (2019). The challenge of engaging all students via self-paced interactive e-learning tutorials for introductory physics. [doi:10.3389/fict.2018.00003](https://doi.org/10.3389/fict.2018.00003)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Self-report survey embedded atop the paired-problem quizzes in two large courses (~385 algebra-based, ~350 calculus-based students). "Only approximately 60% or less of the students reported" working through each tutorial; no time tracking was possible.

> "Only approximately 60% or less of the students reported t hat they worked through each of the tutorials in both the calculus -based and algebra-based classes."

### DeVore 2019 (2)

DeVore, S., Marshman, E., & Singh, C. (2019). The challenge of engaging all students via self-paced interactive e-learning tutorials for introductory physics. [doi:10.3389/fict.2018.00003](https://doi.org/10.3389/fict.2018.00003)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Comparison of tutorial vs non-tutorial groups on recitation quizzes by self-reported tutorial use (Table 3). "statistically significant improvement for both the algebra-based and calculus-based groups" held only for angular momentum; algebra-based p-values printed were 0.001 and 0.003, calculus-based <0.001.

> "The only e -learning tutorial that resulted in a statistically significant improvement for both the algebra-based and calculus-based groups is the conservation of angular momentum tutorial."
