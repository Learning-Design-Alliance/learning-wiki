# Deictic gestures of a human lecturer in an educational video enhance retention performance compared with no gestures and with beat gestures

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In Experiment 1, students in the deictic condition achieved higher retention scores than students in the control and beat conditions (ηp2 = .10). [→ Maik Beege 2020](#maik-beege-2020)

## Evidence

### Maik Beege 2020

Maik Beege, Manuel Ninaus, Sascha Schneider, Steve Nebel, Julia Schlemmel, Jasmin Weidenmüller, Korbinian Moeller, Günter Daniel Rey. (2020). Investigating the effects of beat gestures and deictic gestures of a lecturer in educational videos. Psychology of learning with digital media, Chemnitz University of Technology. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103955

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomized one-factorial between-participant experiment with 108 university students watching a video about weather phenomena under no-gestures, beat, or deictic gesture conditions. Deictic condition retention exceeded both "students in the control condition (Mdiff = 1.33; p = .01)" and the beat condition (Mdiff = 1.27; p = .02); printed effect size ηp2 = .10.

> "Univariate ANOVAs revealed a significant effect with a medium effect size for retention; F(2, 105) = 5.74, p = .004, ηp2 = .10. Sidak-corrected post-hoc analyses revealed that students in the deictic condition achieved higher retention scores than students in the control condition (Mdiff = 1.33; p = .01) and in the beat condition (Mdiff = 1.27; p = .02)."
