# Learners with high prior knowledge benefit more from segmenting in retention performance than learners with no or low prior knowledge

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Prior knowledge moderates the segmenting effect for retention: learners with high prior knowledge benefited more from segmenting than learners with no or low prior knowledge. [→ Rey 2019](#rey-2019)

## Evidence

### Rey 2019

Rey, G.D., Beege, M., Nebel, S., Wirzberger, M., Schmitt, T.H., & Schneider, S. (2019). A Meta-analysis of the Segmenting Effect. Educational Psychology Review, 31, 389–419. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderation analysis within the meta-analysis of 56 investigations found that "learners with high prior knowledge benefitted more from segmenting instructional material than learners with no or low prior knowledge in terms of retention performance." No moderator effect size is printed.

> "Moderation analyses indicate that learners with high prior knowledge benefitted more from segmenting instructional material than learners with no or low prior knowledge in terms of retention performance."
