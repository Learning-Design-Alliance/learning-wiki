## Evidence

### Mavragani A 2025

Mavragani A, Chan S, Doyle AJ, Weisman D, Sugarman A, Huang YM, Gelberg L, Ganz PA, Comulada WS. (2025). Development of a GPT-4–Powered Virtual Simulated Patient and Communication Training Platform for Medical Students to Practice Discussing Abnormal Mammogram Results With Patients: Multiphase Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/65670

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Formative-phase qualitative study: 30-minute Zoom interviews with 5 medical students, 5 PCPs, and 5 breast cancer survivors, transcribed and thematically analyzed with Microsoft Copilot followed by team consensus. Themes validated the need for additional communication skills training beyond current capacity.

> "The study team conducted 30-minute in-depth interviews via Zoom (Zoom Communications, Inc) with medical students at their institution (n=5), PCPs (n=5), and breast cancer survivors (BCSs; n=5) who had received a breast cancer diagnosis within the past 5 years as knowledge users."

### Mavragani A 2025 (2)

Mavragani A, Chan S, Doyle AJ, Weisman D, Sugarman A, Huang YM, Gelberg L, Ganz PA, Comulada WS. (2025). Development of a GPT-4–Powered Virtual Simulated Patient and Communication Training Platform for Medical Students to Practice Discussing Abnormal Mammogram Results With Patients: Multiphase Study. JMIR Formative Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/65670

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Formative-phase interview findings on the patient journey through breast cancer screening. PCPs reported limited early contact, portal or telephone communication for results, and in-person or video visits for more serious news; these findings, plus SPIKES-aligned themes, dictated the simulated telephone-call scenario.

> "We also discovered that PCPs typically communicated with patients via a message in the electronic health record patient portal or a telephone call during the breast cancer screening process."
