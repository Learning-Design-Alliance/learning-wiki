# Evidence for the task distraction hypothesis about normative performance goals is lacking

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Studies using normative goals have not supported the claim that performance goals distract students from task demands; performance-avoidance goals instead interfere with task focus. [→ Senko 2011](#senko-2011)

## Evidence

### Senko 2011

Senko, C., Hulleman, C. S., & Harackiewicz, J. M. (2011). Achievement Goal Theory at the Crossroads: Old Controversies, Current Challenges, and New Directions. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.538646

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of anxiety, self-report distraction, working memory, and stereotype threat studies concludes "there is a dearth of evidence for the task distraction hypothesis" for normative goals. No effect size is printed.

> "To summarize, there is a dearth of evidence for the task distraction hypothesis. Normative goals, like mastery goals, do not appear to distract students to any significant degree and in some cases actually improve task focus."
