# VP exam grades correlate only minimally with bedside oral exam grades (r = 0.101), suggesting the two modalities assess different skills

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Pearson correlation between VP and oral exam grades across 586 students was r = 0.101, statistically significant (p < 0.05) but minimal; yearly correlations ranged 0–0.24 and were significant in only two of five years. [→ Oliven Arie 2021](#oliven-arie-2021)

## Evidence

### Oliven Arie 2021

Oliven Arie, Nave Rachel, Baruch Adam. (2021). Long experience with a web-based, interactive, conversational virtual patient case simulation for medical students' evaluation: comparison with oral examination. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2021.1946896

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of individual final grades of 586 students who took both exams one day apart at the end of the clerkship over five years. The printed effect size is r = 0.101 (small, i = 1); the article states there was "only a minimal relationship".

> "Pearson's correlation coefficient (r) of this relationship was 0.101, indicating that, although statistically significant (p < 0.05), there was only a minimal relationship between the grades obtained in these two modalities of assessment."
