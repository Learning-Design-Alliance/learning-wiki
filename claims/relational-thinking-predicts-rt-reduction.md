## Evidence

### Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge 2018

Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge. (2018). Eye gaze patterns reveal how reasoning skills improve with experience. npj Science of Learning 3:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0035-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Bayesian regression across the full sample with LSAT group as a nuisance variable and RT change as the dependent variable; the article reports "β = 0.72" and ~23% of variance explained by the relational thinking metric. No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is null.

> "there was strong evidence in favor of relational thinking (BF10 ≈ 20.11 ± 1.70%), which accounted for ~23% of the variance in RT reductions (β = 0.72)"

### Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge 2018 (2)

Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge. (2018). Eye gaze patterns reveal how reasoning skills improve with experience. npj Science of Learning 3:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0035-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Bayesian regression comparing models with one or more scaled gaze metrics as predictors; the three-predictor model received BF10 ≈ 27.75 and accounted for ~35% of the variance in RT reduction.

> "We found the strongest evidence in favor of a model that included changes in relational integration, relational thinking, and visual search as predictors of change in RTs (BF 10 ≈ 27.75 ± 0.85%). Together, these metrics accounted for ~35% of the variance in RT reduction"

### Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge 2018 (3)

Belén C. Guerra-Carrillo and Silvia A. Bunge. (2018). Eye gaze patterns reveal how reasoning skills improve with experience. npj Science of Learning 3:18. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0035-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Simpler Bayesian models testing individual gaze predictors; visual search received mild evidence (R2 = 0.14, β = 0.25) while relational integration alone received no evidence (BF10 ≈ 0.53).

> "there was mild evidence for visual search (BF 10 ≈ 2.83 ± 0.79%, R2 = 0.14, β = 0.25) and no evidence in favor of the more speci ﬁc measure of relational integration (BF 10 ≈ 0.53 ± 1.52%)"
