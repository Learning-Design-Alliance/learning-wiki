## Evidence

### Votruba-Drzal 2008

Votruba-Drzal, E., Li-Grining, C. P., & Maldonado-Carreño, C. (2008). A Developmental Perspective on Full- Versus Part-Day Kindergarten and Children's Academic Trajectories Through Fifth Grade. Child Development, 79(4), 957-978. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01170.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Piecewise HLM of ECLS-K trajectories (N = 13,776), Model 1 Panel C: the part-day indicator positively predicted the post-kindergarten slope. The article reports "positive and significant coefficients on the partday indicators for the second slope coefficients"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "the positive and significant coefficients on the partday indicators for the second slope coefficients suggest that from the spring of kindergarten through fifth grade, the academic skills of children in part-day kindergarten grew at a slightly faster rate than did those of children in full-day kindergarten"

### Votruba-Drzal 2008 (2)

Votruba-Drzal, E., Li-Grining, C. P., & Maldonado-Carreño, C. (2008). A Developmental Perspective on Full- Versus Part-Day Kindergarten and Children's Academic Trajectories Through Fifth Grade. Child Development, 79(4), 957-978. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01170.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Figure 1 plots the reading growth curves by program type from fall of kindergarten through spring of third grade based on the first conditional model; the math plot is described as nearly identical. The text states the advantage "has faded out by the spring of third grade."

> "Figure 1 shows that the advantage of full- versus partday kindergarten has faded out by the spring of third grade, with the two groups diverging and the partday kindergarteners pulling ahead."
