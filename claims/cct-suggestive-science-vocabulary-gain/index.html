Evidence¶

Kim et al. 2019¶

James S. Kim, Catherine A. Asher, Mary Burkhauser, Laura Mesite, & Diana Leyva. (2019). Using a Sequential Multiple Assignment Randomized Trial (SMART) to Develop an Adaptive K–2 Literacy Intervention With Personalized Print Texts and App-Based Digital Activities. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419872701

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Researcher-developed proximal measures administered after the spring lessons in Grades 1 and 2 within the SMART trial. Both differences were small and statistically non-significant; the authors characterize them as suggestive evidence only.

"students did score slightly higher on Science Vocabulary Knowledge (ES = 0.10, p = 0.59) and Reading Comprehension (ES = 0.14, p = 0.30), providing suggestive evidence that the CCT lessons may have enabled students to acquire more science vocabulary and content compared with the LT lessons."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The CCT intervention produced statistically significant main effects favoring CCT on reading comprehension (ES = 0.14), TOSREC (ES = 0.28), and taught vocabulary (ES = 0.43), but not on SWE or ORF — reports the opposite
  • Pretest oral reading fluency moderated the effects of the CCT intervention on reading comprehension, with greater benefits for students with lower pretest fluency — related
  • The CCT treatment effect on comprehension was statistically significant only for students with pretest ORF up to 0.20 SD above the sample mean (64.0% of the sample), and not for higher-fluency students — related
  • The CCT intervention showed no statistically significant effect on posttest oral reading fluency, including within the low-fluency region of significance — related
  • Pretest word identification efficiency (SWE) and vocabulary knowledge did not moderate the intervention's effects on reading comprehension — related
  • K–2 children receiving conceptually coherent texts performed similarly to children receiving leveled texts on standardized reading comprehension outcomes — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
