# The average positive effect of rank feedback under relative grading is driven by improvement of low-performing students, consistent with a pass-threshold mechanism

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The authors interpret the average treatment effect as arising because the pass/fail threshold makes rank information more valuable for students with lower midterm scores, for whom rank has more tangible benefits. [→ Kajitani Shinya 2020](#kajitani-shinya-2020)

## Evidence

### Kajitani Shinya 2020

Kajitani Shinya, Morimoto Keiichi, Suzuki Shiba. (2020). Information feedback in relative grading: Evidence from a field experiment. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0231548

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' theoretical interpretation (type e) of why effects differ by prior performance: because scores are never adjusted downward, the pass threshold depends on raw scores as well as rank, so higher midterm scorers need lower final scores for credit. This is an interpretation, not a tested result.

> "These indicate that the rank for students with a higher (lower) midterm examination score has less (more) tangible benefits. If so, the relative performance information feedback could affect high- or low-performing students differentially."
