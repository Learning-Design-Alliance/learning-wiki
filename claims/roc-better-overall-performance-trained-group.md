## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The intervention group's more appropriate decision tendency produced better overall performance on ROC curves even though its sensitivity was no better than the control group's. [→ Harries P 2014](#harries-p-2014)

## Evidence

### Harries P 2014

Harries P, Yang H, Davies M, Gilhooly M, Gilhooly K, Thompson C. (2014). Identifying and enhancing risk thresholds in the detection of elder financial abuse: a signal detection analysis of professionals' decision making. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0268-z

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ROC curves for the intervention and control groups (Figures 3 and 4), computed with Zhang and Mueller's non-parametric procedures; the authors interpret the intervention group's advantage as arising from bias, not sensitivity.

> "We can see that the effect of a more appropriate bias (decision tendency) in the intervention group results in better performance overall - even if their judgement sensitivity is no better than the control group."
