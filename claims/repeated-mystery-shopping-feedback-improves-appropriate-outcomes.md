## Evidence

### Collins Jack Charles 2017

Collins Jack Charles, Schneider Carl Richard, Naughtin Clare Louise, Wilson Frances, de Almeida Neto Abilio Cesar, Moles Rebekah Jane. (2017). Mystery shopping and coaching as a form of audit and feedback to improve community pharmacy management of non-prescription medicine requests: an intervention study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-019462

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Repeated-measures mystery shopping intervention: 61 undergraduate students made weekly visits to 36 community pharmacies over 9 weeks with immediate verbal feedback. "the proportion of visits resulting in the appropriate outcome increased over the course of the nine visits" (rpb=0.192; P<0.001); 54% of 521 visits achieved an appropriate outcome overall.

> "Point–biserial correlations showed that the proportion of visits resulting in the appropriate outcome increased over the course of the nine visits across all scenarios (rpb=0.192; P<0.001)."
