# Student ARS performance differed across courses: biology students averaged 89.3% correct while engineering students averaged 58.5% on initial responses, with peer instruction raising averages to 80.0%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Biology students averaged 89.3% correct on the 31 POGIL-session ARS questions (SD 10.5), while engineering students averaged 58.5% correct on initial responses (SD 20.2) and 80.0% post-PI on the 15 peer-instruction questions. [→ Koretsky 2018](#koretsky-2018)
`q2 i?` Of the questions where peer instruction was used, scores increased by an average of 18.0%, although two questions showed significant score decreases. [→ Koretsky 2018 (2)](#koretsky-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Koretsky 2018

Koretsky, M., Keeler, J., Ivanovitch, J., & Cao, Y. (2018). The role of pedagogical tools in active learning: a case for sense-making. International Journal of STEM Education 5:18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0116-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Observational analysis of student ARS response records collected via the web-based platform, displayed in Fig. 5. Biology students "averaging 89.3% correct" (SD 10.5); engineering initial and post-PI averages are descriptive means as printed.

> "They averaged 58.5% correct (solid dark blue line) on these questions with a standard deviation of 20.2. For the 15 questions where the engineering instructor used the PI pedagogy, the post-PI question results are shown by powder blue circles. Their average correct was 80.0% (powder blue line) with a standard deviation of 14.8%."

### Koretsky 2018 (2)

Koretsky, M., Keeler, J., Ivanovitch, J., & Cao, Y. (2018). The role of pedagogical tools in active learning: a case for sense-making. International Journal of STEM Education 5:18. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-018-0116-5

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same observational response-record analysis of the 15 peer-instruction questions. The 18.0% average increase is a descriptive difference; the article reports two questions with significant decreases without printing a test statistic.

> "Of the questions where PI was used, scores increased by an average of 18.0%, showing benefit of peer discussion, although there were two questions where scores significantly decreased (Q12 and Q21)."
