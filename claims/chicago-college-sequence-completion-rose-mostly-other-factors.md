# Full college-sequence completion rose from 9.6 to 15.8 percent, with about 75 percent of the unadjusted increase due to factors other than student composition

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The percent of high school eligible students passing the full college preparatory sequence in four years rose from 9.6 to 15.8 percent; after adjustment the rate was 12.8 percent, with 25 percent of the unadjusted increase due to composition change and 75 percent due to other factors such as increased requirements. ## Evidence

### Shazia Rafiullah Miller 2002

Shazia Rafiullah Miller, Elaine M. Allensworth, Julie Reed Kochanek. (2002). Student Performance: Course Taking, Test Scores, and Outcomes — The State of Chicago Public High Schools: 1993 to 2000. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/student-performance-course-taking-test-scores-and-outcomes

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Four-year cohort trend analysis of course-taking among all high school eligible Chicago students, adjusted for student characteristics. The report found the full college sequence pass rate rose from 9.6 to 15.8 percent unadjusted and to 12.8 percent adjusted, attributing "75 percent is due to other factors (such as increased requirements)".

> "This suggests that of the 6.2 percent increase in the unadjusted rate, 25 percent is due to the change in student composition and 75 percent is due to other factors (such as increased requirements)."
