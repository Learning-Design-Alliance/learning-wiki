# One year of Future Forward participation raises regular-school-day attendance of lower-primary students relative to business-as-usual literacy instruction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims

`q3 i1` FF participation had a statistically significant, positive impact on school attendance, corresponding to a 1.3 percentage point higher attendance rate than BAU students. [→ Jones 2021](#jones-2021)

`q3 i?` In a 2019-20 RCT across 14 schools, FF participation had a statistically significant positive impact on school attendance, with a standardized impact of 0.13 corresponding to 1.3 percentage points higher attendance than BAU. [→ Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021](#curtis-j-jones-and-dongmei-li-2021)

## Evidence

### Jones 2021

Jones, C. J., & Li, D. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r?`

RCT with 587 kindergarten through second-grade students randomly assigned within 40 school-by-grade blocks across 14 schools; 567 retained. The full GLM estimated a standardized attendance impact of "β = 0.13, SE = 0.06, p = 0.031", equal to 1.3 percentage points.

> "FF participation had a statistically significant, positive impact on attendance. After controlling for block fixed effects and covariates (Full GLM (1)), FF had a 0.13 standardized impact on student attendance (β = 0.13, SE = 0.06, p = 0.031; Table 8)."

### Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li 2021

Curtis J. Jones and DongMei Li, University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee. (2021). Testing the Impact and Scalability of the Future Forward Literacy Program. https://uwm.edu/sreed

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

A randomized control trial assigned 587 kindergarten through second-grade students within 40 school-by-grade blocks to FF or BAU literacy instruction. The full GLM found a statistically significant attendance impact: "β = 0.13, SE = 0.06, p = 0.031", equal to 1.3 percentage points.

> "After controlling for block fixed effects and covariates (Full GLM (1)), FF had a 0.13 standardized impact on student attendance (β = 0.13, SE = 0.06, p = 0.031; Table 8). This standardized impact corresponds to a 1.3 percentage points higher attendance rate for FF than BAU students."
