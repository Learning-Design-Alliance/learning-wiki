# HSGPA strongly predicts six-year college graduation among four-year college enrollees, with odds of graduating doubling per standard deviation of HSGPA

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among Chicago Public Schools graduates who enrolled in four-year colleges, each standard deviation increase in HSGPA doubles the odds of graduating from college within six years, controlling for student background and college characteristics. [→ Allensworth 2019](#allensworth-2019)

## Evidence

### Allensworth 2019

Allensworth, Elaine M., & Clark, Kallie. (2019). Are GPAs an Inconsistent Measure of College Readiness across High Schools? Examining Assumptions about Grades versus Standardized Test Scores. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/are-gpas-inconsistent-measure-achievement-across-high-schools-examining-assumptions

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Hierarchical logistic models of students nested within high schools (n=17,753) predicting six-year college graduation from transcript HSGPA, student background, and IPEDS college covariates. The article prints the unstandardized odds coefficient "odds coefficient = 2.02" and a positive quadratic term.

> "The linear component shows that for every standard deviation increase in HSGPA, the odds of graduating from college double (odds coefficient = 2.02) at the point where the quadratic term is zero"
