## Evidence

### Ronimus 2019

Ronimus, M., Eklund, K., Pesu, L., & Lyytinen, H. (2019). Supporting struggling readers with digital game-based learning. Education Tech Research Dev, 67, 639–663. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09658-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pearson correlation analysis (Table 3, n = 16 for observed engagement) in the intervention group. Observed cognitive engagement correlated .55 with word reading gain and .60 with reading fluency gain, and .70 with GL success rate. Other engagement aspects were not significantly related to gains.

> "The only aspect of engagement significantly related to learning gains was adult-observed cognitive engagement: a higher cognitive engagement was related to larger gains in word decoding and reading fluency"

### Ronimus 2019 (2)

Ronimus, M., Eklund, K., Pesu, L., & Lyytinen, H. (2019). Supporting struggling readers with digital game-based learning. Education Tech Research Dev, 67, 639–663. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09658-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Hierarchical linear regressions in the intervention group. Observed cognitive engagement explained 37.4% of word-decoding gain (F(1, 14) = 8.35, p < .05) and 30.6% of reading-fluency gain; GL success rate fully mediated the effect on reading-fluency gain (β = 0.68, p < .05 for success rate).

> "the cognitive engagement observed by adults explained 30.6% of the gain in reading fluency, F(1, 14) = 6.16, p < .05"
