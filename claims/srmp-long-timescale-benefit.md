# Long program timescale lets students get stuck and teaches patience and perseverance, and supports mentor development

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The months-long timescale, unlike condensed ~10-week summer undergraduate programs, allows students to get stuck and, by solving problems on their own, learn patience and perseverance. [→ Or Graur 2018](#or-graur-2018)
`q1 i?` The year-long timescale also allowed mentors to develop advising skills; half of the first year's mentors returned for a second year, which the author reads as a vote of confidence. [→ Or Graur 2018](#or-graur-2018)

## Evidence

### Or Graur 2018

Or Graur. (2018). The Harvard Science Research Mentoring Program. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1809.08078](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1809.08078)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

The author's interpretation of the September-to-May program design, contrasted with summer undergraduate programs condensed to approximately 10 weeks or less. Mentor testimonials additionally report the full academic year gave them "time to adjust to the role" as advisors.

> "For most students, this is the ﬁrst time they are ever required to solve such problems completely on their own, and it teaches them patience and perseverance, two of the more important skills required for actual research."
