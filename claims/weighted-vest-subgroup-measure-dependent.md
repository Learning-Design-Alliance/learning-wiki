## Evidence

### Zimmerman 2018

Zimmerman, K. N., Pustejovsky, J. E., Ledford, J. R., Barton, E. E., Severini, K. E., & Lloyd, B. P. (2018). Single-Case Synthesis Tools II: Comparing Quantitative Outcome Measures. Research in Developmental Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2018.02.001

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Intervention-type subgroup analyses using the random-effects model with separate intercepts tested weighted vests, sensory seating, and multi-sensory environments. Printed values include Tau = 0.281 and SMD = 0.314 for weighted vests; the LRR measures were null for the same subgroup, so the effect holds only in some measures, warranting mixed strength. No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is uncoded.

> "Tau and SMD produced statistically significant results for weighted vest interventions (Tau = 0.281, 95% CI [0.094, 0.468]; SMD 0.314, 95% CI [0.063, 0.565]). LRRd and LRRi found outcomes for weighted vests interventions were not distinguishable from zero."

### Zimmerman 2018 (2)

Zimmerman, K. N., Pustejovsky, J. E., Ledford, J. R., Barton, E. E., Severini, K. E., & Lloyd, B. P. (2018). Single-Case Synthesis Tools II: Comparing Quantitative Outcome Measures. Research in Developmental Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2018.02.001

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

The same subgroup analysis found sensory seating (stability ball seating studies) produced no outcomes distinguishable from zero on any of the six measures; multi-sensory environments could not be assessed due to only one study.

> "Outcomes were not significantly different from zero for sensory seating interventions across any measures."
