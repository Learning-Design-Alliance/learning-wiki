Evidence¶

Sebring 2021¶

Sebring, Sall, Kheraj, Davis, Allensworth, de la Torre, & Beechum. (2021). Research and Engagement Agenda, 2021–25. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.5-essentials.org/cps/5e/2018/s/610268/essentials/families/#trends?c=district&o=-change

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The agenda reports a Consortium and Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago study (citing Barrow & Sartain 2019) finding GoCPS "did not result in major changes in enrollment patterns" despite easier applications and broader feeder patterns.

"We found that GoCPS did not result in major changes in enrollment patterns, although the system did make it easier to apply to schools, and high schools enrolled students from a broader number of elementary schools than previously."

Sebring 2021 (2)¶

Sebring, Sall, Kheraj, Davis, Allensworth, de la Torre, & Beechum. (2021). Research and Engagement Agenda, 2021–25. UChicago Consortium on School Research. https://www.5-essentials.org/cps/5e/2018/s/610268/essentials/families/#trends?c=district&o=-change

q2 · i? · theoretical · r2

The agenda reports that pre-GoCPS enrollment differences persisted, with "students living in lower-income neighborhoods were less likely" to enroll in high-SQRP schools, and Black students less likely than Latinx, White, and other students to do so.

"students living in lower-income neighborhoods were less likely than students living in better-off neighborhoods to enroll in schools with high SQRP ratings."
