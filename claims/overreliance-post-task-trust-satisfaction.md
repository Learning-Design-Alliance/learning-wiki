## Evidence

### Griffin Pitts 2026

Griffin Pitts, Neha Rani, Weedguet Mildort, and Eva-Marie Cook. (2026). Students' Reliance on AI in Higher Education: Identifying Contributing Factors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-12773-0_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-12773-0_9)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In the fifty-student controlled experiment, no pre-survey measure correlated significantly with the percentage of misleading recommendations incorrectly followed. No effect sizes are printed for these null correlations.

> "None of the pre-survey measures (programming self-efficacy, general trust in AI, concerns for trust, need for cognition, programming literacy, and AI literacy) showed significant correlations with overreliance."

### Griffin Pitts 2026 (2)

Griffin Pitts, Neha Rani, Weedguet Mildort, and Eva-Marie Cook. (2026). Students' Reliance on AI in Higher Education: Identifying Contributing Factors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-12773-0_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-12773-0_9)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Post-task survey correlations in the same fifty-student experiment: the article prints "r = 0.441, p = .001" for trust and "r = 0.348, p = .013" for satisfaction against measured overreliance behavior.

> "Final trust in the chatbot demonstrated a strong positive correlation with overreliance (r = 0.441, p = .001), as did satisfaction (r = 0.348, p = .013)."
