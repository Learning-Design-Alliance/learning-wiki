## Evidence

### Sattler David N. 2015

Sattler David N., McKnight Patrick E., Naney Linda, Mathis Randy. (2015). Grant Peer Review: Improving Inter-Rater Reliability with Training. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0130450

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial, reliability was estimated with two-way intraclass correlations for agreement on single values (ICC(2,k)) across raters' four scored items. Trained raters showed "ricc = 0.89" versus 0.61 untrained.

> "Inter-rater reliability was significantly higher in the training program condition (ricc = 0.89; 0.71–0.99 95% CI) than the no training program condition (ricc = 0.61; 0.32–0.96 95% CI; see Fig 1)"

### Sattler David N. 2015 (2)

Sattler David N., McKnight Patrick E., Naney Linda, Mathis Randy. (2015). Grant Peer Review: Improving Inter-Rater Reliability with Training. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0130450

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The authors interpret the trained-group reliability gain as notable for experienced reviewers (N = 46; mean 19 prior review panels), challenging the assumption that experienced reviewers know the scale well.

> "The increase in reliability for experienced reviewers is notable because it is commonly assumed that reviewers—especially those with experience—have good understanding of the grant review rating scale."
