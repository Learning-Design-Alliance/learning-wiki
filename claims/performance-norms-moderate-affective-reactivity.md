## Evidence

### Poorthuis 2015

Poorthuis, A. M. G., Juvonen, J., Thomaes, S., Denissen, J. J. A., de Castro, B. O., & van Aken, M. A. G. (2015). Do grades shape students' school engagement? The psychological consequences of report card grades at the beginning of secondary school. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(3), 842-854. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000002

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Latent interaction analysis in the SEM model; the printed Grades × Performance Norms interaction coefficient on negative affect was -1.02 (SE .40, significant), consistent with Hypothesis 4 (moderated mediation). "stronger for students who perceived performance norms in their class to be high."

> "Simple slope analyses showed that the link between grades and negative affect was stronger for students who perceived performance norms in their class to be high (i.e., 1 SD above the mean), than for students who perceived performance norms in their class to be low (i.e., 1 SD below the mean)"

### Poorthuis 2015 (2)

Poorthuis, A. M. G., Juvonen, J., Thomaes, S., Denissen, J. J. A., de Castro, B. O., & van Aken, M. A. G. (2015). Do grades shape students' school engagement? The psychological consequences of report card grades at the beginning of secondary school. Journal of Educational Psychology, 107(3), 842-854. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000002

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Probed indirect effects at 1 SD below and above the mean of performance norms (Table 5); printed indirect effects on emotional engagement were .09 (boys) and .01 (girls) at low norms, .18 (boys) and .11 (girls) at high norms. "stronger to the extent that students perceived performance norms in their class to be high."

> "Consistent with Hypothesis 4, the indirect effect of grades via negative affect on emotional engagement was stronger to the extent that students perceived performance norms in their class to be high."
