## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In the Cognitive Tutor data, the correlation between a student's average final P(Ln) and their average spikiness is 0.71, statistically significantly different than chance. [→ Baker 2011](#baker-2011)

## Evidence

### Baker 2011

Baker, Ryan S.J.d., Goldstein, Adam B., Heffernan, Neil T. (2011). Detecting Learning Moment-by-Moment. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. In Press. https://doi.org/10.3233/JAI-2011-015

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of Cognitive Tutor log data linking student-level spikiness to final knowledge; the article reports r = 0.71 with F(1,228)=230.19, p<0.0001.

> "the correlation between a student's average final P(Ln) and their average spikiness is a very high 0.71 in the Cognitive Tutor data, which is statistically significantly different than chance, F(1,228)=230.19, p<0.0001."

### Baker 2011 (2)

Baker, Ryan S.J.d., Goldstein, Adam B., Heffernan, Neil T. (2011). Detecting Learning Moment-by-Moment. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. In Press. https://doi.org/10.3233/JAI-2011-015

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of ASSISTments log data; the article reports r = 0.50 with F(1,4187)=1397.71, p<0.0001, suggesting learning spikes may be an early predictor of eventual successful learning.

> "In ASSISTments, the correlation is 0.50, which is also statistically significantly different than chance, F(1,4187)=1397.71, p<0.0001."
