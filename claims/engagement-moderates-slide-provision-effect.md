# Academic engagement moderates the relationship between slide provision and exam performance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Engagement significantly moderated the effect of slide provision (significant for students scoring one SD above the mean, not for those one SD below). [→ Samuel P. León 2021](#samuel-p-leon-2021)
`q3 i?` Study strategies did not significantly moderate the effect of slide provision. [→ Samuel P. León 2021 (2)](#samuel-p-leon-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Samuel P. León 2021

Samuel P. León, Inmaculada García-Martínez. (2021). Impact of the provision of PowerPoint slides on learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104283

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Moderation analysis with bootstrap SE from 1000 samples, assessing engagement and strategies as moderators of the Y-ppt/N-ppt effect on exam scores. "significant moderation was found in students with high scores (one standard deviation above the mean)" but not low scorers.

> "These analyses showed that engagement may moderate the effect of slide provision (Z = 2.14, p = .032, CI95% [0.042–0.792])."

### Samuel P. León 2021 (2)

Samuel P. León, Inmaculada García-Martínez. (2021). Impact of the provision of PowerPoint slides on learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2021.104283

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The same moderation analysis found study strategies did not moderate the slide-provision effect; the article reports "moderation according to slide provision was only significant in students with high study strategy use" in the figure, while the printed test was non-significant.

> "In contrast, strategies did not seem to be a good moderator of these effect (Z = 1.23, p = .216, CI95% [0.042–0.792])."
