# Self-reported weekly time-on-task is positively correlated with LMS-based time-on-task and activity measures in engineering courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Weekly self-reported time-on-task correlates positively with LMS course time-on-task (r=0.63) and even more strongly with weekly LMS course sessions (r=0.71). [→ Paul V. Sargent 2026](#paul-v-sargent-2026)
`q3 i3` At course level, discussion time-on-task shows the strongest correlation with self-reported time-on-task (r=0.86). [→ Paul V. Sargent 2026 (2)](#paul-v-sargent-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Paul V. Sargent 2026

Paul V. Sargent, Isabel Hilliger, Jorge A. Baier. (2026). Beyond Time on Task: A Novel Analytical Framework for Assessing Student Workload and Its Relationship with Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1), 110–129. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.8725

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of weekly WTS and LMS data from 14 engineering courses (784 students) over a 16-week semester. Weekly self-reported time-on-task correlated with LMS course time-on-task at "r=0.63" and with weekly LMS sessions at "r=0.71".

> "At the week level, the WTS time-on-task exhibited a significant positive correlation (r=0.63 ) with LMS overall course time-on-task (content-agnostic). Notably, the number of weekly LMS course sessions showed an even stronger correlation with WTS time-on-task ( r=0.71 )."

### Paul V. Sargent 2026 (2)

Paul V. Sargent, Isabel Hilliger, Jorge A. Baier. (2026). Beyond Time on Task: A Novel Analytical Framework for Assessing Student Workload and Its Relationship with Learning. Journal of Learning Analytics, 13(1), 110–129. https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2026.8725

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Course-level Spearman correlations across the 14 courses show Discussions Time-on-Task at r=0.86 with WTS course time-on-task (Table 1), the strongest course-level LMS feature; course sessions reached r=0.72.

> "Course-level findings were largely consistent with week-level correlations, with discussion-related indicators again characterizing higher levels of reported time-load."
