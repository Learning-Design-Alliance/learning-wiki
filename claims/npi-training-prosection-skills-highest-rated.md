# NPI trainees rated hands-on prosection and anatomy demonstration skills highest, while perceived improvement of their own teaching skills scored lower

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Learning prosection and anatomy demonstration skills scored 4.30–4.36 across the three cohorts, the highest component ratings in the debriefing questionnaire. [→ Dickman N 2017](#dickman-n-2017)
`q2 i?` Ratings for whether the course improved trainees' teaching style/skills were lower, ranging from 3.14 to 3.75 across years. [→ Dickman N 2017 (2)](#dickman-n-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Dickman N 2017

Dickman N, Barash A, Reis S, Karasik D. (2017). Students as anatomy near-peer teachers: a double-edged sword for an ancient skill. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0996-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Debriefing questionnaire administered voluntarily at the end of each annual NPI training program (12, 14, and 15 responders in 2013–2015), on a five-point Likert scale. Prosection and demonstration skills were the highest-scored components, which the authors read as student interest in continuous anatomy.

> "Learning prosection and anatomy demonstration skills scored on average from 4.30 to 4.36, respectively"

### Dickman N 2017 (2)

Dickman N, Barash A, Reis S, Karasik D. (2017). Students as anatomy near-peer teachers: a double-edged sword for an ancient skill. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0996-y

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same debriefing questionnaire: self-evaluated improvement of teaching style/skills scored lower than the hands-on components. The authors interpret this as students perceiving anatomical skills as easier to gain than didactic, communication and feedback skills, which the program did not explicitly teach.

> "the score for whether it improved their teaching style/skills was lower, at 3.70–3.75 in 2013, 3.33–3.56 in 2014, and 3.14 to 3.50 in 2015"
