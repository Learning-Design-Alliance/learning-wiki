# High logical thinking students significantly outperformed low logical thinking students in information literacy skill (F=83.252, p=0.000)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students with high logical thinking (HLT) scored significantly higher on information literacy than students with low logical thinking (LLT), regardless of instructional method. [→ Ismail et al. 2018](#ismail-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Ismail et al. 2018

Mohd Nasir Ismail, Nurfaezah Mamat, Adnan Jamaludin. (2018). The Effects of Webopac Self Training Tool with Guided Exploration on Information Literacy Skills among First Year Degree Students. The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 17(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1165734

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA comparing the two logical thinking levels (HLT vs LLT), classified by GALT scores of 7-12 and 0-6 respectively, in the 3x2 factorial design (n=150). The article prints "F-value = 83.252, p-value = 0.000" with effect size 0.254 for Level in Table 3.

> "There is a significant difference in the gain scores for the information literacy (IL) skill between HLT and LLT students (F-value = 83.252, p-value = 0.000)."
