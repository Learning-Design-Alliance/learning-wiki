# Developmental spurts in reflective judgment appear at ages 18-20 and 23-25/26 under support and practice, but the predicted 14-15 spurt appears only in RJI highest-stage data

`q3 i?` PRJI Time 2 scores showed significant increases between ages 18 and 19, 17 and 19, 18 and 20, and 23 and 25/26, supporting spurts at about 18-20 and 23-25 years but not at 14-15. [→ Kitchener 1993](#kitchener-1993)

## Evidence

### Kitchener 1993

Kitchener, K. S., Lynch, C. L., Fischer, K. W., & Wood, P. K. (1993). Developmental Range of Reflective Judgment: The Effect of Contextual Support and Practice on Developmental Stage. Developmental Psychology, 29(5), 893-906. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1993-98188-001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

One-way ANOVAs on RJI Time 1 and PRJI Time 2 scores (age effects significant, F(12, 90) = 8.01 and F(12, 91) = 9.58, both p < .01) with Waller-Duncan tests comparing adjacent years and 2-year intervals. No effect size printed.

> "The PRJI condition at Time 2 showed a significant 1-year increase between ages 18 and 19 and significant 2-year increases between ages 17 and 19, 18 and 20, and 23 and 25/26."

### Kitchener 1993 (2)

Kitchener, K. S., Lynch, C. L., Fischer, K. W., & Wood, P. K. (1993). Developmental Range of Reflective Judgment: The Effect of Contextual Support and Practice on Developmental Stage. Developmental Psychology, 29(5), 893-906. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1993-98188-001

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Stage-specific analysis graphing percentage of tasks passed per stage (Figures 2 and 3): Stage 5 tasks passed rose from 37.5% at age 18 to 75% at 19 and 87.5% at 20; Stage 6 tasks passed reached 31% at 19 and 100% at 25/26; Stage 7 rose from 12.5% to 62.5% by 25/26. Descriptive percentages only.

> "For Stage 5, no PRJI tasks were passed at age 14, but at age 15, 37.5% of the tasks were passed"
