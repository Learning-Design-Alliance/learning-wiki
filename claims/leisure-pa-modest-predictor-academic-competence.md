## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a multiple regression model including only leisure-time PA variables, PA frequency significantly predicted girls' overall academic competence (p = 0.024), and PA variables explained about 3% of variance in girls and 4% in boys. [→ Zurc Joca 2022](#zurc-joca-2022)

## Evidence

### Zurc Joca 2022

Zurc Joca, Planinšec Jurij. (2022). Associations between Physical Activity and Academic Competence: A Cross-Sectional Study among Slovenian Primary School Students. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19020623

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional survey of 1520 Slovenian students in grades 4–6; teachers rated academic competence on the SSRS. In model 1 (PA variables only), the article reports "The frequency of PA among girls (p = 0.024)" as significant, with a reported β of 0.10 SD; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "The frequency of PA among girls (p = 0.024) and participation in sports clubs among boys (p = 0.002) were statistically significant predictors of academic achievements among Slovenian 9- to 11-year-olds. The resulting β-value suggests that for every 1 SD increase in the frequency of leisure-time PA, girls’ overall academic competence increases by 0.10 SD."
