## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In unprompted professional-development conversations, teachers affirm that energy is conserved ('it can't ever die') while also describing it as 'gone' or 'gone away from us,' i.e., present but inaccessible. [→ Daane 2014](#daane-2014)

## Evidence

### Daane 2014

Daane, Vokos, and Scherr. (2014). Goals for teacher learning about energy degradation and usefulness. Physical Review Special Topics - Physics Education Research 10, 020111. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.10.020111

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analysis of video episodes from professional development courses for K-12 teachers in the Energy Project. Four elementary teachers discussing waves produced by wind affirm conservation while describing battery energy as gone: "It's gone somewhere, but it's gone," later equating dissipation with "gone away from us."

> "After Joel's question Rosie counters, "but it can't ever die," possibly referring to conservation of energy. Hannah responds with a reference to batteries, which are sources of energy that are said to "die""
