# BKT is the most efficient knowledge tracing approach overall in simulated online mastery learning, though DKT is more efficient for AS and M problems

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across simulated conditions, BKT generally had the best efficiency (predicted correctness divided by practice opportunities), while DKT random showed the highest efficiency for AS and M problems. [→ eff-1](#eff-1)
`q2 i?` Random gives the most training but is the least efficient condition because it administers all problems. [→ eff-2](#eff-2)

## Evidence

### eff-1

Qiao Zhang and Christopher MacLellan “Going Online: A simulated student approach for evaluating knowledge tracing in the context of mastery learning”. 2021. In: Proceedings of The 14th International Conference on Educational Data Mining (EDM21). International Educational Data Mining Society, 331-337. https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2021/

`q2 · i2`

Simulation results summarized in Table 1 report efficiency scores per condition and problem type (e.g., BKT random 0.10 AD, 0.17 AS, 0.15 M; DKT random 0.30 AS, 0.31 M). The authors note "BKT random has lower predictions than Streak, however the model eﬃciency suggests that it is more eﬃcient."

> "BKT also work well for mastery learning and generally seems to have the best eﬃciency of the approaches we compared (although DKT seems to be more eﬃcient for AS and M problems)."

### eff-2

Qiao Zhang and Christopher MacLellan “Going Online: A simulated student approach for evaluating knowledge tracing in the context of mastery learning”. 2021. In: Proceedings of The 14th International Conference on Educational Data Mining (EDM21). International Educational Data Mining Society, 331-337. https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2021/

`q2 · i1`

Efficiency metric computed by dividing AFM+S end-of-training predictions by number of opportunities, averaged over students and KCs. Random always gives all 80 problems per type, yielding the highest prediction but lowest efficiency.

> "Although Random gives the highest prediction in Figure 3 among all KCs, it is the least eﬃcient one as it gives all the problems during training."
