# Low weekly perceived control predicts more memory problems among adults with greater memory decline and low SOC strategy use (three-way interaction)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The three-way interaction of weekly perceived control, SOC strategy use, and memory decline was significant: low weekly control related to more memory problems for people with greater decline and low SOC use. [→ Hahn Elizabeth A. Ph.D. 2014](#hahn-elizabeth-a-phd-2014)
`q3 i?` Participants with greater memory decline who used more SOC strategies were significantly less vulnerable to the effects of low weekly perceived control. [→ Hahn Elizabeth A. Ph.D. 2014](#hahn-elizabeth-a-phd-2014)

## Evidence

`q3 · i?`

Adjusted multilevel model (n = 103) testing the SOC * Control * Memory decline three-way interaction, significant in unadjusted and adjusted models (Est. = -0.28, p = .036). Johnson-Neyman regions of significance for SOC were between mean-centered values of -1.57 and 0.59.

> "Low perceived control reported in a weekly diary was associated with more everyday memory problems among those with greater memory decline and low SOC strategy use (Est.=−0.28, SE=0.13, p=.036)."
