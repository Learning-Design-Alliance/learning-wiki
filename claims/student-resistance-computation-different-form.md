# Student resistance to computation differs from general resistance to research-based methods: students reject learning something new and activities they see as not belonging in physics class

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the computation context, students resist not by rejecting group work or active thinking but by rejecting new computational activities they perceive as not belonging in a physics class, even when capable of the tasks. [→ Ashleigh Leary 2019](#ashleigh-leary-2019)

## Evidence

### Ashleigh Leary 2019

Ashleigh Leary, Paul W. Irving, and Marcos D. Caballero. (2019). The difficulties associated with integrating computation into undergraduate physics. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2018.pr.leary)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview findings on student resistance in the computation context: instructors reported capable students refusing computational tasks "because of their own opinions of it"; one interviewee likened it to asking students to eat vegetables.

> "Instead, they are rejecting learning something new, and ac- tivities they think do not belong in a physics class. In many instances, instructors aired frustrations that they perceived they had students who were perfectly capable of accomplish- ing a computational task but simply refused"
