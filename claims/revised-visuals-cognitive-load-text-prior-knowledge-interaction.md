# Revised visuals reduce cognitive load while reading lesson text for low prior knowledge students but increase it for middle prior knowledge students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There was an interaction between condition and prior knowledge group on average fixation length while reading text, F(2, 51) = 47.27, p < .001, with low prior knowledge students showing longer fixations in the original condition and middle prior knowledge students showing longer fixations in the revised condition. [→ Clinton 2017](#clinton-2017)

## Evidence

### Clinton 2017

Clinton, V., Cooper, J.L., Michaelis, J., Alibali, M.W., & Nathan, M.J. (2017). How Revisions to Mathematical Visuals Affect Cognition: Evidence from Eye Tracking. In Eye-tracking technology applications in educational research (pp. 195-218). IGI Global. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED574984

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Eye-tracking experiment with 62 middle-school students reading a CMP2-derived lesson with original or revised visuals; average fixation length indexed cognitive load. The interaction was significant, and low prior knowledge students had "longer average fixation lengths on the text in the original condition than the revised condition".

> "As predicted, there was an interaction between condition and prior knowledge group, F(2, 51) = 47.27, p < .001 (see Figure 4). Participants in the low prior knowledge group had longer average fixation lengths on the text in the original condition than the revised condition, F(1, 21) = 45.42, p < .001."
