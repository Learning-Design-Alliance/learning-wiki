# The three groups did not differ in distinguishing conventional written signs from other visual signs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children, illiterate adults and literate adults did not differ significantly in identifying writing signs as 'for reading', and the difference on non-writing signs was also not significant. [→ Kurvers 2009](#kurvers-2009)

## Evidence

### Kurvers 2009

Kurvers, J., Van Hout, R., & Vallen, T. (2009). Print awareness of adult illiterates: a comparison with young pre-readers and low-educated adult readers. Read Writ 22, 863-887. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-008-9129-7

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Item-type analysis of the 14-card signs sorting task. Non-readers had a rather clear idea of which signs belonged to written language; 56% of illiterates identified Chinese characters as writing signs.

> "the groups did not differ in identifying writing signs as ''for reading'' (81% correct by the children, 84% by the illiterates and 89% by the literates); the difference between the groups was not signiﬁcant (Fisher Exact 0.42, p = 1.00)."
