# Students who wrote MCQ obtained better exam results than students who did not, but participants also outperformed non-participants on other topics, suggesting self-selection

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In Pathophysiology, students who wrote questions scored higher on all four exam blocks than non-writers (e.g., General Pathology in the previous year: 6.35 vs 5.67, P = 0.005), but participants were also better on topics not included in the study. ## Evidence

### Herrero Jose Ignacio 2019

Herrero Jose Ignacio, Lucena Felipe, Quiroga Jorge. (2019). Randomized study showing the benefit of medical study writing multiple choice questions on their learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1469-2

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline comparison in the Results participation section of the randomized study (75 second-year and 109 third-year writers vs non-writers). The article reports that "participants obtained better results than non-participants" including on other topics, a self-selection confound; no effect size is printed.

> "There were no gender differences between participants and non-participants, but participants obtained better results than non-participants, not only in the topics included in the study, but also in other topics of the subject."
