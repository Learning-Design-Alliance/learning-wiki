## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who had written more than 10 prescriptions as undergraduates had significantly lower rates of inappropriate drug prescribing than those who had written fewer (−8.9%; P < 0.05), and each point increase on the prescribing confidence Likert scale was associated with a significant 12.9% decrease in inappropriate prescribing rate (P < 0.001). ## Evidence

### Brinkman DJ 2017

Brinkman DJ, Tichelaar J, Schutte T, Benemei S, Böttiger Y, Chamontin B, Christiaens T, Likic R, Maˇiulaitis R, Marandi T, Monteiro EC, Papaioannidou P, Pers YM, Pontes C, Raskovic A, Regenthal R, Sanz EJ, Tamba BI, Wilson K, de Vries TP, Richir MC, van Agtmael MA. `q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Poisson regression analysis of associations between prescribing experience (number of prescriptions written) and self‐reported confidence with rates of inappropriate therapy among 895 students. Confidence showed a "12.9%" decrease per Likert point ("95% CI –7.0 to –18.4; P < 0.001").

> "Students who had written more than 10 prescriptions as undergraduates had significantly lower rates of inappropriate drug prescribing than those who had written fewer than 10 prescriptions (–8.9%; 95% CI –0.7 to –16.5; P < 0.05). For each point increase on the Likert scale of prescribing confidence, the rate of inappropriate drug prescribing significantly decreased by 12.9% (95% CI –7.0 to –18.4; P < 0.001)."
