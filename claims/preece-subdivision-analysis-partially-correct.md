# Preece's conclusion that subdivided light is impractical holds for series-connected bulbs but fails for parallel-connected ones because his assumption r + r′ of the same order as R requires thousands of bulbs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r2`–`r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2008 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Preece's series-case analysis is correct: for n ≫ 1 the per-bulb power falls as the reciprocal of n², making each bulb's illumination negligible. [→ A C Tort 2008](#a-c-tort-2008)
`q2 i?` Preece's parallel-case assumption is wrong for a residence: with r + r′ ≈ 0.1 Ω and bulb resistance ≈ 200 Ω, matching their order would require 2000 bulbs. [→ A C Tort 2008 (2)](#a-c-tort-2008-2)

## Evidence

### A C Tort 2008

A C Tort. (2008). Simple circuit theory and the solution of two electricity problems from the Victorian Age. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0954 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0811.0954](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0811.0954)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical retracing of Preece's steps in the paper's circuit model: under Preece's own assumptions the per-bulb power varies with "the reciprocal of the square of the number of bulbs", which is correct for the series case only.

> "Preece concludes that for both cases the power dissipated by a single light bulb varies with the reciprocal of the square of the number of bulbs, hence, for both cases, th e illumination that a single bulb yields is negligible."

### A C Tort 2008 (2)

A C Tort. (2008). Simple circuit theory and the solution of two electricity problems from the Victorian Age. arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0954 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0811.0954](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0811.0954)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Numerical counterexample in the parallel case: with r + r′ ≈ 0.1 Ω and bulb resistance approximately 200 Ω, the paper notes one would need "2000 light bulbs" for Preece's parallel-case assumption to hold, which is not the case for an ordinary residence.

> "If, for instance, we assume r + r ′ ≈ 0, 1 Ω, is easy to see that in order to have r + r ′ of the same order of magnitude as R we will need 2000 light bulbs! Evidently this is not the case for an ordinary re sidence."
