Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante 2023¶

Guadalupe Rodríguez Ferrante, Andrea P. Goldin, Mariano Sigman and María Juliana Leone. (2023). A better alignment between chronotype and school timing is associated with lower grade retention in adolescents. npj Science of Learning 8:21. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00171-0

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Linear mixed-effect model with chronotype (MSFsc), age, school timing and school subject as predictors of grades (n = 259). It reported "a main effect of chronotype" with partial η2 = 0.041, controlling for gender.

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Related Claims¶

  • Chronotype predicts grade retention through its interaction with school timing (synchrony effect), not through a main chronotype effect: morning-attending late chronotypes with low math grades show higher retention odds than their evening peers — reports the opposite
  • The association between later chronotype and lower math grades is strongest in the morning timing, indicating a synchrony effect that partially counteracts the chronotype effect — related
  • Academic performance in adolescents is significantly affected by the interaction of school timing, age and school subject, with better performance in later timings as students age — related
  • First-year grades predict grade retention: 1-point-lower math or language grades are associated with higher odds of repeating a year — related
