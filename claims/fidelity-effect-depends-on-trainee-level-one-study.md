## Evidence

### Lefor Alan Kawarai 2020

Lefor Alan Kawarai, Harada Kanako, Kawahira Hiroshi, Mitsuishi Mamoru. (2020). The effect of simulator fidelity on procedure skill training: a literature review. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5ea6.ae73

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports on the single study (vascular anastomosis) that evaluated junior and senior surgery residents separately on HF and LF simulators; the reviewed authors concluded skill acquisition was affected by both fidelity and level of training.

> "The authors concluded that skill acquisition was significantly affected by both simulator fidelity and level of training as measured by checklist scores and final product analysis on the animal surgery model."

### Lefor Alan Kawarai 2020 (2)

Lefor Alan Kawarai, Harada Kanako, Kawahira Hiroshi, Mitsuishi Mamoru. (2020). The effect of simulator fidelity on procedure skill training: a literature review. International Journal of Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5116/ijme.5ea6.ae73

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's analysis of participant experience levels across the 17 studies found "14/17 used study participants at the same level of training", so the fidelity-by-experience question remains largely unstudied in medical education.

> "Of 17 studies reviewed, 14/17 used study participants at the same level of training so that no conclusions can be made regarding a relationship between fidelity, level of training and effect on performance."
