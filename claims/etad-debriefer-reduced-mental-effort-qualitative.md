## Evidence

### Braund H 2024

Braund H, Hall AK, Caners K, Walker M, Dagnone D, Sherbino J, Sibbald M, Wang B, Howes D, Day AG, Wu W, Szulewski A. (2024). Evaluating the Value of Eye-Tracking Augmented Debriefing in Medical Simulation—A Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial. Simulation in Healthcare. https://doi.org/10.1097/SIH.0000000000000825

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Thematic analysis of a focus group with the four faculty debriefers, transcribed verbatim and analyzed inductively. Debriefers reported the video was "a particularly useful tool for mental effort reduction in the debriefing of poorly performing residents and junior trainees."

> "All debriefers shared examples where they experienced reduced mental effort in the eye-tracking augmented condition. These included being less reliant on notes, the ability to use video timestamps as prompts for review, and reduced mental effort due to the structure and organization of the video."
