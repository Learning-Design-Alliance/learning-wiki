# The TMR-SD memory effect differed by post-learning delay: memory weakening occurred with a 1-hour delay but not with a 1-week delay

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The TMR-SD effect (cued FR divided by uncued FR) was significantly greater in the prior 1-hour-delay study than in the present 1-week-delay study. [→ Nathan W. Whitmore 2024](#nathan-w-whitmore-2024)

## Evidence

### Nathan W. Whitmore 2024

Nathan W. Whitmore, Erika M. Yamazaki & Ken A. Paller. (2024). Targeted memory reactivation with sleep disruption does not weaken week-old memories. npj Science of Learning 9, 64. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00276-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-study comparison quantifying TMR-SD as the ratio of cued to uncued forgetting ratios, using highly similar procedures differing mainly in the 1-hour versus 1-week post-learning delay. The weakening effect was present only in the 1-hour-delay study.

> "the TMR-SD effect differed between the two studies [t(39.84) = 2.99,p = 0.005]; the TMR-SD effect was greater in the prior study than in the present study."
