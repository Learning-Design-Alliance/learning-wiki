# Processing speed, counting skills and knowledge of properties of operations correlate with arithmetic-combination fluency for both LD and MID students, while phonological short-term memory correlates with division fluency only for LD students

## Evidence

### Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli 2016

Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Spearman's rho correlations (Table 6) between cognitive factors and post-intervention fluency; processing speed reached significance for LD in both operations (rho .496 and .469) and for MID multiplication (rho .671), with MID division fluency correlated with no factor.

> "it was found that in the case of students with LD information processing speed had a significant correlation with the final fluency in the use of ACs of both operations, while phonological short-term memory was correlated only with the fluency of division ACs."

### Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli 2016 (2)

Ioannis Agaliotis & Afroditi Teli. (2016). Teaching Arithmetic Combinations of Multiplication and Division to Students with Learning Disabilities or Mild Intellectual Disability: The Impact of Alternative Fact Grouping and the Role of Cognitive and Learning Factors. Journal of Education and Learning, 5(4). https://doi.org/10.5539/jel.v5n4p90

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Spearman's rho correlations between the two learning factors and post-intervention fluency in Table 6; counting skills and properties of operations correlated significantly with fluency of both operations for both LD and MID students at p < .01.

> "Regarding the two learning factors, counting skills and properties of operations, results showed a significant correlation with the fluency of ACs of both operations (multiplication and division) for students with LD and students with MID (p < .01)"
