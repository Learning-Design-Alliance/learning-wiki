# Adapted PCE and MSLQ scales show good model fit and internal consistency for measuring collective and self-efficacy in simulation contexts

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Confirmatory factor analysis supported the a priori single-factor structures of both adapted scales, and Cronbach's α was high at both time points. [→ Tam 2025](#tam-2025)

## Evidence

### Tam 2025

Tam, C. Y. W., Dizon, J. I. W. T., Sin, S. W. C., Tang, G. K. Y., Lai, V. Y. W., Wong, Y. Y., Ganotice, F. A., & Yeung, N. P. (2025). Implementation of hybrid simulation training to promote collective and self-efficacy in medical students: a two-pronged approach. Annals of Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2527362

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

CFA of single-factor models on data from 94 students, with reliability via Cronbach's α (PCE pretest α = 0.929, posttest α = 0.914; MSLQ pretest α = 0.971, posttest α = 0.968).

> "The results indicated that the PCE model had a good fit to the data, χ2 (2) = 3.563, p = 0.168, CFI = 0.995, TLI = 0.984, RMSEA = 0.092, SRMR = 0.018. Similarly, the MSLQ model also had a good fit to the data, χ2 (18) = 29.588, p < 0.05, CFI = 0.988, TLI = 0.981, RMSEA = 0.083, SRMR = 0.019"
