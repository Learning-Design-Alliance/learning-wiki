Evidence¶

Sharon Wolf and Guilherme Lichand 2023¶

Sharon Wolf and Guilherme Lichand. (2023). Nudging parents and teachers to improve learning and reduce child labor in Cote d'Ivoire. npj Science of Learning 8:37. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00180-z

q3 · i0 · causal · r2

School-randomized trial with N = 100 schools and 2246 students; ANCOVA regression with baseline controls, inverse-propensity attrition weights, and school-clustered standard errors. The parents-only arm showed "meaningful but statistically insigni ﬁcant impacts" on the learning summary measure; literacy p = 0.236, numeracy p = 0.108.

"There were meaningful but statistically insigni ﬁcant impacts of the parent treatment on a learning summary measure ( d = 0.081, p = 0.158), and similarly sized but statistically insigni ﬁcant impacts on each of the two learning domains"

Related Claims¶

  • An SMS-based nudge intervention in Cote d'Ivoire did not reduce child labor or raise learning overall, but raised learning and domestic work among children starting with low learning levels — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Parents-only nudge effects on learning were larger and positive for children with below-median baseline skills — related
  • Parents-only nudges marginally significantly increased child-reported child labor (d = 0.113), contrary to intervention goals — related
  • The nudge intervention had no statistically significant effects on parenting mechanisms of motivation and engagement — related
  • Teacher-only and combined parent-teacher nudges showed no statistically significant impacts on learning; point estimates were small and negative for the learning summary and literacy, and near zero for numeracy — related
  • Teacher-targeted nudges showed significant negative interactions with child sex, with negative learning impacts for girls — related
  • Targeting students directly (versus caregivers) produced significantly larger nudge effects on test scores (0.31 vs. 0.08 s.d.) — related
