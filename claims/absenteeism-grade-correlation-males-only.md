# Absence rate was significantly correlated with final grades for male students but not for female students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pearson correlations between absenteeism and final grades were statistically significant for male students (p = 0.0011 first semester; p = 0.0255 second semester) but not for female students (p = 0.2041; p = 0.1537). [→ A. R. Hakami 2021](#a-r-hakami-2021)
`q2 i?` Regression r-squared values were similar for male students across semesters (0.3534 and 0.3086) and much lower for female students (0.0515 and 0.05421). [→ A. R. Hakami 2021 (2)](#a-r-hakami-2021-2)

### A. R. Hakami 2021

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation analysis of absence rate against final grade, separately by sex, in two semesters of the Clinical Virology course; the article reports a "statistically significant correlation" for male students but not female students. Only p-values are printed for these tests, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "A statistically significant correlation was found between the final grades versus absence rates for male students (p = 0.0011 for the first semester; p = 0.0255 for the second semester) but not female students (p = 0.2041 for the first semester; p = 0.1537 for the second semester)."

### A. R. Hakami 2021 (2)
