# Screening assessment benchmarks indicating significant risk shift up and down on the MCAS scale within and across grades, so students with the same skills may be classified differently at different times

## Evidence

### Mariann Lemke 2024

Mariann Lemke, Dan Murphy, Aaron Soo Ping Chow, and Angela Acuña. (2024). Early Literacy Performance in Massachusetts: Results of Ongoing Analysis of Literacy Screening Assessments. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663754

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linking analysis using equipercentile linking of grade 3 (and additional grade) screening benchmark cut scores to MCAS ELA scale scores and performance levels, extending prior MCAS comparison work to more grades.

> "some benchmarks shift from higher to lower (or vice versa) on the MCAS scale within and across grades. These shifts mean that students with the same skills might be classified differently at different time periods, and changes in the percentages of students identified as significantly below benchmark over time may be due in part to changes in the benchmarks themselves."
