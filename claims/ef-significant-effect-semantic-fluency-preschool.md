# Executive function performance significantly predicts semantic fluency in preschool children

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a two-way ANOVA, executive functions (Dimensional Card Sorting pass/fail) had a statistically significant effect on semantic fluency (p=.03). [→ Biscevic 2018](#biscevic-2018)

## Evidence

### Biscevic 2018

Biscevic, I., Pasalic, A., & Memisevic, H. (2018). The effects of executive functions and theory of mind on semantic fluency in preschool children. Problems of Education in the 21st Century, 76(1). https://www.scientiasocialis.lt/pec/ [doi:10.33225/pec/18.76.21](https://doi.org/10.33225/pec/18.76.21)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Two-way ANOVA in a correlational study of 116 preschool children (mean age 58.8 months) tested EF (card-sort pass/fail) and TOM as predictors of animals-category word production in 60 seconds. The article reports "a significant effect of EF on semantic fluency test" (p=.03 in Table 2); no effect size is printed.

> "According to the two-way ANOV A test, there was a significant effect of EF on semantic fluency test."
