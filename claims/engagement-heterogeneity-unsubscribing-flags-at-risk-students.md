## Evidence

### Ross E. O'Hara 2019

Ross E. O'Hara, Betsy Sparrow. (2019). A Summer Nudge Campaign to Motivate Community College STEM Students to Reenroll. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419875715

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory heterogeneity analysis dividing the treatment group by treatment received: first-day unsubscribers (n = 74), partial treatment (n = 132), full treatment (n = 1,161). The authors interpret unsubscribing as flagging "students already on the cusp of withdrawal" and caution the result since only 5% unsubscribed immediately.

> "For students who unsubscribed on the first day, we observed a 26-percentage-point lower rate of reenrollment compared with the control group rate of 62%, p  < .001."

### Ross E. O'Hara 2019 (2)

Ross E. O'Hara, Betsy Sparrow. (2019). A Summer Nudge Campaign to Motivate Community College STEM Students to Reenroll. AERA Open. https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858419875715

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same heterogeneity analysis subdividing full-treatment students into never-responders (n = 898) and responders (n = 263). The authors note the responder advantage is likely largely attributable to unmeasured third variables driving both engagement and reenrollment.

> "students who remained subscribed for the duration of the intervention were more likely to reenroll than the control group: A difference of 6 percentage points for students who never responded (p = .004) and a difference of 24 percentage points for students who responded at least once (p < .001)."
