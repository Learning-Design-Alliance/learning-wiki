# Social contingency in parent–child interaction supports early language learning, and infants actively elicit higher-quality input through a social feedback loop

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Live contingent interaction, not recorded video, sustains infants' phonetic learning and parents' high-pitch prosody, indicating bidirectional parent–child feedback. [→ Schwab Jessica F. 2016](#schwab-jessica-f-2016)

## Evidence

### Schwab Jessica F. 2016

Schwab Jessica F., Lew-Williams Casey. (2016). Language learning, socioeconomic status, and child-directed speech. Wiley interdisciplinary reviews. Cognitive science. https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1393

`q2 · i?`

The review reports a double-video experiment in which mothers interacted via live or replayed video; without contingent responsiveness maternal prosodic quality declined. Relatedly, 9-month-olds learned non-native contrasts from live adults but not video. No effect sizes are printed.

> "During the replayed video sessions (which lacked contingent responsiveness), mothers’ mean pitch height declined, and their overall amount of high-pitch talk declined."
