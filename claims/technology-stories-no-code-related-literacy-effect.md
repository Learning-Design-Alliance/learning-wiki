# Technology-enhanced stories show no significant additional effect on code-related literacy skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 14 contrasts, the combined effect of technology on code-related literacy skills (phonological awareness, word reading, letter knowledge, print concepts) was not significant. [→ Takacs 2015](#takacs-2015)

## Evidence

### Takacs 2015

Takacs, Z. K., Swart, E. K., & Bus, A. G. (2015). Benefits and Pitfalls of Multimedia and Interactive Features in Technology-Enhanced Storybooks: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 85(4), 698-739. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314566989

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic results for code-related literacy skills: 11 of 14 contrasts combined measures of phonological awareness, word reading and recognition, word writing, name writing, letter knowledge, and print concepts. Table 2 reports g+ = 0.16, 95% CI [-0.04, 0.36], p = .11; neither disadvantaged nor nondisadvantaged subgroups reached significance.

> "The combined effect for the 14 contrasts measuring the additional effect of technology was not significant."
