# A specific prebriefing warning of possible manikin death makes no significant difference to students' perceived self-efficacy compared with a conventional prebriefing

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` PSE was similar between the warned group (n = 27) and the unwarned group (n = 29) before the debriefing, and no significant between-group difference emerged after debriefing. [→ Weiss Anne 2017](#weiss-anne-2017)

## Evidence

### Weiss Anne 2017

Weiss Anne, Jaffrelot Morgan, Bartier Jean-Claude, Pottecher Thierry, Borraccia Isabelle, Mahoudeau Gilles, Noll Eric, Brunstein Véronique, Delacour Chloé, Pelaccia Thierry. (2017). Does the unexpected death of the manikin in a simulation maintain the participants’ perceived self-efficacy? An observational prospective study with medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0944-x

`q2 · i?`

Students were randomized into a group with a specific prebriefing on possible manikin death (n = 27) and a group without (n = 29). Between-group PSE comparison after debriefing showed "no significant difference" between PSE levels (p = 0.382); baseline groups were similar (p = 0.41).

> "The PSE of the two groups was similar before the debriefing for all the items assessed (p = 0.41)."

Discussion-level contrast against the published recommendation of Leighton [16]: in this observational study of 56 students, "we did not find any significant difference in perceived self-efficacy" between the two prebriefing conditions.

> "we did not find any significant difference in perceived self-efficacy between the students in Group 1 (with prebriefing on the possible death of the manikin) and Group 2 (with no specific prebriefing). The impact of the prebriefing on PSE was therefore not established."
