# Moderate-intensity physical activity is associated with higher academic performance at the 25th percentile of the APS distribution but not at higher quantiles

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At the 25th percentile of APS, every one-day weekly increase in moderate physical activity was associated with a 0.15-point (0.6%) increase in scores, and this coefficient differed significantly from that at the 90th percentile. [→ Zhang Yunting 2015](#zhang-yunting-2015)

## Evidence

### Zhang Yunting 2015

Zhang Yunting, Zhang Donglan, Jiang Yanrui, Sun Wanqi, Wang Yan, Chen Wenjuan, Li Shenghui, Shi Lu, Shen Xiaoming, Zhang Jun, Jiang Fan. (2015). Association between Physical Activity and Teacher-Reported Academic Performance among Fifth-Graders in Shanghai: A Quantile Regression. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115483

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Quantile regression with school fixed effects on 1470 observations; at the 25th percentile, "every one day increase in moderate physical activity per week was associated with a 0.15 point or 0.6%" increase in APS.

> "In particular, the quantile regression coefficient of MoIPA at the 25th percentile of academic performance suggested that every one day increase in moderate physical activity per week was associated with a 0.15 point or 0.6% ((0.15/25)*100 = 0.6) increase in APS, after controlling for all other factors in the model."

### Zhang Yunting 2015 (2)

Zhang Yunting, Zhang Donglan, Jiang Yanrui, Sun Wanqi, Wang Yan, Chen Wenjuan, Li Shenghui, Shi Lu, Shen Xiaoming, Zhang Jun, Jiang Fan. (2015). Association between Physical Activity and Teacher-Reported Academic Performance among Fifth-Graders in Shanghai: A Quantile Regression. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115483

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Post-estimation test across quantiles: the MoIPA coefficient at the 25th percentile was significantly different from that at the 90th percentile (F = 6.31, P = 0.01).

> "However, for MoIPA, the coefficient at the 25th percentile of APS was significantly different from that at the 90th percentile (F = 6.31, P = 0.01)."
