# Higher-performing students report self-testing, study planning, and evaluating understanding more often than lower performers, who report passive strategies more

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Several self-reported strategies differed significantly by quiz grade category: higher performers more often reported self-testing, planning when to study, and evaluating content understanding; lower performers more often reported rewatching videos and rereading readings. [→ Hazlett 2025](#hazlett-2025)

## Evidence

### Hazlett 2025

Hazlett, Z. S., Jimenez, P. C., & Knight, J. K. (2025). Self-Testing and Follow-Through of Learning Strategies Supports Student Success. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-04-0128

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-squared tests of strategy report frequencies by quiz grade category across 1270 responses (Table 3), with significant differences for self-testing (χ² = 29.06), developing a plan for when to study (χ² = 13.64), evaluating content understanding (χ² = 19.91), rewatching videos (χ² = 19.63), and rereading readings (χ² = 31.17); df = 3, p < 0.05. No effect sizes are printed.

> "Higher performing students more often reported using self-testing, developing a plan for when to study, and evaluating content understanding, when compared with lower performing students (Table 3)."
