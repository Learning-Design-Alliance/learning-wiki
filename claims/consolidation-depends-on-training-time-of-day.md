# Twenty-four-hour consolidation of motor skill differs according to the time of day of training, with deterioration in the morning, stabilization in the afternoon, and improvement in the evening

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` The group x session interaction on skill was significant with a large effect, driven by divergent 24 h consolidation gains (T2_T3) across training-time groups. [→ Charlène Truong 2023](#charlene-truong-2023)
`q2 i2` The morning group (G10am) showed a significant deterioration in skill 24 h after training (d = 0.66; 9/12 participants decreased). [→ Charlène Truong 2023 (2)](#charlene-truong-2023-2)
`q2 i2` The afternoon group (G3pm) stabilized its performance 24 h after training, with a non-significant gain (d = 0.43; 8/12 participants increased). [→ Charlène Truong 2023 (3)](#charlene-truong-2023-3)
`q2 i3` The evening group (G8pm) showed significant further improvement of skill 24 h after training (d = 0.87; 12/12 participants increased). [→ Charlène Truong 2023 (4)](#charlene-truong-2023-4)

## Evidence

### Charlène Truong 2023

Charlène Truong, Célia Ruffino, Jérémie Gaveau, Olivier White, Pauline M. Hilt and Charalambos Papaxanthis. (2023). Time of day and sleep effects on motor acquisition and consolidation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00176-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Main experiment consolidation analysis: one-way ANOVA on 24 h gains gave η2 = 0.32; the "T2_T3 gain of the G10am signiﬁcantly differed" from the afternoon and evening groups, explaining the group x session interaction (F2,66 = 5.68, p = 0.001, η2 = 0.26).

> "One-way ANOVA (F2,33 = 7.44, p = 0.002, η2 = 0.32) showed that T2_T3 gain of the G10am signiﬁcantly differed from that of the G3 pm and G8 pm (p = 0.01 and p = 0.002, respectively; Fig. 2d)."

### Charlène Truong 2023 (2)

Charlène Truong, Célia Ruffino, Jérémie Gaveau, Olivier White, Pauline M. Hilt and Charalambos Papaxanthis. (2023). Time of day and sleep effects on motor acquisition and consolidation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00176-9

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

T-test of G10am's 24 h consolidation gain against reference value zero: a significant deterioration, d = 0.66, with 9 of 12 participants decreasing their performance between T2 and T3.

> "showed a deterioration for the G10 am (t = −2.27, p = 0.04, d = 0.66; 9/12 participants decreased their performance),"

### Charlène Truong 2023 (3)

Charlène Truong, Célia Ruffino, Jérémie Gaveau, Olivier White, Pauline M. Hilt and Charalambos Papaxanthis. (2023). Time of day and sleep effects on motor acquisition and consolidation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00176-9

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

G3pm's 24 h consolidation gain compared with zero was not significant (p = 0.17, d = 0.43), with 8 of 12 participants increasing performance — stabilization rather than improvement.

> "stabiliza-tion for the G3 pm (t = 1.49, p = 0.17, d = 0.43; 8/12 participants increased their performance),"

### Charlène Truong 2023 (4)

Charlène Truong, Célia Ruffino, Jérémie Gaveau, Olivier White, Pauline M. Hilt and Charalambos Papaxanthis. (2023). Time of day and sleep effects on motor acquisition and consolidation. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00176-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

G8pm's 24 h consolidation gain compared with zero was significant (p = 0.01, d = 0.87), with all 12 of 12 participants increasing their performance between the evening post-test and the next-day retest.

> "an improvement of skill for the G8pm (t = 3.02, p = 0.01, d = 0.87; 12/12 participants increased"
