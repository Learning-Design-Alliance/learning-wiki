## Evidence

### Shimizu Ikuo 2019

Shimizu Ikuo, Nakazawa Hideyuki, Sato Yoshihiko, Wolfhagen Ineke H. A. P., Könings Karen D. (2019). Does blended problem-based learning make Asian medical students active learners?: a prospective comparative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1575-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Multiple regression among bPBL students, controlled for gender, prior knowledge, and self-study time, exploring association between e-learning acceptance and PBL-related subscales. The self-directed learning coefficient was B = 0.228 (p = 0.044); "students’ perception of their self-directed learning related positively to the acceptance of blended learning".

> "Multiple regression analysis showed that students’ perception of their self-directed learning related positively to the acceptance of blended learning (p = 0.044)."

### Shimizu Ikuo 2019 (2)

Shimizu Ikuo, Nakazawa Hideyuki, Sato Yoshihiko, Wolfhagen Ineke H. A. P., Könings Karen D. (2019). Does blended problem-based learning make Asian medical students active learners?: a prospective comparative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1575-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Additional item-level regression analysis between self-directed learning and the four TAM acceptance items (usefulness, ease of use, attitude, behavioural intention). Usefulness was positively related with self-directed learning (p = 0.047); the other items showed no relation.

> "The results (Table 4) revealed that the usefulness of e-learning, in particular, was positively related with self-directed learning (p = 0.047)."
