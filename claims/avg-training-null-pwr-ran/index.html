Evidence¶

Bertoni 2024¶

Bertoni, S., Andreola, C., Mascheretti, S., Franceschini, S., Ruffino, M., Trezzi, V., Molteni, M., Sali, M. E., Salandi, A., Gaggi, O., Palazzi, C., Gori, S. & Facoetti, A. (2024). Action video games normalise the phonemic awareness in pre-readers at risk for developmental dyslexia. npj Science of Learning 9, 25. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00230-0

q3 · i1 · causal · r2

ANCOVA null results for the two other reading-related predictors in the same four-group design. Both tests were non-significant with very small printed effects ("ηp2 = 0.026" and "ηp2 = 0.007"); equivalence was not tested.

"On the contrary, the training groups did not show any statistically signiﬁcant difference in individual gain in PWR (F(3,72) = 0.647, p = 0.587, ηp2 = 0.026) and in RAN (F(3,74) = 0.172, p = 0.915, ηp2 = 0.007)"

Bertoni 2024 (2)¶

Bertoni, S., Andreola, C., Mascheretti, S., Franceschini, S., Ruffino, M., Trezzi, V., Molteni, M., Sali, M. E., Salandi, A., Gaggi, O., Palazzi, C., Gori, S. & Facoetti, A. (2024). Action video games normalise the phonemic awareness in pre-readers at risk for developmental dyslexia. npj Science of Learning 9, 25. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00230-0

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Alternative repeated-measures linear mixed effects analysis with time, group and time × group as fixed factors and subject ID as random effects, confirming the specificity of the AVG effect to phoneme discrimination.

"A statistically significant time × group effect was found upon phoneme discrimination (F(3,75) = 3.600, p = 0.017), but not upon PWR (F(3,73.72)= 1.032, p = 0.384) and RAN (F(3,75) = 0.175, p = 0.913)."
