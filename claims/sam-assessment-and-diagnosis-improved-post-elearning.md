## Evidence

### Choi Sunhea 2020

Choi Sunhea, Yuen Ho Ming, Annan Reginald, Monroy-Valle Michele, Pickup Trevor, Aduku Nana Esi Linda, Pulman Andy, Portillo Sermeño Carmen Elisa, Jackson Alan A, Ashworth Ann. (2020). Improved care and survival in severe malnutrition through eLearning. Archives of Disease in Childhood. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2018-316539

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Medical record review of children aged 0–60 months at eleven hospitals, comparing 12 months preintervention with 12 months postintervention. The review found "a notable improvement in the proportion of children with the requisite data for assessing SAM (34.9% vs 15.9%, p<0.001)".

> "postintervention there was a notable improvement in the proportion of children with the requisite data for assessing SAM (34.9% vs 15.9%, p<0.001) and the number and proportion reported as having SAM almost doubled."

### Choi Sunhea 2020 (2)

Choi Sunhea, Yuen Ho Ming, Annan Reginald, Monroy-Valle Michele, Pickup Trevor, Aduku Nana Esi Linda, Pulman Andy, Portillo Sermeño Carmen Elisa, Jackson Alan A, Ashworth Ann. (2020). Improved care and survival in severe malnutrition through eLearning. Archives of Disease in Childhood. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2018-316539

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same record-review analysis of cases classified against WHO SAM criteria (6–60 months): "an improvement in the proportion of cases correctly diagnosed as having SAM (matched 58.5% vs 47.1%, p<0.001)".

> "There was also an improvement in the proportion of cases correctly diagnosed as having SAM (matched 58.5% vs 47.1%, p<0.001)."
