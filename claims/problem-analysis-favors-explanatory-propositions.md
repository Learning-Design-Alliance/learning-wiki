# Problem analysis through small-group discussion particularly stimulates the production of explanatory propositions, via a significant treatment-by-proposition-type interaction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, a significant interaction emerged between experimental manipulation and type of propositions produced, F(1, 37) = 19.27, p < .001, with the effect especially strong for explanatory statements. [→ Schmidt 1989](#schmidt-1989)

## Evidence

### Schmidt 1989

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-0663.81.4.610

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA of Experiment 1 free-recall protocols (condition between subjects, proposition type within subjects) showed the treatment affected both descriptive propositions, F(1, 37) = 12.32, p < .01, and explanations, F(1, 37) = 47.24, p < .001, with explanations showing the larger effect; the experimental group produced "more than three times as many" explanatory propositions.

> "However, a significant interaction effect emerged between experimental manipulation and type of propositions produced, F(1, 37) = 19.27, MSe = 5.84, p < .001."
