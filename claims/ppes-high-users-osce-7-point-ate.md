## Evidence

### George Ann 2025

George Ann, Blaauw Duane, Dangor Ziyaad, Lala Sanjay G. (2025). Evaluation of the impact of an online introductory paediatric physical examination skills course on students’ OSCE performance. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08177-5

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Observational quasi-experimental analysis of 343 matched fourth-year medical students; low and high course users were matched on gender, age, course entry type, 2021 marks and OSCE group. The matched ATE was "7.0 [95%CI: 3.8–10.2] percentage points higher", "a Cohen’s d of 0.249".

> "The analysis of impact after propensity score matching (Table 5) confirmed that increased PPES course usage significantly affected performance in the paediatrics component of the OSCE: high course users scored an average of 7.0 [95%CI: 3.8–10.2] percentage points higher in the OSCE than low users. This equates to a Cohen’s d of 0.249, indicating a small effect size."
