# Dominant-hand training is faster than bimanual training for tying 10 consecutive half knots after a two-day laparoscopic suturing course

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Residents trained with the dominant hand only tied 10 consecutive half knots faster than residents trained bimanually (p = 0.020). [→ Asencio FA 2018](#asencio-fa-2018)

## Evidence

### Asencio FA 2018

Asencio FA, Ribeiro HASA, Romeo A, Wattiez A, Ribeiro PAGA. (2018). The Effect on Performance Time and Quality of the Knots after Mono or Bimanual Training of Laparoscopic Intracorporeal Knot Tying according to the Gladiator Rule Technique. Rev Bras Ginecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0038-1649494

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparative analysis of two training groups in a prospective observational study of 41 residents (group A dominant hand, n = 21; group B bimanual, n = 20); Table 2 reports the 10-consecutive-half-knots time difference as significant, "225.5 s and 289.7 s respectively" with p = 0.020.

> "The amount of time to tie 10 consecutive knots was shorter in group A than in group B (225.5 s and 289.7 s respectively; Table 2)."
