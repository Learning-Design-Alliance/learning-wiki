## Evidence

### Dahlkemper 2023

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

ANCOVA treating presented responses as independent variable, scientific accuracy as dependent variable, and linguistic quality as covariate, for question 2 (waves). The article reports the effect "was more pronounced without correction" (η² = 0.49) than with correction (η² = 0.26).

> "In the second question, the effect was more pronounced without correction [F (3, 376) = 121,p<. 001,η 2 = 0.49] than with correction [ F (3, 376) = 87 .4,p < .001,η 2 = 0.26]."

### Dahlkemper 2023 (2)

Dahlkemper, M. N., Lahme, S. Z., & Klein, P. (2023). How do physics students evaluate artificial intelligence responses on comprehension questions? A study on the perceived scientific accuracy and linguistic quality. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010142)

`q2 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlations between the scientific accuracy and linguistic quality scales across the three questions, as printed in the ANCOVA section, including r = .45 for question 1 and r = .60 for question 2. The article notes the high correlation for question 2 supports the covariate's role.

> "The correlations between both scales are r(384) = .45,p < .01,r (377) = .60,p < .01, and r(374) = .47,p < .01 for questions 1, 2, and 3, respectively."
