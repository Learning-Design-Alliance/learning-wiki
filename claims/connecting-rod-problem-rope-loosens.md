# The connecting-rod problem: when forces would push into the rope from both ends, the rope loosens and the lid and sandbag motions must be separated

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` In parameter constellations where solving the coupled equations would require the rope to push rather than tear, the rope in fact loosens and the lid and sandbag must be treated as separately moving bodies. [→ U Schröter 2009](#u-schroter-2009)

## Evidence

### U Schröter 2009

U Schröter. (2009). Kermit's What-Happens-Next-Machine Reloaded. https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.0110 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0903.0110)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical discussion in section 5 of the article, identifying what it calls the "connecting-rod problem": a difficulty the author says "has so far been overlooked", depending on weights, lengths and initial angles.

> "In this case, in fact, the rope will loosen. The motion of the lid and the sandbag has to be separated, each merely further accelerated by the corresponding gravitational force (our massless seesaw giving way to the sandbag), until again the rope possibly straightens."
