## Evidence

### PERKINS 1989

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Brown and associates' series of laboratory and classroom studies (also citing Gick & Holyoak, 1987), concluding transfer is very much a matter of how knowledge is acquired and applied; negative findings reflect unmet conditions, not domain specificity.

> "transfer to new problems does take place, even among three- and four-year-olds, when (a) learners are shown how problems resemble each other; (b) when learn- ers' attention is directed to the underlying goal structure of comparable problems"

### PERKINS 1989 (2)

D. N. Perkins and Gavriel Salomon. (1989). Are Cognitive Skills Context-Bound? Educational Researcher, 16-23. https://www.jstor.org/stable/1176259

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports this partial replication of Gick and Holyoak's analogy studies: the abstracting group showed impressive transfer only when additionally urged to search memory for a relevant principle, illustrating the high-road mechanism.

> "Salomon and Globerson (1987) showed that college students who were urged to formulate an abstract principle from two problems did not show more transfer to a new, analogous problem than students who were given the principle ready- made."
