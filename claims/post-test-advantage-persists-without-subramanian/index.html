Evidence¶

Pei L 2019¶

Pei L, Wu H. (2019). Does online learning work better than offline learning in undergraduate medical education? A systematic review and meta-analysis. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2019.1666538

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r2

Sensitivity meta-analysis of post-test scores conducted without the article of Subramanian et al. to avoid a potential determinative influence from that single study. The result remained significant: "favoring online learning (SMD = 0.68; 95% CI: 0.35, 1.02)".

"There was still a significant difference (Z = 4.00, p < 0.0001) between the online learning and offline learning groups, favoring online learning (SMD = 0.68; 95% CI: 0.35, 1.02)."

Related Claims¶

  • Online learning produces higher post-test knowledge and skill scores than offline learning in undergraduate medical education (SMD = 0.81)
  • Across the 16 included studies, 9 reported significant improvement for online learning and 7 reported no significant difference; none found online learning less effective
  • Online learning's advantage may be limited to simple learning goals assessed by objective multiple-choice instruments
  • A single retention-test comparison found online learning more effective than offline learning (SMD = 4.64) — related
  • Interactive online learning designs with rich feedback, matched task difficulty and case-based strategies may explain larger online learning effects — related
  • Pre- and post-test score gains show no significant difference between online and offline learning — related
