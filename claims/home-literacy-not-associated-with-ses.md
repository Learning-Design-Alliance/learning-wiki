# Home literacy practices were not associated with parental education or income in the behavioral sample, with negative education tendencies in the fMRI sample

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the behavioral sample, neither parental education nor income was associated with home literacy practices (all p's > 0.19), but in the fMRI sample education tended to be negatively associated with basic (r(64) = −0.259, p = 0.090) and formal advanced (r(64) = −0.280, p = 0.066) practices. ## Evidence

### Girard 2021

Girard, C., Bastelica, T., Léone, J., Epinat-Duclos, J., Longo, L. & Prado, J. (2021). Nurturing the reading brain: home literacy practices are associated with children's neural response to printed words through vocabulary skills. npj Science of Learning 6:34. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00112-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of parental SES measures against home literacy practice frequencies in the 66-child behavioral sample; in the fMRI sample, education showed only marginal negative tendencies that did not reach significance. The authors note the sample concentrated middle-to-high SES families.

> "Correlation analyses indicated that neither education nor income was associated with home literacy practices in the behavioral sample (all p's > 0.19, two-tailed)."
