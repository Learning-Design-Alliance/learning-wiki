# The type of creativity test moderates the IQ–creativity correlation: Guilford tests (r = .250) correlate more strongly with IQ than Wallach-Kogan measures (r = .116), consistent with game-like administration yielding smaller relationships

> **Currency** · newest study 2005 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean correlations differed significantly by creativity test: Guilford Tests (r = .250) vs. Wallach-Kogan divergent thinking measures (r = .116), and creativity tests had a significant independent effect in weighted regression (β = -.01179, p < .001). [→ Kim 2005](#kim-2005)
`q2 i?` The author interprets the difference as an administration-context effect: when creativity tests are administered in a game-like testing context, creativity scores have smaller relationships with IQ scores. [→ Kim 2005 (2)](#kim-2005-2)

## Evidence

### Kim 2005

Kim, K. H. (2005). Can Only Intelligent People Be Creative? A Meta-Analysis. The Journal of Secondary Gifted Education, XVI(2/3), 57-66. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Can+Only+Intelligent+People+Be+Creative

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic moderator analysis across 64 Guilford, 18 TTCT, and 319 Wallach-Kogan correlation coefficients. The Guilford vs. Wallach-Kogan contrast was significant (χ2 = 145.689, p < .0001); the weighted regression gave creativity tests β = -.01179, z = -2.33020, p < .001.

> "Guilford Tests (r = .250) and Wallach-Kogan divergent thinking measures (r = .116) were also statistically different, χ2 = 145.689, p < .0001."

### Kim 2005 (2)

Kim, K. H. (2005). Can Only Intelligent People Be Creative? A Meta-Analysis. The Journal of Secondary Gifted Education, XVI(2/3), 57-66. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Can+Only+Intelligent+People+Be+Creative

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Authors' interpretation (type e), not a directly tested contrast: the article reasons from Wallach and Kogan's game-like, nonevaluative administration and notes that none of the included TTCT studies reported game-like administration, so the TTCT mean (r = .218) fell between the two.

> "Therefore, it can be concluded that, when creativity tests are administered in a game-like testing context, the creativity test scores have smaller relationships with IQ test scores."
