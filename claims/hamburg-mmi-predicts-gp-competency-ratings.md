## Evidence

### Knorr M 2018

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Study 2: GP evaluations after a one-week 1:1 practice placement in first year; data from 195 participants, including 59 admitted via the MMI (56% of MMI-admitted). Spearman correlation "ρ = .32, p < .05" (95% CI .04/.55); ρ of .32 corresponds to a small-to-medium effect.

> "However, MMI results correlated significantly with GP psychosocial competencies (ρ = .32, p < .05), GP suitability (ρ = .42, p < .01) and OSCE score (ρ = .23, p < .05)."

### Knorr M 2018 (2)

Knorr M, Schwibbe A, Ehrhardt M, Lackamp J, Zimmermann S, Hampe W. (2018). Validity evidence for the Hamburg multiple mini-interview. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1208-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Robust regression (M estimator) for GP psychosocial skills: MMI coefficient b = 0.53 (SE 0.20, t = 2.67, p < .05, df = 54); gender and age were non-significant predictors in the model (Table 5). No effect size printed.

> "The robust regression models for each of the three outcomes showed that the MMI remained a significant predictor when the control variables were part of the model."
