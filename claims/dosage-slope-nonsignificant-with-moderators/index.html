Evidence¶

Anna H. Miller 2025¶

Anna H. Miller, Daniel R. Espinas, Daniel McNeish, & Marcia A. Barnes. (2025). Dosage Response in Intensive Mathematics Interventions for Early Elementary Students With or At-Risk for Mathematics Learning Disability. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 91. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10070-y

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Linear random-effects meta-regression with robust variance estimation (Model 2), dosage centered at the median of 20 h, across 164 effect sizes. The authors report "For every 1-h increase in overall intervention dosage, predicted effect sizes increased by 0.03, on average"; the pooled effect at 20 h was 0.73 (95% CI [0.50, 0.96]).

"The slope of dosage (e.g., total hours of intervention) on effect sizes was significant and positive, β = 0.03 (95% CI [0.01, 0.06]). For every 1-h increase in overall intervention dosage, predicted effect sizes increased by 0.03, on average."

Anna H. Miller 2025 (2)¶

Anna H. Miller, Daniel R. Espinas, Daniel McNeish, & Marcia A. Barnes. (2025). Dosage Response in Intensive Mathematics Interventions for Early Elementary Students With or At-Risk for Mathematics Learning Disability. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 91. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10070-y

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Model 3 of the same meta-regression added instructional focus, outcome proximity, implementer, and group size as moderators with dosage interactions. The authors report "total hours of intervention is not a significant predictor of effect sizes at the median of dosage (i.e., 20 h)" in this full model.

"The main effect of dosage decreased and was no longer significant (β = 0.02; 95% CI [− 0.29, 0.33]), indicating that holding the effects of the other predictors constant, total hours of intervention is not a significant predictor of effect sizes at the median of dosage (i.e., 20 h)."

Related Claims¶

  • Instructional focus (number and operations vs. word-problem solving) and intervention implementer (researcher vs. school personnel) did not significantly predict effect sizes or moderate the dose–response relation — related
  • Multi-session game interventions show significantly larger learning benefits than single-session interventions, while total play duration shows no association with effect size — related
  • Tier 2 dosage and scheduling findings are mixed: more frequent kindergarten sessions helped, but first-grade studies found no outcome differences across 16-hour and 32-hour schedules — related
  • The dose–response relation for K-3 intensive mathematics interventions is linear, with no support for a nonlinear (quadratic) curve — related
  • Group size moderates intervention effects: 1:1 interventions had effect sizes about 0.5 points higher than small-group interventions at 20 h of dosage — related
  • Outcome proximity moderates the dose–response relation: distal outcomes outperform proximal outcomes at dosages at or below 5.43 h, while proximal outcomes exceed distal outcomes at or above 22.6 h — related
  • Mathematics outcomes benefit from PA with academic instruction of 6–10-week duration at moderate or moderate-to-vigorous intensity, and language outcomes from enriched PA programmes — related
