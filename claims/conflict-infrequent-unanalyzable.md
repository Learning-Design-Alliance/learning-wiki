## Evidence

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Peer-evaluation data from the quasi-experiment were qualitatively coded for conflict; the authors report conflicts "ranging from 4–9% of students" and that models failed to converge, likely due to low incidence. No effect size printed.

> "Conflicts were very infrequently reported, ranging from 4–9% of students being in groups that reported conflict (Fig 1B)."

### Navlekar Anisha S. 2025 (2)

Navlekar Anisha S., Theobald Elli J., Griffith Ken, Limeri Lisa B. (2025). Strategically creating maximally heterogeneous lab groups did not improve group performance in an introductory biology lab class. PLOS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0323799

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Inspection of raw conflict data (not a fitted model, since models failed to converge) showed a fall-semester difference of "9% in heterogeneous groups vs. 4% in randomly-assigned groups" and no spring difference. Descriptive percentages only.

> "There were more reports of conflict in maximally heterogeneous groups in the fall (9% in heterogeneous groups vs. 4% in randomly-assigned groups), but no difference in the spring (4–5% in both types of groups)."
