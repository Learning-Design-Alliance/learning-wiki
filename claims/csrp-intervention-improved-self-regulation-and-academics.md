# The Chicago School Readiness Project, a classroom-based RCT, improved low-income children's executive function and academic skills with effect sizes of 0.34 to 0.63

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` CSRP significantly improved executive function, reduced behavior problems, and improved vocabulary, letter-naming, and math skills, with effect sizes ranging from 0.34 to 0.63. [→ Blair 2015](#blair-2015)

## Evidence

### Blair 2015

Blair, Clancy & Raver, C. Cybele. (2015). School Readiness and Self-Regulation: A Developmental Psychobiological Approach. Annual Review of Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015221

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

Randomized controlled trial of a classroom-based intervention including 30 hours of teacher training in classroom management strategies and one-on-one consultant work with children showing challenging behavior. The article reports "significant benefits of CSRP for children's academic skills" with printed effect sizes "ranging from 0.34 to 0.63" (ES = 0.34–0.63).

> "Analyses also suggested significant benefits of CSRP for children's academic skills as measured by direct assessments of children's vocabulary, letter-naming, and math skills (Raver et al. 2011). Effect sizes (ESs) of this intervention were substantial, ranging from 0.34 to 0.63."
