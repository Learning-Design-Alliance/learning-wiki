# Pre-literate children already show a left-hemisphere bias for letter processing before any training

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Even before training, left fusiform (especially anterior) responded more to letters than to shapes and pseudo-letters, while right fusiform responded similarly to all three stimulus types. [→ James 2010](#james-2010)

## Evidence

### James 2010

James, K. H. (2010). Sensori-motor experience leads to changes in visual processing in the developing brain. Developmental Science, 13(2), 279-288. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-7687.2009.00883.x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-training scans of twelve pre-literate children (all could name at least 80% of the alphabet) showed left fusiform preference for letters over matched pseudo-letters and shapes; right fusiform showed no such preference. The article reports this as evidence the brain organizes toward left-hemisphere print dominance before reading.

> "The left fusiform gyrus, especially in the anterior portion, responded more to letters than to shapes and pseudo-letters, even prior to our training manipulation (Figures 4 and 5). In contrast, however, the right fusiform responded similarly to letters, shapes and pseudo-letters (Figures 6 and 7)."
