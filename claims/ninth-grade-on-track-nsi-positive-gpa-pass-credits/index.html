Evidence¶

Evaluation of the Networks for School Improvement Initiative: Interim Summary Report 2024¶

Evaluation of the Networks for School Improvement Initiative: Interim Summary Report. (2024). RAND Corporation. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA242-1.html

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Matched comparison analysis of administrative student data comparing NSI schools with similar schools in the same district, reported in Research Question 3. The report states effects were "equivalent to a 0.13 point increase in GPA" plus the printed pass-rate and credit gains; no standardized effect size is printed.

"The effects of the 9th-grade on-track NSI were equivalent to a 0.13 point increase in GPA (from 2.34 to 2.47 on a 4.0 scale), a 4 percentage point increase in the share of core courses passed (from 65 percent to 69 percent), and a 4 percentage point increase in the share of 9th-grade students earning at least five credits (from 83 percent to 87 percent)."

Matthew Johnson 2024¶

Matthew Johnson, Jeffrey Max, Tareena Musaddiq, Breyon Williams, Silvia Robles, Michelle Bennett. (2024). Evaluation of the Networks for School Improvement Initiative: Impacts on Student Outcomes (Interim Report). Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/evaluation-of-the-networks-for-school-improvement-initiative-student-outcomes-interim-report

q3 · i? · causal · r?

Quasi-experimental comparison of students in 9th-grade on-track NSI schools to students in similar non-participating schools after the second year. The report states these NSI "had a positive impact on three of the five outcomes"; no effect sizes are printed in the available text.

"These NSI had a positive impact on three of the five outcomes-GPA, core course pass rate, and credit completion-after schools' second year of participation"
