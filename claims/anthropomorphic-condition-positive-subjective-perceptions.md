# Participants in the anthropomorphic condition reported significantly more positive feelings of clarity, satisfaction, and stimulation while and after working through the difficult-task tutorial, and perceived all instructions as easier to understand

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For task 2, the anthropomorphic condition rated clarity, satisfaction, and stimulation significantly higher both while going through the tutorial and while carrying out the task, and rated instructions as significantly easier to understand. [→ Murano 2012](#murano-2012)

## Evidence

### Murano 2012

Murano, P. & Sethi, T. (2012). Anthropomorphic User Interface Feedback in a Sewing Context and Affordances. International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, Vol. 2, No. 4. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1208.3323)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subjective responses on a five-point Likert post-experiment questionnaire in the 40-participant sewing experiment. Significant higher positive ratings favouring the anthropomorphic condition are reported for clarity, satisfaction, and stimulation during and before task 2 (tables VII–XIII), for overall satisfaction with the learning experience (table XIV), and for ease of understanding of instructions (tables XVIII–XX).

> "The participants in the anthropomorphic condition rated the tutorial with a significantly higher positive score for feelings of clarity in relation to the tutorial and the second  task, than the non-anthropomorphic condition"
