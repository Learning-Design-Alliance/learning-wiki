Evidence¶

Bielak et al. 2013¶

Jakub Bielak, Mirosųaw Pawlak, Anna Mystkowska-Wiertelak. (2013). Teaching the English active and passive voice with the help of cognitive grammar: An empirical study. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching 3 (4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135829

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Independent-samples t tests on the written form and meaning/use test in the quasi-experimental study. None of the between-group differences reached significance (p > .05); printed eta squared values ranged between .06 and .12.

"not only was STAN better than COG at the very beginning (a difference of 3.09), but this initial advantage was maintained on the immediate (a difference of 6.24) and the delayed posttest (a difference of 5.21), with the caveat that none of these divergences turned out to be statistically significant"

Bielak et al. 2013 (2)¶

Jakub Bielak, Mirosųaw Pawlak, Anna Mystkowska-Wiertelak. (2013). Teaching the English active and passive voice with the help of cognitive grammar: An empirical study. Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching 3 (4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135829

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Independent-samples t tests on the written thematic structure test. All between-group differences were non-significant, with printed eta squared values ranging between .0007 and .01.

"the differences between STAN and COG were much smaller throughout the study. Given that, it is not surprising that no one of them proved to be statistically significant and that they were all of small or very small magnitudes (etas squared ranged between .0007 and .01)"
