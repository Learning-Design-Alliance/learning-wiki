## Evidence

### Kaluna 2020

Kaluna, H., Neal, M., Silva, M., & Trent, T. (2020). A collective insight into the cultural and academic journeys of Native Hawaiians while pursuing careers in physics and astronomy. Input for the Astro2020 Decadal Topic: The State of the Profession and Social Impacts. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.14136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.14136)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

First-person narrative account by one author, a first-generation college student from Pāhoa, a low-income region of Hawai'i Island. She reports that "my dropping out of school" followed the loss of funding, and that a later increase in her father's income allowed her to return and eventually pursue a PhD in astronomy.

> "After my first year of college, the combined lack of my college readiness and my parents’ lack of experience in the college financial aid process lead to my dropping out of school as I no longer had funding to pay for it."

### Kaluna 2020 (2)

Input for the Astro2020 Decadal Topic: The State of the Profession and Social Impacts. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2004.14136](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2004.14136)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

First-person narrative account by an author who worked at restaurants throughout college to help pay rent and groceries while coming from a single-parent home. He reports that multiple scholarships covering tuition "made attending the University of Hawai ʻ i possible".

> "It is only because I received multiple scholarships that covered my tuition, did it make attending the University of Hawai ʻ i possible."
