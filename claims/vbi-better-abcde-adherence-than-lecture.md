## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neonatal healthcare providers taught with video-based instruction adhered better to the ABCDE approach in simulated NALS scenarios than those taught with a conventional lecture (38.8% vs 27.8%, p = 0.026). [→ Linders M 2021](#linders-m-2021)

## Evidence

### Linders M 2021

Linders M, Binkhorst M, Draaisma JMT, van Heijst AFJ, Hogeveen M. (2021). Adherence to the ABCDE approach in relation to the method of instruction: a randomized controlled simulation study. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-021-00509-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled single-blinded simulation trial; 65 percentage scores analyzed from 72 assessed participants trained in an existing NALS program, randomized by training day via SNOSE. The abstract reports the between-group contrast: "mean (SD) scores of 38.8% (18.7) and 27.8% (18.2), respectively (p = 0.026)". No effect size printed.

> "The video-based instruction group (28 participants) adhered better to the ABCDE approach than the lecture group (44 participants), with mean (SD) scores of 38.8% (18.7) and 27.8% (18.2), respectively (p = 0.026)."
