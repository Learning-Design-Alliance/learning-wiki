## Evidence

### Peney 2024

Peney, T., & Skarratt, P. A. (2024). Increasing the immersivity of 360° videos facilitates learning and memory: implications for theory and practice. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10402-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Narrative review of prior work: the article attributes to Parong and Mayer (2021) the finding that HMD immersion improved enjoyment and reduced boredom without improving cognitive outcomes.

> "Results showed that although virtual immersion improved affective processing, with higher levels of enjoyment and lower levels of boredom, the benefits were not reflected in measures of cognitive processing or performance outcomes."

### Peney 2024 (2)

Peney, T., & Skarratt, P. A. (2024). Increasing the immersivity of 360° videos facilitates learning and memory: implications for theory and practice. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-024-10402-9

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The article attributes to Christopoulos et al. (2022) a retention advantage for IVR learners at one week that decayed to the machinima group's level by four weeks, alongside greater reported engagement.

> "Relative to students viewing the machinima, IVR learners showed significantly greater knowledge retention following a lag of one week, although this had dropped to the same level as the machinima group by the four-week mark."
