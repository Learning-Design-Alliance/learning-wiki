# Re-analysis of Wiggins et al.'s public biology data confirms significantly higher positivity for Public Exams than control (p=0.00012)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applying the positivity-rate methodology to Wiggins et al.'s publicly available data shows Public Exam comments were significantly more positive than control comments. [→ Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert 2026](#binisha-marahatta-and-matthew-d-sievert-2026)

## Evidence

### Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert 2026

Binisha Marahatta and Matthew D. Sievert. (2026). Adapting Public Exams for Introductory Physics: A Mixed-Methods Investigation of Student Outcomes. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.03847)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Re-analysis of the publicly available source data accompanying Wiggins et al.'s biology study, using the positivity-rate method because the positive-to-negative ratio would have left only 1.1% of students in the dataset. The authors state this "confirms their conclusions" and underscores the difference observed in the physics context.

> "The mean positivity rates for Public Exams and control were (84.8±31.0)% and (66.1±46.2)%, respectively, and the T-test analysis (Fig. 15) returnedp= 0.00012, indicating that this difference is highly significant."
