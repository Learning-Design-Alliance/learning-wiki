# In a course with frequent assessment, cumulative versus non-cumulative midterm format has no effect on final exam performance in introductory biology

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who took seven cumulative midterms and students who took seven non-cumulative midterms showed no difference in midterm or final exam performance in a frequent-assessment biology course. [→ Bailey Elizabeth G. 2021](#bailey-elizabeth-g-2021)

## Evidence

### Bailey Elizabeth G. 2021

Bailey Elizabeth G., Greenall Rebeka F., Tullis Madeleine M., Williams Kurt R. (2021). The retention benefits of cumulative versus non-cumulative midterms in introductory biology may depend on students’ reasoning skills. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0250143

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated measures ANOVA (Greenhouse-Geisser correction) on shared midterm items and a common cumulative final exam for students in the cumulative and non-cumulative sections; no difference between sections "(F(1, 299) = 0.003, p = 0.95; n = 301)" and no interaction. Multiple regression likewise retained only preparation and scientific reasoning, not midterm format.

> "As shown in Fig 4A, we saw no difference in midterm and final exam performance between the two sections (F(1, 299) = 0.003, p = 0.95; n = 301) nor an interaction between midterm type and exam"
