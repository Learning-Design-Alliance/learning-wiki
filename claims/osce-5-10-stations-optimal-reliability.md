# OSCE designs with 5-10 stations achieve good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.83), outperforming designs with fewer than 5 or more than 10 stations

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Meta-analysis of 23 studies shows OSCEs with 5-10 stations achieve good internal consistency (alpha = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.81-0.84), significantly better than fewer than 5 stations (alpha = 0.75) or more than 10 stations (alpha = 0.77). [→ Peng Qiyun 2025](#peng-qiyun-2025)

## Evidence

### Peng Qiyun 2025

Peng Qiyun, Luo Jiani, Wang Chunyan, Chen Lei, Tan Shenglan. (2025). Impact of station number and duration time per station on the reliability of Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) scores: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06691-0

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 23 studies (26 alpha values) categorized by station number (<5, 5-10, >10). The 5-10 station group achieved the highest pooled alpha of 0.83, meeting the threshold for good internal consistency, while both smaller and larger station counts fell below it.

> "good internal consistency was achieved when the station number was between 5 and 10 (Cronbach's alpha = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.81, 0.84). However, internal consistency was poorer when the station number was less than 5 (Cronbach's alpha = 0.75, 95% CI: 0.72-0.78) or more than 10 stations (Cronbach's alpha = 0.77, 95% CI: 0.75-0.80)."
