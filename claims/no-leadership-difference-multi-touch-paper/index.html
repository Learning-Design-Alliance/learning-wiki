Evidence¶

E. M. Mercier 2014¶

E. M. Mercier, S. E. Higgins, Laura da Costa. (2014). Different leaders: Emergent organizational and intellectual leadership in children's collaborative learning groups. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-014-9201-z

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Comparison of leadership moves by task (math or history) and condition (multi-touch or paper) in study one, with descriptive statistics presented in Table 3. The article reports the number of moves "was almost identical across conditions."

"Results indicate that the number of moves was almost identical across conditions, indicating that the technology did not have an obvious influence on the leadership behaviors of group members."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Distinct intellectual and organizational emergent leader roles, taken by different students, can be identified in about half of collaborative groups
  • Some groups had an intellectual leader without an organizational leader, but no group had an organizational leader without an intellectual leader
  • Leadership moves are distributed among members of children's collaborative learning groups rather than concentrated in a single leader
  • A collaboration kit with joint and private multi-touch table spaces outperformed comparison groups, showing less biased communication and more collaborative processes.
