# Medical students using a virtual simulation experiment system achieved higher operational skill scores than students taught by traditional laboratory methods

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a six-week randomized controlled study, the VSE group outperformed the traditional teaching group on instructor-rated operational skills (89.6 ± 5.2 vs. 81.8 ± 6.3, t = 6.343, p < 0.01). [→ Li Kaiyue 2025](#li-kaiyue-2025)

## Evidence

### Li Kaiyue 2025

Li Kaiyue, Li Qun, Wang Linding, Wang Xiaonan, Ding Xiaojuan, Liu Boyu. (2025). Virtual simulation experiments in medical education: technology acceptance, learning outcomes, and motivational impacts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-08025-6

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled experiment with 90 medical students assigned to a VSE experimental group or traditional teaching control group over six weeks; skills were rated by two independent instructors using the Composition Evaluation Scale. The experimental group's score "significantly surpassed the control group's score of 81.8 ± 6.3 (t = 6.343, p < 0.01)".

> "achieving an average score of 89.6 ± 5.2, which significantly surpassed the control group's score of 81.8 ± 6.3 (t = 6.343, p < 0.01)"
