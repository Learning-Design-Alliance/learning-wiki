## Evidence

### Higham 2023

Higham, P. A., Fastrich, G. M., Potts, R., Murayama, K., Pickering, J. S., & Hadwin, J. A. (2023). Spaced Retrieval Practice: Can Restudying Trump Retrieval? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Planned Holm-corrected t-test in Experiment 1 (n = 259 after exclusions) comparing final cued recall of restudied versus covertly retrieved pairs. The printed effect size was dz = 0.49, favoring restudy, whose mean was "M = 0.74, SD = 0.26".

> "Nor were covertly retrieved pairs better recalled than restudied pairs (M = 0.74, SD = 0.26). In fact, a t-test showed that there was a significant difference in the opposite direction, t(258) = 7.86, p < 0.001, dz = 0.49"

### Higham 2023 (2)

Higham, P. A., Fastrich, G. M., Potts, R., Murayama, K., Pickering, J. S., & Hadwin, J. A. (2023). Spaced Retrieval Practice: Can Restudying Trump Retrieval? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory corrected t-test in the same Experiment 1 sample (n = 259), with printed effect size dz = 0.41, showing "restudied pairs were also better recalled than overtly retrieved pairs". Restudy superiority held for both retrieval formats.

> "An additional exploratory sequentially rejective Bonferroni corrected t-test showed that restudied pairs were also better recalled than overtly retrieved pairs, t(258) = 6.61, p < 0.001, dz = 0.41"
