Evidence¶

Asy’ari 2019¶

Asy’ari, M., Ikhsan, M., & Muhali. (2019). The Effectiveness of Inquiry Learning Model in Improving Prospective Teachers’ Metacognition Knowledge and Metacognition Awareness. International Journal of Instruction, 12(2), 455-470. https://doi.org/10.29333/iji.2019.12229a

q1 · i? · causal · r1

Authors' observation from administering the Metacognition Awareness Inventory in their sample: students could not generalize MAI statements to their actual awareness. No quantitative support is printed for this observation; it is the authors' stated interpretation.

"On the other hand, students generally could not make generalization of statements towards MAI items to be suitable with the actual awareness. Therefore, it is essential to develop instruments for metacognition awareness from the perspectives of domestic (local) customs and culture."

Related Claims¶

  • Questionnaire data may capture learners' perceptions rather than the strategies they actually performed — a broader claim this one bears on
  • A general self-report metacognition questionnaire shows no relationship with word problem solving performance and no convergence with on-line metacognitive measures in grade 5 — related
  • In the case study, students answering the questionnaire while thinking aloud showed misunderstanding of items, general rather than task-specific reference perspectives, middle-category 'neutral' answering, and probable social desirability — related
  • Low questionnaire–think-aloud correlations may have two alternative explanations besides poor self-report ability: the methods may measure different strategies or be aimed at different tasks — related
  • Metacognition assessment is shifting from self-report questionnaires toward process-oriented multimodal methods, which face validity and data-alignment challenges — related
