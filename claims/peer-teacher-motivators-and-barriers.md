## Evidence

### George SS 2025

George SS, Bonnevier A, Stenfors T, Kenne E. (2025). Learning to teach by teaching your peers: exploring students’ needs for training in the undergraduate medical education curriculum. BMC Medical Education 25(1):414. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07022-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Results section of the thematic analysis describing PTs' intrinsic motivation to take peer teaching assignments, including "having fun at the job" and helping junior students learn. No effect sizes are reported; findings are qualitative.

> "Personal gain was, according to PTs, an intrinsic factor resulting from the PT assignment and included having fun at the job, participating in a social activity as well as the positive feeling of doing an altruistic deed by helping junior students learn."

### George SS 2025 (2)

George SS, Bonnevier A, Stenfors T, Kenne E. (2025). Learning to teach by teaching your peers: exploring students’ needs for training in the undergraduate medical education curriculum. BMC Medical Education 25(1):414. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07022-z

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Results section on barriers to becoming a PT: students skipped lectures and lost study time, and combining clinical rotations with teaching on campus was difficult. Peer learners also cited lacking confidence as a personal limitation.

> "A common barrier for becoming, or continuing as a PT, was time and logistics. Students had to skip lectures and lost individual study time to work as a PT."
