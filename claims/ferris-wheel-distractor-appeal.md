## Evidence

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

PIQL administration at a large Pacific Northwest research university found 58% correct on Ferris Wheel; validation interviews at another public university in the Pacific Northwest found "fewer than half of those interviewed chose the correct answer." The article notes the two institutions often have slightly different average PIQL scores.

> "while a majority of students that took the PIQL at the large research university answered the question correctly (58%), fewer than half of those interviewed chose the correct answer."

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2020 (2)

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Suzanne White Brahmia, Andrew Boudreaux, Trevor Smith, and Philip Eaton. (2020). Exploring student facility with “goes like” reasoning in introductory physics. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.zimmerman)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Incorrect-choice analysis of Ferris Wheel across the three-quarter sequence: the "circumference-like" distractor (d) drew 24.5% and the "Pythagorean-like" distractor (a) drew 14.7%, with rates that "does not change signiﬁcantly across the three courses." The article cautions the item is not MCMR so it cannot be compared directly with Flag of Bhutan.

> "The most common incorrect choices were (d) and (a) from Figure 2, with an answer rate 24.5% and 14.7% respectively that does not change signiﬁcantly across the three courses."
