# E-learning programs help informal dementia caregivers feel more confident, reduce perceived stress, and enhance empathy

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across six reviewed studies, e-learning portals/programs/courses helped caregivers feel more confident about dementia care, reduced perceived stress, and enhanced empathy, understanding and concern. [→ Klimova Blanka 2019](#klimova-blanka-2019)

## Evidence

### Klimova Blanka 2019

Klimova Blanka, Valis Martin, Kuca Kamil, Masopust Jiri. (2019). E-learning as valuable caregivers’ support for people with dementia – A systematic review. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-019-4641-9

`q2 · i?`

Systematic review synthesis of six included studies (three RCTs, two survey quality studies, one experimental study) on e-learning for informal dementia caregivers; the review reports these outcomes across the detected studies, consistent with recent review studies it cites.

> "The findings of the detected studies showed that e-learning portals/programs/courses helped caregivers feel more confident about dementia care, enhanced their knowledge and skills, reduced their perceived stress, and enhanced their feelings of empathy, understandings and concern."
