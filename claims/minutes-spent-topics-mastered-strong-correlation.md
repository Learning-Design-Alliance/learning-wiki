## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Daniel P. Kelly and Teomara Rutherford 2017

Daniel P. Kelly and Teomara Rutherford. (2017). Khan Academy as Supplemental Instruction: A Controlled Study of a Computer-Based Mathematics Intervention. International Review of Research in Open and Distributed Learning, 18(4). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1146230

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Pearson correlations within the treatment group (N=39). The article reports a printed effect size of r = .76 between minutes spent and topics mastered, a large correlation, while the correlation with test score was r = .132. The authors note minutes spent was "arguably most unrelated to prior student ability" and thus the least biased test of Khan Academy's impact.

> "It was not associated with student test score, r(37) = .132, p = .422 even though there was a strong positive association between minutes spent using Khan Academy and topics mastered,  r(37) = .76, p = < .001."
