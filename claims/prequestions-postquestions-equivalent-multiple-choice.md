## Evidence

### Lima 2020

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 delayed multiple choice comparison among 27 fifth graders; the prequestion-postquestion difference was not significant ("t(26) = 1.54, p = 0.14, d = 0.29"), while both conditions exceeded restudy.

> "postques- tions were more beneficial than prequestions for cued recall performance, t(26) = 3.40, p = 0.002, d = 0.76, although pre- questions and postquestions yielded equivalent multiple choice performances, t(26) = 1.54, p = 0.14, d = 0.29"

### Lima 2020 (2)

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 delayed multiple choice comparison among 100 fourth graders; no significant prequestion-postquestion difference ("t(99) = 1.40, p = 0.164, d = 0.10"), both above restudy.

> "preques- tions and postquestions yielded indistinguishable performances in the multiple choice test, t(99) = 1.40, p = 0.164, d = 0.10, and both were significantly more beneficial to multiple choice per- formance than restudy"
