Evidence¶

Treiman Rebecca 2013¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r3

Longitudinal retest of the Brazilian prephonological spellers 2 ½ years after the original study, using a standardized spelling test scored for conventional correctness. The relation is reported as a tendency, not with an effect size.

"The more closely the frequencies of the digrams in the children’s early prephonological spellings correlated with the frequencies of those sequences in Portuguese words, the better the children tended to score on the later spelling test."

Treiman Rebecca 2013 (2)¶

Treiman Rebecca, Kessler Brett. (2013). Learning to Use an Alphabetic Writing System. Language Learning and Development. https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2013.812016

q2 · i? · review · r3

Same longitudinal retest: heavy reliance on own-name letters in early spellings was associated with poorer later conventional spelling. The review suggests scrutinizing early writing may inform assessment of later potential.

"The more often the children used letters from their own names in their early prephonological spellings, in contrast, the worse the children tended to perform on the later test of conventional spelling."
