Evidence¶

Seidel 2021¶

Seidel, T., Schnitzler, K., Kosel, C., Stürmer, K., & Holzberger, D. (2021). Student Characteristics in the Eyes of Teachers: Differences Between Novice and Expert Teachers in Judgment Accuracy, Observed Behavioral Cues, and Gaze. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09532-2

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Unpaired t tests on open-ended cue reports (Table 4; n = 20 novices, 7 experts for this measure) showed significant differences favoring novice reporting of hand raising (p = .004, d = − 1.37) and quality of answers (p = .005, d = − 1.34); other indicators did not reach significance.

"novices used student hand raising and the quality of answers significantly more often than experts. Experts used all other indicators more often than novices did, although significance was not reached (Table4)."

Seidel 2021 (2)¶

Seidel, T., Schnitzler, K., Kosel, C., Stürmer, K., & Holzberger, D. (2021). Student Characteristics in the Eyes of Teachers: Differences Between Novice and Expert Teachers in Judgment Accuracy, Observed Behavioral Cues, and Gaze. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09532-2

q3 · i? · associational · r1

Open-answer cue data (27 novices, 7 experts) coded with a 108-code inductive scheme aggregated to 10 indicators (interrater κ = .95); the group difference in indicators used was not statistically significant.

"both novices and experts stated about two to three indicators per student profile (Mnovice = 2.66, SDnovice =0 . 7 2 ;Mexpert =2 . 9 4 ,SDexpert = 0.97), resulting in no significant dif- ference in expertise level, t(25) = − 0.82; p = .422; d = 0.36"

Related Claims¶

  • Expert teachers' classroom perception appears knowledge-driven while novices' appears image-driven, guided by salient visual features — related
  • Expert and novice teachers do not differ significantly in overall judgment accuracy of five student characteristic profiles, though experts judge inconsistent profiles more accurately — related
  • Expert teachers fixate more on struggling, underestimating, and uninterested students during individual seatwork, while novices show longer fixations on strong and overestimating students — related
  • Experienced teachers diagnose underestimating, overestimating, and uninterested student profiles more accurately than novices, with partial eye-tracking support — related
  • Experts detect and correct small changes before events become full-blown, while novices are distracted by possibly irrelevant phenomena — related
  • Expert teachers' fixations are significantly more focused (less dispersed) than novices' when viewing classroom scenes, irrespective of video type — related
  • Experts revisited (monitored) more classroom areas showing student interaction, while novices revisited areas lacking classroom activity — related
  • Expert teachers show expertise-related eye movement patterns in some classroom tasks but not others, indicating task and context specificity — related
  • Eye tracking can be used to study student engagement, teacher expertise, and teacher-student interaction in naturalistic classroom contexts — related
