## Evidence

### Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer 2025

Madeleine Hörnlein and Christoph Kulgemeyer. (2025). Learning Science and the Illusion of Understanding: Exploring the Effects of Integrating Learning Tasks after Explainer Videos. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2512.02824)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quartile-based graphical analysis in Study 1, replicating the approach of Dunning et al. (2003), shown in Fig. 5. Correlations were non-significant in the two lower quartiles (e.g., task group r = 0.082, p = 0.597) and stronger in the upper two (e.g., control group r = 0.272, p = 0.064).

> "Participants in the lower knowledge quartiles scored higher in their belief of understanding than their actual understanding warranted (indicating an illusion of understanding), whereas this discrepancy decreased in the higher quartiles, which even showed a slight tendency to underestimate their abilities"
