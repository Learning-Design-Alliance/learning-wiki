## Evidence

### Zhong Siyi 2026

Zhong Siyi, Tang Xin, Cheng Xiaojun, Pan Yafeng. (2026). Bodily maps of subject-specific feelings and academic emotions among high school students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04283-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pixelwise one-sample t-tests (FDR-corrected, q < 0.05) of bodily sensation maps from the cross-sectional high-school cohort showed this humanities profile; the article reports "increased activation in the head and distal upper limbs" with lower-limb deactivations.

> "Humanities subjects (Chinese, English, Politics, History) were primarily associated with increased activation in the head and distal upper limbs, accompanied by deactivations in the lower limbs."

### Zhong Siyi 2026 (2)

Zhong Siyi, Tang Xin, Cheng Xiaojun, Pan Yafeng. (2026). Bodily maps of subject-specific feelings and academic emotions among high school students. BMC Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-026-04283-1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same FDR-corrected pixelwise t-test analysis of student BSMs showed the science profile of "stronger activation in the head, chest, and proximal upper limbs", with Mathematics as a within-sciences exception.

> "Science subjects (Physics, Chemistry, Biology) generally showed stronger activation in the head, chest, and proximal upper limbs, whereas Mathematics displayed a distinct pattern within the sciences, with increased activity in the head, upper limbs, and upper torso."
