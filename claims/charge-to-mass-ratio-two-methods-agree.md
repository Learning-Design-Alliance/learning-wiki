## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applying Method 1 (height vs. DC voltage fitting) and Method 2 (minimal micromotion) to the same particle yields charge-to-mass ratios of -1.57(5) and -1.63(4) x 10^-3 C/kg, and reduced chi-squared values of 3.22 and 3.48 support the analytic model's accuracy. ## Evidence

### Robert E. Thomas 2025

Robert E. Thomas, Cole E. Wolfram, Noah B. Warren, Isaac J. Fouch, Boris B. Blinov, and Maxwell F. Parsons. (2025). An Accessible Planar Charged Particle Trap for Experiential Learning in Quantum Technologies. arXiv:2410.08301. [doi:10.1119/5.0243389](https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0243389)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Over 20 independent trials of particle height and micromotion data versus central electrode voltage, Methods 1 and 2 were applied to positional data. The article reports "γ1 = −1.57(5) × 10−3 C/kg and γ2 = −1.63(4) × 10−3 C/kg" with reduced chi-squared values of 3.22 and 3.48, which the authors interpret as supporting the analytic planar trap model as an accurate predictive model.

> "Applying Methods 1 and 2 to our positional data, one obtains γ1 = −1.57(5) × 10−3 C/kg and γ2 = −1.63(4) × 10−3 C/kg with respective standard deviations of σ(γ1) = 0 .04 × 10−3 C/kg and σ(γ2) = 0.8×10−3 C/kg."
