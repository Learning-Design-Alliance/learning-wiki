## Evidence

### Deutsch G.B. 2015

Deutsch G.B., Sathyanarayana S.A., Giangola M., Akerman M., DeNoto G., Klein J.D.S., Zemon H., Rubach E. (2015). Competence Acquisition for Single-Incision Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy. JSLS: Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/JSLS.2014.00116

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective chronological analysis of surgeon 4's SILC procedure times. The runs test showed "the number of runs (6 runs) was significantly lower than expected by chance (Z = –3.20)"; the fourth 5-case cohort showed a significant drop (P < .0071).

> "However, for surgeon 4 (laparoscopic fellowship trained and <5 years' experience), most of the procedure times that were above the median were before case 15 and most of the procedure times below the median were observed after that time."

### Deutsch G.B. 2015 (2)

Deutsch G.B., Sathyanarayana S.A., Giangola M., Akerman M., DeNoto G., Klein J.D.S., Zemon H., Rubach E. (2015). Competence Acquisition for Single-Incision Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy. JSLS: Journal of the Society of Laparoendoscopic Surgeons. https://doi.org/10.4293/JSLS.2014.00116

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Five-case cohort analysis of operative times (Figure 2). Surgeon 4's drop-off was significant while the other three surgeons showed no appreciable trend; first-versus-last cohort comparison for surgeon 4 did not reach significance (P < .0542).

> "Surgeons 1, 2, and 3 had no appreciable learning curve, whereas surgeon 4 had a significant drop-off in procedure time during the fourth cohort (P < .0071) (Figure 2)."
