# Mastery-approach goals are positively related to time management, while performance-approach relations are inconsistent and performance-avoidance goals show no association

## Evidence

### Christopher A. Wolters 2025

Christopher A. Wolters, Anna C. Brady, Hyun Ji Lee. (2025). Time Management and Achievement Motivation: A Review of What We Know and Directions for Where to Go. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10032-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports Won et al.'s structural model with US college students in which mastery goals positively predicted self-reported time management (β = 0.27). Corroborating studies include Ranellucci et al. (rs = 0.43, 0.40) and Zhou and Wang (r = 0.74 for mastery-approach).

> "These researchers found that US college students' (N = 385) reported focus on mastery goals was positively correlated (r = 0.39) and a positive predictor (β = 0.27) of their self-reported level of time management in a structural model."

### Christopher A. Wolters 2025 (2)

Christopher A. Wolters, Anna C. Brady, Hyun Ji Lee. (2025). Time Management and Achievement Motivation: A Review of What We Know and Directions for Where to Go. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10032-4

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's summary of the small achievement-goal literature reports inconsistent performance-approach findings (e.g., r = 0.15 in one study, positive correlations in others) and null performance-avoidance relations across studies. No two researchers used the same measures of goals or time management.

> "The relation between performance-approach goals and time management has been less consistent with both positive and null relations reported, whereas the adoption of performance-avoidance goals has not been associated with decreased time management as models of SRL might typically predict."
