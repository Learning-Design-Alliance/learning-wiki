# Novice bronchoscopists' overall performance score rose from 5.9% to 25.5% (p = 0.002) after simulation self-training, in one uncontrolled pre-post study

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` After a self-training program on a high-fidelity bronchoscopy simulator, novices' overall performance score improved significantly from 5.9% to 25.5% (p = 0.002). [→ Veaudor Martin 2018](#veaudor-martin-2018)

## Evidence

### Veaudor Martin 2018

Veaudor Martin, Gérinière Laurence, Souquet Pierre-Jean, Druette Loïc, Martin Xavier, Vergnon Jean-Michel, Couraud Sébastien. (2018). High-fidelity simulation self-training enables novice bronchoscopists to acquire basic bronchoscopy skills comparable to their moderately and highly experienced counterparts. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1304-1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prospective cohort study: eight novice residents completed autonomous simulator self-training and a final evaluation identical to the initial one. The overall performance score "climbed from 5.9% (IQR = 5.1) before self-training to 25.5% (IQR = 26.3) after (P = 0.002)". No effect size printed.

> "Resultantly, the overall performance score for Group A climbed from 5.9% (IQR = 5.1) before self-training to 25.5% (IQR = 26.3) after (P = 0.002), thus becoming comparable to the overall performance scores of Group B (25.3%, IQR = 13.8) and Group C (22.2%, IQR = 5.5)."
