## Evidence

### Dionyssopoulos A 2014

Dionyssopoulos A, Karalis T, Panitsides EA. (2014). Continuing medical education revisited: theoretical assumptions and practical implications: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0278-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Qualitative interview study; all interviewees acknowledged the 2012 hands-on workshop with live models provided an ideal context for active learning, enhancing experiential learning.

> "skills workshops taking place in the context of each meeting were indicated to enhance experiential learning. In the 2012 meeting, a hands-on workshop was organized with live models on new fillers and innovative non-surgical face-lifting techniques"

### Dionyssopoulos A 2014 (2)

Dionyssopoulos A, Karalis T, Panitsides EA. (2014). Continuing medical education revisited: theoretical assumptions and practical implications: a qualitative study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0278-x

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

From the same interview analysis: peer-learning was bidirectional rather than linear from older to younger surgeons; a younger participant reported presenting an innovative technique that attracted the interest of more experienced participants.

> "Peer-learning was also outlined to have taken place, pointing out that knowledge-sharing did not follow a linear mode – from older to younger surgeons – but the younger had some innovative techniques to share as well"
