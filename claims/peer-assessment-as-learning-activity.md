# Peer assessment functions as a learning activity: evaluating peers helps students review their own knowledge and clinical skills

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that peer assessment is a useful learning activity because students review their own knowledge and clinical skills through observing and evaluating the performance of others, learning through participating as assessors. [→ Yu Ji Hye 2021](#yu-ji-hye-2021)

## Evidence

### Yu Ji Hye 2021

Yu Ji Hye, Lee Mi Jin, Kim Soon Sun, Yang Min Jae, Cho Hyo Jung, Noh Choong Kyun, Lee Gil Ho, Lee Su Kyung, Song Mi Ryoung, Lee Jang Hoon, Kim Miran, Jung Yun Jung. (2021). Assessment of medical students’ clinical performance using high-fidelity simulation: comparison of peer and instructor assessment. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02952-w

`q1 · i?` · `associational · r1`

This is the authors' interpretive argument in the Discussion, citing prior work [8, 9]; the article offers no direct measurement of this learning effect in its own data. The article states it "can be inferred" that simulation-based peer observation has an educational effect.

> "Peer assessment is a useful learning activity that allows one to review one’s own knowledge and clinical skills through observing and evaluating the performance of others [9]. In other words, it is meaningful as a learning tool in that students learn through the process of participating in evaluation as assessors [8]."
