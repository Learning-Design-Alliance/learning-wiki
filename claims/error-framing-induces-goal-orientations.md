# Error framing is expected to induce differential mastery and performance-avoid orientations, shaping self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q1`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Framing errors as instructive induces a mastery orientation, while instructing trainees to avoid errors induces a performance-avoid orientation; neither framing is expected to affect performance-prove goals. [→ Bell 2008](#bell-2008)

## Evidence

### Bell 2008

Bell, B. S., & Kozlowski, S. W. J. (2008). Active learning: Effects of core training design elements on self-regulatory processes, learning, and adaptability. Journal of Applied Psychology, 93(2), 296-316. http://digitalcommons.ilr.cornell.edu/articles/410/

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Theoretical argument in the Training Frame section: errors serve as salient feedback in complex task learning, and how learners interpret errors distinguishes goal-orientation dimensions. The article also states the inductions are not expected to significantly impact performance-prove goals since neither approach emphasizes performance demonstration.

> "Thus, we expect that the differential framing of errors will induce different levels of mastery and performance-avoid orientations, which, in turn, are expected to influence trainees' self-efficacy and intrinsic motivation."
