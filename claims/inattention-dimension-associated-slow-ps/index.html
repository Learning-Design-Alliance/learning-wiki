Evidence¶

Braaten EB 2020¶

Braaten EB, Ward AK, Forchelli G, Vuijk PJ, Cook NE, McGuinness P, Lee BA, Samkavitz A, Lind H, O'Keefe SM, Doyle AE. (2020). Characteristics of child psychiatric outpatients with slow processing speed and potential mechanisms of academic impact. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01455-w

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Logistic regression in 492 youth with complete dimensional ratings (Table 5) tested eight symptom dimensions as predictors of slow PS, controlling for age, sex, and medication. Girls were less at risk (OR = 0.46, p = .001) and medicated youth more at risk (OR = 2.00, p = .003).

"when we related all dimensions to slow PS in a single model, only inattention symptoms showed a significant association. Here, an increase of one SD in inattention symptoms increased the risk of endorsing PS problems 1.4 fold ([95% CI 1.10 – 1.87], p = .007)."

Braaten EB 2020 (2)¶

Braaten EB, Ward AK, Forchelli G, Vuijk PJ, Cook NE, McGuinness P, Lee BA, Samkavitz A, Lind H, O'Keefe SM, Doyle AE. (2020). Characteristics of child psychiatric outpatients with slow processing speed and potential mechanisms of academic impact. European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-019-01455-w

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Diagnostic-group logistic regressions were re-run with inattention symptoms in the model. Associations for psychosis and ASD remained significant, suggesting inattention symptoms were not driving these associations; the ADHD trend was reduced further (OR = 1.11, p = .66).

"significant OR's were found for psychosis (OR = 5.51, 95% CI 2.16 – 14.05, p < .001), ASD (OR = 1.98, 95% CI 1.25 – 3.14, p = .004) and inattention symptoms (OR = 1.31, 95% CI 1.06 – 1.63, p = .01)."
