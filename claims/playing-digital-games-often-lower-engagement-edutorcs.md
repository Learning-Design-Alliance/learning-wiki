## Evidence

### Coller et al. 2011

B. D. Coller, D. J. Shernoff, and A. D. Strati. (2011). Measuring Engagement as Students Learn Dynamic Systems and Control with a Video Game. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076061

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Separate two-level models were conducted for each of six dependent variables using only the participants and self-reports in Year 3 in which students indicated that they were playing the video game. Playing Sports and Driving games in particular was not a significant predictor of any of the experiential variables.

> "playing digital games often outside of class corresponded to significantly lower levels of Engagement, Intellectual Intensity, and Intrinsic Motivation, and higher levels of Negative Affect. Gamers were also less likely to describe their homework “like work and play” than those who played digital games less frequently."
