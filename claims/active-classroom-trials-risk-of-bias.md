# Most included active-classroom trials carry some or high risk of bias, chiefly from the randomization process

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Only one of 25 studies had low overall risk of bias; randomization-process bias was rated at least some concern in 14 of 25 studies. [→ Bedard Chloe 2019](#bedard-chloe-2019)

## Evidence

### Bedard Chloe 2019

Bedard Chloe, St John Laura, Bremer Emily, Graham Jeffrey D., Cairney John. (2019). A systematic review and meta-analysis on the effects of physically active classrooms on educational and enjoyment outcomes in school age children. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0218633

`q3 · i?`

Risk of bias assessment of the 25 included trials using the Cochrane RoB 2.0 tool with cluster-trial considerations, by two independent reviewers. Poor reporting of randomization and imbalanced baseline factors drove the randomization-domain concerns (Table 1).

> "Only one study had low risk of bias and the remaining studies were judged as either having some concerns or high risk of bias. Risk of bias arising due to the randomization process was most problematic as it was rated as at least some concern for bias in 14 of the 25 studies"
