## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Paul Justice 2026

Paul Justice, Emily Marshman, and Chandralekha Singh. (2026). Supporting physics instructors to use a variety of evidence-based approaches to improve student learning: An example from quantum mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.00329](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.00329)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Cross-year comparison using the same rubric as the tutorial study of Zhu et al. (N=26, effect sizes not available); the article reports similar posttest scores for the CQS and tutorial groups on questions (a) and (c), while tutorial students did better on question (d).

> "the comparison of the posttest scores of the CQS group and the tutorial group in Tables 1 and 2 suggests that the CQS is effective in helping students learn to construct a complete set of basis states (question (a)) and calculate matrix elements for the magnetic field -spin interaction Hamiltonian (question (c)), garnering similar posttest scores to those of students who engaged with the tutorial."
