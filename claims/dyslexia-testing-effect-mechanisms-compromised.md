## Evidence

### Wilschut 2026

Wilschut, T., Sense, F. & van Rijn, H. (2026). Trends in testing effect research: from lab to classroom, but not yet for all learners. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00400-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument grounded in reviewed EEG/MEG findings of reduced or delayed N400 responses in dyslexic readers. The authors argue that "the associative spreading activation processes that support elaborative246 retrieval are compromised in at least some learning contexts."

> "Regardless of244 whether the bottleneck occurs at the level of low-level sensory processing or higher-level semantic245 integration, it is likely that the associative spreading activation processes that support elaborative246 retrieval are compromised in at least some learning contexts."

### Wilschut 2026 (2)

Wilschut, T., Sense, F. & van Rijn, H. (2026). Trends in testing effect research: from lab to classroom, but not yet for all learners. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00400-2

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The perspective reports, citing Eskenazi & Nix, that increasing difficulty improved learning for skilled spellers but impaired it for less skilled spellers, and cites parallel findings from adapted e-learning, item-exposure, and rule-learning studies — "Optimal difficulty levels appear to differ252 between learners with and without dyslexia."

> "This leads to aninverse-U shaped251 relationship between task difficulty and learning gains [72]. Optimal difficulty levels appear to differ252 between learners with and without dyslexia."
