## Evidence

### Shavelson et al. 1999

Richard J. Shavelson, Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo, Edward W. Wiley. (1999). Note on Sources of Sampling Variability in Science Performance Assessments. Journal of Educational Measurement, 36(1), 61-71. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Note+on+Sources+of+Sampling+Variability+in+Science+Performance+Assessments

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Retest data from a random sample of 29 students allowed computing DO-NB correlations with performances separated by several months. The authors report "TDO.NB = .520 and .525 for electric mysteries and bugs, respectively", which jibe with computer-simulation correlations, showing the original DO-NB convergence was inflated by same-occasion confounding.

> "Averaging the two estimates, we get TDO.NB = .520 and .525 for electric mysteries and bugs, respectively."

### Shavelson et al. 1999 (2)

Richard J. Shavelson, Maria Araceli Ruiz-Primo, Edward W. Wiley. (1999). Note on Sources of Sampling Variability in Science Performance Assessments. Journal of Educational Measurement, 36(1), 61-71. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Note+on+Sources+of+Sampling+Variability+in+Science+Performance+Assessments

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors cite a multivariate G study that disattenuated the method correlations, reported here as the authors' summary of that analysis rather than a new computation in this note.

> "Gao, Shavelson, Brennan, and Baxter, (1996) showed a pattern of high correlations among the direct observation, notebook, and computer simulation methods but not short-answer or multiple-choice, even when DO and NB were based on the same investigation."
