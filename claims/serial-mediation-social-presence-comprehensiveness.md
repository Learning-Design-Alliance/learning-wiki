# The modality effect on inference-based questions is serially mediated by personal references and comprehensiveness of explanations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A significant indirect effect via number of personal references → number of concepts emerged (a1 × d21 × b2 = .09, 95% CI [.010, .313]), with no direct effects via social presence or comprehensiveness alone. [→ Jacob 2019](#jacob-2019)

## Evidence

### Jacob 2019

Jacob, L., Lachner, A., & Scheiter, K. (2019). Learning by Explaining Orally or In Written Form? Text Difficulty Matters. https://aspredicted.org/sv3zv.pdf

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Bootstrapped serial mediation analysis (10,000 simulations, Hayes 2013) with learning activity as predictor, personal references and concepts as serial mediators, and inference-based performance in the high-difficult text as dependent variable. The article reports "a significant indirect effect" with no direct effects via social presence or comprehensiveness.

> "Results indicated a significant indirect effect (via number of personal references → number of concepts) of a1 × d21 × b2 = .09, 95% CI [.010, .313], as zero was not included in the confidence interval (see Figure 1, for the full mediation model)."
