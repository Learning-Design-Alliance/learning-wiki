Evidence¶

Brevik A 2026¶

Brevik A, Jerpseth H and Lafontan SR (2026). Large language models as grading assistants in public health education: a method-comparison study of essay-style exam assessment. Front. Educ. 11:1904452. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2026.1904452

q3 · i? · design · r2

Single-file comparison of four LLMs against human grades on 32 submissions. Table 1 shows fast-mode kappas of 0.269 (ChatGPT), 0.287 (Gemini), 0.070 (LeChat), 0.227 (Kimi); thinking mode raised them for ChatGPT (0.718), Kimi (0.571) and LeChat (0.186) but lowered Gemini's (0.237); Gemini Pro, tested only in thinking mode, reached 0.380. In thinking mode "the LLMs utilized more of the full grading scale".

"The weighted Cohen's kappa measuring inter-rater reliability for grading method comparisons indicated that the agreement between human graders and fast LLM grading was not high."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • ChatGPT in thinking mode shows the strongest agreement with human examiners on essay-style exam grading (weighted kappa 0.718) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Kimi shows reasonable but weaker agreement with human examiners than ChatGPT (weighted kappa 0.571) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • LLMs use fewer extreme grades than human examiners, with grade-dependent opposing biases compressing grades toward the middle — related
  • Hint-based prompts raise ChatGPT scoring agreement with human experts to 98–100%, while no-hint prompts are unstable (Q2 precision 0.42) — related
  • Prior non-LLM automated coding approaches yielded variable and often lower agreement with human coders on teacher knowledge measures, especially PCK — related
  • GPT-4 classifies self-affirmation essays reasonably in zero-shot mode but below fine-tuned BERT, and few-shot prompting does not appreciably improve it — related
  • Human inter-rater agreement for FRISCO essay scoring was reliable, with mean kappa 0.78–0.84 and percentage of agreement above 75% in all six dimensions — related
  • LLM graders show model-specific precision/recall trade-offs, so a single metric is insufficient to judge grading quality — related
  • Claude3 and PaLM2 show substantial agreement but fall short on identifying failing grades — related
  • Newer LLM generations do not consistently agree better with human essay graders — related
  • Gemini and Gemini Pro show the highest internal grading consistency across file upload volumes, with no clear monotonic lost-in-the-middle effect — related
  • ChatGPT 3.5 grading with the best prompt was significantly lower than official IELTS examiner grades, indicating systematic bias in absolute grading — related
