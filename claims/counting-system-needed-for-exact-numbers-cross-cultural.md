## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adults in cultures with limited number lexicons and no counting system, and deaf adults with number signs not embedded in a counting system, do not develop exact representations of large numerosities. [→ Noël M-P and Rousselle L 2011](#noel-m-p-and-rousselle-l-2011)

## Evidence

### Noël M-P and Rousselle L 2011

Noël M-P and Rousselle L (2011) Developmental changes in the profiles of dyscalculia: an explanation based on a double exact-and-approximate number representation model. Front. Hum. Neurosci. 5:165. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00165

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review cites cross-cultural studies (Gordon, 2004; Pica et al., 2004) and, separately, deaf adults with home-sign number systems (Spaepen et al., 2011), attributing these findings to those works; the review's own synthesis reports the pattern without effect sizes.

> "adults leaving in cultures with very limited number lexicon and no counting system are unable to develop an exact representa- tion of numbers beyond 3 or 4, although their ANS is quite normal (Gordon, 2004; Pica et al., 2004 )"
