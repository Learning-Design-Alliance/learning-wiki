Flowers 1996¶

Flowers, C. P., Oshima, C., & Raju, N. (1996). A Description and Demonstration of the Polytomous-DFIT Framework. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED401319

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Monte Carlo simulation study generating graded-response data (five categories, 1,000 examinees per group) across manipulated test length, impact, DIF proportion, direction, and type. Related Claims¶

  • The polytomous DFIT framework shows Type I error rates close to nominal alpha except when the number of DIF items and DIF magnitude are highest — related
  • Type of DIF affected detection: nonuniform DIF items with higher a-parameters were not detected whereas lower a-parameter items were — related
  • Test length, focal group distribution, number of DIF items, and direction of DIF had little effect on detection rates — related
  • C-DIF was less stable than NC-DIF across simulated conditions — related
  • Type I error rates of all three polytomous DIF indices increase as the number of DIF items and DIF magnitude increase — related
  • DIF detection rates for all indices are higher with larger samples, equivalent distributions, fewer DIF items, greater DIF magnitude, and larger a-parameters — related
  • The chi-square significance test for NC-DIF is overly sensitive at large sample sizes, requiring an empirical cutoff value — related
  • NCDIF Type I error rates are unaffected by focal group distribution, unlike SIBTEST and Lord's chi-square — related
