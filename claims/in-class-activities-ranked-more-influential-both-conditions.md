# Students in both conditions rank in-class instructor-facilitated activities as more influential to learning than at-home work, regardless of phase

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Majorities in both conditions ranked in-class activities above at-home activities, and the percentages did not differ between conditions. [→ Jensen Jamie L. 2015](#jensen-jamie-l-2015)

## Evidence

### Jensen Jamie L. 2015

Jensen Jamie L., Kummer Tyler A., Godoy Patricia D. d. M. (2015). Improvements from a Flipped Classroom May Simply Be the Fruits of Active Learning. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.14-08-0129

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Survey ranking item (n = 47 nonflipped, 50 flipped): cross-tabulation showed the percentages equal (χ2 = 1.19, p = 0.28). The authors interpret this as instructor and/or peer presence mattering more than which learning phase the activity serves.

> "the in-class activities were ranked as more influential in student learning than the assignments done at home in both conditions (66.0% in the nonflipped condition, 76.0% in the flipped condition)."
