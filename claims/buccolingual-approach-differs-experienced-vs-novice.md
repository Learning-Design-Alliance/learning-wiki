## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean buccolingual deviation was +4.0° ± 1.4° for Novice, −4.8 ± 1.1° for Experienced (t = 5.004, P < 0.0001), and +2.4 ± 0.7° for Simulator, with no significant difference between Novice and Simulation groups. [→ Joseph David 2014](#joseph-david-2014)

## Evidence

### Joseph David 2014

Joseph David, Jehl Jean-Philippe, Maureira Pablo, Perrenot Cyril, Miller Neal, Bravetti Pierre, Ambrosini Pascal, Tran Nguyen. (2014). Relative Contribution of Haptic Technology to Assessment and Training in Implantology. BioMed Research International. https://doi.org/10.1155/2014/413951

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Three-group resin-model comparison of buccolingual angulation; the article reports the Experienced-versus-Novice contrast as significant and states the significance level "was not reached between the "Novice" and "Simulation" groups". Only t and P values are printed.

> "the average buccolingual deviation was −4.8 ± 1.1° (t = 5.004, P < 0.0001 versus "Novice"). After completing eight simulation sessions, 3rd year students in the "Simulator" group still drilled with a slightly buccolingually directed axis, the mean deviation being measured as an angle of +2.4 ± 0.7°."
