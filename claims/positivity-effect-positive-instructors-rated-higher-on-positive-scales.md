# Participants rated happy and content instructors higher on positive than negative emotion scales, supporting the positivity hypothesis

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The happy instructor was rated significantly higher on positive scales than negative scales (d = 1.35), regardless of agent presence. [→ Lawson 2022](#lawson-2022)
`q3 i3` The content instructor was rated significantly higher on positive scales than negative scales (d = 1.18), regardless of agent presence. [→ Lawson 2022 (2)](#lawson-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Lawson 2022

Lawson, A. P., & Mayer, R. E. (2022). The Power of Voice to Convey Emotion in Multimedia Instructional Messages. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00282-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

The 2×2×2 valence-by-activity-by-agent ANOVA on happy-instructor ratings in the Mturk experiment showed a main effect for positive vs. negative emotion, with "no significant interaction with presence of agent".

> "participants rated the happy instructor significantly higher on positive scales ( M = 3.75, SD = 0.71) than on negative scales ( M = 1.96, SD = 1.20), F(1, 99) = 182.99, p < 0.001, d = 1.35"

### Lawson 2022 (2)

Lawson, A. P., & Mayer, R. E. (2022). The Power of Voice to Convey Emotion in Multimedia Instructional Messages. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00282-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

The 2×2×2 ANOVA on content-instructor ratings in the same experiment showed a main effect for positive vs. negative emotion, with no significant interaction with presence of agent.

> "participants rated the content instructor significantly higher on positive scales ( M = 3.42, SD = 0.67) than on negative scales (M = 2.15, SD = 1.14), F(1, 99) = 142.25, p < 0.001, d = 1.18"
