# Idaho charter school students show stronger reading growth than matched TPS peers, with no significant difference in math growth

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Charter students in Idaho experience higher academic progress than their TPS peers in reading, equivalent to 24 additional days of learning in a 180-day school year. [→ CREDO 2019](#credo-2019)
`q2 i?` The difference in math growth between Idaho charter students and their TPS VCRs is not statistically significant. [→ CREDO 2019 (2)](#credo-2019-2)

## Evidence

### CREDO 2019

CREDO, Center for Research on Education Outcomes, Stanford University. (2019). Charter School Performance in Idaho. https://credo.stanford.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Statewide quasi-experimental analysis comparing all matched Idaho charter students with their Virtual Control Records across two growth periods, 2014-15 to 2016-17. Charter students show "higher academic progress than their TPS peers in reading," equal to 24 additional days of learning.

> "charter students in Idah o experience higher academic progress than their TPS peers in reading. This advantage for charter students is equivalent to 24 additional days of learning in reading in a 180 -day school year."

### CREDO 2019 (2)

CREDO, Center for Research on Education Outcomes, Stanford University. (2019). Charter School Performance in Idaho. https://credo.stanford.edu

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The same statewide VCR comparison finds no statistically significant math difference; the report states charter students experience "similar growth in the 180 -da y period as they would have in a traditional school setting." No effect size is printed.

> "Because the difference in the growth in math is no t statistically significant, Idah o charter students experience similar growth in the 180 -da y period as they would have in a traditional school setting."
