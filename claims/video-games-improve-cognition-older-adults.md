### Kueider Alexandra M. 2012

Kueider Alexandra M., Parisi Jeanine M., Gross Alden L., Rebok George W. (2012). Computerized Cognitive Training with Older Adults: A Systematic Review. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0040588

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Review synthesis of eight video game studies in older adults aged 50 to 87. The review reports video games "appear to be an effective means of enhancing reaction time, processing speed, executive function, and global cognition" with median effect sizes of 0.77 and 0.72 for reaction time and processing speed respectively.

> "Based on the evidence reviewed, video games appear to be an effective means of enhancing reaction time, processing speed, executive function, and global cognition in older adults. Effect sizes for reaction time ranged from 0.33 [55] to 1.11 [56] with a median effect size of 0.77; for processing speed effect sizes ranged from 0.31 to 0.98 [54] with a median effect size of 0.72."
