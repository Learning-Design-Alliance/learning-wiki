# The Elevate Math summer program had no significant effects on students' math interest or math self-efficacy

## Evidence

### Snipes 2016

Snipes, J., Huang, C.-W., Jaquet, K., & Finkelstein, N. (2016). The effects of the Elevate Math summer program on math achievement and algebra readiness. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567052

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Null result from the summer 2014 randomized trial's student survey measures: a five-item math interest scale and a seven-item math self-efficacy scale drawn primarily from a survey developed by Gilbert (2008). No effect size is printed for either outcome, so impacts are reported as not statistically significant without a magnitude.

> "There were no significant impacts on math interest or math self-efficacy. The estimated level of interest in math for the treatment group was higher than that of the control group, but the difference was not statistically significant; there was no evidence of positive effects on math self- efficacy"
