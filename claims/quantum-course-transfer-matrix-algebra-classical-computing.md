## Evidence

### Joel A. Walsh 2021

Joel A. Walsh, Mic Fenech, Derrick L. Tucker, Catherine Riegle-Crumb, and Brian R. La Cour. (2021). Piloting a Full-year, Optics-based High School Course on Quantum Computing. Physics Education. https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ac3dc2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

End-of-the-year focus groups conducted after the pilot course, a qualitative outcome beyond the expected quantum topics. Students self-reported stronger understanding of "matrix algebra and classical computing"; the article prints no effect size.

> "While it is certainly expected that students would leave with a better grasp of topics central to quantum information science, like optics or waves, our end-of-the-year focus groups revealed that some of the students also left with a stronger self-reported understanding of matrix algebra and classical computing."

### Joel A. Walsh 2021 (2)

Joel A. Walsh, Mic Fenech, Derrick L. Tucker, Catherine Riegle-Crumb, and Brian R. La Cour. (2021). Piloting a Full-year, Optics-based High School Course on Quantum Computing. Physics Education. https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ac3dc2

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Focus-group report from one three-student group in the pilot course, describing application of matrices outside solving linear systems. The students reported "a better grasp on how to perform matrix operations" and that matrices can represent physical objects and model transformations; no quantitative measure is given.

> "For example, all three of the students in one of the focus groups reported that they had a better grasp on how to perform matrix operations, and also remarked that they also learned how matrices can represent physical objects like optical wave plates, and that matrices can be used to model transformations."
