## Evidence

### Knight 1921

Knight, F. B., & Franzen, R. H. (1921). Pitfalls in rating schemes. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073817

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

A second rating study of 71 juniors used A–E grades with a normal-curve caution instead of ranking 1 to 34, to reduce task difficulty acting as chance error. The article reports the introspective order was again nearer the ideal, substantiating over-rating.

> "As the introspective order is again nearer the ideal, the data further substantiate the fact of serious tendency of over-rating one's self."

### Knight 1921 (2)

Knight, F. B., & Franzen, R. H. (1921). Pitfalls in rating schemes. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0073817

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Sixty juniors rated themselves and the typical junior on a ten-item trustworthiness Self-Analysis Test, anonymously. The article reports 'Always' totals of 53 objective versus 301 introspective, and 'Sometimes' totals of 242 versus 44.

> "Looking at the column headed "Always," which denotes superiority when rating is objective, the total is 53; when introspective, 301."
