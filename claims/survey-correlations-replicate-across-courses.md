## Evidence

### N. M. Chase 2024

N. M. Chase. (2024). Survey on the Teacher-Learner Collaboration. MCPHS University. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.02964](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.02964)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The author's extension of the survey to a colleague's organic chemistry course; the article attributes minor differences to physics courses' greater emphasis on problem-solving versus organic chemistry's emphasis on factual recall.

> "When an almost identical survey was used in a large lecture course in Organic Chemistry (taught by a colleague  of the author)[3], the resulting  bar graphs showed correlations  similar  to  many of  those presented here for physics"

### N. M. Chase 2024 (2)

N. M. Chase. (2024). Survey on the Teacher-Learner Collaboration. MCPHS University. Originally published 2020. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2401.02964](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.02964)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of merged Physics Lab Survey data for Sp 2015 through F 2019 for two lab instructors, analyzed separately; sample sizes printed as 134 and 194. The author speculates differences in teaching styles may be the root cause but defers further study to future work.

> "for one lab instructor, the “Lab Overall ” rating was significantly correlated with both  Mathematics  Ability  and  GPA;  for  the  second  instructor,  there  was  only  a  weak  correlation between the same pairs of variables."
