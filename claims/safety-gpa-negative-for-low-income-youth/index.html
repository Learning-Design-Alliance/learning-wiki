Evidence¶

Martin-Storey 2014¶

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Multi-group SEM by income-to-needs ratio (n = 199 low-income; n = 725 other) predicting grade-9 GPA. The printed magnitude for the low-income group was a 17% of an SD decrease per SD of perceived safety; no standardised effect size was printed.

"For youth from low-income families, each standard deviation increase in perceived neighborhood safety was associated with 17% of a standard deviation decrease in GPA."

Martin-Storey 2014 (2)¶

Martin-Storey, A., & Crosnoe, R. (2014). Perceived Neighborhood Safety and Adolescent School Functioning. Applied Developmental Science, 18(2), 61-75. https://doi.org/10.1080/10888691.2014.876276

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Same multi-group models: the safety-GPA association was non-significant in the non-low-income group, and a constrained-model comparison indicated the group difference was significant.

"For youth who were not from low-income families, no significant association between perceived safety and GPA was observed. Follow-up multi group analyses with a simplified constrained model suggested that this differences was significant (Chi2 diff = 106.80, p < .001)."
