Evidence¶

Barth 2000¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Analysis of 1993-94 data reported in Table 5 compares teachers lacking a major or minor in their field across poverty levels. The article prints the contrast as a likelihood ratio (1.8 times), not a standardized effect size.

"students attending secondary schools with large concentrations (75%) of poor children are 1.8 times as likely to be taught by teachers without a major in their fields as students attending low-poverty (10%) schools."

Barth 2000 (2)¶

Barth, Patte, Ed. (2000). Honor in the Boxcar: Equalizing Teacher Quality. Thinking K-16; v4 n1 Spr 2000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED458350

q2 · i? · associational · r1

The same Table 5 analysis compares teachers lacking a field major across school racial composition. The article reports the ratio as 1.4 times, with no standardized effect size printed.

"Similarly, students attending high minority (75% Black and/or Latino) schools are 1.4 times as likely as students in low minority (10% Black and/or Latino) to be taught by a teacher without a major in the subject."
