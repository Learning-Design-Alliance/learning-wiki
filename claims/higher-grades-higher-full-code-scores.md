# Students with higher course grades achieved higher Full Code scores, significant overall and in the clinical medicine courses but not in surgery courses

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Median Full Code scores rose across grade bands (71.1 for grades 9–13, 82.3 for 14–16, 82.5 for 17–19), a significant difference overall (p < 0.001) and in Clinical Medicine I (p = 0.001) and Clinical Medicine II (p = 0.006), but not in Surgery I (p = 0.679) or Surgery II (p = 0.324). [→ Salafia Maria Amalia 2025](#salafia-maria-amalia-2025)

## Evidence

### Salafia Maria Amalia 2025

Salafia Maria Amalia, Perez-Ochoa María Elena. (2025). Game-based educational experience in clinical simulation and academic achievement in medical students: a retrospective study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07070-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis H comparisons of Full Code scores across the three course-grade bands in the retrospective study of 518 case-completing students. The article reports medians of 71.1, 82.3 and 82.5 with a significant difference between Clinical Medicine I and II; Table 3 prints p < 0.001 globally.

> "The median global score was 71.1 (range: 17.0, 99.1) for students with grades between 9 and 13, 82.3 (range: 4.9, 100.0), and 82.5 (range: 1.8, 100.0) for those with grades between 14 and 16 and 17–19, respectively, which represented a significant difference (p < 0.05) between Clinical Medicine I and II."
