# Greater SLD stigma is associated with lower self-esteem, with a pooled medium-sized correlation (r = −.39) across nine effect sizes from six studies

## Evidence

### Haft Stephanie L. 2023

Haft Stephanie L., Greiner de Magalhães Caroline, Hoeft Fumiko. (2023). A Systematic Review of the Consequences of Stigma and Stereotype Threat for Individuals With Specific Learning Disabilities. Journal of Learning Disabilities. https://doi.org/10.1177/00222194221087383

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis using robust variance estimation with a random-effects model pooled nine correlation coefficients from six studies of SLD stigma and self-esteem (including one self-worth measure). The pooled association was "statistically significant and negative overall association (r = −.39, 95% CI = [−.60, −.24], p = .002)" with moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 42.2%, τ2 = 0.014); sensitivity analyses found no outliers.

> "In pooling the nine effect sizes (correlation coefficients) from six studies of the association between SLD stigma and self-esteem, there was a statistically significant and negative overall association (r = −.39, 95% CI = [−.60, −.24], p = .002). There was moderate heterogeneity in effect sizes (I2 = 42.2%, τ2 = 0.014)."
