## Evidence

### Mike Verostek 2021

Mike Verostek, Casey Miller and Benjamin Zwickl. (2021). Analyzing admissions metrics as predictors of graduate GPA and whether graduate GPA mediates PhD completion. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.17.020115)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Point-biserial comparisons among US students in the dataset, shown in Figure 3(b) and 3(c). The article reports no GGPA difference by gender while the GRE-P gap between males and females is statistically significant.

> "there is no statistical diﬀerence between average GGPA for males and females ( rpb =−0.01, p = 0.57, equivalent to a non-signiﬁcant independent t-test), as demonstrated in Figure 3(b). Yet there exists a sta­tistically signiﬁcant diﬀerence between males and fe­males in GRE-P performance in our data ( rpb =−0.30, p<. 001, equivalent to a statistically signiﬁcant indepen­dent t-test)."
