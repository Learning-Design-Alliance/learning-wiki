## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2017

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Lecture-demonstration interviews with an electron beam and bar magnets, including the collinear field-and-velocity case: students "explained that the particle will slow down" when the field opposes the velocity, inferring a force opposite to the velocity.

> "explained that the particle will slow down because the magnitude of the magnetic field is opposite to the direction of velocity and this implies that the force on the charged particle must be opposite to the velocity"

### Chandralekha Singh 2017 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2017). Improving Students' Understanding of Magnetism. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1701.01523)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Pre/post results for the net-force question with oppositely directed electric and magnetic fields: "49% of students provided the correct response on post-test, three times the number on the pre-test"; the common error was option (c).

> "Table 1 shows that 49% of students provided the correct response on post-test, three times the number on the pre-test."
