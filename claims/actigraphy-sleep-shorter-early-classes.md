# Actigraphy-verified nocturnal sleep is more than an hour shorter before 08:00 classes because students wake earlier, with greater social jet lag

## Evidence

### Yeo Sing Chen 2023

Yeo Sing Chen, Lai Clin K. Y., Tan Jacinda, Lim Samantha, Chandramoghan Yuvan, Tan Teck Kiang, Gooley Joshua J. (2023). Early morning university classes are associated with impaired sleep and academic performance. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01531-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Six-week actigraphy study in 181 undergraduates (7,329 nocturnal sleep recordings), sorted by first class of the day with linear mixed-effects models; the printed Tukey difference of −1.67 h is a mean difference, not a standardised effect size, so no i value is claimed.

> "Students whose first class took place at 08:00 advanced their sleep offset by about 1.7 h relative to non-school days (difference in sleep offset relative to days with no classes, Tukey's test: 08:00, −1.67 h, 95% CI = −1.78 h to −1.54 h, P < 0.001"

### Yeo Sing Chen 2023 (2)

Yeo Sing Chen, Lai Clin K. Y., Tan Jacinda, Lim Samantha, Chandramoghan Yuvan, Tan Teck Kiang, Gooley Joshua J. (2023). Early morning university classes are associated with impaired sleep and academic performance. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01531-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same actigraphy dataset; nocturnal total sleep time and time in bed decreased with earlier class start times, and 08:00 nights were shorter than all other start times (e.g. −1.27 h vs 16:00). The printed values are mean differences; no standardised effect size appears in the quote.

> "Sleep was more than 1 h shorter in students with classes at 08:00 relative to non-school days, adjusting for covariates (difference in nocturnal total sleep time relative to days with no classes, Tukey's test: 08:00, −1.16 h, 95% CI = −1.28 h to −1.05 h, P < 0.001"

### Yeo Sing Chen 2023 (3)

Yeo Sing Chen, Lai Clin K. Y., Tan Jacinda, Lim Samantha, Chandramoghan Yuvan, Tan Teck Kiang, Gooley Joshua J. (2023). Early morning university classes are associated with impaired sleep and academic performance. Nature Human Behaviour. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01531-x

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Same actigraphy study; the −1.02 h midpoint advance is interpreted by the authors as greater social jet lag. Sleep onset varied little across class start times. No standardised effect size is printed in the quote, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "Students' midpoint of sleep occurred about 1 h earlier when they had a class at 08:00 on the following day, compared with days with no classes (difference in midpoint of sleep relative to days with no classes, Tukey's test: 08:00, −1.02 h, 95% CI = −1.11 h to −0.93 h, P < 0.001"
