# No media effect for process knowledge: animation did not improve learning of dynamic content

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The animation+voice setting did not produce better process knowledge than picture+voice or picture+text; the hypothesis that animations better support dynamic content learning was rejected. [→ Guttormsen Schär 2007](#guttormsen-schar-2007)

## Evidence

### Guttormsen Schär 2007

Guttormsen Schär, S., & Zimmermann, P. G. (2007). Investigating Means to Reduce Cognitive Load from Animations: Applying Differentiated Measures of Knowledge Representation. Journal of Research on Technology in Education, 40(1), 64–78. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ826063 [doi:10.1080/15391523.2007.10782497](https://doi.org/10.1080/15391523.2007.10782497)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

In the same 36-participant three-group experiment, the process-knowledge measures (Table 5 means PT 7.07, PV 9.27, AV 8.76) showed no significant media order effect, so the static-dynamic assumption could not be evaluated in this experiment.

> "Hypothesis 3 was rejected, because there was no effect of media for process knowledge at all."
