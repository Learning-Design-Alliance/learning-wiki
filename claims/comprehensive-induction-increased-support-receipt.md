## Evidence

### Glazerman 2010

Glazerman, S., E. Isenberg, S. Dolfin, M. Bleeker, A. Johnson, M. Grider, and M. Jacobus. (2010). Impacts of Comprehensive Teacher Induction: Final Results From a Randomized Controlled Study (NCEE 2010-4027). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Randomized experiment of 418 elementary schools in 17 urban districts; teacher surveys administered six times over four years. Treatment teachers were "more likely to have a mentor assigned to them" (90 vs 72 percent one-year districts; 96 vs 79 percent two-year districts), significant at the 0.05 level.

> "in the first year they were more likely to have a mentor assigned to them (90 versus 72 percent in one-year districts and 96 versus 79 percent in two-year districts)"
