# Physics identity at the end of physics 2 is directly influenced by perceived recognition (largest effect, b = 0.71) and self-efficacy, with a small direct effect from interest

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q2`–`q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` Perceived recognition has the largest direct standardized effect on physics 2 identity (b = 0.71); self-efficacy also influences identity directly and interest has a small direct effect. [→ Sonja Cwik 2020](#sonja-cwik-2020)

`q2 i?` Perceived recognition, interest, and self-efficacy all significantly predict physics identity, with perceived recognition the strongest (β = 0.52, p < 0.001); the article prints no standardized effect size for this finding. [→ Li 2023](#li-2023)

## Evidence

### Sonja Cwik 2020

Sonja Cwik, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How the learning environment predicts male and female students' motivational beliefs in algebra-based introductory physics courses. Physics Education Research Conference Proceedings. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.cwik)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Gender-mediation SEM (Fig. 1) on matched survey data from 544 students; the printed path coefficient b = 0.71 is a standardized regression coefficient, not an effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect). The authors add that both women and men have mean recognition below the positive threshold score of 3 and women score lower, so instructors and TAs should meaningfully recognize their students.

> "self-efficacy and perceived recognition influence physics identity in physics 2 directly, with perceived recognition having the largest direct effect (b = 0.71)."

### Li 2023

Li, Y., & Singh, C. (2023). Statistically equivalent models with different causal structures: An example from physics identity. Originally published 2007. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2303.13786](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2303.13786)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Path analysis within the SEM of survey data from 1203 students, estimated with DWLS in lavaan. The authors report "perceived recognition is the strongest predictor (with b = 0.52)", consistent with prior work they cite by Godwin et al. and Kalender et al.; all shown paths have p < 0.001. No effect size in the d/r/η² sense is printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "perceived recognition, interest, and self-efficacy are all significant predictors of physics identity and perceived recognition is the strongest predictor (with b = 0.52)"
