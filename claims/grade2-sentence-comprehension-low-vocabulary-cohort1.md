# The standalone intervention significantly improved grade 2 FRA Sentence Comprehension only among cohort 1 students with a low vocabulary baseline (0.38 standard deviation)

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among grade 2 cohort 1 students with an FRA Vocabulary Pairs baseline one standard deviation below the mean, the standalone intervention produced a 38-point higher FRA Sentence Comprehension outcome, equivalent to 0.38 standard deviation and 15 percentile rank points. [→ Foorman 2017](#foorman-2017)

## Evidence

### Foorman 2017

Foorman, B., Herrera, S., Dombek, J., Schatschneider, C., & Petscher, Y. (2017). The relative effectiveness of two approaches to early literacy intervention in grades K–2 (REL 2017–251). Washington, DC: U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences, National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Regional Educational Laboratory Southeast. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Language-outcome subgroup probe from the grade 2 hierarchical linear model (table 3); among cohort 1 students with a low FRA Vocabulary Pairs baseline, adjusted means were "597 in standalone intervention schools and 559 in embedded intervention schools". The printed effect size is 0.38 standard deviation (38 points, 15 percentile rank points), significant after the Benjamini–Hochberg correction; the effect did not appear in cohort 2.

> "The average estimated FRA Sentence Comprehension outcome among grade 2 students in cohort 1 with an FRA Vocabulary Pairs baseline score of 414 (one standard deviation below the mean) was a score of 597 in standalone intervention schools and 559 in embedded intervention schools"
