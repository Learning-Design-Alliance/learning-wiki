Narrative review synthesis of animal research on parental presence as a safety cue. The review reports "both rodent pups and infant primates demonstrating reduced glucocorticoid release and decreased amygdala activation in the presence of the mother," but notes buffering does not appear under abusive maternal rearing. No effect sizes are printed.

Narrative review synthesis of human studies of parental buffering. The review reports "parental presence has been demonstrated to dampen both cortisol and amygdala reactivity to stress in children," with parent voice associated with faster cortisol recovery, and high parent support mitigating poverty-related connectivity alterations. No effect sizes are printed.

"parental presence has been demonstrated to dampen both corti- sol [ 217, 218] and amygdala reactivity [ 219] to stress in children. Presentation of parent voice during speech stress has been associated with faster cortisol recovery post-stressor"
