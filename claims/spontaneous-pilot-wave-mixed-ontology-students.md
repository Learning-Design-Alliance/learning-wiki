## Evidence

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2010

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2010). Refined Characterization of Student Perspectives on Quantum Physics. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.6.020113](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.6.020113)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview finding from the 19-student study: three students expressed a mixed wave-particle ontology in the double-slit context, e.g. the electron "rides on a track" through both slits while the physical particle takes one path.

> "The realist beliefs of three other students were of a decidedly nonlocal character: localized quantum entities follow trajectories determined by the interaction of nonlocal quantum waves with the environment. None of these three students claimed to be aware of any formal pilot-wave interpretation"

### Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein 2014

Charles Baily and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2014). Ontological Flexibility and the Learning of Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1409.8499](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1409.8499)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data from 19 modern physics students. Student G described the electron riding a track through both slits while the physical electron picks one; similar explanations were volunteered in written responses (Student H's fluid-flow visualization). All instructors considered were explicit that particle and wave descriptions are mutually exclusive and mentioned no pilot-wave interpretation.

> "Three of the interviewees were fairly consistent in talking about electrons as being simultaneously a particle and a wave, such as Student G, whose description of the double-slit experiment is remarkably reminiscent of Fig. 2."
