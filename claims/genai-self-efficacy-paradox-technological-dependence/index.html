Evidence¶

Yan 2025¶

Yan, L., Greiff, S., Lodge, J. M., & Gašević, D. (2025). Distinguishing performance gains from learning when using generative AI. Nature Reviews Psychology, 4, 435–436. https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-025-00467-5

q2 · i? · associational · r?

The article reports, citing Zhang and Xu, a study of university students in which frequent generative AI use enhanced perceived confidence and efficiency but "simultaneously increased technological dependence". No effect size is printed.

"a study reported that frequent generative AI use among university students enhanced learners’ perceived confidence and efficiency during academic tasks but simultaneously increased technological dependence[10]."

Wang Guanhua 2026¶

Wang Guanhua, Wang Wenna, Yang Daozhou, Ren Jifan. (2026). Generative AI, Cognitive Offloading, and Learner Agency in Higher Education: A Scoping Review. Behavioral Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071150

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review attribution: the review reports, citing L. Zhang and Xu (2025), that frequent GenAI use raised confidence and efficiency while intensifying dependence. The review adds that Jia et al. (2025) similarly linked GenAI dependency to distorted, inflated academic self-evaluation.

"L. Zhang and Xu (2025) found that more frequent GenAI use simultaneously enhanced students' confidence and learning efficiency while intensifying technological dependence."
