# Child-parent communication frequency is not associated with CMS scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Correlations between the level of child-parent communication and the CMS scores were not significant for either metacognition or self-judgment accuracy. [→ dos Santos Kawata KH 2021](#dos-santos-kawata-kh-2021)

## Evidence

### dos Santos Kawata KH 2021

dos Santos Kawata KH, Ueno Y, Hashimoto R, Yoshino S, Ohta K, Nishida A, Ando S, Nakatani H, Kasai K and Koike S (2021) Development of Metacognition in Adolescence: The Congruency-Based Metacognition Scale. Front. Psychol. 11:565231. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.565231

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pearson correlations tested whether child-parent communication frequency influenced CMS scores. Correlations were not significant for metacognition (r = 0.07, p = 0.33) or self-judgment accuracy (r = 0.01, p = 0.86); equivalence was not tested.

> "The correlations between the level of communication and the CMS scores were not signiﬁcant (metacognition:r= 0.07, p= 0.33; self-judgment accuracy: r= 0.01, p= 0.86)."
