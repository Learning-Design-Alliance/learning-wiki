# With depth cues added, the phantom cubes in the moving-dice visualization are perceived as sheared rather than rotated

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2007 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When skeleton cubes slide over a plane showing their footprints, the visualization conveys the shear of the phantom object, though much guidance to the eye is needed. [→ U Kraus 2007](#u-kraus-2007)

## Evidence

### U Kraus 2007

U Kraus. (2007). First-person visualizations of the special and general theory of relativity. The European Journal of Physics (accepted for publication). https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3454 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0708.3454](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0708.3454)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Design experiment from section 2.1: modifying the scene to add perception-in-depth cues (hollow cubes sliding over a marked plane) made the shear visible, whereas isolated cubes appear rotated. The authors report that "Much guidance to the eye must be provided to dispel the prevailing impression of rotation."

> "In figure 1(d) the cubes slide over a plane and, in addition, are hollow with the sides cut away so that only the edges remain and the ‘footprints’ on the plane are visible. This image gives a visual impression of the sheared phantom cubes. While creating these simulations, it became apparent that it is not easy to make the cubes look sheared."
