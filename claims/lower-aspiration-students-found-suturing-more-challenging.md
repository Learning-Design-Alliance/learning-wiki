## Evidence

### Onishi Shun 2026

Onishi Shun, Sugita Koshiro, Murakami Masakazu, Yano Keisuke, Harumatsu Toshio, Tabata Yumiko, Tsuruno Yudai, Kedoin Chihiro, Nagano Ayaka, Kawano Takafumi, Ieiri Satoshi. (2026). Objective suturing skill assessment in a hands-on course increases medical students’ aspiration to become surgeons. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39306-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group t-test comparison of 185 students classified by baseline VAS into S group (VAS ≥ 50, N=118) and P group (VAS < 50, N=67). Enjoyment VAS was "significantly higher in the S group than in the P group"; no effect size printed.

> "the VAS for enjoying the training (I enjoyed the training) was significantly higher in the S group than in the P group (S vs. P = 94.39 ± 8.14 vs. 90.13 ± 12.99, p = 0.007)"

### Onishi Shun 2026 (2)

Onishi Shun, Sugita Koshiro, Murakami Masakazu, Yano Keisuke, Harumatsu Toshio, Tabata Yumiko, Tsuruno Yudai, Kedoin Chihiro, Nagano Ayaka, Kawano Takafumi, Ieiri Satoshi. (2026). Objective suturing skill assessment in a hands-on course increases medical students’ aspiration to become surgeons. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-39306-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal–Wallis comparison among the four change groups (N=185 total). Students whose aspiration dropped below 50 after training (S-to-P, N=8) rated difficulty highest; the article reports "felt that suture training was difficult compared to the S-to-S and P-to-P groups".

> "the students in the S-to-P group felt that suture training was difficult compared to the S-to-S and P-to-P groups (P-to-P group vs. S-to-S group vs. S-to-P group = 73.00 [63.00–88.00] vs. 74.00 [55.00- 87.75] vs. 96.00[91.25–99.25], p < 0.001)"
