## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a controlled crossover study of 154 medical students in nine seminar groups, summative assessment success rates were independent of whether the topic was taught with the audience response system (paired t-test, p = 0.98). [→ Schmidt Thorsten 2020](#schmidt-thorsten-2020)

## Evidence

### Schmidt Thorsten 2020

Schmidt Thorsten, Gazou Anastasia, Rieß Angelika, Rieß Olaf, Grundmann-Hauser Kathrin, Falb Ruth, Schadeck Malou, Heinrich Tilman, Abeditashi Mahkameh, Schmidt Jana, Mau-Holzmann Ulrike A., Schnabel Kai P. (2020). The impact of an audience response system on a summative assessment, a controlled field study. BMC Medical Education 20, 218. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02130-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled crossover field study during regular Human genetics seminars at the University of Tuebingen; 119 consenting students analysed with dummy coding (statistical total 2*n = 308). The study found "There was no difference between both groups (paired t-test, p = 0.98)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Shown is the mean % (± SEM) of correct answers given by students in the summative assessment for the respective topic for which the audience response system was used during teaching (with ARS, 82.1% ± 1.7%) and for the respective topic for which no audience response system was used (without ARS, 82.2% ± 1.9%). There was no difference between both groups (paired t-test, p = 0.98)."
