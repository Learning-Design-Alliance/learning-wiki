# Core GPA was not affected immediately after closures, but students from closed schools showed small negative effects in years three and four, more pronounced for students in grades 3-5 at announcement

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Core GPA for closed-school students did not increase as much as the comparison group in years three and four post-closures, with effects more pronounced for students in primary grades 3-5. [→ Gordon 2018](#gordon-2018)

## Evidence

### Gordon 2018

Gordon, M.F., de la Torre, M., Cowhy, J.R., Moore, P.T., Sartain, L.S., & Knight, D. (2018). School closings in Chicago: Staff and student experiences and academic outcomes. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/school-closings-chicago-staff-and-student-experiences-and-academic-outcomes

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multi-year administrative-data analysis of core GPA (English, math, science, social studies grades). The report finds GPA initially improved at similar rates for both groups, but in 2015-16 and 2016-17 closed-school students' gains lagged, with effects "more pronounced for students who were in primary grades (3-5)."

> "the core GPA for students from closed schools did not increase as much as the comparison group. The effects on core GPA were small, but negative, in years three and four post-closures."
