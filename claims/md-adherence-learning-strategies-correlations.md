# Mediterranean diet adherence is positively but weakly correlated with elaboration strategies, organizational strategies, critical thinking, self-regulation, time and study habits, self-regulation of effort and intrinsically oriented goals

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Chacón-Cuberos 2018

Chacón-Cuberos, R.; Zurita-Ortega, F.; Martínez-Martínez, A.; Olmedo-Moreno, E.M.; Castro-Sánchez, M. (2018). Adherence to the Mediterranean Diet Is Related to Healthy Habits, Learning Processes, and Academic Achievement in Adolescents: A Cross-Sectional Study. Nutrients 10(11):1566. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu10111566

`q2 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Bivariate Pearson correlations (Table 5) in the 1059-adolescent sample: MD adherence "positively related with elaboration strategies" (r = 0.116) and six further MSLQ-SF dimensions plus PA (r = 0.167), all p < 0.01; printed correlations fall in the r = 0.09–0.17 range (small).

> "Adherence to a MD was positively related with elaboration strategies (r = 0.116; p < 0.01), organizational strategies (r = 0.109; p < 0.01), critical thinking (r = 0.116; p < 0.01), self-regulation (r = 0.159; p < 0.01), time and study habits (r = 0.160; p < 0.01), self-regulation of effort (r = 0.118; p < 0.01), intrinsically oriented goals (r = 0.1090; p < 0.01) and engagement in PA (r = 0.167; p < 0.01)."
