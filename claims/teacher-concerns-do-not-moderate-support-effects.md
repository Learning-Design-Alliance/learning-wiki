# Teacher concerns do not significantly moderate the support-confidence relationship nor directly predict confidence or attitudes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 2 associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Concerns did not significantly moderate the relationship between support and confidence (V = 0.021, p = 0.661) and had no significant direct effect on confidence (V = −0.104, p = 0.625). [→ mmr-model1-moderation](#mmr-model1-moderation)
`q2 i?` Concerns did not significantly predict attitudes (V = 0.220, p = 0.151) and did not moderate the support-attitude relationship (V = -0.047, p = 0.173). [→ mmr-model2-moderation](#mmr-model2-moderation)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Moderated multiple regression on the survey of 260 Philippine teachers found no significant moderation of the support-confidence relationship and no direct effect of concerns on confidence; the article reports these as null findings without effect sizes.

> "However, concerns did not significantly moderate the relationship between support and confidence (V =0.021, p =0.661), nor did they have a di- rect effect on confidence (V = −0.104, p = 0.625)."

### mmr-model2-moderation

Vanessa Baltazar Sibug, Maria Anna David Cruz, Vicky Pineda Vital, Juvy Cruz Grume, Almer Balingit Gamboa, Emerson Quiambao Fernando, Lloyd David Feliciano, Jordan Lansang Salenga, and John Paul Palo Miranda. 2025. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3761843.3761881

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the second regression model, which controlled for confidence, concerns neither predicted attitudes nor moderated the support-attitude relationship among the 260 teachers; no effect sizes are printed for these null results.

> "Concerns also did not significantly predict attitudes (V = 0.220, p = 0.151) and did not moderate the support-attitude relationship (V = -0.047, p = 0.173)."
