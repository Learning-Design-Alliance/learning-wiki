Evidence¶

Tabatabaeichehr Mahbubeh 2022¶

Tabatabaeichehr Mahbubeh, Babaei Samane, Dartomi Mahdieh, Alesheikh Peiman, Tabatabaee Amir, Mortazavi Hamed, Khoshgoftar Zohreh. (2022). Medical students’ satisfaction level with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic and its related factors: a systematic review. Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2022.19.37

q2 · i? · review · r2

Synthesis of the 9 included studies that reported factors related to satisfaction. The review reports these factors "showed a significant relationship with students’ satisfaction with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic"; no effect sizes are printed.

"The following factors showed a significant relationship with students’ satisfaction with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic: gender (n=3) [3,21,41], experience of e-learning before COVID-19 (n=3) [19,36,41], level of study (n=1) [37], adaptation (n=1) [37], content of course materials (n=1) [29], interactivity (n=1) [29], understanding the content (n=1) [29], active participation of the instructor in the discussion (n=1) [19], using multimedia in teaching sessions (n=1) [19], and adequate time dedicated to e-learning (n=1) [19]."

Tabatabaeichehr Mahbubeh 2022 (2)¶

Tabatabaeichehr Mahbubeh, Babaei Samane, Dartomi Mahdieh, Alesheikh Peiman, Tabatabaee Amir, Mortazavi Hamed, Khoshgoftar Zohreh. (2022). Journal of Educational Evaluation for Health Professions. https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2022.19.37

q2 · i? · review · r2

Same synthesis section: each of these design- and delivery-related factors was reported significant in a single included study (n=1 each), so each rests on one cross-sectional study. No effect sizes are printed.

"The following factors showed a significant relationship with students’ satisfaction with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic: gender (n=3) [3,21,41], experience of e-learning before COVID-19 (n=3) [19,36,41], level of study (n=1) [37], adaptation (n=1) [37], content of course materials (n=1) [29], interactivity (n=1) [29], understanding the content (n=1) [29], active participation of the instructor in the discussion (n=1) [19], using multimedia in teaching sessions (n=1) [19], and adequate time dedicated to e-learning (n=1) [19]."

Related Claims¶

  • Convenience showed a significant positive relationship with medical students' satisfaction with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic — related
  • Satisfactory levels of acceptance of distance learning were reported in 3 of 4 studies during the pandemic — related
  • About half (51.79%) of medical science students were satisfied with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic across 24 cross-sectional studies — related
  • Age, clinical year, and stress perception showed negative and significant relationships with medical students' e-learning satisfaction during the COVID-19 pandemic — related
  • Learners reported higher perceived learning and satisfaction and lower mental effort with the instructor video, despite no performance gains — a perception-performance gap — related
