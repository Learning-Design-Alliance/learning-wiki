Evidence¶

Tsvetomila Mihaylova 2026¶

Tsvetomila Mihaylova, Evanfiya Logacheva, Arto Hellas, Jing Fan, Francisco Castro, Bita Akram, Narges Norouzi, Peter Brusilovsky, Juho Leinonen. (2026). The Effects of Structured LLM-Generated Feedback on Programming Assignment Performance. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16933 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.16933

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Observational analysis of assignment–user pairs from an online introductory Web Development course, using linear mixed-effects models on log-transformed time with random intercepts and slopes for students and exercises. Fixed effects: Single-Issue β=−0.214, Diagnostic β=−0.194, General β=−0.122 versus No Feedback.

"All hint types were associated with a reduction in completion time relative to the No Feedback baseline. The largest average reduction was observed for Single-Issue Feedback (β=−0.214), followed by Diagnostic Feedback (β=−0.194) and General Feedback (β=−0.122)."

Related Claims¶

  • Advanced students showed reduced attempts with all hint types, while novices did not clearly benefit and made slightly more submissions with Single-Issue Feedback — related
  • Attempt-reduction patterns differed across course parts: Diagnostic and General Feedback were associated with fewer attempts for New Content, Single-Issue for Old Content — related
  • Feedback type did not affect whether students solved assignments; solution rates were very similar across all conditions — related
  • Feedback effectiveness varied by content recency: guidance-heavy feedback performed less consistently on newer material released after the model's knowledge cutoff — related
  • Feedback effects on time to solution did not differ significantly between novice and advanced students, though predicted trends suggest feedback was more beneficial for advanced students — related
  • Natural language AI feedback is associated with faster convergence to correct solutions — related
  • All three LLM feedback types credibly reduced attempts to success relative to the baseline, with the largest reduction for Diagnostic Feedback — related
  • Student-voted Good feedback was associated with far fewer attempts and shorter solution times than Poor feedback, and thematic analysis identified outdated references and false positives as common failure modes — related
  • Compiler errors accounted for over half of diagnosis interactions in the log analysis
