Evidence¶

Jennie K. Grammer 2021¶

Jennie K. Grammer, Keye Xu and Agatha Lenartowicz. (2021). Effects of context on the neural correlates of attention in a college classroom. npj Science of Learning 6:15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00094-8

q2 · i3 · causal · r1

Portable EEG multilevel regression analysis of normalized occipital alpha power across four instructional activities in a college classroom; activity type varied significantly (χ2(3) = 41.39, p < 0.001). Contrast showed "alpha power was signiﬁcantly higher" for teacher-initiated than student-initiated activities, r = 0.65.

"Planned contrasts revealed that alpha power was signiﬁcantly higher, consistent with lower student attention, in teacher-initiated activities (lecture and video watching) than in student-initiated activities (group work and independent work), b = −0.43, t (55) = −6.32, p < 0.001, r = 0.65."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students were less attentive during video watching than during lecture, with video showing the greatest alpha power of the four activities — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Time-related changes in attention within activities occurred only during independent work, not across the other activities — related
  • Teacher-initiated activities also increased theta power and decreased beta and gamma power, converging with the alpha-based inference of lower wakefulness and attention — related
  • No difference in alpha power between student-driven group work and independent work — related
  • Observer ratings diverged from neural measures: behavioral ratings showed no teacher-initiated versus student-initiated difference and rated group work as more inattentive than independent work — related
  • Perception performance was superior for independent-study events (6–7) than for lecture events (1–5) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
