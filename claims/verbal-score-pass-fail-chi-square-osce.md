## Evidence

### Chan Michael 2015

Chan Michael, Bax Nigel, Woodley Caroline, Jennings Michael, Nicolson Rod, Chan Philip. (2015). The first OSCE; does students’ experience of performing in public affect their results? BMC Medical Education, 15, 59. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0343-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-square analysis of pass versus borderline/fail outcomes in the cohort. The article reports 55 students with verbal scores >3 passed while only 7 failed, and 44 of 59 borderline/fail students (75%) had scored zero for verbal activities.

> "There is a significantly increased chance of passing the OSCE if the verbal score >3, compared to a significantly increased chance of achieving borderline/fail grade if the verbal score is zero, (Chi square, Yates correction, one tail p = 0.045.)"
