# Within-group average scores rose by 15.87% for the da Vinci simulator group and 24.6% for the cognitive training group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The simulator group's average scores rose by 15.87% from the first to last training trial across eight training measures. [→ Schönburg S 2021](#schonburg-s-2021)
`q2 i?` The cognitive training group's average scores rose by 24.6% from the first to last training trial across three modules and five trials, with the last trial used because most participants did not practice 20 times. [→ Schönburg S 2021 (2)](#schonburg-s-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Schönburg S 2021

Schönburg S, Anheuser P, Kranz J, Fornara P, Oubaid V. (2021). Cognitive training for robotic surgery: a chance to optimize surgical training? A pilot study. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-020-01167-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group descriptive analysis of the da Vinci simulator group (n=8 analyzed), computed as the difference between first and last training trial scores across eight modules; shown in Fig. 3a. The percentage gain is descriptive; no effect size or significance test is printed.

> "The average training gain for group 1 was 15.87% over all eight training measures (see Fig. 3)."

### Schönburg S 2021 (2)

Schönburg S, Anheuser P, Kranz J, Fornara P, Oubaid V. (2021). Cognitive training for robotic surgery: a chance to optimize surgical training? A pilot study. Journal of Robotic Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11701-020-01167-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-group descriptive analysis of the cognitive training group (n=7 analyzed) across spatial orientation I and II and concentration modules; trial 5 was used because most participants did not follow the instruction to practice 20 times. Descriptive percentage only; no test statistic printed.

> "The average training gain for group 2 was 24.6% over all three modules and five training trials (see Fig. 3b)."
