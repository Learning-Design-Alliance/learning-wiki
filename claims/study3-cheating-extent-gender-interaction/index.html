Evidence¶

Li Zhao 2024¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q4 · i3 · causal · r3

ANOVA on cheating extent among cheaters in Study 3 (n = 25 cheaters across conditions; means M = 2.00 unproctored vs. M = 3.37 proctored). The article reports "ηp2 = 0.24" for the exam type main effect, with only weak Bayes evidence (BF10 = 1.57).

"The main effect of exam type was significant, F (1, 21) = 6.74, p = 0.017, ηp2 = 0.24, BF10 = 1.57."

Li Zhao 2024 (2)¶

Li Zhao, Junjie Peng, Shiqi Ke, Kang Lee. (2024). Effectiveness of Unproctored vs. Teacher-Proctored Exams in Reducing Students’ Cheating: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Field Experimental Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09965-z

q4 · i3 · causal · r3

Post hoc tests following the significant gender by exam type interaction (F(1, 21) = 5.36, p = 0.031, ηp2 = 0.20) in Study 3. For female students who cheated, extents were similar between conditions (M = 2.33 vs. 2.50, t(12) = –0.21, p = 0.835).

"For male students, the cheating extent was significantly lower in the unproctored exam condition than in the proctored exam condition, M = 1.33 vs. 4.25, SD = 0.58 vs. 1.39; t(9) = –3.43, p = 0.001, BF10 = 6.04."
