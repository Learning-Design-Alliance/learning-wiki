# The massed-versus-distributed question is tied to the whole-versus-part question: the hard problem remains hard unless learned in parts under massed conditions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The hard problem becomes easy only if learned under massed conditions by the part method, and remains hard when learned as a whole under either condition or in parts under distributed conditions. [→ Pechstein 1921](#pechstein-1921)

## Evidence

### Pechstein 1921

Pechstein, L. A. (1921). Massed vs. Distributed Effort in Learning. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070137

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The author's fifth conclusion, framing the paper's central thesis that the distribution-of-effort question cannot be separated from the whole-versus-part question in difficult motor learning.

> "The hard problem becomes easy if it is learned under massed conditions by the part method and in no other way; it remains hard if it is learned as a whole under massed or distributed conditions, or even in parts under distributed conditions."
