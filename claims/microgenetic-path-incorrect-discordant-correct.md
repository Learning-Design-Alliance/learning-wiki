## Evidence

### Alibali and Goldin-Meadow 1993

Martha Wagner Alibali and Susan Goldin-Meadow. (1993). Gesture-Speech Mismatch and Mechanisms of Learning: What the Hands Reveal about a Child's State of Mind. Cognitive Psychology 25, 468-523. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/001002859390017M

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

In this study, 63 Gesturers among 90 fourth-grade children solved and explained series of equivalence problems across pretest, training, posttest and follow-up; the authors report "the majority of children who learned" followed the hypothesized path. No effect size is printed.

> "We found that the majority of children who learned to correctly solve equivalence problems did so by adhering to the hypothesized path: They first produced a single, incorrect procedure. They then entered a discordant state"
