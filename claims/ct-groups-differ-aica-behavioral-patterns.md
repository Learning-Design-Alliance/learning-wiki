# High CT students used the AICA for code understanding and debugging while low CT students used it for direct answer retrieval

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Lag Sequential Analysis of screen recordings showed distinct behavioral transitions: high CT students showed unique transitions such as RC → AAEC and AAEC → DP, while low CT students showed UT → AANQ, AACC → CPC and RC → PPC, indicating dependence on AICA-provided answers. [→ Shu Zhao 2026](#shu-zhao-2026)

## Evidence

### Shu Zhao 2026

Shu Zhao, Ziqi Wang, Tingting Chen, Chunchen Kang, Yu-Ju Lan. (2026). Exploring the effect of computational thinking levels on students' learning performance, cognition, and behavior when using AI coding assistants. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-026-10663-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

LSA of 52 screen recordings coded with a ten-category behavioral scheme (GESQ 5.1), each group showing 15 significant sequences. The article reports the low CT group's unique transitions and concludes "low CT students only sought answers by querying AICA during coding."

> "the unique transitions for low CT group but not appearing in high CT group were UT → AANQ, AANQ → AANQ, RC → AACC, AACC → CPC and RC → PPC. It revealed that low CT students only sought answers by querying AICA during coding."
