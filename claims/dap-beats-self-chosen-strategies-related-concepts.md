# Differential-associative processing yields higher multiple-choice accuracy than student-chosen strategies in paired- and single-presentation control conditions

`q3 i2` Students using differential-associative processing answered 82.2% of multiple-choice questions correctly versus 69.9% (paired) and 71.5% (single) in control conditions. [→ Hannon 2013](#hannon-2013)

## Evidence

### Hannon 2013

Brenda Hannon. (2013). The Influences of Pre-testing Reviews and Delays on Differential-associative Processing versus Conditions in which Students chose their Learning Strategy. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v1i2.225

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Main effect of learning condition in a 3x3x3x2 ANOVA on accuracy for 216 randomly assigned university students learning 16 pairs of related concept definitions; the article reports "F (2, 207) = 24.04" with "η2 = .08".

> "students who learned concepts using differential -associative process ing answered 82.2% of the multiple-choice questions correctly whereas students who learned concepts using a strategy of their own choice in the paired- and single-presentation conditions answered 69.9% and 71.5% of the questions correctly respectively, F (2, 207) = 24.04, MSE = 121.84, η2 = .08"

### Hannon 2013 (2)

Brenda Hannon. (2013). The Influences of Pre-testing Reviews and Delays on Differential-associative Processing versus Conditions in which Students chose their Learning Strategy. Journal of Education and Training Studies. https://doi.org/10.11114/jets.v1i2.225

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Pairwise t-tests comparing the differential-associative processing condition to each control condition; the article reports "d = 1.11" and "d = 1.01" for the two comparisons and "d = .12" for the non-significant difference between the two controls.

> "significantly more questions were answered correctly in the differential -associative processing condition than in either the paired - or single-presentation control conditions, t (142) = 6.67, p < .0001, d = 1.11 and t (142) = 6.07, p < .0001, d = 1.01 respectively, there was no difference in the number of questions answered correctly in the paired - and single-presentation control conditions, t < 1.00, d = .12"
