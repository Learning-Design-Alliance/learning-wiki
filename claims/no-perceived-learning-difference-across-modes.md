# No significant difference in perceived learning across the three modes (p = .845), despite the unrestricted mode's nearly double objective learning delta

### Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2609.00584](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2609.00584)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-experiment questionnaire ratings of perceived learning on a 0-10 scale across the three modes. The authors call this the most unexpected finding and suggest Full-mode participants may have been aware their results were driven by answer retrieval rather than deep understanding.

> "A one-way ANOVA revealed no significant difference between groups (𝐹( 2, 47)= 0.169,𝑝=. 845). Participants in Full mode, who achieved nearly double the learning delta of the other two conditions, did not perceive themselves as having learned more."

### Alexandre Clin Deffarges 2026 (2)

Alexandre Clin Deffarges, Nataliya Kosmyna, and Pattie Maes. (2026). Socrates went Nuclear: Comparing Interaction Strategies for AI systems in a Learning Context using Brain Sensing. arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.00584. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00584

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis of post-questionnaire ratings against EEG engagement. Perceived difficulty was significantly higher in Socratic than Full mode (t=-2.143, p=.041, d=-0.74), but showed "no correlation (𝑟=. 126,𝑝=. 388,𝑛= 49)" with mean EEG engagement during training, and did not correlate with perceived learning either (r=-.06).

> "perceived difficulty and mean EEG engage- ment during training showed no correlation (𝑟=. 126,𝑝=. 388,𝑛= 49), with the same null pattern within each condition."
