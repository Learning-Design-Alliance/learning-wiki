# The quality (level) of reasoning children were exposed to, not rule difference, was the most significant factor in cognitive change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Children exposed to higher-level reasoning were overwhelmingly more likely to move to a higher rule, and the effect of reasoning type was highly significant (p < .0001). [→ Tudge 1986](#tudge-1986)
`q2 i?` Children who heard same-level reasoning tended to keep the same rule; of those who heard lower-level reasoning, half regressed and half stayed the same. [→ Tudge 1986 (2)](#tudge-1986-2)

## Evidence

### Tudge 1986

Tudge, Jon. (1986). Beyond Conflict: The Role of Reasoning in Collaborative Problem Solving. Paper presented at the Annual Symposium of the Jean Piaget Society. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED275395

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Chi-square analysis over the reasoning-exposure categories (39 low, 38 same, 29 higher level) across the unequal and equal rule samples. Table 4 shows high-level exposure means of +1.138 at treatment and +0.964 at second posttest (F = 15.24 and 10.50, p = .0001).

> "Chi-square analyses reveal that the effect of the type of reasoning to which the children were exposed was highly signifinant (p(.0001)."

### Tudge 1986 (2)

Tudge, Jon. (1986). Beyond Conflict: The Role of Reasoning in Collaborative Problem Solving. Paper presented at the Annual Symposium of the Jean Piaget Society. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED275395

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive pattern across the same-level (n=38) and lower-level (n=39) reasoning-exposure groups, complementing the higher-level effect; the article reports these as observed tendencies from the interaction analysis.

> "Children who heard reasoning at the same level as their own pretest rule were likely to continue to use that same rule.Of children who heard reasoning at a lower level, half came to use a lower rule, while the other half continued to use the same rule,"
