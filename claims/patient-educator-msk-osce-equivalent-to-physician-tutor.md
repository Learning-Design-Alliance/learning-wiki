# Students taught MSK physical examination by trained patient educators show no significant differences in OSCE physical examination or interpersonal skills scores compared with students taught by non-MSK specialist physician tutors

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` There are no significant differences in MSK physical examination OSCE scores or interpersonal skills OSCE scores between students taught by trained patient educators and those taught by usual non-MSK specialist physician tutors. [→ Oswald Anna E 2011](#oswald-anna-e-2011)

## Evidence

### Oswald Anna E 2011

Oswald Anna E, Bell Mary J, Wiseman Jeffrey, Snell Linda. (2011). The impact of trained patient educators on musculoskeletal clinical skills attainment in pre-clerkship medical students. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-11-65

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled cross-over study of 83 pre-clerkship students (42 patient educator taught, 41 physician tutor taught) assessed with a five-station MSK tOSCE. The article reports no significant differences at any station after Bonferroni correction; the hip station physical exam difference (p = 0.026) favoured the patient educator group but did not reach the corrected alpha of 0.005.

> "This appropriately powered study demonstrates that there are no significant differences in MSK physical examination OSCE scores or interpersonal skills OSCE scores between students taught by trained patient educators and those taught by usual non-MSK specialist physician tutors."
