## Evidence

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r3`

Post-tutorial examination results on the Explosions question (introductory N=84, advanced N=25). Performance was comparable to graduate students after traditional instruction in an interview version, on which 7 of 12 (about 60%) answered correctly.

> "After working through the pair of tutorials descri bed, about 60% of introductory students and 70% of advanced undergraduates answered correctly."

### Rachel E. Scherr 2002 (2)

Rachel E. Scherr, Peter S. Shaffer, and Stamatis Vokos. (2002). The challenge of changing deeply-held student beliefs about the relativity of simultaneity. https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1507792

`q3 · i?` · `design · r2`

Examination results after standard instruction (introductory N=128, advanced N=31; interviews N=17). In many cases conceptual difficulties seemed to prevent correct answers, such as claims that the location of the moving observer would determine the order of events.

> "After traditional instruction, about 45% of the introductory students and about 30% of the advanced undergraduates answered the Explosions  question correctly."
