Evidence¶

Li 2026¶

Li, X., Liu, Z., Jiang, S., Chen, J., & Chen, W. (2026). The impact of an LLM-based educational agent on learning achievement, cognitive dynamics, and student perceptions in computer science education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Descriptive statistics over coded interaction logs from both paradigms: 102 student-instructor records versus 17,856 student-agent records, showing students "exhibit frequent help-seeking behaviors" with the agent. Coding achieved Cohen's Kappa κ = 0.79.

"In traditional learning, they rarely ask for help (cognitive code = 102), whereas in human-DBagent interaction learning scenarios, students exhibit frequent help-seeking behaviors (cognitive code = 17856)."

Li 2026 (2)¶

Li, X., Liu, Z., Jiang, S., Chen, J., & Chen, W. (2026). The impact of an LLM-based educational agent on learning achievement, cognitive dynamics, and student perceptions in computer science education. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Distribution of Bloom-based cognitive codes across interaction paradigms shows agent-context engagement was "non-uniform", with lower-order at 54.52% and higher-order only 3.92%, versus a uniform distribution (30.39%/38.24%/31.37%) in traditional learning.

"In the agent-enriched learning contexts, cognitive engagement exhibits a non-uniform distribution that differs from traditional learning models (Lower order = 54.52%, Medium order = 41.56%, and Higher order = 3.92%)."

Related Claims¶
