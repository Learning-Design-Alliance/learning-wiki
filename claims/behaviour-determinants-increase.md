## Evidence

### Niederhauser A 2019

Niederhauser A, Züllig S, Marschall J, Schweiger A, John G, Kuster SP, Schwappach DLB. (2019). Change in staff perspectives on indwelling urinary catheter use after implementation of an intervention bundle in seven Swiss acute care hospitals: results of a before/after survey study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028740

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Repeated cross-sectional survey using a 15-item scale based on the theory of planned behaviour (alpha 0.72/0.74). Table 4 prints an overall effect size of 0.43, rising to 0.58 for matched participants; the authors call these medium effect sizes of practical relevance.

> "The mean score for the scale assessing the determinants of behaviour increased significantly between baseline and follow-up (p<0.001). Positive changes in mean scores were observed for all three constructs (attitudes, subjective norms and perceived behavioural control)."

### Niederhauser A 2019 (2)

Niederhauser A, Züllig S, Marschall J, Schweiger A, John G, Kuster SP, Schwappach DLB. (2019). Change in staff perspectives on indwelling urinary catheter use after implementation of an intervention bundle in seven Swiss acute care hospitals: results of a before/after survey study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2018-028740

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' interpretation of item-level results in the supplementary appendix; the article states participants felt higher expectations from their social environment to reduce catheters, suggesting a change in culture could be initiated.

> "They were particularly strong for items assessing perceived social expectations to use catheters restrictively (see online supplementary appendix 5)."
