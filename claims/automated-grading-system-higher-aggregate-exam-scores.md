# Use of an automated grading system for first-order logic translation exercises was associated with marginally significantly higher aggregate scores on knowledge representation final-examination questions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who used the automated grading system received higher aggregate scores on the four relevant final-examination questions, with a marginally significant effect of group membership (F(1,280) = 96.5; p = 0.066). [→ Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle 2015](#angelo-kyrilov-and-david-c-noelle-2015)

## Evidence

### Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle 2015

Angelo Kyrilov and David C. Noelle. (2015). Using Automated Theorem Provers to Teach Knowledge Representation in First-Order Logic. 4th International Conference on Tools for Teaching Logic. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1507.03670](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1507.03670)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A quasi-experimental comparison across course offerings: 113 control students (2007–2011, no system access) versus 169 test students (2012–2014, with system access), scored on four final-exam questions worth 24 points. The ANOVA of group and question factors found "a marginally significant eﬀect of group membership" favoring the system users; no effect size is printed.

> "This analysis revealed a marginally significant eﬀect of group membership, with the group making use of our automated grading system receiving higher aggregate scores (F (1, 280) = 96 .5; p = 0.066)."
