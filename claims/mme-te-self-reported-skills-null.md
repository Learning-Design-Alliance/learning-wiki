## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Post-intervention mean total perceived WMC skill score was slightly higher for MME students (3.18) than TE students (3.09) on a 1–4 scale, not statistically significant (p=0.25). [→ Ockene JK 2021](#ockene-jk-2021)

## Evidence

### Ockene JK 2021

Ockene JK, Pbert L, Crawford S, Frisard CF, Pendharkar JA, Sadasivam RS, Faro J, Okuliar C, Eno C, Margo K, Shaw MA, Soleymani T, Stadler DD, Warrier S, White K, Geller AC. (2021). Teaching Medical Students to Help Patients Manage Their Weight: Outcomes of an Eight-School Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of General Internal Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06571-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up survey of MS3 students (MME N=474, TE N=432) using a 16-item self-reported skill measure analyzed with linear mixed modeling adjusted for MS1 score; the printed comparison was "slightly higher for MME students (3.18) than TE students (3.09)" with no effect size reported.

> "At post-intervention, mean total score (SD) was slightly higher for MME students (3.18) than TE students (3.09) (p=0.25, scale of 1 to 4)."

### Ockene JK 2021 (2)

Ockene JK, Pbert L, Crawford S, Frisard CF, Pendharkar JA, Sadasivam RS, Faro J, Okuliar C, Eno C, Margo K, Shaw MA, Soleymani T, Stadler DD, Warrier S, White K, Geller AC. (2021). Teaching Medical Students to Help Patients Manage Their Weight: Outcomes of an Eight-School Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of General Internal Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06571-x

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Item-level binomial logistic regressions with school as random effect (Table 2); the largest absolute differences were for discussing health benefits of losing weight, recommending/referring to resources, and advising weight loss (p=0.02 for the latter).

> "Although only one item-specific difference was statistically significant, a higher percentage of MME versus TE students reported feeling moderately/very skilled in 14 of the 16 WMC skills assessed"
