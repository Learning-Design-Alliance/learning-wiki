## Evidence

### Andrew Boudreaux 2014

Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2014). Student facility with ratio and proportion: Mapping the reasoning space in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bobbing block task administered to over 530 students in 7 sections of the WWU general education physics course, with no prior instruction on oscillations; 'Correct response rates varied from 44% to 59%,' and nearly all incorrect responses involved the inverse ratio B/10.

> "The task has been administered to over 530 students in 7 sections of the general education physics course at WWU. Students had not received course instruction on oscillations. Correct response rates varied from 44% to 59%."

### Andrew Boudreaux 2014 (2)

Andrew Boudreaux, Stephen E. Kanim, and Suzanne White Brahmia. (2014). Student facility with ratio and proportion: Mapping the reasoning space in introductory physics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1511.08960)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with student volunteers from introductory physics and science education courses; the authors interpret that students 'exhibited an intuitive, almost primitive tendency to focus on rates of change with respect to time' rather than the alternative ratio construction.

> "Overall, students in the interviews exhibited an intuitive, almost primitive tendency to focus on rates of change with respect to time, rather than considering the alternative ratio construction."
