# The amount of AI-generated code in a submission was the dominant factor influencing students' ethical and rule-compliance judgments

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a repeated-measures ANOVA over eight scenarios, all three coded factors (amount, understanding, stage) were significant (p<0.0001), but the amount of generated code in the final submission emerged as the dominant factor, with the largest cross-country differences in high/medium-amount scenarios. ## Evidence

### Brian Harrington 2025

Brian Harrington, Irina Zlotnikova, Gayathri Nadarajan, and Samuel Ekundayo. (2025). Did Alice Do Wrong? Cross-Cultural Differences in Student Perceptions of Generative AI Use in University Computing Education. ACM Trans. Comput. Educ. 26, 1, Article 15. https://doi.org/10.1145/3776558

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Repeated-measures ANOVA with Scenario (eight levels) as within-subject factor and Country as between-subject factor. Amount of generated code had F=351.663, p<0.0001 for the unethicality prompt; Scenarios 4, 5, and 7 were exactly those coded High or Medium on amount, indicating students at both sites judged heavy AI-code submission more harshly.

> "All factors are significant (p<0.0001), but the "Amount of generated code in final submission" emerged as the dominant factor influencing student perceptions, with Scenarios 4, 5, and 7 exhibiting the largest mean differences between students in CAN and KOR across both ethicality and rule compliance ratings."
