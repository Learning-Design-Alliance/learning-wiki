# Conceptions of self-regulated learning had small, non-significant relationships with actual reported self-regulation activities

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Believing in the value of self-regulated learning did not significantly predict reported self-regulation in the structural models, though students endorsing it were more likely to report self-regulated behaviour in correlations. [→ Loyens 2008](#loyens-2008)

## Evidence

### Loyens 2008

Loyens, S. M. M., Rikers, R. M. J. P., & Schmidt, H. G. (2008). Relationships between students' conceptions of constructivist learning and their regulation and processing strategies. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-008-9065-6

`q3 · i?`

SEM path analysis (N = 98); the path from conceptions of self-regulated learning to ILS self-regulation (standardized .13) and to MSLQ self-regulation (standardized .11) were both non-significant. Correlationally, students who believe self-regulation is beneficial were more likely to report self-regulated behaviour.

> "Conceptions of self-regulated learning had small, but not signiﬁcant, relationships with actual reported self-regulation activities."
