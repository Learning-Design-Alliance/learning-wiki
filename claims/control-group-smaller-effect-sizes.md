# Studies using a no-input control group yield significantly smaller effect sizes, protecting against overestimation

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Studies with a true control group receiving no target word input yielded significantly lower effect sizes (difference of 0.47 standard deviation units). [→ de Vos 2018](#de-vos-2018)

## Evidence

### de Vos 2018

de Vos, J. F., Schriefers, H., Nivard, M. G., & Lemhöfer, K. (2018). A Meta-Analysis and Meta-Regression of Incidental Second Language Word Learning from Spoken Input. Language Learning. https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12296

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression methodological predictor (Table 3: Control group: yes, β = −0.47, p = .04). The article interprets this as evidence that "the use of a no-input control group seemed to protect against an overestimation of learning effects".

> "studies that used a control group that received no target word input yielded signiﬁcantly lower effect sizes than studies that did not use a control group, with a difference of 0.47 standard deviation units"
