# No demographic or prior-knowledge risk factors for being excluded from small groups or dropping out could be identified; students with and without grades were similar

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who were rarely strongly connected did not share any demographic characteristic, and students who received a grade were similar to those who did not in MBT, CLASS and attendance; the MBT difference was not statistically significant. [→ Kontro 2020](#kontro-2020)
`q2 i?` The authors conclude that factors influencing dropping out are more subtle than simple demographics, and that improving the social dynamics is unlikely to change the drop-out rate. [→ Kontro 2020 (2)](#kontro-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Kontro 2020

Kontro, I.; Génois, M. (2020). Combining surveys and sensors to explore student behaviour. [doi:10.3390/educsci10030068](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci10030068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of 65 students who passed the first assignment with 28 who did not (Table 6): attendance 5.26 ± 0.16 vs 5.18 ± 0.20 present, 77 ± 3% vs 73 ± 5% strongly connected. The article reports the MBT difference (15.1 ± 0.6 vs 16.3 ± 0.9) is "not statistically signiﬁcant"; no effect size is printed. Individually, two students always percolated away before the TA and seven were strongly connected only once, with demographics matching the course at large.

> "Surprisingly, the students with a grade have slightly lower MBT scores than those without (15.1 ± 0.6 and 16.3 ± 0.9, respectively) but this difference is not statistically signiﬁcant."

### Kontro 2020 (2)

Kontro, I.; Génois, M. (2020). Combining surveys and sensors to explore student behaviour. [doi:10.3390/educsci10030068](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci10030068)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation in the Discussion and Conclusions, based on the null demographic findings across the course: "factors inﬂuencing dropping out are more subtle than simple demographics". No statistical test supports this interpretive statement.

> "Clearly, for this course, factors inﬂuencing dropping out are more subtle than simple demographics. This means that the social dimension of the course works equally well for students of e.g. different major subjects."
