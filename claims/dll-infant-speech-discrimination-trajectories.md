# DLL infants can discriminate their two languages from early infancy, though within-language contrast discrimination trajectories show mixed findings

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At four months, DLL and monolingual infants do not differ in ability to discriminate between two different languages. [→ Hammer 2014](#hammer-2014)
`q2 i?` Some studies find a U-shaped pattern in which DLL infants fail to discriminate within-language contrasts at eight months before re-emerging at twelve months; other studies find DLL infants maintain discrimination. [→ Hammer 2014 (2)](#hammer-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Hammer 2014

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports, citing Bosch and Sebastián-Gallés (2001), that Spanish monolingual, Catalan monolingual, and Spanish-Catalan DLL infants showed no differences in language discrimination at four months of age.

> "Bosch and Sebastián-Gallés (2001) found no differences among Spanish monolingual infants, Catalan monolingual infants, and Spanish-Catalan DLL infants’ abilities to discriminate Spanish from Catalan at four-months of age."

### Hammer 2014 (2)

Hammer, C. S., Hoff, E., Uchikoshi, Y., Gillanders, C., Castro, D., & Sandilos, L. E. (2014). The Language and Literacy Development of Young Dual Language Learners: A Critical Review. Early childhood research quarterly. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecresq.2014.05.008

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that some studies (Bosch & Sebastián-Gallés, 2003; Burns et al., 2007; Sundara et al., 2008) found a U-shaped discrimination pattern, while others found DLL infants maintained discrimination, possibly when the two languages are rhythmically different (Sundara & Scutellaro, 2011).

> "This U-shaped developmental pattern, one in which discrimination is evident at four months, not at eight months, and then reemerges at twelve months, has been found in more than one study"
