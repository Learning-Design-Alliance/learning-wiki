## Evidence

### Phuong 2017

Phuong, A. E., Nguyen, J., & Mariea, D. (2017). Evaluating an Adaptive Equity-Oriented Pedagogy: A Study of its Impacts in Higher Education. The Journal of Effective Teaching, 17(2), 5-44. https://uncw.edu/jet

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Student survey and feedback data from the first three weeks of the treatment section, before instructional adjustment. "Two -thirds of students" judged the opening problem-posing dialogue inequitable; only 6 of 54 students spoke during the first two class meetings.

> "Two -thirds of students stated that having the problem-posing portion of democratic education at the beginning of the class was inequ i-table, because it privileged the verbally confident students and those who already had a significant amount of background knowledge on the subject."

### Phuong 2017 (2)

Phuong, A. E., Nguyen, J., & Mariea, D. (2017). Evaluating an Adaptive Equity-Oriented Pedagogy: A Study of its Impacts in Higher Education. The Journal of Effective Teaching, 17(2), 5-44. https://uncw.edu/jet

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Student feedback from the same pre-adjustment period in the treatment section. The article reports "the multimodal pedagogy was inequitable" for students without computers, those facing platform glitches, and those lacking training in drawing or acting.

> "the multimodal pedagogy was inequitable because two students stated that they did not own c omputers due to their socioeconomic status, and thus had difficulty participating in multimodal forms of communication online."
