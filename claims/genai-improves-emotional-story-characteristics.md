# GenAI access improves evaluators' emotional assessments of stories (better written, more enjoyable, more plot twists, less boring) but not funniness

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Stories from writers with up to five GenAI ideas were rated better written, more enjoyable, more likely to have plot twists, and less boring; the funniness difference was not significant. [→ Anil R. Doshi and Oliver P. Hauser 2023](#anil-r-doshi-and-oliver-p-hauser-2023)

## Evidence

### Anil R. Doshi and Oliver P. Hauser 2023

Anil R. Doshi and Oliver P. Hauser. (2023). Generative artificial intelligence enhances creativity but reduces the diversity of novel content. https://aspredicted.org/136723

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Exploratory evaluator-rated outcome analysis in the results section of the randomized experiment. Enjoyment (b=0.375, p<0.001 for five ideas) and plot twists (b=0.468, p<0.001) also improved; the article reports funniness "not evaluated as more funny than Human only" (b= –0.106, p=0.115).

> "when the writer has access to up to five GenAI ideas, the stories are considered to be better written (b=0.372, p<0.001), have more of an effect on the evaluator’s expectations of future stories (b=0.251, p=0.005), and be less boring (b= –0.200, p=0.049)."
