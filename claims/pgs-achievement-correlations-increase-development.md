# Correlations between polygenic scores and academic achievement increase across development, with EA polygenic score correlations rising from r = 0.23 at age 7 to r = 0.35 at age 16

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Correlations between polygenic scores and academic achievement were statistically significant at all ages and tended to increase across development, particularly for the EA polygenic score (r from 0.23 at age 7 to 0.35 at age 16) and the NonCog polygenic score (r from 0.11 to 0.24). ## Evidence

### Quan Zhou 2026

Quan Zhou, Liao Wangjingyi, Allegrini Andrea G., Rimfeld Kaili, Wertz Jasmin, Morris Tim T., Raffington Laurel, Plomin Robert, Malanchini Margherita. (2026). Non-cognitive skills mediate education-related polygenic score associations with academic achievement across development. Nature Communications. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72838-2

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analyses in TEDS on unrelated individuals examined associations between polygenic scores and academic achievement across ages 7 to 16. Correlations "tended to increase across development", with the EA polygenic score rising from r = 0.23 to r = 0.35 and the NonCog polygenic score from r = 0.11 to r = 0.24.

> "Their magnitude tended to increase across development, particularly for the EA polygenic score (r ranging from 0.23 at age 7 to 0.35 at age 16) and the NonCog polygenic score (r ranging from 0.11 at age 7 to 0.24 at age 16)."
