# The effect of multiple intelligence practices does not differ significantly by type of study (article, master's thesis, doctoral thesis)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Average effect sizes for articles, master's thesis and doctoral thesis were 1,317; 1,276 and 1,317 respectively, with no significant difference (QB=0,079; p=0,961). [→ Eyüp Yurt & Seyat Polat 2015](#eyup-yurt-seyat-polat-2015)

## Evidence

### Eyüp Yurt & Seyat Polat 2015

Eyüp Yurt & Seyat Polat. (2015). The Effectiveness of Multiple Intelligence Applications on Academic Achievement: A Meta-Analysis. Journal of Social Studies Education Research 6(1), 84-122. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1105368

`q3 · i?`

Sub-group moderator analysis of the 81 comparisons classified by study type. The article reports quite approximate values and a non-significant homogeneity test result.

> "As a result of the analysis, average effect size of practices in articles, master's thesis and doctoral thesis were calculated as 1,317; 1,276 and 1,317 respectively. The result of the homogeneity test showed that there was not a significant difference between the effect size values of studies according to their type (QB=0,079; p=0,961)."
