# COVID-19 third-grade school closures in Southern Germany showed no significant short-term or long-term effect on reading fluency

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Neither the short-term (one month after closures) nor the long-term (one year after closures) deviation from the expected reading fluency trend was statistically significant in the DiD analysis. [→ Kraus 2024](#kraus-2024)

## Evidence

### Kraus 2024

Kraus, E. B., Wild, J., Schilcher, A., & Hilbert, S. (2024). Exploring the Short-Term and Long-Term Effects of the COVID-19 School Closures on Reading Fluency in Southern Germany: A Longitudinal Causal Effects Analysis. https://osf.io/bu2ad/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Longitudinal DiD analysis with propensity score weighting on n = 9,083 Bavarian students (38,088 data points), comparing a COVID-affected third-grade cohort to a control cohort. The lagged closure effects were non-significant: "χ2 β4β2 (1) = 1.33, pβ4β2 = .250"; no effect size was printed.

> "Finally, the regression analysis supported the descriptive finding that school closures had no causal effect on reading fluency. Comparing β4 and β3 to β2, yielded non-significant results (χ2 β3β2 (1) = 0.0045, pβ3β2 = .947; χ2 β4β2 (1) = 1.33, pβ4β2 = .250)."
