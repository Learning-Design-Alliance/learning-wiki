## Evidence

### Deary and Smith 2007

Ian J. Deary, Steve Strand, Pauline Smith, Cres Fernandes. (2007). Intelligence and educational achievement. Intelligence 35, 13–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2006.02.001

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Sex comparisons in the matched national dataset (Table 2); the printed effect size for overall GCSE points is Cohen's d = 0.30, a small effect, with significant differences in all subjects except Physics.

> "Girls performed better than boys on overall GCSE points scores, with a Cohen's d of 0.30 (Table 2). There were significant sex differences (p < 0.001) in all GCSE scores except Physics."

### Deary and Smith 2007 (2)

Ian J. Deary, Steve Strand, Pauline Smith, Cres Fernandes. (2007). Intelligence and educational achievement. Intelligence 35, 13–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2006.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

ANCOVA models re-run without the residual verbal factor showed the increase in the sex effect was "around or below 10% of the effect of sex" seen in the full model, per the article; no effect size is printed for this finding.

> "Therefore, girls' better scores on the verbal factor account for only a very small part of their better scores on GCSE examinations."
