# Knowledge of an adverse clinical outcome lowers supervisors' diagnostic performance scores for identical resident reasoning (outcome bias)

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Vignettes ending in adverse clinical outcomes received lower diagnostic performance scores than vignettes ending in favorable outcomes, despite identical diagnostic processes (η² = 0.40). [→ van Sassen C 2025](#van-sassen-c-2025)

## Evidence

### van Sassen C 2025

van Sassen C, Mamede S, van den Broek W, Bindels P, Zwaan L. (2025). Does Knowledge of Clinical Case Outcome Influence Supervisor Evaluation of Resident Clinical Reasoning? Academic Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000006122

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Within-subjects experiment in which 62 GP supervisors scored residents' diagnostic performance in 6 ambiguous vignettes, each with a favorable and an adverse outcome version. The repeated-measures ANOVA found lower scores for adverse outcomes ("5.25 [0.12] vs 6.26 [0.16]"), η² = 0.40.

> "A significant difference was found based on the vignette version (F1,61 = 40.92, P < .001, η2 = 0.40), indicating that scores were generally lower for cases with adverse clinical outcomes compared with favorable clinical outcomes (mean [SE] scores, 5.25 [0.12] vs 6.26 [0.16]; P < .001)."
