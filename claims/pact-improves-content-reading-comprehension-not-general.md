# The modified PACT treatment improved content-related reading comprehension (ES = .20) but not general standardized reading comprehension

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Vaughn 2017

Vaughn, S., Martinez, L. R., Wanzek, J., Roberts, G., Swanson, E., & Fall, A.-M. (2017). Improving Content Knowledge and Comprehension for English Language Learners: Findings From a Randomized Control Trial. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(1), 22–34. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000069

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel regression on the MASK, a 21-item content-area reading comprehension test (alpha .92), in the same class-randomized RCT. The main treatment effect was significant with a printed effect size of ES = .20; no other effect size is reported for this finding.

> "The main treatment effect on the MASK differs statistically from zero ( /H9253010 /H110051.00, p /H11021.05, ES /H11005.20), meaning that non-ELs in treatment classrooms with 10% ELs scored about 1 point higher on the posttest than non-ELs in comparison class-rooms with 10% ELs."

### Vaughn 2017 (2)

Vaughn, S., Martinez, L. R., Wanzek, J., Roberts, G., Swanson, E., & Fall, A.-M. (2017). Improving Content Knowledge and Comprehension for English Language Learners: Findings From a Randomized Control Trial. Journal of Educational Psychology, 109(1), 22–34. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000069

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel model on the standardized Gates–MacGinitie subtest in the same RCT; the intervention effect (Table 5 coefficient 1.71, ES .12) was not significant, and only Class % of ELs was significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "On the Gates–MacGinitie test, only the main effect of Class % of ELs (/H9253020 /H11005/H11002.10, p /H11021.01) is significant and negative, similar to the trend on the MASK."
