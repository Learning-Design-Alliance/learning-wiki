Evidence¶

Daniel R. Espinas 2024¶

Daniel R. Espinas, Brennan W. Chandler. (2024). Correlates of K-12 Students' Intertextual Integration. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09889-8

q3 · i? · review · r3

Norwegian tenth-grade study (Andresen et al., n = 44), half neurotypical readers and half with school-based diagnoses of developmental dyslexia, reading three webpages and orally responding to open-ended questions. The review reports "r = 0.45 (p < 0.01)" for the entire sample.

"They found an association of r = 0.45 (p < 0.01) between word reading and intertextual integration for the entire sample."

Daniel R. Espinas 2024 (2)¶

Daniel R. Espinas, Brennan W. Chandler. (2024). Correlates of K-12 Students' Intertextual Integration. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09889-8

q3 · i? · review · r3

Elementary-level Italian study (n = 334, grades 4-5) measuring word reading fluency with real words and intertextual integration with an argumentative essay based on a document set. The review reports "a correlation of r = 0.21 (p < 0.001)".

Related Claims¶

  • Across 25 studies of K-12 readers, individual differences in literacy, cognition, metacognition, knowledge, beliefs, and motivation are positively and moderately associated with intertextual integration — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Single-document comprehension is the most studied literacy correlate of K-12 intertextual integration, with correlations ranging from near zero to moderate-strong — related
  • Research on K-12 intertextual integration is dominated by secondary-level samples, essay tasks, and informational socioscientific document sets — related
  • Oral and silent text reading fluency correlate strongly in children but only moderately in adolescents, and a standardized oral word reading test relates to silent reading differently by group — related
  • Children with dyslexia read texts significantly more slowly than typical readers in both oral and silent reading — related
