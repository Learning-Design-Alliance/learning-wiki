## Subclaims
`q1 i?` QTris' game design makes evident that QM measurements are probabilistic (via die rolls), that probabilities can be modified deterministically (via the operations phase), and that incompatible properties exist (via Color and Orientation). [→ Amabile 2026](#amabile-2026)

## Evidence

### Amabile 2026

Amabile, A., Bondani, M., De Simone, I., Nazzaro, M., Viscardi, M., Hamma, A. (2026). QTris: a pedagogical board game to teach Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2608.09430](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.09430)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r?`

Authors' design-based interpretation in the structural-analogy section: die rolls exemplify probabilistic measurement, color tiles exemplify certainty as a limiting case, and the operations phase embodies the deterministic modification of probabilities. This is the authors' argument, not a tested result.

> "The first point, that the results of measurements in QM are in generalprobabilistic, is made evident by the fact that in QTris the measurement is made by rolling a die, the random processpar excellence."
