Imagine IM: Evidence Rating 2024¶

Imagine IM: Evidence Rating. (2024). https://evidenceforessa.org/program/imagine-learning-illustrative-mathematics/

q2 · i2 · causal · r?

Retrospective matched study in a large suburban district with over 3,000 elementary students, measuring achievement with the Galileo Comprehensive Assessment System (GCAS) and the Missouri Assessment Program (MAP); "significant positive impacts were found on the MAP (effect size = +0.38)".

"While no differences were found on GCAS for grades 3–5, significant positive impacts were found on the MAP (effect size = +0.38), with especially strong effects for students with disabilities and economically disadvantaged students."

Related Claims¶

  • Imagine IM shows consistent positive impacts on student math achievement across three prospective quasi-experimental studies, with an average effect size of +0.11 across four studies — a broader claim this one bears on
  • In a small urban district in Iowa with over 4,400 students in grades K-5, Imagine IM showed directionally positive impacts on FastBridge earlyMath (+0.10) and aMath (+0.04), with significant subgroup effects and no meaningful differences on the state math assessment — related
  • In a Maryland rural fringe district study of over 700 students in grades K-5, Imagine IM showed significant impacts on Acadience Math (+0.26) and Grade 2 NWEA MAP Growth Math (+0.39), with positive but non-significant effects on the state MCAP assessment — related
  • A small retrospective matched study of 502 students in grades K-4 found positive impacts on Acadience (+0.13) and Grade 2 MAP Growth (+0.42), with no differences for 4th grade students on a state math assessment — related
  • iRCL effects were consistent across grades, especially pronounced in Grades 4 and 5, with significant benefits for Hispanic, Black, economically disadvantaged, English learner, and disability subgroups — related
