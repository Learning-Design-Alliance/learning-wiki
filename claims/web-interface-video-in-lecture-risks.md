## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Micolich 2008

Micolich, A. (2008). The latent potential of YouTube – Will it become the 21st Century Lecturer's Film Archive? CAL-laborate, 2008. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3441 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

Author's argument in the section 'Showing the video using a web-interface' about the advantage over out-of-class viewing: it "provides an opportunity for a whole additional layer of in-class discussion". Experiential claim, no data reported.

> "Not only does this allows a lecturer to more effe ctively link the content of the video to the concepts being taught, but it provides an opportunity for a whole additional layer of in-class discussion that would never occur if the students view the same footage individually on their own time."

### Micolich 2008 (2)

Micolich, A. (2008). The latent potential of YouTube – Will it become the 21st Century Lecturer's Film Archive? CAL-laborate, 2008. https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3441 [doi:10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.0808.3441)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The author summarises four listed problems (Internet unreliability, slow streaming, small or low-resolution players with distracting surrounding page content, and awkward seeking within streams). This is the author's assessment; no measurements are reported.

> "Each of these problems can cost  a lot of useful lecture time and produce a negative, distracting effect that can far outweigh the benefits that might be obtained by showing the video in the first place."
