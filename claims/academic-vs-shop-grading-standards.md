# Academic subjects were graded to a markedly higher standard than shop subjects, with far larger shares of grades below passing

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In 1913-14, fifty-one per cent of academic grades were passing or below, versus twenty-six per cent for practical or shop courses. [→ J. L. Zerbe 1917](#j-l-zerbe-1917)
`q2 i?` In individual subjects, forty-five per cent of grades in one academic subject were below passing while only two per cent were in one shop subject. [→ J. L. Zerbe 1917 (2)](#j-l-zerbe-1917-2)

## Evidence

### J. L. Zerbe 1917

J. L. Zerbe. (1917). Distribution of Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology, VIII(10), 575-588. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074694

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Tabulation of 1913-14 grades by course type: practical or shop courses summarized as 25 H/M, 49 C, 26 below; theoretical or academic as 19, 30, 51. The author infers the academic "standard is too high for the class of students taking the work."

> "If fifty-one per cent, of all grades assigned is valued as passing or below, as is indicated for the academic subjects, is it not to be inferred that the standard is too high for the class of students taking the work?"

### J. L. Zerbe 1917 (2)

J. L. Zerbe. (1917). Distribution of Grades. The Journal of Educational Psychology, VIII(10), 575-588. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074694

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Course-level comparison from Plate II analysis of grades by subject, day and night 1913-14. Academic subjects showed thirty-five to forty-five per cent below passing while one shop subject had "only two per cent, fall below tnat grade."

> "In two very fundamental subjects forty per cent, of all grades given are below passing, while in another subject forty-five per cent, of all grades are below passing. In one academic subject thirty-five per cent, are below passing, while in one of the shop subjects only two per cent, fall below tnat grade."
