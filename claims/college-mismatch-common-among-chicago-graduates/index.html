The mismatch analysis compared the selectivity of colleges Chicago students were eligible to attend, based on ACT scores, GPAs, and coursework, against the Barron's-rated selectivity of colleges in which they enrolled (Figure 4). Only 16 percent of students with access to selective colleges matched.

"At the end of senior year, only 38 percent of students with highest qualifications enrolled in a match college, that is, a very selective college."

The same comparison of accessed versus enrolled selectivity found the dominant mismatch pattern was enrolling in two-year colleges or not enrolling at all, rather than attending a four-year college slightly below match.

"Across all, about two-thirds (62 percent) of students attended a college whose selectivity level was below the kinds of colleges to which they would have most likely been accepted given their level of qualifications."
