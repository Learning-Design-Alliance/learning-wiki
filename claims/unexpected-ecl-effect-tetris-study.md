# An unexpected effect: task difficulty variation in the Tetris study also produced differences in reported extraneous cognitive load

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In the Tetris study, ECL differed significantly between difficulty conditions, with the boredom condition reporting the lowest ECL, contrary to the hypothesis of no ECL differences. [→ Melina Klepsch 2020](#melina-klepsch-2020)

## Evidence

### Melina Klepsch 2020

Melina Klepsch, Tina Seufert. (2020). Understanding instructional design effects by differentiated measurement of intrinsic, extraneous, and germane cognitive load. Instructional Science (2020) 48:45–77. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09502-9

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Between-subject Tetris experiment (N = 62) varying task difficulty across boredom, flow, and overload conditions. The article reports a significant ECL main effect "that was not expected", with η2 = 0.29, and lowest ECL in the boredom condition.

> "For ECL, we found a significant main effect (F(2,59) = 12.19, p < 0.001, η2 = 0.29) that was not expected. Contrasts showed that ECL was lowest for participants in the boredom condition."
