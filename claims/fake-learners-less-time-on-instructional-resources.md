## Evidence

### Alexandron 2019

Alexandron, G., Yoo, L. Y., Ruipérez-Valiente, J. A., Lee, S., & Pritchard, D. E. (2019). Are MOOC Learning Analytics Results Trustworthy? With Fake Learners, They Might Not Be! International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 29, 484–506. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-019-00183-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the MITx 8.MReVx Summer 2014 MOOC, the authors compared time on resources for 72 fake vs 406 true certificate earners using a two-sided Mann-Whitney U test, reporting "fake learners spent significantly less time on each type of the instructional resources" with p < 0.01. Medians: watching 0.3 vs 1.6 hours, reading 7.2 vs 15.9 hours, homework 7.7 vs 16.0 hours.

> "fake learners spent significantly less time on each type of the instructional resources. This is confirmed with a two-sided Mann-Whitney U test ( nfake = 72, ntrue = 406, p.value < 0.01)."
