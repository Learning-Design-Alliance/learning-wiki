Evidence¶

Tsigarides Jordan 2017¶

Tsigarides Jordan, Wingfield Laura R., Kulendran Myutan. (2017). Does a PBL-based medical curriculum predispose training in specific career paths? A systematic review of the literature. BMC Research Notes. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5219658/

q2 · i? · review · r3

Study of final-year medical students (n = 511) at the University of New Mexico School of Medicine examining self-reported student interest in specialties and how this changes throughout medical school with a PBL vs non-PBL curriculum. The review reports both retention and switching findings at P = 0.05.

"They found that significantly more PBL students retained an interest in a career in primary care (P = 0.05) and significantly more PBL students switched to an interest in primary care during medical school (P = 0.05)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Three studies reported a significantly increased number of PBL graduates working in primary care compared to non-PBL graduates — related
  • One study found significantly fewer PBL graduates working in primary care, with the authors suggesting medical school admission as a confounding factor — related
  • Matsui et al. found more non-PBL graduates working in primary care (9% difference) with no significance reported — related
  • PBL-based medical curricula do not predispose students to a career in a specific specialty, with 7 of 11 studies showing no significant difference in career choice between PBL and non-PBL graduates — a broader claim this one bears on
  • Woodward et al. found no significant difference in specialty choice but PBL graduates held a greater number of certificates in family medicine — related
