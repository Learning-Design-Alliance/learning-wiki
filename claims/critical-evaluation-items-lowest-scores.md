# Survey items on critical evaluation and validation of ChatGPT outputs (Q11–Q13) received the lowest scores in both courses despite explicit validation instructions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Items Q11, Q12, and Q13, addressing evaluation and validation of information provided by ChatGPT, received the lowest scores across both courses; for example Q12 scored 8.62 ± 2.20 in biology and 8.43 ± 2.31 in mathematics. [→ Elizondo-García ME 2025](#elizondo-garcia-me-2025)

## Evidence

### Elizondo-García ME 2025

Elizondo-García ME, Hernández-De la Cerda H, Benavides-García IG, Caratozzolo P and Membrillo-Hernández J (2025) Who is solving the challenge? The use of ChatGPT in mathematics and biology courses using challenge-based learning. Front. Educ. 10:1417642. https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1417642

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Item-level survey analysis in the Results section. Although both courses required cross-checking ChatGPT outputs with at least two academic sources, the critical-thinking items on independent judgment and skepticism "also scored lower," and the authors conclude the integration "did not strongly foster critical evaluation or validation skills."

> "Q12, which asked whether the activity encouraged students to look for information from other sources, scored 8.62 ± 2.20 in “Fundamentals of Biological Systems” and 8.43 ± 2.31 in “Mathematics and Data Science for Decision Making”. These scores are notable, given that students were explicitly instructed to validate ChatGPT’s outputs using formal sources."
