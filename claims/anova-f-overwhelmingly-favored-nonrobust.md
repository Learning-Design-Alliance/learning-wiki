# The conventional ANOVA F test is overwhelmingly favored in between-subjects and repeated-measures analyses despite its sensitivity to variance heterogeneity

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Keselman 1998

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of between-subjects univariate design articles. The review found the ANOVA F test "overwhelmingly favored," used in more than 90% of articles, with nonparametric analyses in only four articles, planned contrasts in two, and trend analysis in one.

> "The ANOVA test wasF overwhelmingly favored, and was used by research ers in more than 90% of the articles. A nonparametric analysis was performed by the authors of only four articles"

### Keselman 1998 (2)

Keselman, H. J., Huberty, C. J., Lix, L. M., Olejnik, S., Cribbie, R. A., Donahue, B., Kowalchuk, R. K., Lowman, L. L., Petoskey, M. D., Keselman, J. C., & Levin, J. R. (1998). Statistical Practises of Educational Researchers: An Analysis of Their ANOVA, MANOVA and ANCOVA Analyses. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Statistical+Practises+of+Educational+Researchers

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Content analysis of repeated-measures design articles (Table 5). For mixed designs the conventional ANOVA F test was "overwhelmingly favored (68.9%)," with MANOVA used in only two mixed-design articles and MANCOVA once.

> "As Table 5 reveals, for mixed desi gns, the conventional ANOVA test wasF overwhelmingly favored (68.9%). A small number of articles (3.9%) reported the use of a mixed design involving covariates for which the aut hors adopted the conventional ANCOVA test"
