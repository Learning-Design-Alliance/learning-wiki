# EBM-trained students use PubMed/Medline, Medscape and the Cochrane Library significantly more, while use of Google, Google Scholar and Wikipedia does not differ

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` EBM-trained medical students used PubMed/Medline (84.0% vs 57.9%), Medscape (36.2% vs 18.2%) and the Cochrane Library (12.3% vs 2.8%) significantly more than non-trained students (p<0.001 each). [→ Csertő Mónika 2019](#cserto-monika-2019)

## Evidence

### Csertő Mónika 2019

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis of search-engine use among medical students (n = 697 analysed for Table 3; n = 1465 for the question due to a technical problem). Printed percentages show trained students using PubMed/Medline, Medscape and Cochrane Library significantly more (p<0.001).

> "Pubmed/Medline (84.0% vs. 57.9%, [EBM trained vs. EBM non-trained], p<0.001), Medscape (36.2.0% vs. 18.2%, p<0.001), and the Cochrane Library (12.3% vs. 2.8%, p<0.001) were used to a significantly higher extent for healthcare information retrieval by EBM trained than non-trained medical students."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (2)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same subgroup analysis of medical students: null results for Google (p = 0.35), Google Scholar (p = 0.27) and Wikipedia (p = 0.16). Equivalence was not tested; the article reports only non-significant comparisons.

> "There were no significant differences in the use of Google (92.6% vs. 94.6%, p = 0.35), Google Scholar (30.7% vs. 26.0%, p = 0.27) and Wikipedia (73.8% vs. 68.1%, p = 0.16) between the EBM trained and non-trained medical students."

### Csertő Mónika 2019 (3)

Csertő Mónika, Berényi Károly, Decsi Tamás, Lohner Szimonetta. (2019). Self-reported attitudes, knowledge and skills of using evidence-based medicine in daily health care practice: A national survey among students of medicine and health sciences in Hungary. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0225641

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Health sciences subgroup analysis: trained students used PubMed/Medline, Medscape and Google Scholar significantly more. The article separately reports Cochrane Library use did not significantly differ (6.4% vs 3.9%, p = 0.14) in this group.

> "Pubmed/Medline (62.7% vs. 43.6%, [EBM trained vs. EBM non-trained], p<0.001), Medscape (25.3.0% vs. 14.0%, p<0.001), and Google scholar (26.6% vs. 17.9%, p = 0.01) were used to a significantly higher extent by EBM trained than non-trained health sciences faculty students"
