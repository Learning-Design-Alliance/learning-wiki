## Evidence

### Burke 2025

Burke, K. L. A., Snowdon, D. A., Milne, S. C., & Farlie, M. K. (2025). Exploring Physiotherapists' Perspectives of Group Clinical Supervision Effectiveness for Aquatic Physiotherapy Skill Development: A Qualitative Study. Physiotherapy Research International. https://doi.org/10.1002/pri.70044

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interpretive description study with three focus groups of 16 physiotherapists (14 female; nine grade one, seven grade two) at a Melbourne rehabilitation hospital. Inductive thematic analysis identified that participants "perceived group supervision as an effective model for skill development" via reflection. Perceptions only; no objective skill measure.

> "Participants perceived group supervision as an effective model for skill development in aquatic physiotherapy, believing that it provided them the opportunity to reflect on their clinical practice and develop their clinical reasoning and confidence in aquatic physiotherapy."
