# Self-reported exam preparation time did not differ between groups and did not positively correlate with examination scores

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Control and TA students spent similar median time preparing for the examination (33.58 vs 34.50 min, p = .915), and study time correlated weakly to slightly negatively with examination scores (−0.048 to −0.170). [→ Hoefer 2017](#hoefer-2017)

## Evidence

### Hoefer 2017

Hoefer, S. H., Sterz, J., Bender, B., Stefanescu, M.-C., Theis, M., Walcher, F., Sader, R., Ruesseler, M. (2017). Conveying practical clinical skills with the help of teaching associates—a randomised trial with focus on the long term learning retention. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-0892-5

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Structured questionnaire answered by 94 students (48 control, 46 TA) on self-study duration and preparation type; preparation time did not differ between groups (p = .915) or genders (p = .867). Correlations are descriptive, no effect size framework applied.

> "If one correlates the length of study time with examination scores, a weak to slightly negative correlation can be found (−0.048 to −0.170)."
