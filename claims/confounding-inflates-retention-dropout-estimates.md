# Uncontrolled estimates of retention effects on dropout are larger than propensity-adjusted estimates, suggesting confounding inflates naive associations

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Without adjusting for baseline confounders, the odds ratio for dropout versus graduation was 3.50, larger than the adjusted estimates, consistent with selection effects biasing naive estimates upward. [→ Hughes 2018](#hughes-2018)

## Evidence

### Hughes 2018

Hughes, J. N., West, S. G., Kim, H., & Bauer, S. S. (2018). Effect of Early Grade Retention on School Completion: A Prospective Study. Journal of Educational Psychology, 110(7), 974-991. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000243

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Authors' interpretive comparison in the Discussion between the unadjusted association and the propensity-adjusted estimates in the same sample. The article reports "a substantially larger odds ratio = 3.50" unadjusted, and offers equating on confounds as one explanation for the smaller adjusted effect.

> "In our analysis in which we did not adjust for any potentially confounding baseline factors, we found a substantially larger odds ratio = 3.50."
