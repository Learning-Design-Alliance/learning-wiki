# In a North Carolina high school study with teacher fixed effects, achievement returns to experience rose up to 20 years, from about 0.06 SD at 1–2 years to about 0.27 SD beyond 27 years

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` With teacher fixed effects, a teacher with more than 27 years of experience raised achievement by about 0.27 SD versus about 0.06 SD for a teacher with 1–2 years. [→ Kini 2016](#kini-2016)

## Evidence

### Kini 2016

Kini, T., & Podolsky, A. (2016). Does Teaching Experience Increase Teacher Effectiveness? A Review of the Research. Learning Policy Institute. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/our-work/publications-resources/does-teaching-experience-increase-teacher-effectiveness-review-research

`q3 · i2` · `review · r2`

A statewide study of North Carolina high school students over four years; initial analyses showed gains largely in the first two years, but teacher fixed effects models comparing each teacher to him or herself over time printed effect sizes of about "0.27 standard deviations" for teachers with more than 27 years versus about 0.06 standard deviations at 1–2 years.

> "a teacher with 1-2 years of experience raises student achievement by about 0.06 standard deviations, while a teacher with more than 27 years of experience raises student achievement by about 0.27 standard deviations."
