# Lesbian, gay and homosexual candidates are significantly less likely than heterosexual candidates to pass written and clinical examinations at first attempt

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Lesbian, gay or homosexual candidates were nearly 20% less likely than straight or heterosexual candidates to pass written (OR 0.82) and clinical (OR 0.81) examinations at first attempt. [→ Ellis Ricky 2025](#ellis-ricky-2025)
`q3 i?` Bisexual candidates were 26% less likely to pass clinical examinations (OR 0.74), but bisexuality was not an independent significant predictor of written outcomes. [→ Ellis Ricky 2025 (2)](#ellis-ricky-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Ellis Ricky 2025

Ellis Ricky, Knapton Andy, Cannon Jane, Lee Amanda J., Cleland Jennifer. (2025). Differential attainment in UK postgraduate medical examinations: examining the relationship between sociodemographic differences and examination performance. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-025-04034-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariate logistic regression of UKMED first-attempt outcomes adjusted for prior attainment and other sociodemographic factors; sexual orientation was a significant independent predictor for both examination formats.

> "Lesbian, gay or homosexual candidates were nearly 20% less likely than straight or heterosexual candidates to pass written and clinical examinations at the first attempt (OR 0.82 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.91) and OR 0.81 (95% CI 0.70 to 0.94) respectively)."

### Ellis Ricky 2025 (2)

Ellis Ricky, Knapton Andy, Cannon Jane, Lee Amanda J., Cleland Jennifer. (2025). Differential attainment in UK postgraduate medical examinations: examining the relationship between sociodemographic differences and examination performance. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-025-04034-w

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same all-candidates regression models; the bisexual disadvantage appeared only for clinical examinations and did not hold for written examinations.

> "Bisexual candidates were 26% less likely to pass clinical examinations (OR 0.74 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.98)). Identifying as bisexual was not found to be an independent statistically significant predictor of written examination outcomes."
