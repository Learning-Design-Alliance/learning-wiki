# Learning deliberate reflection through example study plus learning-by-teaching did not improve general-practice residents' diagnostic accuracy on future cases diagnosed without reflection instructions

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized two-condition experiment, residents taught deliberate reflection via examples and video explanation did not diagnose new cases more accurately than residents who only diagnosed cases. [→ Kuhn Josepha 2022](#kuhn-josepha-2022)

## Evidence

### Kuhn Josepha 2022

Kuhn Josepha, Mamede Silvia, van den Berg Pieter, Zwaan Laura, van Peet Petra, Bindels Patrick, van Gog Tamara. (2022). Learning deliberate reflection in medical diagnosis: does learning-by-teaching help? Advances in Health Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-022-10138-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Test-phase one-way ANOVAs in the randomized experiment (56 residents, learning-by-teaching n = 28 vs control n = 28) found "no main effects of condition on diagnostic accuracy, F (1, 54) = 1.28, p = 0.263, ηp2 = 0.02". Mean accuracy was .59 (learning-by-teaching) vs .51 (control), descriptively only. Equivalence was not tested.

> "One-way ANOVAs showed no main effects of condition on diagnostic accuracy, F (1, 54) = 1.28, p = 0.263, ηp2 = 0.02, on time to diagnose, F (1, 54) = 0.28, p = 0.598"
