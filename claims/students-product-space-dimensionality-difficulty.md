# More than 85% of advanced undergraduates tried to represent a two spin one-half particle system with a 2×2 matrix instead of the four-dimensional product space

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` More than 85% of students who had learned about product space tried to construct a 2×2 Hamiltonian matrix for two spin one-half particles, not realizing the product space is four dimensional. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Helping Students Learn Quantum Mechanics for Quantum Computing. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Written survey and interviews asked students given two spin one-half particles to choose a basis and write a Hamiltonian proportional to S1·S2 in matrix form. Even after being told the space is four dimensional, none of the interviewed students could choose a basis and construct the Hamiltonian correctly.

> "More than 85% of students tried to construct a 2 ×2 matrix because they did not realize that they should consider a product space which is four dimensional."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Helping Students Learn Quantum Mechanics for Quantum Computing. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06666)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Research surveys found students add instead of multiply individual Hilbert space dimensionalities; the addition happens to work for two spin one-half particles (2+2 = 2×2) but introduces error for two spin-one particles, where students claim a 6-dimensional product space.

> "For example, we have found that for two spin-one particles, many students believe that the product space is 6-dimensional as opposed to 9-dimensional."
