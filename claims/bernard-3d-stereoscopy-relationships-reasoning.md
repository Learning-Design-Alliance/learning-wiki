## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a single-site controlled study, the 3D group's post-test average scores were higher than the 2D group's on anatomical linkages and clinical inference/reasoning, while basic anatomical knowledge scores were comparable. [→ K Anniesmitha 2026](#k-anniesmitha-2026)

## Evidence

### K Anniesmitha 2026

K Anniesmitha, Rao K G Mohandas, Aithal P Ashwini, Bandekar Nikita N., Mandal Tatiyana, George Bincy M. (2026). Effectiveness of technology-enhanced learning in neuroanatomy education among health professionals - a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09764-w

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Prospective controlled study of 3D vs 2D stereoscopic neuroanatomy video (175 baseline participants; 2D n = 86, 3D n = 91). The 3D group scored "mean 86.4% vs. 63.5%, p = 0.004" on anatomical linkages; the total-score comparison (p = 0.07) was not significant.

> "In terms of anatomical linkages (mean 86.4% vs. 63.5%, p = 0.004), clinical inference/reasoning (mean 76.8% vs. 67.6%, p = 0.023), and total notes (mean 76.8% vs. 67.6%, p = 0.07), the 3D group's average scores on the post-test were higher."
