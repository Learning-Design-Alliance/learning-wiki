# The hour and break effects equal benchmark shifts of roughly 10–19 school days or 1,000–1,900 USD of household income

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` An hour later corresponds to 1,000 USD lower household income, a month less parental education, or 10 school days; a break corresponds to about 1,900 USD higher income, almost 2 months of parental education, or 19 school days. [→ Sievertsen HH 2016](#sievertsen-hh-2016)

## Evidence

### Sievertsen HH 2016

Sievertsen HH, Gino F, Piovesan M. (2016). Cognitive fatigue influences students' performance on standardized tests. PNAS 113(10): 2621–2624. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516947113

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Benchmarking exercise using simple correlations between test score and parent income, parental education, and school days (details in SI Text). The authors state "the effect sizes are small but nonnegligible compared to the unconditional influence of individual characteristics". These are correlational equivalences, not causal estimates of income effects. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "an hour later in the day causes a deterioration in test score that corresponds to 1,000 USD lower household income, a month less parental education, or 10 school days. A break causes an improvement in test score that corresponds to about 1,900 USD higher household income"
