Bilal Ashraf 2021¶

Bilal Ashraf, Akansha Singh, Germaine Uwimpuhwe, Tahani Coolen-Maturi, Jochen Einbeck, Steve Higgins and Adetayo Kasim. (2021). Individual participant data meta-analysis of the impact of EEF trials on the educational attainment of pupils on Free School Meals: 2011 - 2019. Education Endowment Foundation. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED625398

q3 · i0 · quant-synthesis · r2

Pooled attainment-gap estimate from the sIPD model with a treatment-by-FSM interaction term, reported in the executive summary; the printed gap of 0.01 is below d = 0.2 and its interval includes zero. The report defines the gap as the difference in gain between FSM and non-FSM pupils when both receive the intervention.

"The attainment gap observed for the literacy outcomes was improved in favour of FSM pupils with an overall attainment gap of 0.01 (-0.01, 0.04)."

Related Claims¶

  • Literacy benefits for FSM pupils were greatest in Key Stage 1 (ES 0.09) and Key Stage 3 (ES 0.08) and smallest in Key Stages 2 and 4 — related
  • EEF-funded interventions improved FSM pupils' literacy outcomes with a pooled effect size of 0.06, equivalent to about one month's progress — related
  • EEF-funded interventions showed no evidence of positive or negative overall effects on FSM pupils' mathematics outcomes — related
  • Small group and one-to-one interventions produced larger literacy gains for FSM pupils than whole class or whole school approaches — related
  • Passive dissemination showed no evidence of improving literacy attainment for the subgroup of pupils who have ever been eligible for free school meals — related
  • Passive dissemination of research summaries and evidence-based literacy materials to primary schools showed no evidence of improving pupils' Key Stage 2 English attainment compared with control — related
  • The trial found a statistically significant negative literacy impact for FSM-eligible pupils, treated with caution — related
  • Across 82 EEF RCT evaluations, overall meta-analysis weighted mean effect sizes were small: +0.04 SD for primary ITT, +0.01 SD for secondary ITT, and +0.03 SD for FSM subsample attainment outcomes — related
  • The TextNow programme showed a differential effect by free school meal eligibility: a small non-significant positive effect for FSM-eligible pupils and a significant negative effect for non-FSM pupils — related
  • An additional analysis using Key Stage 2 administrative test scores also found indeterminate, close-to-zero effects for Year 6 pupils. — related
  • The evaluation found no differential impact of PLC for pupils eligible for free school meals or with low prior attainment — related
  • The two interventions showed no significant transfer effects across domains and no significant impact on Key Stage 1 attainment — related
  • The chess intervention showed no evidence of a positive impact on Key Stage 2 scores for children eligible for free school meals — related
