Bellan Steve E., Pulliam Juliet R. C., Scott James C., Dushoff Jonathan, the MMED Organizing Committee. (2012). How to Make Epidemiological Training Infectious. PLoS Biology. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001295

`q2 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' observational report from the MMED 2010 and 2011 clinics, where participants ran and analyzed simulated MMF outbreaks. The authors state that 'dynamical epidemiologists developed empirical skills such as study design and learned concepts of bias' while classical epidemiologists gained systems thinking. No quantitative learning assessment is reported.

> "During the exercise, dynamical epidemiologists developed empirical skills such as study design and learned concepts of bias while classical epidemiologists were trained in systems thinking and began to understand epidemics as dynamic nonlinear processes."
