# Emergency medicine residents scored lower on an eclamptic-seizure simulation case with an added language barrier than another group of residents scored on the prior version without it

## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Residents scored lower on the modified case with the language barrier (median 10 of 27) than on the prior version without it (median 12 of 24), with a significant difference in percentage of total items correct (p = .02). The two versions were scored in different groups of residents, not randomised, so the difference is not attributable to the barrier alone. [→ Zdradzinski Michael J. 2019](#zdradzinski-michael-j-2019)

## Evidence

### Zdradzinski Michael J. 2019

Zdradzinski Michael J., Backster Anika, Heron Sheryl, White Melissa, Laubscher Deborah, Siegelman Jeffrey N. (2019). A Novel Simulation to Assess Residents’ Utilization of a Medical Interpreter. MedEdPORTAL. https://doi.org/10.15766/mep_2374-8265.10853

`q2 · i1` · `design · r2`

Non-randomised comparison of scores from the fall 2016 implementation with 60 emergency medicine residents against the prior version of the same eclamptic-seizure case without the language barrier. The article reports "a median of 10 out of 27" versus 12 of 24, an odds ratio = 0.43 (95% CI, 0.18-0.99), and p = .02. The authors write that this "suggests" the barrier made the case more challenging; the versions used different item totals and different groups of residents.

> "Overall scores on the modified case were a median of 10 out of 27 (odds ratio = 0.43; 95% confidence interval, 0.18-0.99) compared to a median score of 12 out of 24 achieved on the prior version, with a significant difference between the percentage of total items correct for the two groups (p = .02)."
