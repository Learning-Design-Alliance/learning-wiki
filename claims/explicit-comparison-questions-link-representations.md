# Explicit, separately numbered comparison questions in activities increased students' detailed engagement with connections between mathematical and simulation representations

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A substantial fraction of students did not comment on, or responded only superficially to, comparison questions before revision (30% and 34% omitted a comment; 16% and 30% superficial, N=81). [→ Antje Kohnle 2013](#antje-kohnle-2013)
`q2 i?` In subsequent observation sessions after the activities were revised, students did not skip the comparison questions and gave detailed, explicit responses. [→ Antje Kohnle 2013 (2)](#antje-kohnle-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Antje Kohnle 2013

Antje Kohnle, Charles Baily, Christopher Hooley and Bruce Torrance. (2013). Optimization of Simulations and Activities for a New Introductory Quantum Mechanics Curriculum. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/35/1/015001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/35/1/015001)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Analysis of homework responses in the St Andrews Quantum Physics course. The printed percentages show 30% and 34% (N=81) gave no comment on the comparison and 16% and 30% responded only superficially.

> "We  found that a substantial fraction of students (30% and 34% respectively, N=81) did not comment on having made this comparison despite calculating the probabi lities, or responded only superficially (16% and 30% respectively)."

### Antje Kohnle 2013 (2)

Antje Kohnle, Charles Baily, Christopher Hooley and Bruce Torrance. (2013). Optimization of Simulations and Activities for a New Introductory Quantum Mechanics Curriculum. [doi:10.1088/0143-0807/35/1/015001](https://doi.org/10.1088/0143-0807/35/1/015001)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Qualitative observation-session finding after revision: students engaged with the separately numbered comparison questions and "gave detailed and explicit responses", including pointing to specific displayed graphs.

> "In subsequent observati on sessions, we found that students did not skip over  these questions, and that they gave detailed and explicit responses."
