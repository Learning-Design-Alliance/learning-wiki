## Subclaims
`q2 i?` An avid-gamer student criticized the gamification for lacking a clearly defined goal and reward, while some non-gamers found it difficult to understand. [→ Bajko et al. 2016](#bajko-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Bajko et al. 2016

Robert Bajko, Jaigris Hodson, Katie Seaborn, Pamela Livingstone, Deborah Fels. (2016). Edugamifying Media Studies: Student Engagement, Enjoyment, and Interest in Two Multimedia and Social Media Undergraduate Classrooms. Information Systems Education Journal 14(6). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135365

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Open-ended survey and interview commentary contrasting an avid gamer, who said there "wasn't any sort of clearly defined goal", with non-gamers who found the gamification difficult to understand. Qualitative, small-number comments; no measure of the difference is printed.

> "In contrast, the opposite seemed to be true for some students who identified as non -gamers, such as in the case of one student who commented, "I find it sometimes difficult to understand. Great for people who love gaming, not as great for those who don't.""

### Bajko et al. 2016 (2)

Robert Bajko, Jaigris Hodson, Katie Seaborn, Pamela Livingstone, Deborah Fels. (2016). Edugamifying Media Studies: Student Engagement, Enjoyment, and Interest in Two Multimedia and Social Media Undergraduate Classrooms. Information Systems Education Journal 14(6). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135365

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey item on attention cited in the gamer/non-gamer challenge section: "77% reported that they disagreed or strongly disagreed" that the course did not hold their attention. The authors present this as evidence their accessible-to-casual-gamers design kept engagement consistent.

> "However, for the most part, we strove for a balanced approach, and this was reflected in a consistent level of engagement throughout the course: 77% reported that they disagreed or strongly disagreed with the question "this course didn't hold my attention at all.""
