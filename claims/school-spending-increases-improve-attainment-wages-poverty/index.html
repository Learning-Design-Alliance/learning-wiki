The brief reports this finding, citing Jackson, Johnson and Persico (2015); it is a second-hand summary of that work, not the brief's own analysis. The brief adds that recession-era spending cuts widened test-score gaps between high- and low-poverty districts, citing Jackson, Wigger and Xiong (2020).

"In one study, a 10 percent increase in per-pupil spending led to more completed years of education, higher adult wages, and a reduction in the annual incidence of adult poverty, and these effects were stronger for children from low-income families."
