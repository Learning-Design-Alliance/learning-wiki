Evidence¶

O'Rourke 2017¶

O'Rourke, J., Main, S., & Hill, S. M. (2017). Commercially available Digital Game Technology in the Classroom: Improving Automaticity in Mental-maths in Primary-aged Students. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 42(10). https://doi.org/10.14221/ajte.2017v42n10.4

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Quasi-experimental study of 236 Year 4/5 students in ten intact classrooms; ANCOVA with pre-test as covariate showed the intervention group "performed significantly better than the comparison group" on the Westwood One Minute Test of Basic Number Facts, with printed effect size ηp2 = .255.

"At post-test, however, the intervention group performed significantly better than the comparison group (F(1,233) = 79.686, p < .001) after controlling for the effect of pre-test performance."

O'Rourke 2017 (2)¶

O'Rourke, J., Main, S., & Hill, S. M. (2017). Commercially available Digital Game Technology in the Classroom: Improving Automaticity in Mental-maths in Primary-aged Students. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 42(10). https://doi.org/10.14221/ajte.2017v42n10.4

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

Non-parametric Mann-Whitney U tests on gain scores confirmed the ANCOVA result, with median gains of 5 for comparison and 19 for intervention students, reported as "U = 2771, p < .001"; the article prints r = .519 for this comparison.

"This pattern was also borne out by the non-parametric analyses on students' gain scores (Median gains: comp. = 5; exp. = 19; U = 2771, p < .001) using the Mann-Whitney U test."

O'Rourke 2017 (3)¶

O'Rourke, J., Main, S., & Hill, S. M. (2017). Commercially available Digital Game Technology in the Classroom: Improving Automaticity in Mental-maths in Primary-aged Students. Australian Journal of Teacher Education, 42(10). https://doi.org/10.14221/ajte.2017v42n10.4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Pre-test equivalence check using independent samples t-test showed "no significant difference between the two groups before commencing the intervention", supporting comparability of intact classes at baseline.

"Independent t test analysis of the pre-test scores revealed no significant difference between the two groups before commencing the intervention (t(234) = -1.263, p = .208)."

Related Claims¶

  • Comparison classes that later received the HGC intervention showed greater improvement after using HGCs than during traditional instruction, replicating the initial finding — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Brain games produce statistically significant cognitive performance gains in older adults, but gains are modest and do not detect improvements over control tasks or produce far transfer — related
  • A 2×2 mixed ANOVA showed a significant time-by-group interaction in narrative writing scores favoring DS-integrated instruction over traditional writing instruction — related
  • The HGC intervention group significantly outperformed the comparison group on each of the four Basic Number Facts sub-tests (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) — related
  • Teacher pedagogical approach with HGCs appeared to have little influence on positive student perceptions and mental-maths improvements, though the teacher's role remained vital for strategy support — related
