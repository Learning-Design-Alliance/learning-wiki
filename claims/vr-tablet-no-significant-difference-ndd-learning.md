## Evidence

### Francesco Vona 2024

Francesco Vona, Eleonora Beccaluva, Marco Mores, and Franca Garzotto. (2024). Shared Boundary Interfaces: can one fit all? A controlled study on virtual reality vs touch-screen interfaces on persons with Neurodevelopmental Disorders. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-60881-0_25](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-60881-0_25)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Between-group comparison of learning deltas for the 20 participants randomized to VR (condition A) or tablet (condition B). The unpaired t-test showed "no significant difference (t=0.81; df=18; p=0.93)"; equivalence was not tested and no effect size is printed.

> "An unpaired t-test was conducted to explore whether there was a significant difference between the VR and Tablet conditions in improving participants’ performance. The results showed no significant difference (t=0.81; df=18; p=0.93) between the two conditions."
