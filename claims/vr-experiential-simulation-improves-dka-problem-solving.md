# A Kolb-based VR simulation program produces significantly greater improvement in problem-solving ability than e-book-based case simulation among nursing students

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Baek Gyuli; Lee Eunju 2026

Baek Gyuli; Lee Eunju. (2026). Effectiveness of a virtual reality simulation based on experiential learning theory for enhancing diabetic ketoacidosis management competencies: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08970-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-blind RCT with 44 third- and fourth-year nursing students randomly assigned to a four-session Kolb-based VR program or e-book-based case simulation. The experimental group rose from "3.25 ± 0.31" to 4.34 ± 0.47 versus 3.39 ± 0.45 to 3.84 ± 0.40 in controls; the group-by-time interaction was F = 11.51, p = .002. No effect size was printed.

> "The problem-solving ability of the experimental group increased from 3.25 ± 0.31 before the intervention to 4.34 ± 0.47 after the intervention, which was higher than that of the control group"
