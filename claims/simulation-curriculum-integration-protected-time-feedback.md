# Participants recommend embedding simulation in curricula with structured goals, protected time and tailored facilitator feedback

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants wanted simulation built into education frameworks rather than used ad hoc, with dedicated protected time and progression relying on feedback tailored to the learner. [→ Heskin L. 2021](#heskin-l-2021)
`q2 i?` Meaningful feedback from an experienced, trained facilitator was viewed as necessary, with mixed views on whether the facilitator should remain beside the learner throughout. [→ Heskin L. 2021 (2)](#heskin-l-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Heskin L. 2021

Heskin L., Simms C., Traynor O., Galvin R. (2021). Designing a synthetic simulator to teach open surgical skills for limb exploration in trauma: a qualitative study exploring the experiences and perspectives of educators and surgical trainees. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-021-01417-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 2 of the qualitative interviews: participants proposed frameworks incorporating simulation into the curriculum to increase mastery, precision teaching and fluency, with set goals by learner level and dedicated protected time during the working week.

> "Simulation should be built into the education framework and not just used ad hock"

### Heskin L. 2021 (2)

Heskin L., Simms C., Traynor O., Galvin R. (2021). Designing a synthetic simulator to teach open surgical skills for limb exploration in trauma: a qualitative study exploring the experiences and perspectives of educators and surgical trainees. BMC Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-021-01417-7

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

In Theme 2, participants held that facilitators should be trained in observation and formative feedback, that feedback should be constructive with clear endpoints, and that automated feedback in a synthetic simulator would be challenging, leaving high dependency on a facilitator.

> "Meaningful feedback by an experienced facilitator was viewed as necessary for improvement."
