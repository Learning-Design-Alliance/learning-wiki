Jake Renzella 2026¶

Jake Renzella, Christian Bergh, Natasha Banks, and Alexandra Vassar. (2026). Gender Differences in AI Literacy Workshop Outcomes and Deepfake Engagement. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14718 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.14718

q3 · i2 · causal · r1

Within-gender pre–post OLS comparisons (outcome ~ phase + year level + school) on npost = 136 students. All four reported effects are medium by the article's thresholds, e.g. "d=.63" for male AI knowledge, all p<.001.

Related Claims¶

  • AI literacy interventions show a large, statistically significant overall positive effect on student AI literacy outcomes (g = 0.837) — the general claim this finding bears on, with the studies behind it
  • Female students show broader post-workshop gains than males, including significant increases in AI and computer science career interest, while male career interest remains unchanged — related
  • Both genders improve in recognising AI's role in entertainment platforms after the workshop, with females additionally gaining in identifying TikTok — related
  • Male students report significantly higher overall technological self-efficacy than female students (p = 0.001) — related
  • Female students are significantly more likely than male students to use AI for schoolwork and to seek advice from AI tools — related
  • After gamified security training, female students gave more positive confidence and format-preference ratings than male students (descriptive survey percentages, no inferential test) — related
  • Deepfake behaviours show gender patterns: males more likely to have shared deepfakes, females more likely to have reported them — related
  • Students' overall AI knowledge improved significantly after four integrated AI-biology lessons — related
  • CT self-efficacy gains varied by gender, grade, and prior Minecraft experience, with significant increases for female students, Grade 8 students, and students with limited Minecraft experience — related
