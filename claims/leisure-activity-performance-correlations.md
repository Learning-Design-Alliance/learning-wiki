## Evidence

### Haubruck P 2018

Haubruck P, Nickel F, Ober J, Walker T, Bergdolt C, Friedrich M, Müller-Stich BP, Forchheim F, Fischer C, Schmidmaier G, Tanner MC. (2018). Evaluation of App-Based Serious Gaming as a Training Method in Teaching Chest Tube Insertion to Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.9956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Spearman correlation of questionnaire-reported leisure activities with OSATS performance in the RCT; the sport correlation was ".214; P=.04" and was higher in the control group than the intervention group.

> "Regular sportive activity had a significant positive correlation with the operative performance during CTI regardless of prior training (Spearman index: .214; P=.04)."

### Haubruck P 2018 (2)

Haubruck P, Nickel F, Ober J, Walker T, Bergdolt C, Friedrich M, Müller-Stich BP, Forchheim F, Fischer C, Schmidmaier G, Tanner MC. (2018). Evaluation of App-Based Serious Gaming as a Training Method in Teaching Chest Tube Insertion to Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.9956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Null secondary correlation in the same RCT: "no correlation ... (Spearman index: −.007; P>.95)". The authors suppose the video-gaming influence depends on the type of surgical intervention, citing contrasting laparoscopic findings.

> "playing video games had no correlation with operative performance during CTI in the total study collective (Spearman index: −.007; P>.95; Table 3)"

### Haubruck P 2018 (3)

Haubruck P, Nickel F, Ober J, Walker T, Bergdolt C, Friedrich M, Müller-Stich BP, Forchheim F, Fischer C, Schmidmaier G, Tanner MC. (2018). Evaluation of App-Based Serious Gaming as a Training Method in Teaching Chest Tube Insertion to Medical Students: Randomized Controlled Trial. Journal of Medical Internet Research. https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.9956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Group-split correlation analysis in the same RCT: handicraft experience showed "no correlation ... in the intervention group (Pearson index: −.071; P=.63)" but a significant correlation in the control group (.353; P=.02).

> "previous experience in handicraft work had no correlation with operative performance in the intervention group (Pearson index: −.071; P=.63), whereas within the control group, participants that had previous experience in handicraft work performed significantly better than participants that did not (Pearson index: .353; P=.02; Table 3)"
