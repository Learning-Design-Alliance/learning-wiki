## Evidence

### Brown Denver M. Y. 2022

Brown Denver M. Y., Porter Carah, Hamilton Faith, Almanza Fernanda, Narvid Christina, Pish Megan, Arizabalo Diego. (2022). Interactive Associations between Physical Activity and Sleep Duration in Relation to Adolescent Academic Achievement. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192315604

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive interpretation of the spline interaction model (Figure 3) in the discussion section of the YRBSS analysis. The authors report the contrasting optimal combinations: "7–8 h of sleep duration alongside 5–7 days of weekly physical activity participation" versus at least nine hours for low-activity adolescents.

> "optimal academic achievement was observed with 7–8 h of sleep duration alongside 5–7 days of weekly physical activity participation, whereas for lower amounts of physical activity (i.e., 1–2 days/week), grades were highest among adolescents accruing at least nine h of sleep each night."

### Brown Denver M. Y. 2022 (2)

Brown Denver M. Y., Porter Carah, Hamilton Faith, Almanza Fernanda, Narvid Christina, Pish Megan, Arizabalo Diego. (2022). Interactive Associations between Physical Activity and Sleep Duration in Relation to Adolescent Academic Achievement. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph192315604

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Authors' interpretation of the interaction model results in the discussion, stating a boundary condition of the tradeoff pattern: outside the optimal ranges, physical activity did not compensate for inadequate or excessive sleep duration.

> "Outside of these optimal ranges, however, we found that any level of physical activity cannot buffer the detrimental effects of low amounts of sleep or too much sleep."
