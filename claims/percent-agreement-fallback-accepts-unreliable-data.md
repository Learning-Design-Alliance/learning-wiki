# Lombard et al.'s fallback criterion (alpha below .700, accept if %-agreement above .900) accepts data whose chance-corrected coefficients indicate near-zero reliability

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A variable with %-agreement of 96.5% passed the criterion while its chance-corrected coefficients were near zero, and the two coders never once agreed on the category occurrence being reported. [→ Klaus Krippendorff 2004](#klaus-krippendorff-2004)

## Evidence

### Klaus Krippendorff 2004

Klaus Krippendorff. (2004). Reliability in Content Analysis: Some Common Misconceptions and Recommendations. Human Communication Research 30, 3: 411-433. https://astro.temple.edu/~lombard/carman.htm

`q2 · i?`

Re-analysis of the reliability data for Lombard et al.'s 25th variable (Figure 4): all 96.5% coincidences pertain to absences; the 1-1 cell is empty. Chance-corrected coefficients were near zero, yet the variable was accepted and a finding of 2 mentions in 137 articles was reported.

> "Unable to accept the data on account of =.012, which measures significantly less than .700, the criterion relies on the fact that the %-agreement of 96.5% is well above the 90%"
