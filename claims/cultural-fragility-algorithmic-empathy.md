# Algorithmic empathy is culturally fragile: emotional inference errors in socio-emotional AI can invalidate teachers' experiences, so systems require local validation and participatory design

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Socio-emotional AI systems operate through universalist assumptions about expression and emotional normality, and when these fail due to cultural bias, context, irony, or ambivalence, the system can invalidate experiences, pathologise legitimate responses, or induce performative pressure. [→ Aponte ML 2026](#aponte-ml-2026)
`q1 i?` Systems trained or validated in one cultural, linguistic, or institutional context may misread teachers' affective signals in another, so implementation requires local validation, participatory design, and cultural and linguistic adaptation. [→ Aponte ML 2026 (2)](#aponte-ml-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Aponte ML 2026

Aponte ML, Vargas Sánchez AD, Chiappe A and Martínez-Pérez S. (2026). Artificial and emotional intelligence: two key forces for teachers' professional development in an era of uncertainty. Front. Psychol. 17:1935683. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1935683

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

A theoretical argument in this conceptual analysis, contrasting correctable content errors with emotional errors that touch "recognition, dignity, belonging, and psychological safety." The article argues this is especially risky for teachers, whose emotion is intertwined with professional identity and pedagogical authority.

> "When those assumptions fail because of cultural bias, context, irony, or ambivalence, the system does not simply make a mistake: it can invalidate experiences, pathologise legitimate responses, or induce performative pressure."

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`
