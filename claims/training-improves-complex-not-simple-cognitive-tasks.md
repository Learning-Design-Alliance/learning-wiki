## Evidence

### Ferguson M. A. 2014

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Combined-group pre- to post-training analysis (t1–t2) in the RCT, after the between-group MANOVA showed only weak evidence of a group difference (p = 0.06). Post hoc tests gave "Cohen’s d = 0.53" for TEA DTD, d = 0.50 for VLM1/s and d = 0.34 for VLM1/2s.

> "Post hoc pairwise testing showed a significant effect of training for the TEA DTD (t(42) = 3.45, p = 0.001, Cohen’s d = 0.53), VLM1/s (t(39) = 3.14, p = 0.003, d = 0.50), and VLM1/2s (t(37) = 2.10, p = 0.04, d = 0.34)"

### Ferguson M. A. 2014 (2)

Ferguson M. A., Henshaw H., Clark D. P. A., Moore D. R. (2014). Benefits of Phoneme Discrimination Training in a Randomized Controlled Trial of 50- to 74-Year-Olds With Mild Hearing Loss. Ear and Hearing. https://doi.org/10.1097/AUD.0000000000000020

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The waitlist control period showed no cognitive change, ruling out test-retest effects; the IT group's simple tasks (TEA single task p = 0.06, Digit Span p = 0.12) did not reach significance.

> "For the DT group, there was no change in performance between t0 and t1 (F(5, 10) = 0.61, p = 0.69), suggesting no test-retest effects"
