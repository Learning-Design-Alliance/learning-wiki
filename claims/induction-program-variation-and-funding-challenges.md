## Evidence

### Wechsler 2024

Wechsler, M. E., Patrick, S. K., Thompson, C., & Levin, S. (2024). On the path to leadership: California's administrator induction programs. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/636.224

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Statewide landscape analysis of induction programs between 2017–18 and 2020–21 reports the distribution of program administration across county offices of education, districts, higher education, and ACSA. Descriptive percentages only.

> "From 2017–18 to 2020–21, 40% of all California administrators completing induction attended a program run by a county office of education, 12% by a district, 30% by an institution for higher education, and the remaining 18% by the statewide professional organization, the Association of California School Administrators (ACSA)."

### Wechsler 2024 (2)

Wechsler, M. E., Patrick, S. K., Thompson, C., & Levin, S. (2024). On the path to leadership: California's administrator induction programs. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/636.224

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The report's landscape and funding analysis states advertised costs "ranged from $0 to $10,000" and that actual participant cost depended on district or external contributions; it also reports coach stipends of $2,000 to $7,500 per participant for 2 years and no dedicated state funding.

> "The advertised cost for induction programs ranged from $0 to $10,000 for the 2-year program, and the actual cost paid by participating administrators depended on whether their districts or other external organizations paid some or all of the cost."
