# Scaffolding did not affect dogs' latency to solve the task or persistence in initial direction on the first test trial

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Exploratory Bayesian t-tests found no condition differences in latency to solve the first test trial or time to change direction. [→ Neilands P. 2021](#neilands-p-2021)

## Evidence

### Neilands P. 2021

Neilands P., Kingsley-Smith O., Taylor A. H. (2021). Dogs’ insensitivity to scaffolding behaviour in an A-not-B task provides support for the theory of natural pedagogy. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-79557-8

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Exploratory Bayesian independent t-tests in the same experiment, controlling for final learning-trial latency. Control latency was "4.59 ± 2.94 s" vs pointing 2.32 ± 0.76 s and demonstration 2.07 ± 0.51 s, with BF values near 1. A second t-test on direction-change time was also null.

> "dogs’ latency to solve the task in the first test trial did not differ from the control condition (mean ± 95% CI: 4.59 ± 2.94 s) in either the pointing condition (2.32 ± 0.76 s; Bayesian independent t-test: BF = 0.812) or the demonstration condition (2.07 ± 0.51 s; Bayesian independent t-test: BF = 1.002)."
