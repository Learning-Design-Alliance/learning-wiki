# ChatGPT users were significantly less likely to correctly compare and evaluate animal and plant protein sources

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A significantly smaller proportion of ChatGPT participants answered Q7 (comparing and evaluating animal and plant protein sources) correctly compared to Google participants (21% vs. 57%). [→ Shravika Mittal 2026](#shravika-mittal-2026)

## Evidence

### Shravika Mittal 2026

Shravika Mittal, Su Lin Blodgett, Q. Vera Liao. (2026). Learning by Chatting? Investigating the Impact of Generative AI on Information Seeking and Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11669 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2606.11669](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2606.11669)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Fisher's exact test on Q7 correctness in the field experiment. The article attributes the gap to content bias: diary entries reported that "ChatGPT by default provided meat-based sources of protein," with one participant noting it "automatically assumes that everyone eats meat."

> "Again, a significantly smaller proportion of participants in the ChatGPT condition answered correctly compared to those in the Google condition (21%vs.57%; odds ratio:0.21; Fisher'sp:0.04)."
