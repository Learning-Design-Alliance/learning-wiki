# Combining domain-general and domain-specific scaffolds yields the highest effect, and domain-general scaffolds outperform domain-specific ones

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Mode of scaffolds significantly moderated effects (Q = 42.77, df = 2, p = 0.000): both modes combined had the highest effect (ES = 1.699), domain-general scaffolds (ES = 0.432) exceeded domain-specific scaffolds (ES = 0.102). [→ Lanqin Zheng 2016](#lanqin-zheng-2016)

## Evidence

### Lanqin Zheng 2016

Lanqin Zheng. (2016). The effectiveness of self-regulated learning scaffolds on academic performance in computer-based learning environments: a meta-analysis. Asia Pacific Education Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12564-016-9426-9

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analysis of scaffold mode across the 29 studies: 25 studies coded domain-general (2392 subjects, ES = 0.432), three domain-specific (205 subjects, ES = 0.102), one using both (51 subjects, ES = 1.699). The between-group test was significant.

> "The highest effect size was associated with both modes (ES = 1.699). The lowest effect size was correlated with the domain-speciﬁc scaf- folds (ES = 0.102) (Table 7). The test of heterogeneity in effect sizes indicated a signiﬁcant difference between the two kinds of modes ( Q = 42.77, df = 2, p = 0.000)"
