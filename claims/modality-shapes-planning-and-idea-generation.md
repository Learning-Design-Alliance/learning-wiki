# Students reported text supports structured, iterative planning while some valued voice for getting initial ideas out

## Evidence

### planning

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the qualitative ABSA analysis of reflections, the Planning & Thinking Process category was strongly positive for text (44 positive vs 1 negative) and mixed for voice (6 positive vs 17 negative); students referenced "the opportunity to structure cohesive thoughts" using text inputs.

> "Students frequently referenced the opportunity to structure cohesive thoughts or the ability to engage in non-linear problem-solving using text inputs"

### voice-ideas

Kaitlin Riegel, Yan Cathy Hua, Paul Denny, Victor-Alexandru Pădurean, Juho Leinonen, James Prather, and Adish Singla. 2026. Say What? Examining Text and Voice Input Modalities for Prompt-Based Programming in Computing Education. Proceedings of the 31st ACM Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education V. 1 (ITiCSE 2026). https://doi.org/10.1145/3803400.3809397

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The qualitative analysis also found "some students thought voice input served a valuable role in their prompt construction – often to get initial ideas out", with one student reporting voice made it easier to think about what the code needed to do.

> "some students thought voice input served a valuable role in their prompt construction – often to get initial ideas out"
