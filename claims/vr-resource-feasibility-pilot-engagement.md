## Evidence

### Sapci AH 2026

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). Development of Therapeutic Competencies in Health Care Students: Qualitative Focus Group Study Using 360-Degree Video and Virtual Reality Technology. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Overview finding of the thematic analysis of focus group interviews with 12 students from occupational therapy, psychology, and dentistry programs: students perceived the resource as a more interactive and engaging learning experience than traditional teaching, and found it immersive and effective in promoting learning.

> "Overall, the results showed that the learning resource engaged the students and contributed to an active exploration of the learning material, leading to a deeper understanding and better retention of information."

### Sapci AH 2026 (2)

Sapci AH, Ugbor A, Ayub R, Lauritzen C, Reedtz C, Kristensen KB, Mathisen V, Næss ET, Furu R, Nermo H, Jentoft R. (2026). Development of Therapeutic Competencies in Health Care Students: Qualitative Focus Group Study Using 360-Degree Video and Virtual Reality Technology. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/75776

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The authors state the study was designed for preliminary insights into feasibility and perceived educational value rather than definitive conclusions, and recommend larger, multi-institutional studies and comparative studies with control groups using traditional teaching methods.

> "This pilot study involved a small sample size (n=12) and a single-case context, which limits the generalizability of the findings."
