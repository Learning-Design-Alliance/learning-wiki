# Although the three learning approaches form a hierarchy of abstraction, there is no clear hierarchy in learning outcome as measured by success

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Learning only through one approach (e.g. theoretical) does not necessarily imply an ability to solve problems more effectively solved at the conceptual or empirical level. [→ T. Sampson 2013](#t-sampson-2013)

## Evidence

### T. Sampson 2013

T. Sampson, and M. Hilke. (2013). Student Variability in Learning Advanced Physics. arXiv:1307.6144v1 [physics.ed-ph]. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.6144)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Theoretical argument (type e) from the Advanced Physics Learning section: a student expert in solving Maxwell's equations might not grasp implications, e.g. cannot give an approximate solution where the interface is rough. This is an authors' interpretation, not a tested result; no effect size is printed.

> "Hence, while there is a hierarchy in terms of level of abstraction, there is no clear hierarchy in terms of outcome as measured by success."
