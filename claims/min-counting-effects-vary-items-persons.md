## Evidence

### Rhodes 2018

Rhodes, K. T., Lukowski, S., Branum-Martin, L., Opfer, J., Geary, D. C., & Petrill, S. A. (2018). Individual Differences in Addition Strategy Choice: A Psychometric Evaluation. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11360214/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 2 added random slopes for min-counting to the crossclassified EIRT model of the 376-adolescent addition task. The article reports a significant average slowdown and "significant variation in adolescents' efficiency of using min-counting"; item slope variance was also significant (τ1i=3.70 seconds, p < .001).

> "The overall effect of min-counting (relative to the use of non-counting strategies such as retrieval) was about a 3 s an increase in solution times (person solution time slope mean, ɣ10=2.59, p < .001). However, the use of min-counting strategies varied significantly across people (person solution time slope standard deviation, τ1p=1.90 seconds, p < .001)"
