# Self-assessment does not statistically significantly predict performance in cybersecurity games

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Regression models using the 3-item self-assessment were not statistically significant and had R² below 0.07, so self-assessment was an unreliable skill predictor in this context. [→ Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018](#valdemar-svabensky-and-jan-vykopal-2018)

## Evidence

### Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal 2018

Valdemar Švábenský and Jan Vykopal. (2018). Challenges Arising from Prerequisite Testing in Cybersecurity Games. Proceedings of SIGCSE '18: The 49th ACM Technical Symposium on Computing Science Education. https://doi.org/10.1145/3159450.3159454

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Regression analyses of the G1 sessions (67 participants overall in the experiment; self-assessment aggregated as the median of three four-level tool-expertise ratings). No statistically significant fit for game score or levels finished; the article prints no effect size, only R² below 0.07 and no significance.

> "However, the regression models employing self-assessment were not statistically signi/f_icant, and their R2 values were below 0.07."
