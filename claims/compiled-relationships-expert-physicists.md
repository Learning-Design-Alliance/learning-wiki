# Expert physicists use compiled relationships—previously known relationships between quantities—to draw graphs rather than considering the problem directly

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` In think-aloud interviews, physics graduate students applied known physics models (e.g., uniform circular motion, V ∝ 1/R) to assign graph forms, spending their time on initial conditions rather than the graph's form. [→ Charlotte Zimmerman 2020](#charlotte-zimmerman-2020)

`q2 i?` Participants deployed relationships between familiar quantities, such as substituting time for total distance traveled or applying trigonometric functions to uniform circular motion, rather than considering the covariational relationship directly. [→ Charlotte Zimmerman 2020](#charlotte-zimmerman-2020)

## Evidence

### Charlotte Zimmerman 2019

Charlotte Zimmerman, Alexis Olsho, Michael Loverude, Suzanne White Brahmia. (2019). Identifying Covariational Reasoning Behaviors in Expert Physicists in Graphing Tasks. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1911.02044](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1911.02044)

`q2 · i?`

Qualitative analysis of think-aloud interviews with physics graduate students performing graphing tasks with animations. The authors define the behavior: participants "use a previously known relationship between quantities to draw the graph rather than considering the problem at hand directly." Examples include applying a uniform circular motion model in Ferris Wheel and V ∝ 1/R in Rigamarole.

> "Using compiled relationships describes when participants use a previously known relationship between quantities to draw the graph rather than considering the problem at hand directly."
