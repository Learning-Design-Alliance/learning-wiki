Evidence¶

Kristen Hengtgen 2024¶

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2024). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework in Texas. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641978

q2 · i? · associational · r2

EdTrust's TAPR analysis grouping districts by low-income student share (fewer than 45%, 45-80%, more than 80%) finds "in districts with more students from low-income backgrounds, Latinos completed advanced courses at somewhat higher rates," while completion declined slightly in high-poverty districts.

"In districts with fewer students from low-income backgrounds, students completed advanced coursework at higher rates overall. Yet in districts with more students from low-income backgrounds, Latinos completed advanced courses at somewhat higher rates."

Kristen Hengtgen 2024 (2)¶

Kristen Hengtgen, Hector Biaggi. (2024). Increasing Access to Advanced Coursework in Texas. EdTrust. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641978

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The same district-poverty analysis of graduates with dual credit reports that growth among "Black and Latino graduates with DC in high-poverty districts" reversed the 2018-19 gap, leaving high-poverty districts slightly ahead by 2020-21.

"Growth in the share of Black and Latino graduates with DC in high-poverty districts contributed to the fact that, in 2020-21, high-poverty districts had just slightly higher shares of graduates with DC than low-poverty districts."
