# Titration of storage task difficulty reduces the age difference in dual task costs to a non-significant level

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The age difference in the concurrence cost is significant in non-titrated studies (-0.246) but smaller and non-significant in titrated studies (-0.111), and the two effect sizes do not differ significantly. [→ Jaroslawska 2019](#jaroslawska-2019)

## Evidence

### Jaroslawska 2019

Jaroslawska, A. J., & Rhodes, S. (2019). Adult Age Diﬀerences in the Eﬀects of Processing on Storage in Working Memory: A Meta-Analysis. Psychology & Aging. https://doi.org/10.1037/pag0000358

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-regression of the paired dual task cost with titration as moderator. Titration "did not signiﬁcantly reduce heterogeneity in eﬀect sizes, Q(1) = 1.583, p = 0.208", and the two moderator-level effect sizes did not differ significantly (difference 0.135, p = 0.208).

> "There was a small, signiﬁcant, age diﬀerence in the concurrence cost for studies that did not titrate, -0.246 [-0.38, -0.113]. The eﬀect size for studies that did titrate was smaller and not signiﬁcantly diﬀerent from zero, -0.111 [-0.274, 0.053]."
