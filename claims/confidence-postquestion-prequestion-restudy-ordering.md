## Evidence

### Lima 2020

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 confidence analysis (three-point face scale after each multiple choice item) among 27 fifth graders; significant condition effect with a large effect size ("F(2,54) = 54.86, p < 0.001, ␩p2 = 0.68") and follow-up t-tests confirming each pairwise difference.

> "For the confidence data, the ANOVA showed a significant difference between learning conditions, F(2,54) = 54.86, p < 0.001, ␩p2 = 0.68. As shown in Table 1, children responded more confidently when target-words were learned through postquestions, followed by prequestions, and by restudy."

### Lima 2020 (2)

Lima, N. K. d., & Jaeger, A. (2020). The Effects of Prequestions versus Postquestions on Memory Retention in Children. Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.08.005

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 confidence analysis among 100 fourth graders replicated the ordering with a large effect size ("F(2,198) = 31.04, p < 0.001, ␩p2 = 0.24") and significant follow-up t-tests.

> "the ANOVA on the confidence data revealed a sig- nificant difference for learning condition, F(2,198) = 31.04, p < 0.001, ␩p2 = 0.24. As shown in Table 1, children responded more confidently when target-words were learned through postquestions, followed by prequestions, and by restudy."
