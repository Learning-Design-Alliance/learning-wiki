# Rapid guessing on achievement tests tends to be fairly state-like rather than a stable trait-like behavior, compared to academic achievement scores which are fairly stable

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r?` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across six test administrations over three years, rapid guessing behaves mostly as a situational (state-like) behavior, while academic achievement scores are fairly stable over time. [→ James Soland 2020](#james-soland-2020)

## Evidence

### James Soland 2020

James Soland, Steven Wise. (2020). Do students rapidly guess repeatedly over time? A longitudinal analysis of student test disengagement, background, and attitudes. NWEA Research presentation, 2020 National Council on Measurement in Education virtual conference. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/do-students-rapidly-guess-repeatedly-over-time-a-longitudinal-analysis-of-student-test-disengagement-background-and-attitudes/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Longitudinal analysis of achievement test data from six administrations over three years, modeled with a Latent State Trait (LST) model. The authors report that "rapid guessing tends to be fairly state-like compared to academic achievement scores, which are fairly stable." No effect size is printed.

> "We find that rapid guessing tends to be fairly state-like compared to academic achievement scores, which are fairly stable."
