## Evidence

### Zainab Abolfazli Khonbi & Karim Sadeghi 2017

Zainab Abolfazli Khonbi & Karim Sadeghi. (2017). Improving English Language Learners’ Idiomatic Competence: Does Mode of Teaching Play a Role? Iranian Journal of Language Teaching Research 5(3), 61-79. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1156202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Tukey HSD post-hoc comparisons on posttest scores of the four groups (N = 47) established the descending effectiveness order role-play, movie, sentence-use, definition; the article states this implies teaching mode plays an important role in learning idioms.

> "The groups outperformed each other in a descending order with the RG4 -Role-play Group outperforming the MG1 -Movie Group, and both outperforming the SG2 -Sentence-use Group, and, finally, all three groups outperforming the DG3-Definition Group."

### Zainab Abolfazli Khonbi & Karim Sadeghi 2017 (2)

Zainab Abolfazli Khonbi & Karim Sadeghi. (2017). Improving English Language Learners’ Idiomatic Competence: Does Mode of Teaching Play a Role? Iranian Journal of Language Teaching Research 5(3), 61-79. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1156202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc Tukey comparisons found the Sentence-use Group mean (M = 11.46, SD = 3.54) significantly different from the Role-play Group (M = 17.60, SD = 1.95); the Movie–Definition and Role-play–Definition differences were also significant at the 0.05 level.

> "The SG2-Sentence-use G roup (M = 11.46, SD = 3.54) displayed a mean significantly different from the RG4 -Role-play Group (M = 17.60, SD = 1.95)."
