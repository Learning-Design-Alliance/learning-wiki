## Evidence

### Garrud Paul 2018

Garrud Paul, McManus I. C. (2018). Impact of accelerated, graduate-entry medicine courses: a comparison of profile, success, and specialty destination between graduate entrants to accelerated or standard medicine courses in UK. BMC Medical Education 18:250. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1355-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Chi-square analysis of specialty applications and appointments from UKMED cohort data (Table 12). GP appointment rates differed: "18.5% of 4-yr applicants being accepted, compared with 12.7% of 5-yr graduates (p = .005)".

> "For the four largest specialties in terms of applicants, only one, General Practice, showed any significant differences between those on 4-yr and 5-yr courses, 18.5% of 4-yr applicants being accepted, compared with 12.7% of 5-yr graduates (p = .005)."

### Garrud Paul 2018 (2)

Garrud Paul, McManus I. C. (2018). Impact of accelerated, graduate-entry medicine courses: a comparison of profile, success, and specialty destination between graduate entrants to accelerated or standard medicine courses in UK. BMC Medical Education 18:250. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1355-3

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Application-rate comparison across specialties in the same cohort (Table 12): "no differences in application rates to different specialties, with the single exception of anaesthetics".

> "Graduates from 4-yr and 5-yr programmes showed no differences in application rates to different specialties, with the single exception of anaesthetics, for which Ns were small."
