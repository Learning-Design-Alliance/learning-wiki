## Evidence

### Gray-Lobe 2021

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 7 on disciplinary outcomes measured in high school for the randomized sample, where "Preschool attendance reduces the total number of suspensions students receive in high school by 0.24". Truancy and absence estimates are negative but less precise.

> "Preschool attendance reduces the total number of suspensions students receive in high school by 0.24 (p < 0.1). Estimates for the number of times a student is truant and the number of days a student is absent are also negative, though estimates for these outcomes are less precise."

### Gray-Lobe 2021 (2)

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS estimates from Table 7 using juvenile incarceration defined as ever observed attending a Massachusetts Department of Youth Services school, a measure the authors call "a conservative measure of interaction with the criminal justice system". Preschool enrollment is estimated to reduce juvenile incarceration by 0.8 percentage points (p < 0.1).

> "Only 0.7% of non-offered preschool applicants are ever in- carcerated according to this definition.13 Incarceration is a conservative measure of interaction with the criminal justice system, since a student must be arrested before they are incarcerated."
