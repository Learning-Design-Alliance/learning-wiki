## Evidence

### Melissa Costan 2026

Melissa Costan, Kasim Costan, Anna Weißbach and Christoph Kulgemeyer (2026). Exploring Physics Teachers' Views on Physics Education Research: A Case of Science Scepticism?. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2026.2629345](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2026.2629345)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Multinomial logistic regression on survey data from 174 physics teachers (profile membership as dependent variable, The Sceptical Ones as reference group) found that "only the frequency of reading instructional journals has a significant effect on profile membership".

> "Among all potential predictors, only the frequency of reading instructional journals has a significant effect on profile membership."

### Melissa Costan 2026 (2)

Melissa Costan, Kasim Costan, Anna Weißbach and Christoph Kulgemeyer (2026). Exploring Physics Teachers' Views on Physics Education Research: A Case of Science Scepticism?. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1080/09500693.2026.2629345](https://doi.org/10.1080/09500693.2026.2629345)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' summary of the same regression model: teaching experience, holding a PhD, and traditionally versus alternatively qualified teacher status "do not predict profile membership"; no statistically significant effects were found for the other explored covariates.

> "However, many personal variables – such as teaching experience, holding a PhD, or being a traditionally or alternatively qualified teacher (with the latter having had no exposure to science education research during their studies) – do not predict profile membership."
