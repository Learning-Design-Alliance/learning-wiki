# Reporting frequency of demographic categories in EDM research is inversely proportional to how sensitive the category is, with race (9%) and SES (5%) reported least

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Categories tied to individual students, especially race and SES, were reported far less often than study-level categories, and the authors attribute this to collection effort and re-identification risk. [→ Paquette 2020](#paquette-2020)

## Evidence

### Paquette 2020

Paquette, Li, Baker, Ocumpaugh, & Andres. (2020). Who's learning? Using demographics in EDM research. Journal of Educational Data Mining, Volume 12, No 3. https://jedm.educationaldatamining.org/index.php/JEDM/article/view/404

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Interpretive finding from the survey of applicable EDM publications: individual-level categories (age 19%, gender 25%) lag behind study-level ones, and "race and SES reported quite infrequently" at 9% and 5% respectively.

> "the frequency in which different demographic categories were reported was inversely proportional to how sensitive it is considered, with race and SES reported quite infrequently (respectively 9% and 5%)."
