### Smith 2000

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The S&EI-2000 survey found "36% of adults" calling astrology scientific, with a comparable percentage believing in UFOs and alien landings, and about half believing in extrasensory perception. The article attributes the trend partly to media dominance.

> "The S&EI-2000 study is only a little more sanguine: it found 36% of adults agreeing that astrology is "very scientific" or "sort of scientific," and notes that a roughly comparable percentage believes in UFOs and that aliens have landed on Earth"

### Smith 2000 (2)

Smith, Howard A. (2000). Challenges in Affecting US Attitudes Towards Space Science. COSPAR 2000, Warsaw. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0008224)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The article reports a York University survey in which "53% of first year students" subscribed to astrology's principles, an increase of 16 percentage points for science students since 1991; students also agreed astronomers can predict character and future.

> "In a recent survey undertaken by York University in Toronto, 53% of first year students in both the arts and the sciences, after hearing a definition of astrology, said they "somewhat" or "completely" subscribed to its principles"
