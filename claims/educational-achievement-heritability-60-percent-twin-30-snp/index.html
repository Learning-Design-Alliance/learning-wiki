Evidence¶

Rimfeld 2018¶

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Univariate twin model-fitting (ACE) on achievement measures from age 7 to 16 in the TEDS sample, reported in Results; estimates did not vary systematically across subjects or school years, and "All achievement measures show substantial heritability (A ~60%)." These are variance components; no standardised effect size is printed.

"All achievement measures show substantial heritability (A ~60%). Shared (C) and non-shared (E) environmental factors both explained about 20% of the variance."

Rimfeld 2018 (2)¶

Rimfeld, K., Malanchini, M., Krapohl, E., Hannigan, L. J., Dale, P. S. & Plomin, R. (2018). The stability of educational achievement across school years is largely explained by genetic factors. npj Science of Learning 3:16. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0030-0

q2 · i? · associational · r3

GCTA SNP-heritability analysis of the same achievement measures in the TEDS genotyped subsample; the article reports estimates were "substantial (~30%)" and about half as large as twin estimates. This is a variance component, not a printed effect size.

"Figure 1b shows that SNP heritabilities were substantial (~30%) but, as expected, only about half as large as the twin estimates"
