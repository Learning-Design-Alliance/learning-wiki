Evidence¶

Thibault Asselborn 2021¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Grade-wise comparison of the Median of the Power Spectral of Tremor Frequencies across the two alphabets (Fig. 2D); the article interprets the shrinking gap as transfer between alphabets being in line with the level of automation acquired.

"children writing in Latin presented a signi ﬁcantly higher level of shakiness compared to when writing in Cyrillic in the lower grades (1st and second grade), while the difference was nearly non-existent by grade 4."

Thibault Asselborn 2021 (2)¶

Thibault Asselborn, Wafa Johal, Bolat Tleubayev, Zhanel Zhexenova, Pierre Dillenbourg, Catherine McBride and Anara Sandygulova. (2021). The transferability of handwriting skills: from the Cyrillic to the Latin alphabet. npj Science of Learning 6:6. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00084-w

q2 · i? · causal · r1

In-Air-Time Ratio, previously correlated with handwriting problems in Latin- and Hebrew-alphabet studies, was always smaller for Cyrillic; the difference was statistically significant only in grade 1 and absent in later grades.

"Although the difference between the two alphabets was statistically signi ﬁcant for the lowest grade, no differences were found for subsequent grades."
