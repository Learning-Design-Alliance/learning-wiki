## Evidence

### Curtis E 2015

Curtis E, Wikaire E, Jiang Y, McMillan L, Loto R, Airini, Reid P. (2015). Quantitative analysis of a Māori and Pacific admission process on first-year health study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0470-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression analysis of 368 First Year Tertiary MAPAS students (2009–2012), adjusted for interview year, gender, ancestry and school decile, showed "Students who followed MAPAS advice had on average a GPA that was 1.2 points higher". The logistic model gave OR 5.40 (CI: 2.36-12.39; p < 0.0001) for passing all eight courses.

> "Students who followed MAPAS advice had on average a GPA that was 1.2 points higher (out of a total 9) than students who did not (CI: 0.57-1.78; p = 0.0002)."

### Curtis E 2015 (2)

Curtis E, Wikaire E, Jiang Y, McMillan L, Loto R, Airini, Reid P. (2015). Quantitative analysis of a Māori and Pacific admission process on first-year health study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0470-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression analysis of 242 First Year Bachelor MAPAS students found "Students who followed MAPAS advice had on average a GPA that was 1.1 points higher" for all eight and Core 4 courses; odds of passing all courses were 3.3 times higher (CI: 1.45-7.69; p = 0.005).

> "Students who followed MAPAS advice had on average a GPA that was 1.1 points higher than students who did not follow MAPAS advice for all eight courses (CI: 0.45-1.73; p = 0.0009) and Core 4 courses (CI: 0.60-2.04; p = 0.0004)."
