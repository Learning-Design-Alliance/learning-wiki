## Evidence

### Kupf Irma 2024

Kupf Irma, Thanner Gabriele, Gerken Michael, Crispin Alexander, Braess Jan. (2024). How accurate is clinical prognostication by oncologists during routine practice in a general hospital and can it be improved by a specific prognosis training programme: a prospective interventional study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-081661

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Phase 3 comparison in the prospective study, where tools were available and suitable for only 34.6% of patients; the article reports the tool-versus-physician contrast with p<0.0005 and no effect size.

> "When used alone for the SQ the accuracy of these prognostic tools was only 70.2%, which was significantly worse (p<0.0005) than the performance of the whole group (84.3%) and of the oncologists (85.6%)."

### Kupf Irma 2024 (2)

Kupf Irma, Thanner Gabriele, Gerken Michael, Crispin Alexander, Braess Jan. (2024). How accurate is clinical prognostication by oncologists during routine practice in a general hospital and can it be improved by a specific prognosis training programme: a prospective interventional study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2023-081661

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Within-physician paired comparison on 443 patients in phase 3, each estimate made first without and then with the tool result; the article reports no significant increase, with no effect size printed.

> "In a subgroup (n=443 patients) physicians made PE on CPS first without and then with the respective prognostic tool—however, accuracy was not significantly increased (28.0% to 29.4%)."
