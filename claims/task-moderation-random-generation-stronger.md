## Evidence

### Zhu 2025

Zhu, X., Tang, Y., Lu, J., Song, M., Yang, C., & Zhao, X. (2025). Inhibitory Control and Mathematical Ability in Elementary School Children: A Preregistered Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37(1), 1. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09976-w

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Omnibus moderator test across seven coded task categories in the three-level meta-analysis was non-significant. Inhibitory control measured by different tasks was positively related with all six mathematical ability domains (rs = 0.16–0.39, ps ≤ 0.018).

> "We found no significant moderating effect of inhibitory control task (see Table 1), F(6, 232) = 0.76, p = 0.601."

### Zhu 2025 (2)

Zhu, X., Tang, Y., Lu, J., Song, M., Yang, C., & Zhao, X. (2025). Inhibitory Control and Mathematical Ability in Elementary School Children: A Preregistered Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37(1), 1. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09976-w

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Pairwise subgroup comparisons (online SM Appendix D Table S2) showed the Random generation task correlate (r = 0.39) exceeded both the Go/No-go task (t = 1.97, p = 0.050) and other tasks (t = 2.06, p = 0.041).

> "inhibitory control measured by the Random generation task (r = 0.39, 95% CI [0.18, 0.56], p < 0.001) was more strongly associated with mathematical ability than that measured by the Go/No-go task (r = 0.17, 95% CI [0.08, 0.25], p < 0.001; t = 1.97, p = 0.050) and other tasks (r = 0.16, 95% CI [0.08, 0.24], p < 0.001; t = 2.06, p = 0.041)."
