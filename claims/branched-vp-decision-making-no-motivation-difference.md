## Evidence

### Woodham Luke A. 2019

Woodham Luke A., Round Jonathan, Stenfors Terese, Bujacz Aleksandra, Karlgren Klas, Jivram Trupti, Riklefs Viktor, Poulton Ella, Poulton Terry. (2019). Virtual patients designed for training against medical error: Exploring the impact of decision-making on learner motivation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215597

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Aggregated analysis of post-intervention MSLQ survey responses in a two-arm post-test cluster pseudo-randomised controlled trial across six institutions in three countries; two-tailed unpaired t-tests found "no significant differences between the linear and branched groups in any of the subscales".

> "When the results for all institutions were aggregated and analysed as a single dataset, the results showed no significant differences between the linear and branched groups in any of the subscales."

### Woodham Luke A. 2019 (2)

Woodham Luke A., Round Jonathan, Stenfors Terese, Bujacz Aleksandra, Karlgren Klas, Jivram Trupti, Riklefs Viktor, Poulton Ella, Poulton Terry. (2019). Virtual patients designed for training against medical error: Exploring the impact of decision-making on learner motivation. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0215597

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Per-institution t-tests across the six replicated administrations of the trial; the article reports that "in the majority of the subscales and institutions there was not a significant difference between the responses of the linear and the branching group".

> "in the majority of the subscales and institutions there was not a significant difference between the responses of the linear and the branching group"
