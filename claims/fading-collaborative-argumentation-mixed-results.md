# Fading of instructional support in collaborative argumentation has produced mixed results, with effectiveness depending on the fading criterion used

## Evidence

### Noroozi 2018

Noroozi, O., Kirschner, P. A., Biemans, H. J. A., & Mulder, M. (2018). Promoting Argumentation Competence: Extending from First- to Second-Order Scaffolding Through Adaptive Fading. Educational Psychol Rev, 30, 153–176. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9400-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports a study in which fading over time produced "stronger explanations themselves in terms of their reasoning compared to the continuous support group"; no effect size is printed.

> "McNeill et al. (2006) showed that when support is faded over time, students provide stronger explanations themselves in terms of their reasoning compared to the continuous support group."

### Noroozi 2018 (2)

Noroozi, O., Kirschner, P. A., Biemans, H. J. A., & Mulder, M. (2018). Promoting Argumentation Competence: Extending from First- to Second-Order Scaffolding Through Adaptive Fading. Educational Psychol Rev, 30, 153–176. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9400-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports that message-count-based fading "did not yield better acquisition of argumentation compe- tence" than continuous or peer-monitoring support; the review adds peer-monitoring students outperformed both fading and continuous groups.

> "Bouyias and Demetriadis ( 2012) found that fading controlled by the number of posted messages during discourse did not yield better acquisition of argumentation compe- tence than continuous script support or peer-monitoring support."

### Noroozi 2018 (3)

Noroozi, O., Kirschner, P. A., Biemans, H. J. A., & Mulder, M. (2018). Promoting Argumentation Competence: Extending from First- to Second-Order Scaffolding Through Adaptive Fading. Educational Psychol Rev, 30, 153–176. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9400-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

The review reports a study by Lee and Songer in which phase-controlled fading "yielded a lower quality of reasoning explanations than continuous support", a negative result for fading in CABLe.

> "fading that was controlled by the learning progression phase during discourse yielded a lower quality of reasoning explanations than continuous support"
