## Evidence

### Agrawal 2026

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r1`

Randomised trial of 206 first-attempt neonatal intubations by doctors with six months' tertiary neonatal experience, comparing visible versus covered VL screen for the instructor. The review reports the visible-screen group succeeded in "66% (69/104)" versus 41%, OR 2.81.

> "The success rate when the instructor was able to view the VL screen was 66% (69/104) compared with 41% (42/102) when the screen was covered (Odds Ratio 2.81, 95% CI 1.54–5.17)."

### Agrawal 2026 (2)

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r1`

Randomised cross-over study of 24 trainees intubating 144 infants aged 1 day to 6 months, comparing VL-assisted verbal feedback with conventional feedback. First-attempt success was "83.3% vs. 44.4%" favouring VL-assisted feedback; no effect size is printed.

> "Saran et al.18 in a randomised cross-over study of 24 trainees performing ETI on 144 infants aged 1 day to 6 months found the first attempt success rate to be higher with VL-assisted feedback compared to conventional feedback (83.3% vs. 44.4%)."

### Agrawal 2026 (3)

Agrawal, S., Rakshasbhuvankar, A., Rath, C., Brown, J. A., McEvoy, P. M., Rao, S., & Patole, S. (2026). Neonatal endotracheal intubation: development of a comprehensive educational program for procedural skills. Pediatric Research, 99, 484–489. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04217-4

`q3 · i?` · `design · r1`

RCT randomising 49 neonatal learners with under one year of intubation experience to a VL/DL or standard DL curriculum. The review reports the standard DL group was "more successful in the clinical intubation setting (70% vs 33%, p = 0.048)".

> "Compared to the VL/DL curriculum, participants in the standard DL curriculum were more successful in the clinical intubation setting (70% vs 33%, p = 0.048)."
