# Invention activities lead to greater mastery-related goal adoption during learning than tell-and-practice activities

## Evidence

### Belenky 2012

Daniel M. Belenky and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2012). Motivation and Transfer: The Role of Mastery-Approach Goals in Preparation for Future Learning. The Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.651232

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Activity questionnaire administered after the variability activity in the laboratory experiment; groups were equivalent on initial and final achievement goal orientations (all ts(102) < 1.11, ns), supporting a task-goal-adoption interpretation.

> "Participants in the invention condition were more likely to be concerned with the quality of the procedure they were using, t(102) = 2.82, p \< .05, d = 0.56; as well as their level of understanding of the procedure, t(102) = 2.75, p \< .05, d = 0.54."

### Belenky 2012 (2)

Daniel M. Belenky and Timothy J. Nokes-Malach. (2012). Motivation and Transfer: The Role of Mastery-Approach Goals in Preparation for Future Learning. The Journal of the Learning Sciences. https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.651232

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Third activity-questionnaire item in the same laboratory experiment: invention students reported more concern that their procedure was not correct, d = 0.80, a large effect by the article's own convention.

> "Participants given invention activities were also more likely to be concerned with whether their procedure was correct, t(102) = 4.02, p \< .05, d = 0.80."
