## Evidence

### van Det M. J. 2011

van Det M. J., Meijerink W. J. H. J., Hoff C., Middel L. J., Koopal S. A., Pierie J. P. E. N. (2011). The learning effect of intraoperative video-enhanced surgical procedure training. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-010-1545-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Item-level analysis of the randomized trial's OSATS ratings found "a statistically significant (P < 0.05) and clinically relevant (ES > 0.80) difference in skills acquisition in favor of the INVEST group" on the four named items; Table 1 prints per-item ES values of 2.82, 2.34, 1.99 and 1.97.

> "Analysis of the absolute and relative improvements in the seven separate skills on the OSATS global rating scale indicated a statistically significant (P < 0.05) and clinically relevant (ES > 0.80) difference in skills acquisition in favor of the INVEST group for the following skills: time and motion, use of assistants, flow of operation, and knowledge of the procedure (Table 1)."
