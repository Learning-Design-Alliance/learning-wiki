## Evidence

### Martin O. Archer 2020

Martin O. Archer. (2020). Schools of all backgrounds can do physics research: On the accessibility and equity of the PRiSE approach to independent research projects. arXiv. [doi:10.5194/gc-2020-37](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-2020-37)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Benchmarking analysis of publicly available school data: PRiSE schools' free school meal percentages compared via Wilcoxon rank-sum tests against HiSPARC and IRIS schools and UK national statistics, with PRiSE "considerably more disadvantaged students than both HiSPARC" and IRIS at the printed p-values.

> "This means that PRiSE is serving schools with considerably more disadvantaged students than both HiSPARC ( +7% and 2.09×,p = 0.033) and IRIS (+8% and 2.18×,p = 2 × 10−4) in this regard."

### Martin O. Archer 2020 (2)

Martin O. Archer. (2020). Schools of all backgrounds can do physics research: On the accessibility and equity of the PRiSE approach to independent research projects. arXiv. [doi:10.5194/gc-2020-37](https://doi.org/10.5194/gc-2020-37)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same school-level benchmarking study's index of multiple deprivation comparison: PRiSE schools' catchment-area deprivation scores are considerably higher than national statistics (median +9.40, 1.54×, p = 7 × 10−4), while "IRIS schools again clearly favour fewer disadvantaged students than PRiSE".

> "In contrast, IRIS schools again clearly favour fewer disadvantaged students than PRiSE (−6.56 and 0.65×,p = 4 × 10−4) and thus also the national statistics ( −5.07 and 0.71×,p = 2 × 10−6)"
