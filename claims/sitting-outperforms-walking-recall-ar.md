## Evidence

### Janaka 2025

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Study 1 (N=12) repeated-measures ANOVA with ART showed significant main effects of Mobility (p < 0.05). Sitting outperformed Walking on Free-Recall, Word-Recall, and Seen-Sentence-Recall in immediate recall.

> "In immediate Recall, Sitting yielded significantly higher Free-Recall, Word-Recall, and Seen-Sentence-Recall scores (𝑝 < 0.05)."

### Janaka 2025 (2)

Janaka, N., Zhao, S., Ram, A., Sun, R., Tan, S. T. J. W., Li, D., & Hsu, D. (2025). Progressive Sentences: Combining the Benefits of Word and Sentence Learning. MobileHCI '25 Adjunct. [doi:10.1145/3737821.3749564](https://doi.org/10.1145/3737821.3749564)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Study 1 (N=12) subjective cognitive-load ratings via ART-based ANOVA: significant main effects of Mobility on all scales. Participants attributed this to divided attention during multitasking diminishing capacity for remembering.

> "All subjective scales showed significant main effects of Mobility (𝑝 < 0.05) via ART-based ANOVA. Sitting was rated lower in DifficultToUnderstand and Confusing, and higher in EasyToRemember, AbsorbedInLearning, and Enjoyable."
