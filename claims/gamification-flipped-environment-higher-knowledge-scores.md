# Gamification in a flipped learning environment produced higher knowledge-evaluation scores than gamification, flipped class and lecture methods in nursing students learning health assessment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a four-group randomized trial, knowledge evaluation scores after intervention differed significantly between the gamification methods (in the flipped learning environment and alone) and the flipped class and lecture methods (P < 0.001). [→ Ghafouri Raziyeh 2024](#ghafouri-raziyeh-2024)
`q3 i?` The two gamification methods also differed significantly from each other (P = 0.03), while gamification and flipped class did not (P = 0.054). [→ Ghafouri Raziyeh 2024 (2)](#ghafouri-raziyeh-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Ghafouri Raziyeh 2024

Ghafouri Raziyeh, Zamanzadeh Vahid, Nasiri Malihe. (2024). Comparison of education using the flipped class, gamification and gamification in the flipped learning environment on the performance of nursing students in a client health assessment: a randomized clinical trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05966-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

ANOVA on post-intervention knowledge scores in a parallel randomized clinical trial of 166 nursing students assigned by colored cards to four teaching groups. The article reports a significant difference "considering equal variance (P < 0.001)"; no effect size is printed.

> "In the analysis of the groups, there was a significant difference in the comparison of the knowledge evaluation scores, such that there was a significant difference between the average of the gamification methods in the flipped learning environment group and the gamification compared to the inverted class and lecture, considering equal variance (P < 0.001)."

### Ghafouri Raziyeh 2024 (2)

Ghafouri Raziyeh, Zamanzadeh Vahid, Nasiri Malihe. (2024). Comparison of education using the flipped class, gamification and gamification in the flipped learning environment on the performance of nursing students in a client health assessment: a randomized clinical trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05966-2

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc pairwise comparisons of knowledge scores between the four intervention groups in the same randomized trial, as printed in the Results section; only P values are reported, with no effect size.

> "There were significant differences at the 0.05 level between the two gamification methods in the flipped learning environment group and the gamification group (P = 0.03). Gamification and flipped classes had no significant difference (P = 0.054)."
