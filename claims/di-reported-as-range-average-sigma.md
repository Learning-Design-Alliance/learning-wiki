# Reporting DI as an average with standard deviation over question combinations represents questions better than a single DI from the total of all marks

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For the INJSO 2016 data, DI estimates from small and large combination sets agree within standard deviation, and combination-based values represent questions better than DI from the total of all marks. [→ Joshi 2019](#joshi-2019)

## Evidence

### Joshi 2019

Joshi, P.K., Jain, Y., Khunyakari, R. and Basu, S. (2019). A novel alternative to analyzing multiple choice questions via discrimination index. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1906.07941)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of the INJSO 2016 screening test computed DI distributions over random combinations of the remaining questions; the two combination-based estimates "agree within the range of standard deviation," and the 3-29 combination values "tend to represent the question more appropriately than when calculated" using total of all marks.

> "The two values agree within the range of standard deviation and hence the 3-29 combi- nation DI values tend to represent the question more appropriately than when calculated"
