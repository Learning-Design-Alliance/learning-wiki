## Evidence

### Hayward Mark F 2014

Hayward Mark F, Curran Vernon, Curtis Bryan, Schulz Henry, Murphy Sean. (2014). Reliability of the Interprofessional Collaborator Assessment Rubric (ICAR) in Multi Source Feedback (MSF) with post-graduate medical residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0279-9

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

One-way ANOVA of overall ICAR scores by rater characteristics in the MSF field test. The article reports "F1,5 = 7.184, p = .008, η2 = .045" (4.5% of variance) and means of 6.12 for female versus 6.82 for male raters.

> "The only significant, main-effect on overall ICAR score was found to be the gender of the rater (F1,5 = 7.184, p = .008, η2 = .045) providing a moderate effect size constituting 4.5% of the variance. Female raters scored residents significantly lower than male raters (6.12 v. 6.82)."

### Hayward Mark F 2014 (2)

Hayward Mark F, Curran Vernon, Curtis Bryan, Schulz Henry, Murphy Sean. (2014). Reliability of the Interprofessional Collaborator Assessment Rubric (ICAR) in Multi Source Feedback (MSF) with post-graduate medical residents. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-014-0279-9

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression analysis of the field-test data identified rater gender as the only significant predictor of overall ICAR score, with the printed odds ratios of 3.08 and 3.28. No standardised effect size was printed.

> "Male raters were 3.08 times more likely than female raters to provide an overall ICAR score of above 6.0 (p = .013) and 3.28 times more likely to score above 7.0 (p = .005)."
