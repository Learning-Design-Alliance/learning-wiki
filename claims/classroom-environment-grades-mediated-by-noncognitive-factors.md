## Evidence

### Camille A. Farrington 2019

Camille A. Farrington, Shanette Porter, Joshua Klugman. (2019). Do Classroom Environments Matter for Noncognitive Aspects of Student Performance and Students' Course Grades? University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/do-classroom-environments-matter-for-noncognitive-aspects-of-student-performance-and-students-course-grades

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Within-student multilevel mediation analysis (Table 5) of BEL-S survey and administrative grade data from 8,318 students. The baseline classroom-environment coefficient (β=0.042) was reduced considerably when each noncognitive factor was controlled one at a time, and in Model 9 with all noncognitive factors the relationship was "virtually zero" (β=0.002).

> "Classroom environments have a small, but reliable, positive within-student relationship with student grades (β=0.04), and this relationship is mediated by student noncognitive factors."
