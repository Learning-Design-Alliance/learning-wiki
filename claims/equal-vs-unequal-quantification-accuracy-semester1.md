# First-semester students who performed unequally across 2-D and 3-D formats had lower quantification accuracy than equal performers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2022 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students stratified as unequal performers (performance difference between 2-D and 3-D estimates ≥6 of 12 items, n = 20) achieved lower food quantification accuracy than equal performers (n = 45): 16% vs. 28%, p = 0.041. [→ Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022](#ho-dang-khanh-ngan-2022)
`q2 i?` Unequal performers also had higher median percentage errors in estimating amorphous food than equal performers (14.75% vs. 6%, p < 0.05). [→ Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022 (2)](#ho-dang-khanh-ngan-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022

Ho Dang Khanh Ngan, Lee Yu-Chieh, Chiu Wan-Chun, Shen Yi-Ta, Yao Chih-Yuan, Chu Hung-Kuo, Chu Wei-Ta, Le Nguyen Quoc Khanh, Nguyen Hung Trong, Su Hsiu-Yueh, Chang Jung-Su. (2022). COVID-19 and Virtual Nutrition: A Pilot Study of Integrating Digital Food Models for Interactive Portion Size Education. Nutrients 14(16):3313. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14163313

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

First-semester stratified analysis splitting the 65 students into equal (n = 45) and unequal (n = 20) performers by 2-D vs. 3-D estimation differences. The study reports "equal performers: 28% vs. unequal performers: 16%, p = 0.041"; only the p value is printed, no effect size.

> "Figure 3A shows that equal performers achieved higher food quantification accuracies than did unequal performers (equal performers: 28% vs. unequal performers: 16%, p = 0.041)."

### Ho Dang Khanh Ngan 2022 (2)

Ho Dang Khanh Ngan, Lee Yu-Chieh, Chiu Wan-Chun, Shen Yi-Ta, Yao Chih-Yuan, Chu Hung-Kuo, Chu Wei-Ta, Le Nguyen Quoc Khanh, Nguyen Hung Trong, Su Hsiu-Yueh, Chang Jung-Su. (2022). COVID-19 and Virtual Nutrition: A Pilot Study of Integrating Digital Food Models for Interactive Portion Size Education. Nutrients 14(16):3313. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14163313

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Wilcoxon/Mann–Whitney analysis of first-semester median percentage errors for amorphous foods, comparing the same equal (n = 45) and unequal (n = 20) performer groups. The study prints the medians and IQRs with "p < 0.05" and no effect size.

> "Unequal performers also had higher median percentage errors in estimating amorphous food compared to equal performers [equal performers: median 6% (IQR: −2.75%, 23.25%) vs. unequal performers: median 14.75% (IQR: −2.75%, 33.38%), p < 0.05] (Figure 3B)."
