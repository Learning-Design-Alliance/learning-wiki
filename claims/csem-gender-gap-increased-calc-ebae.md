## Evidence

### Nafis I. Karim 2018

Nafis I. Karim, Alexandru Maries and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Do evidence-based active-engagement courses reduce the gender gap in introductory physics? https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010103

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Intra-group CSEM pre/posttest analysis (Table II) of pooled calculus-based EBAE courses. The gender gap grew from 4% at pretest to 10% at posttest; male normalized gain was 39% versus 29% for female students. Percentages are descriptive; no effect size for this contrast is printed in the quote.

> "However, in the EBAE calculus-based course, the gender gap increased slightly from 4% to 10 %, and it appears that male students may have benefited more from evidence-based pedagogies than female students (normalized gain for male students was 39% in EBAE courses compared to 29% for female students)."
