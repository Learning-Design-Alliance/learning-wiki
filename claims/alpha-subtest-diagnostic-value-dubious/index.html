Evidence¶

Stone 1920¶

Stone, Charles Leonard. (1920). The significance of alpha in colleges. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+significance+of+alpha+in+colleges%22+Stone

q1 · i? · associational · r2

Author interpretation of subtest-level percentile data for the class of 1923 comparing language and science groups of tests. The article concludes the "diagnostic value of Alpha is very dubious" and that specific instances invalidate definite deductions.

"The very nature of Alpha, and the inclusion of certain tests of little significance—at least in their present form and degree of difficulty —make the diagnostic value of Alpha very dubious."

Stone 1920 (2)¶

Stone, Charles Leonard. (1920). The significance of alpha in colleges. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22The+significance+of+alpha+in+colleges%22+Stone

q2 · i2 · associational · r1

Correlational analysis of subtest combinations against subject grades. Printed correlation coefficients: arithmetic-plus-number-completion with mathematics r = 0.315 ± 0.027, above either test alone (0.272, 0.307) but below total Alpha's r = 0.379 ± 0.026; the synonym-antonym plus disarranged-sentence pair matched total Alpha for English at 0.497.

"The combination of the arithmetic problems and number completion series tests correlates higher with mathematics (0.315 ± 0.027) than either test separately (0.272 ± 0.028 and 0.307 + 0.027); but this specific combination does not correlate so high with mathematics as total Alpha (0.379 + 0.026)."
