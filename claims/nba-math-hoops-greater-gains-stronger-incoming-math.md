# NBA Math Hoops math gains are larger for students entering with stronger incoming math scores, with no significant variation by gender, IEP status, or incoming SEL

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A significant treatment-by-pre-math interaction (β = 0.26, p < 0.01) shows larger math gains for students with stronger incoming math; no significant interaction with gender, IEP, or incoming SEL. [→ McKinney 2026](#mckinney-2026)

## Evidence

### McKinney 2026

McKinney, D., Walters, K., Powell, K., Sabalburo, E., & Morton, C. (2026). The impact of NBA Math Hoops on students' math achievement: A randomized controlled trial. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED681343

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory moderation analyses in the RCT found a significant treatment × pre-math interaction (β = 0.26, p < 0.01). Students 1 SD above mean pre-math were predicted to outperform by ~0.5 SD; students 1 SD below showed similar predicted performance in both conditions.

> "Results indicated that there was a significant interaction between pre-math composite score and treatment (𝛽 = 0.26, 𝑝 < 0.01) and that there was no significant interaction with the other covariates explored in this manner."
