## Evidence

### Ashley A. August 2011

Ashley A. August, Kenneth C. Bretey, Bryant T. Cory, Elliott R. Finkley III, Robbie D. Jones, Dennis W. Marshall, Phillip C. Rowley, and W. Brian Lane. (2011). Enhancing Introductory Student Motivation with a Major-Managed Course Blog: A Pilot Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Content analysis of introductory students' blog comments using Duda & Garrett's five evaluation dimensions. The article reports comments "progress from surface-level to substantive," and separately that students commented more on articles by authors they had met face-to-face, tentatively concluding such relationships encourage responses.

> "The introductory students’ comments did progress from surface-level to sub-stantive over the course of the semester, eventually demonstrating ﬂuency with terminology, precision, and a willingness to apply the concepts of the course to new situations."

### Ashley A. August 2011 (2)

Ashley A. August, Kenneth C. Bretey, Bryant T. Cory, Elliott R. Finkley III, Robbie D. Jones, Dennis W. Marshall, Phillip C. Rowley, and W. Brian Lane. (2011). Enhancing Introductory Student Motivation with a Major-Managed Course Blog: A Pilot Study. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1108.1591)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Frequency analysis of blog comments in the same pilot. The article prints that "Three of the seven introductory students posted a total of 15 comments," and notes one student who credited the blog for interest never commented, suggesting students read more than they commented.

> "The frequency of student comments on the blog was somewhat minimal. Three of the seven introductory students posted a total of 15 comments."
