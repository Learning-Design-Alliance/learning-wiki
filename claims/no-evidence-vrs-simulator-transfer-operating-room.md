## Evidence

### Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019

Rasmussen Rasmus Christian, Grauslund Jakob, Vergmann Anna Stage. (2019). Simulation training in vitreoretinal surgery: a systematic review. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-019-1098-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's conclusion based on only seven included studies, of which one attempted transfer to cadavers. This is a statement of absent evidence, not evidence of absence of transfer.

> "Currently, no evidence supports that simulator-based training of novices in VRS is capable of transferring to the operating room."

### Rasmussen Rasmus Christian 2019 (2)

Rasmussen Rasmus Christian, Grauslund Jakob, Vergmann Anna Stage. (2019). Simulation training in vitreoretinal surgery: a systematic review. BMC Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12886-019-1098-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Randomized trial (n = 14) by Jonas et al. as described in the review: simulator practice versus no training before vitrectomy on enucleated pig eyes; all three comparisons non-significant. Equivalence was not tested.

> "Results from the three vitrectomies were 45.7% vs. 42.9% (p = 0.71), 62.9% vs. 51.4% (p = 0.26), and 64.3% vs. 57.1% (p = 0.54), simulator-trained group vs. untrained group respectively. Thus, no results showed a statistically significant difference."
