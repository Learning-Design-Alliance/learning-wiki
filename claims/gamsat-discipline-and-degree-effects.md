# GAMSAT performance depends on discipline of prior degree, with Honours graduates scoring highest, and profile variables explaining only about 10% of score variance

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Discipline background significantly predicted all four 2014 GAMSAT scores, and multiple regression showed profile variables explained only 7–11% of score variance. [→ Mercer Annette 2015](#mercer-annette-2015)
`q3 i?` Candidates with Honours degrees performed best on all scores, followed by doctorate holders. [→ Mercer Annette 2015 (2)](#mercer-annette-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Mercer Annette 2015

Mercer Annette, Crotty Brendan, Alldridge Louise, Le Luc, Vele Veronica. (2015). GAMSAT: A 10-year retrospective overview, with detailed analysis of candidates’ performance in 2014. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0316-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multiple regression on 2014 scores with dummy-coded profile variables (alpha 5%). Most predictors were significant (p <0.05) except Masters degree on all scores and several others. Discipline ANOVA (Table 3) F (8903,10) = 66.19, p 0.000 for Overall.

> "Results show that the profile variables explain only 10% of the variation in Overall score, 10% of variation in Section 1 score, 7% of variation in Section 2 score, and 11% of variation in Section 3 score."

### Mercer Annette 2015 (2)

Mercer Annette, Crotty Brendan, Alldridge Louise, Le Luc, Vele Veronica. (2015). GAMSAT: A 10-year retrospective overview, with detailed analysis of candidates’ performance in 2014. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-015-0316-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

2014 ANOVA by highest degree (Table 2): Honours Overall mean 59.07, highest in all four scores; Masters lowest (55.19); F (9303,3) = 35.11, p 0.000 for Overall, all p 0.000.

> "Candidates with an Honours degree performed best in all scores, followed by those with a doctorate."
