## Evidence

### Noroozi 2018

Noroozi, O., Kirschner, P. A., Biemans, H. J. A., & Mulder, M. (2018). Promoting Argumentation Competence: Extending from First- to Second-Order Scaffolding Through Adaptive Fading. Educational Psychol Rev, 30, 153–176. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9400-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The review reports an empirical study by Tsovaltzi et al. (2010) concluding adaptive fading "continuously adjusted to the quality of discourse activities can be much more effective than fixed regimes"; no effect size is printed.

> "the conclusion was drawn that adaptive fading in which external script components are continuously adjusted to the quality of discourse activities can be much more effective than fixed regimes for the development of learners 'internal script components"

### Noroozi 2018 (2)

Noroozi, O., Kirschner, P. A., Biemans, H. J. A., & Mulder, M. (2018). Promoting Argumentation Competence: Extending from First- to Second-Order Scaffolding Through Adaptive Fading. Educational Psychol Rev, 30, 153–176. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9400-z

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors state plainly that "there is no evidence for longer-term effects of adaptive fading", limiting the scope of the adaptive-fading advantage to the short term.

> "there is no evidence for longer-term effects of adaptive fading on developing an internal script, and for application of the acquired argumentation competence in similar learning situations"
