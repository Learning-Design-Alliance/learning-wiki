# A brief hands-on training activity with a small transformer significantly increased CS1 students' attributions of incorrect model output to training data, from 13% to 38%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a one-week hands-on training activity, the proportion of students attributing incorrect model output to training data increased from 13% pre-test to 38% post-test, a statistically significant increase (z=5.09, p<.001). ## Evidence

### Nicolas Pope and Matti Tedre 2026

Nicolas Pope and Matti Tedre. (2026). Turning Language Model Training from Black Box into a Sandbox. arXiv. [doi:10.1109/educon67543.2026.11574425](https://doi.org/10.1109/educon67543.2026.11574425)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre/post study in a mandatory first-year CS1 course at a Finnish university; 162 students completed both open-ended pre- and post-tests plus a homework training activity, analyzed with qualitative content analysis. The quoted fragment reports "increased from 13% in the pre-test to 38% in the post-test" with a significant two-proportion z-test; no effect size is printed.

> "Quantitatively, the proportion of students attributing incorrect model output to training data increased from 13% in the pre-test to 38% in the post-test. A two-proportion z-test confirmed this increase to be statistically significant (z= 5.09, p < .001)"
