## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 117 first-year high-school students with no prior differentiation training, the average in-game learning gain was 0.65, and all performance percentiles showed a large gain. [→ Pedersen 2016](#pedersen-2016)

## Evidence

### Pedersen 2016

Pedersen, M. K., Svenningsen, A., Dohn, N. B., Lieberoth, A., & Sherson, J. (2016). DiffGame: Game-based mathematics learning for physics. [doi:10.1016/j.sbspro.2016.07.047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbspro.2016.07.047)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Analysis of time-stamped in-game responses from the second test with 117 Grade 10 students, comparing each student's first three and last three answers per category to compute gain; the reported average was "large  (0.65 with a 0.12 standard -error-of-mean". No control group was used.

> "The average gain for all 117 students was large  (0.65 with a 0.12 standard -error-of-mean."
