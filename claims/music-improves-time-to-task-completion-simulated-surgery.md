# Music exposure shows mixed but partly beneficial effects on time to task completion in simulated surgical tasks

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two of the studies assessing absolute time to task completion reported statistically beneficial effects of preferred music, hiphop and Jamaican music on time to task completion, while one study reported no significant difference between groups. [→ Oomens P 2019](#oomens-p-2019)

## Evidence

### Oomens P 2019

Oomens P, Fu VX, Kleinrensink GJ, Jeekel J. (2019). The effect of music on simulated surgical performance: a systematic review. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-019-06868-x

`q3 · i?`

Systematic review qualitative synthesis of eight studies assessing time to task completion; the review reports that two studies found statistically beneficial effects of "preferred music, hiphop and Jamaican music", while one study reported no significant difference. No effect sizes were pooled.

> "Two studies reported a statistically beneficial effects of both preferred music, hiphop and Jamaican music, on time to task completion [22, 26]. One study did not present exact values, but reported no significant difference between groups [23]."
