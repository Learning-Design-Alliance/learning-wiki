Evidence¶

Richman 2014¶

Richman, E. L., Rademacher, K. N., & Maitland, T. L. (2014). Coaching and college success. Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability, 27(1), 33-52. https://www.ahead.org/publications/jped

q2 · i? · qualitative · r3

Thematic analysis of the six qualitative interviews addressing research question 4 on benefits and limitations. The article reports students described gains in critical thinking, self-reflection, flexible thinking, action on goals, and managing negative emotions; no effect size is printed.

"Students’ comments about coaching’s beneﬁ ts can be categorized into three broad themes: improved cog- nitions, behaviors, and subjective well-being."

Richman 2014 (2)¶

Richman, E. L., Rademacher, K. N., & Maitland, T. L. (2014). Coaching and college success. Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability, 27(1), 33-52. https://www.ahead.org/publications/jped

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Same interview analysis; the article reports students found the half-hour sessions limiting, with one suggesting an hour might be more effective, and some wanting an accountability plan and support for life after college. No effect size is printed.

"Many of their comments focused on logistical restrictions, such as building location and length of sessions. Two students wanted even greater accountability than coaching provided."
