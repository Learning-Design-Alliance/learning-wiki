# Multiple-choice retrieval practice produced a negative suggestion effect (increased lure production on the final test) in Experiment 3, where restudy replaced direct feedback, but not in Experiments 1 and 2, which provided direct feedback

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 3, subjects who answered initial multiple-choice questions produced more lures on the final assessment than those who answered short-answer questions. [→ Smith 2014](#smith-2014)

## Evidence

### Smith 2014

Megan A. Smith and Jeffrey D. Karpicke. (2014). Retrieval practice with short-answer, multiple-choice, and hybrid tests. Memory, 22(7), 784-802. https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2013.831454

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3 lure analysis: multiple-choice subjects produced lures 22% of the time on incorrect final-test responses vs 8% for short-answer subjects, F(1, 46) = 9.28, η²p = .17 — a negative suggestion effect. Multiple-choice subjects produced lures 22% of the time versus 8% for short-answer subjects.

> "Subjects who answered initial multiple-choice questions produced more incorrect information previously presented to them relative to those who answered short-answer questions, F(1, 46) 9.28; g2p ¼ :17."
