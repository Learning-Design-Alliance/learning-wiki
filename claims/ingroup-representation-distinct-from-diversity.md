# Ingroup representation and racial/ethnic diversity are distinct constructs that are often conflated in school diversity research

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Increasing the proportion of same-racial/ethnic peers does not necessarily increase diversity and may decrease it, so labeling ingroup representation as diversity risks inaccurate interpretations. [→ Chan 2023](#chan-2023)

## Evidence

### Chan 2023

Chan, M.-k., & Benner, A. D. (2023). A Consideration of Racial/Ethnic Diversity Conceptualization and Measurement: Clarifying Ambiguities and Advancing Scholarship. Educational Psychologist (accepted manuscript). https://orcid.org/0000-0001-6451-2417

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Conceptual analysis of the review's findings: ingroup representation (percentage of same-racial/ethnic peers) does not portray the dispersion of racial/ethnic groups. The review argues it "operationalizes an important construct but one that is distinct from racial/ethnic diversity", citing Cheon et al. (2020) labeling same-ethnic peers as peer diversity.

> "Increasing the proportion of same-racial/ethnic peers, however, does not necessarily increase diversity and may actually decrease diversity in some cases."
