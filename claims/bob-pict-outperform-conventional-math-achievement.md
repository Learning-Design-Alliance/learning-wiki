## Evidence

### Nandwa et al. 2015

Odhiambo marble Nandwa, Dr. Duncan Wekesa Wasike, Professor Toili William Wanjala. (2015). Influence of Instructional Practices on Secondary School Students’ Achievement in Mathematics. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.6, No.26. https://www.iiste.org/Journals/index.php/JEP/article/view/31336

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pretest-posttest non-equivalent group study of 327 form three students in eight Kenyan secondary schools. Post hoc analysis confirmed each experimental group outperformed the control group, with the trend "BO-PICT (E3) > PICT (E2) BOB > C (TI)" yielded for the MAT measure.

> "The Scheffe post hoc analysis procedure that was ca rried out supported the results of the t-test. BOB (E1), PICT (E2) and BO-PICT (E3) groups are signifi cantly different from control (C) group in favor of the experimental groups."

### Nandwa et al. 2015 (2)

Odhiambo marble Nandwa, Dr. Duncan Wekesa Wasike, Professor Toili William Wanjala. (2015). Influence of Instructional Practices on Secondary School Students’ Achievement in Mathematics. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.6, No.26. https://www.iiste.org/Journals/index.php/JEP/article/view/31336

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Omnibus ANOVA on post-test MAT scores across the four groups (N=327) yielded F = 61.77, exceeding the critical value of 3.84, indicating "a difference in performance between at least any two groups".

> "From table 5, it is clear that the F-ratio (61.77) was greater than the table value (3.84) showing tha t there was a difference in performance between at least any two groups."
