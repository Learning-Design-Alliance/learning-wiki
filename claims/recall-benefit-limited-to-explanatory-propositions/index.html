Evidence¶

Schmidt 1989¶

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.81.4.610

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Follow-up tests within the Experiment 2 three-way ANOVA showed the experimental effect on recall "was entirely due to the processing and retrieval of explanative information"; interactions of treatment by type, F(1, 82) = 7.77, p < .01, and expertise by type, F(1, 82) = 20.47, p < .001, were significant, the three-way interaction was not, F(1, 82) = 1.32, p < .25.

"For explanations, the difference between treat- ments was significant, F(1, 82) = 4.73, MSe = 254.93,/9 < .05; for descriptions, however, the difference was not signifi- cant, F(1, 82) = .18, MS~ = 54.40,/9 < .67."

Schmidt 1989 (2)¶

Schmidt, H. G., De Volder, M. L., De Grave, W. S., Moust, J. H. C., & Patel, V. L. (1989). Explanatory Models in the Processing of Science Text: The Role of Prior Knowledge Activation Through Small-Group Discussion. Journal of Educational Psychology, 81(4), 610-619. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.81.4.610

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 2 three-way ANOVA (treatment and expertise between subjects, proposition type within) showed significant treatment × type, F(1, 82) = 7.77, p < .01, and expertise × type, F(1, 82) = 20.47, p < .001, interactions; the effect was stronger for experts.

"For explanations, the difference between treat- ments was significant, F(1, 82) = 4.73, MSe = 254.93,/9 < .05; for descriptions, however, the difference was not signifi- cant, F(1, 82) = .18, MS~ = 54.40,/9 < .67."
