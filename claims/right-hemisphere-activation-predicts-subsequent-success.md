# Right-hemisphere activation during correct retrieval consistently predicts subsequent success, whether on the first or second correct recall

## Evidence

### Liu 2016

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

ROI subsequent memory analysis of correct second-test trials in the STT-T condition, back-sorted on Day 2 recall (19 analyzed subjects). "For the second test, significant subsequent memory effects were observed in right HPC (t 18 = 2.92, P = 0.009, d = 0.66), right PFC (t 18 = 2.33, P = 0.032, d = 0.54), and right PPC".

> "For the second test, significant subsequent memory effects were observed in right HPC (t 18 = 2.92, P = 0.009, d = 0.66), right PFC (t 18 = 2.33, P = 0.032, d = 0.54), and right PPC (t 18 = 2.36, P = 0.03, d = 0.54)."

### Liu 2016 (2)

Liu, X. L., & Reder, L. M. (2016). fMRI exploration of pedagogical benefits of repeated testing: when more is not always better. Brain and Behavior. https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.476

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

ROI subsequent memory analysis of first-test trials in the SST-T condition, back-sorted on Day 2 recall. "For the first test, significant or marginally significant subsequent memory effects were observed in all six ROIs", with the largest effect in left PFC (d = 1.08).

> "For the first test, significant or marginally significant subsequent memory effects were observed in all six ROIs (left PFC, t 18 = 4.69, P < 0.001, d = 1.08; right PFC, t 18 = 2.64, P = 0.017, d = 0.61; left PPC, t 18 = 2.85, P = 0.011, d = 0.67; right PPC, t 18 = 3.12, P = 0.006, d = 0.72; left HPC, t 18 = 1.87, P = 0.078, d = 0.43; right HPC, t 18 = 2.17, P = 0.044, d = 0.50)."
