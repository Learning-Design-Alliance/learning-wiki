# Effects of Boston public preschool on college enrollment, SAT-taking, high school graduation, and discipline are generally larger for boys than for girls; differences by race and income are generally statistically insignificant.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r3` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For boys, preschool produces significant increases in SAT-taking (16 percentage points), high school graduation (16 percentage points), and the discipline index (0.33σ), with small and insignificant corresponding estimates for girls, and statistically significant differences by sex (p < 0.07). [→ Gray-Lobe 2021](#gray-lobe-2021)
`q3 i?` Differences in estimates by race and income are generally statistically insignificant, with none of the racial differences significant at conventional levels (p > 0.11). [→ Gray-Lobe 2021 (2)](#gray-lobe-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Gray-Lobe 2021

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS subgroup estimates from Table 8 splitting the randomized sample by sex, where "for boys we find significant increases in SAT-taking (16 percentage points), high school graduation (16 percentage points), and the discipline index (0.33σ)". Gender differences in ever enrolling in a four-year college and ever graduating from college are significant at p ≈ 0.05.

> "for boys we find significant increases in SAT-taking (16 percentage points), high school graduation (16 percentage points), and the discipline index (0.33σ), with small and insignificant corresponding estimates for girls, and statistically significant differences by sex for each of these outcomes (p < 0.07)."

### Gray-Lobe 2021 (2)

Gray-Lobe, G., Pathak, P. A., & Walters, C. R. (2021). The Long-Term Effects of Universal Preschool in Boston. NBER Working Paper No. 28756. http://www.nber.org/papers/w28756

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

2SLS subgroup estimates from Table 8 splitting the randomized sample by race (Black, Hispanic, white) and by free or reduced price lunch eligibility, finding "Differences in estimates by race and income are generally statistically insignificant". Marginally significant differences for four-year college enrollment and MCAS scores (p = 0.08) may be chance findings given the many splits examined.
