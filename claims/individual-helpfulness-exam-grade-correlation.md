## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Using individual helpfulness ratings, significant Pearson correlations between helpful-method study time and exam grades appeared in 13 of the 21 exams. [→ Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters 2020](#rahul-jilakara-and-david-p-waters-2020)
`q2 i?` The direction of the correlation is positive for individually helpful methods and negative for individually unhelpful methods in the Exam 1 example. [→ Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters 2020 (2)](#rahul-jilakara-and-david-p-waters-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters 2020

Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters. (2020). Determination of the helpfulness of physics exam study methods. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.10693](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.10693)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlations were computed with the rcorr function in R (Pearson method) between helpfulness-grouped study time and exam grades; only correlations with p < 0.05 were reported, with stars for p < 0.01 and p < 0.001.

> "For the individual helpfulness rating, signiﬁcant correlations showed up in 13 of the 21 exams."

### Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters 2020 (2)

Rahul Jilakara and David P. Waters. (2020). Determination of the helpfulness of physics exam study methods. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2012.10693](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2012.10693)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlation table example in Figure 4 from the first exam of fall 2019: significant positive correlations for individual helpfulness across overall exam grades and grades from problems, and significant negative correlations for individually unhelpful methods.

> "when a student spent a greater percentage of their time on study methods that they labeled as more helpful than the OHR, the student’s grades were higher."
