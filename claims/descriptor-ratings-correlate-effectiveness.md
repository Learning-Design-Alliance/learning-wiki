# Final student ratings of all instructor descriptors except strict correlate significantly with perceived teaching effectiveness, with strong positive associations for confident, engaging, enthusiastic, organized, relate, relaxed, respect, and understanding

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Final ratings of teaching effectiveness correlated significantly with all 13 descriptor ratings; associations were strong and positive for confident (r = 0.568), engaging (r = 0.696), enthusiasm (r = 0.588), organized (r = 0.667), relate (r = 0.653), relaxed (r = 0.612), respect (r = 0.686), and understanding (r = 0.619), and negative for boring (r = −0.522), distant (r = −0.476), nervous (r = −0.342), uncertain (r = −0.472), and weakly for strict (r = −0.120). ## Evidence

### Kendall 2013

Kendall, K. D., & Schussler, E. E. (2013). Evolving Impressions: Undergraduate Perceptions of Graduate Teaching Assistants and Faculty Members over a Semester. CBE—Life Sciences Education 12(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-07-0110

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Spearman rank correlations on pooled final-survey data (GTAs and faculty together) found effectiveness ratings correlated significantly with all descriptors (all p = 0.000 except strict, p = 0.005), including "strong positive associations between final survey ratings of instructor effectiveness and ratings of confident (r = 0.568), engaging (r = 0.696)."

> "There were strong positive associations between final survey ratings of instructor effectiveness and ratings of confident (r = 0.568), engaging (r = 0.696), enthusiasm (r = 0.588), organized (r = 0.667), relate (r = 0.653), relaxed (r = 0.612), respect (r = 0.686), and understanding (r = 0.619)."
