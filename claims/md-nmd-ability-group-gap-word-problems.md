## Evidence

### Swanson 2013

Swanson, H. L., Lussier, C. M., & Orosco, M. J. (2013). Cognitive Strategies, Working Memory, and Growth in Word Problem Solving in Children With Math Difficulties. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 48(4), 339–358. [doi:10.1177/0022219413498771](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219413498771)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Intent-to-treat mixed ANOVA across four testing waves; "children without MD outperformed children with MD" (M = 1.06 vs. M = 0.04 as printed in the article).

> "A significant effect was found for ability group F(1, 447) = 114.73, p < .001, testing wave, F(3, 447) = 273.42, and WMC, F(1, 447) = 24.63, p < .001. As expected children without MD outperformed children with MD"

### Swanson 2013 (2)

Swanson, H. L., Lussier, C. M., & Orosco, M. J. (2013). Cognitive Strategies, Working Memory, and Growth in Word Problem Solving in Children With Math Difficulties. Journal of Learning Disabilities, 48(4), 339–358. [doi:10.1177/0022219413498771](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219413498771)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Year 1 Wave 2 analysis; the article reports "accuracy scores were statistically comparable between children with and without MD at Wave 2 on isolated treatment conditions."

> "Although children without MD clearly out performed children with MD at Wave 1 across all treatment conditions, accuracy scores were statistically comparable between children with and without MD at Wave 2 on isolated treatment conditions."
