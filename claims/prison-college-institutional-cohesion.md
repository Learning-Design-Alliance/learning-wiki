# Prison college participation was reported to increase institutional cohesion, including among non-participating incarcerated women

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reported that higher education changed interactions across the facility, benefiting even women not enrolled in college. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018](#jillian-baranger-2018)
`q2 i?` Students formed study groups, tutored peers, and lent course texts to non-enrolled women, spreading program effects through the institution. [→ Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)](#jillian-baranger-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Jillian Baranger 2018

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?`

Student interview in the pilot qualitative study on social relationships pre-release. Elizabeth described how participation changed the "whole vibe between everybody", including people not in college.

> "“…it changes the whole vibe between everybody. So it was like something that benefited me, as well as people around me too who weren’t in college, because it was something they could actually put their mind to.”"

### Jillian Baranger 2018 (2)

Jillian Baranger, Danielle Rousseau, Mary Ellen Mastrorilli, James Matesanz. (2018). Doing Time Wisely: The Social and Personal Benefits of Higher Education in Prison. The Prison Journal. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032885518776380

`q2 · i?`

Interview data on institutional cohesion: non-enrolled women borrowed Elizabeth's course books as reading material, indicating effects of the program beyond immediate participants.

> "She expressed that many students came to her and asked to borrow her school books just for reading material. She said they would return her books and be “amazed” at the content."
