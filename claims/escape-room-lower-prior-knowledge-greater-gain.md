## Evidence

### Baessler F 2025

Baessler F, Hornung TP, Schuster TJ, Hardt N, Felter E, Anzenberger E, Ely DR, Lichtenstein T, Steiner K, Koelkebeck K, Flasbeck V, Spitzer P, Speerforck S, Pinilla S, Khatamzas E, Mueller B, Zafar A. `q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

In the correlational analysis of the 45-student pre-post study, the printed result was a "significant negative correlation between the pre-test score and knowledge gain" with r = -.536, p < .001, reported as an observational association, not a manipulated contrast.

> "A two-sided Pearson correlation showed a significant negative correlation between the pre-test score and knowledge gain (r=-.536, p <.001, 95% CI [−0.717, − 0.288]), indicating that higher pre-test scores were associated with lower knowledge improvement."
