# End-of-secondary learners recognize field geometry far more readily from centered unit vector representations than from conventional unit vector representations

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Christoph Hoyer 2024

Christoph Hoyer, Raimund Girwidz. (2024). Vector representations and unit vector representations of fields — problems of understanding and possible teaching strategies. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010150](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010150)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest of the study's 124 11th-grade participants on the VR and CV item scales. Means as printed: 3.15/10 (VR) versus 8.23/10 (CV), showing "an average score of 3.15 out of 10 possible points" for conventional vectors.

> "The participants achieved an average score of 3.15 out of 10 possible points when answering the items regarding conventional unit vectors (VR). In contrast, in the tasks with centered unit vectors (CV), they scored an average of 8.23 out of 10 points."

### Christoph Hoyer 2024 (2)

Christoph Hoyer, Raimund Girwidz. (2024). Vector representations and unit vector representations of fields — problems of understanding and possible teaching strategies. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010150](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010150)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Wilcoxon signed-rank test on pretest VR versus CV median scores, reporting "Z = 9.138, p < 0.01, r = 0.82, R2 ≈ 0.67", which the article states corresponds to a large effect size according to Cohen.

> "The Wilcoxon signed-rank test results reveal significant differences in the median scores achieved in the two task categories (Z = 9.138, p < 0.01, r = 0.82, R2 ≈ 0.67)."
