# Average marginal effects show older age and suspension reduce GPA while high school grades and achievement test scores raise it

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Age has a negative average marginal effect of -0.08 GPA points per year (p < 0.001), while high school grade shows +0.04 GPA points per grade unit (p < 0.001). [→ Almalki Sultan A. 2024](#almalki-sultan-a-2024)
`q2 i?` Suspended students show an average GPA reduction of -0.45 points (p < 0.001); achievement test score adds +0.01 per unit (p = 0.022); several other variables are not statistically significant. [→ Almalki Sultan A. 2024 (2)](#almalki-sultan-a-2024-2)

## Evidence

### Almalki Sultan A. 2024

Almalki Sultan A., AlJameel AlBandary H., Alghomlas Ziyad, Alothman Tameem, Alhajri Fawaz. (2024). Assessing the predictive validity of pre-admission criteria on dental students’ academic performance: a cross-sectional study. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-023-03839-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Average marginal effects analysis of pre-admission variables on GPA (Table 7), from the cross-sectional questionnaire study. The article prints the marginal effects with standard errors and 95% confidence intervals; no standardized effect size is reported.

> "Age has a negative average marginal effect, indicating that older students tend to have lower GPAs, with an average decrease of -0.08 GPA points per year (p < 0.001). Conversely, High School Grade positively correlates with student performance, showing an increase of + 0.04 GPA points per grade unit (p < 0.001)."

### Almalki Sultan A. 2024 (2)

Almalki Sultan A., AlJameel AlBandary H., Alghomlas Ziyad, Alothman Tameem, Alhajri Fawaz. (2024). Assessing the predictive validity of pre-admission criteria on dental students’ academic performance: a cross-sectional study. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-023-03839-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Average marginal effects analysis (Table 7). The article also prints an achievement test score effect of +0.01 (p = 0.022) and reports that aptitude test score, parental education, living conditions, gender, marital status, and study year did not show statistically significant effects.

> "Students who have been suspended exhibit a significant decrease in GPA, with an average reduction of -0.45 GPA points (p < 0.001)."
