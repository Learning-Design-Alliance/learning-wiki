# Formal early childhood experience is associated with reduced likelihood of being identified as significantly below benchmark, most pronounced for English learner students, particularly Black English learner students

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who attended formal EC programs were less likely to be identified as significantly below benchmark multiple times, with effects most pronounced for English learner students and for Black English learner students in particular. [→ Lemke 2024](#lemke-2024)

## Evidence

### Lemke 2024

Lemke, M., Murphy, D., Soo Ping Chow, A., & Acuña, A. (2024). Intersections: Student background and early literacy performance. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED663858

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Figure 2 of the 2022/23 analysis (females shown; males show the same patterns) shows formal EC experience reduces risk across ethnoracial groups; low income students showed about a 1 or 2 percentage point decrease. Formal EC experience was a statistically significant predictor for all grades K–3.

> "The likelihood of Black English learner students being identified as significantly at risk is reduced by about 20 percentage points when they have attended formal EC programs (24 percent, compared to 44 percent, for females; 21 percent, compared to 40 percent, for males)."
