# Classroom placement was highly stable across K–3, with no children moving between autism-labeled and inclusive settings, and initial placement was driven by district policy rather than child characteristics

## Evidence

### Strain 2017

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Follow-up placement tracking across kindergarten through third grade found absolute consistency in individual children's placements over time; no child moved from an autism-labeled setting to an inclusive one or vice versa.

> "nor did we see any examples of children being placed in a more restrictive setting once they were origi- nally in an inclusive kindergarten program"

### Strain 2017 (2)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

The author describes a disturbing pattern of child-independent placement decisions; some districts placed by label, others by preschool progress, and parent advocacy occasionally altered policy.

> "Simply put, where children were placed was driven by a district-level decision conditioned on opinions about “other” individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and/or historical information."

### Strain 2017 (3)

Phillip S. Strain. (2017). Four-Year Follow-Up of Children in the LEAP Randomized Trial: Some Planned and Accidental Findings. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education, 37(2), 121–126. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417711531

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Table 1 shows children in the top preschool-outcome quartile were least represented in inclusive settings (18% inclusive vs. 21% autism classes), contrary to expectations.

> "Overall, the distribution of children in Table 1 demonstrates no correspondence between relative growth on study outcome measures in preschool and kindergarten placements."
