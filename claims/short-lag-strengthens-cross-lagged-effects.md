## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Studies with lags of 7 weeks or less showed stronger cross-lagged relationships than studies with 3–12 month lags; the difference was statistically significant for the performance → self-efficacy path. [→ Talsma 2018](#talsma-2018)

## Evidence

### Talsma 2018

Talsma, K., Schüz, B., Schwarzer, R., & Norris, K. (2018). I believe, therefore I achieve (and vice versa): A meta-analytic cross-lagged panel analysis of self-efficacy and academic performance. Learning and Individual Differences, 61, 136-150. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.015

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Moderator analysis on the k=11 pooled studies, comparing subgroup path models for short (≤7 weeks) versus long (3–12 months) lags. The article reports the difference was "statistically significant for the βP1SE2 path"; for long lags the constrained-equal-paths model was not significantly worse (p=.07).

> "Studies with shorter lags showed stronger reciprocal effects than those with longer delays between measurement waves; this difference was statistically significant for the βP1SE2 path."
