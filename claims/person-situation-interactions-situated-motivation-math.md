# Person × Situation interactions: prior achievement amplifies performance effects on motivation, gender moderates the performance–self-concept link, and difficulty effects depend on prior math exposure

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` High-school math achievement boosted the positive effect of situation-specific performance on self-concept and buffered against costs; at very low prior achievement the link to self-concept was not significant (b = 0.05, p = .134). [→ Benden 2026](#benden-2026)
`q2 i?` The performance–self-concept association was stronger for male (b = 0.38) than female (b = 0.32) students. [→ Benden 2026 (2)](#benden-2026-2)
`q2 i?` For regular-course students, test difficulty significantly predicted self-concept (b = 0.39), but for advanced-course students it did not. [→ Benden 2026 (3)](#benden-2026-3)
`q2 i?` With increasing prior achievement, intrinsic interest became less dependent on test difficulty; difficulty effects were nonsignificant at above-average achievement. [→ Benden 2026 (4)](#benden-2026-4)

## Evidence

### Benden 2026

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-level interaction (Model 4) between situation-specific performance and high-school math grade (0.04 ≤ |b| ≤ 0.05, ps ≤ .006); simple slopes decreased from b = 0.23 (+2 SD) to a nonsignificant b = 0.05 (−2 SD) for self-concept.

> "There was a synergistic effect of prior math achievement and test-specific performance: Students' high-school math achievement boosted the positive effect of test performance on their test-specific self-concept and buffered against experiencing high costs during the test"

### Benden 2026 (2)

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Significant cross-level interaction between situation-specific performance and gender (b = 0.05, p = .034) in Model 4; both simple slopes were significant, with male students receiving a greater self-concept boost from the same performance level.

> "The association between students' situation-specific test performance and their self-concept was stronger for male compared to female students (bmale = 0.38, p < .001; bfemale = 0.32, p < .001, see Figure 4)"

### Benden 2026 (3)

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Interaction between content-level difficulty and advanced versus regular high-school math course (−0.15 ≤ b ≤ −0.13, ps < .020) in Model 4; for advanced-course students difficulty was not significantly related to self-concept, intrinsic, or utility value (ps ≥ .052).

> "for students who had attended regular math courses, the test difficulty was a significant predictor of their test-specific math self-concept (b = 0.39, p = .008; see Figure 5)"

### Benden 2026 (4)

Benden, D. K., & Lauermann, F. (2026). The Interplay of Personal and Situational Factors in Situated Expectancy-Value Theory: Cross-Classified Analyses of Students' Situation-Specific Motivations in the Math Domain. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0001032

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Negative interaction between content difficulty and high-school math achievement predicting intrinsic value (b = −0.08, p = .004); difficulty–interest slopes were significant at −2, −1, and average SD but not at +1 SD (b = 0.00, p = .922) or +2 SD (b = −0.09, p = .156).

> "With increasing prior math achievement, students' intrinsic interest in the tested content was less dependent on test difficulty."
