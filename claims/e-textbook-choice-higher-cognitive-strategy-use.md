## Evidence

### Amanda J. Rockinson-Szapkiw 2013

Amanda J. Rockinson-Szapkiw, Jillian Wendt, and Rebecca Lunde. (2013). Electronic Versus Print Textbooks: The Influence of Textbook Format on University Students' Self-Regulated Learning Strategies, Motivation, and Text Anxiety. American Journal of Distance Education, 27(3), 179-188. https://doi.org/10.1080/08923647.2013.796230

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r1`

Causal-comparative study of 538 undergraduates, masters, and doctoral students who self-selected textbook format; MSLQ subscales compared by format via MANOVA follow-up. E-textbook users scored higher on cognitive strategy use (M = 73.12 vs 66.25), with the printed effect size eta squared = .07 for F(1, 536) = 37.05.

> "These results indicated that the students who chose and used e-textbooks were more likely to use cognitive strategies and self-regulation strategies than students who chose and used traditional print textbooks for their courses, F(1, 536) = 37.05, p > .001, eta squared = .07"
