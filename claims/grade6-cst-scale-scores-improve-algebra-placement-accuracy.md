# Using grade 6 math CST scale scores rather than proficiency status increases the accuracy of grade 8 algebra I placement decisions from 69 to 75 percent

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Predicting grade 8 algebra I proficiency from continuous grade 6 math CST scale scores with a logistic regression increased placement accuracy by 6 percentage points over decisions based on proficiency status alone. [→ Chun-Wei Huang 2016](#chun-wei-huang-2016)

## Evidence

### Chun-Wei Huang 2016

Chun-Wei Huang, Jason Snipes, Neal Finkelstein, WestEd. (2016). Using assessment data to guide math course placement of California middle school students. SREE Spring 2016 Conference. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED567123

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression analysis of 2,579 grade 8 algebra I students in SVRA districts (2011/12) predicted algebra I CST proficiency from grade 6 math CST scale scores; the study reports this approach "increases the accuracy of algebra I placement decisions in grade 8 from 69 percent to 75 percent."

> "Using students’ grade 6 math CST scale score rather than proficiency status increases the accuracy of algebra I placement decisions in grade 8 from 69 percent to 75 percent."
