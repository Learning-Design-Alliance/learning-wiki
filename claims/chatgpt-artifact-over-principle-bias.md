# ChatGPT biased information access toward solution-oriented artifacts over principle-oriented information

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` ChatGPT participants extracted principle-oriented information in 66.33% of entries versus 97.14% for Google, and extracted artifacts (meal plans, grocery lists) in 46.94% versus 6.43%. [→ Shravika Mittal 2026](#shravika-mittal-2026)
`q2 i?` Even principle-oriented queries to ChatGPT returned sample meal plans and grocery lists, leading one participant to plan to rely on ChatGPT for future meal planning. [→ Shravika Mittal 2026 (2)](#shravika-mittal-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Shravika Mittal 2026

Shravika Mittal, Su Lin Blodgett, Q. Vera Liao. (2026). Learning by Chatting? Investigating the Impact of Generative AI on Information Seeking and Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11669

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of diary entries coded for extracting principle-oriented information. Artifact extraction was observed in 46.94% (nC=46/98) of ChatGPT entries versus 6.43% (nG=9/140) of Google entries, per the same Table 1 analysis.

> "66.33%(n C=65/98) entries from ChatGPT participants described this behavior, compared to97.14%(n G=136/140) of entries from participants in the Google group."

### Shravika Mittal 2026 (2)

Shravika Mittal, Su Lin Blodgett, Q. Vera Liao. (2026). Learning by Chatting? Investigating the Impact of Generative AI on Information Seeking and Learning. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11669

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Diary excerpt from ChatGPT participant P11 illustrating that even a principle-oriented query yielded a meal plan and grocery list; the same participant stated plans to keep using ChatGPT to create meal plans after the study.

> ""I asked ChatGPT how much of each macronutrient one should have for a balanced meal. It gave me a balanced meal plan. [...] It asked if I'd want a grocery list, which it provided.""
