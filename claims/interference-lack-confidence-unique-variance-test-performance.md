# Interference and lack of confidence, but not worry or arousal, explain unique variance in university students' standardized test performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In multiple regressions, interference and lack of confidence explained unique variance in performance across intelligence, arithmetic fact retrieval, arithmetic procedures, and higher-order mathematics tests, while worry and arousal did not. [→ Schillinger 2021](#schillinger-2021)

## Evidence

### Schillinger 2021

Schillinger, F. L., Mosbacher, J. A., Brunner, C., Vogel, S. E., & Grabner, R. H. (2021). Revisiting the Role of Worries in Explaining the Link Between Test Anxiety and Test Performance. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09601-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multiple linear regression study of 730 university students who completed the PAF and a battery of standardized tests (BIS-T, AF-T, M-PA). The article reports "worry did not remain significantly related to any performance measure" while interference and lack of confidence explained unique variance; test anxiety accounted for 2 to 5% of variance (e.g., R2adj = .046 for facts).

> "Surprisingly, worry did not remain significantly related to any performance measure. Interference and lack of confidence, instead, explained unique variance in performance across tests. Taken together, test anxiety accounted for 2 to 5% of the variance in students' test performance."
