Evidence¶

Jan A. A. Engelen 2018¶

Jan A. A. Engelen, Gino Camp, Janneke van de Pol, Anique B. H. de Bruin. (2018). Teachers' monitoring of students' text comprehension: can students' keywords and summaries improve teachers' judgment accuracy? Metacognition and Learning 13:287–307. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-018-9187-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Experiment 1: 255 sixth-graders read six texts, gave delayed JOLs after no cue prompt, delayed keywords, or summaries, then took multiple-choice tests. Student monitoring accuracy was "better than chance" (grand mean .103) but cue prompt showed no significant effect, F(2, 213) = .685, p = .505.

"The grand mean of the model was .103, 95% CI [.020 .186], suggesting that students' monitoring accuracy was better than chance. The effect of cue prompt was not significant, F(2, 213) = .685, p = .505, suggesting that monitoring accuracy did not differ across conditions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The quality of sixth-graders' keywords is not diagnostic of intra-individual variation in test performance (Experiment 1).
  • Teachers' intra-individual monitoring accuracy of students' text-by-text learning is not better than chance and is not improved by students' keywords or summaries (Experiment 1).
  • Test performance differs by cue-prompt condition in Experiment 1, with higher scores in the no-cue condition than the summary condition.
  • Teachers' inter-individual judgment accuracy (predicting differences between students) is well above chance (.528) even though their intra-individual accuracy is not (Experiment 1). — related
  • Teachers show stronger regulation than students: both select lower-JOL texts for restudy, but the JOL–restudy correlation is significantly stronger for teachers (Experiment 1). — related
  • Generative activities improve students' monitoring accuracy but not their actual learning after restudy — related
