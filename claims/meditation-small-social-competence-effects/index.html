Evidence¶

Lea Waters 2015¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i3 · review · r2

The review reports this non-random experiment trial of a 10-lesson mindfulness education intervention (intervention=139, control=107) with teacher-rated outcomes and a printed effect size of "social –emotional competence ( d=1.27)". The review attributes the finding to Schonert-Reichl and Lawlor (2010).

"With respect to increased pro-social behaviour, Schonert-Reichl and Lawlor ( 2010)o b - served significant, strong improvements in a range of teacher-reported social –emotional competence ( d=1.27) from pre- to post-test in the ME group compared to controls."

Lea Waters 2015 (2)¶

Lea Waters, Adam Barsky, Amanda Ridd, Kelly Allen. (2015). Contemplative Education: A Systematic, Evidence-Based Review of the effect of Meditation Interventions in Schools. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9258-2

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's percentage-of-significant-effects comparison for facilitator type on social competence outcomes, the only programme characteristic with enough effects to compare. No effect size is printed for this comparison, so no magnitude is claimed.

"A higher percentage of interventions were found where the teacher was the facilitator (75 %; n=4) compared to other instructors (20 %; n=5)."
