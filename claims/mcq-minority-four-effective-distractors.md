## Evidence

### Fozzard Nicolette 2018

Fozzard Nicolette, Pearson Andrew, du Toit Eugene, Naug Helen, Wen William, Peak Ian R. (2018). Analysis of MCQ and distractor use in a large first year Health Faculty Foundation Program: assessing the effects of changing from five to four options. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-018-1346-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Distractor analysis of end-of-semester exam responses at the first offering across four courses, classing distractors effective if more than 5% of students chose them. The study found "19% of the five-option questions (n = 195 across all four courses) had four effective distractors" and 32% had three; 7% had none.

> "In our study, 19% of the five-option questions (n = 195 across all four courses) had four effective distractors. The most frequent number of effective distractors per question was three, with 32% of the 195 questions."
