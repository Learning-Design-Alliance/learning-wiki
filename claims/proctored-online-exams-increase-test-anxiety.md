# Students taking online and proctored online exams report higher test anxiety than students taking on-campus exams and non-proctored online exams

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in online exams showed higher test anxiety than students in on-campus exams or assignments (β = 0.82, SE = 0.15, p < 0.001). [→ Conijn 2022](#conijn-2022)

## Evidence

### Conijn 2022

Conijn, R., Kleingeld, A., Matzat, U., & Snijders, C. (2022). The fear of big brother: The potential negative side-effects of proctored exams. Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 38(6), 1521-1534. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12651

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-level regression on test anxiety (38% of variance at student level, 14% at course level) from the four-wave survey of 1760 students in 105 courses. Online exams showed "higher test anxiety, compared to students in on-campus exams or assignments ( β = 0.82, SE = 0.15, p < 0.001)".

> "students in (non-proctored and proctored) online exams showed higher test anxiety, compared to students in on-campus exams or assignments ( β = 0.82, SE = 0.15, p < 0.001)."

### Conijn 2022 (2)

Conijn, R., Kleingeld, A., Matzat, U., & Snijders, C. (2022). The fear of big brother: The potential negative side-effects of proctored exams. Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 38(6), 1521-1534. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12651

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Separate multi-level model on the same survey data comparing exam types. The study reports "Proctored online exams showed higher test anxiety compared to non-proctored online exams ( β = 0.60, SE = 0.17, p < 0.001)", with proctoring becoming insignificant (p = 0.06) when online status was in the model.

> "Proctored online exams showed higher test anxiety compared to non-proctored online exams ( β = 0.60, SE = 0.17, p < 0.001)."
