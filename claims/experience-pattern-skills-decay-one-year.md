## Evidence

### Kalabamu FS 2026

Kalabamu FS, Daudi V, Moshiro R, Kamala B, Mdoe P, Bishanga D, Kvaløy JT, Ersdal H, Mpembeni R. (2026). Neonatal resuscitation skills retention among healthcare providers one year after implementation of in situ low-dose high-frequency simulations using innovative tools across two regions in Tanzania. Global Health Action. https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2026.2639774

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Paired comparisons of baseline and one-year scores across participant characteristics (Table 2); the article reports significant decay in most categories except work experience under one year (p = 0.35), with borderline decay for age groups (p = 0.16 and 0.06).

> "There was significant skills decay among various participant categories, except for those with work experience of less than a year (p = 0.35) and borderline skills decay among those aged under 30 years and between 31 and 40 years (p = 0.16 and 0.06), respectively."

### Kalabamu FS 2026 (2)

Kalabamu FS, Daudi V, Moshiro R, Kamala B, Mdoe P, Bishanga D, Kvaløy JT, Ersdal H, Mpembeni R. (2026). Neonatal resuscitation skills retention among healthcare providers one year after implementation of in situ low-dose high-frequency simulations using innovative tools across two regions in Tanzania. Global Health Action. https://doi.org/10.1080/16549716.2026.2639774

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Univariable mixed-effects analysis of one-year skills scores; the more-than-5-years experience group scored 2.7 points lower than the 1–5 years group (p = 0.05). This effect was not among the final multivariable findings.

> "Moreover, experience working in a labour ward had a significant effect on skills; those in the more than 5 years group scored 2.7 points lower than the 1–5 years group (p = 0.05). The overall effect is that the more experience, the lower the skills scores."
