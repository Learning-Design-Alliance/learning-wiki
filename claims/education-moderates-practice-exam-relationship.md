# Education level moderates the practice–exam score relationship: the interactive model fits better, with a descriptive trend toward steeper practice slopes at lower education levels

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A likelihood ratio test favored the interactive practice × education model over the additive model, and group-level slopes descriptively trended larger at lower education levels, though no individual doing × education interaction was statistically significant. [→ Darren Butler 2025](#darren-butler-2025)

## Evidence

### Darren Butler 2025

Darren Butler, Conrad Borchers, Michael W. Asher, Yongmin Lee, Sonya Karnataki, Sameeksha Dangi, Samyukta Athreya, John Stamper, Amy Ogan, and Paulo F. Carvalho. 2025. Does the Doer Effect Exist Beyond WEIRD Populations? Toward Analytics in Radio and Phone-Based Learning. In LAK25: The 15th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK 2025). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706505

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Likelihood ratio comparison of additive versus interactive regression models on the N = 234 learner dataset; only the F statistic is printed, so no effect size is reported. The authors report "no significant interactions between doing and education levels (𝑝′𝑠 >.05)", attributing this to low within-group power (average M = 33.43 samples per group).

> "The likelihood ratio test revealed that Model 3 (interactive) described the variation in exam score better than Model 1 (additive), F(6, 220) = 2.141, p = 0.049, which suggests that the interaction between practice and education level significantly contributes to explaining differences in exam points across learners."
