# Overall summative assessment scores did not differ between students by 3MTL usage, though pairwise comparisons favoured heavy users

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Overall mean scores on the podcast-supplemented test were not different when compared with preceding and succeeding assessments or among heavy, intermediate and non-user groups. [→ Prakash S.S. 2017](#prakash-ss-2017)
`q3 i?` Pairwise differences (Test3MTL minus TestP; Test3MTL minus TestS) were statistically significant overall (mean paired difference 1.67, p < 0.001; 3.3, p < 0.001), with heavy users showing the largest differences. [→ Prakash S.S. 2017 (2)](#prakash-ss-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Prakash S.S. 2017

Prakash S.S., Muthuraman N., Anand R. (2017). Short-duration podcasts as a supplementary learning tool: perceptions of medical students and impact on assessment performance. BMC Medical Education 17:167. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1001-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Exploratory comparison of departmental written-assessment scores (maximum 30) among three self-reported usage groups (heavy N = 41; intermediate N = 34; non-users N = 19); no statistically significant difference; equivalence was not tested. Reported in Results as not significant (Fig. 3ii, NS).

> "Overall, average scores in Test3MTL were not different when compared with TestP or TestS or among student groups based on 3MTLs usage (Fig. 3)."

### Prakash S.S. 2017 (2)

Prakash S.S., Muthuraman N., Anand R. (2017). Short-duration podcasts as a supplementary learning tool: perceptions of medical students and impact on assessment performance. BMC Medical Education 17:167. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1001-5

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Paired t-tests on intraindividual score differences (Table 5): overall Test3MTL − TestP mean 1.67, p < 0.001; Test3MTL − TestS mean 3.3, p < 0.001; heavy users 2.32 (p = 0.003) and 4.20 (p < 0.001); intermediate and non-users non-significant for TestP. All three subgroups were significant for TestS.

> "However, the difference of scores in pairwise comparisons (Test3MTL vs. TestP; Test3MTL vs. TestS) was statistically significant (Table 3). In the pairwise comparisons with students groups stratified based on 3MTL usage, the difference was significant amongst 3MTL: Heavy-users in Test3MTL vs. TestP."
