Evidence¶

de Jonge 2026¶

de Jonge, T. H. S., de Koning, B. B., & van Wermeskerken, M. (2026). It's all a matter of expectancy: How expectancies about task execution perspective influence learning through video modeling examples. Learning and Instruction, 105, 102422. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2026.102422

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Exploratory linear mixed-effects model adding MRT as moderator. MRT significantly moderated the alignment effect on task performance, "β = 0.23"; higher MRT ability meant more benefit from alignment. MRT did not significantly moderate the three-way match effect (p = .164).

"MRT significantly moderated the effect of alignment between the recording angle and the expected angle (t(123) = 2.51, p = .013, SE = 0.08, β = 0.23), with a moderately small effect size. Specifically, the higher their MRT ability, the more participants appeared to benefit from alignment between these angles."

q3 · i2 · causal · r2

Exploratory model for cognitive load during the task: MRT main effect significant, "β = -0.20"; higher MRT scores were associated with less cognitive load during the task. MRT did not significantly moderate the recording–task angle alignment effect (p = .118). Signs restored: the PDF's extracted text had lost its minus signs.

"In the model for cognitive load during the task, MRT had a significant main effect (t(126) = -2.36, p = .020, SE = 0.04, β = -0.20). On average, individuals with higher MRT scores experienced less cognitive load during the task (a moderately small effect)."

Discussion¶

Learner Variables¶

  • Spatial Ability — moderator: an instructional effect differs with it

Related Claims¶

  • Alignment between recording angle and expected task angle strongly improves task performance, supporting the Expectation Alignment Hypothesis — related
  • First-person video modeling examples yield better task performance than third-person videos regardless of expected or task angle, supporting the Embodied Resonance Hypothesis — related
  • Participants informed in advance about a misaligned task angle performed worse and reported higher video cognitive load than those surprised by the misalignment — related
  • Mismatch between recording angle and expected angle increases cognitive load both during video observation and during task execution — related
  • Recording angle alone does not affect self-reported cognitive load during video observation or task execution — related
