## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Physlet problems that provide no stated variables force students to determine relevant information and interact with the applet to obtain it, so a plug-n-chug approach will not be effective. [→ Melissa Dancy 2002](#melissa-dancy-2002)

## Evidence

### Melissa Dancy 2002

Melissa Dancy, Wolfgang Christian and Mario Belloni. (2002). Teaching with Physlets: Examples from Optics. The Physics Teacher. [doi:10.1119/1.1526622](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1526622)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' analysis of their mirror focal-length Physlet problem (Figure 1), in which students are told a point source can be dragged and must find the mirror's focal length with no variables given. The authors argue that "there is nothing to “plug” until some conceptualization of the problem has been done."

> "A “plug-n-chug” approach will not be effective in this problem as there is nothing to “plug” until some conceptualization of the problem has been done."
