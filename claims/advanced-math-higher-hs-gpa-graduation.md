# High-achieving underserved students who took advanced math had higher high school GPAs and four-year graduation rates than those who did not

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average high school GPA was 2.9 versus 2.3 for high-achieving Black students who did versus did not take advanced math, and 3.1 versus 2.3 for Latino high achievers. [→ Melodie Baker 2023](#melodie-baker-2023)
`q2 i?` Among high achievers who took advanced math, graduation rates ranged only from 96% to 99% across groups, while rates among those who did not varied by 10 percentage points across race/ethnicity and 14 points by SES. [→ Melodie Baker 2023](#melodie-baker-2023)

## Evidence

### Melodie Baker 2023

Melodie Baker, Ivy Morgan, and Gizella Wade. (2023). Opportunities Denied: High-Achieving Black and Latino Students Lack Access to Advanced Math. EdTrust and Just Equations. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED641831

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive HSLS:09 comparison of high-achieving students by advanced course-taking status. Endnotes state all GPA and graduation differences are statistically significant, though no effect sizes are printed.

> "the average high school GPA for high-achieving Black students was 2.9 for students who did take advanced courses and 2.3 for students who did not take advanced courses. Similarly, the average GPA for Latino high achievers with advanced coursework was 3.1, compared with 2.3 for those without advanced coursework."
