Evidence¶

Allensworth 2005¶

Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2005). The On-Track Indicator as a Predictor of High School Graduation. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/track-indicator-predictor-high-school-graduation

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Observational analysis of CPS longitudinal student records for the September 1999 entering cohort, reported with Figure 2. The report states "A full 81 percent of the students on-track at the end of freshman year graduated" versus 22 percent of off-track students; five-year rates were 85 percent versus 28 percent.

"A full 81 percent of the students on-track at the end of freshman year graduated from high school in 2003, four years after beginning high school; however, only 22 percent of the off-track students graduated in four years."

Pitcher 2016¶

Pitcher, M. A., Duncan, S. J., Nagaoka, J., Moeller, E., Dickerson, L., & Beechum, N. O. (2016). The Network for College Success: A Capacity-Building Model for School Improvement. University of Chicago. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/network-college-success-capacity-building-model-school-improvement

q2 · i? · design · r2

The paper reports, citing the UChicago Consortium's 2007 report What Matters for On-Track, that the indicator was validated as a much stronger graduation predictor than eighth-grade test scores and demographics. The comparison is reported second-hand; no effect size is printed here.

"The report revealed that a ninth-grader who was On-Track was three and one-half times more likely to graduate from high school in four years than an off-track student."

Roderick 2021¶

Roderick, M., & Kelley-Kemple, T., with Johnson, D. W., & Ryan, S. (2021). The Preventable Failure: Improvements in High School Graduation Rates when High Schools Focus on the Ninth-Grade Year. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/the-preventable-failure

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Consortium analysis of CPS cohort data reports that in 2005, 40 percent of first-time ninth-graders were off-track and "only 22 percent of these students graduated four years later," versus 81 percent of on-track students. The article reports the 3.5-times ratio but no standardized effect size.

"In 2005, 40 percent of CPS first-time ninth-graders were off-track at the end of ninth grade and only 22 percent of these students graduated four years later. In a dramatic contrast, 81 percent of students who were on-track at the end of ninth grade graduated within four years."
