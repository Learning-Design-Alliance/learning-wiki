`q2 i?` Intervention 1, in which students first tried the friction problem on their own before receiving the solved tension problem, was consistently the better intervention in helping students refrain from incorrectly equating static friction with its maximum value in both algebra- and calculus-based courses. ## Evidence

### Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh 2016

Shih-Yin Lin and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Using Analogical Problem Solving with Different Scaffolding Supports to Learn about Friction. Originally published 2008. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.06390](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.06390)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' summary of the three-group recitation-quiz comparison, in which intervention group 1 spent the first 10 minutes attempting the quiz alone before receiving the solved tension problem and then retook the quiz. The article states this ordering was "the most beneficial to students" in both courses and attributes the benefit to the clear targeted goal and thinking process of the first attempt facilitating transfer.

> "This result suggests that providing the solved problem to stude nts only after they have tried to solve the quiz proble m on their own was the most beneficial to students in bo th the calculus- and algebra-based courses."
