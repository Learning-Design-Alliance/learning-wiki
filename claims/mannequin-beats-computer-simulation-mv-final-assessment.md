# Mannequin-based simulation training outperforms computer screen-based training on final in situ high-fidelity ARDS assessment scores among anesthesiology residents

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the final in situ high-fidelity simulation assessment, the mannequin group scored significantly higher than the computer group on both global rating score (P = 0.005) and percentage of key action score (82% vs. 71%, P = 0.001). [→ Spadaro S 2017](#spadaro-s-2017)

## Evidence

### Spadaro S 2017

Spadaro S, Karbing DS, Fogagnolo A, Ragazzi R, Mojoli F, Astolfi L, Gioia A, Marangoni E, Rees SE, Volta CA. (2017). Simulation Training for Residents Focused on Mechanical Ventilation: A Randomized Trial Using Mannequin-Based Versus Computer-Based Simulation. Simulation in Healthcare 12(6):349-355. https://doi.org/10.1097/SIH.0000000000000249

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized trial of 50 anesthesiology residents randomized after a common didactic lecture to mannequin (n = 25) or computer screen-based (n = 25) simulator training. In the final in situ HFS ARDS scenario, "a statistically significant difference was found both in GRS" and key action score favoring the mannequin group, with medians and percentages as printed; no standardized effect size was reported.

> "When comparing the ability of the two groups to deal with the same scenario in HFS, a statistically significant difference was found both in GRS [MG: (3.0 2.5–4.0) vs. CG: 2.0 (2.0–3.0), P = 0.005] and key action score (MG: 82% vs. CG: 71%, P = 0.001)"
