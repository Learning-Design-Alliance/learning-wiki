# Segmenting principle: learner-paced segments of narrated animation improve transfer over continuous presentation

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · review `r2` · `q3` · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In 3 of 3 experiments, learners who received segmented lessons performed better on transfer tests than learners who received continuous lessons, median d = 0.98. [→ Richard E. Mayer 2008](#richard-e-mayer-2008)

## Evidence

### Richard E. Mayer 2008

Richard E. Mayer. (2008). Applying the Science of Learning: Evidence-Based Principles for the Design of Multimedia Instruction. American Psychologist. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066x.63.8.760

`q3 · i3` · `review · r2`

Controlled experiments breaking a continuous narrated animation into learner-paced segments advanced via a CONTINUE button, on lightning and electric motors; the article reports the quoted 3-of-3 result and that "The median effect size was 0.98, which is a large effect."

> "In 3 out of 3 experiments, involving computer-based lessons on lightning and electric motors, learners who received segmented lessons performed better on transfer tests than did learners who received continuous lessons"
