# The QuILT on representations of quantum states produces large learning gains: average normalized gain 0.75 for undergraduates and 0.76 for Ph.D. students, with Cohen's d of 1.6 and 0.60 respectively

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` In-class pretest-posttest evaluation showed average normalized gains of 0.75 (undergraduate) and 0.76 (Ph.D.), with effect sizes d = 1.6 (undergraduate, large) and d = 0.60 (Ph.D., moderate). [→ Marshman 2024](#marshman-2024)

## Evidence

### Marshman 2024

Marshman, E., Maries, A., & Singh, C. (2024). Using multiple representations to improve student understanding of quantum states. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020152](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.020152)

`q3 · i2` · `design · r3`

In-class evaluation with upper-level undergraduates (85 pretest cohort) and 97 Ph.D. students across four years; students worked through the QuILT in small groups after a pretest following traditional instruction. The article reports "The average effect size on questions Q1-Q7 is 1.6 for undergraduate students" and 0.60 for Ph.D. students.

> "The average normalized  gain from the pretest to the posttest was  0.75 for undergraduate students and 0.76 for Ph.D. students, which indicate significant learning from the QuILT."
