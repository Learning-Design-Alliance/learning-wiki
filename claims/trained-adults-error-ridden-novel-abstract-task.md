## Evidence

### Thorndike 1922

Thorndike, E. L., & Upton, C. B. (1922). An Experiment in Learning an Abstract Subject. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 13(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/h0065229

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Observational study of a score of college graduates learning electricity and magnetism over ten hours. The authors report the problem-score distribution: "the median was 5; half were from 3 to 7 inclusive."

> "This is evidenced by the records with the problems. The highest score was 12-S^ correct out of 15; the lowest was 0; the median was 5; half were from 3 to 7 inclusive."

### Thorndike 1922 (2)

Thorndike, E. L., & Upton, C. B. (1922). An Experiment in Learning an Abstract Subject. The Journal of Educational Psychology, 13(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/h0065229

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Analysis of the adults' written problem work found "341 answers given of which only 53 per cent were right," with little apology or doubt expressed about wrong answers.

> "In the book problems there were (counting each task requiring a separate answer as one problem) 341 answers given of which only 53 per cent were right."
