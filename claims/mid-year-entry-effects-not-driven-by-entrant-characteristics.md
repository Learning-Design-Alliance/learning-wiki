# Mid-year entry effects on stable students are not driven by mid-year entrants' characteristics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mediation analysis indicates mid-year entrants' characteristics do not explain the negative impact of mid-year entry on stable students' achievement. [→ Emilyn Ruble Whitesell 2016](#emilyn-ruble-whitesell-2016)

## Evidence

### Emilyn Ruble Whitesell 2016

Emilyn Ruble Whitesell, Leanna Stiefel, Amy Ellen Schwartz. (2016). Unexpected Arrivals: The Spillover Effects of Mid-Year Entry on Stable Student Achievement in New York City. Education Evaluation and Policy Analysis, vol. 38, no. 4. https://www.mathematica.org/publications/unexpected-arrivals-the-spillover-effects-of-mid-year-entry-on-stable-student-achievement-in-new

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

The study determines "the extent to which mid-year entrants' characteristics mediate the impact of mid-year entry" and concludes the impacts are not driven by those characteristics. No mediation statistics are printed on this page.

> "These impacts are not driven by mid-year entrant characteristics and are somewhat larger for Asian students and those who do not qualify for free or reduced-price lunch."
