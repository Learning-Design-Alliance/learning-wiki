# Among 7th-grade ASSISTments users, there is no significant overall gender difference in homework time or number of problems completed

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` HLM models with students nested in classes and schools found no significant difference between girls and boys in homework time (mins_s) or problems completed (probs_c). [→ Mingyu Feng 2016](#mingyu-feng-2016)

## Evidence

### Mingyu Feng 2016

Mingyu Feng, Jeremy Roschelle, Craig Mason, Ruchi Bhanot. (2016). Investigating Gender Differences on Homework in Middle School Mathematics. Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Educational Data Mining, June 29-July 2, 2016, Raleigh, NC. https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2016/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

3-Level Hierarchical Linear Regression models (students nested in classes and classes in schools) on ASSISTments log data of 1033 7th graders (514 boys, 519 girls) with gender as predictor; "no significant difference between girls and boys" on mins_s (p = 0.350) and probs_c (p = 0.177).

> "the results suggest that overall there is no significant difference between girls and boys in terms of the amount of time they spent on homework or the number of problems they completed."
