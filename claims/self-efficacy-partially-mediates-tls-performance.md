## Subclaims
`q2 i?` TLS relates to self-efficacy (reward β = 0.31; punishment β = 0.14, both p < 0.001), and TLS effects on performance remain significant but smaller when self-efficacy is included, indicating partial mediation. [→ Deng Guangwei 2019](#deng-guangwei-2019)

## Evidence

### Deng Guangwei 2019

Deng Guangwei, Zhao Di, Lio Jonathan, Chen Xinyu, Ma Xiaopeng, Liang Liang, Feng Chenpeng. (2019). Strategic elements of residency training in China: transactional leadership, self-efficacy, and employee-orientation culture. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1792-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical mediated regression analysis on the same 247-resident survey sample. The article reports significant TLS-to-self-efficacy paths and reduced-but-significant direct paths to performance, from which the authors "deduce that self-efficacy plays a partially mediating role"; no separate effect size for the mediation is printed.

> "In Table 3, contingent reward was significantly related to self-efficacy (β = 0.31, p < 0.001), the same as contingent punishment (β = 0.14, p < 0.001). The effects of contingent reward (β = 0.35, p < 0.001) and contingent punishment (β = 0.22, p < 0.001) on internship performance were smaller but still significant when incorporating self-efficacy."
