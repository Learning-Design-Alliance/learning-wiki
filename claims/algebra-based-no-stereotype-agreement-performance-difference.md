# In algebra-based courses, female students who agreed with the gender stereotype performed similarly (within 2%) to those who disagreed on FCI and CSEM

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` No statistically significant performance differences on FCI or CSEM between algebra-based female students who agreed vs. disagreed with the stereotype, on pretest or post-test. [→ Alexandru Maries 2018](#alexandru-maries-2018)

## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2018

Alexandru Maries, Nafis I. Karim, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Is agreeing with a gender stereotype correlated with the performance of female students in introductory physics? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 020119. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.020119

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of FCI and CSEM performance of algebra-based female students grouped by stereotype agreement (agree/strongly agree vs. disagree/strongly disagree) on pretest and post-test; performance was similar within 2% and no statistically significant differences were found (Figs. 2 and 3).

> "in an algebra-based course female students who agreed with the gender stereotype (men generally perform better in physics than women) and female students who disagreed with the stereotype had similar performance (within 2%) on both the pretest and the post-test."
