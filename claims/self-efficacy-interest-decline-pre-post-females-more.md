# Both male and female students' physics self-efficacy and interest declined from the beginning to the end of the course, with female students declining more

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students' self-efficacy dropped generally from pre to post, and female students' self-efficacy dropped more (d = 0.52) than male students' (d = 0.27); female students' interest also dropped more (d = 0.30) than male students' (d = 0.19). [→ Yangqiuting Li 2020](#yangqiuting-li-2020)

## Evidence

### Yangqiuting Li 2020

Yangqiuting Li, Kyle Whitcomb, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). How learning environment predicts male and female students' physics motivational beliefs in introductory physics courses. Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Pittsburgh. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.li](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.li)

`q3 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Pre-post t-tests reported in Table I of the two-semester survey study. Male self-efficacy fell from 3.12 to 2.98 and female from 2.96 to 2.68; male interest fell from 3.19 to 3.08 and female from 2.89 to 2.70, all p < 0.001, with "effect size d = 0.52" for female self-efficacy decline.

> "female students' self-efficacy dropped (effect size d = 0.52) more than male students' (effect size d = 0.27). Similarly, Table I shows that female students' interest in physics dropped (effect size d = 0.30) more than male students' (effect size d = 0.19)"
