Evidence¶

Soland 2019¶

Soland, J. & Kuhfeld, M. (2019). Identifying students who are off-track academically in 9th Grade: the role of social-emotional learning trajectories. (NWEA Collaborative for Student Growth Research Brief). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/identifying-students-who-are-off-track-academically-in-9th-grade-the-role-of-social-emotional-learning-trajectories/

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Structural equation models regressing the 9th-grade suspension indicator on each latent status and growth variable for a single California district cohort, controlling for background covariates. The article reports "significantly lower" odds for both self-management status and growth (Table 5: OR 0.590 and 0.340).

"the odds of being suspended were significantly lower for students who were above average in self-management at the start of middle school as well as students who showed above-average growth in self-management across middle school."

Soland 2019 (2)¶

Soland, J. & Kuhfeld, M. (2019). Identifying students who are off-track academically in 9th Grade: the role of social-emotional learning trajectories. (NWEA Collaborative for Student Growth Research Brief). https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/identifying-students-who-are-off-track-academically-in-9th-grade-the-role-of-social-emotional-learning-trajectories/

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Same district-cohort SEM analysis predicting GPA below a C; the printed Table 5 odds ratios are 0.493 (self-management intercept) and 0.444 (self-management growth), both p<.05. No standardized effect size is printed, so impact is not coded.

"for GPA, coefficients on the self-efficacy and self-management latent intercepts were significant at the .05 level, as was the coefficient on the self-management growth."
