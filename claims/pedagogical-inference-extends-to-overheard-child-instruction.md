# Preschoolers constrain exploration after direct instruction and after overhearing instruction to another child, but not after overhearing instruction to an adult or a non-pedagogical intentional action

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Children in the Direct and Indirect Child conditions performed fewer different kinds of actions than children in the Indirect Adult and Intentional conditions. [→ Bonawitz Elizabeth 2011](#bonawitz-elizabeth-2011)
`q3 i?` Children in the Direct and Indirect Child conditions discovered fewer non-demonstrated functions than children in the Indirect Adult and Intentional conditions, even controlling for play duration. [→ Bonawitz Elizabeth 2011 (2)](#bonawitz-elizabeth-2011-2)

## Evidence

### Bonawitz Elizabeth 2011

Bonawitz Elizabeth, Shafto Patrick, Gweon Hyowon, Goodman Noah D., Spelke Elizabeth, Schulz Laura. (2011). The Double-edged Sword of Pedagogy: Instruction limits spontaneous exploration and discovery. Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.10.001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 2 randomly assigned 64 preschoolers to Direct, Indirect Child, Indirect Adult, or Intentional conditions. The planned contrast (weights 1,1,-1,-1) showed the Direct and Indirect Child groups performed "fewer different kinds of actions," F(1,60)=11.50, p<0.01; overall playtime did not differ between conditions (F(1,60)=.92, p=ns).

> "Children in the Direct (M=3.13) and Indirect Child (M=3.94) condition performed fewer different kinds of actions on the toy than children in the Indirect Adult (M=5.31) and Intentional (M=5.13) conditions, F(1,60)=11.50, p<0.01."

### Bonawitz Elizabeth 2011 (2)

Bonawitz Elizabeth, Shafto Patrick, Gweon Hyowon, Goodman Noah D., Spelke Elizabeth, Schulz Laura. (2011). The Double-edged Sword of Pedagogy: Instruction limits spontaneous exploration and discovery. Cognition. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.10.001

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In Experiment 2, discovery of non-demonstrated functions (0–3 scale) was lower in the Direct and Indirect Child conditions than in the Indirect Adult and Intentional conditions, F(1,60)=6.38, p<.05; the contrast persisted for the first 60 seconds of play (F(1,60)=9.88, p<0.01).

> "Children’s mean score was lower in the Direct (M=.50) and Indirect Child (M=.75) conditions than the Indirect Adult (M=1.31) and Intentional (M=1.00) conditions, F(1,60)=6.38, p<.05, even when controlling for duration of play by considering only the first 60 seconds"
