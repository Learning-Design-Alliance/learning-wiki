# Structured guidance strategies (G1, G3, G4) reduce unrelated student queries to an LLM tutor, while example-based instruction increases them

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a formative classroom study, students who did not receive G1-List of Suggestions, G3-Metacognitive Questioning, or G4-Solve then refine tended to ask more unrelated questions to the LLM, while G2-Example-based instruction increased such queries. [→ Harsh Kumar 2024](#harsh-kumar-2024)

## Evidence

### Harsh Kumar 2024

Harsh Kumar, Ilya Musabirov, Mohi Reza, Jiakai Shi, Xinyuan Wang, Joseph Jay Williams, Anastasia Kuzminykh, and Michael Liut. 2024. Impact of Guidance and Interaction Strategies for LLM Use on Learner Performance and Perception. Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. 8, CSCW2, Article 499 (November 2024). https://doi.org/10.1145/3687038

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Formative randomized factorial classroom study (N=145 enrolled, 138 LLM users) in a third-year databases course; first queries to the LLM tutor were coded into four categories with high inter-rater agreement (Cohen's Kappa = 0.814). The authors report that "students tend to ask unrelated questions more" when structured guidance is absent.

> "We find that when G1-List of Suggestions, G3-Metacognitive Questioning and G4-Solve then refine with LLM are absent, students tend to ask unrelated questions more, compared to when it is present. On the other hand, the presence of G2-Example based instruction resulted in more students asking unrelated questions, or trying to break the chatbot."
