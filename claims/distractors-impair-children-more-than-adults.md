## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Additional distractors on the screen had negative consequences for children's tracking performance but less so for adults, even when tracking a single target. [→ Kimiko Ryokai 2013](#kimiko-ryokai-2013)

## Evidence

### Kimiko Ryokai 2013

Kimiko Ryokai, Faraz Farzin, Eric Kaltman, Greg Niemeyer. (2013). Assessing multiple object tracking in young children using a game. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-012-9278-x

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Accuracy was analyzed as a function of target and distractor number (Fig. 5) for both children and adults. The article also reports that for children the presence of an additional distractor affected tracking 3 targets by 6% while adults showed a fixed performance level.

> "for children, tracking a single target with 2 versus 3 distractors on the screen, reduced performance by 20 %, but dropped performance by only 10 % for adults."
