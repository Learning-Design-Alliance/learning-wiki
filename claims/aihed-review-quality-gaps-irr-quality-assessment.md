# AIHEd evidence syntheses show methodological quality weaknesses: many omit inter-rater reliability reporting, quality assessment, and full search strings

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Bond 2024

Bond, M., Khosravi, H., De Laat, M., Bergdahl, N., Negrea, V., Oxley, E., Pham, P., Chong, S. W. & Siemens, G. (2024). A meta systematic review of artificial intelligence in higher education: a call for increased ethics, collaboration, and rigour. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-023-00436-z

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Quality assessment of the 66-review AIHEd corpus against 10 criteria adapted from DARE, AMSTAR 2, and Buntins et al. The article reports the cited percentages directly; the corpus averaged "6.57" out of 10 overall. No effect sizes are printed.

> "The most concerning findings were that 31.8% of studies only searched in one or two databases, 51.5% did not report anything about inter-rater reliability or how screening and coding decisions were decided between review teams, only 24.2% provided their exact data extraction coding scheme, 45.5% did not undertake any form of quality assessment"
