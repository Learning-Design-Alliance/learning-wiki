# Training more than three sessions per week neutralizes CCT efficacy, while one or 2–3 sessions per week are effective

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q4` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Significant effects were found for one session per week (g = 0.34) and 2–3 sessions per week (g = 0.28), but not for more than three sessions per week (g = 0.07, p = 0.28). [→ Lampit A 2014](#lampit-a-2014)

## Evidence

### Lampit A 2014

Lampit A, Hallock H, Valenzuela M (2014) Computerized Cognitive Training in Cognitively Healthy Older Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Effect Modifiers. PLoS Med 11(11): e1001756. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001756

`q4 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Mixed-effects subgroup analysis of session frequency across all outcomes. Effects were significant at one and 2–3 sessions per week but non-significant above three sessions per week; the between-subgroup Q test was significant (p = 0.011).

> "significant effect estimates in studies that administered one (g = 0.34, 95% CI 0.16 to 0.51, p,0.001) or 2–3 sessions per week (g = 0.28, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.37,p,0.001) but not in studies that trained their participants more than three times per week (g = 0.07, 95% CI 20.06 to 0.19, p = 0.28; Q = 9.082, df = 2, p = 0.011)."
