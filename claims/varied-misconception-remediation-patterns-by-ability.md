## Evidence

### Segado 2026

Segado, M., Adair, A., Stewart, J., and Pritchard, D. (2026). Discovering Misconceptions and Misunderstandings From Administrations of Research-Designed Multiple Choice Instruments. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2606.08986](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.08986)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Pre/post comparison of misconception scores by ability band across the ~34k-administration dataset; the article states it finds "dramatic differences on the effect of instruction" across misconceptions and ability levels. Quote is from the introduction summarizing the remediation analysis.

> "We find dramatic differences on the effect of instruction, with numerous cases where the instruction is effective mainly for students of high initial ability but relatively ineffective for most students (in accord with previous work on misconceptions), but also a significant number that are held primarily by students of average initial ability and that are remediated effectively by current instruction."
