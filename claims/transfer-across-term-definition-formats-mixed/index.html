Evidence¶

Michelle L. Rivers 2025¶

Michelle L. Rivers, Paige E. Northern, Sarah K. Tauber. (2025). Does Retrieval Demand Moderate the Effectiveness of Covert Retrieval Practice? Comparing Covert and Overt Retrieval Practice for Learning Key Terms and Definitions. Educational Psychology Review (2025) 37:61. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10040-4

q3 · i? · causal · r2

In Experiment 2's test-on-terms group, participants who practiced definitions showed no significant difference between overt (M = 0.44) and covert retrieval (M = 0.39); equivalence was not tested. Overt still beat restudy (gav = 0.25).

"However, recall did not significantly differ between overt retrieval and covert retrieval (M = 0.39, SE = 0.03), t(88) = 1.50, p = 0.138, 95% CI [− 0.01, 0.10], g av = 0.16."

Related Claims¶

  • Covert retrieval is the most time-efficient practice strategy, yielding the largest gains per minute of practice for both terms and definitions — related
  • The overt-retrieval advantage does not significantly differ by definition complexity (number of idea units), contrary to a retrieval-dynamics prediction — related
  • Overt retrieval practice produces higher delayed recall of definitions than covert retrieval practice for learners studying declarative concepts — related
  • Overt retrieval also outperforms covert retrieval for key terms, but with a smaller advantage than for definitions — related
  • Response format (overt vs. covert retrieval) did not affect final cued recall in spaced retrieval practice, and list structure (blocked vs. random; proportion of overt trials) did not moderate the effect. — related
