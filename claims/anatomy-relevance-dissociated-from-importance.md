## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For junior students, perceived relevance of anatomy was not synonymous with perceived importance, which was tied to how severely a subject was assessed; senior students reached full realisation of anatomy's relevance only after extensive clinical experience. ## Evidence

### Bergman EM 2013

Bergman EM, de Bruin ABH, Herrler A, Verheijen IWH, Scherpbier AJJA, van der Vleuten CPM. (2013). Students' perceptions of anatomy across the undergraduate problem-based learning medical curriculum: a phenomenographical study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-152

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative focus-group analysis across four curriculum years. The study found "perceived relevance of anatomy and perceived importance are not synonymous" for junior students, whose study priority followed assessment severity; sixth-year students reported realising anatomy's value during clerkships such as neurology.

> "Perceived relevance of anatomy and perceived importance are not synonymous for these students, and stricter assessment is a frequently mentioned method as an incentive for them to study harder for anatomy (or any other subject)."
