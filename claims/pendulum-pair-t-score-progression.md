# A student pair's iterative redesign raised their pendulum t'-score from 0.18 to 3.71, leading them to conclude the two amplitude measurements differed

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` One student pair, across three self-designed measurement attempts, increased their |t'|-score from 0.18 to 2.39 to 3.71 by timing more swings per measurement, concluding the 10° and 20° periods were different. [→ N.G. Holmes & D.A. Bonn 2015](#ng-holmes-da-bonn-2015)

## Evidence

### N.G. Holmes & D.A. Bonn 2015

N.G. Holmes & D.A. Bonn. (2015). Quantitative comparisons to promote inquiry in the introductory physics lab. The Physics Teacher. [doi:10.1119/1.4928350](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4928350)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Documented case of one pair of students in the Pendulum for Pros SQILab, shown in Table 2 with three measurement attempts (e.g. attempt 3: 1.830±0.004s vs 1.851±0.004s). The table prints |t'| values 0.18, 2.39, and 3.71 across the three iterations; the running text cites 3.66 for the third attempt.

> "In their third attempt, they increased the number of swings per measurement to 20, thus reducing their uncertainty in the previous measurement by a factor of 2, and increasing their t'-score to 3.66, at which point they concluded that the values were different."
