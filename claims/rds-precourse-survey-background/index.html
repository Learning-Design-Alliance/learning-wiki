Lewis Armanda; Stoyanovich Julia 2021¶

Lewis Armanda; Stoyanovich Julia. (2021). Teaching Responsible Data Science: Charting New Pedagogical Territory. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00241-7

q2 · i? · design · r2

Pre-course survey administered in Spring 2020 to collect student expectations and initial knowledge, modeled partly on a Mozilla Foundation survey. Descriptive demographics only; no inferential test reported.

"The survey had 48 respondents, 41 (86%) of whom were enrolled in masters programs in data science or related disciplines, 6 (12%) were doctoral students, and 1 was an undergraduate student."

Lewis Armanda; Stoyanovich Julia. (2021). Teaching Responsible Data Science: Charting New Pedagogical Territory. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00241-7

q2 · i? · design · r2

Self-reported prior knowledge from the same Spring 2020 pre-course survey of 48 respondents, which sought background information rather than measuring a specific construct. No effect size or test statistic is printed.

"Approximately half of the class reported having little to no prior knowledge of legal studies or social science."

Lewis Armanda; Stoyanovich Julia 2021 (3)¶

Lewis Armanda; Stoyanovich Julia. (2021). Teaching Responsible Data Science: Charting New Pedagogical Territory. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00241-7

q2 · i? · design · r2

Pre-course interpretive-prompt data (n = 48 respondents) shown in Fig. 2 on students' views of the role of data science and AI. The authors describe the figure's pattern descriptively; no test statistic is reported.

"Figure 2 shows that the majority of students do maintain the view that data science and AI should reflect ethical principles, though it will be interesting for future study to examine views that are reported as neutral."
