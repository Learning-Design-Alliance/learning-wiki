# Seniors outperformed juniors in determining taxonomy and method-technique, but not in self-efficacy or strategy determination

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Senior pre-service teachers scored significantly higher than juniors in determining taxonomy (t = −2.58, p < 0.05) and determining method-technique (t = 2.13, p < 0.05), while self-efficacy, outcome expectancy, total TSMTDF, and strategy scores showed no significant grade-level difference. [→ Kaygısız 2018](#kaygsz-2018)

## Evidence

### Kaygısız 2018

Kaygısız, G.M., Uygun, N., & Uçar, F.M. (2018). The Relationship between the Levels of Self-efficacy Beliefs of Pre-service Teachers and their Levels of Determining Suitable Taxonomy, Strategy, and Method-technique for Science Objectives. Science Education International 31(1), 117-126. https://doi.org/10.33828/sei.v31.i1.12

`q2 · i?`

Independent samples t-tests comparing 69 juniors and 32 seniors (Table 2). Significant differences favored seniors on taxonomy and method-technique; efficacy belief (t = −0.10), outcome expectancy (t = 0.93), self-efficacy (t = 0.44), total TSMTDF (t = 0.27), and strategy (t = 1.61) showed no significant difference (ρ > 0.05).
