## Evidence

### Peng 2020

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Latent change score modeling studies of IQ and reading from first through 12th grades (Ferrer et al., 2007, 2010) found bidirectional effects strongest in grades 1–3 and weakening later, and per the article these effects were "obvious only among typically developing children, not among children with learning disabilities."

> "such bidirectional effects were obvious only among typically developing children, not among children with learning disabilities"

### Peng 2020 (2)

Peng Peng, Kievit Rogier A. (2020). The Development of Academic Achievement and Cognitive Abilities: A Bidirectional Perspective. Child Development Perspectives. https://doi.org/10.1111/cdep.12352

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that early academic intervention effects for low-SES children or those with special needs generally fade after the intervention, with fadeout more likely among children who had very low academic skills before treatment. No effect sizes are printed.

> "the effects of such early academic intervention generally fade after the intervention (Jenkins et al., 2018), and fadeout is more likely among those who had very low academic skills before treatment (Bailey et al., 2016)"
