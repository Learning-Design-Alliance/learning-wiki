# Co-teaching predicts better teacher-rated mathematics grades at year end, but not test performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Teaching condition predicted teacher-rated grade at the end of sixth grade (β = 0.25, p = .008), over and above prior grade and test performance. [→ Lehikoinen 2025](#lehikoinen-2025)
`q2 i?` Teaching condition did not predict end-of-year mathematics test performance. [→ Lehikoinen 2025 (2)](#lehikoinen-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Lehikoinen 2025

Lehikoinen, H., Väisänen, P., Havu-Nuutinen, S., Lappalainen, K., & Niemivirta, M. (2025). Developmental relations between mathematics self-concept, interest, and achievement: A comparison of solo- and co-taught classes. Instructional Science, 53, 239–261. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09678-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Full latent growth model on 146 sixth-graders in a natural experiment comparing co-taught (70 students, 3 classes) and solo-taught (76 students, 4 classes) conditions. Co-taught students received better end-of-year grades; the printed β = 0.25 is a standardised path coefficient, not an effect size, so impact is null.

> "the teaching condition predicted teacher-rated grade ( β = 0.25, p = .008), over and above the significant effects of both previous teacher-rated grade (β = 0.62, p < .001) and test performance (β = 0.11, p = .094)"

### Lehikoinen 2025 (2)

Lehikoinen, H., Väisänen, P., Havu-Nuutinen, S., Lappalainen, K., & Niemivirta, M. (2025). Developmental relations between mathematics self-concept, interest, and achievement: A comparison of solo- and co-taught classes. Instructional Science, 53, 239–261. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09678-4

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Teaching condition showed no effect on post-test performance (β = −0.00, p = .977 in Table 4). The authors attribute the discrepancy to the 10-minute RMAT test measuring mostly basic numeracy while teacher grades reflect broader achievement.

> "That these efforts were not similarly seen in test performance might be because the tests measured mostly basic mathematics skills, while teacher evaluations likely reflected a more comprehensive view into students' mathematics achievement"
