Evidence¶

Means 2009¶

Means, Toyama, Murphy, Bakia, & Jones. (2009). Evaluation of Evidence-Based Practices in Online Learning: A Meta-Analysis and Review of Online Learning Studies. U.S. Department of Education. https://www.ed.gov/about/offices/list/opepd/ppss/reports.html

q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative synthesis of experimental and quasi-experimental studies contrasting different versions of online learning (77 studies without a face-to-face condition were set aside for this synthesis; five involved K–12 learners). The synthesis reports student learning is "usually comparable across the two conditions."

"Blended and purely online learning conditions implemented within a single study generally result in similar student learning outcomes. When a study contrasts blended and purely online conditions, student learning is usually comparable across the two conditions."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Students in online learning conditions performed better on average than those receiving face-to-face instruction (mean effect size +0.24, p < .01, across 51 contrasts).
  • Of 13 online learning practice variables examined, only use of a blended approach and expanded time on task significantly influenced effectiveness; the other 11 did not. — reports the opposite
  • Blended online-plus-face-to-face instruction showed a larger advantage over face-to-face instruction (+0.35, p < .001) than purely online instruction did (+0.14, p < .05). — related
  • Repeated practice and blended learning showed no advantage in RCR course effectiveness, failing to replicate prior review findings — related
  • A systematic search found almost no rigorous studies contrasting online and face-to-face instruction for K–12 students: none with computable effect sizes through 2006, and only five meeting meta-analysis criteria through July 2008. — related
  • Online learning effectiveness was broadly positive across content areas and learner types, but the mean effect was not significant for the seven K–12 contrasts and the learner-type moderator test was nonsignificant. — related
  • Most included studies used quasi-experimental pretest/posttest designs, and online delivery was the predominant mode of instruction — related
