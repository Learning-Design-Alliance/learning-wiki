# Self-assessed nontechnical, technical and bleeding management self-efficacy significantly increased after a multidisciplinary trauma masterclass

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Pooled nontechnical skills self-efficacy rose from 3.4 to 3.8 on a 5-point scale after the course (p < 0.001, n = 40 paired responses). [→ Haverkamp Frederike J. C. 2022](#haverkamp-frederike-j-c-2022)
`q2 i?` Pooled technical skills self-efficacy rose from 2.9 to 3.6 after the course (p < 0.001, n = 40 paired responses). [→ Haverkamp Frederike J. C. 2022 (2)](#haverkamp-frederike-j-c-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Haverkamp Frederike J. C. 2022

Haverkamp Frederike J. C., Rahim Idris, Hoencamp Rigo, Fluit Cornelia R. M. G., Van Laarhoven Kees J. H. M., Tan Edward C. T. H. (2022). Self-efficacy and application of skills in the workplace after multidisciplinary trauma masterclass participation: a mixed methods survey and interview study. European Journal of Trauma and Emergency Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00068-022-02159-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pre-post comparison in the quantitative questionnaire of the mixed methods study, using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on 40 paired responses on a 5-point scale. The pooled nontechnical row shows "3.4 (SD 0.6) 3.8 (SD 0.5) < 0.001"; no effect size was printed.

> "Pooled nontechnical skills 3.4 (SD 0.6) 3.8 (SD 0.5) < 0.001"
