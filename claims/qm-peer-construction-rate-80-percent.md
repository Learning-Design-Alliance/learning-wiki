# Groups constructed knowledge at a high average rate of 80% when at least one member answered correctly individually

> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across QMFPS questions, groups chose the correct answer 80% of the time (SD 11%) when at least one member had answered correctly and one incorrectly individually. [→ Mary Jane Brundage 2023](#mary-jane-brundage-2023)
`q2 i?` Construction rates were similar across content topics, between 74% and 85%. [→ Mary Jane Brundage 2023 (2)](#mary-jane-brundage-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Mary Jane Brundage 2023

Mary Jane Brundage, Alysa Malespina, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Peer interaction facilitates co-construction of knowledge in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Question-level analysis across the survey found "the overall average rate of construction is quite high (80%, SD=11%)"; the correlation between construction and individual scores was non-significant (r = 0.02, p = 0.90).

> "However, the overall average rate of construction is quite high (80%, SD=11%), and the correlation between construction and individual survey scores was non-significant (r = 0.02, p = 0.90) [54]."

### Mary Jane Brundage 2023 (2)

Mary Jane Brundage, Alysa Malespina, and Chandralekha Singh. (2023). Peer interaction facilitates co-construction of knowledge in quantum mechanics. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020133)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Topic-level aggregation found "All topics had between 74% and 85% construction", which the authors describe as remarkably similar between topics.

> "Additionally, the rates of construction were remarkably similar between topics (for example “ measurement” or “Dirac notation”, see Table II). All topics had between 74% and 85% construction, as seen in Table II."
