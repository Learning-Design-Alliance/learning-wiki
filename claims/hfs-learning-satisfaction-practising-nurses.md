# High-fidelity simulation training yields a large effect on learning satisfaction among practising registered nurses

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Meta-analysis of three studies (102 nurses) found HFS produced a large, significant improvement in learning satisfaction compared with traditional methods (SMD = 1.24), with high heterogeneity. [→ Zhou Jie 2026](#zhou-jie-2026)

## Evidence

### Zhou Jie 2026

Zhou Jie, Zhang Guowen, Song Peige, Liaw Sok Ying, Luk Tzu Tsun. (2026). Effects of High‐Fidelity Simulation Training on Learning Outcomes and Satisfaction for Practising Registered Nurses: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis. Journal of Clinical Nursing. https://doi.org/10.1111/jocn.70206

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of two RCTs and one quasi-experiment (102 nurses) on learning satisfaction, only one using an existing instrument. The review reports "a large pooled effect on learning satisfaction" with high heterogeneity; fixed-effect results were similar (SMD = 1.09).

> "The random‐effects meta‐analysis showed a large pooled effect on learning satisfaction [SMD = 1.24, 95% CI, (0.35, 2.13), p < 0.01], with high heterogeneity (I2 = 67%, p = 0.05)"
