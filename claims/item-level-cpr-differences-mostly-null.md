## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Of the 10 theoretical questions, only question #2 (first action for an unconscious person) showed a significant group difference (81.8% vs 34.8%, p = 0.002). [→ de Sena David P. 2019](#de-sena-david-p-2019)

## Evidence

### de Sena David P. 2019

de Sena David P., Fabrício Daniela D., da Silva Vinícius D., Bodanese Luiz Carlos, Franco Alexandre R. (2019). Comparative evaluation of video-based on-line course versus serious game for training medical students in cardiopulmonary resuscitation: A randomised trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214722

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Fisher's exact tests with Bonferroni correction (p < 0.0022) compared correct answers per question. Question #2 was answered correctly by 81.8% of the video group versus 34.8% of the game group; "there was a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.002)".

> "Only in question #2 ('If you find an unconscious person, what is the first thing to do?'), however, there was a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p = 0.002)"

### de Sena David P. 2019 (2)

de Sena David P., Fabrício Daniela D., da Silva Vinícius D., Bodanese Luiz Carlos, Franco Alexandre R. (2019). Comparative evaluation of video-based on-line course versus serious game for training medical students in cardiopulmonary resuscitation: A randomised trial. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214722

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Binary checklist items were compared between groups with Fisher's exact test. Item #1 occurred in 56.5% of the game group and 0% of the video group; "only item #1 ('Did not check responsiveness') showed a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p < 0.001)".

> "only item #1 ('Did not check responsiveness') showed a statistically significant difference between the two groups (p < 0.001)"
