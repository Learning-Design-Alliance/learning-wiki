Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10043-1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in the rebuttal section on baseline equivalence: the authors contend that without reporting or statistically verifying pre-test equivalence, equivalence remains an untested assumption. No empirical data are offered for this statement.

> "However, randomization does not ensure baseline equivalence, par - ticularly in small-sample studies common in motor learning research."

### Ammar 2025 (2)

Ammar, A., Trabelsi, K., Salem, A., Jahrami, H., & Schöllhorn, W. I. (2025). Advancing Contextual Interference: Addressing Methodological Debates, Reflecting on Meta-Analytic Practices and Generalizability, and Guiding Future Directions in Motor Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10043-1

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors argue that given small sample sizes and variability in the CI literature, baseline differences are especially impactful and pose a serious threat to internal validity. This is an authors' interpretive argument, not a tested result.

> "Studies with unbalanced pre-tests can easily skew acquisition, retention and/or transfer comparisons, overstating the bene- fits of random practice."
