## Evidence

### Siva Raja Sindiramutty 2026

Siva Raja Sindiramutty. (2026). A Comparative Study on the Impact of Traditional Learning and Interactive Learning on Students' Academic Performance and Emotional Well-Being. Taylor's University. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Independent samples t-test on the 15-item Reyes et al.-adapted engagement survey in the randomized two-group experiment (n = 50 per group). The study reports "t(48) = 5.70, p < .001" and "Cohen’s d = 1.61", a 67.01% increase, attributed to real-time interaction and gamified elements in tools like Kahoot and Padlet.

> "Behavioural  engagement  was significantly greater among interactive learners ( M = 6.48 ) compared to traditional learners (M = 3.88 ), t(48) = 5.70, p < .001 , with a very large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.61)"

### Siva Raja Sindiramutty 2026 (2)

Siva Raja Sindiramutty. (2026). A Comparative Study on the Impact of Traditional Learning and Interactive Learning on Students' Academic Performance and Emotional Well-Being. Taylor's University. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same randomized two-group experiment, engagement survey emotional subscale. The interactive group's mean of 5.40 exceeded the traditional group's 3.08; the study reports "t(48) = 4.55, p < .001, d = 1.29", a 75.32% gain the authors link to dynamic, participatory environments.

> "emotional engagement  was notably higher in the interactive group ( M = 5.40 ) than the traditional group (M = 3.08 ), t(48) = 4.55, p < .001, d = 1.29"

### Siva Raja Sindiramutty 2026 (3)

Siva Raja Sindiramutty. (2026). A Comparative Study on the Impact of Traditional Learning and Interactive Learning on Students' Academic Performance and Emotional Well-Being. Taylor's University. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.15335)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Same engagement survey, cognitive subscale, showing the reverse trend: traditional learners scored "M = 4.12" versus interactive learners' 2.48, with "t(48) = -3.72, p = .001, d = -1.05". The authors interpret the 39.81% decline as possible cognitive overload from excessive stimuli hindering deep processing.

> "traditional learners scoring significantly higher (M = 4.12 ) than their interactive peers ( M = 2.48 ), t(48) = -3.72, p = .001, d = -1.05. This 39.81% decline suggests that interactive methods, while emotionally and behaviorally stimulating, may overwhelm students cognitively"
