## Evidence

### Qing X. Ryan 2015

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Coding of exam solutions at ACER element E1 (algebraic manipulation) for a problem with the boundary at z=d. The article reports "Over a third of the solutions (36%, 32 out of 90) canceled the exponential terms inappropriately"; no effect size printed.

> "However, students still cancelled the exponentials when the boundary was located at z=d. Over a third of the solutions (36%, 32 out of 90) canceled the exponential terms inappropriately."

### Qing X. Ryan 2015 (2)

Qing X. Ryan, Steven J. Pollock, and Bethany R. Wilcox. (2015). Student Difficulties with Boundary Conditions in Electrodynamics. arXiv. [doi:10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2015.pr.066)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Think-aloud interviews with a pure-math version of the expression (physical symbols replaced with arbitrary math variables). "None of the interview students made the same cancellation mistake", but 3 of 4 spontaneously connected it to the E&M wave context; no effect size printed.

> "None of the interview students made the same cancellation mistake. Interestingly, 3 (out of 4) students still made the connection to the wave context spontenously"
