# Internet use at age 11 showed a positive adjusted association with mathematics ability at age 17 in females, though unadjusted correlations were null

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among females, Internet use at least once a month, once a week, or most days at age 11 was associated with 24.9%, 18.6% and 16.6% increases in mathematics ability score at age 17 in adjusted regression models. [→ Guan 2026](#guan-2026)
`q2 i?` Unadjusted Spearman correlations showed no significant association between Internet use and mathematics ability in either sex. [→ Guan 2026 (2)](#guan-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Guan 2026

Guan, K., Zhang, Z., Liu, Z. et al. (2026). Longitudinal associations between sedentary behavior types and mathematics ability mediated by externalizing and internalizing problems. npj Sci. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00424-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Negative binomial regression of Millennium Cohort Study data, stratified by sex, associated female leisure Internet use at age 11 with mathematics ability at 17; use at least once a month showed "IRR = 1.249, 95%CI: 1.081–1.444"; no standardized effect size is printed.

> "Further, Internet use at least once a month (IRR  = 1.249, 95%CI: 1.081–1.444), once a week (IRR  = 1.186, 95%CI: 1.043–1.349), or most days (IRR  = 1.166, 95%CI: 1.030–1.319) were associated with 24.9%, 18.6% and 16.6% increase in mathematics ability score, respectively."

### Guan 2026 (2)

Guan, K., Zhang, Z., Liu, Z. et al. (2026). Longitudinal associations between sedentary behavior types and mathematics ability mediated by externalizing and internalizing problems. npj Sci. Learn. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00424-8

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Spearman correlation analysis of the same cohort data found Internet use had "no significant association with mathematics ability in either group (male: r = -0.040, female: r = -0.030)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "Notably, Internet use showed no significant association with mathematics ability in either group (male: r = -0.040, female: r = -0.030), and for males, listening to music had a null relationship with mathematics ability (r = -0.014)."
