Evidence¶

Duong et al. 2022¶

Mylien T. Duong, Larissa M. Gaias, Eric Brown, Sharon Kiche, Lillian Nguyen, Catherine M. Corbin, Cassandra J. Chandler, Joanne J. Buntain-Ricklefs, Clayton R. Cook. (2022). A Cluster Randomized Pilot Trial of the Equity-Explicit Establish-Maintain-Restore Program among High School Teachers and Students. School Mental Health. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12310-022-09516-3

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Unexpected-findings analysis in the pilot trial reports the reversed-direction effect for low-baseline conduct-problem students. The article notes post scores were "0.12 SD above the mean, well within the normative range".

"For instance, among students who scored low on conduct problems relative to their peers at baseline, students in E-EMR, compared to those in the control group, showed an increase in conduct problems."

Duong et al. 2022 (2)¶

Mylien T. Duong, Larissa M. Gaias, Eric Brown, Sharon Kiche, Lillian Nguyen, Catherine M. Corbin, Cassandra J. Chandler, Joanne J. Buntain-Ricklefs, Clayton R. Cook. (2022). A Cluster Randomized Pilot Trial of the Equity-Explicit Establish-Maintain-Restore Program among High School Teachers and Students. School Mental Health. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12310-022-09516-3

q1 · i? · causal · r2

Authors' interpretation, not a tested result: they suggest the unexpected effects may stem from the equity-explicit focus and pose open questions about acceptable trade-offs in equity work. No effect size is printed for this interpretation.

"We also found some unexpected effects, where high-performing and/or advantaged groups in the E-EMR condition had less favorable outcomes at post, compared to those in the control group, which may be a result of the equity-explicit focus of E-EMR."
