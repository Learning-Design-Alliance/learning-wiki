## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students who initially underperformed on clicker questions often showed the largest increases in performance from ICT to GCT after peer interaction, but some students' performance did not increase (e.g., one student in Group F), in one uncontrolled pre-post study. ## Evidence

### Ryan Sayer 2016

Ryan Sayer, Emily Marshman and Chandralekha Singh. (2016). The impact of peer interaction on the responses to clicker questions in an upper-level quantum mechanics course. [doi:10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2016.pr.071)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Per-student comparison of GCT vs ICT performance averaged across all comparison questions (Fig. 1), showing that with few outliers all students performed well on GCT regardless of ICT performance. The article notes "sometimes the peer interaction did not appear to help certain students (e.g., one of the students in Group F)".

> "Also, students who initially underperformed on clicker questions often benefited the most from interactions with their peers, as measured by the  difference in the average performance on GCT vs. ICT."
