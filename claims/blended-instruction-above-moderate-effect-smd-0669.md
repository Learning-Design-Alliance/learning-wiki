## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Across 142 effect sizes from experimental and quasi-experimental studies, blended instruction has an above-moderate positive effect on student performance compared with traditional teaching. [→ Li 2022](#li-2022)

## Evidence

### Li 2022

Li, B., Yu, Q., & Yang, F. (2022). The effect of blended instruction on student performance: A meta-analysis of 106 empirical studies from China and abroad. Best Evidence in Chinese Education, 10(2):1395-1403. https://doi.org/10.15354/bece.22.ar018

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 106 experimental and quasi-experimental studies published January 2000 to September 2021, pooling 142 effect sizes from an experimental group of 7064 students and a control group of 6733 students. The summary effect is "0.669", above Cohen's moderate benchmark of 0.5.

> "This study uses the standardized mean difference as effect value and the summary effect of 142 effect sizes is 0.669. Cohen (1992) prescribes that an effect size less than 0.2 means a weak effect, an effect size of around 0.5 signifies a moderate effect"
