Evidence¶

Leidig 2018¶

Leidig, T., Grünke, M., Urton, K., Knaak, T., & Hisgen, S. (2018). The Effects of the RAP Strategy Used in a Peer-tutoring Setting to Foster Reading Comprehension in High-Risk Fourth Graders. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 16(2), 231-253. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1194573

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Descriptive responder analysis of individual tutee gains from t1 to t3. All 12 group 1 tutees improved, while in group 2 three students scored lower, six gained at least two points, and one gained one point. A median split found 9 responders vs 3 nonresponders in group 1 and a 5-5 split in group 2.

"With respect to the differences between t3 and t1, all students in group 1 achieved better results in the reading comprehension tests after the whole intervention was completed."

Related Claims¶

  • RAP strategy instruction without a motivational system produced no noteworthy reading comprehension enhancement in either group during the first three weeks — related
  • Between-group differences in reading comprehension gains were not statistically significant despite group 1 showing larger effect sizes — related
  • Group 1 tutees showed smaller score variability at t3 than group 2, suggesting more uniform benefit from the longer intervention with mid-course motivational addition — related
  • RAP strategy instruction combined with a multicomponent motivational system produced significant reading comprehension gains with very large effect sizes in both peer-tutoring groups — related
