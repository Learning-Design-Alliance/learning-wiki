# The total context shift on ten transformed FCI questions is only +2.72%, so contextual shifts substantially cancel and low FCI scores cannot be attributed to context effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The context effect accumulated over ten restrictively transformed FCI questions is a statistically significant but small total shift of +2.72%, indicating cancellation rather than accumulation of individual question shifts. [→ Stewart et al. 2007](#stewart-et-al-2007)

## Evidence

### Stewart et al. 2007

John Stewart, Heather Griffin, and Gay Stewart. (2007). Context sensitivity in the force concept inventory. Physical Review Special Topics - Physics Education Research 3, 010102. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.3.010102

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired t-test on individual student total-score differences between the FCI and the 10-question FCIT, administered to 647 students over five semesters. The difference was "significant at the 1% level" with t(647) = 5.08, and the printed mean shift was +2.72% out of 100%. No effect size is printed.

> "This showed that the difference in total score was significant at the 1% level, t(647) = 5.08, p ⬍ 0.01."
