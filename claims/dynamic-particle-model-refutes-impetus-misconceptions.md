## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Students guided to propose a dynamic particle model with a non-zero x-direction force, and to compare it with the real video data, are compelled to rethink their assumption of a non-zero horizontal force. [→ Loo Kang WEE 2012](#loo-kang-wee-2012)
`q1 i?` Keying in positive fy values and seeing the resulting upward parabolic path helps students appreciate that the y-direction acceleration is constant and negative (gy = – 10.08 ms–2). [→ Loo Kang WEE 2012 (2)](#loo-kang-wee-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Loo Kang WEE 2012

Loo Kang WEE, Charles CHEW, Giam Hwee GOH, Samuel TAN, Tat Leong LEE. (2012). Using Tracker as a Pedagogical Tool for Understanding Projectile Motion. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

Authors' report of the section IV.A classroom activity: students key fx = 10 N into a dynamic particle model and compare its path with the real data; the mismatch, per the authors, "compels students to rethink their assumptions on non-zero force in x-direction".

> "The evidence of the incorrect model against the real data compels students to rethink their assumptions on non-zero force in x-direction"

### Loo Kang WEE 2012 (2)

Loo Kang WEE, Charles CHEW, Giam Hwee GOH, Samuel TAN, Tat Leong LEE. (2012). Using Tracker as a Pedagogical Tool for Understanding Projectile Motion. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1206.6489)

`q1 · i?` · `design · r2`

Section IV.B activity: students key in positive fy values and infer their naive impetus theory cannot be validated with the real video; the Cartesian convention "allows students to appreciate the negative sign of gy = – 10.08 ms–2 easily".

> "As the computer axis adopts the conventional Cartesian coordinate system, this allows students to appreciate the negative sign of gy = – 10.08 ms–2 easily as a positive fy results in a parabolic upwards path (Figure 6)."
