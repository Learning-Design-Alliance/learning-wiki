# The restudy advantage in Experiment 1 was not explained by longer exposure during the memory rating: restudy participants spent the least time on the rating.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i3` large
> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Rating-completion time was shortest in the restudy condition (M = 3067 ms) versus overt (M = 3657 ms) and covert (M = 3314 ms) retrieval, dz = 1.13 versus overt. [→ Higham 2023](#higham-2023)

## Evidence

### Higham 2023

Higham, P. A., Fastrich, G. M., Potts, R., Murayama, K., Pickering, J. S., & Hadwin, J. A. (2023). Spaced Retrieval Practice: Can Restudying Trump Retrieval? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r3`

Exploratory one-way between-subjects ANOVA on rating reaction times in Experiment 1 (n = 259), F(2, 516) = 34.78, p < 0.001, followed by corrected t-tests with printed effect size dz = 1.13 (restudy vs. overt): "participants spent less time completing the memory rating in the restudy condition (M = 3067 ms, SD = 862 ms)" than both retrieval conditions.

> "participants spent less time completing the memory rating in the restudy condition (M = 3067 ms, SD = 862 ms) than in either the overt (M = 3657 ms, SD = 1002 ms) or covert (M = 3314 ms, SD = 916 ms) retrieval conditions, t(258) = 18.20, p < 0.001, dz = 1.13"
