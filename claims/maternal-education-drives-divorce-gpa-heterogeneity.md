# The divorce–GPA heterogeneity by parental education is driven by maternal education, with no significant paternal-education interactions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The estimated reduction in GPA by having divorced parents was statistically significantly higher when maternal education was at ISCED 3–5, 6, or 7–8 levels compared to ISCED 0–2, after adjusting for paternal education and income. [→ Nilsen 2020](#nilsen-2020)
`q2 i?` No significant interaction effects between paternal educational levels and divorce on adolescents' GPA were observed while holding maternal education and income constant. [→ Nilsen 2020 (2)](#nilsen-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Nilsen 2020

Nilsen, S. A., Breivik, K., Wold, B., Askeland, K. G., Sivertsen, B., Hysing, M., & Bøe, T. (2020). Divorce and adolescent academic achievement: Heterogeneity in the associations by parental education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229183

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

OLS models with maternal and paternal education entered as separate predictors (Table 4; Fig 2A/2B). Maternal-education-by-divorce interactions were significant at all three levels above ISCED 0–2, "after adjustments for paternal education and income measures".

> "the estimated reduction in GPA by having divorced parents was statistically significantly higher when maternal educational levels were at secondary school levels (ISCED 3–5; b = -0.120, p < 0.05), at Bachelor’s levels (ISCED 6; b = -0.175, p < 0.05) and at Master’s or PhD-level (ISCED 7–8; b = -0.209, p < 0.05) compared to basic-level education (ISCED 0–2)"

### Nilsen 2020 (2)

Nilsen, S. A., Breivik, K., Wold, B., Askeland, K. G., Sivertsen, B., Hysing, M., & Bøe, T. (2020). Divorce and adolescent academic achievement: Heterogeneity in the associations by parental education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229183

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The same Table 4 models tested paternal-education-by-divorce interactions (e.g., ISCED 7–8 x divorce b = 0.096, non-significant). No paternal interaction reached significance, so equivalence was not tested.

> "There were, however, no significant interaction effects between paternal educational levels and divorce on the adolescents’ GPA while holding the effects of maternal education and income constant"
