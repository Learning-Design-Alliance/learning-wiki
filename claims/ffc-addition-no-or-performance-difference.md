# Adding fresh frozen cadaver training to the curriculum shows no significant difference in OR performance versus VR-plus-bench training

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For semi-rigid URS, no differences in OR performance were observed between FFC-exposed trainees (n = 9, Mean 70.1% ± 14) and VR-plus-bench trainees (n = 18, Mean 70.6%; p = 0.95); flexible URS comparison also insignificant (p = 0.79). [→ Aydın Abdullatif 2021](#aydn-abdullatif-2021)

## Evidence

### Aydın Abdullatif 2021

Aydın Abdullatif, Ahmed Kamran, Baig Umair, Raison Nicholas, Lantz Powers Andrea G., Macchione Nicola, Al-Jabir Ahmed, Abe Takashige, Khan Muhammad Shamim, Dasgupta Prokar, the SIMULATE Trial Contributors. (2021). The SIMULATE ureteroscopy training curriculum: educational value and transfer of skills. World Journal of Urology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00345-021-03604-w

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Secondary unpaired t-test comparison of OR OSATS performance between FFC (n = 9) and non-FFC groups; the flexible URS comparison was also insignificant (Mean 67.7% vs 69.9%, p = 0.79). Equivalence was not tested.

> "In terms of semi-rigid URS, no differences were observed in OR performance between trainees exposed to FFCs (n = 9, Mean: 70.1% ± 14) and those who trained using VR and bench models (n = 18, Mean 70.6%; p = 0.95)."
