# After two years, RSF Cohort 1 students generally performed at the same levels as propensity-score-matched comparison students, with few statistically significant differences

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Comparing RSF Cohort 1 students with matched comparison students from sending schools after 2 years yielded few statistically significant differences: School 4 scored 11 points higher in reading, while Schools 2 and 6 scored significantly below their controls in both reading and mathematics. [→ Young 2008](#young-2008)

## Evidence

### Young 2008

Young, V. M., Humphrey, D. C., Wang, H., Bosetti, K. R., Cassidy, L., Wechsler, M. E., Rivera, E., Murray, S., & Schanzenbach, D. W. (2008). Renaissance Schools Fund-Supported Schools: Early Outcomes, Challenges, and Opportunities. SRI International. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/renaissance-schools-fund-supported-schools-early-outcomes-challenges-and-opportunities

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Propensity score matching on ethnicity, lunch status, ELL status, and prior performance created comparison groups from sending schools; ISAT scale scores and standards-passing rates were compared after 2 years. School 4 also had "23 percentage points more students (59% versus 36%)" meeting reading standards; School 6 had lower passing rates in both subjects.

> "At the end of the second year of operation, School 4 students scored a statistically significant 11 points higher than their matched control group in reading. In contrast, School 2 and School 6 students’ performance was statistically significant and below their respective control groups in both reading and mathematics."
