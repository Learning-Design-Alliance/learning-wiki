# The effect of drama on social studies achievement does not differ significantly by duration of application (1–4 vs 5–10 weeks)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Between-group homogeneity testing showed no statistically significant difference in effect sizes between studies lasting 1–4 weeks (d = 0.906) and 5–10 weeks (d = 1.222) (QB=2.429 < X2=3.841; p=0.119). [→ Yildirim 2022](#yildirim-2022)

## Evidence

### Yildirim 2022

Yildirim, E., Tikman, F., & Senturk, M. (2022). Meta-analysis on the effectiveness of using the drama approach in social studies course. International Journal on Social and Education Sciences (IJonSES), 4(1), 17-33. https://doi.org/10.46328/ijonses.301

`q3 · i?`

Moderator analysis comparing 13 studies of 1–4 weeks (d = 0.906, moderate) with 25 studies of 5–10 weeks (d = 1.222, strong). The between-group test found "no statistically significant difference between the groups", so achievement did not change by duration.

> "Since the homogeneity value of QB=2.429, calculated in this study, was smaller than the accepted critical value of 3.841 (QB=2.429<X2=3.841; p=0.119>.05), it could be assumed that there was no statistically significant difference between the groups."
