# Explanations citing both the arithmetic operation and the operands' magnitudes are strongly associated with accurate direction of effect judgments, while unconditional operation rules are associated with very low accuracy

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 causal `r2`, 1 associational `r1` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Operation-and-operand explanations, though stated on only 26% of multiplication and 16% of division trials with operands below one, accompanied 65% of correct multiplication and 54% of correct division judgments. [→ Lortie-Forgues 2017](#lortie-forgues-2017)
`q3 i?` Unconditional operation explanations (e.g., multiplication makes bigger) were associated with less than 10% correct judgments on multiplication and division problems with operands below one. [→ Lortie-Forgues 2017 (2)](#lortie-forgues-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Lortie-Forgues 2017

Lortie-Forgues, Hugues and Siegler, Robert S (2017) Conceptual knowledge of decimal arithmetic. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 374-391. ISSN: 0022-0620. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000148

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Study 2 judgment-plus-explanation task; two raters classified explanations (91% agreement, Cohen's kappa = 0.85). "Despite this type of explanation being stated on only 26% of multiplication and 16% of division trials", operation-and-operand explanations accompanied most correct judgments.

> "Despite this type of explanation being stated on only 26% of multiplication and 16% of division trials with operands below one, it was advanced on 65% of trials with correct multiplication judgments and 54% of trials with correct division judgments."

### Lortie-Forgues 2017 (2)

Lortie-Forgues, Hugues and Siegler, Robert S (2017) Conceptual knowledge of decimal arithmetic. Journal of Educational Psychology. pp. 374-391. ISSN: 0022-0620. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000148

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the same Study 2 explanation analysis, "unconditional operation explanations were associated with very low accuracy" on multiplication and division problems with operands below one, reflecting the assumption that operand size is irrelevant to direction of effect.

> "In contrast, unconditional operation explanations were associated with very low accuracy on both multiplication and division problems with operands below one, less than 10% correct."
