## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Coding skills (90% of companies) and statistical data-analysis experience (90%) were the most commonly mentioned valued skills across the quantum industry, followed by laboratory experience (81%), electronics (76%), troubleshooting (71%), materials knowledge (67%), and quantum algorithms/computer science (62%). ## Evidence

### Michael F. J. Fox 2020

Michael F. J. Fox, Benjamin M. Zwickl, and H. J. Lewandowski (2020). Preparing for the quantum revolution - what is the role of higher education?. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020131](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020131)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview study of 21 U.S. quantum-industry companies asked about skills valued across job types; the most cited were "coding skills (90%) and experience with using statistical methods for data analysis (90%)". Percentages are company counts, not effect sizes.

> "Almost all the companies mentioned the importance of coding skills (90%) and experience with using statistical methods for data analysis (90%)."
