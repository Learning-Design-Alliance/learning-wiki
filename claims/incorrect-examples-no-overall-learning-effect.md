# Studying incorrect worked examples during algebra practice did not improve post-test learning overall relative to correct worked examples or problem-solving alone

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A 3(condition) x 2(time) repeated measures ANOVA found no main effect of condition on algebra post-test scores, providing no support for the hypothesis that incorrect examples improve learning overall. [→ Barbieri 2016](#barbieri-2016)

## Evidence

### Barbieri 2016

Barbieri, C. & Booth, J.L. (2016). Support for struggling students in algebra: Contributions of incorrect worked examples. Learning and Individual Differences, 48, 36–44. https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Support+for+struggling+students+in+algebra%22+Barbieri+Booth+incorrect+worked+examples. [doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2016.04.001)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Classroom experiment: 125 middle-school students randomly assigned within classroom to problem-solving (n=41), correct examples (n=44), or incorrect examples (n=40) worksheets over a systems-of-equations unit. The 3x2 RMANOVA showed Condition F(2,122)=1.040, p=.357; all students improved from pre- to post-test.

> "participants demonstrated improvement from pre- to post-test4 but this did not vary by condition providing no supporting evidence for  Hypothesis 1."
