## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In a randomized online fact-checking experiment, three of four politically biased assistants improved post-interaction veracity evaluations relative to the default assistant, with the somewhat Republican-leaning assistant improving accuracy by 9.5% (g = 0.23). [→ Lai 2025](#lai-2025)

## Evidence

### Lai 2025

Lai, S., Choi, J., Kim, J., Kunievsky, N., Potter, Y., & Evans, J. (2025). Biased AI improves human performance but reduces perceived helpfulness. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.09297)

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r3`

Randomized online experiment with 2,500 U.S. participants rating news headline veracity before and after conversing with GPT-4o assistants assigned randomized political stances. The somewhat Republican-leaning arm showed "9.5%" improvement over default (g = 0.23, adj. p = 0.001); the somewhat Democrat-leaning arm showed no detectable effect (adj. p = 0.777).

> "three of the four biased assistants improved veracity evaluations relative to the default—the somewhat Republican-leaning assistant by 9.5% (95% CI [2.9%, 16.6%]; Δ = 0.058, Hedges' g = 0.23, adj. p = 0.001)"
