## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Measured performance on a summative assessment did not differ significantly between the learner–content and learner–learner groups, supporting the substitutivity of interaction types. [→ Hauert 2026](#hauert-2026)

## Evidence

### Hauert 2026

Hauert, S., Luo, T., & Yen, C.-J. (2026). Assessing the impact of substituting interaction types: an empirical study of the interaction equivalency theory. Education Tech Research Dev, 74, 1–27. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-025-10563-1

`q4 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial (n = 66; 33 per group, no attrition) measuring performance with a 10-item, 50-point summative assessment of transfer and application. Means were M = 35.93 (SD = 9.54) for learner–content and M = 37.98 (SD = 8.56) for learner–learner; "Cohen's d was .23". Equivalence was not formally tested.

> "The two interaction groups did not differ in performance, t(64) = -0.92, p = .363. Cohen's d was .23, suggesting a small effect size (Cohen, 1992)."
