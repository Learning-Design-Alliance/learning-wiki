# The deviation of a correlation coefficient from unity varies inversely as the square of the variability of the group, per a corrected heterogeneity-adjustment formula (worked example 0.75 to 0.84)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Correcting a correlation from Grade VI to a combined five-grade group with larger σ raises the coefficient from 0.75 to 0.84 via the corrected formula. [→ Arthur S. Otis 1922](#arthur-s-otis-1922)
`q2 i?` The general rule stated by the author is that deviation from unity varies inversely as the square of group variability. [→ Arthur S. Otis 1922 (2)](#arthur-s-otis-1922-2)

## Evidence

### Arthur S. Otis 1922

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Correction section: an erratum to the author's May 1922 article on inferring change in a coefficient under changed heterogeneity; the example assumes Grade VI reliability 0.75 with σ 40 and combined-grade σ 50, yielding r'xv = 0.84.

> "Then r'xv = 1 - (1 - 0.75) ~t r'xv = 0.84."

### Arthur S. Otis 1922 (2)

Arthur S. Otis. (1922). The Method for Finding the Correspondence Between Scores in Two Tests. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0075112

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The article's closing mnemonic summarizing the corrected heterogeneity formula, given as the author's own interpretive statement rather than a tested result.

> "It may be remembered simply that the deviation of the coefficient from unity varies inversely as the square of the variability of the group."
