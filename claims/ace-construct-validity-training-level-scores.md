`q3 i?` Mean ACE scores rose sequentially across EBM-novice, EBM-intermediate and EBM-advanced cohorts (8.6, 9.5, 10.4 of 15; p < 0.0001), supporting construct validity. [→ Ilic Dragan 2014](#ilic-dragan-2014)

## Evidence

### Ilic Dragan 2014

Ilic Dragan, Nordin Rusli Bin, Glasziou Paul, Tilson Julie K, Villanueva Elmer. (2014). Development and validation of the ACE tool: assessing medical trainees' competency in evidence based medicine. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-14-114

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional study of 342 medical trainees (98 novice, 108 intermediate, 136 advanced) completing the ACE tool. Mean scores were 8.6 ± 2.4, 9.5 ± 1.8 and 10.4 ± 2.2, with "a statistically significant linear trend for sequentially improved mean score corresponding to the level of training". No effect size is printed.

> "A statistically significant linear trend for sequentially improved mean score corresponding to the level of training was observed (p < 0.0001)"
