## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Rankings of incorrect answers differed between the data sets for most items, contradicting the initial hypothesis of largely consistent rankings. [→ Trevor I. Smith 2024](#trevor-i-smith-2024)

## Evidence

### Trevor I. Smith 2024

Trevor I. Smith, Mohammad A. Kazmi, Richard R. Sarles, III, Joshua A. Sbrana, Cody W. Soper, and Nasrine Bendjilali. (2024). Comparing item response theory models for ranking incorrect response options. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.05231](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.05231)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Numerical comparison of NRM rankings across DS1full (N = 12,388) and DS2full (N = 9,875) for all 47 FMCE items. The article reports rankings of incorrect answers "differed between the data sets for most items".

> "however, the rankings of the incorrect answers differed be-tween the data sets for most items."

### Trevor I. Smith 2024 (2)

Trevor I. Smith, Mohammad A. Kazmi, Richard R. Sarles, III, Joshua A. Sbrana, Cody W. Soper, and Nasrine Bendjilali. (2024). Comparing item response theory models for ranking incorrect response options. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2410.05231](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.05231)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis attempting to identify consistent rankings by removing less commonly chosen responses. The article reports rankings for 22 items contained only two consistently-ranked responses and another 18 items had only three, which the authors call not very useful.

> "rankings for 22 of the items contained only two consistently-ranked responses (the correct and one incorrect), and rankings for another 18 items had only three consistently-ranked responses."
