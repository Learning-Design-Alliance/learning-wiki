Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science 2012¶

Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science. (2012). WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED580362

q1 · i? · qualitative · r1

This insight comes from the WestEd K-12 Alliance / CSU Long Beach / Montebello USD program at three K-4 schools with high English learner and low-income populations, as reported via Gomez-Zwiep et al. (2011). It is a practitioner-reported qualitative insight with no effect size.

"teachers learned that sentence frames often limit student responses (same frame yields similar responses), resulting in student work that does not show a range of student conceptual understanding"

Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science 2012 (2)¶

Equal Access to Content Instruction for English Learners: An Example from Science. (2012). WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED580362

q1 · i? · qualitative · r1

The report describes teacher-perception change in the Montebello program: "All participating teachers commented on the changes they have seen within their own teaching practices." This is self-reported qualitative evidence from the program's teachers; no effect size is printed.

"All participating teachers commented on the changes they have seen within their own teaching practices, most prominently in terms of raising their expectations of English learners. Teachers now focus on what students can do rather than what they cannot do."
