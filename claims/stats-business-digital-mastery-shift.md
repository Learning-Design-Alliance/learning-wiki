## Evidence

### Louise Starkey 2023

Louise Starkey, Anne Yates, Mairead de Roiste, Karsten Lundqvist, Adreanne Ormond, John Randal, Allan Sylvester. (2023). Each discipline is different: teacher capabilities for future-focussed digitally infused undergraduate programmes. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10196-2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The Statistics for Business case study describes pandemic-driven assessment change in one course: all assessment became "uninvigilated" and timing flexibility let students manage workload; the confidence benefit is stated as presumption, not measured.

> "Because remote learners could not easily be invigilated at scale, all assessment became uninvigilated. Students now do major tests on the device on which they learn, which presumably helps with confidence and performance, and greater flexibility in the timing of the assessment has enabled students to manage their workload to a greater extent than was previously possible."

### Louise Starkey 2023 (2)

Louise Starkey, Anne Yates, Mairead de Roiste, Karsten Lundqvist, Adreanne Ormond, John Randal, Allan Sylvester. (2023). Each discipline is different: teacher capabilities for future-focussed digitally infused undergraduate programmes. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10196-2

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

The case study describes applying approximations of practice (Grossman et al., 2009) so students observe, then practise with similar data toward mastery, with digital tools enabling self-paced practice and "immediate feedback" from AI; no outcome data are reported.

> "The digital tools enable students to do this in their own time, at their own pace using examples with Artificial Intelligence (AI) providing immediate feedback and differing next steps."
