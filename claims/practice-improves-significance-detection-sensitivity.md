# Practice on repeat survey attempts significantly improves sensitivity for Reference, Best Fit, and Lower n plots, with overall accuracy gains for Reference and Best Fit but not Lower n

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Among 101 students who retook the survey, sensitivity improved significantly for the Reference, Best Fit, and Lower n categories (OR=5.27, 2.98, and 4.51). [→ Aaron Fisher 2014](#aaron-fisher-2014)
`q3 i?` For Reference and Best Fit, sensitivity gains were not associated with significant specificity changes, indicating improved overall accuracy; for Lower n, specificity significantly decreased (OR=.163). [→ Aaron Fisher 2014 (2)](#aaron-fisher-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Aaron Fisher 2014

Aaron Fisher, G. Brooke Anderson, Roger Peng, Jeff Leek. (2014). A randomized trial in a massive online open course shows people don't know what a statistically significant relationship looks like, but they can learn. arXiv. [doi:10.7717/peerj.589](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.589)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of first versus second survey attempts among the 101 students who completed the quiz multiple times. "sensitivity improved signiﬁcantly" for Reference, Best Fit, and Lower n categories with odds ratios of 5.27, 2.98, and 4.51.

> "We found that sensitivity improved signiﬁcantly for the “Reference”, “Best Fit”, and “Lower n” categories (OR=5.27, 2.98, and , 4.51, with 95%CIs: 2.69-10.33, 1.28-6.92, and 1.79-11.37; Figure 2)."

### Aaron Fisher 2014 (2)

Aaron Fisher, G. Brooke Anderson, Roger Peng, Jeff Leek. (2014). A randomized trial in a massive online open course shows people don't know what a statistically significant relationship looks like, but they can learn. arXiv. [doi:10.7717/peerj.589](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.589)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the repeat-attempt analysis, Lower n specificity significantly decreased (OR=.163), so practice increased the odds of calling any graph significant rather than improving overall accuracy. For remaining categories there were no statistically significant changes in sensitivity or specificity.

> "In the “Lower n” plot category however, the increased sensitivity came at the cost of a signiﬁcant decrease in speciﬁcity (OR=.163, 95%CI: .059-.447)."
