# Weak instructional program coherence impairs sustained school improvement even when staff share a test-score goal

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` At Wilson school, a shared goal of raising test scores was the only common understanding; teachers used different materials and dealt with frustrations independently, showing how weak coherence can impair sustained improvement efforts. [→ Newmann 2001](#newmann-2001)

## Evidence

### Newmann 2001

Newmann, F. M., Smith, B., Allensworth, E., & Bryk, A. S. (2001). School Instructional Program Coherence: Benefits and Challenges. Consortium on Chicago School Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Case study of Wilson school from the 11-school Chicago Annenberg field study, based on classroom observations and teacher interviews. Despite a shared test-score goal, "Teachers have the same frustrations, but they deal with them independently in their classes within the context of the [test] goal," and teachers within and across grade levels used very different text series and materials.
