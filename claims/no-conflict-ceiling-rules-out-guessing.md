# No-conflict control accuracy remained at ceiling after training, arguing against guessing and a reversed-heuristic account

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Initial and final accuracy on no-conflict control problems was consistently high in both groups and blocks, ruling out systematic guessing or distrust of all intuitive responses. [→ Nina Franiatte 2024](#nina-franiatte-2024)

## Evidence

### Nina Franiatte 2024

Nina Franiatte, Esther Boissin, Alexandra Delmas, Wim De Neys. (2024). Boosting Debiasing: Impact of Repeated Training on Reasoning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101845

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

No-conflict trial analysis in Session 1: final-response accuracy was also high (M = 88.0% control, M = 92.3% training), which the authors state argues against a general guessing confound and against a "reversed heuristic" training account.

> "the no -conflict trials analysis revealed that performance was consistently at ceiling in pre- and post-intervention blocks for initial responses (M = 84.7%, SD = 29.3 in the control group; M  = 90.9%, SD = 11.6 in the training group)"
