# Kindergarten logical thinking correlates with arithmetic but does not explain unique variance once other predictors are included

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Logical thinking assessed in kindergarten correlated significantly with kindergarten and grade 1 arithmetic, but was not significant (p = .362) in the combined regression. [→ Praet 2013](#praet-2013)

## Evidence

### Praet 2013

Praet, M., Titeca, D., Ceulemans, A., & Desoete, A. (2013). Language in the prediction of arithmetic in kindergarten and grade 1. Learning and Individual Differences (manuscript LAEIND-D-12-OO174R1). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/LAEIND-D-12-OO174R1

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the third cross-sectional regression (F(6, 62) = 14.503, p < .001, R² = .608), logical thinking (Tedi-Math seriation and classification, alpha = .73) was non-significant (p = .362) despite significant bivariate correlations.

> "However when other variables were added to explore the combined effect of these predictors, logical thinking no longer explained a significant amount of variance in arithmetic skills among young children."
