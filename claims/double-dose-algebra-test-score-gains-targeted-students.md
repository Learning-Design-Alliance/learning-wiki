# Double-dose algebra improved algebra test scores for targeted students just below the cutoff, with a larger post-policy gain than for students just above the cutoff

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Students just below the national median cutoff scored .69 points higher in 2003 than their 2002 counterparts (effect size=.26), a larger improvement than the .47-point (effect size=.15) gain among students just above the cutoff. [→ Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2008](#takako-nomi-and-elaine-m-allensworth-2008)

## Evidence

### Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2008

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth. (2008). “Double-dose” Algebra as an Alternative Strategy to Remediation: Effects on Students’ Academic Outcomes. Consortium on Chicago School Research, University of Chicago. http://www.informaworld.com/smpp/content~db=all?content=10.1080/19345740802676739

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Regression discontinuity combined with interrupted time series on CPS cohorts; the discontinuity estimate for 10th-grade PLAN algebra test scores shows the below-cutoff gain exceeded the above-cutoff gain, with printed effect sizes of .26 versus .15 — "effect size=.26".

> "Students just below the cutoff scored .69 points higher in 2003 than their counterparts in 2002 (effect size=.26) while the pre- and post-policy difference among students just above the cutoff was .47 (effect size =.15)."
