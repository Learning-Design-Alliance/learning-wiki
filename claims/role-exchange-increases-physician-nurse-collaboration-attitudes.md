# Profession-role exchange produces larger gains in physician-nurse collaboration attitudes than the control IPE activity, including significantly more positive views of nurse's autonomy and physician's dominance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Total JSAPNC score increases were larger for medical and nursing students who experienced profession-role exchange (31.6% and 29.7%) than for control students (17.4% and 16.1%). [→ Wang J 2020](#wang-j-2020)
`q2 i?` After profession-role exchange, medical and nursing students showed significantly more positive attitudes toward Nurse's autonomy and Physician's dominance (p < 0.05), factors reflecting perceptions of professional role. [→ Wang J 2020 (2)](#wang-j-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Wang J 2020

Wang J, Guo J, Wang Y, Yan D, Liu J, Zhang Y, Hu X. (2020). Use of profession-role exchange in an interprofessional student team-based community health service-learning experience. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02127-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre/post survey of medical and nursing students using the JSAPNC in the same quasi-experiment; the Results text reports the percentage gains for exchange versus control groups. No effect size is printed.

> "the increases of the total score in the medical and nursing students who had experienced profession-role exchange (31.6 and 29.7%, respectively) was more markedly than those of the control students (17.4 and 16.1%, respectively)"

### Wang J 2020 (2)

Wang J, Guo J, Wang Y, Yan D, Liu J, Zhang Y, Hu X. (2020). Use of profession-role exchange in an interprofessional student team-based community health service-learning experience. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02127-z

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subscale analysis of the JSAPNC in the same study; the article reports significant post-intervention differences on the two role-perception factors at "p < 0.05". No effect size is printed.

> "medical and nursing students displayed significantly more positive attitudes toward “Nurse’s autonomy” and “Physician’s dominance” (p < 0.05), which are factors reflecting students’ perceptions of professional role"
