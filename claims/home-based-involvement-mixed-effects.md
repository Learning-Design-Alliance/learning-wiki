## Evidence

### Pomerantz et al. 2007

Eva M. Pomerantz, Elizabeth A. Moorman and Scott D. Litwack. (2007). The How, Whom, and Why of Parents' Involvement in Children's Academic Lives: More Is Not Always Better. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430305567

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of research on cognitive-intellectual involvement (e.g., reading with children, taking them to libraries and museums), including longitudinal work by Grolnick and colleagues and Senechal and LeFevre, finds it 'foreshadows enhanced achievement among children.'

> "On one hand, parents' home-based involvement geared toward children's intellectual enrichment not directly related to school foreshadows enhanced achievement among children."

### Pomerantz et al. 2007 (2)

Eva M. Pomerantz, Elizabeth A. Moorman and Scott D. Litwack. (2007). The How, Whom, and Why of Parents' Involvement in Children's Academic Lives: More Is Not Always Better. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430305567

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review reports that meta-analytic correlations for parents' homework involvement 'rang[e] from -.22 to .40,' and that several concurrent studies found homework assistance associated with poor performance. Pomerantz and Eaton's longitudinal work suggests reverse causation: children's poor performance predicted mothers' heightened assistance 6 months later.

> "Cooper's (1989) meta-analysis revealed correlations between parents' involvement in children's homework and children's achievement ranging from -.22 to .40."
