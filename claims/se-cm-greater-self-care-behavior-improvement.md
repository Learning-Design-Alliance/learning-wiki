## Evidence

### Ji H 2019

Ji H, Chen R, Huang Y, Li W, Shi C, Zhou J. (2019). Effect of simulation education and case management on glycemic control in type 2 diabetes. Diabetes/Metabolism Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.1002/dmrr.3112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In the randomized trial's 3.3 analysis, behavior was measured with a tailored 24-item DSCQ for a Chinese diabetic population. Table 3 reports between-group differences of "0.10 (0.04 to 0.15)" P = 0.001 for healthy diet, 0.06 P = 0.043 for activity, "0.32 (0.21 to 0.43)" P < 0.001 for self-monitoring, and "0.78 (0.53 to 1.03)" P < 0.001 for reducing risks.

> "Scores of healthy diet, physical activity, self‐monitoring of blood glucose, and risk reduction were significantly improved in the experimental group."

### Ji H 2019 (2)

Ji H, Chen R, Huang Y, Li W, Shi C, Zhou J. (2019). Effect of simulation education and case management on glycemic control in type 2 diabetes. Diabetes/Metabolism Research and Reviews. https://doi.org/10.1002/dmrr.3112

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same DSCQ analysis: medication-regimen behavior improved significantly in both groups, while problem-solving scores improved significantly only in the experimental group (P = 0.018 within group; between-group P = 0.694).

> "Scores for healthy diet, physical activity, self‐monitoring of blood glucose, and diabetes medication regimens, and reducing risks were significantly improved in both groups, whereas only in the experimental group were problem solving scores significantly improved by the treatment"
