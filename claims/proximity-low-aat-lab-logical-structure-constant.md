# With logical structure held constant, proximity improves accuracy of low-achieving participants by 28%

> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a controlled lab experiment, proximal questions improved accuracy among low-achieving participants but not high-achieving participants, with identical logical structure across versions. [→ Britt Hadar 2023](#britt-hadar-2023)

## Evidence

### Britt Hadar 2023

Britt Hadar, Maayan Katzir, Sephi Pumpian, Tzur Karelitz and Nira Liberman. (2023). Psychological proximity improves reasoning in academic aptitude tests. npj Science of Learning 8:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00158-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Lab experiment (N = 129 undergraduates; 112 with both covariates) using AAT questions rewritten in proximal/distal versions with the same logical structure, distance between participants. The printed interaction (B = 0.536, p = 0.022) indicated the proximal advantage among low achievers only.

> "an interaction between distance and AAT score, B = 0.536, SE = 0.235, z = 2.28, p = 0.022, indicated an advantage of proximal questions over distal questions among low-achieving participants but not among high-achieving participants."

### Britt Hadar 2023 (2)

Britt Hadar, Maayan Katzir, Sephi Pumpian, Tzur Karelitz and Nira Liberman. (2023). Psychological proximity improves reasoning in academic aptitude tests. npj Science of Learning 8:10. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00158-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Descriptive means from the same Study 2 experiment: for below-average-AAT participants, "accuracy with proximal questions ( M = 0.50) was 28% higher than with distal questions ( M = 0.39)"; no effect size was printed for this contrast.

> "The effect of distance for participants with below-average AAT score was quite sizeable: accuracy with proximal questions ( M = 0.50) was 28% higher than with distal questions ( M = 0.39)"
