# Video-didactic training is comparable to simulation-based training for teaching novices basic lung isolation skills

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2016 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` No differences were found between video-didactic and simulation-based groups after training in any lung isolation skill assessment. [→ Latif Rana K. 2016](#latif-rana-k-2016)

## Evidence

### Latif Rana K. 2016

Latif Rana K., VanHorne Edgar M., Kandadai Sunitha Kanchi, Bautista Alexander F., Neamtu Aurel, Wadhwa Anupama, Carter Mary B., Ziegler Craig H., Memon Mohammed Faisal, Akça Ozan. (2016). Teaching basic lung isolation skills on human anatomy simulator: attainment and retention of lung isolation skills. BMC Anesthesiology 16:7. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-015-0169-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized 1:1 comparison of 15 simulation-trained vs 15 video-trained novices. Post-training pass rates were 93% (DLT) and 87–100% (bronchial blocker) in both groups; the article reports "no differences between the Simulation-based and Video-didactic groups after the training".

> "Similarly, there were no differences between the Simulation-based and Video-didactic groups after the training in any of the lung isolation skills’ assessments (Fig. 2 and Table 2)."
