# Textbook circle representations of waves do not convey that wave speed is unchanging in a medium

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The common textbook representation of waves as a group of circles cannot give students an intuitive understanding that wave speed is unchanging in an underlying medium, leaving students likely to develop the misconception that a moving source changes wave speed. [→ Hangtian Lei 2025](#hangtian-lei-2025)

## Evidence

### Hangtian Lei 2025

Hangtian Lei. (2025). An intuitive model for illustrating the classical Doppler effect equation. The Physics Teacher. https://doi.org/10.1119/5.0245140

`q1 · i?` · `design · r1`

The author's interpretation, in the introduction, of the standard textbook illustration that uses circles to represent waves. The article offers this as a pedagogical judgment without empirical data: "Students are likely to have a misconception that the wave speed will be affected if the source is moving."

> "Such a representation cannot provide students with an intuitive understanding that the wave speed is unchanging in an underlying medium (e.g., the air). Students are likely to have a misconception that the wave speed will be affected if the source is moving."
