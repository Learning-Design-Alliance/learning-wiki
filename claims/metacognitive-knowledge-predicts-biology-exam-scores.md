`q3 i?` In OLS regression, ranking low-impact strategies (listening, rewatching videos) higher was associated with slightly lower exam scores, indicating that better strategy knowledge predicts better performance. [→ Williams Adrienne E. 2026](#williams-adrienne-e-2026)

## Evidence

### Williams Adrienne E. 2026

Williams Adrienne E., Denaro Kameryn. (2026). Studying with Friends in Introductory Biology Is Associated with Low Impact Study Strategies and No Improvement of Exam Scores. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.25-04-0074

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

OLS regression of total midterm scores on Week 0 strategy-effectiveness ratings, demographics, and high school GPA (n = 502; adjusted R² = 0.415). Students rating low-impact strategies higher scored slightly lower ("blisten = −0.06, p < 0.001"), supporting an association between metacognitive knowledge and performance.

> "Students who ranked low-impact strategies higher did slightly worse than students who ranked low-impact strategies lower (having someone explain concepts to me while studying: blisten = −0.06, p < 0.001; watching noncourse videos on the topic: bwatch other videos = −0.04, p = 0.008, re-watching course videos: brewatch course videos = −0.06, p = 0.001)."
