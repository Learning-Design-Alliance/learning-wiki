Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Umbrella review synthesis: reported team sizes ranged variously (e.g., 4–12; commonly 5–7 or 6–8 members) across three reviews; team formation and size were the most frequently studied TBL elements.

> "Additionally, a meta-analysis indicated that team size acted as a mediator of content knowledge outcomes, with smaller group sizes leading to better content knowledge outcomes [27]."

### Alizadeh Maryam 2024 (2)

Alizadeh Maryam, Masoomi Rasoul, Mafinejad Mahboobeh Khabaz, Parmelee Dean, Khalaf Rusul Jasim, Norouzi Ali. (2024). Team-based learning in health professions education: an umbrella review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06147-x

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Umbrella review synthesis: gender and experience were sometimes used to ensure heterogeneity; gender-based formation could negatively affect cohesive teams in some non-Western countries. Reimschisel's review reported peer evaluation was criticized for incomplete forms or identical ratings.

> "Regarding team formation methods, three reviews out of the five reported random or semi-random allocation as the dominant method [29, 41, 43]."
