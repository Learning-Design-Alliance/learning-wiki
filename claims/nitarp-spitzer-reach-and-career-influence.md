## Evidence

### Fitzgerald 2014

Fitzgerald, M.T., Hollow, R., Rebull, L.M., Danaia, L. and McKinnon, D.H. (2014). A Review of High School Level Astronomy Student Research Projects over the last two decades. Publications of the Astronomical Society of Australia. [doi:10.1017/pasa.2014.30](https://doi.org/10.1017/pasa.2014.30)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports limited evaluation of the Spitzer program before NITARP started, in which 32 teachers had been through the program and "A little over 100 students reported that the program had inﬂuenced them to pursue careers in science".

> "A little over 100 students reported that the program had inﬂuenced them to pursue careers in science, and 42 had entered Spitzer-related science-fair projects, including contributions that made it to the Intel Science Fair level."

### Fitzgerald 2014 (2)

Fitzgerald, M.T., Hollow, R., Rebull, L.M., Danaia, L. and McKinnon, D.H. (2014). A Review of High School Level Astronomy Student Research Projects over the last two decades. Publications of the Astronomical Society of Australia. [doi:10.1017/pasa.2014.30](https://doi.org/10.1017/pasa.2014.30)

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

A brief Spring 2014 survey of NITARP+Spitzer alumni with a 50% response rate, reported in Rebull et al. (2014), found "13,000 students have beneﬁtted from skills or resources the educator learned about via NITARP" and 4300 other educators reached.

> "the NITARP par- ticipants estimate that 13,000 students have beneﬁtted from skills or resources the educator learned about via NITARP, 21,000 students are taught by NITARP ed- ucators per year, and 4300 other educators have been reached with NITARP information."
