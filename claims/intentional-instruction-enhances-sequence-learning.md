## Evidence

### Dahm 2026

Dahm, S. F., Kraft, V., Martini, M., Krause, D. & Hermsdoerfer, J. (2026). Motor sequences resist automatization as attentional demands increase with sequence learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00412-y

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on the SRTT study; the Instruction*Sequence interaction showed intentional learning facilitated sequence learning (d = 0.5), which the authors describe as a tendency that detailed explicit knowledge facilitates learning of the sequence.

> "The significant Instruction*Sequence interaction, F (1, 83) = 1088, p < 0.001, ƞ2 p = 0.93, indicated that the difference between RTs in the Practice Sequence and the Control Sequence was significantly larger when the sequence was learned under intentional learning conditions ( Δ M ± SD = 87 ± 20 ms) than under incidental learning conditions ( Δ M ± SD = 77 ± 22 ms, p = 0.038, d = 0.5)."

### Dahm 2026 (2)

Dahm, S. F., Kraft, V., Martini, M., Krause, D. & Hermsdoerfer, J. (2026). Motor sequences resist automatization as attentional demands increase with sequence learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00412-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r?`

Free Generation Test at Posttest (N = 87), scored as practice minus control triplet matches in response and visual modalities. Intentional groups produced more explicit sequence knowledge than incidental groups, effect size ƞ2 p = 0.2.

> "Significantly more correct practice sequence triplets were generated when learning took place under intentional learning conditions (M ± SD = 6.2 ± 3.8) compared to incidental learning conditions (M ± SD = 2.9 ± 4.3), F (1, 83) = 20.2, p < 0.001, ƞ2 p = 0.2."
