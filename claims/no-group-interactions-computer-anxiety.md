## Evidence

### Onah BI 2023

Onah BI, Ede MO, Eze BN, Uzoegwu CR, Mgboji C, Agbo GC, Uzuagu AU, Chiamagu C, Agbo PA, Oneli JO, Ogbueghu SN. (2023). Effectiveness of rational emotive behavior education (REBE) on computer anxiety among school teachers enrolled in e-learning. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000034303

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction analysis from the randomized controlled trial reported in Table 2; the article prints the test statistics and reports no significant group-by-gender interaction at either posttest or follow-up.

> "Concerning the interaction effect of groups and gender at the posttreatment and follow-up levels, Table 2 reveals that there was no significant interaction effect in the mean computer anxiety score among teachers, F (1101) = .558, P = .457, ηp2 = 0.006; and F (1101) = 0.932, P = .337, ηp2 = 0.011, respectively."

### Onah BI 2023 (2)

Onah BI, Ede MO, Eze BN, Uzoegwu CR, Mgboji C, Agbo GC, Uzuagu AU, Chiamagu C, Agbo PA, Oneli JO, Ogbueghu SN. (2023). Effectiveness of rational emotive behavior education (REBE) on computer anxiety among school teachers enrolled in e-learning. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000034303

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction analysis from the same trial's Table 2; the article reports no significant group-by-working-experience interaction at posttest or follow-up, printing only test statistics.

> "Concerning the interaction effect of groups and working experiences at the posttreatment and follow-up levels, Table 2 reveals that there was no significant interaction effect in the mean computer anxiety score among teachers, F (1101) = 0.772, P = .513, ηp2 = 0.026; and F (1101) = 0.320, P = .811, ηp2 = 0.011, respectively."
