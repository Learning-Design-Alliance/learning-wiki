# DI-PS significantly outperformed PS-DI on the two supplier-reasoning problem-solving criteria (d = .60 and d = .64)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` The DI-PS group significantly outperformed the PS-DI group in arguing for or against the specific suppliers, with large effect sizes on both reasoning criteria. [→ Sabrina Ludwig 2026](#sabrina-ludwig-2026)

## Evidence

### Sabrina Ludwig 2026

Sabrina Ludwig, Andreas Rausch, Michelle Taub. (2026). Effects of instructional design, instructional preferences, and cognitive load on problem solving and knowledge acquisition in a computer-based office simulation. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2025.102255

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

T-tests on problem-solving performance sub-categories in the supplier selection scenario, scored by two trained raters (average ICC of .83). The evaluation criteria '3.2 correct reasoning supplier 1' and '3.3 correct reasoning Supplier 2' showed "significant differences (p = .01 and p = .01, respectively)" with d = .60 and d = .64 favouring DI-PS.

> "We found significant differences (p = .01 and p = .01, respectively) and large effect sizes (d = .60 and d = .64 respectively). This means that the DI-PS group significantly outperformed the PS-DI in arguing for or against the specific suppliers."
