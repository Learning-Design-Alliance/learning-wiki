# Receiving instruction before problem solving improves procedural fluency more than problem solving before instruction when both phases are otherwise identical

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students who received instruction before problem solving scored significantly higher on procedural fluency than students who solved problems first (η² = 0.04). [→ Loibl 2020](#loibl-2020)

## Evidence

### Loibl 2020

Loibl, K., Tillema, M., Rummel, N., & van Gog, T. (2020). The effect of contrasting cases during problem solving prior to and after instruction. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-020-09504-7

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r3`

ANCOVA in a randomized 2×2 experiment (N = 181, prior mathematics score as covariate) on the concept of variance. The instruction-first conditions outperformed problem-solving-first on procedural fluency, "F(1, 176) = 7.92, p = 0.01, η2 = 0.04".

> "students performed significantly better on procedural fluency when they received instruction before problem solving (M = 1.34, SD = 0.68), compared to students who engaged in problem solving before receiving instruction (M = 1.02, SD = 0.76), F(1, 176) = 7.92, p = 0.01, η2 = 0.04"
