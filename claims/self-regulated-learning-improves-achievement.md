# Self-regulated learning improves achievement

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3`–`q4` · `i1`–`i2`

Learners who plan, monitor, and evaluate their own learning — and who are explicitly taught to do so — achieve better outcomes than learners who study the same material without these self-regulatory processes.

## Subclaims

`q4 i2` Across 84 intervention studies in primary and secondary school, training students in self-regulated learning produced an average effect of 0.69 on academic performance, strategy use and motivation combined, with larger effects when researchers rather than regular teachers delivered the training and in mathematics than in reading/writing. [→ Dignath & Büttner 2008](#dignath-buttner-2008)

`q3 i1` In elementary and secondary students, measured self-regulated learning is only weakly correlated with academic achievement (metacognitive processes r = 0.20; cognitive strategies r = 0.11), and the correlation varies by process, subject, grade level and how SRL and achievement are measured. [→ Dent & Koenka 2016](#dent-koenka-2016)

## Evidence

### Dignath & Büttner 2008

Dignath, C., & Büttner, G. (2008). Components of fostering self-regulated learning among students. A meta-analysis on intervention studies at primary and secondary school level. *Metacognition and Learning, 3*(3), 231–264. [doi:10.1007/s11409-008-9029-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-008-9029-x)

`q4 · meta-analysis of intervention studies` · `i2 · medium-to-large effect, average ES = 0.69` · `n=84 studies (357 effect sizes)`

Two separate meta-analyses pooled self-regulated learning training studies: 49 with primary school students and 35 with secondary school students, 357 effect sizes in all. Outcomes were academic performance, strategy use and motivation, and the 0.69 average covers all three together. The abstract does not give a separate figure for achievement. Meta-regression found larger effects when researchers, not regular teachers, delivered the training, and in mathematics compared with reading/writing or other subjects. The theory behind the programme and the type of strategy taught had different effects at the two school levels. Read as abstract only.

### Dent & Koenka 2016

Dent, A. L., & Koenka, A. C. (2016). The relation between self-regulated learning and academic achievement across childhood and adolescence: A meta-analysis. *Educational Psychology Review, 28*(3), 425–474. [doi:10.1007/s10648-015-9320-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-015-9320-8)

`q3 · meta-analysis of correlational studies` · `i1 · small correlation, r = 0.20 (metacognitive processes), r = 0.11 (cognitive strategies)` · `n=?`

Two meta-analyses of elementary and secondary school students related achievement to (1) metacognitive processes of self-regulated learning and (2) use of cognitive strategies. Both overall correlations were small: r = 0.20 for metacognitive processes and r = 0.11 for cognitive strategies. The correlations differed significantly by the specific process or strategy, academic subject, grade level, type of SRL measure and type of achievement measure. This is the correlational arm of the claim. It supports a real but modest association, not a causal effect. The number of studies was not in the abstract. Read as abstract only.

## Discussion

Self-regulated learning (SRL) is not a single technique but a cycle of forethought (goal setting, strategic planning), performance (self-monitoring, self-control), and self-reflection (self-evaluation, adaptive adjustment). The claim covers both (a) correlations between learners' existing self-regulatory skill and achievement, and (b) causal effects of explicitly training or scaffolding SRL strategies. These are distinct claims with distinct evidence bases: correlational studies cannot rule out that stronger students self-regulate more, while training studies vary widely in dosage, duration, and whether strategies are taught generically or embedded in a specific domain.

Key moderators to establish when evidence is added: the learner's age and prior achievement (younger and lower-achieving learners typically have the most room to gain from SRL instruction, because they default to passive rehearsal); whether support is a standalone curriculum or woven into subject-matter instruction (embedded support usually transfers better); and whether the strategies taught are actually appropriate to the task — teaching monitoring strategies without accompanying control strategies can leave learners aware of their confusion but unable to act on it. SRL also interacts with motivation: strategies are only deployed when learners value the goal and expect success, which links this claim to motivational mechanisms such as autonomy and competence support (see [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) and [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation](autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md)).

Boundary conditions expected from the broader literature: benefits shrink for highly expert learners who already regulate effectively, and poorly designed strategy training (short, decontextualized, or focused on surface behaviors like highlighting) can produce no gain or even displace more effective study time. Measurement is a further open question — much of the literature relies on self-report questionnaires, which correlate only modestly with trace or online measures of actual regulatory behavior, so the correlational arm of this claim may look stronger or weaker depending on how SRL is operationalized. The theoretical framing lives in [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md).

**Evidence status.** No studies are currently recorded on this page. Before this claim can be rated, evidence is needed for at least two distinct subclaims: (1) the correlational association between measured SRL skill and achievement, and (2) experimental or quasi-experimental effects of SRL strategy training on achievement outcomes, ideally distinguishing embedded from standalone instruction. Until then, treat the claim as plausible but unverified within this wiki.
