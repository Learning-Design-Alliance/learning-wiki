# A 90-minute teaching intervention with 3D-printed height models increased correct determination of voltages in simple and series circuits among year-8 students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For a simple circuit with one light bulb, students giving all three correct voltage values rose from 5% in the pre-test to 37% in the post-test, and 73% coloured the circuit correctly according to potential values. [→ Bodensiek 2018](#bodensiek-2018)
`q2 i?` For a series circuit with two equal resistive elements, identification of a 3 V voltage drop at each light bulb rose from 29% to 63%, and correct determination of all four voltages rose from 3% to 29%. [→ Bodensiek 2018 (2)](#bodensiek-2018-2)

## Evidence

### Bodensiek 2018

Bodensiek, O., Sonntag, D., Glawe, I., & Müller, R. (2018). 3D-Printable Height Models for DC Circuits. Originally published 2006. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1805.02522](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1805.02522)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Exploratory case study of the 3D-printed height-model intervention (N=59, two year-8 courses, pre-post design, no control group). The quoted results are the printed percentages for the simple-circuit test item, including "All three correct values were given by only 5% before and by 37% after the intervention."

> "In the pre-test 75% believed the source voltage of 4.5 V to be the voltage between all three pairs of points in the circuit. After the teaching intervention this answer was given by only 5% of the students. All three correct values were given by only 5% before and by 37% after the intervention."

### Bodensiek 2018 (2)

Bodensiek, O., Sonntag, D., Glawe, I., & Müller, R. (2018). 3D-Printable Height Models for DC Circuits. Originally published 2006. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1805.02522](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1805.02522)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same exploratory pre-post case study (N=59), printed results for the series-circuit test item: the intervention shows rising correct-answer percentages, e.g. "29% identified a voltage drop of 3 V at each light bulb before the intervention and 63% afterwards."

> "A similar learning gain can be observed for a series circuit with two equal  resistive elements, see Figure 5, where 29% identified a voltage drop of 3 V at each light bulb before the intervention and 63% afterwards. The correct determination of all four voltages (A -B, B-C, C-D, D-E) was only achieved by 3% before, but by 29% afte r the intervention."
