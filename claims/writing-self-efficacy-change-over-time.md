## Evidence

### Kim M. Mitchell 2023

Kim M. Mitchell, Sharon Zumbrunn, Danielle N. Berry, Lisa Demczuk. (2023). Writing Self-Efficacy in Postsecondary Students: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09798-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of the 20 studies (40%) assessing change over time reports significant pre-to-post improvement in most single-term studies; three studies did not reach significance and two found decreases from pre to post term.

> "Most studies found that writing self-efficacy demonstrated statistically significant improvement in a context where writing self-efficacy scores were assessed at the beginning and the end of a single term of study"

### Kim M. Mitchell 2023 (2)

Kim M. Mitchell, Sharon Zumbrunn, Danielle N. Berry, Lisa Demczuk. (2023). Writing Self-Efficacy in Postsecondary Students: a Scoping Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09798-2

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

The review's synthesis of the four longitudinal studies with three or more time points reports improvement early on that flattened or declined by a third measurement; in one cohort, self-efficacy dropped significantly two years after a writing course ended before rising again.

> "Rather than continuing to increase over time once a course ends, writing self-efficacy seems to consistently drop when students move into a new course or new writing context"
