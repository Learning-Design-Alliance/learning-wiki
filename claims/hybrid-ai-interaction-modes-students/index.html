Martin Lnenicka and Petr Coufal 2026¶

Martin Lnenicka and Petr Coufal. (2026). Navigating AI in STEM: what secondary students actually do with generative AI-driven tools. International Journal of STEM Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40594-026-00637-8

Survey item B5 on interaction modes in the n = 416 sample. The article reports "A smaller but still substantial group reported that AI solves the task for them ( n = 85)", and interprets hybrid use as operational pragmatism, with verification-only use aligning with self-regulated learning practices.

"Students most frequently reported a hybrid approach, with the majority selecting I combine multiple methods ( n = 211)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Secondary students most frequently use generative AI for step-by-step theoretical explanation and for checking their own solutions, more than for calculations
  • Students exercise ethical agency in GenAI use through human-AI collaboration strategies, personal ethical frameworks, strategic calibration of use, and resistance to over-reliance
  • Reviewed literature reports risks of overreliance, AI errors in complex tasks, and students' uncritical acceptance of AI-generated outputs
  • Student reflections identify speed and scaffolding as the dominant theme, with verification burden, overreliance worries, and prompt precision also prevalent
  • Student-AI exchanges in an undergraduate research methods course are empirically classifiable into three interaction paths, with Passive Review most frequent (46%) and Strategic Dialogue 36% — related
