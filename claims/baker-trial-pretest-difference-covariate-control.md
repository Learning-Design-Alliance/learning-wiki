# Random assignment in the Baker (1997) trial produced pretest differences favoring the comparison curriculum, controlled by using the pretest as a covariate

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In the Baker (1997) randomized trial, students in the two Transition Math comparison classrooms scored 5.3 points higher on average on the math pretest than students in the two Expert Mathematician classrooms; the pretest was used as a covariate in posttest analyses. ## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2006

What Works Clearinghouse. (2006). WWC Intervention Report: The Expert Mathematician, Middle School Mathematics, Revised October 14, 2006. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/InterventionReport/513

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Baseline characteristic of the randomized controlled trial of 90 eighth-grade students randomly assigned to The Expert Mathematician (n = 45) or Transition Math (n = 45). The report states "the pretest was used as a covariate in the posttest analyses" to control for post-attrition pretest differences, and attrition rates were similar across groups.

> "Differences in a math pretest, administered at the start of the school year, favored students in the two Transition Math classrooms, who scored 5.3 points higher on average than students in the two Expert Mathematician classrooms."
