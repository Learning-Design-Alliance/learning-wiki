## Subclaims
`q3 i?` College physics subjects trained with a hierarchical organization scored highest on composite complex-task scores; treatment differed significantly overall, and in a priori t-tests the hierarchical group beat the single-level-twice group, which in turn beat the single-level-once group (no direct hierarchical versus single-level-once test is reported). ## Evidence

### Eylon 1979

Eylon, Bat-Sheva; Reif, F. (1979). Effects of Internal Knowledge Organization on Task Performance. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED171804

`q3 · i? · no effect size printed; the entry gives only the ANOVA test, F(2,27) = 10.84, p < 0.001` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1: 36 paid volunteers from an introductory college physics course, blocked by ability and randomly assigned to hierarchical (H), single-level once (S1), or single-level twice (S2) treatments. A two-way ANOVA on composite complex-task scores showed statistically significant treatment effects [F(2,27)=10.84, p<0.001], A priori one-tailed t-tests showed H significantly better than S2 (t = 2.68, df = 21, p < 0.01) and S2 significantly better than S1 (t = 1.96, df = 27, p < 0.05); no direct H-versus-S1 test is reported. Scores on "local" tasks did not differ significantly between treatments.

> "Consistently, subjects in the II-treatment performedbetter than subjects in the S2-treatment, who performed better than (or equivalently to) subjects in the S1-treatment."
