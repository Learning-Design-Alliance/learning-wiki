## Evidence

### Alexandru Maries 2018

Alexandru Maries, Nafis I. Karim, and Chandralekha Singh. (2018). Is agreeing with a gender stereotype correlated with the performance of female students in introductory physics? Physical Review Physics Education Research 14, 020119. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.020119

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of over 1800 calculus-based and over 1600 algebra-based students who rated agreement with the statement "According to my own personal beliefs, I expect men to generally perform better in physics than women" on a five-point Likert scale; agreement was 7%–13% across courses and genders.

> "Only 7% –13% of algebra-based and calculus-based students (regardless of their gender) agreed with this gender stereotype. Thus, it appears that this stereotype was not very common amongst college introductory physics students."

### Alexandru Maries 2017

Alexandru Maries, Nafis I. Karim, and Chandralekha Singh. (2017). The impact of stereotype threat on gender gap in introductory physics. 2017 PERC Proceedings. https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.059

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey of over 1100 students in first-semester algebra- and calculus-based introductory physics courses, who rated agreement with the statement "I expect men to generally perform better in physics than women" on a five-point Likert scale. Descriptive percentages only; no effect size or test is printed.

> "Only approximately 8% of algebra-based students and 10% of calculus-based students agreed with the stereotype."
