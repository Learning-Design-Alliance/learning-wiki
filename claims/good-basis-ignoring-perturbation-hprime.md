# Students determining a good basis often focus only on diagonalizing H0 and do not consider the perturbation H′

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r?` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person · **overdue for a recheck since 2025**: baselines and conditions change

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Some students incorrectly claimed the uncoupled representation forms a good basis for step 1 of the weak field Zeeman effect because it diagonalizes H0, without checking whether the perturbation is diagonal in each degenerate subspace. [→ Emily Marshman 2020](#emily-marshman-2020)

## Evidence

### Emily Marshman 2020

Emily Marshman, Christof Keebaugh, and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). Student difﬁculties with ﬁnding the corrections to the energy spectrum of the hydrogen atom for the strong and weak ﬁeld Zeeman effects using degenerate perturbation theory. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2006.07144)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r?`

Written responses and interviews in advanced QM courses. Students considering only H0 in the weak-field step 1 "did not give considera- tion to ˆH ′ when ﬁnding a good basis"; interviews suggest they did not realize H′fs is not diagonal in each degenerate subspace of H0 in the uncoupled representation, yielding incorrect values inconsistent with experiments.

> "Students with this type of difﬁculty focused on the bases that make ˆH 0 diagonal but did not give considera- tion to ˆH ′ when ﬁnding a good basis."
