# In Grade 3 only, higher initial fall status was associated with larger within-year growth, suggesting baseline gaps may widen

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The correlation between initial status and linear growth was negative in Grades 1 and 2 but positive in Grade 3 (student-level r = .20, school-level r = .14). [→ Hiroyuki Yamada 2024](#hiroyuki-yamada-2024)

## Evidence

### Hiroyuki Yamada 2024

Hiroyuki Yamada. (2024). New Normal in Early Elementary Mathematics Learning: Part III - Learning from Variation in Grade 3. E4 Center, Northwestern University. https://e4.northwestern.edu/2024/12/19/new-normal-in-early-elementary-mathematics-learning-part-iii-learning-from-variation-in-grade-3/

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Grade 3 hierarchical growth model of the same longitudinal cohort; Table 1 random effects print student-level r = .20 and school-level r = .14 for the fall-score/linear-growth correlation, described as "small but substantial".

> "The correlations in Grades 1 and 2 were negative; however, those in Grade 3 were positive. Their size was small but substantial (especially the correlation at the student level). The positive correlation means that the higher initial status is associated with larger growth over the school year."
