## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Authors' observations during three treatment sessions showed experimental-group students enthusiastic, actively questioning and competitive, while many control-group students avoided discussion and were embarrassed to answer. [→ Supandi 2018](#supandi-2018)

## Evidence

### Supandi 2018

Supandi, S., Waluya, B., Rochmad, R., Suyitno, H., & Dewi, K. (2018). Think-Talk-Write Model for Improving Students' Abilities in Mathematical Representation. International Journal of Instruction, 11(3), 77-90. https://doi.org/10.12973/iji.2018.1136a

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Authors' qualitative class observations during the treatment (students' activities of both groups were observed three times); this is the authors' interpretation, not a tested result. They conclude experimental students "had greater self-confidence" than control students.

> "Based on the authors’ class observations during the learning process with the think -talk-write strategy, many students were enthusiastic about learning mathematics, and actively presented questions and competitively addressed the problems and answers."
