## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Every student interviewed on the ellipse problem spontaneously considered the special case a = b (a circle) to evaluate answers, but no students spontaneously checked the extreme case a → 0 without interviewer prompting. [→ Mark Eichenlaub 2019](#mark-eichenlaub-2019)

## Evidence

### Mark Eichenlaub 2019

Mark Eichenlaub, Edward F. Redish. (2019). Blending physical knowledge with mathematical form in physics problem solving. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2018. [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-04627-9_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-04627-9_6)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the interview study, all students spontaneously used the special case a = b for the ellipse problem, while "No students, on the other hand, spontaneously checked the extreme case a → 0" without prompting. When prompted, most used the extreme case correctly.

> "every student interviewed on the ellipse problem (Figure 2) considered the special case a = b, a circle, and used it to evaluate the given answers. No students, on the other hand, spontaneously checked the extreme case a → 0, however, when prompted by the interviewer to consider "a long, skinny ellipse", most did use this extreme case to answer the question correctly."
