# Student-reported engagement, instructor enthusiasm, and helpful feedback improved across a semester in both trained and untrained sections, suggesting rapport effects independent of UDL training

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in all sections, intervention and control, reported more active engagement, more instructor enthusiasm, and more helpful feedback at semester end, so these changes cannot be attributed to UDL training. [→ Davies 2013](#davies-2013)

## Evidence

### Davies 2013

Davies, P. L., Schelly, C. L., & Spooner, C. L. (2013). Measuring the Effectiveness of Universal Design for Learning Intervention in Postsecondary Education. Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability, 26(3), 195-220. http://accessproject.colostate.edu/udl

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Controlled pre-post survey analysis; Table 2 shows Q2 rose significantly in both groups (intervention t = 5.15; control t = 6.11, both p < .002), and Q33/Q34 rose significantly only in the control group. The authors interpret this as familiarity and rapport developing across the semester.

> "students in all course sections, both intervention and control groups, reported that they were more actively engaged in learning at the end of the semester compared to the beginning. Students in all sections also reported that their instructors expressed their personal enthusiasm more at the end of the semester compared to the beginning."
