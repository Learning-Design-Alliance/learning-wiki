## Evidence

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The authors cite this correlational finding from the original CLASS work to argue the category structure captures distinct, useful information; no coefficient is printed in this comment.

> "When analyzing who becomes a physics major, the strongest correlation is with the Personal Interest category, rather than the complete survey score or any other category."

### Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams 2014 (2)

Carl E. Wieman and Wendy K. Adams. (2014). On the Proper Use of Statistical Analyses; a Comment on "Evaluation of Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey" by Douglas et al. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.10.020128)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Data from Table VIII of the original CLASS paper, covering four course types at two universities. The authors state there is "a three to four fold increase in sensitivity to gender differences" in the Personal Interest category alone.

> "The difference between the Overall scores for males and females range from 2 to -13%; however, in every case the difference between male and female views in the Personal Interest category was substantially greater, ranging from -11 – -34%."
