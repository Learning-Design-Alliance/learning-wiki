# Students' grades are a more important predictor of college enrollment than ACT scores, which mainly constrain the selectivity of colleges attended

## Evidence

### Jenny Nagaoka 2008

Jenny Nagaoka, Melissa Roderick, and Vanessa Coca. (2008). Barriers to College Attainment: Lessons from Chicago. The Consortium on Chicago School Research at The University of Chicago / Center for American Progress. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/barriers-college-attainment-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

A series of multivariate analyses estimated differences among Chicago graduates by ACT score and unweighted GPA in likelihood of enrolling in colleges of varying selectivity. Grades predicted enrollment across all outcomes; the article reports significance without effect sizes.

> "Students' grades emerge as a more important predictor of college enrollment than ACT scores. Among students with similar ACT scores, students with higher GPAs were significantly more likely to enroll in college, in four-year colleges, and in selective or very selective four-year colleges."

### Jenny Nagaoka 2008 (2)

Jenny Nagaoka, Melissa Roderick, and Vanessa Coca. (2008). Barriers to College Attainment: Lessons from Chicago. The Consortium on Chicago School Research at The University of Chicago / Center for American Progress. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/barriers-college-attainment-lessons-chicago

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

The same multivariate analyses found poor test performance did not significantly lower overall college enrollment but constrained students' range of college options toward two-year and nonselective institutions.

> "Students with ACT scores lower than 17 had only slightly lower probabilities of enrolling in college than their classmates with ACT scores over 24. However, these students were much more likely to enroll in two-year colleges instead of four-year colleges, and few enrolled in selective or very selective four-year colleges."
