# Child-centered instruction positively predicts children's self-monitoring accuracy and, for second graders, restudy effectiveness

## Evidence

### Mariëtte H. van Loon 2021

Mariëtte H. van Loon, et al. (2021). Connecting teachers' classroom instructions with children's metacognition and learning in elementary school. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-020-09248-2

`q2 · i2`

Multilevel regression of instructional practices in the 21-classroom sample (Table 5c, 5b). Child-centered practices predicted self-monitoring accuracy β = .23, p = .019; the grade interaction on restudy effectiveness was β = .32, p = .021, with no significant main effect on restudy (β = −.10, p = .172).

> "Interestingly and in contrast to teacher-directed instructions, child-centered instructions were positively related to children's self-monitoring accuracy. That is, in classrooms where teachers gave more child-centered instructions, children more accurately discriminated between well-learned and less-learned items."
