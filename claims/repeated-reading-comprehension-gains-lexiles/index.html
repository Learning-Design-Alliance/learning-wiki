Evidence¶

Still 2012¶

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Scholastic Reading Inventory pre-post lexile data for the 12 treatment students over the 12-week repeated peer reading intervention. The article reports "the students who did increase improved by at least 50 lexiles"; no effect size printed.

"six of the students’ lexile scores increased and one was the exact same. The other five students’ lexile score decreased. However, the students who did increase improved by at least 50 lexiles."

Still 2012 (2)¶

Still, K. L., & Flynt, C. A. (2012). Does Repeated Reading Improve Reading Fluency and Comprehension for Struggling Adolescent Readers? JAASEP, Winter 2012. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1135781

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Author's comparison of SRI lexile gains between the small-group treatment class and the co-teach control class, from the Discussion of Results section. The comparison is descriptive; no statistical test or effect size is printed.

"The treatment group participants who improved their comprehension had a larger increase in lexile numbers than those students from the control group who increased their comprehension."
