# A brief BLS training session with real-time feedback improves future schoolteachers' CPR knowledge, raising self-declared ability to perform CPR from 58% to 100%

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After brief theory plus hands-on training with real-time feedback, the proportion of participants declaring they knew how to perform CPR on an adult rose from 58% to 100%, and knowledge of the compression/ventilation ratio rose from 62.2% to 99%. ## Evidence

### Jorge-Soto Cristina 2019

Jorge-Soto Cristina, Abilleira-González Maite, Otero-Agra Martin, Barcala-Furelos Roberto, Abelairas-Gómez Cristian, Szarpak Łukasz, Rodríguez-Núñez Antonio. (2019). Schoolteachers as candidates to be basic life support trainers: A simulation trial. Cardiology Journal. https://doi.org/10.5603/CJ.a2018.0073

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Pre-post survey of the same 98-participant convenience sample before and after the 30-min theory and 1-h hands-on training. The article reports "a figure that increased to 100% after training"; post-training, 99% knew the ratio, 92.9% the rate and 85.7% the depth. No effect size is printed.

> "Fifty-eight percent declared to know how to perform CPR on an adult victim, a figure that increased to 100% after training. More than half of participants (62.2%) knew the correct chest compressions/ventilations ratio in an adult"
