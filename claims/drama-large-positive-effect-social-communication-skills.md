## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Across 24 studies analyzed with a random effects model, drama method showed a positive and large effect (g = 0.93) on social communication skills. [→ Batdi 2020](#batdi-2020)

## Evidence

### Batdi 2020

Batdi, V. & Elaldi, S. (2020). Effects of drama method on social communication skills: A comparative analysis. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 6(3), 435-457. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1258528

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analysis of 24 studies (2 articles, 19 MA theses, 3 Ph.D. dissertations) using the Comprehensive Meta-Analysis and MetaWin programs with Hedges' d. The REM effect size was 0.93, "positive and large according to Cohen‟s (1992) classification"; the z-test value was 6.022.

> "The effect size was 0.93, positive and large according to Cohen‟s (1992) classification, and therefore, it can be remarked that drama method had a positive effect on social communication skills."
