# The evidence that PBL improves specialist transplant training outcomes is limited by single-institution quasi-experimental design, unblinded assessment, and possible Hawthorne effects

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors state the quasi-experimental design without randomization introduces possible selection bias and unmeasured confounding, and that a new PBL curriculum may have produced Hawthorne effects contributing to improved outcomes independent of the instructional method. ## Evidence

### Gao C 2026

Gao C, Li J, Shui K, Xie X, Peng L. (2026). Effectiveness of Problem-Based Learning (PBL) in specialist physician training for kidney transplantation. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000047684

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Authors' own stated limitations in the Discussion: single institution, quasi-experimental design without randomization, unblinded examiners, educational-measure-only outcomes, and possible Hawthorne effects limiting internal validity.

> "the implementation of a new PBL curriculum may have been associated with increased instructor enthusiasm, heightened trainee attention, and potentially greater allocation of educational resources, which could have contributed to improved outcomes independent of the instructional method itself (Hawthorne effect)"
