## Evidence

### Bozmaoğlu Beyza 2026

Bozmaoğlu Beyza, Yaprak Emre, Gündoğdu Ahmet Doğukan, Akansel Yasemin Turan, Alvur Tuncay Müge. (2026). Comparative evaluation of knowledge levels and attitudes regarding periodontal health and periodontal disease among internal medicine physicians and dentists in Turkey: a cross-sectional survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09365-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Cross-sectional descriptive survey (April–December 2021, Turkey) of 240 participants via a content-validated online questionnaire; the IMS group (n = 107) was compared with dentists (n = 133) using chi-square, Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney U tests. The article reports "significantly lower knowledge levels compared to dentists" and that after Bonferroni correction "14 of 22 comparisons remained statistically significant".

> "Internal medicine specialists/residents demonstrated significantly lower knowledge levels compared to dentists across most periodontal knowledge domains (p < 0.05)."

### Bozmaoğlu Beyza 2026 (2)

Bozmaoğlu Beyza, Yaprak Emre, Gündoğdu Ahmet Doğukan, Akansel Yasemin Turan, Alvur Tuncay Müge. (2026). Comparative evaluation of knowledge levels and attitudes regarding periodontal health and periodontal disease among internal medicine physicians and dentists in Turkey: a cross-sectional survey study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09365-7

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression on total knowledge score (0–22) adjusting for age, sex, marital status and years since graduation; the adjusted group effect was "virtually unchanged from the unadjusted model (B = 4.804)" and the model explained 42.9% of variance (R² = 0.429).

> "After adjusting for age, sex, marital status, and years since graduation, being a dentist was associated with 4.65 points higher knowledge (B = 4.647, 95% CI: 3.86–5.44, p < 0.001)"
