# Among 131 students answering both problems in a work-on-ramp isomorphic pair, correct-answer rates fell from 65% on the frictionless ramp work problem to 27% on the rough-ramp problem

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Of 131 students answering both problems, 65% answered the frictionless ramp work problem correctly but only 27% answered the rough-ramp problem correctly, even though the frictional force was irrelevant to the question asked. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Effect of Misconception on Transfer in Problem Solving. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07686)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Written multiple-choice responses from 131 introductory students on the paired bookstore box-on-ramp work problems. The paper reports 65% correct on the frictionless problem versus 27% on the rough ramp; interviews suggested students conflated work done by the person with total work and insisted friction must make the work different.

> "Out of the 131 students who answered both questions, 65% and 27% provided the correct responses to questions (5) and (6), respectively."
