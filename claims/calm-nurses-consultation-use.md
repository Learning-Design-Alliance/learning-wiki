# Nurses in CALM used more consultation than CALM-R nurses, but consultation use was not associated with adherence ratings or child outcomes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` CALM nurses used more consultation than CALM-R nurses, yet no significant differences were found in adherence/competence ratings or CGI-S outcomes between nurses who did and did not use consultation. [→ Golda S. Ginsburg 2019](#golda-s-ginsburg-2019)

## Evidence

### Golda S. Ginsburg 2019

Golda S. Ginsburg, Kelly L. Drake, Michela A. Muggeo, Catherine E. Stewart, Paige J. Pikulski, Di Zheng & Ofer Harel (2019): A pilot RCT of a school nurse delivered intervention to reduce student anxiety, Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2019.1630833

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Feasibility comparison in the pilot RCT (Table 4): more CALM nurses used consultation (75% vs 61.54%), and consultation minutes differed between conditions (p = .042). The article reports "Nurses in CALM, compared to CALM-R, used more consultation."

> "Nurses in CALM, compared to CALM-R, used more consultation."

### Golda S. Ginsburg 2019 (2)

Golda S. Ginsburg, Kelly L. Drake, Michela A. Muggeo, Catherine E. Stewart, Paige J. Pikulski, Di Zheng & Ofer Harel (2019): A pilot RCT of a school nurse delivered intervention to reduce student anxiety, Journal of Clinical Child & Adolescent Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1080/15374416.2019.1630833

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analyses of consultation effects: adherence/competence ratings did not differ by consultation use (t(13) = 1.71, p = .112), nor did post CGI-S (t(49) = −0.63, p = .531) or follow-up CGI-S (t(45) = −0.06, p = .953). No effect sizes were printed.

> "There was no signiﬁcant difference in the overall adherence/competence ratings for nurses who used consultation ( M = 2.95, SD = 0.36) and nurses who did not (M = 2.47, SD = 0.71), t(13) = 1.71, p = .112."
