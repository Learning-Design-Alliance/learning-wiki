# Expert categorization of upper-level quantum mechanics problems is based primarily on the process involved (measurement or time evolution) rather than on fundamental principles

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In quantum mechanics, faculty categorization of problems is primarily based on the process involved — measurement or time evolution in the absence of measurement — and secondarily on change of basis, unlike introductory physics where categorization follows fundamental principles. [→ Giacomo Zuccarini 2019](#giacomo-zuccarini-2019)

## Evidence

### Giacomo Zuccarini 2019

Giacomo Zuccarini. (2019). Analyzing the structure of basic quantum knowledge for instruction. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.04231](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.04231)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Secondary analysis of the Lin and Singh categorization study, in which 22 physics juniors and seniors and six faculty categorized 20 QM problems; the author regrouped the best-scoring categories, with Measurement placed in 10 problems and Time-Evolution in 9, Change of Basis in 4, Spin in 1 and Symmetry Argument in 1 (Table I). The analysis shows categorization is "primarily based on the process involved".

> "A pattern emerges: while in introductory physics categorization is typically based on the fundamental principles used in order to solve problems (e.g., conservation principles and Newton's laws), in QM it is primarily based on the process involved (either measurement or time evolution in the absence of measurement)"
