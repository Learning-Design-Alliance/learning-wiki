## Evidence

### Jourdi Georges 2025

Jourdi Georges, Selmi Mayssa, Gaussem Pascale, Truchot Jennifer, Margaill Isabelle, Siguret Virginie. (2025). Evaluation of the Inverted Classroom Approach in a Case-Study Course on Antithrombotic Drug Use in a PharmD Curriculum: French Monocentric Randomized Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/67419

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Six-question stress self-assessment completed at the beginning of the course by control (n=93) and IC (n=65) groups, compared with chi-square test; no significant difference on any question.

> "Although approximately 50% of the students reported sleep disorders and a feeling of fear during the week preceding the in-class session, no significant difference was observed between the 2 groups for any of the above-mentioned parameters."

### Jourdi Georges 2025 (2)

Jourdi Georges, Selmi Mayssa, Gaussem Pascale, Truchot Jennifer, Margaill Isabelle, Siguret Virginie. (2025). Evaluation of the Inverted Classroom Approach in a Case-Study Course on Antithrombotic Drug Use in a PharmD Curriculum: French Monocentric Randomized Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/67419

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Three empathy items rated on a 7-point Likert scale at the end of the course by both groups; opinions were quite similar despite only IC students assuming the instructor role, with no difference between groups.

> "Approximately 30% of the students considered that it is hard for the instructor to perceive things as the students while 50% confirmed the importance of the sense of humor of the instructor in enhancing students’ performance and of reading the students’ minds through nonverbal communication and body language. These results did not differ between both groups."
