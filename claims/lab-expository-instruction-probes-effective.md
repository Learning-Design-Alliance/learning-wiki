## Evidence

### Eva Erdosne Toth 2000

Eva Erdosne Toth, David Klahr, and Zhe Chen. (2000). Bridging Research and Practice: A Cognitively Based Classroom Intervention for Teaching Experimentation Skills to Elementary School Children. Cognition and Instruction, 18(4), 423–459. https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1804_1

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

This article's summary of the laboratory training study (87 second through fourth graders randomly assigned to training–probe, no-training–probe, or no-training–no-probe conditions) that provided the basis for the classroom intervention. The article attributes the result to Chen and Klahr (1999).

> "Only children in the training–probe condition increased their CVS knowledge significantly across the four phases in the laboratory study conducted by Chen and Klahr (1999); that is, expository instruction combined with probes led to learning, whereas neither probes alone nor unguided exploration did so."

### Eva Erdosne Toth 2000 (2)

Eva Erdosne Toth, David Klahr, and Zhe Chen. (2000). Bridging Research and Practice: A Cognitively Based Classroom Intervention for Teaching Experimentation Skills to Elementary School Children. Cognition and Instruction, 18(4), 423–459. https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1804_1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Grade-moderated transfer from the same summarized laboratory study: third and fourth graders transferred to near domains, but only fourth graders retained the skill on a 7-month posttest compared to untrained fourth graders; second graders dropped back to baseline in transfer phases.

> "the third and fourth graders who participated in expository instruction successfully transferred their newly acquired CVS skills to near transfer domains, whereas only fourth graders were able to retain the skill and show significantly better CVS performance (as compared to untrained fourth graders) on the paper-and-pencil posttest administered 7 months later."
