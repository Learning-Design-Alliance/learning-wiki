# Young women outperformed young men in every grading category family in unweighted points, with the largest gap in assignments and smallest in assessments; weighting reversed this ordering

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In unweighted points the gender difference was largest in assignments (6.13) and smallest in assessments (3.47); after weights were applied, assessments showed the largest difference (4.06) and assignments near zero (0.11), with a total weighted difference of 4.11 points. [→ Easton 2023](#easton-2023)
`q2 i?` With weights applied, the total weighted gender difference was 4.11 points (77.98 vs. 73.87), approximating the 0.33 grade-point gap. [→ Easton 2023 (2)](#easton-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Easton 2023

Easton, J.Q. & Diaz, B. (2023). Lasting differences: Math grades and gender. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/lasting-differences

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive Gradebook analysis of unweighted points for 29,229 students: assignments 80.93 vs. 74.8, behavior 87.64 vs. 82.9, assessments 75.04 vs. 71.57. The report states the assignments difference "was almost twice the gender difference in assessments."

> "The gender difference in assignments (largest difference) was almost twice the gender difference in assessments (smallest difference; see Figure 6. )"

### Easton 2023 (2)

Easton, J.Q. & Diaz, B. (2023). Lasting differences: Math grades and gender. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/lasting-differences

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Weighted-points analysis of the same Gradebook data: assessments 4.06 (42.87 vs. 38.81), behavior 0.18, assignments 0.11. The report notes "young women's grades were 4.11 (77.98 vs. 73.87) higher in total weighted points."

> "Figure 7 shows that, on average, young women's grades were 4.11 (77.98 vs. 73.87) higher in total weighted points on a 100-point scale."
