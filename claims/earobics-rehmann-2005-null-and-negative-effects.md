# In an Anchorage RCT with severe differential attrition, Earobics® effects on alphabetics were not statistically significant and one subtest showed a substantively important negative effect

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2009

What Works Clearinghouse. (2009). WWC Intervention Report: Earobics®. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://www.earobics.com

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

RCT at a Title I school in Anchorage, Alaska, with severe differential attrition (14 of 80 discontinued), leaving 66 students; intervention was Earobics® Step 1 for 20 minutes, three days a week, for 10 weeks. Study average effect size was –0.01.

> "Rehmann (2005) found that the overall Earobics® effect across the three of four alphabetics measures of beginning reading (DIBELS: Initial Sound Fluency, Letter Naming Fluency, and Phoneme Segmentation Fluency) was not statistically significant."

### What Works Clearinghouse 2009 (2)

What Works Clearinghouse. (2009). WWC Intervention Report: Earobics®. U.S. Department of Education, What Works Clearinghouse. https://www.earobics.com

`q2 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Grade 1 subsample (n = 35) in the same trial; Appendix A3.1 reports a mean difference of –9.50 and an effect size of –0.35 favoring the comparison group on DIBELS Nonsense Words Fluency.

> "For one subtest (Nonsense Words Fluency), the WWC determined that the negative effect was substantively important (that is, an effect size with an absolute value of at least 0.25)."
