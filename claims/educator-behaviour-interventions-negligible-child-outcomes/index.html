Related Claims¶

  • Universal school-based SEL programs improve students' behaviour, with a small and, in recent randomised trials, negligible effect on academic achievement — related
  • Improvements in caregiver book-sharing behaviour mediate the intervention's impact on child language (one mediation analysis) — reports the opposite
  • Effects on secondary behavioural outcomes (conduct problems, delinquency, substance use) are negligible or non-significant — related
  • SEL interventions improve attitudes, self-regulation, behaviour, and academic performance across reported effect-size ranges — related
  • Measures of individual children's experiences within the ECEC group predict child development outcomes with higher effect sizes than global quality measures — related
  • Test-based Quality Rating and Improvement Systems have been costly, inefficient, and subject to manipulation, while collaborative quality-improvement approaches are emerging — related
  • In-service professional development for ECEC teachers produces a medium improvement in externally rated process quality (ES = 0.68) — related
  • Global ECEC quality indices show little evidence of relating to child outcomes — related
  • Prior meta-analyses report few or weak associations between ECEC quality indices and child progress, with process quality more predictive than structural quality — related
