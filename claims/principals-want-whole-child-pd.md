# Elementary principals most frequently want professional development on whole child education, with rural principals especially likely to want physical and mental health content

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Principals most often wanted content on supporting students’ social-emotional development (83%), developing systems supporting children’s physical and mental health (82%), and creating an environment that develops responsible young people (76%). [→ Levin 2020](#levin-2020)
`q2 i?` Significantly more rural principals (87%) than city principals (70%) wanted professional development on developing systems to support children’s physical and mental health. [→ Levin 2020 (2)](#levin-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Levin 2020

Levin, S., Leung, M., Edgerton, A. K., & Scott, C. (2020). Elementary school principals’ professional learning: Current status and future needs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/professional-learning-principals

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive survey results on wanted professional development (Figure 4); all 23 content options were wanted by a majority, with managing school operations least requested at 51%.

> "principals reported their need for content on leading schools in supporting students’ social-emotional development (83%), developing systems supporting children’s physical and mental health (82%), creating an environment that develops responsible young people (76%), redesigning school organization/structure for deeper learning (75%)"

### Levin 2020 (2)

Levin, S., Leung, M., Edgerton, A. K., & Scott, C. (2020). Elementary school principals’ professional learning: Current status and future needs. Palo Alto, CA: Learning Policy Institute. http://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/professional-learning-principals

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Subgroup analysis by community type (Figure 5) reported in the findings section; the article states other community-type differences on whole child items were not statistically significant.

> "Significantly more principals in rural communities (87%) wanted professional development on developing systems to support children’s physical and mental health compared with principals in cities (70%)"
