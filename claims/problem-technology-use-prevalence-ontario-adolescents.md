## Evidence

### Sampasa-Kanyinga 2022

Sampasa-Kanyinga, H., Hamilton, H. A., Goldfield, G. S., & Chaput, J.-P. (2022). Problem Technology Use, Academic Performance, and School Connectedness among Adolescents. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 19(4), 2337. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19042337

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sectional analysis of self-reported data from 4837 students in grades 9 to 12 (mean age 15.9 years; 49.5% females) from the 2019 Ontario Student Drug Use and Health Survey. The article reports that "35.8% of students used their screen device for at least 5 h a day" and prevalence of moderate-to-serious problem use was "higher in females (22.4%) than males (14.9%)".

> "We found that 35.8% of students used their screen device for at least 5 h a day and about 18.6% had moderate-to-serious symptoms of problem technology use, a prevalence that was higher in females (22.4%) than males (14.9%)."
