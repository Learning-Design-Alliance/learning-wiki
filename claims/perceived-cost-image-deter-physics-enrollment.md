## Evidence

### Samina S. Masood 2014

Samina S. Masood. (2014). How to Control a Decrease in Physics Enrollment?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1405.6442](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1405.6442)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Qualitative analysis based on the author's interaction with students from culturally diverse backgrounds. She argues that financial pressures push students toward quick-learning strategies and away from involved courses, noting that "the image of Physics being a difficult subject" eclipses its attraction.

> "Most of the domestic US students work part- time or even full-time. They do not have any financial sharing or family support system and have to worry a bout paying their bills and supporting their families during their college education."

### Samina S. Masood 2014 (2)

Samina S. Masood. (2014). How to Control a Decrease in Physics Enrollment?. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1405.6442](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1405.6442)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r1`

The author's problem-identification section attributes declining enrollment to the time and rigor demanded by physics and mathematics relative to their job-market payoff, so most students take introductory physics only as a required service course.

> "well-accepted subj ects that are very involved such as mathematics and Physics are losing their popularity because it needs more commitment of time, focused study, regularity, rigorous training and a real aptitude. Above all, these difficult programs do not lead to high-enough paying jobs."
