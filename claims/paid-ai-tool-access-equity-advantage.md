# Staff raised equity concerns that students who pay for upgraded GenAI tools gain an advantage over peers using free versions

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Unequal access to paid versus free GenAI tools was perceived to create a tiered system advantaging students who can afford more capable tools. [→ Perkins 2026](#perkins-2026)

## Evidence

### Perkins 2026

Perkins, M., Postma, D., Roe, J., Sisay, S., & Holdcroft, C. (2026). 'A bit of chaos and madness': The AI Assessment Scale and the work of assessment reform. Preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26729

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Theme 5 (Ethics in Practice) of the focus-group study. Staff compared paid AI access to disparities in affording books, framing equity of access as essential to responsible adoption alongside critical questioning of AI outputs.

> "One participant warned that “the student that has access to and has paid for the upgraded version of ChatGPT, definitely has an advantage over the person who is using the free version… you’re making it a tiered education system”"
