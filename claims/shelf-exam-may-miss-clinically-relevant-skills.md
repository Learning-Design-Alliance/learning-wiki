## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Post-rotation surveys identified skills rated poorly learned (e.g., delivering bad news, lumbar puncture, neurological exam without references) that were outside the virtual rotation's objectives, leading the authors to question the shelf exam's validity for clinically relevant performance. ## Evidence

### Rybinnik I 2024

Rybinnik I, Pal G, Farrukh H, Deverett B, Przydzial P, Mani R. (2024). Impact of a 2-week flipped classroom virtual neurology clerkship versus a traditional 4-week rotation on NBME shelf exam scores. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-06239-8

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Optional anonymous online quality-assurance surveys completed by 41 of 68 students at the conclusion of the virtual rotation, using 5-point Likert items and open-ended comments. The survey was descriptive, reporting self-perceptions rather than tested outcomes.

> "Students reported learning the following skills poorly or not at all, despite assigning medium-to-high importance to them: delivering bad news to patients and families"
