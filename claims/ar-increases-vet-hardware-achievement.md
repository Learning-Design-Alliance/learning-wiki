## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Undergraduate students who learned motherboard assembly with the HardwareAR application scored significantly higher on the computer hardware achievement test than students who used print textbooks (U=164, p=.026). [→ Mustafa Sirakaya and Ebru Kilic Cakmak 2018](#mustafa-sirakaya-and-ebru-kilic-cakmak-2018)

## Evidence

### Mustafa Sirakaya and Ebru Kilic Cakmak 2018

Mustafa Sirakaya and Ebru Kilic Cakmak. (2018). Effects of Augmented Reality on Student Achievement and Self-Efficacy in Vocational Education and Training. International Journal for Research in Vocational Education and Training (IJRVET). https://doi.org/10.13152/IJRVET.5.1.1

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Quasi-experimental pre-test post-test study with matched pairs randomly assigned; 23 experimental and 23 control students. Posttest achievement means were 67.61 (AR) versus 57.39 (control); the Mann Whitney U-test showed "a signiﬁcant diﬀerence in achievement" (U=164, p=.026).

> "Table 3 points to a signiﬁcant diﬀerence in achievement between the experimental group which took the courses with AR application and the control group which used the as-sembly manual (U=164, p <.05)."
