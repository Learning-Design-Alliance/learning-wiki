# PTA traits show stronger statistical explanatory power than dialogue-act and Bloom's taxonomy coded baselines on StudyChat

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Hunter McNichols 2026

Hunter McNichols, Kai Du, Andrew Lan. (2026). PRINCIPAL TRAIT ANALYSIS: TOWARDS DERIVING "SKILLS" IN HUMAN-AI COLLABORATION. A preprint - August 13, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11460 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.11460](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2608.11460)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

StudyChat-only comparison against two coded feature sets: 8 broad dialogue-act categories and 6 revised-Bloom's-taxonomy levels scored with the same model and scale. Table 1 shows non-significant gains (dialogue acts +0.046 p=.373 Fall, +0.045 p=.158 Spring; Bloom's +0.041 p=.368, +0.032 p=.283).

> "We further observe that the added explanatory power of the principal traits is more statistically powered than coded dialogue act or Bloom's taxonomy baselines. This observation suggests that baseline coding schemes are either too specific to explain overall behavior in the case of dialogue acts and too general in the case of Bloom's."
