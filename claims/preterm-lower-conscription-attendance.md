## Evidence

### Sejer EPF 2024

Sejer EPF, Ladelund AK, Bruun FJ, Slavensky JA, Mortensen EL, Kesmodel US. (2024). Preterm birth and subsequent intelligence and academic performance in youth: A cohort study. Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand. https://doi.org/10.1111/aogs.14796

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Descriptive and logistic regression results from the Danish register cohort (Tables 2 and 3); conscription appearance p < 0.001, adjusted OR 0.47 (0.39–0.56) for extremely preterm vs. term. The article reports attendance "ranging from 90.1% if born extremely preterm to 95.9% if born at term" for LSE and 52.6%–75.0% for conscription.

> "Table 2 shows a statistically significant association between preterm birth and lower academic performance, with children born extremely preterm having the lowest likelihood of graduating from LSE (ranging from 90.1% if born extremely preterm to 95.9% if born at term), USE (40.6%–55.4%), and appearing before the conscription board (52.6%–75.0%)."
