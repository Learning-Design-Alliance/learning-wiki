Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Self-management interventions targeted math calculation skills most often, with mean effect sizes of 2.66 for social studies, 2.28 for reading, 1.97 for math, and 1.13 for writing — related
  • Effects on secondary behavioural outcomes (conduct problems, delinquency, substance use) are negligible or non-significant — related
  • Academic self-management interventions for students with emotional and behavioral disorders show a large overall mean effect size of 1.80 across 22 studies — related
  • One study found the intervention effect on conduct problems was stronger for youth at greater risk of developing conduct problems. — related
  • Adolescent substance use is associated with low academic outcomes, delinquency, and risky sexual behaviors, per cited prior work — related
  • SEL interventions improve attitudes, self-regulation, behaviour, and academic performance across reported effect-size ranges — related
  • Positive developmental relationships and school connectedness protect against violence, absenteeism, and substance abuse, and increase threat reporting — related
  • Most preschool SR/EF interventions report at least one significant outcome, but only about half of evaluated effects are significant — related
  • School-only substance use interventions such as HeadOn and SUPEP improved knowledge and attitudes without corresponding behavioural change — related
  • No school-based intervention model for students already using substances was found: all examined models were preventive or protective in approach — related
