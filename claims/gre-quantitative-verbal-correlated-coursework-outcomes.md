## Evidence

### Burmeister Jay 2014

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Retrospective correlational analysis of 108 medical physics graduate students (2004-2011, one institution), analyzed with t-tests, ANOVA, and linear regression. The quantitative score was "correlated with both course average (p=0.003) and exit exam score (p=0.003)" but not with faculty rating or first-year GGPA.

> "The GRE quantitative score, while not statistically correlated to faculty rating (p=0.345) or first‐year GGPA (p=0.467), was correlated with both course average (p=0.003) and exit exam score (p=0.003)."

### Burmeister Jay 2014 (2)

Burmeister Jay, McSpadden Erin, Rakowski Joseph, Nalichowski Adrian, Yudelev Mark, Snyder Michael. (2014). Correlation of admissions statistics to graduate student success in medical physics. Journal of Applied Clinical Medical Physics 15(1):375-385. https://doi.org/10.1120/jacmp.v15i1.4451

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Same retrospective cohort correlational analysis of admissions statistics against four performance metrics. The authors report "the correlation between GRE verbal score and course average (p=0.048), first‐year GGPA (p=0.030), and exit exam (p=0.009)", a result they found surprising.

> "More surprising was the correlation between GRE verbal score and course average (p=0.048), first‐year GGPA (p=0.030), and exit exam (p=0.009)."
