## Evidence

### Li Zhien 2026

Li Zhien, Asoodar Maryam, Keulers Tom, Joosten-ten Brinke Desirée, Dolmans Diana. (2026). Ensuring Knowledge Transfer: The Critical Role of “What-If” Discussions in Virtual Patient Simulation. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2363

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Group interview thematic analysis (n = 8). Students described comparing cases as feeling “similar to a “what-if” exercise” and that discussing feedback helped them “correct ourselves and help each other understand why it was like that”. Perceptions are self-reported, not performance data.

> "By discussing similarities and differences between cases, students reported becoming more aware of the underlying principles guiding their clinical reasoning, which they perceived as helpful when approaching new cases."
