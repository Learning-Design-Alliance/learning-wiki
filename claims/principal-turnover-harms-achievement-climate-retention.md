# Principal turnover is associated with lower student achievement, worse climate, and higher teacher attrition, with effects lasting up to 5 years

## Evidence

### Darling-Hammond 2026

Darling-Hammond, L., Fitz, J., Giani, M. V., Gordon, M. F., & Wechsler, M. (2026). The principal effect: How investing in school leaders is key to solving education's challenges. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/644.197

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports that after a principal's departure "student test scores typically go down, both in terms of their overall levels and rates of growth," with negative effects lasting up to 5 years and larger in high-poverty, low-achievement schools.

> "The negative effects of principal turnover can last up to 5 years and tend to be larger in high-poverty schools and schools with a history of low student achievement."

### Darling-Hammond 2026 (2)

Darling-Hammond, L., Fitz, J., Giani, M. V., Gordon, M. F., & Wechsler, M. (2026). The principal effect: How investing in school leaders is key to solving education's challenges. Learning Policy Institute. https://doi.org/10.54300/644.197

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Longitudinal studies in Texas and New York City reported in the review found internal hires or experienced successors "seemed to buffer some of the negative impacts of turnover." In New York, external hires raised teacher turnover and sharply dropped trust, order, and vision ratings; these effects were not observed for internally promoted principals.

> "researchers found that replacing a principal with either an internal hire, such as an assistant principal, or someone with prior administrative expertise seemed to buffer some of the negative impacts of turnover, including teacher turnover and negative impacts on school climates."
