Evidence¶

Social Skills Training 2013¶

Social Skills Training. (2013). What Works Clearinghouse Intervention Report: Early Childhood Education Interventions for Children with Disabilities. U.S. Department of Education. https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/InterventionReport/578

q3 · i2 · quant-synthesis · r1

WWC synthesis of two randomized controlled trials (Ferentino 1991; Guglielmo & Tryon 2001) totaling 103 children with disabilities; the printed domain average across studies is effect size 0.47, improvement index +18. The report states there are "two studies with statistically significant positive effects" and no contrary evidence.

"Thus, for the social-emotional development and behavior domain, there are two studies with statistically significant positive effects, one study failing to show evidence of an effect (positive or negative), and no studies showing a statistically significant or substantively important negative effect."

Related Claims¶

  • In Ferentino (1991), social skills training produced a large significant effect on teacher-rated Vineland Socialization but not on four other social-emotional measures — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Social skills training shows no discernible effects on cognition for preschool children with disabilities — related
  • LeBlanc and Matson (1995) found a statistically significant positive effect on one social-emotional outcome but insufficient information prevented WWC verification or inclusion in the rating — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • In Guglielmo and Tryon (2001), social skills training plus reinforcement increased sharing behaviors significantly more than reinforcement alone, while effects on being in a group were positive but not significant — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Inclusive preschool special education benefits students with and without disabilities and reduces later special education identification — related
