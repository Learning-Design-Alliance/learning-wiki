# Stimulus change can selectively reinforce behaviors that produce change and elicit exploratory behavior

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Exploratory behavior strength is positively related, within limits, to degree of stimulus change, but too great or too abrupt change disrupts and may preclude exploration; organisms also learn behaviors that secure novel stimuli. [→ Glaser 1966](#glaser-1966)

## Evidence

### Glaser 1966

Glaser, Robert. (1966). Variables in "Discovery Learning." Learning Research and Development Center, University of Pittsburgh. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED010518

`q2 · i?`

The paper reviews mostly infra-human research on curiosity and exploration (citing Fowler, 1965) showing exploratory strength rises with novelty, complexity, and incongruity within limits, and that learned behaviors can secure stimulus change. No effect size is printed.

> "stimulus change or sensory variation may be employed to selectively reinforce behaviors which result in change, and that this variation in the situation will serve concomi- tantly to elicit exploratory behavior."
