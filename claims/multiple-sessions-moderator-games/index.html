Evidence¶

Clark Douglas B. 2016¶

Clark Douglas B., Tanner-Smith Emily E., Killingsworth Stephen S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Meta-regression moderator analysis of media comparisons: single-session comparisons averaged g = 0.08 (CI including zero) versus g = 0.44 for multiple sessions. No standardized effect size was printed for the moderator coefficient, so impact is uncoded. The result remained significant after controlling for visual realism, anthropomorphism, variety of actions, viewpoint, story relevance, and story depth.

"Furthermore, effects were significantly smaller when games were played in one game session versus more than one session (b = −0.37, p = .03, 95% CI [−0.70, −0.04])."

Clark Douglas B. 2016 (2)¶

Clark Douglas B., Tanner-Smith Emily E., Killingsworth Stephen S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r3

Null moderator result from the same meta-regression models; media comparison game interventions averaged 11 sessions over 38 days for a total duration of 347 minutes. No effect size was printed for this null association. The null persisted in the controlled re-estimation.

"Results from a meta-regression model provided no evidence of an association between total game play duration and effect size magnitude (b = 0.00, p = .99, 95% CI [−0.0005, 0.001])."
