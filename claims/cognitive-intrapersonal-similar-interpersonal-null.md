# Game benefits are similar in magnitude for cognitive and intrapersonal outcomes, while the single interpersonal outcome was not significant

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Clark 2016

Clark, D. B., Tanner-Smith, E. E., & Killingsworth, S. S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 86(1). https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-regression across media-comparison effect sizes comparing outcome domains; Table 3 reports cognitive g = 0.35 (CI [0.20, 0.51]) and intrapersonal g = 0.35 (CI [0.06, 0.65]), both small-to-borderline effects. The review reports "no evidence of a difference in the magnitude of the mean effect sizes."

> "Results from a meta-regression model using robust variance estimates provided no evidence of a difference in the magnitude of the mean effect sizes for the cognitive and intrapersonal learning outcomes (b = 0.03, 95% CI [−0.32, 0.38])."

### Clark 2016 (2)

Clark, D. B., Tanner-Smith, E. E., & Killingsworth, S. S. (2016). Digital Games, Design, and Learning: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research, 86(1). https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Single-study interpersonal outcome from the media-comparison set; g = 0.25 with a confidence interval including zero, so no significant effect was detected and no reliable effect size is established from one study.

> "Only one study provided an effect size on an interpersonal learning outcome (not shown separately in Table 3), which was positive and favored the digital game conditions but was not significantly different from zero (g¯= 0.25, 95% CI [−0.28, 0.77])."
