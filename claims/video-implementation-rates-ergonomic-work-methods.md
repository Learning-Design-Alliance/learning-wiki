# Video analysis showed ergonomic work methods were fully implemented in 49% of observed care situations, with bed height raised correctly in 44.7% of bedside situations and a stool used in 67.3% of bathroom situations

## Evidence

### Kozak Agnessa 2017

Kozak Agnessa, Freitag Sonja, Nienhaus Albert. (2017). Evaluation of a Training Program to Reduce Stressful Trunk Postures in the Nursing Professions: A Pilot Study. Annals of Work Exposures and Health. https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxw002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Skilled-observer video analysis of follow-up measurements: 217 bedside and 52 bathroom care situations were coded for correct, partial, or no implementation of the recommended measures; 132 of 269 (49%) were fully implemented. The article reports "in 49% of care situations, ergonomic measures were implemented properly".

> "Video analysis showed that in 49% of care situations, ergonomic measures were implemented properly, either at the bedside or in the bathroom."

### Kozak Agnessa 2017 (2)

Kozak Agnessa, Freitag Sonja, Nienhaus Albert. (2017). Evaluation of a Training Program to Reduce Stressful Trunk Postures in the Nursing Professions: A Pilot Study. Annals of Work Exposures and Health. https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxw002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Video-coded bedside care situations at follow-up (n = 217): full implementation (hip height) in 44.7%, partial (thigh height) in 44.2%, none in 11.1%. The article states "the bed was raised to hip height in 44.7% of all care situations".

> "As recommended by the seminar instructor, the bed was raised to hip height in 44.7% of all care situations. However, in 44.2% of situations, the bed was partially raised, and in 11.1%, the bed was not raised at all."

### Kozak Agnessa 2017 (3)

Kozak Agnessa, Freitag Sonja, Nienhaus Albert. (2017). Evaluation of a Training Program to Reduce Stressful Trunk Postures in the Nursing Professions: A Pilot Study. Annals of Work Exposures and Health. https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxw002

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Video-coded bathroom care situations at follow-up (n = 52): sitting care on a stool in 67.3%, standing or kneeling in 32.7%. The article reports "a stool was used in 67.3% of these situations".

> "A stool was used in 67.3% of these situations to perform basic care in the sitting position; in 32.7% of the situations, the stool was not used by the nurses (Table 5)."
