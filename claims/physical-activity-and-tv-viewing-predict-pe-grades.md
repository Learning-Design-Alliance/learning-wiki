## Evidence

### Poulain T 2018

Poulain T, Peschel T, Vogel M, Jurkutat A, Kiess W. (2018). Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations of screen time and physical activity with school performance at different types of secondary school. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-018-5489-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Longitudinal regression in the LIFE Child cohort (N = 512) predicting Physical Education grades at follow-up from baseline activity and screen time, adjusted for controls and baseline grades. The article reports higher physical activity "predicted by higher total-physical-activity-scores at baseline".

> "Better performance in Physical Education at follow-up was predicted by higher total-physical-activity-scores at baseline (b = -0.04, p < 0.01, see also Fig. 2)."

### Poulain T 2018 (2)

Poulain T, Peschel T, Vogel M, Jurkutat A, Kiess W. (2018). Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations of screen time and physical activity with school performance at different types of secondary school. BMC Public Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-018-5489-3

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Detailed longitudinal analysis (Table 3) separating organized (sports clubs) and non-organized physical activity as predictors of Physical Education grades. The article reports organized activity "showed no association with grades in Physical Education at follow-up".

> "In contrast to non-organized physical activity, organized physical activity showed no association with grades in Physical Education at follow-up (b = -0.03, p = 0.12)."
