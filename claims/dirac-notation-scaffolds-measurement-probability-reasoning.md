## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2015

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2015). Student difficulties with the probability distribution for measuring energy and position in quantum mechanics. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the Dirac-notation questions administered to 272 upper-level students, "85% correctly recognized that the statement is true" for D1 (position measurement probability |⟨x|Ψ⟩|²dx), and Table III shows 91% correctly recognized D2 about energy measurement probability |⟨n|Ψ⟩|².

> "Table III  shows that 85% correctly recognized that the statement is true."

### Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman 2015 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Emily Marshman. (2015). Student difficulties with the probability distribution for measuring energy and position in quantum mechanics. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1509.04081)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison across the 272-student Dirac-notation survey and the 210-student position-representation survey: 67% and 60% recognized D3(I) and D3(II) as correct, versus "~20% correct on each question" for questions 1–4. Interviews suggest students sometimes used statement D3(I) in Dirac notation to determine that D3(II) was correct.

> "Student performance on questions 1 -4 (~20% correct on each question) about probabilities of energy and position measurement in position representation discussed earlier are worse than on statement D3 (II) here even though it is about the measurement probability of a generic observable 𝑄."
