# Identical example–problem pairs showed an early order advantage but no detected final-test difference

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` For identical repeated tasks, EP outperformed PE during learning, but the difference was not detected on the test after PE learners had studied the example twice.

## Evidence

### van Gog 2011

van Gog, T. (2011). *Effects of identical example-problem and problem-example pairs on learning*. Computers & Education, 57(2), 1775–1779. [Author institutional abstract](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/25659). Verification scope: abstract only; full methods, assignment, exclusions, baseline balance and instrument properties are not checked here.

The between-subjects experiment compared example–problem–example–problem with problem–example–problem–example, n=16 per condition, on one particular problem. The abstract reports the phase-specific result above. It supplies a configuration/horizon qualification, not evidence of equivalence, an individual effect estimate or a general problem-first advantage.
