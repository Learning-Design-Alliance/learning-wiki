## Evidence

### Zha 2019

Zha, Shenghua; Estes, Michele D.; and Xu, Ling. (2019). A meta-analysis on the effect of duration, task, and training in peer-led learning. Journal of Peer Learning, 12. https://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol12/iss1/2

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Conditional mixed-effects meta-regression (k = 28): the PBL-facilitation group mean ES was 0.56 (CI 0.43–0.71) versus 0.17 (CI 0.05–0.29) for non-PBL tasks; the task-type moderation effect was 0.30 standard deviation units (p = 0.00, CI 0.15–0.45), and the interval width suggested the effect could range from 0.15 to 0.45.

> "Groups where student leaders facilitated PBL activities as their core tasks were 0.30 standard deviation units higher than groups where student leaders facilitated non-PBL types of tasks, such as lecturing."

### Zha 2019 (2)

Zha, Shenghua; Estes, Michele D.; and Xu, Ling. (2019). A meta-analysis on the effect of duration, task, and training in peer-led learning. Journal of Peer Learning, 12. https://ro.uow.edu.au/ajpl/vol12/iss1/2

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Omnibus QM test of the joint regression coefficients for task type, training, and duration in the two-level conditional meta-regression model (k = 28); the model was significant and explained 53% of between-study variability in true effect sizes.

> "The result for the conditional model showed a statistical significance (QM = 24.12, df = 3, p < 0.01), meaning that this set of characteristics was a practically significant predictor of ES differences across studies. More than half (53%) of the between-study variability in true ES can be explained by this conditional model."
