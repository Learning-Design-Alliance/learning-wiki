Evidence¶

Jeffrey Buckley 2018¶

Jeffrey Buckley, Niall Seery, Donal Canty. (2018). A Heuristic Framework of Spatial Ability: a Review and Synthesis of Spatial Factor Literature to Support its Translation into STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9432-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports Shea et al.'s longitudinal tracking of 563 talent-search participants (top 0.5% for their age group), in which "spatial ability was found to account for a statistically significant amount of additional variance" beyond the SAT scales for math–science criteria.

Jeffrey Buckley 2018 (2)¶

Jeffrey Buckley, Niall Seery, Donal Canty. (2018). A Heuristic Framework of Spatial Ability: a Review and Synthesis of Spatial Factor Literature to Support its Translation into STEM Education. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9432-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review attributes to Wai et al. (2009) the finding, based on Project TALENT and modern longitudinal comparisons, that "contemporary talent searches miss many intellectually talented students by restricting selection criteria to mathematical and verbal ability measures."
