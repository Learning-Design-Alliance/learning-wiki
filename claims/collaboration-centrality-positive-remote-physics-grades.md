## Evidence

### Javier Pulgar 2022

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, Iván Sánchez. (2022). Long-term collaboration with strong friendship ties improves academic performance in remote and hybrid teaching modalities in high school physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.18.010146)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Exploratory OLS multiple regression on 2020 remote-teaching data from students in two Chilean schools, controlling for prior-year grades, school and gender. The model "yielding a positive coeﬃcient (.2, p < . 05)" indicates more working partners was associated with higher physics grades.

> "On model A we used degree centrality of the collaboration network as the main predic- tor, yielding a positive coeﬃcient (.2, p < . 05) thus, sug- gesting that more working partners is associated with success in physics across schools."

### Javier Pulgar 2021

Javier Pulgar, Diego Ramírez, Abigail Umanzor, Cristian Candia, and Iván Sánchez. (2021). Student networks on online teaching due to COVID-19: Academic effects of strong friendship ties and perceived academic prestige in physics and mathematics courses. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2109.06245](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2109.06245)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression (OLS) on 101 secondary students from two Chilean schools during remote 2020 teaching, controlling for prior-year grades, school, and gender. The collaboration network degree centrality yielded a "positive coefﬁcient (.29, p < . 01)" for physics grades. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "On model A we used degree centrality of the collaboration network as the main predictor, yielding a positive coefﬁcient (.29, p < . 01) and thus, suggesting that more working partners foster success in physics."
