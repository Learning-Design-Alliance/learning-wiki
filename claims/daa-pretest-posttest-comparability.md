# Prior knowledge favored the lecture condition at pretest and conditions were equivalent at posttest 2

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Lecture First students had significantly higher prior knowledge of the camera's part-function relationships at pretest than DAA First students. [→ Dalrymple 2013](#dalrymple-2013)
`q3 i?` At posttest 2 there was no significant difference between the DAA First and Lecture First sequences. [→ Dalrymple 2013 (2)](#dalrymple-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Dalrymple 2013

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pretest of matching eight camera components to functions (maximum 8): DAA First 1.01 ± .952 versus Lecture First 1.28 ± 1.14, indicating overall low prior knowledge. No effect size printed.

> "The results of the independent t-test, indicate a significant difference in the mean pretest scores of the two sequences with students in the Lecture First sequence having an advantage, with respect to prior knowledge of the content, to students in the DAA First sequence, t (314) = -2.26 (equal variances not assumed), p = 0.024"

### Dalrymple 2013 (2)

Dalrymple, O., Sears, D. A., & Evangelou, D. (2013). The Relative Pedagogical Value of Disassemble/Analyze/Assemble (DAA) Activities. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076105

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttest 2 (selecting components preventing image superimposition, scores 0-1): DAA First 0.81 ± 0.13 versus Lecture First 0.80 ± 0.11. The authors state this establishes condition comparability at the end of the lab; equivalence was not tested.

> "Based on the results of the independent t-test, there was no significant difference in the performance of students in the DAA First and Lecture First sequence, t (323) = .341, p = 0.733"
