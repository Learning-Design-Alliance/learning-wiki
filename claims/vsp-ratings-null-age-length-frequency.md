## Evidence

### Daniel García-Torres 2025

Daniel García-Torres, César Fernández, José Joaquín Mira, Alexandra Morales, María Asunción Vicente. (2025). Using AI-Based Virtual Simulated Patients for Training in Psychopathological Interviewing: Cross-Sectional Observational Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/78857

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation of participant age against tool ratings in the observational VSP study; "no significant correlation was found between participants' age and their overall rating." Equivalence was not tested.

> "Concerning age, no significant correlation was found between participants' age and their overall rating of the tool (r=0.02, 95% CI –0.03 to 0.06; P=.42)."

### Daniel García-Torres 2025 (2)

Daniel García-Torres, César Fernández, José Joaquín Mira, Alexandra Morales, María Asunción Vicente. (2025). Using AI-Based Virtual Simulated Patients for Training in Psychopathological Interviewing: Cross-Sectional Observational Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/78857

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Pearson correlation of interview length (question–answer turns) against ratings; "no significant correlation against the overall rating of the tool (r=0.04...P=.18)." Equivalence was not tested.

> "Similar results were obtained for interview length (number of questions posed by participants), with no significant correlation against the overall rating of the tool (r=0.04, 95% CI –0.2 to –0.10; P=.18)."

### Daniel García-Torres 2025 (3)

Daniel García-Torres, César Fernández, José Joaquín Mira, Alexandra Morales, María Asunción Vicente. (2025). Using AI-Based Virtual Simulated Patients for Training in Psychopathological Interviewing: Cross-Sectional Observational Study. JMIR Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.2196/78857

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Kruskal-Wallis test of ratings across participation-frequency groups (once, 2-5, 6-10, >10 times) after normality transformations failed; "the effect of frequency of participation was not statistically significant."

> "The results of this test showed that the effect of frequency of participation was not statistically significant (H=4.62, P=.20; Table 3)."
