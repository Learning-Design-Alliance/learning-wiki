Evidence¶

Marie Geurten & Patrick Lemaire 2018¶

Geurten, M., & Lemaire, P. (2018). Metacognition for strategy selection during arithmetic problem-solving in young and older adults. Aging, Neuropsychology, and Cognition, 26(3), 424–446. doi:10.1080/13825585.2018.1464114

q3 · i3 · causal · r2

ANOVA main effects on RCJ A'ROC in the same 66-participant experiment: "higher metacognitive accuracy for the selection-only task (.67) than for the selection-execution task (.60), F(1,61) = 13.39, MSe = 1.65, η²p = .17, B > 10", and homogeneous (.71) exceeded heterogeneous (.57), η²p = .42, B > 10.

"Participants also had higher metacognitive accuracy for the selection-only task (.67) than for the selection-execution task (.60), F(1,61) = 13.39, MSe = 1.65, η²p = .17, B > 10. They also had higher metacognitive accuracy on problems with homogeneous unit digits (.71) than on problems with heterogeneous unit digits (.57), F(1,61) = 45.87, MSe = 1.87, η²p = .42, B > 10"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Absolute ease-of-selection judgments did not differ between homogeneous and heterogeneous problems in either age group or task — related
  • Low-confidence judgments on one problem predict better strategy selection on the immediately following problem, equally in young and older adults — related
  • Older adults show higher retrospective metacognitive accuracy than young adults in the selection-only task, especially on homogeneous unit-digit problems, but comparable accuracy in the selection-execution task — related
  • Collecting metacognitive judgments did not disrupt typical strategy selection and execution: both age groups selected the better strategy more on rounding-up homogeneous problems and executed rounding-down faster on homogeneous problems — related
  • Young and older adults make accurate retrospective confidence judgments but not prospective ease-of-selection judgments about their arithmetic strategy selections — related
  • Eliciting confidence ratings leads to poorer metacognitive monitoring in older adults — related
