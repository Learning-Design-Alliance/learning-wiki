## Evidence

### Bateman J 2013

Bateman J, Allen M, Samani D, Kidd J, Davies D. (2013). Virtual patient design: exploring what works and why. A grounded theory study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.12151

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group analysis identified three broad reasons for skipping: a student's perspective of 'efficiency', perceived credibility of the case, and the style of learning material. The authors consider this skipping "in many ways analogous to mind wandering".

> "The skipping threshold describes a point at which a student disengages with the case narrative, and the reasons why students skip through without reading or interacting with information presented."

### Bateman J 2013 (2)

Bateman J, Allen M, Samani D, Kidd J, Davies D. (2013). Virtual patient design: exploring what works and why. A grounded theory study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.12151

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Data triangulation (Table 4) links Student JC's reported skipping to logged behaviour: JC spent three seconds on the examination-findings window versus 15–52 seconds for peers and reviewers, yet the case score of 13 was comparable to peers' 12–18.

> "JC spent three-seconds on examination findings, shorter than peers and two reviewers, but performed satisfactorily compared to peers on the case score"
