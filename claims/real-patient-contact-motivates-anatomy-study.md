## Evidence

### Bergman EM 2013

Bergman EM, de Bruin ABH, Herrler A, Verheijen IWH, Scherpbier AJJA, van der Vleuten CPM. (2013). Students' perceptions of anatomy across the undergraduate problem-based learning medical curriculum: a phenomenographical study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/1472-6920-13-152

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative template analysis of verbatim transcripts from two focus-group sessions with 78 medical students in years 2, 3, 4 and 6 of a PBL curriculum. Students reported that "the strongest stimulus by far was one-to-one (supervised) contact with a real patient"; simulated patients also worked for juniors.

> "The prime motivating factor to study anatomy was clinical exposure. For junior students this could be an encounter with a simulated patient, talking with a real patient in the small group or watching an operation from behind a window. But the strongest stimulus by far was one-to-one (supervised) contact with a real patient."
