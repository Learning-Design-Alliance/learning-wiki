## Evidence

### Hamza K 2026

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-level analysis of a 5-point terminology-knowledge scale in the same 709-student cross-sectional survey; percentages are descriptive, no effect size printed.

> "terminology knowledge showed a clear gradient with foundational concepts were better understood (e.g., sample size: 48.0% reported "understand and can explain"; prevalence and incidence: 46.0% each), whereas advanced concepts central to critical appraisal and evidence synthesis were less well understood (e.g., test power: 20.7%; heterogeneity: 19.7%; number needed to treat: 18.5%)"

### Hamza K 2026 (2)

Hamza K, Qawasmeh H, Ahmad Rasha K, Alkhateeb S, Awawdeh F, Douden B, Zrineh A, Akwan R. (2026). Knowledge, attitudes, and skills towards evidence-based medicine among Palestinian undergraduate medical students: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08750-6

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Mann-Whitney U comparisons of term-knowledge scores between EBM-trained (n=262) and non-trained (n=447) students in the same survey; printed means and SDs reported, no standardised effect size.

> "Prior EBM training was consistently associated with higher mean knowledge scores across most terms (typically p < 0.001), including heterogeneity (3.10 ± 1.27 vs. 2.82 ± 1.20), number needed to treat (3.06 ± 1.28 vs. 2.89 ± 1.15), and meta-analysis (3.13 ± 1.36 vs. 2.84 ± 1.17)"
