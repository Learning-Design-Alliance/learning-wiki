## Evidence

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Research-based assessment of students’ beliefs about experimental physics: When is gender a factor?. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Intersectional analysis of BFY students by major (Table VIII) found the gap significant for BFY non-physics majors only, with "no signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the scores of men and women for BFY physics majors".

> "However, the gender gap in both pre- and postinstruction scores was statistically signiﬁ- cant for BFY nonphysics majors only; there was no signiﬁcant diﬀerence between the scores of men and women for BFY physics majors."

### Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski 2016 (2)

Bethany R. Wilcox and H. J. Lewandowski. (2016). Research-based assessment of students’ beliefs about experimental physics: When is gender a factor?. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.12.020130)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same intersectional analysis found the disappearance of the gap in BFY physics majors "was not replicated in the population of FY students", where a significant moderate gap persisted across majors.

> "The disappearance of the gender gap in the BFY physics major data was not replicated in the population of FY students, where a statistically signiﬁcant, mod- erate gender gap persisted even when the data were disag- gregated by major."
