## Evidence

### Zou Ya 2026

Zou Ya, Xie Wenzhao, Huang Hui, Liu Dan, Zhou Qing, Yi Qifeng, Tong Xiaoliang, Mao Ping. (2026). The effects of case-based, problem-based, and team-based learning on nursing students’ problem-solving, self-directed learning, and communication skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08602-3

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Study-design subgroup analysis for problem-solving abilities (Table 4): both RCTs and quasi-experimental designs show significant effects, with a larger point estimate in RCTs. Duration, sample-size, and grade subgroups are also reported as significant except third-year students.

> "the subgroup analysis revealed that both RCTs (SMD = 1.08, 95% CI: 0.27–1.78, I2 = 96%, P < 0.001) and quasi-experimental studies with control groups (SMD = 0.51, 95% CI: 0.11–0.83, I2 = 93%, P < 0.001) demonstrated that modern teaching methods could significantly enhanced the problem-solving abilities of nursing students, although the effect size was larger in RCTs"

### Zou Ya 2026 (2)

Zou Ya, Xie Wenzhao, Huang Hui, Liu Dan, Zhou Qing, Yi Qifeng, Tong Xiaoliang, Mao Ping. (2026). The effects of case-based, problem-based, and team-based learning on nursing students’ problem-solving, self-directed learning, and communication skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08602-3

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Grade subgroup for problem-solving (Table 4): third-year (junior) students show no significant effect, with very high heterogeneity ("I2 = 97%").

> "the third-year students did not (SMD = 1.74, 95% CI: -0.65-4.13, I2 = 97%, P = 0.15)"
