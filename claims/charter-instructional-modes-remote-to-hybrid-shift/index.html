Related Claims¶

  • Surveyed charter schools transitioned to remote education delivery in less than a week after physical closure in March 2020, averaging 3.5 days — related
  • Schools reported that about 40 percent of their students experienced considerable or major academic losses since March 2020 — related
  • Most New York charter schools reported decreases in learning time across subjects during closure, with low prior reading proficiency associated with larger ELA/Reading time losses — related
  • Nearly all surveyed charter schools provided remote-learning professional development to teachers, compared with under half of district reopening plans — related
  • Immediately after closure, New York charter students lacked adequate devices (44.4% on average) more often than adequate internet access (27.0%), while teachers were almost universally equipped — related
  • Few New York charter teachers reported technology challenges during the remote transition (66% of schools said under a quarter of teachers did), with attention and engagement the more common difficulties — related
  • Just under half of New York charter schools (45.8%) reported no challenges supporting ELL/MLL students remotely; high-poverty schools were more likely to report challenges — related
  • Self-directed and blended learning time increased for nearly all New York charter schools during remote instruction, regardless of whether those modes were part of their prior models — related
