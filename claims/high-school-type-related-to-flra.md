# High school type was significantly related to post-training FLRA scores, with Anatolian high school graduates most anxious and vocational graduates least anxious

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A Kruskall-Wallis test showed a statistically significant relationship between high school type (ATT/AH/VOC/other) and post-test FLRAS scores (χ² = 8.797, p = .032). [→ Çapan and Pektaş 2013](#capan-and-pektas-2013)
`q2 i?` Anatolian high school graduates had the highest FLRA levels and vocational high school graduates the lowest. [→ Çapan and Pektaş 2013 (2)](#capan-and-pektas-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Çapan and Pektaş 2013

Seyit Ahmet Çapan & Rumeysa Pektaş. (2013). An Empirical Analysis of the Relationship between Foreign Language Reading Anxiety and Reading Strategy Training. English Language Teaching, 6(12). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n12p181

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Kruskall-Wallis test on post-test FLRAS scores by high school type in the experiment group; Table 4 prints χ² = 8.797, p = .032 across ATT, AH, VOC and other school graduates. No effect size is printed.

> "The analysis  demonstrated that there was a statistically significant correlation between the FLRAS scores and high school type (See Table 4)."

### Çapan and Pektaş 2013 (2)

Seyit Ahmet Çapan & Rumeysa Pektaş. (2013). An Empirical Analysis of the Relationship between Foreign Language Reading Anxiety and Reading Strategy Training. English Language Teaching, 6(12). https://doi.org/10.5539/elt.v6n12p181

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive mean-rank ordering from the same Kruskall-Wallis analysis (Table 4): AH highest, then ATT and Other, with VOC graduates least anxious. The authors hypothesized prior high-school reading experiences shaped perceptions of FL reading.

> "The participants who graduated from AH were identified with the highest FLRA levels followe d by those having graduated from ATT and Other high schools whereas the graduates of vocational high schools were found to be the least anxious in FL reading."
