# Students and teachers viewed online patient encounters as more distanced, hindering relationship-building with patients

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · qualitative `r3` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reported online history taking loses first impressions, non-verbal aspects and body posture, so a holistic picture of the patient may not evolve, and relationship-building was more strenuous with higher inhibition thresholds for personal questions. [→ Reinhart A 2021](#reinhart-a-2021)
`q2 i?` Teachers feared building a relationship with the patient, which they see as essential for future patient-physician relationships, was possible only to a very limited extent online. [→ Reinhart A 2021 (2)](#reinhart-a-2021-2)
`q2 i?` Students reported a positive attitudinal impact: camera-mediated communication's limits made them choose words more consciously and value non-verbal communication. [→ Reinhart A 2021 (3)](#reinhart-a-2021-3)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Focus group finding on communication with patients: students found relationship-building 'more strenuous in the online format because they experience a higher inhibition threshold to ask personal questions'; listed barriers include group interviews, transmission quality, and patient unease with cameras.

> "It is more difficult to establish a relationship with the patient and to get to know each other via a camera. Students view building their relationship with the patient more strenuous in the online format because they experience a higher inhibition threshold to ask personal questions (e.g., sexual history)."
