# In one uncontrolled pre-post study, medical students’ median self-rated confidence scores rose from pre-test to post-test after a 2-week obstetric simulation course on comprehension and preparation, knowledge of procedures, and expectation items

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

> **Currency** · newest study 2023 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In one single-group pre-post study with no comparison group, fifth-year medical students’ self-rated median scores were significantly higher at post-test than at pre-test on all items of the comprehension and preparation, knowledge of procedures, and expectation subscales after a 2-week obstetric simulation course. [→ Nomura Roseli Mieko Yamamoto 2023](#nomura-roseli-mieko-yamamoto-2023)

## Evidence

### Nomura Roseli Mieko Yamamoto 2023

Nomura Roseli Mieko Yamamoto, Reis Fabiana Medeiros D., Gonçalves Amanda Menezes, de Proença Carolina Matos. (2023). Obstetric simulation for undergraduate medical education: how to improve students’ self-confidence and expectation according to gender. Revista da Associação Médica Brasileira. https://doi.org/10.1590/1806-9282.20221625

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Before-and-after questionnaire study of 115 fifth-year medical students in a 2-week obstetric simulation course; Wilcoxon paired tests compared initial and final subscale medians, with the reported medians rising significantly, e.g. "comprehension and preparation" (18 vs. 22, p<0.001).

> "Comparing initial and final scores, the median results of the subscales “comprehension and preparation” (18 vs. 22, p<0.001), “knowledge of procedures” (14 vs. 20, p<0.001), and “expectation” (22 vs. 23, p<0.01) were significantly higher at the end of the training period"
