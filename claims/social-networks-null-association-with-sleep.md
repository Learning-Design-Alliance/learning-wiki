## Evidence

### Zhao Yao 2026

Zhao Yao, Zhou Haoyu. (2026). Functional data analysis of college students’ sleep patterns and their relationships with academic performance and social networks: A four-year longitudinal study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0351120

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Pointwise functional t-tests with 1000 permutations compared sleep curves of high (n = 41) versus low (n = 35) network-size students; the 12.6-minute descriptive difference was not significant ("maximum t-statistic = 1.93, critical value = 2.84, p > 0.05"). Equivalence was not tested.

> "For network size, students in the high activity group (n = 41) averaged 417.5 minutes (6.96 hours) of sleep compared to 430.1 minutes (7.17 hours) for the low activity group (n = 35), a difference of 12.6 minutes (maximum t-statistic = 1.93, critical value = 2.84, p > 0.05)."

### Zhao Yao 2026 (2)

Zhao Yao, Zhou Haoyu. (2026). Functional data analysis of college students’ sleep patterns and their relationships with academic performance and social networks: A four-year longitudinal study. PLoS One. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0351120

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

The same permutation-based functional t-test approach compared high (n = 40) versus low (n = 36) relationship-strength students; the 7.9-minute difference did not reach significance. The authors note the directional gap widened from 10.3 minutes in freshman year to 15.5 minutes in senior year.

> "For relationship strength, students with high scores (n = 40) averaged 419.6 minutes (6.99 hours) compared to 427.4 minutes (7.12 hours) for those with low scores (n = 36), a difference of 7.9 minutes (maximum t-statistic = 1.68, critical value = 2.85, p > 0.05)."
