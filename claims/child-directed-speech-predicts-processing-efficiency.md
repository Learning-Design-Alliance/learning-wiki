# Child-directed speech predicts real-time language-processing efficiency at 19 and 24 months and explains gains in efficiency

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Amount of child-directed speech was correlated with processing efficiency at 19 months (r = 0.44) and 24 months (r = 0.51), and predicted gains in efficiency from 19 to 24 months (r = 0.47 controlling for 19-month processing). [→ Weisleder Adriana 2013](#weisleder-adriana-2013)

## Evidence

### Weisleder Adriana 2013

Weisleder Adriana, Fernald Anne. (2013). Talking to children matters: Early language experience strengthens processing and builds vocabulary. Psychological Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613488145

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis linking LENA-measured child-directed speech to performance in the looking-while-listening task, where efficiency was the proportion of time fixating the target picture 300-1800 ms after word onset. Controlling for 19-month processing, "children who heard more child-directed speech were more efficient in language processing at 24 months" (r = 0.47, p < 0.05).

> "Amount of exposure to child-directed speech was reliably correlated with children's processing efficiency at 19 (r = 0.44, p < 0.05) and 24 months (r = 0.51, p < 0.01)"
