Martin 2026¶

Martin, A. J., Malmberg, L.-E., Holliman, A. J., Teig, N., Putwain, D. W., Collie, R. J., Burns, E. C., Bostwick, K. C. P., Kennett, R., & Pearson, J. (2026). q2 · i? · associational · r?

Latent cross-lagged SEM analysis on a subset of pooled data with parallel multi-item factors for both constructs (Studies 1, 2, 5, 6), estimating buoyancy and adaptability as latent constructs with correlated item residuals across time. Both cross-lagged paths were "β = .07 (p < .01)" and are described as "medium cross-lagged effect sizes". No participant n is printed in this section.

"The cross-lagged path from Time 1 buoyancy to Time 2 adaptability was β = .07 (p < .01), and the cross-lagged path from Time 1 adaptability to Time 2 buoyancy was β = .07 (p < .01)—both medium cross-lagged effect sizes (Orth et al., 2024)."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The authors conclude that, when adjusting for unreliability via latent modeling, earlier buoyancy predicts later adaptability gains and earlier adaptability predicts later buoyancy gains — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Across basic and extended RI-CLPMs and CLPMs of five-wave secondary-school data, math self-concept and achievement show consistent reciprocal (bidirectional) directional ordering
  • Test performance predicts subsequent enjoyment, boredom, and anxiety in primary school students, but only anxiety relates reciprocally to performance
  • LCSM on the same simulated PALMA-like data shows negative cross-lagged effects, contradicting the reciprocal effects model
