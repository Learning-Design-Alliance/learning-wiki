## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses yielded the same two-factor solution in 8- to 10-year-olds and 11- to 13-year-olds, contradicting the hypothesis that the STM/WM distinction is blurred in children. [→ Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001](#robert-kail-and-lynda-k-hall-2001)

## Evidence

### Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall 2001

Robert Kail and Lynda K. Hall. (2001). Distinguishing short-term memory from working memory. Memory & Cognition, 29(1), 1-9. https://link.springer.com/journal/13421/29/1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Samples were split into 8- to 10-year-olds and 11- to 13-year-olds in both studies; two-factor solutions emerged in exploratory analyses, and one-factor models fit worse for both age groups (Study 1 c2s(1) = 6.16 and 4.74; Study 2 c2s(1) = 3.88 and 5.36, p < .05). The authors report "the memory tasks distinguishedSTM and WM constructs" across the age range.

> "Thus, throughoutthe age range studied here, the memory tasks distinguishedSTM and WM constructs."
