## Evidence

### Anna-Sophia Dersch · Ian Thacker · Alexander Eitel 2026

Anna-Sophia Dersch · Ian Thacker · Alexander Eitel (2026). Should we Tell Teachers That They Are (In)Correct? How Personalized Refutation Text Promotes or Hampers Conceptual Change. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10116-9

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Preregistered randomized 2×2 online experiment; orthogonal contrast analysis on the subsample of 96 participants (28.57%) who held both misconceptions at pre-test. The article reports that "contrast #1 significantly predicted conceptual change scores, b = 0.26, SE = 0.13" in the personalized refutation condition versus the other three conditions.

> "Among those participants (n = 96; 28.57%) who had held both misconceptions in the pre-test and hence received the feedback of having these misconceptions before reading the refutation texts, results revealed that contrast #1 significantly predicted conceptual change scores, b = 0.26, SE = 0.13"
