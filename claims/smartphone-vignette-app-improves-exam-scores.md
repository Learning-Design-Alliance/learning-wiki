# Medical interns randomised to a smartphone application of EUNACOM-style internal medicine vignettes improve exam-simulation scores more than interns receiving no intervention

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Intention-to-treat analysis showed interns allocated to the smartphone application had score increases about 5 points (9%) higher than the no-intervention group (p = 0.03). [→ Martínez F 2017](#martinez-f-2017)

## Evidence

### Martínez F 2017

Martínez F, Tobar C, Taramasco C. (2017). Implementation of a Smartphone application in medical education: a randomised trial (iSTART). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1010-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Double-masked randomised trial of 80 interns at Universidad de Valparaíso, analysed intention-to-treat with multiple imputation. The app group's absolute score change was "14.5 ± 8.9" versus 9.4 ± 11.6 points in the no-intervention group.

> "On average, interns allocated to the smartphone application had an increase in scores that was 5 points (9%) higher than those observed in the no-intervention group (p = 0.03)."

### Martínez F 2017 (2)

Martínez F, Tobar C, Taramasco C. (2017). Implementation of a Smartphone application in medical education: a randomised trial (iSTART). BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-017-1010-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Complete-case analysis among the 81.3% of interns (65 of 80) who sat the final test 4 weeks after randomisation; the between-group difference favoured the app but was not statistically significant.

> "When overall scores were analysed, an absolute difference of 3.5 points was observed between groups in favour of those allocated to the smartphone application, but statistical significance was not reached (p = 0.22)."
