# The advantage of cadaver training over anatomical models is region-specific: models matched or beat cadavers for carpal tunnel and knee infiltrations, while cadaver training produced more correct steps for the lateral epicondyle and knee anterior approach

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For glenohumeral, subacromial and carpal tunnel infiltrations, model- and cadaver-trained participants performed more steps correctly than lecture-only participants; for the lateral epicondyle and knee (anterior approach), cadaver-trained participants outperformed both other groups. [→ Michels Nele R. 2019](#michels-nele-r-2019)
`q3 i?` Self-efficacy results diverge by region: cadaver trainees rated knee-infiltration skills higher than model trainees, and model trainees rated carpal-tunnel skills higher than lecture-only trainees immediately after training. [→ Michels Nele R. 2019 (2)](#michels-nele-r-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Michels Nele R. 2019

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Between-group OSCE comparison of correctly performed steps in the randomized three-group study (Table 2), tested with Kruskal-Wallis (p ≤ 0.05). The contrast between named training groups differs by anatomical region, exactly as the quoted passage states. No effect size is printed.

> "For infiltrations of the glenohumeral joint, the subacromial region and the carpal tunnel participants who followed the training on anatomical models or cadavers significantly performed more steps in a correct way than participants who followed only the theoretical lecture. For infiltrations of the lateral epicondyle and the knee joint (anterior approach) participants who followed the training on cadavers significantly performed more steps in a correct way than participants who followed the theoretical lecture only or the training on anatomical models."

### Michels Nele R. 2019 (2)

Michels Nele R., Vanhomwegen Els. (2019). An educational study to investigate the efficacy of three training methods for infiltration techniques on self-efficacy and skills of trainees in general practice. BMC Family Practice. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-019-1023-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Self-efficacy comparison between the three training groups on the Bandura-based questionnaire (Table 1), Kruskal-Wallis tests, p ≤ 0.05; the knee and carpal tunnel contrasts are quoted as printed, with the carpal-tunnel effect present only immediately after training.

> "Participants in the training on cadavers estimated their skills for infiltrations of the knee joint significantly higher than participants in the training on anatomical models. This effect was observed immediately after and 3 months after training. Participants in the training on anatomical models estimated their skills for infiltrations of the carpal tunnel significantly higher than participants in the group of theoretical lecture only."
