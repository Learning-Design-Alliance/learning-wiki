## Evidence

### Hobbs F. Collin 2013

Hobbs F. Collin, Johnson Daniel J., Kearns Katherine D. (2013). A Deliberate Practice Approach to Teaching Phylogenetic Analysis. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe-13-03-0046

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-case matched pre/post analysis using the normalized change metric with 121 intervention students who completed both assessments in 2010 and 2011. The overall "Cave score for all questions was 0.139 ± 0.006." Scoring used a rubric with Cohen's kappa values of 0.78–0.89.

> "The overall Cave score for all questions was 0.139 ± 0.006, indicating an overall improvement between pre- and postconcept assessments."

### Hobbs F. Collin 2013 (2)

Hobbs F. Collin, Johnson Daniel J., Kearns Katherine D. (2013). A Deliberate Practice Approach to Teaching Phylogenetic Analysis. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe-13-03-0046

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Normalized change analysis (2010 and 2011 combined) of the pre/post concept assessment. Three skill items improved significantly (Q.6 Cave 0.227; Q.7c Cave 0.470; Q.7d Cave 0.911), as did two concept items (Q.1 Cave 0.113; Q.3 Cave 0.433; all p < 0.05).

> "Students showed significant improvements in three skill-based items: inferring ancestral traits when provided with information about the traits of extant taxa (Q.6), mapping character state changes while constructing trees (Q.7c), and aligning extant taxa at branch tips while constructing trees (Q.7d; Table 3 and Figure 3)."

### Hobbs F. Collin 2013 (3)

Hobbs F. Collin, Johnson Daniel J., Kearns Katherine D. (2013). A Deliberate Practice Approach to Teaching Phylogenetic Analysis. CBE—Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe-13-03-0046

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Normalized change analysis of item Q.7b (Cave −0.714, p < 0.05). The authors attribute the decrease to a mismatch: the intervention assignments presented only extant outgroups, and students drew the extinct common ancestor as if it were an extant outgroup.

> "although a substantial percentage of students (31.4%) continued to place the extinct common ancestor correctly at the base of the tree, students whose scores did change on this item showed a significant decrease in ability in the postconcept assessment."
