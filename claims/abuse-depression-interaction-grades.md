## Evidence

### Nagin Perry M.D. 2019

Nagin Perry M.D., Salandy Anthony Ph.D., MSc., Diaz Angela M.D., Ph.D. (2019). Unpacking the Influence of Abuse and Depression on Grades among Urban Ethnic Minority Adolescents and Young Adults. Annals of Global Health. https://doi.org/10.5334/aogh.2478

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Two-way ANOVA results section reporting the omnibus abuse × depression interaction on self-reported grades in the 476-participant sample. The article reports "a statistically significant interaction between abuse and depression on grades"; no standardized effect size beyond partial η² is printed.

> "There was a statistically significant interaction between abuse and depression on grades, F(1, 472) = 4.26, p < .05, partial η2 = .009 (see Table 2)."

### Nagin Perry M.D. 2019 (2)

Nagin Perry M.D., Salandy Anthony Ph.D., MSc., Diaz Angela M.D., Ph.D. (2019). Unpacking the Influence of Abuse and Depression on Grades among Urban Ethnic Minority Adolescents and Young Adults. Annals of Global Health. https://doi.org/10.5334/aogh.2478

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Interaction contrast comparing non-depressed abused and non-abused youth. Mean grade scores were 2.88 (.11) for non-depressed abused youth versus 2.53 (.10) for non-depressed non-abused youth; the contrast was "a statistically significant difference, p < .01". No standardized effect size is printed.

> "Non-depressed adolescents who reported no abuse had 36 (95% CL, –.53 to –.18) lower grades scores than their non-depressed counterparts who reported abuse—a statistically significant difference, p < .01 (see Figure 1)."
