# Stroop Effect shows no statistically significant correlation with university students' grade averages

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r1` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Among 107 university students, the correlation between the Stroop Effect and grade averages was very weak, negative, and not statistically significant (r(107) = − 0.13, p = .20). [→ Dvorak Martin 2024](#dvorak-martin-2024)

## Evidence

### Dvorak Martin 2024

Dvorak Martin. (2024). Inhibitory control and academic achievement – a study of the relationship between Stroop Effect and university students’ academic performance. BMC Psychology 12:498. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-024-01984-3

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r1`

Pearson bivariate correlation analysis of 107 university students' Stroop Test scores against their computed grade averages found "a very weak negative correlation of low statistical significance, r(107) = − 0.13, p = .20"; the printed effect size r = −0.13 (a small correlation, in the r = .10–.19 band) did not reach statistical significance (p = .20).

> "a very weak negative correlation of low statistical significance, r(107) = − 0.13, p = .20, between the Stroop Effect and participants’ grade averages"
