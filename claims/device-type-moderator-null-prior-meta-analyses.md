# Prior meta-analyses found the screen inferiority effect did not differ as a function of digital device type

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The review reports, citing Delgado et al. (2018) and Öztop and Nayci (2021), that device type (computers vs. handheld devices) did not significantly moderate the screen inferiority effect, though few handheld-device studies were included. [→ Salmerón 2023](#salmeron-2023)

## Evidence

### Salmerón 2023

Salmerón, L., Altamura, L., Delgado, P., Karagiorgi, A., & Vargas, C. (2023). Reading comprehension on handheld devices vs. on paper: A narrative review and meta-analysis of the medium effect and its moderators. Journal of Educational Psychology. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000830

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

The review attributes this to two prior meta-analyses that grouped studies by computer versus handheld device. It cautions the results "must be taken with caution" because handheld-device studies were few (n = 12 in Delgado et al., 2018; n = 2 in Öztop & Nayci, 2021).

> "In both cases, the effects were not significant, indicating that the screen inferiority effect did not differ as a function of digital device."
