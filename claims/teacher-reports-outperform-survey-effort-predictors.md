## Subclaims
`q2 i?` All teacher reports (grit, work and interpersonal self-control, redirection, homework completion) significantly predicted senior GPA, graduation, SAT attempting, end-of-year tests, and college enrollment, with generally larger effect sizes than survey effort or performance tasks. [→ Zamarro Gema 2020](#zamarro-gema-2020)

## Evidence

### Zamarro Gema 2020

Zamarro Gema, Nichols Malachi, Duckworth Angela L., D'Mello Sidney K. (2020). Validation of survey effort measures of grit and self-control in a sample of high school students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0235396

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of regression models in Tables 4-6; for example teacher-reported grit had a standardized GPA coefficient of "[0.505]" versus about -0.2 for item non-response. The only significant predictor of SAT scores among test-takers was teacher-reported interpersonal self-control.

> "Student self-reported measures of grit and self-control appear to be comparable predictors than survey effort measures but teacher reports appear to be a better predictor of student academic outcomes than survey effort or performance tasks."

### Zamarro Gema 2020 (2)

Zamarro Gema, Nichols Malachi, Duckworth Angela L., D'Mello Sidney K. (2020). Validation of survey effort measures of grit and self-control in a sample of high school students. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0235396

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression results for teacher-reported measures (Table 5) across the nine academic outcomes, with KBIT and socio-demographic controls, showing uniformly significant prediction of the listed outcomes.

> "We find that all types of teacher reports considered significantly predict senior year GPA, the probability of high school graduation, attempting the SAT, performance in the Keystone reading and math tests and college enrollment."
