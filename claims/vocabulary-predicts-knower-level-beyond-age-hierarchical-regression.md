# Vocabulary adds significantly to predicting knower-level after age, but age does not add after vocabulary, suggesting age does not mediate the vocabulary–number-knowledge link.

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In hierarchical regression, both vocabulary scores added significantly to fit when entered after age, while age did not contribute significantly when entered after vocabulary. [→ Negen 2012](#negen-2012)
`q3 i?` The correlation between age and knower-level controlling for vocabulary was not significant, r(29) = .184, p = .320. [→ Negen 2012 (2)](#negen-2012-2)

## Evidence

### Negen 2012

Negen, J., & Sarnecka, B. W. (2012). Number-Concept Acquisition and General Vocabulary Development. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01815.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression on the merged Experiment 2 data (Table 2): Model 1 entered age (R2 = .468), then receptive vocabulary (ΔR2 = .133**) and expressive vocabulary (ΔR2 = .074*); Model 2's final age step (ΔR2 = .020) was not significant.

> "For Model 1, we entered age, then receptive and productive vocabulary, revealing that all steps added to the fit. For Model 2, we entered receptive and productive vocabulary, then age. In this case, age did not contribute significantly to the fit."

### Negen 2012 (2)

Negen, J., & Sarnecka, B. W. (2012). Number-Concept Acquisition and General Vocabulary Development. Child Development. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2012.01815.x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Secondary analysis of the Experiment 2 data found the age–knower-level partial correlation "r(29) = .184, p = .320" was not significant, supporting the claim that language skill, not age, accounts for the shared variance.

> "Finally, we calculated the correlation between age and knower-level when controlling for the vocabulary scores. This correlation was not significant r(29) = .184, p = .320."
