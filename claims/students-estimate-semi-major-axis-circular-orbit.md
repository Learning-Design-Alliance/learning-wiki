# Using circular-orbit geometry, students estimated the semi-major axis of Kepler-2 as 0.0366±0.0011 AU, near the accepted value

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Combining the orbital period and transit length under a circular-orbit assumption, students estimated the semi-major axis as 0.0366±0.0011 AU versus the accepted 0.03796±0.00063 AU. [→ Ollé 2022](#olle-2022)

## Evidence

### Ollé 2022

Ollé, H. and Kovács, T. (2022). What Hides within the Photograph: the Analysis of a Light Curve in the Classroom. Physics Education. [doi:10.1088/1361-6552/ac68c3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6552/ac68c3)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Geometric derivation from the student-derived period and transit length, matching two orbital-velocity expressions; because the eccentricity of Kepler-2 is very small, it was "a minor deviation to assume the planetary orbit to be circular". Result: "0.0366±0.0011 AU".

> "According to the students’ estimate the semi-major axis is 0.0366±0.0011 AU, while the currently accepted value is 0.03796 ± 0.00063 AU [12, 13]."
