# Advertising and providing GPT-4 access significantly decreased diagnostic exam participation in an online coding class

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students offered GPT-4 access took the optional diagnostic exam at a significantly lower rate than control students (44.1% vs 48.5%). [→ Allen Nie 2024](#allen-nie-2024)

## Evidence

### Allen Nie 2024

Allen Nie, Yash Chandak, Miroslav Suzara, Ali Malik, Juliette Woodrow, Matt Peng, Mehran Sahami, Emma Brunskill, and Chris Piech. (2024). The GPT Surprise: Offering Large Language Model Chat in a Massive Coding Class Reduced Engagement but Increased Adopters' Exam Performances. [doi:10.31219/osf.io/qy8zd](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/qy8zd)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Randomized control trial in the Code-in-Place online coding class (N=5,831 active students; 3,581 treatment, 2,250 control). The treatment group's exam participation was 44.1% versus 48.5% in control, "representing a significant difference in means (∆=-4.3pp" with a 95% CI of [-6.9, -1.7].

> "We found that only 44.1% of the students in the experiment group took the diagnostic exam, compared to 48.5% of the students in the control group who took the exam (Figure 3(b)), representing a significant difference in means (∆=-4.3pp, SE=1.3, 95% CI=[-6.9, -1.7])."
