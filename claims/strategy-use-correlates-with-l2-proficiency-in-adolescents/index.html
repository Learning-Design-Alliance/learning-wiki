Evidence¶

Mantovani 2026¶

Mantovani, A. (2026). Investigating language learning strategies through the strategy inventory: A study among immigrant adolescents in Italian secondary schools. Glottodidactica, 53(1), 221–239. doi:10.14746/gl.2026.53.1.10

q2 · cross-sectional correlational self-report study · i? · correlation reported as present, magnitude not given · n=157 · associational · r1

157 newly arrived migrant students across nine secondary schools in north-east Italy completed an adapted Strategy Inventory for Language Learning. The paper reports mean strategy use, the indirect-over-direct preference, the correlation with proficiency, and variation by cultural background, and recommends explicitly promoting cognitive, social and compensatory strategies through goal-oriented and collaborative tasks.

Discussion¶

Strategy use is a real axis of learner variation, not a proxy for effort or motivation. It is also the actionable one in this set: unlike first-language background, it is something instruction can change — which is what Strategy instruction is more effective when contextualized in authentic tasks [+M] says about how.

The learners using fewest strategies are the ones who need most support, and are least likely to go looking for it. Help placed behind an optional control therefore reaches the learners who least need it. This argues for putting pattern notes, worked reasoning and review into the flow rather than behind a hint button — the same asymmetry that makes Incidental vocabulary exposure produces reliable learning, but learners pick up only a small share of the unknown words they meet [-M] a caution against relying on learners to pick things up unaided.

Population. Migrant adolescents learning the majority language in-country: a high-stakes immersion context, not foreign-language study abroad. Self-report, cross-sectional, correlational.
