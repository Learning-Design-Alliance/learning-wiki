# Magical health beliefs predict willingness to use spinal manipulation preventively on an asymptomatic child — about 2 times for spinal disorders and 4 times for future diseases

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Believers in magic were about 2 times more likely to believe SMT can prevent MSK problems (OR = 2.0, p = 0.018) and 4 times more likely for future diseases (OR = 4.0, p = 0.001) in an asymptomatic 5-year-old. [→ Innes SI 2022](#innes-si-2022)

## Evidence

### Innes SI 2022

Innes SI, Goncalves G, Leboeuf-Yde C. (2022). Who are the chiropractic students favouring a limitless scope of practice? Exploring the relationship with personality, magical thinking, and academic achievement. Chiropractic & Manual Therapies. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12998-022-00440-6

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Two multivariate binary logistic regressions in the same survey (Tables 6 and 7): for preventing spinal disorders MHB OR = 2.0 (95% CI 1.1–3.5, p = 0.018); for preventing diseases MHB OR = 4.0 (95% CI 1.8–9.0, p = 0.001); IU-12, ATAR and year of program were not significant in either model.

> "Tables 6 and 7 are constructed identically, thus showing how, as compared to students who do not hold magical health beliefs, the believers in magic are, respectively, 2 and 4 times more likely also to believe that SMT can prevent (i) MSK and (ii) future diseases in an asymptomatic 5-yr-old."
