# Working with a high-competency pedagogical agent improves novice learners' application of learning (d = 0.46)

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Students in the high-competency PAL condition scored significantly higher on application of learning than students in the low-competency condition (Cohen's d = 0.46). [→ Kim 2006](#kim-2006)

## Evidence

### Kim 2006

Kim, Y., Baylor, A. L., & PALS Group. (2006). Pedagogical Agents as Learning Companions: The Role of Agent Competency and Type of Interaction. Educational Technology Research and Development, 54(3), 223-243. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-006-8805-z

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

A 2x2 between-subjects factorial experiment with 72 undergraduates in a computer-literacy course; univariate analysis of application after a significant MANCOVA main effect for competency. High-competency condition "M = 2.63, SD = 1.39" vs. low-competency M = 2.03, d = 0.46.

> "Students in the high-competency condition (M = 2.63, SD = 1.39) scored significantly higher than students in the low-compe tency condition (M = 2.03, SD = 1.19). The standardized effect size for this dif ference was Cohen 's d = 0.46"
