## Evidence

### Connolly Murray 2026

Connolly Murray, Iohom Gabriella, O’Brien Niall, Volz James, O’Muircheartaigh Aogán, Serchan Paschalitsa, Shorten George. (2026). Delivering remote procedural training on peripheral intravenous cannulation to medical students using the Microsoft Hololens 2: a mixed-methods evaluation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08648-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Semi-structured interviews with three HL-group students, analysed thematically from an interpretive approach. All three rated willingness to recommend the format 6 or 7 out of 7 and found the tutorials "equally or more effective than standard in-person tutorials".

> "All three interviewees stated that they found the HL facilitated tutorials equally or more effective than standard in-person tutorials. All three interviewees reported a 6 or 7 out of 7 on a Likert scale when asked if they would recommend that HoloLens facilitated tutorials are incorporated in medical education programmes"

### Connolly Murray 2026 (2)

Connolly Murray, Iohom Gabriella, O’Brien Niall, Volz James, O’Muircheartaigh Aogán, Serchan Paschalitsa, Shorten George. (2026). Delivering remote procedural training on peripheral intravenous cannulation to medical students using the Microsoft Hololens 2: a mixed-methods evaluation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08648-3

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Modified ETELM-LP questionnaire (seven-point Likert) completed by the eight HL-group students immediately after tutorials. Students reported little prior MR experience (1.63, 1.19), rated visual quality and artefact usefulness 6.75, and gave mixed audio-clarity ratings (4.75, 1.5); Cronbach's Alpha was 0.776.

> "deemed the visual quality to be clear (6.75,0.46), and found the MR artefacts useful (6.75,0.46). They agreed that the tutorial replicated an in-person tutorial (5.75, 0.7) and that the technology supported the learning objectives (6.5, 0.75)."
