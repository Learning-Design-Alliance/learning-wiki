# One relearning session to one correct recall nearly eliminated the effect of initial learning criterion on cued recall, indicating subadditive effects

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Experiment 1, after one relearning session, cued recall in Session 3 showed no effect of Session 1 criterion level, providing the first indication that the effects of initial criterion and relearning are subadditive. [→ Rawson 2011](#rawson-2011)

## Evidence

### Rawson 2011

Rawson, K. A., & Dunlosky, J. (2011). Optimizing Schedules of Retrieval Practice for Durable and Efficient Learning: How Much Is Enough? Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 140(3), 283–302. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1 one-way ANOVA of cued recall in Session 3 across the four initial-criterion groups of undergraduates. The article reports "no effect of Session 1 criterion level on cued recall in Session 3 (F ⬍ 1)" with no significant pairwise differences; no effect size was printed.

> "A one-way ANOVA showed no effect of Session 1 criterion level on cued recall in Session 3 (F ⬍ 1), and pairwise comparisons revealed no significant differences between any groups (all ts ⬍ 0.64, dfs ⱖ 39)."
