# Training shows small trends toward better conflict detection on bat-and-ball and base-rate tasks but the opposite trend on the conjunction fallacy task

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The conflict detection index rose from pre- to post-intervention on bat-and-ball and base-rate tasks in the training group, but not in control, and showed the opposite trend for conjunction fallacy problems. [→ Nina Franiatte 2024](#nina-franiatte-2024)

## Evidence

### Nina Franiatte 2024

Nina Franiatte, Esther Boissin, Alexandra Delmas, Wim De Neys. (2024). Boosting Debiasing: Impact of Repeated Training on Reasoning. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2023.101845

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Exploratory confidence-based conflict detection analysis (De Neys et al., 2011 index) on initial responses, reported as trends: base-rate index rose +4.6 points (Session 1) and +7.4 points (Session 2), whereas the conjunction fallacy task showed a trend toward the opposite effect (-4.7 points in Session 1, -0.4 in Session 2).

> "we found a small  trend towards a better conflict detection after training on bat-and-ball (from pre- to post-intervention, the index rose by 10.5 points in Session 1 and 1.2 points in Session 2)"
