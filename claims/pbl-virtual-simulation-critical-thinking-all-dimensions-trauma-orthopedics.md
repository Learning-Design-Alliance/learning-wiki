## Evidence

### Xue Gang 2026

Xue Gang, Zhang Xin, Ji Shengyi, Qi Jin. (2026). Integrating problem-based learning with virtual simulation in trauma orthopedic education: effects on learning outcomes and student satisfaction. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09558-0

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Dimension-level critical thinking results in the same assessor-blinded RCT of 50 clinical medical students, outlined in Table 2 and Fig. 5. The experimental group "had higher scores than the control group on all dimensions of critical thinking ability (all P < 0.05)", with printed means and standard deviations for each of the seven dimensions. No effect size is printed for the individual dimensions.

> "The experimental group had higher scores than the control group on all dimensions of critical thinking ability (all P < 0.05). Specifically, higher scores were observed for truth-seeking (46.08 ± 3.42 vs. 38.88 ± 5.71), open-mindedness (44.52 ± 4.07 vs. 38.48 ± 4.39), analyticity (47.36 ± 3.08 vs. 39.16 ± 4.84), systematicity (41.96 ± 4.88 vs. 36.48 ± 5.29), critical thinking self-confidence (46.44 ± 4.37 vs. 40.12 ± 3.48), curiosity (45.56 ± 3.63 vs. 38.84 ± 4.56), and cognitive maturity (42.20 ± 4.43 vs. 38.32 ± 5.66)"
