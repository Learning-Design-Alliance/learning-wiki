## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For PDA tasks, students in the natural language group were more likely to succeed on their first attempt (opposite to CFG), and several variations that hindered CFG performance were neutral or even beneficial. [→ Schmalstieg 2025](#schmalstieg-2025)

## Evidence

### Schmalstieg 2025

Schmalstieg, F., Schmellenkamp, M., Schwerter, J., & Zeume, T. (2025). Difficulty Generating Factors for Context-free Language Construction Assignments. arXiv. [doi:10.1145/3702652.3744201](https://doi.org/10.1145/3702652.3744201)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Fixed-effects panel regression on PDA tasks (Table 5). Natural language increased first-attempt success probability (coefficient 0.10, SE 0.05, p<0.05) with no significant effects on attempts or time. Hypotheses I-1 (a) and (b) were not supported for PDA tasks.

> "In contrast to CFG tasks, for which natural language significantly impaired performance, PDA tasks showed the opposite trend: students in the natural language group were more likely to succeed on their first attempt, without affecting the other outcomes"

### Schmalstieg 2025 (2)

Schmalstieg, F., Schmellenkamp, M., Schwerter, J., & Zeume, T. (2025). Difficulty Generating Factors for Context-free Language Construction Assignments. arXiv. [doi:10.1145/3702652.3744201](https://doi.org/10.1145/3702652.3744201)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Fixed-effects panel regression on PDA tasks across Task groups 1–3. Verbose set notation increased first-attempt success (coefficient 0.24, SE 0.08, p<0.01); nesting increased first-attempt success (0.20, SE 0.10, p<0.05) with no consistent effects on other outcomes.

> "Table 5 reveals no re- duction in performance; instead, students in theverbose set notation group were more likely to succeed on their first attempt, although this was not accompanied by improvements in time or attempts."
