## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The gender difference in achievement under concept mapping was statistically significant (p = 0.003), with male mean gain 0.74 versus female mean gain 0.51. [→ T. D. Bot & John E. Eze 2016](#t-d-bot-john-e-eze-2016)

## Evidence

### T. D. Bot & John E. Eze 2016

T. D. Bot & John E. Eze. (2016). Effects of concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies on senior secondary school students' achievement in trigonometry in Kano state, Nigeria. European Journal of Science and Mathematics Education, 4(1), 56-66. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1107765

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Within the concept mapping group of the quasi-experiment (Table 4: 38 males, 32 females), males' mean gain of 0.74 exceeded females' 0.51; Table 5 ANCOVA gives gender F = 8.702, "the p‐value of 0.003 for gender is less than α=0.05". No effect size printed.

> "the post‐test achievement mean score of the male students exposed to concept mapping was 13.74 while that of female students was 13.70. The mean gain for the male students (0.74) was better than the mean gain for the female students (0.51)"
