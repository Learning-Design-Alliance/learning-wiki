## Evidence

### Griffin 2025

Griffin, S. M., Lebedová, A., Cruwys, T., McMahon, G., Foran, A. M., Skrodzka, M., Gallagher, S., Ginty, A. T., & Muldoon, O. T. (2025). Identity change and the transition to university: Implications for cortisol awakening response, psychological well‐being and academic performance. Applied Psychology: Health and Well‐Being, 17(1), e12608. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12608

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Longitudinal path analysis with end-of-year QCA grade data (retrieved for 114 consenting participants) as the T3 outcome: gain was not associated with "academic performance (p = .407)"; no effect size printed.

> "However, group membership gain was not associated with depressive symptoms (p = .492), life satisfaction (p = .338) or academic performance (p = .407)."

### Griffin 2025 (2)

Griffin, S. M., Lebedová, A., Cruwys, T., McMahon, G., Foran, A. M., Skrodzka, M., Gallagher, S., Ginty, A. T., & Muldoon, O. T. (2025). Identity change and the transition to university: Implications for cortisol awakening response, psychological well‐being and academic performance. Applied Psychology: Health and Well‐Being, 17(1), e12608. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12608

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same model tested indirect effects on academic performance through depressive symptoms, CAR and life satisfaction; "H4 was not supported", with confidence intervals crossing zero for both pathways.

> "Neither social identity continuity nor gain indirectly predicted academic performance via the mediators of depressive symptoms, CAR and life satisfaction (continuity; b = −.006, 95% CI [−0.13, 0.11], gain; b = .065, [−0.10, 0.24]); H4 was not supported."
