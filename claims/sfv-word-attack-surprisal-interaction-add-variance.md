# Word attack and the word attack × max P→O surprisal interaction add variance beyond phonology, indicating phonology-to-orthography knowledge contributes to SfV performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The phonology-orthography connection model added an extra 13.61% child-level, 0.90% word-level, and 15.52% school-level variance above the phonological predictor model, with a significant word attack × max P→O surprisal interaction. [→ Edwards 2022](#edwards-2022)
`q2 i?` Max P→O surprisal did not contribute significantly as a main effect across participants, while the interaction showed better decoders were impacted by greater surprisal. [→ Edwards 2022 (2)](#edwards-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Edwards 2022

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Unpacking the Unique Relationship Between Set for Variability and Word Reading Development: Examining Word- and Child-Level Predictors of Performance. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model 3 of the cross-classified analysis, adding max P→O surprisal and word attack. The article reports the model "explained 69.44% of the child-level variance" with the printed extra variance above the phonological predictor model.

> "This model explained 69.44% of the child-level variance, 48.69% of the word-level variance, and 92.18% of the school-level variance, an extra 13.61%, 0.90%, and 15.52% respectively above the phonological predictor model."

### Edwards 2022 (2)

Edwards, A. A., Steacy, L. M., Seigelman, N., Rigobon, V. M., Kearns, D. M., Rueckl, J. G., & Compton, D. L. (2022). Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(6). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000696

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Results text describing the Figure 1 interaction in the phonology-orthography connection predictor model. The interaction was significant, whereas the article reports order, inattention, and max P→O surprisal main effects did not contribute significantly.

> "The interaction between word attack and max P→O surprisal is depicted in Figure 1 and shows that those with better decoding skills are impacted by greater max P→O surprisal"
