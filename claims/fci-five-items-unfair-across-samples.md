## Evidence

### Traxler 2018

Traxler, A., Henderson, R., Stewart, J., Stewart, G., Papak, A., & Lindell, R. (2018). Gender Fairness within the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010103](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010103)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Equal-ability CTT/IRT difficulty plots for Sample 1 posttest (Fig. 1): most items were significantly unfair to women, and "ﬁve items, 14, 21, 22, 23, and 27, stand out as substantially unfair to women"; similar plots for discrimination showed no bias pattern.

> "(2) ﬁve items, 14, 21, 22, 23, and 27, stand out as substantially unfair to women by falling well oﬀ the fairness line"

### Traxler 2018 (2)

Traxler, A., Henderson, R., Stewart, J., Stewart, G., Papak, A., & Lindell, R. (2018). Gender Fairness within the Force Concept Inventory. arXiv. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010103](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.14.010103)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Cross-sample comparison (Fig. 3) of CTT posttest difficulty for Samples 2 and 3 alongside Sample 1: "the similarities, particularly in the CTT plot, are quite strong," supporting general unfairness; IRT results for the smaller samples should be interpreted with caution.

> "Figure 3 overlays plots of items 14, 21, 22, 23, and 27 for all samples; the similarities, particularly in the CTT plot, are quite strong."
