Evidence¶

Sikstrom L 2019¶

Sikstrom L, Saikaly R, Ferguson G, Mosher PJ, Bonato S, Soklaridis S. (2019). Being there: A scoping review of grief support training in medical education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224325

q2 · i? · review · r3

Quantitative synthesis of the 37 included publications. The review tabulated evaluation designs and reports that "Most of these (81%) relied on self-assessments (e.g., Likert scale)" administered immediately after training.

"The impact of grief training was measured in 57% of articles (n = 21). Most of these (81%) relied on self-assessments (e.g., Likert scale) that occurred immediately after the training was complete."

Sikstrom L 2019 (2)¶

Sikstrom L, Saikaly R, Ferguson G, Mosher PJ, Bonato S, Soklaridis S. (2019). Being there: A scoping review of grief support training in medical education. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0224325

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review's discussion of evaluation gaps reports that "only a few (11%) of the studies measured longitudinal outcomes", leaving behavioural and practice impact unclear.

"the actual impact on behaviour and practice in clinical settings was unclear since only a few (11%) of the studies measured longitudinal outcomes."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Only 14% of the reviewed grief-training articles provided a theoretical framework or empirical definition of grief, and all focused on bereavement grief
  • Evidence on transfer to clinical behavior and patient outcomes is scarce: only 2 studies reached Kirkpatrick level 3 and 1 reached level 4
  • In a Danish RCT, trained physicians identified more cases of complicated grief and prescribed fewer psychotropic drugs than untrained control physicians
  • In a Spanish RCT, widows whose family physician was trained in bereavement care experienced poorer emotional outcomes than widows in the control group
  • A US randomized controlled trial found significantly decreased burnout after physicians attended four modules on stress, grief and burnout in medicine — related
  • Grief training in medical education is most often delivered as short voluntary workshops focused on transmitting knowledge about death, dying and bereavement — related
  • No reviewed game study measured behavioural change or patient/health-care outcomes; most measured knowledge and skills — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Trainees valued grief training activities involving reflection, debriefing with peers and the direct involvement of bereaved caregivers — related
