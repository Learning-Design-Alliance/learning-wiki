# The deFinetti simulation shows natural selection moves a population to its local fitness peak but not necessarily to the global peak

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Adding a third allele at one locus produces selection toward a local, not global, fitness peak, challenging the idea that selection produces survival of the fittest. [→ Jungck John R. 2010](#jungck-john-r-2010)

## Evidence

### Jungck John R. 2010

Jungck John R., Gaff Holly, Weisstein Anton E. (2010). Mathematical Manipulative Models: In Defense of “Beanbag Biology”. CBE-Life Sciences Education. https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.10-03-0040

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Numerical simulation result from the deFinetti software (Weisstein et al., 2005b), illustrated in Figure 6 with sickle-cell anemia alleles A, C, and S over 15 generations; populations near the AS polymorphism climb to their local peak rather than the global peak. The article presents this as a simulation demonstration, not a human study.

> "Adding a third allele at the same locus yields an unexpected result: natural selection moves the population to its local fitness peak in the complex adaptive landscape but not necessarily to its global peak."
