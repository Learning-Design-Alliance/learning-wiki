# Students allowed an open textbook during exams performed significantly worse than students with no aids or instructor-prepared aids

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The open-textbook condition scored significantly lower than both the no-aids condition (M difference = 11.57, p < 0.001) and the instructor-prepared aids condition (M difference = 7.45, p < 0.001). [→ Larwin et al. 2008](#larwin-et-al-2008)

## Evidence

### Larwin et al. 2008

Karen H. Larwin, David A. Larwin, Jennifer Gorman. (2008). Mnemonic aids during tests: Worthless frivolity or effective tool in statistics education? Journal of Instructional Pedagogies. https://eric.ed.gov/?q=Mnemonic+aids+during+tests+statistics+education

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Scheffe post hoc comparisons from the factorial ANOVA on course exam scores in the four-condition quasi-experiment. The article prints mean differences and p-values for the open-textbook condition against the no-aids and instructor-prepared aids conditions; no effect size is printed for these contrasts, so none is reported.

> "significant differences in mean exam scores were found between the Open-textbook condition and both the No aids testing condition, M Difference = 11.57, p < 0.001, and the Instructor-prepared aids condition, M Difference = 7.45, p < 0.001."
