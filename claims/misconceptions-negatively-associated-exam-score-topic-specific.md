## Evidence

### Olde Bekkink M 2016

Olde Bekkink M, Donders ART, Kooloos JG, de Waal RM, Ruiter DJ. (2016). Uncovering students’ misconceptions by assessment of their written questions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0739-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Comparison of formal examination scores between students whose questions contained a misconception and those without, in the Tumour Pathology topic. The article reports "5.0 (SD 2.0) in the group with misconceptions and 6.7 (SD 2.4) in the group without misconceptions (p = 0.003)"; no effect size is printed.

> "Formal examination score on Tumour Pathology amounted to 5.0 (SD 2.0) in the group with misconceptions and 6.7 (SD 2.4) in the group without misconceptions (p = 0.003)."

### Olde Bekkink M 2016 (2)

Olde Bekkink M, Donders ART, Kooloos JG, de Waal RM, Ruiter DJ. (2016). Uncovering students’ misconceptions by assessment of their written questions. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0739-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Null comparison on the three other course topics: scores were "not significantly different" (6.9 vs 6.9), indicating the underperformance was topic-specific; equivalence was not tested.

> "The average formal examination score on the other topics of the course, including: (1) Principles of Diagnosis and Cellular Damage; (2) Inflammation and Repair; and (3) Circulatory Disorders, was not significantly different: 6.9 (SD 0.95) in the group with misconceptions versus 6.9 (SD 1.1) in the group without misconceptions"
