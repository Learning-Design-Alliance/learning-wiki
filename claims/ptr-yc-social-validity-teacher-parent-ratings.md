## Evidence

### Glen Dunlap 2018

Glen Dunlap, Phillip Strain, Janice K. Lee, Jaclyn Joseph, and Nancy Leech. (2018). A Randomized Controlled Evaluation of Prevent-Teach-Reinforce for Young Children. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417724874

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttreatment social validity survey of teachers in the PTR-YC condition using an adapted Treatment Acceptability Rating Form on a 5-point Likert scale; 69% of teachers returned the form and average ratings were favorable. No effect size is printed.

> "Inspection of these data indicate that the teachers generally found PTR-YC to be acceptable, likely to produce favorable outcomes, and unlikely to be associated with discomfort or negative side effects."

### Glen Dunlap 2018 (2)

Glen Dunlap, Phillip Strain, Janice K. Lee, Jaclyn Joseph, and Nancy Leech. (2018). A Randomized Controlled Evaluation of Prevent-Teach-Reinforce for Young Children. Topics in Early Childhood Special Education. https://doi.org/10.1177/0271121417724874

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Posttreatment family questionnaire on a 5-point Likert scale completed by 54% of PTR-YC-condition parents; average ratings across topics such as effectiveness, simplicity, and social acceptability were favorable. The article states "These data reflect generally favorable perceptions"; no effect size is printed.

> "These data reflect generally favorable perceptions about the acceptabil- ity and effects of the PTR-YC approach."
