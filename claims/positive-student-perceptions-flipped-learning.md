## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most respondents were satisfied with the flipped-learning setting, agreeing at over 90% rates that videos were available and relevant, activities improved understanding, and the lecturer engaged, clarified and expanded on content. [→ Almodaires et al. 2019](#almodaires-et-al-2019)
`q2 i?` Agreement was lower for having adequate pre-class time (78.9%), inspiration to pursue further learning (74.2%), and desire for more lectures in FL mode (67.9%). [→ Almodaires et al. 2019 (2)](#almodaires-et-al-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Almodaires et al. 2019

Abdullah M. Almodaires, Ghaida M. Alayyar, Tareq O. Almsaud & Faisal M. Almutairi. (2019). The Effectiveness of Flipped Learning: A Quasi-Experimental Study of the Perceptions of Kuwaiti Pre-Service Teachers. International Education Studies, 12(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v12n1p10

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Descriptive analysis of a Likert-scale feedback questionnaire (adapted from Pierce and Fox, 2012) administered to the 128 EG students; total response rate 100% (n = 128), adjusted 30.56%. Items Q1, Q3, Q5, Q8, Q9 and Q10 each exceeded 90% agreement (e.g., Q3 96.1%, Q9 96.9%).

> "The results revealed that most of the respondents were satisfied with the FL setting, with over 90% of the students ‘agreeing’ with six items in the feedback questionnaire"

### Almodaires et al. 2019 (2)

Abdullah M. Almodaires, Ghaida M. Alayyar, Tareq O. Almsaud & Faisal M. Almutairi. (2019). The Effectiveness of Flipped Learning: A Quasi-Experimental Study of the Perceptions of Kuwaiti Pre-Service Teachers. International Education Studies, 12(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/ies.v12n1p10

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same questionnaire's lower-agreement items: adequate time to view videos (Q2, 78.9%), inspiration to pursue further learning (Q6, 74.2%), and desire for more lectures in FL mode (Q7, 67.9%), as printed in Table 6.

> "However, although 74.2% of the students ‘agreed’ that the FL session inspired them to pursue further learning for the module (Item 6), some students (67.9%) expressed the desire for more lectures in the FL mode."
