# Subjective effort ratings are affected by invalid cues and only weakly valid as cues to judged learning outcomes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · theoretical `r3` · `q1`–`q3`

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Learners who report higher effort judge their performance as being lower, suggesting effort is read as a sign of task strenuousness. [→ Katharina Scheiter 2025](#katharina-scheiter-2025)
`q3 i?` A meta-analysis by David et al. found a negative indirect relation between mental effort judgments and learning outcome that is rather minor. [→ Katharina Scheiter 2025 (2)](#katharina-scheiter-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Katharina Scheiter 2025

Katharina Scheiter. (2025). Commentary: How can We Come to Terms when Discussing the Role of Effort from the Perspective of Cognitive Load Theory and Theories of Self-regulated Learning? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10037-z

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary synthesizes findings from several special-issue and prior studies: higher reported effort goes with lower performance judgments, implying learners treat effort as an indication a task is strenuous.

> "learners and problem solvers who report higher effort judge their performance as being lower (David et al., 2024; De Baars et al., 2020; Waldeyer et al., 2024)"

### Katharina Scheiter 2025 (2)

Katharina Scheiter. (2025). Commentary: How can We Come to Terms when Discussing the Role of Effort from the Perspective of Cognitive Load Theory and Theories of Self-regulated Learning? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10037-z

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The commentary reports, citing David et al. (2024), a meta-analysis finding the effort cue's validity limited: a negative indirect relation between mental effort judgments and learning outcome of minor magnitude; no effect size is printed.

> "David et al. furthermore show by means of a meta-analysis that the use of effort as a cue to judge one’s learning outcome is valid only to some extent; while there is a negative indirect relation between mental effort judgments and learning outcome, this effect is rather minor."
