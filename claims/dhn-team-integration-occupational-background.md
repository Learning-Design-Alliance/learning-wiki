## Evidence

### Freisberg L 2026

Freisberg L, Meier-Diedrich E, Heinze M, Schönbeck J, Schubert D, Speck J, Strudwick G, Torous J, Schwarz J. (2026). Expected Competencies and Personal Attributes of Digital Health Navigators to Support Digital Mental Health Care: Focus Group and Interview Study With Patients and Health Care Professionals. JMIR Mental Health. https://doi.org/10.2196/83073

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Qualitative content analysis of the role requirements domain (35 participants). "8/35, 22.86%" named medical assistants, nurses, and physician assistants; others doubted their communicative-therapeutic training and proposed social workers, psychologists, or psychotherapists, and some suggested external peer supporters.

> "Some (8/35, 22.86%) identified medical assistants, nurses, and physician assistants as particularly appropriate candidates, as these professionals are often long-term employees within clinics or practices, possess relevant medical and organizational knowledge, and maintain close relationships with patients."

### Freisberg L 2026 (2)

Freisberg L, Meier-Diedrich E, Heinze M, Schönbeck J, Schubert D, Speck J, Strudwick G, Torous J, Schwarz J. (2026). Expected Competencies and Personal Attributes of Digital Health Navigators to Support Digital Mental Health Care: Focus Group and Interview Study With Patients and Health Care Professionals. JMIR Mental Health. https://doi.org/10.2196/83073

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Same role requirements domain. Participants noted medical assistants operate within tightly scheduled routines, questioned who in a team has capacity for DHN tasks, and emphasized reliable long-term structural anchoring for integration into the treatment concept.

> "Feasibility and workload constraints were thus identified as important practical boundaries that must be considered when defining the scope of DHN responsibilities."
