## Evidence

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2020

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). In a physics curriculum only introductory physics course grades show gender differences but do not predict future course performance for physics majors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multi-group SEM testing factor loadings, indicator intercepts, residual variances, then regression paths; model fit was moderate to good at each step (CFI > 0.90, TLI > 0.90, RMSEA < 0.08). The model was converted to include gender as a categorical predictor.

> "Each step produced statistically non-signiﬁcant changes from the previous according to LRTs, indicating that the es- timates could be ﬁxed to equality across the two groups (p > 0.10 for each step)."

### Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh 2020 (2)

Kyle M. Whitcomb and Chandralekha Singh. (2020). In a physics curriculum only introductory physics course grades show gender differences but do not predict future course performance for physics majors. arXiv preprint. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ab9f1d)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In all three SEM models (Figs. 1, 2, 3) the gender differences took the same form, confined to the high school GPA and introductory physics intercepts; the inconsistent differences in mathematics and advanced physics courses were statistically non-significant controlling for high school GPA.

> "on average, women have slightly higher high school GPA ( β = 0.14, p = 0.002), while men are predicted to have slightly higher grades in introductory physics ( β = −0.19, p < 0.001) when controlling for the high school GPA diﬀerence, and no other gender diﬀerences are predicted anywhere else in the model."
