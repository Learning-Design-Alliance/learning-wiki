Evidence¶

Anna Filighera 2024¶

Anna Filighera, Sebastian Ochs, Tim Steuer, Thomas Tregel. (2024). Cheating Automatic Short Answer Grading with the Adversarial Usage of Adjectives and Adverbs. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 34, 616–646. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-023-00361-2

q2 · i? · causal · r2

Attack experiments on BERT and T5 across SciEntsBank (UA, UQ, UD) and GLUE/SuperGLUE entailment and paraphrase datasets, reported in the Results section (Table 2). The article states "our attack deteriorates a model’s accuracy by 8 to 22 percentage points" across all tested models and datasets.

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • The insertion attack slightly outperforms TextFooler on ASAG but is outperformed by TextFooler on entailment and paraphrase tasks, making attack performance dataset-dependent — related
  • Successful attack adjectives and adverbs mostly appear more often in correct than incorrect training answers, supporting the spurious-correlation explanation for most, but not all, insertion words — related
  • The insertion attack succeeds primarily on low-confidence predictions, where soon-to-be adversarial examples receive probabilities between roughly 0.45 and 0.65 — related
  • Human graders perceive answers with inserted adjectives and adverbs as less natural than unmodified student answers — related
  • The attack does not increase graders' suspicion of cheating; graders even intended to act on manipulated answers less often than on control answers — related
