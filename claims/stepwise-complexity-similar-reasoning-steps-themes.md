### Li Zhien 2026

Li Zhien, Asoodar Maryam, Keulers Tom, Joosten-ten Brinke Desirée, Dolmans Diana. (2026). Ensuring Knowledge Transfer: The Critical Role of “What-If” Discussions in Virtual Patient Simulation. Perspectives on Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.2363

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Thematic analysis of three group interviews (n = 8) conducted by an external interviewer and one researcher, coded deductively–inductively. Students described that “it builds up” across cases and that the VP assignment “used a lot of the same steps”. No effect sizes apply to qualitative findings.

> "Analysis revealed four key themes: “Step-by-step learning with increasing complexity facilitated learning”, “Recognizing the similar steps in clinical reasoning was helpful”, “Reflective thinking through “what-if” discussions led to deeper understanding of the underlying principles and enhanced knowledge transfer”, and “The peer dialogue on VP system feedback facilitated concept abstraction”."
