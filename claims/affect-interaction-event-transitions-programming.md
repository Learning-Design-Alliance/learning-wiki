## Evidence

### Bosch 2017

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Transition analysis interleaving affective states with logged interaction events by timestamp, using only cohort 2 (N=70) because cohort 1 logs lacked activity context; significance threshold p<.000275 after Bonferroni correction over 182 potential transitions.

> "confusion and frustration were both preceded by an incorrect solution submission (SubmitError; L=.07, p<.000275 for confusion, L=.0 9, p<.000275 for frustration) and were followed by a hint request (ShowHint; L=.07, p<.000275 for confusion, L=.09, p<.000275 for frustration)"

### Bosch 2017 (2)

Bosch, N., & D'Mello, S. (2017). The Affective Experience of Novice Computer Programmers. Int J Artif Intell Educ. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0069-5

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same scaffolded-phase event-affect transition analysis; the article reports curiosity, engagement, and boredom linked to reading and coding but not to SubmitError or ShowHint, and that boredom transitioned into coding (L=.44), possibly re-engaging students.

> "curiosity ( L=.20 to reading, L=.2 2 to coding, p<.000275), engagement (L=.09 from reading, L=.51 to coding, p<.000275),and boredom ( L=.07 from reading, L=.44 to coding, p<.000 275) were mainly asso -ciated with assimilation (Reading) and generation (Coding) activities but not with evaluation (SubmitError; all p >.000275) and help-seeking (ShowHint; allp>.000275) activities."
