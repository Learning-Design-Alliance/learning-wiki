q2 · i? · associational · r1

Single cross-sectional study (Wessel et al. 2002) correlating an eight-station real-life OSCE with supervised clinical practice scores. It "found a very weak correlation of ρ = -0.13". No standardised effect size beyond the correlation coefficient was printed.

"used Spearman Rank correlation (ρ) to describe the association between 48 postgrad physical therapy students’ scores across an eight-station real life OSCE examination and their performance scores obtained during supervised clinical practice and found a very weak correlation of ρ = -0.13."

q2 · i3 · quant-synthesis · r1

Single cross-sectional study (Gorman et al. 2010) correlating OSCE scores with course grade and GPA. The printed effect sizes were Pearson r = 0.78 ("0.78 indicating very strong correlation") with the course grade and Pearson r = 0.44 ("0.44 indicating moderate correlation") with GPA.

"compared 66 postgrad physical therapy students’ OSCE score across an eight-station format of a real-life course examination, to their final grade in stated course and to their total GPAs and found a Pearson’s correlation of 0.78 indicating very strong correlation and a Pearson’s correlation of 0.44 indicating moderate correlation respectively."

"Silva et al. 2011 [22] compared the six-station simulated OSCE examination scores of 47 undergrad students with their scores of a traditional exam including four theoretical and one practical question. Pearson’s correlation was = -0.1 indicating a very weak correlation."
