Evidence¶

Kang and Noh 2017¶

Houn Tae Kang, Suk Goo Noh. (2017). The Effect on Elementary Science Education Based on Student's Pre-inquiry. Universal Journal of Educational Research 5(9). https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2017.050908

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Posttest comparison of interest-in-science subelements in the two-class quasi-experiment; overall interest favored the experimental group with a "significant difference". Interest in science learning (3.62 vs 2.66) and science-related activities (3.10 vs 2.50) were also significant.

"Overall, the interest in science was 2.73 points for the controlled group and 3.36 points for the experimental group, indicating a significant difference."

Kang and Noh 2017 (2)¶

Houn Tae Kang, Suk Goo Noh. (2017). The Effect on Elementary Science Education Based on Student's Pre-inquiry. Universal Journal of Educational Research 5(9). https://doi.org/10.13189/ujer.2017.050908

q2 · i? · causal · r1

The article reports that for the science and anxiety subelement "there was no significant difference" between groups (Table 5: 3.65 vs 3.56, p = 0.63); equivalence was not tested.

"Interest in science- related job was also high after the experim ent, and there was no significant difference in the science and anxiety."
