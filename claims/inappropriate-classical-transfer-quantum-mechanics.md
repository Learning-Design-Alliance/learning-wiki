# Undergraduate quantum mechanics students inappropriately transfer classical mechanics concepts into quantum mechanics contexts

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students assume that objects with different labels, such as x and y, are orthogonal or cannot influence each other, carrying over vector-component label conventions from introductory physics. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)
`q2 i?` Students conclude that successive measurements of continuous variables produce somewhat deterministic outcomes whereas successive measurements of discrete variables such as spin can produce very different outcomes. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)
`q2 i?` Asked how to verify experimentally the period of a Quantum Harmonic Oscillator, students commonly proposed performing two successive position measurements separated by a time T, transferring the classical deterministic notion of period. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (3)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-3)
`q2 i?` A majority of students transferred the classical notion of bound states and looked for classical turning points to determine quantum bound states. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016 (4)](#chandralekha-singh-2016-4)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

From written free-response questions and individual interviews in undergraduate QM courses: students assume an object labelled x "is orthogonal to or cannot inﬂuence an object with a label", reflecting a missing quantum knowledge schema.

> "one common difﬁculty is assuming that an object with a label “x" is orthogonal to or cannot inﬂuence an object with a label “y"."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Interview evidence of a classical-quantum paradigm conflict: students concluded that "successive measurements of discrete variables, e.g., spin, can produce very different outcomes" while expecting continuous-variable outcomes to change only gently.

> "Successive measurements of continuous variables of a particle, e.g., position, produce “somewhat" deterministic outcomes whereas successive measurements of discrete variables, e.g., spin, can produce very different outcomes."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (3)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

In a written task on experimentally verifying the QHO period, "A common response was that one must ﬁgure out some way to perform two successive measurements of the position of a particle"; few students proposed ensemble measurements.

> "A common response was that one must ﬁgure out some way to perform two successive measurements of the position of a particle separated by a time T to make sure they are the same."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (4)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Transfer of Learning in Quantum Mechanics. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.05655)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Written responses and interviews on distinguishing classical and quantum bound states showed that "A majority of the students trans- fered the classical notion and looked for classical turning points to determine quantum bound states."

> "A majority of the students trans- fered the classical notion and looked for classical turning points to determine quantum bound states."
