## Evidence

### Petersen Sandra L. 2018

Petersen Sandra L., Erenrich Evelyn S., Levine Dovev L., Vigoreaux Jim, Gile Krista. (2018). Multi-institutional study of GRE scores as predictors of STEM PhD degree completion: GRE gets a low mark. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206570

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Analysis of engineering doctoral students in the four-institution cohort (Table 7). Men who left had mean GRE Q 741.8 versus 723.0 for men who completed, significantly higher at p<0.05; GRE V scores were similar (516.1 both groups).

> "In contrast, men who left engineering doctoral programs had significantly higher GRE Q scores than those who completed degrees."

### Petersen Sandra L. 2018 (2)

Petersen Sandra L., Erenrich Evelyn S., Levine Dovev L., Vigoreaux Jim, Gile Krista. (2018). Multi-institutional study of GRE scores as predictors of STEM PhD degree completion: GRE gets a low mark. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0206570

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Quartile comparison shown in Fig 4 for male engineering and non-engineering students; engineering Q4 mean was 654.4 versus 792.8 for Q1, and completion rates were compared with Chi-Squared tests (p<0.0001).

> "men in the lowest quartile of GRE Q scores in both engineering and non-engineering STEM doctoral programs finished at higher rates than those in the highest quartile."
