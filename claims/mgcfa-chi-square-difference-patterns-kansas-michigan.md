# Chi-square difference tests show significant loading invariance constraints for Kansas Reading but all constraints significant for Michigan Mathematics

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For Kansas MAP Reading tests, chi-square increases are significant for testing L invariance but not significant for testing LV invariance; for Michigan Mathematics tests, all chi-square increases are significant for both L and LV invariance. [→ Shudong Wang 2013](#shudong-wang-2013)

## Evidence

### Shudong Wang 2013

Shudong Wang, Marty McCall, Hong Jiao, & Gregg Harris. (2013). Construct Validity and Measurement Invariance of Computerized Adaptive Testing: Application to Measures of Academic Progress (MAP) Using Confirmatory Factor Analysis. Journal of Educational and Developmental Psychology, 3(1). https://doi.org/10.5539/jedp.v3n1p88

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Nested-model chi-square difference tests in the MGCFA analyses of Kansas MAP Reading and Michigan MAP Mathematics tests across grades. The article notes the chi-square test may be misleading with large samples and recommends other GOF differences as adjuncts.

> "For the Kansas MAP Reading Tests (see Table 8),  χ2 increases ( Δχ2) are significant for testing L invariance at different terms, but not significant for testing LV invariance. The rest of  the states’ results show a similar pattern. For Michigan Mathematics Tests, all  χ2 increases are significant for both L and LV invariance."
