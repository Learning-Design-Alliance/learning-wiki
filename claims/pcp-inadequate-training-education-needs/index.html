q2 · i? · review · r3

Narrative review report of one included UK survey study (Walter et al.) comparing desired education topics; percentages are descriptive findings from that single study.

"In the study by Walter et al., there was a great desire for education on physical effects following cancer treatment (76–86%), but to a lesser extent on psychosocial effects (36-52%)"

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Qualitative studies consistently describe PCPs' lack of knowledge for survivorship care, with confidence depending on experience, sex, and survivor population — related
  • A greater proportion of primary care physicians report confidence in managing psychosocial effects of cancer (24–47%) than physical effects (10–37%) — related
  • Only 9–12% of PCPs felt at least somewhat familiar with surveillance guidelines, and lack of guideline knowledge was a reported barrier — related
  • PCP confidence in managing physical effects varies by symptom: 79% felt confident managing fatigue versus 16% for chemobrain
  • PCPs report stronger skills in detecting cancer recurrences (62–78%) than confidence to screen for recurrence (6–42%)
