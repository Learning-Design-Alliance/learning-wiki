# Students' rules of operation for signed-number arithmetic are applied inconsistently by non-masters and stably by masters

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

The rule a non-mastering student uses on one occasion is a weak predictor of the rule the same student uses on an identical item later in the same test.

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` On two identical 32-item subtests of signed-number operations presented in different random orders, inconsistent rule application was common among students who had not mastered signed-number arithmetic, while students at mastery, who used the right rules, showed a stable pattern. ## Evidence

### Tatsuoka 1989

Tatsuoka, K. K., Birenbaum, M., & Arnold, J. (1989). On the stability of students' rules of operation for solving arithmetic problems. *Journal of Educational Measurement, 26*(4), 351–361. [doi:10.1111/j.1745-3984.1989.tb00339.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3984.1989.tb00339.x)

`q2 · within-student repeated-measures comparison` · `i? · no effect size reported in the abstract` · `n=161 eighth graders` · `associational · r?`

A test of 64 open-ended items, made of two 32-item subtests containing identical items in random order, was given to 161 eighth graders. The rules of operation underlying each student's answers were identified separately for each subtest and compared. Non-masters' rule use was often inconsistent across the two subtests; masters' was stable. The authors discuss the result in terms of a hypothesis-testing account of learning, in which a learner still trying out rules switches between them. Coded from the published abstract; the full text was not read.

## Discussion

A diagnosis of a non-mastering student's error rule from one pass through a test is unstable even within a single sitting, so diagnostic tests that infer a "bug" from one response pattern need repeated items. The finding fits the [knowledge-in-pieces](../theories/knowledge-in-pieces.md) view of errors as context-dependent below mastery, and parallels the instability of algebra mal-rules ([Payne & Squibb 1990](algebra-mal-rules-are-unstable-skewed-and-school-specific.md)).
