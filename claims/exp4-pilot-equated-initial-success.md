# In Experiment 4, piloted question selection and shorter texts narrowed but did not eliminate the initial retrieval success gap between multiple-choice and short-answer formats

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a pilot for Experiment 4, initial success on the selected questions was nearly equal across formats (multiple-choice 89%, short-answer 83%, F B1). [→ Smith 2014](#smith-2014)
`q3 i2` In Experiment 4 itself, initial multiple-choice performance (M = .83) remained higher than short-answer performance (M = .72), F(1, 70) = 8.81, η²p = .11, but the gap was much smaller than in the first two experiments. [→ Smith 2014 (2)](#smith-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Smith 2014

Megan A. Smith and Jeffrey D. Karpicke. (2014). Retrieval practice with short-answer, multiple-choice, and hybrid tests. Memory, 22(7), 784-802. https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2013.831454

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A 12-subject pilot for Experiment 4 selected questions matching initial success across formats: "the multiple-choice group successfully answered 89% and the short-answer group successfully answered 83% correct (F B1)" for the 32 questions used in the experiment.

> "During the pilot the multiple-choice group successfully answered 89% and the short-answer group successfully answered 83% correct (F B1) for the 32 questions that we ultimately used in Experiment 4."

### Smith 2014 (2)

Megan A. Smith and Jeffrey D. Karpicke. (2014). Retrieval practice with short-answer, multiple-choice, and hybrid tests. Memory, 22(7), 784-802. https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2013.831454

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 4 (144 undergraduates, four between-subjects format conditions, shorter TOEFL texts, verbatim questions only) still found higher initial multiple-choice (M = .83) than short-answer (M = .72) performance, F(1, 70) = 8.81, η²p = .11, despite the piloted equating procedure.

> "A one-way ANOVA revealed that performance was higher in the multiple-choice condition (M .83) than in the short-answer condition (M .72); F(1, 70) 8.81, g2p ¼ :11."
