Related Claims¶

  • No included randomized comparison was at low risk of bias, and all 17 non-randomized comparisons were rated serious risk under ROBINS-I — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • The SBLE evidence base contains no randomized experiments and carries high risk of bias — related
  • In randomized trials, long-term outcomes rated high risk of bias had larger significant effects (g = .29) than low/some-concerns outcomes (g = .07, nonsignificant) — related
  • Peer tutoring in clinical simulation is associated with improved technical skills such as laparoscopic tasks, lumbar puncture, and cardiovascular examination — related
  • The overall certainty of evidence for educational programme effects on nurses' knowledge and practice is very low (GRADE), driven by bias, inconsistency and imprecision — related
  • Peer tutoring in clinical simulation is associated with gains in non-technical skills including self-efficacy, self-confidence, self-perception of learning, and reflective abilities — related
  • The evidence base on inclusion effects is methodologically weak: no randomised studies and nearly all synthesised studies at serious risk of bias — related
