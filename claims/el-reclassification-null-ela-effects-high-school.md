# Eighth grade EL reclassification has statistically insignificant effects on 9th–11th grade ELA test scores, with effects bounded between −0.17 and 0.09 SDs

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Reclassification in 8th grade produced statistically insignificant effects on ELA scores in grades 9, 10, and 11; negative effects smaller than −0.17 SDs and positive effects larger than 0.09 SDs can be ruled out. [→ Angela Johnson 2020](#angela-johnson-2020)

## Evidence

### Angela Johnson 2020

Angela Johnson. (2020). The impact of English Learner reclassification on high school reading and academic progress. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis 42 (1), 46-65. https://www.nwea.org/research/publication/the-impact-of-english-learner-reclassification-on-high-school-reading-and-academic-progress/

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Regression discontinuity analysis of administrative data from a large California district estimating causal effects of 8th grade EL reclassification. Effects on 9th–11th grade ELA scores were "statistically insignificant," with a confidence interval ruling out effects below −0.17 SDs or above 0.09 SDs.

> "The estimated effects of eighth grade reclassification on 9th, 10th, and 11th grade ELA test scores were statistically insignificant, and I can rule out negative effects less than −0.17 SDs and positive effects larger than 0.09 SDs."
