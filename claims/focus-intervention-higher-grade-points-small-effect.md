## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Among students who did not withdraw, FOCUS Intervention students earned significantly more grade points than baseline comparison students, though the effect explained only 1% of variation. [→ Mireles 2014](#mireles-2014)

## Evidence

### Mireles 2014

Mireles, S. V., Acee, T. W., & Gerber, L. N. (2014). FOCUS: Sustainable Mathematics Successes. Journal of Developmental Education, 38(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071436

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Multiple regression (bootstrapped, 2,000 samples) on grade points (A=4 to F=0) for non-withdrawing students: FOCUS (n = 119) mean 2.62 (SD = .95) versus comparison (n = 1668) mean 2.11 (SD = 1.22); model F = 7.28(6), p < .01, R2 = .02, intervention η²p = .01. Hispanic and Black, non-Hispanic students earned fewer grade points than White, non-Hispanic students.

> "The results suggested that students in the FOCUS Intervention earned significantly more grade points towards their GPA from College Algebra as compared to students in the baseline comparison group. However, the effect size was small; it only accounted for one percent of the variation in course grade points earned."
