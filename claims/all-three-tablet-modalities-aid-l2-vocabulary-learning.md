## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Pupils gained significant vocabulary knowledge from pre-test to post-test and delayed post-test regardless of practice modality, with no significant main effect of practicing modality on memorization. [→ van Hove 2017](#van-hove-2017)

## Evidence

### van Hove 2017

van Hove, S., vanderhoven, E., & Cornillie, F. (2017). The tablet for Second Language Vocabulary Learning: Keyboard, Stylus or Multiple Choice. Comunicar, 50, XXV. https://doi.org/10.3916/C50-2017-05

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures ANOVA on the quasi-experimental classroom study (282 pupils, three Flemish schools) found only a main effect of time on memorization scores: pre-test M=20.41% rose to post-test M=71.85% and delayed post-test M=60.29%. The article reports "no significant main effect of practicing modality (F(1,170)=.80, p=.45)".

> "Pupils succeeded in gaining significant more knowledge of the provided vocabulary when the pre-test is compared to the two following posttests (p=.00). Scores on the pretest were the lowest (M=20.41%, Sd=11.09%), whereas scores on the posttest were the highest (M=71.85%; Sd=21.05%)"
