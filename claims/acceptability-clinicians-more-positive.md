## Evidence

### Michalowski Martin 2021

Michalowski Martin, Wilk Szymon, Michalowski Wojtek, O'Sullivan Dympna, Bonaccio Silvia, Parimbelli Enea, Carrier Marc, Le Gal Grégoire, Kingwell Stephen, Peleg Mor. (2021). A Health eLearning Ontology and Procedural Reasoning Approach for Developing Personalized Courses to Teach Patients about Their Medical Condition and Treatment. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18147355

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Mixed-methods acceptability study with three clinical specialists and six patient representatives using a Technology Acceptance Model-derived questionnaire with five-point scales and open-ended questions; results summarized in Table 4 and Appendix A tables. Responses were qualitative with quantitative ratings; no effect sizes are printed.

> "Responses to our questionnaires show that both end-user groups were positive about our proof-of-concept and liked how it structures the learning process."

### Michalowski Martin 2021 (2)

Michalowski Martin, Wilk Szymon, Michalowski Wojtek, O'Sullivan Dympna, Bonaccio Silvia, Parimbelli Enea, Carrier Marc, Le Gal Grégoire, Kingwell Stephen, Peleg Mor. (2021). A Health eLearning Ontology and Procedural Reasoning Approach for Developing Personalized Courses to Teach Patients about Their Medical Condition and Treatment. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph18147355

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Same questionnaire study, Results section: the group contrast in perceived advantage of personalization is reported descriptively, with no statistical test or effect size printed.

> "Overall, clinical specialist end-users saw more advantages from having personalized educational materials as compared to the patient representatives. This was especially true for the questions around personalization of the educational materials."
