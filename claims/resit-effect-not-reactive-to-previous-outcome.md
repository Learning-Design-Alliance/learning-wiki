# The resit effect is not explained by a reactive response to passing on the previous trial

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A post-hoc analysis found no evidence that passing (versus failing) the previous exam lowered study-time investment on the current trial, arguing against a gain-driven reactive account of the resit effect. [→ Nijenkamp 2016](#nijenkamp-2016)

## Evidence

### Nijenkamp 2016

Nijenkamp, R., Nieuwenstein, M. R., de Jong, R., & Lorist, M. M. (2016). Do Resit Exams Promote Lower Investments of Study Time? Theory and Data from a Laboratory Study. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161708

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-hoc analysis pooling the no-resit and unconditional-resit conditions of Experiments 1 and 2, comparing trials preceded by passed versus failed exams. Study-time investment for NR was 6.1 (SD = 0.5) after failure versus 6.2 (SD = 0.5) after passing, with "no evidence for lower study-time investment after passing".

> "Bayes factors analyses comparing the effects of passing (Previous Passed—Previous Failed) for the two conditions showed no evidence for lower study-time investment after passing, with Bayes factors of 17.1 and 31.3 in support of the null hypothesis for NR and R1, respectively"
