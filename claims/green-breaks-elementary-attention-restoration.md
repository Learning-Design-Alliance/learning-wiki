# In elementary school students, short-term green breaks restore attention and working memory, with effects on inhibition mixed

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Lucia Mason 2022

Lucia Mason, Angelica Ronconi, Sara Scrimin, Francesca Pazzaglia. (2022). Short-Term Exposure to Nature and Benefits for Students' Cognitive Performance: a Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09631-8

`q3 · i2` · `review · r3`

Within-subjects quasi-experimental study of N = 48 fourth and fifth graders (ages 9–11) in Italy, comparing a 90-minute alpine wood walk with mindful silence and courtyard play. The review's Table 1 reports Hedges' g of 0.44 versus mindful silence and 1.16 versus courtyard play for CPT accuracy.

> "Greater scores (accuracy) in the CPT after walking in nature than sitting for mindful silence (0.44) and playing in the school courtyard (1.16)"

### Lucia Mason 2022 (2)

Lucia Mason, Angelica Ronconi, Sara Scrimin, Francesca Pazzaglia. (2022). Short-Term Exposure to Nature and Benefits for Students' Cognitive Performance: a Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09631-8

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental Study 1 with N = 82 fourth and fifth graders comparing a 30-minute recess in the school garden versus the built courtyard. The review reports significant attention restoration after the garden break and states "no effects emerged for inhibition skill."

> "Results showed that only after the break in the garden, students restored their attention as the scores for sustained and selective attention were significantly higher after the contact with greenness."

### Lucia Mason 2022 (3)

Lucia Mason, Angelica Ronconi, Sara Scrimin, Francesca Pazzaglia. (2022). Short-Term Exposure to Nature and Benefits for Students' Cognitive Performance: a Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09631-8

`q3 · i?` · `review · r3`

Within-subjects quasi-experimental study of N = 62 Danish fourth and fifth graders comparing sitting in a forest versus the classroom before a d2 test. The review reports no beneficial effects of the forest environment on d2 performance; practice effects occurred regardless of environment.

> "After controlling for a number of variables, results showed no beneficial effects of the forestry environment on cognitive performance in d2 test as children had better scores the second time they executed the test, regardless of the environment"
