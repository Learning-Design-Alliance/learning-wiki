`q3 i?` In the reviewed comparison reported by Deslauriers and Wieman, the group taught with interactive engagement methods (quizzes, simulations, clicker questions) scored significantly higher on the QMCS than the traditionally taught group. ## Evidence

### K. Krijtenburg-Lewerissa 2017

K. Krijtenburg-Lewerissa, H. J. Pol, A. Brinkman, and W. R. van Joolingen (2017). Insights into teaching quantum mechanics in secondary and lower undergraduate education. Physical Review Physics Education Research 13, 010109. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.13.010109

`q3 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports, citing Deslauriers and Wieman, a comparison in which one group (N = 57) was taught traditionally while the other (N = 67) experienced interactive engagement methods; "significantly higher scores" were found for the interactive group. No effect size is printed.

> "The QMCS was used to test understanding, and comparison of the results for the two groups showed that the use of interactive engagement methods resulted in significantly higher scores."
