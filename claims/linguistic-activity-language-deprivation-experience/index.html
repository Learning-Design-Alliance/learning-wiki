Evidence¶

Lee et al. 2010¶

Cheu-jey Lee, Glenda Moss, & Elaine Coughlin. (2010). Engaging Pre-Service Teachers in an Exploration of the Politics of Language. Scholar-Practitioner Quarterly, 5(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ974353

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Question 1 responses at Midwest University (30 participants): only two reported their language was not changed; the remaining 28 said their first group's language was changed, letting most relate to having a language taken away.

"The results showed that only two out of 30 participants reported that their language was not changed. Yet the remaining 28 participants said that the language they created in their first group was changed."

Lee et al. 2010 (2)¶

Cheu-jey Lee, Glenda Moss, & Elaine Coughlin. (2010). Engaging Pre-Service Teachers in an Exploration of the Politics of Language. Scholar-Practitioner Quarterly, 5(3). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ974353

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Unguided written reflections at Northwest University (12 participants): one participant's language survived the entire process; the remaining 11 indicated their first-round language was changed, mirroring the Midwest finding.

"Only one out of the 12 participants at Northwest Univer- sity experienced having his/her language survive."
