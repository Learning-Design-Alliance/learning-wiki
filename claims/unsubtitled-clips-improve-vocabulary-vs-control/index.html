Evidence¶

Sirmandi and Sardareh 2016¶

Effat Heidari Sirmandi, Sedigheh Abbasnasab Sardareh. (2016). The Effect of BBC World Clips With and Without Subtitles on Intermediate EFL Learners' Vocabulary Development. Malaysian Online Journal of Educational Sciences. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1116325

q2 · i? · causal · r2

In the same three-group experiment, the unsubtitled group improved from a pretest mean of 18.5 to a posttest mean of 34.9 while the control reached 30.0; the article reports the no-subtitle hypothesis "Is rejected". No effect size is printed.

"Regarding the presented information on pre and posttest of the unsubtitled group in Table 1 and 2, it revealed that the second research hypothesis "Watching BBC World clips without English subtitles have no significant impact on Intermediate Iranian EFL learners' vocabulary development." Is rejected."

Related Claims¶

  • Intermediate EFL learners who watched BBC World clips with bimodal English subtitles significantly outperformed the no-subtitle and control groups on a vocabulary posttest — related
  • One-way ANOVA on posttest scores showed a statistically significant difference among the subtitle, no-subtitle, and control groups (F = 84.161, Sig. = .000) — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The three groups did not differ significantly in vocabulary knowledge at pretest, supporting group homogeneity — related
  • Watching cartoons with English subtitles produces no statistically significant vocabulary-gain advantage over watching without subtitles among upper-intermediate ELT students — related
  • Vocabulary improvement occurred incidentally regardless of subtitles, attributed to target words being contextualized in the cartoons — related
