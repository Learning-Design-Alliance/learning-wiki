## Evidence

### Trejo-Mejía Juan Andrés 2016

Trejo-Mejía Juan Andrés, Sánchez-Mendiola Melchor, Méndez-Ramírez Ignacio, Martínez-González Adrián. (2016). Reliability analysis of the objective structured clinical examination using generalizability theory. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v21.31650

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Random-effects ANOVA-based G-study (n=278) decomposed OSCE score variance into facets; Table 2 prints Station 42.157 (11.4%), Version 0.737 (0.2%), Site 0 (0%), and Station×student residual 187.374 (51%).

> "The generalizability study revealed that the greatest source of variance (Table 2) was the residual error (51%). The stations were another source of variance (11.4%). No significant source of variance was found in the sites or in the test versions (0 and 0.2%, respectively)."

### Trejo-Mejía Juan Andrés 2016 (2)

Trejo-Mejía Juan Andrés, Sánchez-Mendiola Melchor, Méndez-Ramírez Ignacio, Martínez-González Adrián. (2016). Reliability analysis of the objective structured clinical examination using generalizability theory. Medical Education Online. https://doi.org/10.3402/meo.v21.31650

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Discussion interpretation of the same variance-component analysis: the article reports "the students’ variance was 4.8%" and attributes version×station (10.6%) and site×station×version (9.4%) interactions to differences in station scores across versions and sites.

> "Regarding the estimates of the variance components in our OSCE, the students’ variance was 4.8%, which shows how much they differed in their performance."
