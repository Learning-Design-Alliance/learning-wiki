## Evidence

### Davies DJ 2022

Davies DJ, Sam AH, Murphy KG, Khan SA, Choe R, Cleland J. (2022). BMAT's predictive validity for medical school performance: A retrospective cohort study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14819

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multivariable robust linear regressions on ICSM data controlling for A-level scores (1013 students eligible for incremental validity analyses) reported in Results Section 3.1.4. The article reports it "found no associations with effect size over 0.1"; only a test-based significance pattern is printed, so no effect size magnitude is available.

> "Importantly, we found no associations with effect size over 0.1."

### Davies DJ 2022 (2)

Davies DJ, Sam AH, Murphy KG, Khan SA, Choe R, Cleland J. (2022). BMAT's predictive validity for medical school performance: A retrospective cohort study. Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.14819

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Incremental validity regressions at ICSM adjusting for A-level scores reported in Results Section 3.1.4 found "significant negative associations" of BMAT Section 2 with Year 3 and 6 clinical performance, with standardised coefficients β = −0.08 to −0.1, a printed effect size below 0.1 in magnitude.

> "We also found significant negative associations (coefficients ranging from −0.08 to −0.1) between BMAT Section 2 scores and clinical assessment performance in Years 3 and 6."
