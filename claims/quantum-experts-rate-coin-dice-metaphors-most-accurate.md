## Evidence

### Meinsma 2026

Meinsma, A. L., Reijnierse, W. G., & Cramer, J. (2026). Like a coin spinning in the air: the effect of (non-)metaphorical explanations on comprehension and attitudes towards quantum technology. PREPRINT. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1075/msw.25018.mei](https://doi.org/10.1075/msw.25018.mei)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Small survey of Dutch-speaking quantum experts (n = 22 completed surveys) rating five ChatGPT-generated metaphors per phenomenon on 7-point accuracy and validity; these descriptive means selected the metaphors for the experimental condition.

> "Table 1 shows that the coin metaphor scored highest for superposition (M = 4.13, SD = 1.86) and the dice metaphor for entanglement (M = 5.30, SD = 1.89)."

### Meinsma 2026 (2)

Meinsma, A. L., Reijnierse, W. G., & Cramer, J. (2026). Like a coin spinning in the air: the effect of (non-)metaphorical explanations on comprehension and attitudes towards quantum technology. PREPRINT. Originally published 2025. [doi:10.1075/msw.25018.mei](https://doi.org/10.1075/msw.25018.mei)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Same expert survey: on a 7-point scale, experts reported it "very likely" they would use a metaphor with non-quantum experts (M = 5.68, SD = 1.36); descriptive result with no inferential test.

> "Experts indicated it was very likely (M = 5.68, SD = 1.36) that they would use a metaphor in a conversation with non-quantum experts to explain superposition or entanglement."
