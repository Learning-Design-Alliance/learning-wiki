# Exam scores in an interactive Astro 101 class showed no statistical difference between periods when the textbook was required versus optional

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Two-sample t-tests on three common exams found no statistical difference between the textbook-required and textbook-optional groups. [→ Rudolph 2013](#rudolph-2013)

## Evidence

### Rudolph 2013

Rudolph, A. L. (2013). Do You Always Need a Textbook to Teach Astro 101?. Astronomy Education Review. Originally published 2011. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3635](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3635)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental comparison of nine offerings of the same course by one instructor with common exams: five terms with the textbook required (Spring 2007-Spring 2009) and four with it optional (Fall 2009-Spring 2011). The t-tests "strongly confirm the null hypothesis in all three cases"; only p-values are printed, no effect size.

> "The two-tail p-values of 0.367, 0.255, and 0.543 for the first midterm, second midterm, and final exam, respectively suggest that it is between 25-55% likely that any differences between the groups are due to statistical fluctuations rather than to any real differences between the groups."
