# Fish pond effects: above-norm students became more likely to fail algebra when their skills were low relative to classroom peers after sorting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The likelihood of passing decreases if students have substantially lower initial skills than their classroom peers (coefficient of –.25), while it increases if students have stronger skills than classmates (coefficient of .28). ## Evidence

### Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth 2013

Takako Nomi and Elaine M. Allensworth. (2013). Sorting and Supporting: Why Double-Dose Algebra Led to Better Test Scores but More Course Failures. American Educational Research Journal. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212469997

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Logistic models predicting algebra pass rates for above-norm students post-policy. "The likelihood of passing decreases if students have substantially lower initial skills than their classroom peers" — the fish pond effect. The decline in pass rates shrinks from –.24 to –.14 logits and is no longer significant after controlling for relative skills.

> "The likelihood of passing decreases if students have substantially lower initial skills than their classroom peers (coefficient of –.25), while the likelihood of passing increases if students have stronger skills than their classmates (coefficient of .28)."
