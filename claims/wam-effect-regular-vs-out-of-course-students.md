# The weighted average mark of passed exams predicts Bachelor-Master drop-out positively for regular students but not for out-of-course students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Among students who fulfil the regular programme duration, those with a higher weighted average mark have a greater probability of dropping out after the Bachelor degree, at almost all quantile levels. [→ Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015](#cristina-mollica-and-lea-petrella-2015)
`q2 i?` For out-of-course undergraduates, the summed WAM effect is not statistically significant over the full range of quantile values, so acquired competence level does not modify their drop-out decision. [→ Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015 (2)](#cristina-mollica-and-lea-petrella-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015

Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella. (2015). Bayesian binary quantile regression for the analysis of Bachelor-Master transition. [doi:10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bayesian BQR estimates from the Sapienza administrative cohort data show significant positive WAM coefficients for regular students; the authors conjecture brilliant students find jobs earlier or move to other institutions.

> "The significant and positive estimates of the coeﬃcient associated to the WAM regressor reveal that, for almost all quantile levels, this variable inﬂuences the interruption choice of regular students (reference group)."

### Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella 2015 (2)

Cristina Mollica and Lea Petrella. (2015). Bayesian binary quantile regression for the analysis of Bachelor-Master transition. [doi:10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2016.1263835)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same BQR analysis, the WAM effect for out-of-course undergraduates (reference coefficient plus interaction) is not statistically significant at any quantile; the article reports no effect size, only significance status.

> "The latter regression coeﬃcient turns out to be not statistically signiﬁcant over the full range of τ values, meaning that the level of acquired competence does not modify the drop-out decision of out-of-course students."
