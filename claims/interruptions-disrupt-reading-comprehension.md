## Subclaims
`q3 i3` In Experiment 1, participants answered significantly fewer comprehension questions correctly under interruption than no-interruption conditions (d = 2.37). [→ Foroughi 2015](#foroughi-2015)

## Evidence

### Foroughi 2015

Foroughi, C. K., Werner, N. E., Barragán, D., & Boehm-Davis, D. A. (2015). Interruptions Disrupt Reading Comprehension. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/xge0000074

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 1, a within-subjects study of 24 George Mason University students reading SAT passages with 15-second math-problem interruptions between paragraphs. The paired-samples t-test showed significantly poorer comprehension when interrupted, "d = 2.37".

> "A paired-samples t-test revealed a significant difference between the number of correct responses in the interruption (M = 9.04, SD = 2.03, 95% CI [8.23, 9.85]) and no interruption (M = 10.8, SD = 2.11, 95% CI [9.96, 11.64]) conditions, t(23) = 11.63, p < .001, d = 2.37"
