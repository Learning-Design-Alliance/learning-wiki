## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Gamification significantly increased continuous evaluation performance (β=0.54, p<0.0011), which in turn significantly affected course total score (β=0.58, p<0.001), supporting the mediation hypothesis. [→ Ríos-Muñoz 2025](#rios-munoz-2025)

## Evidence

### Ríos-Muñoz 2025

Ríos-Muñoz, G. R., Fuster-Barceló, C., & Muñoz-Barrutia, A. (2025). The impact of gamification on learning outcomes: experiences from a Biomedical Engineering course. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2509.06126](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2509.06126)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Regression analysis of the moderated-mediation model (Fig. 5, Table 5) on N=41 students with gamification as binary independent variable and continuous evaluation and course total score normalized 0-1. The indirect effect Gamification→CE→CT was 0.10 (p<0.05, Table 6). Coefficients are regression coefficients, not standardized effect sizes.

> "The regression analysis in Table 5 shows a significant positive effect of gamification on the mediating continuous evaluation variable, with a coefficientβof 0.54 (p <0.0011, see Table 5), which is also reflected in the significant effect (β= 0.58,p <0.001) of the mediating variable on the dependent one, the course total score."
