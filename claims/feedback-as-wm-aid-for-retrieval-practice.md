# The authors propose feedback as a working-memory aid that may increase the testing effect for learners with lower WM capacity

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` The authors propose that feedback may serve as a WM aid that raises the testing effect for lower-WM-capacity learners, and call for studies testing feedback across WM capacities. [→ Yicong Zheng 2023](#yicong-zheng-2023)

`q2 i?` Providing feedback after retrieval practice reduces WM load, allowing low-WMC learners to benefit from testing similarly to higher-WMC learners; without feedback, older adults showed a negative testing effect. [→ Zheng 2024](#zheng-2024)

`q2 i?` Studies with immediate feedback found no disadvantage for low-WMC individuals in testing effect magnitude, while Tse & Pu found a WMC-by-test-anxiety interaction with foreign-language materials that a follow-up with native-language materials did not replicate. [→ Zheng 2024 (2)](#zheng-2024)

## Evidence

### Yicong Zheng 2023

Yicong Zheng, Pengyuan Sun and Xiaonan L. Liu. (2023). Retrieval practice is costly and is beneficial only when working memory capacity is abundant. npj Science of Learning 8:8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00159-w

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

An author interpretation in the Discussion, offered as one possibility to explain prior findings that older adults benefited from testing only when feedback was provided; it was not tested in this study.

> "One possibility is that feedback serves as a WM aid that reduces WM demands, thus increasing the testing effect for those with lower WM capacity."

### Zheng 2024

Zheng, Y., Shi, A. & Liu, X. L. (2024). A working memory dependent dual process model of the testing effect. npj Science of Learning 9:56. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00268-0

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Cross-study comparison of published studies (Agarwal et al.; Bertilsson et al.; Minear et al.) reported in this Perspective. The article attributes the reversed pattern to feedback: "providing feedback and presenting the correct answer after retrieval can reduce the working memory load for participants with low WMC."

> "participants with lower WMC experienced a more pronounced testing effect compared to those with higher WMC. However, this pattern was speci ﬁc to the condition where feedback was provided after each retr ieval practice trial."
