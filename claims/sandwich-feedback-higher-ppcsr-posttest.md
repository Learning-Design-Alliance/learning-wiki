# Apprenticeship training with sandwich feedback after DOPS assessment yields higher post-test perceived perioperative competence than traditional training in final-semester OR technology students

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Final-semester OR technology students trained with sandwich feedback-based learning scored significantly higher on the PPCS-R total at post-test than students in traditional-based training (P < 0.001; Cohen's d = 2.56). [→ Hosseinpour Azam 2024](#hosseinpour-azam-2024)

## Evidence

### Hosseinpour Azam 2024

Hosseinpour Azam, Nasiri Morteza, Keshmiri Fatemeh, Arabzadeh Tayebeh Tarabzadeh, Sharafi Hossein. (2024). Comparison of the effects of apprenticeship training by sandwich feedback and traditional methods on final-semester operating room technology students' perioperative competence and performance: a randomized, controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05598-6

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled trial of 30 final-semester OR technology students stratified-randomized to FBL (n = 15) or TBT (n = 15), analyzed with the Mann-Whitney U test. Table 2 reports post-test total perioperative competence of 106.66 ± 4.40 (FBL) vs 95.93 ± 3.28 (TBT), with a printed Cohen's d of 2.56 for the mean change. The trial "confirmed that the mean of the post-test total score obtained by the students in the FBL group was significantly higher".

> "Yet, this test confirmed that the mean of the post-test total score obtained by the students in the FBL group was significantly higher than that acquired by the students in the TBT group (P < 0.001)."
