## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students reporting rare video game play (once per week or fewer) showed lower gains in the VR treatment than in the video and image treatments, with differences not significant at the 0.05 level. [→ J. R. Smith 2018](#j-r-smith-2018)

## Evidence

### J. R. Smith 2018

J. R. Smith, A. Byrum, T. M. McCormick, N. Young, C. Orban, and C. D. Porter. (2018). A controlled study of stereoscopic virtual reality in freshman electrostatics. Originally published 2017. [doi:10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2017.pr.089)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Secondary analysis of the randomized electric field study splitting data by self-reported prior video game play. The authors interpret both this and the frequent-player result as supporting the hypothesis that successful VR use depends on video game experience as a proxy for 3D visuospatial rotation familiarity.

> "Among those students who reported rarely playing video games, students given the VR treatment had lower gains than either of the other treatments, but these differences were not signiﬁcant to the 0.05 level."
