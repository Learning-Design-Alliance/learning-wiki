# Pedagogical agents significantly improve learners' self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.29), with effects moderated by grade level, facial expressions, and reliability reporting

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1`–`i2`

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Across 57 comparisons from 29 studies, PAs had a small significant positive effect on self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.29, p < 0.001). [→ Gladstone 2025](#gladstone-2025)
`q3 i2` PAs using facial expressions had a moderate significant effect on self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.46, p < 0.01), while those without had no significant impact. [→ Gladstone 2025 (2)](#gladstone-2025-2)
`q3 i2` The strongest self-efficacy effect was for students in grades 7-9 (g = 0.77, p < 0.001), though from only two comparisons in one study; adult learners outside postsecondary education showed g = 0.44 (p < 0.01). [→ Gladstone 2025 (3)](#gladstone-2025-3)

## Evidence

### Gladstone 2025

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Three-level meta-analysis with CHERVE of PA-versus-non-PA comparisons measuring self-efficacy expectations. Pooled effect was "g = 0.29, p < 0.001" with significant within-study heterogeneity (Q(56) = 475.51).

> "A random effects 3LMA with CHERVE found that the overall impact of PAs on learners' self-efficacy expectations was g = 0.29, p < 0.001 (kcomparisons = 57, kstudies = 29)."

### Gladstone 2025 (2)

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator analysis within the self-efficacy 3LMA, based on 12 studies with 24 comparisons, found facial expressions significantly moderated the effect (F(2.00, 9.41) = 6.46, p = 0.02).

> "PAs that used facial expressions had a moderate impact on learners' self-efficacy expectations (g = 0.46, p < 0.01), while those without facial expressions had no significant impact compared to non-PA systems."

### Gladstone 2025 (3)

Gladstone, J. R., Schroeder, N. L., Heidig, S., Zhang, S., Palaguachi, C., & Pitera, M. (2025). Do Pedagogical Agents Enhance Student Motivation? Unraveling the Evidence Through Meta-Analysis. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10050-2

`q3 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Grade level significantly moderated self-efficacy effects (F(4.00, 6.83) = 11.42, p < 0.01). Primary, upper secondary, and post-secondary learners showed no significant benefits or detriments.

> "the strongest effect was found for students in grades 7–9 (g = 0.77, p < 0.001); however, there were only two comparisons extracted from one study (Plant et al., 2009). We also found a moderate, significant impact on adult learners outside of post-secondary education (g = 0.44, p < 0.01)."
