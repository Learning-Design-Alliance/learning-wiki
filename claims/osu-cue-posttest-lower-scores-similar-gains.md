# OSU students scored lower on the CUE post-test than CU students (36.6% vs 47.8%) but showed similar normalized learning gains (~33%) in PER-based courses

## Evidence

### Zwolak 2015

Zwolak, J. P. and Manogue, C. A. (2015). Assessing student reasoning in upper-division electricity and magnetism at Oregon State University. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Analysis of CUE post-test scores from OSU (N = 37) collected 2010–2013, compared with published CU data. OSU students scored "on average 36.6± 2.4%" versus 47.8±1.9% at CU; scores were verified normal via Shapiro-Wilk (p = 0.36).

> "Students at OSU scored on the post-test on average 36.6± 2.4% (compared to 47.8± 1.9% at CU reported in Ref. [5]), with the spread of their performance ranging from about 12% to 70%."

### Zwolak 2015 (2)

Zwolak, J. P. and Manogue, C. A. (2015). Assessing student reasoning in upper-division electricity and magnetism at Oregon State University. [doi:10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevstper.11.020125)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Paired pre/post analysis for the N = 24 OSU students taking both tests, using Hake's normalized gain. The gain was confirmed significant by paired t-test (p < 10−4); the authors note it is "similar to gains of 34%" at CU.

> "For students who took both the pre- and post-tests (N = 24), we found an average normalized gain of 33% (28% non-normalized), which is similar to gains of 34% (normalized) and 24% (non-normalized) at CU reported in Ref. [5]."
