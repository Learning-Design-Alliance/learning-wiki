# Teacher PD on differentiated instruction produced a small significant positive effect on mathematics achievement growth in Year 1 (β = 0.15, p = .007)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In Year 1, participation in the PD programme significantly predicted a steeper slope of mathematics achievement growth (β = 0.15, p = .007), with Cohort 1 students gaining about 2.5 CMT points more than other cohorts. [→ Prast 2018](#prast-2018)

## Evidence

### Prast 2018

Prast, E. J., Van de Weijer-Bergsma, E., Kroesbergen, E. H., & Van Luit, J. E. H. (2018). Differentiated instruction in primary mathematics: Effects of teacher professional development on student achievement. Learning and Instruction, 54, 22-34. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.009

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Latent growth curve analysis (Model 2) of Cito Mathematics Test scores from 4751 Year 1 students in 30 schools, with PD participation as a predictor of the growth slope and cluster-robust standard errors. The article reports a significant but small positive slope effect; adding the intervention explained an additional 2% of slope variance.

> "PD in Year 1 had a significant but small positive eﬀect on the slope: β = 0.15, p = .007. Thus, students in Cohort 1 gained about 2.5 points more on the CMT in the course of Year 1 than students in the other cohorts (average growth is 14.4 points)."
