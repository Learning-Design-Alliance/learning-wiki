## Evidence

### Nguyen 2018

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Random-effects meta-analysis of 20 U.S. on-time degree completion estimates from causal studies; the pooled estimate is that grant aid "increases on-time and delayed completion by 2.4 percentage points and 2.5 percentage points respectively." No standardized effect size is printed.

> "In terms of degree completion, we find that grant aid increases on-time and delayed completion by 2.4 percentage points and 2.5 percentage points respectively with reasonably precise standard errors."

### Nguyen 2018 (2)

Nguyen, T.D., Kramer, J.W., & Evans, B.J. (2018). The Effects of Grant Aid on Student Persistence and Degree Attainment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of the Causal Evidence (CEPA Working Paper No. 18-04). Stanford Center for Education Policy Analysis. http://cepa.stanford.edu/wp18-04

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Same random-effects meta-analysis, delayed completion subgroup of 19 U.S. estimates; the pooled delayed-completion effect is 2.5 percentage points with reasonably precise standard errors, as printed in the same results sentence. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "In terms of degree completion, we find that grant aid increases on-time and delayed completion by 2.4 percentage points and 2.5 percentage points respectively with reasonably precise standard errors."
