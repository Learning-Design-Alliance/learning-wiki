# Students practicing the ABCDE approach in VR liked the way they practiced and rated it a better way to learn more often than students practicing with traditional equipment

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Berg Helen 2020

Berg Helen, Steinsbekk Aslak. (2020). Is individual practice in an immersive and interactive virtual reality application non-inferior to practicing with traditional equipment in learning systematic clinical observation? A randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-020-02030-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Questionnaire secondary outcomes in the same randomized controlled trial (VR n=149, TP n=140), dichotomized Likert items. VR students reported "liking the type of practice" at 82.6% vs 36% and "a good way to learn" at 85.1% vs 48.2%, both p < 0.001; no standardized effect size printed.

> "Students practicing VR scored higher on liking the type of practice (82.6% vs. 36%, absolute difference 46.6% points, 95% CI 36.5 to 56.6) and on the training and practice were a good way to learn the ABCDE approach (85.1% vs. 48.2%, absolute difference 36.9% points, 95% CI 26.8 to 47.0)"
