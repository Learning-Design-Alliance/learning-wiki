Evidence¶

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken 2023¶

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken. (2023). How Learners Use Their Hands for Learning: an Eye-Tracking Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09833-2

q2 · i? · causal · r?

Narrative review synthesis by the authors, attributing benefits to studies such as Macken and Ginns (2014), Hu et al. (2015), Agostinho et al. (2015), Tang et al. (2019), and Wang et al. (2022); the review's own assertion, not a pooled estimate.

"this review of studies including paper-based and digital material (iPad and touchscreens) and participants of different age groups (children, adults) consistently confirms the benefits of tracing across a range of performance measures, including retention, comprehension, and transfer problem solving"

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken 2023 (2)¶

Babette Park · Andreas Korbach · Paul Ginns · Roland Brünken. (2023). How Learners Use Their Hands for Learning: an Eye-Tracking Study. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09833-2

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review reports mixed cognitive-load findings: "hypotheses concerning cognitive load have not been confirmed in all studies", while noting recent experiments (Ginns & King, 2021; Tang et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022) confirmed lower extraneous load and tracing-with-imagination studies lower intrinsic load.

"However, hypotheses concerning cognitive load have not been confirmed in all studies"
