# Higher ADHD scores increase the variability of semantic search paths via more long-distance jumps, but not mean semantic distance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean semantic distance did not depend on ADHD traits, but the standard deviation of distances increased significantly with ADHD score. [→ Van den Driessche 2019](#van-den-driessche-2019)

## Evidence

### Van den Driessche 2019

Van den Driessche, C., Chevrier, F., Cleeremans, A. & Sackur, J. (2019). Lower Attentional Skills predict increased exploratory foraging patterns. Scientific Reports 9:10948. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-46761-0

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regressions on per-child mean and SD of word2vec semantic distances in the 87-child study; variability rose with ADHD ratings while the mean did not. Quantile regression was significant only at the 0.95 quantile (β = 0.012, p = 0.03), and median-split groups differed from quantile 0.87 to 0.99.

> "we found that the mean distance did not depend on ADHD traits (p > 0.3). However, we found a significant and a positive effect on the standard deviation (β = 0.0006, SD = 0.0003, z = 2.38, p < 0.02) of the distances"
