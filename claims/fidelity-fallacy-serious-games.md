# Increased perceptual fidelity fails to reliably improve serious game learning outcomes (the fidelity fallacy)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Empirical evidence shows that increased fidelity (visual realism and immersion) does not reliably improve learning or training outcomes in serious games, and in some cases increases extraneous cognitive load without proportional learning benefit. [→ Tripathi 2026](#tripathi-2026)

## Evidence

### Tripathi 2026

Tripathi, P., & Kapralos, B. (2026). AI-Enabled Serious Games: Integrating Intelligence and Adaptivity in Training Systems. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21962

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The chapter reports, citing prior systematic reviews, that the assumption that more realistic environments would improve training effectiveness was contradicted by empirical evidence. In some cases higher fidelity "increase[s] extraneous cognitive load... without proportional learning benefit while escalating development costs."

> "However, empirical evidence contradicted this assumption and has shown that increased fidelity (sometimes called the fidelity fallacy) fails to reliably improve learning outcomes [42]."
