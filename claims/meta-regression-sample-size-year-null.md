# The effect size does not vary significantly with sample size or publication year in meta-regression

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q2` quasi-experiment
> **Currency** · newest study 2018 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Meta-regression (mixed effect regression, method of moments) found no significant differences for sample size (slope 0.00647, p = 0.43835) or year (slope 0.03715, p = 0.19629). [→ San 2018](#san-2018)

## Evidence

### San 2018

San, I. & Kis, A. (2018). Effect of traditional methods in geometry and numbers learning domains on academic achievement: A Meta-analysis study. International Journal of Research in Education and Science (IJRES), 4(2), 544-554. https://doi.org/10.21890/ijres.428950

`q2 · i?`

Continuous moderator analysis reported in Table 5 of the meta-analysis; neither continuous moderator significantly predicted the effect size (p > .05 for both).

> "It is seen in Table 4 that there is no significant difference in meta -regression of sample size and year variables (p>.05). In other words, the effect of traditional methods in geometry and numbers learning domain on academic achievement does not vary significantly according to sample size and year."
