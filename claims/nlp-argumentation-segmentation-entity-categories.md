# Replacing context-specific terms with syntactic descriptors and entity categories allows NLP rules for argumentation-component segmentation that are less context dependent

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` A pre-processing step replacing context-specific terms with syntactic descriptors and nouns with entity categories (e.g., substituting "location" or "city" for "Sydney") lets software compute rules less dependent on context, addressing over-generalization to specific training sets. [→ Gerry Stahl 2012](#gerry-stahl-2012)

## Evidence

### Gerry Stahl 2012

Gerry Stahl. (2012). Cognizing mediating: Unpacking the entanglement of artifacts with collective minds. Computer-Supported Collaborative Learning 7:187–191. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-012-9148-x

`q2 · i?`

Editorial description of the approach by Mu, Stegmann, Mayfield, Rosé & Fischer for automating analysis of online collaborative discussion into utterances expressing argumentation components such as claims, evidence, and critique. The editorial calls it "a promising approach to automating such analysis."

> "To overcome typical over-generalization to specific training sets, the approach tested here replaces the context-specific terms in a corpus with syntactic descriptors and replaces the nouns with entity categories—e.g., substituting "location" or "city" for "Sydney.""
