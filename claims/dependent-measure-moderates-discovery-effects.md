# Dependent measure moderates discovery effects: acquisition scores amplify the explicit-instruction advantage, and reaction-time measures reverse the enhanced-discovery advantage

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` In the unassisted meta-analysis, acquisition scores (d = -0.95) yielded larger explicit-instruction advantages than post-tests or time to solution; in the enhanced meta-analysis, reaction-time measures (d = -0.72) favored comparison conditions, opposite to post-tests (d = 0.28). [→ Alfieri 2011](#alfieri-2011)

## Evidence

### Alfieri 2011

Alfieri, L., Brooks, P. J., Aldrich, N. J., & Tenenbaum, H. R. (2011). Does Discovery-Based Instruction Enhance Learning? Journal of Educational Psychology, 103(1), 1–18. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021017

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Dependent-measure moderator analysis in the enhanced-discovery meta-analysis, Q(4) = 64.60, p < .001. Post-test (d = 0.28), acquisition (d = 0.54), and self-rating (d = 1.25) measures favored enhanced discovery, whereas reaction-time measures (d = -0.72) favored comparison conditions, as the quote shows.

> "the mean effect size derived from reaction time measures was significantly different (i.e., significantly opposite in effect size direction) from both the mean effect size derived from acquisition scores, Q(1) = 10.19, p = .001, and the mean effect size derived from post-tests, Q(1) = 31.61, p < .001."
