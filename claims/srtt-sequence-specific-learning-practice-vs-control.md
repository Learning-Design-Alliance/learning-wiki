## Evidence

### Dahm 2026

Dahm, S. F., Kraft, V., Martini, M., Krause, D. & Hermsdoerfer, J. (2026). Motor sequences resist automatization as attentional demands increase with sequence learning. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00412-y

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Mixed ANOVA on response times in the ten-session SRTT study (N = 87 after exclusions), with Instruction and Anticipatory Cues as between-subject factors. The Sequence main effect was significant, and the Sequence*Session interaction showed the practice-control difference grew from ΔM = 4 ± 14 ms at Pretest to ΔM = 161 ± 42 ms at Posttest (d = 3.5).

> "Sequence-specific learning was indicated by the significant main effect of Sequence, F (1, 83) = 1328, p < 0.001, ƞ2 p = 0.94, by shorter RTs in the Practice Sequence than in the Control Sequence."
