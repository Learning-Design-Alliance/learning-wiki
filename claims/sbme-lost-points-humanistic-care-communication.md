`q3 i?` In the lost-score analysis, the SBME group improved most on humanistic care (69.2%), doctor–patient communication (56.3%), and aseptic concept (50.0%), and least on basic knowledge (15.5%) and operation skills (18.4%). [→ Wang S 2021](#wang-s-2021)

## Evidence

### Wang S 2021

Wang S, Ren X, Ye J, Wang W, Huang H, Qin C. (2021). Exploration of simulation-based medical education for undergraduate students. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000025982

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Lost-points analysis in the Results section classified lost scores into five aspects; per capita losses (e.g., humanistic care 0.4 SBME vs 1.3 control) yielded the printed improvement rates. The SBME group outperformed the control group across all five aspects.

> "SBME may significantly improve the performance of students in humanistic care (improvement rate: 69.2%), doctor–patient communication (56.3%), and aseptic concept (50.0%) yet only slightly improve their basic knowledge (15.5%) and operation skills (18.4%)."
