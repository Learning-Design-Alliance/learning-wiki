# Lower T1w/T2w ratios predicted response-time gains in primary visual and motor cortex too, suggesting a non-FPS-specific cortical property, while VTA connectivity with control ROIs predicted nothing

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` VTA connectivity with visual and motor control ROIs did not predict learning changes, but lower baseline T1w/T2w ratios in visual and motor ROIs predicted greater response-time improvements. [→ Boroshok 2022](#boroshok-2022)
`q3 i?` The authors interpret the visual/motor T1w/T2w finding as indicating the response-time effect may reflect a more global property of cortex rather than an FPS-specific one. [→ Boroshok 2022 (2)](#boroshok-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Boroshok 2022

Boroshok, A. L., Park, A. T., Fotiadis, P., Velasquez, G. H., Tooley, U. A., Simon, K. R., Forde, J. C. P., Delgado Reyes, L. M., Tisdall, M. D., Bassett, D. S., Cooper, E. A. & Mackey, A. P. (2022). Individual differences in frontoparietal plasticity in humans. npj Science of Learning 7:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00130-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Sensitivity analysis in the same 46 adults using two control ROIs (primary visual and primary motor cortex) not expected to predict learning. VTA connectivity with neither control ROI predicted accuracy or response-time change, and control-ROI T1w/T2w ratios were not associated with accuracy change.

> "Resting-state functional connectivity between the VTA and visual and motor ROIs did not predict changes in accuracy or response time following training. Baseline T1w/T2w ratios in visual and motor ROIs were not associated with changes in accuracy."

### Boroshok 2022 (2)

Boroshok, A. L., Park, A. T., Fotiadis, P., Velasquez, G. H., Tooley, U. A., Simon, K. R., Forde, J. C. P., Delgado Reyes, L. M., Tisdall, M. D., Bassett, D. S., Cooper, E. A. & Mackey, A. P. (2022). Individual differences in frontoparietal plasticity in humans. npj Science of Learning 7:14. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-022-00130-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same sensitivity analysis: lower baseline T1w/T2w ratios in primary visual (β = 0.407, pFDR = 0.01) and primary motor (β = 0.426, pFDR = 0.01) cortex predicted greater response-time improvements. The article states this may indicate the effect "is representative of a more global property of cortex".

> "However, lower baseline T1w/T2w ratios were associated with greater improvements in response time (visual: β = 0.407, 95% CI [0.150, 0.665], p = 0.005, pFDR = 0.01; motor: β = 0.426, 95% CI [0.136, 0.715], p = 0.003, pFDR = 0.01)."
