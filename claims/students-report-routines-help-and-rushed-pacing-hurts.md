# Students value knowing class routines and report that rushed pacing, especially in Advanced Placement courses, is a barrier to mastering content

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Knowing the instructional routines of their classes helps students learn effectively by providing clear teacher expectations and helping them manage time and workload. [→ Viki M. Young 2026](#viki-m-young-2026)
`q2 i?` Pacing is a barrier when teachers move on before students master content; in many courses, especially Advanced Placement courses, time pressure leads teachers to move too quickly with little opportunity to address errors. [→ Viki M. Young 2026 (2)](#viki-m-young-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Viki M. Young 2026

Viki M. Young, Ph.D., Mai Chou Vang, Zareen Kasad, Isabelle Acosta, and Alicia Frausto with Cassie Graves. (2026). Reimagining High School: Student Experiences Today, Hopes for Tomorrow. Digital Promise, Center for Learner Pathway Innovations. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/22bd5fad-ec7b-4ec7-a86f-0bed0800ba30

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Learning and Engagement findings. An 11th-grader described a very organized class where "I knew every single week what assignment was going to happen, and it just felt more reliable," making learning easier alongside sports, jobs, and clubs.

> "Knowing the instructional routines of their classes helps students learn eﬀectively; by providing them with a clear sense of teacher expectations and how to manage their time and workload."

### Viki M. Young 2026 (2)

Viki M. Young, Ph.D., Mai Chou Vang, Zareen Kasad, Isabelle Acosta, and Alicia Frausto with Cassie Graves. (2026). Reimagining High School: Student Experiences Today, Hopes for Tomorrow. Digital Promise, Center for Learner Pathway Innovations. https://digitalpromise.dspacedirect.org/items/22bd5fad-ec7b-4ec7-a86f-0bed0800ba30

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Same section: students greatly appreciate when teachers slow the pace for questions or deeper exploration even if it means falling behind the pacing guide, and appreciate access to teachers and tutoring for one-on-one support when pacing feels rushed.

> "In many courses, especially Advanced Placement courses, pressure to get through a unit within a certain timeframe can lead teachers to move through the material too quickly without giving students enough time to fully understand the concepts."
