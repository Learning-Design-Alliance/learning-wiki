# In the empirical growth mindset study, MIRT scoring yields substantively higher intercept and slope means and variances, including an intercept-slope covariance of .13 that is indistinguishable from zero under other approaches

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Applying three IRT scoring approaches to growth mindset survey responses from 2,319 to 3,266 students, the MIRT model produced substantively higher intercept and slope means and variances, and an intercept-linear slope covariance of .13 versus approximately zero for other approaches. [→ Kuhfeld 2020](#kuhfeld-2020)

## Evidence

### Kuhfeld 2020

Kuhfeld, Megan, and James Soland. (2020). Estimating Student Growth on Psychological and Social-emotional Constructs: A Comparison of Multiple Scoring Approaches. EdWorkingPaper No. 20-193. https://www.edworkingpapers.com/ai20-193

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The empirical study applied IRT approaches 1-3 to growth mindset item responses from a single California district cohort followed from 5th to 8th grade, fitting LGCMs with a quadratic term in lavaan. The article reports the intercept variance was .49 for the MIRT model compared to ~.24 for the other two IRT models.

> "The most pronounced differences across models were for the covariances of the random effects. In particular, the covariance between the intercept and linear slope was .13 for the MIRT model, but was indistinguishable from zero for the other approaches."
