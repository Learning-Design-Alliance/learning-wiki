## Subclaims
`q4 i?` In the pooled two-province RCT sample, providing free eyeglasses improved myopic students' standardized math scores by 0.060 SD (unadjusted) and 0.062 SD (adjusted), significant at the 1% level. [→ Du Kang 2022](#du-kang-2022)

## Evidence

### Du Kang 2022

Du Kang, Wang Huan, Ma Yue, Guan Hongyu, Rozelle Scott. (2022). Effect of Eyeglasses on Student Academic Performance: What Matters? Evidence from a Randomized Controlled Trial in China. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph191710923

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Intention-to-Treat OLS analysis of the cluster-randomized trial (2087 myopic students followed up in 168 schools), reported in Table 2. The article reports the ITT effect of "0.060 standard deviations" unadjusted and 0.062 SD adjusted, both significant at the 1% level; no standardized effect size beyond the SD coefficient is printed.

> "Compared to the control group, providing subsidized eyeglasses improved the average test score of students with myopia by 0.060 standard deviations using the unadjusted model (Column 1). The coefficient increases to 0.062 standard deviations when controlling for all baseline covariates (Column 2). These estimates are both statistically significant at the 1% level."
