## Evidence

### Xin Kang 2021

Xin Kang, Stephen Matthews, Virginia Yip and Patrick C. M. Wong. (2021). Language and nonlanguage factors in foreign language learning: evidence for the learning condition hypothesis. npj Science of Learning 6:28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00104-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple linear regression with L2 (English) HKDSE grades as dependent variable, controlling for gender, musical training, family SES, nonverbal IQ, and age. Musical training (β = 0.31, p = 0.015), family SES (β = 0.02, p < 0.001), and age (β = −0.16, p = 0.015) also significantly predicted L2.

> "Importantly, L1 (Chinese) HKDSE grades also signi ﬁcantly predicted L2 (English) HKDSE grades and exerted the strongest effect of any of the signi ﬁcant predictors ( ΔR2 = 0.06, p < 0.001)."

### Xin Kang 2021 (2)

Xin Kang, Stephen Matthews, Virginia Yip and Patrick C. M. Wong. (2021). Language and nonlanguage factors in foreign language learning: evidence for the learning condition hypothesis. npj Science of Learning 6:28. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00104-9

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Second regression model with L3 Global scores as dependent variable, additionally including motivational and affective predictors. Attitude (β = 0.13, p = 0.043) and age (β = −0.08, p = 0.044) also significantly predicted L3 proficiency.

> "Importantly, L2 pro ﬁciency ( ΔR2 = 0.06, p < 0.001) was the most signiﬁcant predictor of L3 pro ﬁciency, while L1 pro ﬁciency ( β = −0.02, p = 0.580) was not a signiﬁcant contributor."
