## Subclaims
`q2 i2` The humor treatment significantly lowered post-treatment disgust scores relative to the control group, explaining about 7% of the variance (partial eta-squared = 0.072). [→ Randler et al. 2016](#randler-et-al-2016)

## Evidence

### Randler et al. 2016

Christoph Randler, Peter Wüst-Ackermann & Eda Demirhan. (2016). Humor Reduces Anxiety and Disgust in Anticipation of an Educational Dissection in Teacher Students. International Journal of Environmental & Science Education, 11(4), 421-432. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1094636

`q2 · i2` · `causal · r2`

General linear model with post-disgust as dependent variable, treatment as fixed factor and pretest disgust as covariate, in a study of 114 teacher students in intact classes. The treatment effect was significant with "about 7% of the variance" explained (partial eta-squared 0.072, Table 2).

> "However, treatment showed a signi ficant influence on post -disgust scores (Table 2). The estimated marginal means (and SE) were 2.44 ± 0.04 after the life history film and 2.26 ± 0.05 after the humor film clips. The treatment explained about 7% of the variance"

### Randler et al. 2016 (2)

Christoph Randler, Peter Wüst-Ackermann & Eda Demirhan. (2016). Humor Reduces Anxiety and Disgust in Anticipation of an Educational Dissection in Teacher Students. International Journal of Environmental & Science Education, 11(4), 421-432. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1094636

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired-samples t-tests within each treatment group showed no significant change in the life-history group, while the humor group's disgust dropped significantly after the film (t = 4.574, p < 0.001).

> "Disgust scores did not change significantly in the life-history film treatment (t = 0.975, p = 0.333; Figure 1). In the humor treatment, disgust scores were significantly lower after the treatment (t = 4.574, p < 0.001)."
