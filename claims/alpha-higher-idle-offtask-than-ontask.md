# Posterior alpha power is higher during idle and off-task states than during on-task states in cooperative learning

## Subclaims
`q2 i2` Alpha power showed a significant main effect of state (F(2, 184) = 7.36, p < 0.001, η²partial = 0.07); idle vs. on-task d = 0.56 and off-task vs. on-task d = 0.55, with no idle–off-task difference (d < 0.01). [→ Ido Davidesco 2026](#ido-davidesco-2026)

## Evidence

### Ido Davidesco 2026

Ido Davidesco, Yushuang Liu, Kim Chaloner, Emma Laurent, Gabriella A. Ali, Sarah C. Creider, Sean Hughes, Laura Noejovich, Henry Valk, Dana Bevilacqua, David Poeppel & Suzanne Dikker. (2026). The dynamics of student engagement in cooperative science learning: a multimodal approach. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-026-00457-z

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

EEG analysis of 24 students with adequate data, comparing relative alpha power (8–12 Hz) across video-coded engagement states with states and ROI as fixed effects. An omnibus main effect of ROI (η²partial = 0.30) was also significant; the state × ROI interaction was not.

> "Post hoc tests revealed that, across ROIs, when students were idle or off-task, they showed higher levels of alpha power than when they were on-task (Idle vs. on-task:t(184) = 3.35,p = 0.003,d = 0.56; off-task vs. on-task:t(184) = 3.30, p = 0.003,d = 0.55). No difference in alpha power was observed between idle and off-task states ( t(184) = 0.06, p = 0.99, d < 0.01)."
