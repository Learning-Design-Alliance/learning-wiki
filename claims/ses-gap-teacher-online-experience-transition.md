## Evidence

### Aymee Alvarez-Rivero 2023

Aymee Alvarez-Rivero, Candice Odgers and Daniel Ansari. (2023). Elementary school teachers' perspectives about learning during the COVID-19 pandemic. npj Science of Learning 8:40. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00191-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-sectional survey analysis of 911 US and Canadian elementary teachers. The article reports the SES gradient in prior online teaching experience as statistically significant, τc = 0.22, p < 0.001, and notes it as an association that was "statistically significant"; no standardised effect size such as d or r is printed.

> "the proportion of teachers with no previous experience teaching online goes from 40% for high-SES students, to more than 75% for low SES students. This association was statistically significant ðτc ¼ 0:22; p< 0:001Þ"

### Aymee Alvarez-Rivero 2023 (2)

Aymee Alvarez-Rivero, Candice Odgers and Daniel Ansari. (2023). Elementary school teachers' perspectives about learning during the COVID-19 pandemic. npj Science of Learning 8:40. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-023-00191-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Survey correlational analysis reporting that perceived challenge of the switch was associated with less online experience (τc = −0.18) and poor digital skills (τc = −0.11), both p < 0.001. The article calls these associations significant without printing a standardised effect size or claiming causation.

> "switching to remote education was increasingly challenging for teachers with less experience teaching online ðτc ¼/C0 0:18; p< 0:001Þ, and those with poor digital skills ðτc ¼/C0 0:11; p< 0:001Þ"
