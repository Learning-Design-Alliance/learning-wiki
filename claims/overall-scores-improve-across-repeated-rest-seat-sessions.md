## Evidence

### Thai NP 2026

Thai NP, Hoang HT, Vu CTB, Nguyen ATN, Doan TM. (2026). Improvement of learner’s preclinical skill in occlusal rest seat preparation using digital and conventional training. BMC Oral Health. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08402-7

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In this randomized two-group study of 80 fourth-year dental students preparing 240 rest seats, a linear mixed-effects model showed a significant main effect of Session on overall scores. The abstract reports the printed means: "scores increased from 2.61 ± 0.46 to 2.77 ± 0.29 and 3.04 ± 0.37" in the conventional group and "2.69 ± 0.37 to 2.86 ± 0.31 and 3.18 ± 0.28" in the digital group.

> "The overall scores for occlusal rest seats improved significantly across the three sessions in both the CG and DG (p < 0.001). In the CG, scores increased from 2.61 ± 0.46 to 2.77 ± 0.29 and 3.04 ± 0.37, while in the DG, scores rose from 2.69 ± 0.37 to 2.86 ± 0.31 and 3.18 ± 0.28."
