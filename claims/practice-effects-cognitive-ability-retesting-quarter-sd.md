## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Meta-analysis of 107 samples shows scores improve about one-quarter of a standard deviation across first and second administrations (sample-weighted d = .24, corrected δ = .26). [→ Hausknecht 2005](#hausknecht-2005)
`q3 i1` Gains continue across further administrations: d = .18 from second to third administration and d = .51 from first to third (non-consecutive) tests. [→ Hausknecht 2005](#hausknecht-2005)

## Evidence

### Hausknecht 2005

Hausknecht, J. P., Halpert, J. A., Di Paolo, N. T., & Moriarty Gerrard, M. O. (2005). Retesting in Selection: A Meta-Analysis of Practice Effects for Tests of Cognitive Ability. Journal of Applied Psychology (in press). https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/apl

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Meta-analysis of 107 independent samples (N = 134,436) from 50 studies of cognitive ability retesting. The article reports "a sample-weighted mean d of .24" and corrected δ = .26, plus d = .18 (second to third) and d = .51 (first to third).

> "Results based on 107 samples revealed a sample-weighted mean d of .24 (see Table 1). When these values were further corrected for sampling error, the overall mean (δ) was .26."
