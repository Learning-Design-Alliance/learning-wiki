# Implementing VAD raised facilitator cognitive demand, debriefing time, and attention competition with the video, with segment-search time greater in lower-tech VAD

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Facilitators reported high cognitive demand from handling VAD technology while leading the simulation, and participants noted facilitators needed more debriefing time, especially in lower-tech VAD to locate video segments. [→ Stefan A. Schreder 2025](#stefan-a-schreder-2025)
`q2 i?` Facilitators reported that once the video was turned on, participants only paid attention to the video, interrupting the flow of verbal explanations. [→ Stefan A. Schreder 2025 (2)](#stefan-a-schreder-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Stefan A. Schreder 2025

Stefan A. Schreder, Helen Jossberger, Hans Gruber. (2025). Mountain rescuers' experiences with video-assisted and verbal debriefings: a qualitative study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09716-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Facilitator interviews on current difficulties: high cognitive demand arose from combining technology management with simultaneously leading the simulation, plus anticipating failures (backup plans needed). Demand reportedly reduced after becoming used to the equipment.

> "They indicated factoring in possible technical failures as well, which in turn increased their perceived cognitive demand."

### Stefan A. Schreder 2025 (2)

Stefan A. Schreder, Helen Jossberger, Hans Gruber. (2025). Mountain rescuers' experiences with video-assisted and verbal debriefings: a qualitative study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09716-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Facilitator FA 4 on flow during debriefing: the video drew participants' attention away from verbal explanations, matching prior findings that VAD can distract from facilitator input (Savoldelli et al., 2006; Reed et al., 2013).

> "As soon as you turn on the video, the participants only pay attention to the video. So, you have to stop, then talk again and then switch on the video again"

### Stefan A. Schreder 2025 (3)

Stefan A. Schreder, Helen Jossberger, Hans Gruber. (2025). Mountain rescuers' experiences with video-assisted and verbal debriefings: a qualitative study. Instructional Science. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-025-09716-9

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Participant reports on current difficulties: lower-tech VAD lacked an option to mark important sections during the simulation, so facilitators needed more time to find segments, which "took away time from the debriefing"; participants reported occasional technical failures with high-tech VAD.

> "the participants found that the facilitators needed more time during lower-tech V AD, to find the video segments that they wanted to display"
