# An audio-augmented toy produced significantly longer cooperative play than the same toy without augmentation in triads of 6- to 11-year-olds

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Children playing with the augmented Knights' Castle spent significantly more time in cooperative play than those with the non-augmented version (planned comparison F(1, 14) = 4.47, p < 0.05, η2 = 0.24). [→ Yuill N 2014](#yuill-n-2014)

## Evidence

### Yuill N 2014

Yuill N, Hinske S, Williams SE and Leith G (2014) How getting noticed helps getting on: successful attention capture doubles children's cooperative play. Front. Psychol. 5:418. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00418

`q3 · i3`

Study 1: video data of 48 6- to 11-year-olds randomly allocated to triads with the augmented (AKC) or non-augmented (KC) playset; MANOVA of play durations showed "a significant interaction between play category and toy type, [ F(2, 28) = 10.50, p < 0.001, 05, η2 = 0.43]" and the planned comparison yielded η2 = 0.24. The discussion reports cooperative play was "more than double the length of time".

> "a planned comparison supported the hypothesis that cooperative play would be more common for AKC than KC, [ F(1, 14) = 4.47, p < 0.05, η2 = 0.24], observed power of 0.51"
