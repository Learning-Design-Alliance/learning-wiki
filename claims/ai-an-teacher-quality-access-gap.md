# AI/AN students have comparatively low access to well-prepared teachers: 27 percent were taught mathematics by teachers with a mathematics degree, significantly lower than any comparison group

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Nelson-Barber 2015

Nelson-Barber, S., & Trumbull, E. (2015). The Common Core Initiative, Education Outcomes, and American Indian/Alaska Native Students: Observations and Recommendations [a report from the Center on Standards & Assessment Implementation (CSAI) at WestEd]. San Francisco: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED570362

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The report cites a recent study (Akiba, Chiu, & Zhuang, 2008) finding "the percentage of AI/AN students taught mathematics by teachers with a degree in mathematics (27 percent) was significantly lower than that of any comparison group." No effect size is printed.

> "the percentage of AI/AN students taught mathematics by teachers with a degree in mathematics (27 percent) was significantly lower than that of any comparison group (White, African-American, Latino) (Akiba, Chiu, & Zhuang, 2008)."

### Nelson-Barber 2015 (2)

Nelson-Barber, S., & Trumbull, E. (2015). The Common Core Initiative, Education Outcomes, and American Indian/Alaska Native Students: Observations and Recommendations [a report from the Center on Standards & Assessment Implementation (CSAI) at WestEd]. San Francisco: WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED570362

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

An eighth-grade NAEP mathematics data study with teacher and student surveys (Akiba, Chiu, & Zhuang, 2008) found AI/AN students "had among the lowest exposure" to standards-knowledgeable, standards-trained, and standards-practicing teachers.

> "AI/AN students had among the lowest exposure to teachers who reported that they were knowledgeable about standards, who participated in standards-based professional development, and who practiced standards-based instruction"
