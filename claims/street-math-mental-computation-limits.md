## Subclaims
`q1 i?` The authors argue that the informal mental strategy of multiplying through repeated chunked additions has an inherent limitation: it becomes grossly inefficient when large numbers are involved. [→ Carraher 1985](#carraher-1985)

## Evidence

### Carraher 1985

Carraher, T. N., Carraher, D. W., & Schliemann, A. D. (1985). Mathematics in the streets and in schools. British Journal of Developmental Psychology, 3, 21-29. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-835x.1985.tb00951.x

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r1`

The authors' own interpretive argument in the discussion section, offered as a reason not to abandon school mathematics: written school computation can overcome limitations of mental computation. This is an authors' interpretation, not a tested result, and no effect size is printed.

> "One is the inherent limitation placed on multiplying through successive chunking, that is, on multiplying through repeated chunked additions-a procedure which becomes grossly inefficient when large numbers are involved."
