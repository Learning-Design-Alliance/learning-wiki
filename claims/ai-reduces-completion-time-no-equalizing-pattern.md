# AI assistance reduces task completion time in both education groups, the only outcome without an equalizing pattern

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Completion time falls by 0.961 minutes for low-education and 1.514 minutes for high-education participants; the difference in treatment effects is not statistically significant. [→ Guillermo Cruces 2026](#guillermo-cruces-2026)

## Evidence

### Guillermo Cruces 2026

Guillermo Cruces, Diego Fernández Meĳide, Sebastian Galiani, Ramiro H. Gálvez, María Lombardi. (2026). Does generative AI narrow education-based productivity gaps? Evidence from a randomized experiment. https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/16607

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Results-section analysis of preregistered task completion time (minutes, top-coded at 20) in the randomized experiment. The article reports time "falls by 0.961 minutes" for low-education and "1.514 minutes" for high-education participants.

> "Among low-education subjects, time falls by 0.961 minutes relative to a control-group average of 10.697 minutes (significant at the 10% level). Among high-education participants, the reduction is larger at 1.514 minutes relative to a control-group average of 10.423 minutes (significant at the 1% level)."
