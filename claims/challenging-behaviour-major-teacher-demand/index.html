Evidence¶

Bly 2026¶

Bly, S., Houen, S., Staton, S., & Thorpe, K. (2026). Community, Children, Child: New Directions for Equitable Delivery Of High-Quality Early Childhood Education and Care (ECEC). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10215-7

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this school-sector twin study, attributing the finding to Houts et al. (2010); the review notes the authors' point that one child presenting behavioural challenges can limit classmates' access to the educator.

"Houts and colleagues ( 2010), using a powerful behavioural-genetic twin design of children aged 5 to 12 years found that children’s challenging behaviour (e.g., irritability, negativity, impulsivity, distractibility, and hyperactivity), but not ability (IQ), was the major source of demand on the teacher."

Bly 2026 (2)¶

Bly, S., Houen, S., Staton, S., & Thorpe, K. (2026). Community, Children, Child: New Directions for Equitable Delivery Of High-Quality Early Childhood Education and Care (ECEC). Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10215-7

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports this experimental class-grouping study from the school sector, attributing it to Goulas et al. (2024); it is presented as raising hypotheses of similar effects in ECEC.

"Goulas et al., ( 2024) in an experimental study that manipulated class grouping in primary school settings found that those placed in class -rooms including children with behavioural difficulties had poorer academic outcomes."
