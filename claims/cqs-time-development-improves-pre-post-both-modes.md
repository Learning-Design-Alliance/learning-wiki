# Student performance rose from pre-test to post-test in both virtual and in-person classes after a research-validated clicker question sequence on time-development of two-state quantum systems, in one uncontrolled pre-post study, with per-question effect sizes ranging from 0.34 to over 1

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students in both virtual (2020) and in-person (2021) classes showed a rise in performance from pre-test to post-test after engaging with the CQS, in a single-group pre-post study with no comparison group, with effect sizes for questions ranging from 0.34 to over 1. ## Evidence

### Peter Hu 2022

Peter Hu, Yangqiuting Li, and Chandralekha Singh. (2022). Challenges in addressing student difficulties with time-development of two-state quantum systems using a multiple-choice question sequence in virtual and in-person classes. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1088/1361-6404/ac49f4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6404/ac49f4)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental pre/post comparison in a mandatory junior-/senior-level quantum course (N = 29 virtual 2020; N = 25 in-person 2021), with students completing the pre-test after traditional lecture and the post-test after the CQS. The article reports that "effect sizes for questions ranged from 0.34 to over 1" across both years; no single pooled effect size is printed for the overall contrast.

> "We are encouraged that students in both virtual and in-person classes appear to have benefited from the CQS: across both years, from the pre-test to the post-test, effect sizes for questions ranged from 0.34 to over 1."
