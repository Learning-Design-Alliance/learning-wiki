## Evidence

### Tamblyn R 2007

Tamblyn R, Abrahamowicz M, Dauphinee D, et al. (2007). Physician Scores on a National Clinical Skills Examination as Predictors of Complaints to Medical Regulatory Authorities. JAMA. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.9.993

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Likelihood ratio test comparison of nested Poisson models in the same cohort. Improvement in fit was assessed by likelihood ratio test; the added communication score remained significant for retained complaints (P<.001) but not for quality-of-care complaints. No standardized effect size was printed, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "The CSE communication score, when added to a model that included traditional written examination CDM score, significantly improved the prediction of overall retained complaints and communication complaints, but not complaints about quality of care"

### Tamblyn R 2007 (2)

Tamblyn R, Abrahamowicz M, Dauphinee D, et al. (2007). Physician Scores on a National Clinical Skills Examination as Predictors of Complaints to Medical Regulatory Authorities. JAMA. https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.298.9.993

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Population attributable fraction estimated in the same cohort after adjustment for the CDM score: "an additional 9.2% (95% CI, 4.7%-13.1%) of retained complaints" were explained by the bottom communication quartile; the attributable fraction is not a standardized effect size, so impact is coded i? (no magnitude reported; not a null effect).

> "After adjustment for the traditional written examination CDM score, an additional 9.2% (95% CI, 4.7%-13.1%) of retained complaints and 11.2% (95% CI, 5.8%-16.9%) of communication complaints were explained by physicians in the bottom communication score quartile."
