## Evidence

### Stefan Küchemann 2023

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Prior-knowledge check in the randomized controlled trial using the half-length Force Concept Inventory: intervention participants averaged "an average FCI score of 0.62 ± 0.05" versus 0.69±0.05 in the control group, a difference reported as not significant.

> "The intervention group participants achieved an average FCI score of 0.62 ± 0.05, while control group participants achieved an average FCI score of 0.69±0.05 (overall average: 0.65±0.05), but the diﬀerence between the two groups was not signiﬁcant (p> 0.05)."

### Stefan Küchemann 2023 (2)

Stefan Küchemann, Steffen Steinert, Natalia Revenga, Matthias Schweinberger, Yavuz Dinc, Karina E. Avila, and Jochen Kuhn. (2023). Physics task development of prospective physics teachers using ChatGPT. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.020128)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Logistic regression between students' FCI scores and the six binary task ratings, plus a linear regression for the two Likert-scale categories, found "no signiﬁcant relationship between FCI scores and ratings in the ﬁrst six categories" and no linear relationship for the latter.

> "We found no signiﬁcant relationship between FCI scores and ratings in the ﬁrst six categories."
