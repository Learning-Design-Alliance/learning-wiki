# Older entrants show higher kindergarten growth rates but lower growth rates in 1st and 2nd grades

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r?` · `q3`

## Subclaims

`q3 i?` A year of additional age at entry is associated with faster kindergarten growth but slower growth during 1st and 2nd grades. [→ Angela Johnson 2020](#angela-johnson-2020)

`q2 i?` Older school entry may confer an early advantage, but that advantage fades in later grades. [→ Angela Johnson 2020](#angela-johnson-2020)

`q2 i?` The finding comes from a multi-state regression discontinuity analysis of academic growth through second grade. [→ Angela Johnson 2020](#angela-johnson-2020)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Regression discontinuity estimates from a piecewise multilevel growth model applied to three-state assessment data. The abstract reports "higher kindergarten growth rates but lower growth rates during 1st and 2nd grades"; no effect sizes are printed in the available text.

> "Being a year older leads to higher initial achievement and higher kindergarten growth rates but lower growth rates during 1st and 2nd grades."
