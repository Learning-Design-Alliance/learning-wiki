Evidence¶

Salthouse 2012¶

Salthouse, T. (2012). Consequences of Age-Related Cognitive Declines. Annual Review of Psychology, 63, 201-226. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-120710-100328

q3 · i? · review · r2

Review of cognitive epidemiology. The review reports that Batty et al. (2007) "described nine studies" of early-life cognition and later mortality, in all of which higher cognitive ability predicted decreased mortality risk, including a dose-response pattern in nearly one million Swedish men.

"All nine studies reported that higher levels of cognitive ability were associated with decreased risk of mortality"

Salthouse 2012 (2)¶

Salthouse, T. (2012). Consequences of Age-Related Cognitive Declines. Annual Review of Psychology, 63, 201-226. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-120710-100328

q2 · i? · review · r2

Review of hypothesized mechanisms for cognition-mortality relations. The review reports medication-adherence findings (Deary et al. 2009; Stilley et al.) and that controlling health behaviors "reduced the relation between cognitive ability and mortality", consistent with a healthy-lifestyle mediator.

"Sabia et al. (2010) found that controlling health variables such as smoking, alcoholic consumption, fruit and vegetable consumption, and hours of physical exercise reduced the relation between cognitive ability and mortality"
