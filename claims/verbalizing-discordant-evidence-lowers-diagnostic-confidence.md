# Confidence in a predicted diagnosis rose slightly from Step 1 to Step 2 and fell slightly from Step 2 to Step 3 among medical students reasoning on vignettes, in one group with no comparison group

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across 107 valid respondents, mean confidence rose from 5.01 at Step 1 to 5.20 at Step 2, then fell to 4.98 at Step 3; no effect size is printed for the descriptive means. [→ Kojima J 2025](#kojima-j-2025)
`q2 i1` Bonferroni-adjusted multiple comparisons showed significant differences between Steps 1–2 (Cohen’s d = 0.25) and Steps 2–3 (Cohen’s d = 0.29). [→ Kojima J 2025 (2)](#kojima-j-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Kojima J 2025

Kojima J, Uehara T, Ohira Y, Tsukamoto T, Shikino K, Yokokawa D, Li Y, Yanagita Y, Sato R, Ikusaka M. (2025). Potential for reducing confirmation bias using the OMP model “6-microskills” with verbalizing discordance: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06869-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Cross-sectional vignette study of medical students (107 valid responses of 108) rated confidence on a 10-point scale at three steps. Descriptive means show the rise at Step 2 and fall at Step 3: "5.20 (4.78–5.62) for Step 2, and 4.98 (4.45–5.42) for Step 3". No effect size is printed for these means.

> "The mean confidence level regarding the predicted hypothesis at each step was 5.01 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 4.6–5.43) for Step 1, 5.20 (4.78–5.62) for Step 2, and 4.98 (4.45–5.42) for Step 3."

### Kojima J 2025 (2)

Kojima J, Uehara T, Ohira Y, Tsukamoto T, Shikino K, Yokokawa D, Li Y, Yanagita Y, Sato R, Ikusaka M. (2025). Potential for reducing confirmation bias using the OMP model “6-microskills” with verbalizing discordance: a cross-sectional study. BMC Med Educ. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06869-6

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

Two-way ANOVA with repeated measures (two between-participant levels: correct/incorrect diagnosis; three within-participant steps) with Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise tests. Both step contrasts were significant with small effects: "P =.04, Cohen’s d = 0.25" and "P =.01, Cohen’s d = 0.29".

> "Multiple comparisons using the Bonferroni method showed significant differences between Steps 1 and 2 (P =.04, Cohen’s d = 0.25, df = 106) and between Steps 2 and 3 (P =.01, Cohen’s d = 0.29, df = 106)"
