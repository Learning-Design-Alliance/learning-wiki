# On six FCI force-identification questions, student answers cluster on a pair of dominant answers containing the correct choice and a superset wrong choice

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` For each of the six questions, the pair of dominant answers is the same pre and post, contains the correct choice, and the wrong member is a superset of forces including extra 'fictitious' forces. [→ Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014](#allan-l-alinea-and-wade-naylor-2014)
`q2 i?` The dominant answer pair captures 64%-89% of pretest and 81%-98% of posttest answers across the six questions. [→ Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014 (2)](#allan-l-alinea-and-wade-naylor-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014

Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor. (2014). Polarisation of physics on global courses. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Item analysis (Fig. 2) of student answer distributions on six selected FCI questions (5, 11, 13, 18, 29, 30) for the same 47-student cohort. The article finds two dominant answers per question, the same pair pre and post, always containing the correct choice, with the wrong choice "a superset of CC".

> "Furthermore, both CC and X contains the right set of forces acting on a given body but X contains one or more extra forces that do/does not legitimately act on a given body. In other words, X is a superset of CC."

### Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor 2014 (2)

Allan L. Alinea and Wade Naylor. (2014). Polarisation of physics on global courses. [doi:10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9120/50/2/210)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r?`

Figure 3 analysis of the same six-question item data shows the wide gap between dominant-answer shares and all other choices; the article reports the printed ranges of 64%-89% (pre) and 81%-98% (post).

> "The percent of students who answered the dominant answers ranges from 64% to 89% for the pretest and from 81% to 98% for the posttest."
