## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Twelve participants (46%) endorsed code writing only when scaffolding such as filling in incomplete code is in place. [→ Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe 2024](#justin-gambrell-and-eric-brewe-2024)

## Evidence

### Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe 2024

Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe. (2024). Towards a Generalized Assessment of Computational Thinking for Introductory Physics Students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview coding under the Barrier Reduction Strategies theme; participants described filling in incomplete or minimally working code as "scaffolding to being able to write the whole function or sentence or whatever yourself."

> "Twelve participants (46%) mentioned including codec writing as a skill for students but only prefaced by the fact that scaffolding be in place."

### Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe 2024 (2)

Justin Gambrell and Eric Brewe. (2024). Towards a Generalized Assessment of Computational Thinking for Introductory Physics Students. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.20.010128)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview coding under the Constraints theme; one instructor reported never having students write from scratch because it requires a deeper mastery they lack time to build, and another said they had given up that goal.

> "Nine participants (35%) advocated that students should not be coding c from scratch in an computationally integrated introductory physics course."
