Evidence¶

Hertzog C 2011¶

Hertzog C, Dunlosky J. (2011). Metacognition in Later Adulthood: Spared Monitoring Can Benefit Older Adults' Self-regulation. Current Directions in Psychological Science. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3903298/

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports a study of older adults' JOLs for a word recognition test with recollect (R), familiar (F), and new (N) response options, finding "reliable age differences in JOL resolution when R responses were contrasted with pooled F and N responses."

"There were no age differences in JOL resolution when R and F responses were contrasted to N responses, but there were reliable age differences in JOL resolution when R responses were contrasted with pooled F and N responses."

Discussion¶

Related Claims¶

  • Aging does not materially reduce the relative accuracy (resolution) of judgments of learning during encoding, with cross-sectional evidence of no age declines and even age-related increases in aggregate JOL resolution across ages 18 to 81 — related
  • The literature is mixed on age differences in the accuracy of retrospective confidence judgments during recognition memory tests, with older adults sometimes producing more high-confidence false alarms and poorer resolution with deceptive lures — related
