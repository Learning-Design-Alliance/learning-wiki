## Subclaims
`q3 i?` IEP percentages were significantly higher for 35-36 weeks children in kindergarten and first grade and for every grade in 32-34 weeks children, with kindergarten adjusted odds of 2.43 (32-34 weeks) and 1.44 (35-36 weeks) versus term children. [→ Lock Nicole E. 2024](#lock-nicole-e-2024)

## Evidence

### Lock Nicole E. 2024

Lock Nicole E., DeBoer Mark D., Scharf Rebecca J., Miller Sarah E. (2024). Academic performance in moderately and late preterm children in the United States: are they catching up? Journal of Perinatology 44(6). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41372-024-01938-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Multivariable logistic regression of IEP records in the weighted ECLS-K:2011 cohort; kindergarten adjusted odds were "2.43 (CI 1.48, 4.0)" for 32-34 weeks and "1.44 (CI 0.96, 2.16)" for 35-36 weeks children versus term children.

> "with the highest odds at kindergarten for both 32–34 6/7 weeks’ and 35–36 6/7 weeks’ children at 2.43 (CI 1.48, 4.0) and 1.44 (CI 0.96, 2.16), respectively"

### Lock Nicole E. 2024 (2)

Lock Nicole E., DeBoer Mark D., Scharf Rebecca J., Miller Sarah E. (2024). Academic performance in moderately and late preterm children in the United States: are they catching up? Journal of Perinatology 44(6). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41372-024-01938-y

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Chi-square comparison of school-recorded IEPs found "a significantly larger percentage" for 35-36 weeks children in kindergarten and first grade and for 32-34 weeks children in every grade; at first grade, 16%, 12.4%, and 9.5% respectively had IEPs.

> "Individualized education plans (IEP) were on file for a significantly larger percentage of 35–36 6/7 weeks’ children compared to term children in kindergarten and first grade, and for every grade from kindergarten to fifth grade in 32–34 6/7 weeks’ children"
