# A 60-minute session with Prompt Coach significantly improved professional developers' prompt quality, a 13.73% relative gain overall

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` After a 60-minute PC learning session, mean prompt-quality scores rose from 63.04 to 71.69 (+13.73%, paired Wilcoxon p < .001). [→ Rohit Mehra 2026](#rohit-mehra-2026)
`q2 i?` 13 of 15 participants improved and no participant showed a meaningful decline. [→ Rohit Mehra 2026 (2)](#rohit-mehra-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Rohit Mehra 2026

Rohit Mehra, Kapil Singi, Vikrant Kaulgud, Vibhu Saujanya Sharma, Swapnajeet Gon Choudhury, Swati Sharma, Adam P. Burden, Majd Sakr. (2026). Prompt Coach: An Empirical Evaluation of an Agentic Tutor for Learning Prompt Engineering in Software Development. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06074 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.06074](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2607.06074)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Single-arm, within-subjects pre/post study of 15 professional developers (mean 9.6 years experience); prompts scored via LLM-as-judge across eight dimensions. Table 2 reports the mean rise and paired Wilcoxon p < .001, described as "a 13.73% relative improvement over the baseline".

> "After a 60-minute learning session with PC, post-learning prompts achieved a mean score of 71.69, representing a 13.73% relative improvement over the baseline"

### Rohit Mehra 2026 (2)

Rohit Mehra, Kapil Singi, Vikrant Kaulgud, Vibhu Saujanya Sharma, Swapnajeet Gon Choudhury, Swati Sharma, Adam P. Burden, Majd Sakr. (2026). Prompt Coach: An Empirical Evaluation of an Agentic Tutor for Learning Prompt Engineering in Software Development. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06074

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Participant-level results from the same pre/post study: "13 out of 15 participants showed improvements"; the small negative change (-0.28%) is attributed by the authors to variability in LLM-as-a-judge scoring.

> "Notably, 13 out of 15 participants showed improvements, while 2 participants exhibited no measurable gain on average. Importantly, no participant showed a meaningful decline in performance."
