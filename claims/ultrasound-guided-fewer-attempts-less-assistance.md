## Evidence

### Liu Enci 2026

Liu Enci, Qin Junjie, Li Xiaoyu, Chi Zhanghuan, An Xiujun, Chen Junping, Jiang Jingyan, Lu Bo. (2026). Ultrasound reliance and landmark-based proficiency in vascular access training: a multicenter cross-sectional survey of anesthesia trainees and teaching faculty in Eastern China. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09702-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Self-reported cannulation attempt data from 454 trainees in the multicenter cross-sectional survey. Figure 4 reports a higher proportion of first-attempt cannulation with ultrasound guidance for IJV (71.37% vs. 36.34%) and RA (64.76% vs. 30.40%), both P < 0.001.

> "The self-reported number of attempts required for successful cannulation was also fewer with ultrasound‑guided than with the landmark-based technique for both vascular sites (all P < 0.001; Fig. 4)."

### Liu Enci 2026 (2)

Liu Enci, Qin Junjie, Li Xiaoyu, Chi Zhanghuan, An Xiujun, Chen Junping, Jiang Jingyan, Lu Bo. (2026). Ultrasound reliance and landmark-based proficiency in vascular access training: a multicenter cross-sectional survey of anesthesia trainees and teaching faculty in Eastern China. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09702-w

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Self-reported supervisor assistance frequency from 454 trainees, dichotomized as frequent (often/sometimes) versus infrequent (seldom/never) and compared with McNemar's test. The article reports "40.22% vs. 63.96%" for IJV and "36.26% vs. 63.30%" for RA.

> "The proportion reporting frequent supervisor assistance (often or sometimes) was lower with ultrasound-guided than with landmark-based cannulation for both IJV (40.22% vs. 63.96%) and RA (36.26% vs. 63.30%) access (all P < 0.001; Fig. 3)."
