# Students judge preparatory math courses as at least somewhat helpful for physics, but helpfulness varies: introductory calculus and vector calculus were widely valued while lower-division linear algebra was insufficient preparation for quantum mechanics

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Students universally appreciated introductory calculus as preparing them well for introductory physics, and vector calculus was seen as especially helpful for electricity and magnetism. [→ Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024](#jeffrey-m-rabin-2024)

## Evidence

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Interview theme on preparatory math courses: students valued learning vector calculus in math and then seeing it applied to physical systems in electromagnetism about a week and a half later, which made the physics course easier to digest.

> "Based on our interviews, students taking introductory physics seem to universally appreciate introductory calculus and feel it prepares them well for introductory physics."

### Jeffrey M. Rabin 2024 (2)

Jeffrey M. Rabin, Andrew Meyertholen, Brian Shotwell. (2024). Students' Experience of Cultural Differences Between Mathematics and Physics. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2409.02427)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data on linear algebra: the lower-division course never focused on abstract or infinite-dimensional vector spaces, complex numbers, or Hermitian/unitary operators, leaving students overwhelmed when learning the math alongside quantum mechanics applications and new notation.

> "Almost uniformly, students mentioned that th e one quarter of lower- division linear algebra that they had in a math class was insuﬃcient prep aration for upper- division quantum mechanics."
