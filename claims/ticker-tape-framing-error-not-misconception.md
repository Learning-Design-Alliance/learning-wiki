# In a ticker-tape Tutorial, students' confidently wrong answer about segment timing is an epistemological framing error, corrected when later questions shift the context

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · 1 qualitative `r1`, 1 theoretical `r3` · `q1`–`q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` Student groups initially answered that shorter tape segments took less time to generate, then corrected themselves after a later question shifted the context. [→ Redish 2012](#redish-2012)

`q2 i?` Groups of students in a University of Washington-style tutorial initially answered from immediate association that shorter tape segments took less time, and after later worksheet questions shifted frame and corrected their answers. [→ Edward F. Redish 2013](#edward-f-redish-2013)

## Evidence

### Redish 2012

Redish, E. F. (2012). The role of context and culture in teaching physics: The implication of disciplinary differences. Keynote address, World Conference on Physics Education 2012, Istanbul. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1207.4840](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1207.4840)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

Videotaped observations of groups in an algebra-based introductory physics recitation using University of Washington-style Tutorials with ticker-tape segments. The author interprets the pattern as "epistemological reframing" triggered by the later question about arranging segments by speed.

> "We saw this again and again. At the beginning the students gave a quick answer - longer tapes take longer times, shorter ones take shorter. Just a few minutes later, the light dawns and they all get it right."

### Edward F. Redish 2013

Edward F. Redish. (2013). Oersted Lecture 2013: How should we think about how our students think?. [doi:10.1119/1.4874260](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4874260)

`q2 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Videotaped observations of a first-semester recitation tutorial on instantaneous velocity, in which groups of 3-5 students work with ticker-tape segments. The article reports the same error "again and again" across groups, followed by correct analysis after later questions; no statistics are printed.

> "At the beginning the students gave a quick answer – longer tapes take more time, shorter ones take less. Just a few minutes later, the light dawns and they all get it right (Fig. 8)."
