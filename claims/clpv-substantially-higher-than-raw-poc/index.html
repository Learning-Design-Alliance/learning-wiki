Evidence¶

McManus IC 2013¶

McManus IC, Dewberry Chris, Nicholson Sandra, Dowell Jonathan S, Woolf Katherine, Potts Henry WW. (2013). Construct-level predictive validity of educational attainment and intellectual aptitude tests in medical student selection: meta-regression of six UK longitudinal studies. BMC Medicine. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3827328/

q3 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Meta-regression of 57 POC/CLPV pairs from six UK cohort studies (entry 1972–2009). The article reports "Mean CLPVs are substantially higher (.450) than mean POCs (.171)", with CLPVs correcting for range restriction, unreliability and right-censorship via an MCMC-adapted Hunter-Schmidt-Le method.

"Meta-regression analyzed 57 separate predictor-outcome correlations (POCs) and construct-level predictive validities (CLPVs). Mean CLPVs are substantially higher (.450) than mean POCs (.171)."

Related Claims¶

  • Educational attainment CLPVs decline somewhat during training but continue to predict postgraduate performance, with A-levels' YearOfTraining effect significant (b = -.040, P = .035) — related
  • Raw predictor-outcome correlations in medical selection are small (mean .171), requiring very large samples (~290) for significance, explaining why selection measures appear weakly valid in single-cohort studies — related
  • A-levels also show the highest construct-level predictive validity for postgraduate performance (.556), significantly exceeding GCSEs/O-levels and aptitude tests, which do not differ from each other — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
