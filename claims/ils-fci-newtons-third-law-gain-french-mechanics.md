## Subclaims
`q2 i1` Students in interactive mechanics sections scored statistically significantly better than traditional-section students on the four FCI Newton's Third Law questions, p < 0.01, Cohen's D = 0.583. [→ Rudolph 2013](#rudolph-2013)

## Evidence

### Rudolph 2013

Rudolph, A. L., Lamine, B., Joyce, M., Vignolles, H., & Consiglio, D. (2013). Introduction of interactive learning into French university physics classrooms. Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Review. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622)

`q2 · i1` · `causal · r1`

T-test comparison of mean normalized gain on four FCI Newton's Third Law questions between interactive (N=39) and non-interactive (N=72) second-semester mechanics sections at UPMC. The article reports "Cohen’s-D effect size for this difference is 0.583" and notes the four-question subset is not itself validated.

> "Here we find that the students in the interactive sections performed statistically significantly better than those in the traditional, non-interactive sections (p < 0.01).  The Cohen’s-D effect size for this difference is 0.583, indicating a medium-to-large effect size."

### Rudolph 2013 (2)

Rudolph, A. L., Lamine, B., Joyce, M., Vignolles, H., & Consiglio, D. (2013). Introduction of interactive learning into French university physics classrooms. Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Review. Originally published 2012. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1311.3622)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

T-test on entire-FCI normalized gain for interactive (N=60, <g>=0.119) versus non-interactive (N=122, <g>=0.049) mechanics sections. The article notes the FCI content mostly matches the first-semester course, and a 38% response rate raises non-response-bias concerns.

> "although the average normalized gain was more than twice as high in the interactive classes, the difference in the mean normalized gain was not statistically significant (p > 0.05)."
