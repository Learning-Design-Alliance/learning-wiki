# Perfectionistic concerns are consistently related to maladaptive outcomes in academically gifted students while perfectionistic strivings show mixed outcomes

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across variable-based studies, PC are consistently related to maladaptive outcomes (neuroticism, depression, alienation from parents) whereas PS relate to both adaptive and maladaptive outcomes in gifted students. [→ Grugan 2021](#grugan-2021)

## Evidence

### Grugan 2021

Grugan, M. C., Hill, A. P., Madigan, D. J., Donachie, T. C., Olsson, L. F., & Etherson, M. E. (2021). Perfectionism and Academically Gifted Students: A Systematic Review. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09597-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review's synthesis of 24 variable-based studies reports that "PC were consistently related to maladaptive outcomes in students 6 identified as academically gifted", including "positive relationships 7 with neuroticism, depression, and alienation from parents". No pooled effect sizes are reported for this synthesis.

> "PC were consistently related to maladaptive outcomes in students 6 identified as academically gifted (Stoeber & Otto, 2006). This included positive relationships 7 with neuroticism, depression, and alienation from parents, as well as negative relationships 8 with achievement motivation, self-esteem, and creative strivings."
