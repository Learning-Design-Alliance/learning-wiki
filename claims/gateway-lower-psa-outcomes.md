`q2 i?` SEMED students have significantly higher mean PSA z-scores than gateway students (F = 149.48, P < 0.001). No effect size is printed for this comparison. [→ Curtis Sally 2020](#curtis-sally-2020)
`q2 i?` After controlling for entry attainment and aptitude, course type remains significant for PSA but accounts for less than 1% of variance (down from 5.3%). No effect size is printed for this regression finding. [→ Curtis Sally 2020 (2)](#curtis-sally-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Curtis Sally 2020

Curtis Sally, Smith Daniel. (2020). A comparison of undergraduate outcomes for students from gateway courses and standard entry medicine courses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1918-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r3`

PSA z-score comparison by course type (SEMED mean −0.117 vs gateway mean −0.746, F = 149.48, P < 0.001) in Table 4 of the UKMED cohort analysis. The article reports significance but no effect size.

> "As seen in Table 4, SEMED students have significantly higher mean PSA z-scores."

### Curtis Sally 2020 (2)

Curtis Sally, Smith Daniel. (2020). A comparison of undergraduate outcomes for students from gateway courses and standard entry medicine courses. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1918-y

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Multiple regression predicting PSA z-scores with and without entry controls, N = 2580. In Model 1 course type accounted for 5.3% (sr2) of variance; in Model 2 "it accounts for less than 1%" but SEMED retained a significant advantage. No effect size is printed.

> "PSA Model 1 (R2 adj = 0.102. N = 2580, P < 0.001) shows that the association between course type and PSA result is greater without including the measures of prior attainment and aptitude on entry, but their inclusion in PSA Model 2 (R2 adj = 0.149. N = 2580, P < 0.001) does not account for all the variance associated with course type in the first model."
