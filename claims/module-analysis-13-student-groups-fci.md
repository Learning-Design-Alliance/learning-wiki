### Bartley 2019

Bartley, J. E., Riedel, M. C., Salo, T., Boeving, E. R., Bottenhorn, K. L., Bravo, E. I., Odean, R., Nazareth, A., Laird, R. W., Sutherland, M. T., Pruden, S. M., Brewe, E. & Laird, A. R. (2019). Brain activity links performance in science reasoning with conceptual approach. npj Science of Learning 4:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0059-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Module analysis using nonparametric sparsification and InfoMapR community detection on the Students × Responses matrix from 107 students. After excluding one group for greater head motion, three normative groups (n = 24, 17, 10) were carried into group-level neuroimaging analyses.

> "Module analysis detected 13 student groups across 107 students who answered similarly to each other during FCI problem solving with a modularity of Q = 0.53 (Fig. 3a). Four groups had ten or more members (i.e., normative groups)."

### Bartley 2019 (2)

Bartley, J. E., Riedel, M. C., Salo, T., Boeving, E. R., Bottenhorn, K. L., Bravo, E. I., Odean, R., Nazareth, A., Laird, R. W., Sutherland, M. T., Pruden, S. M., Brewe, E. & Laird, A. R. (2019). Brain activity links performance in science reasoning with conceptual approach. npj Science of Learning 4:20. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-019-0059-8

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Group-level characterization of response profiles against nine previously measured physics conceptual modules. Group B (n = 17) achieved 73% accuracy mixing impetus force and gravitational-action confusions; Group C (n = 10) achieved 53% accuracy with incorrect answers spread across five modules including impetus force and mass-determines-falling.

> "Group A (n = 24) achieved an accuracy rate of 77% across all FCI questions, indicative of being highly Newtonian thinkers."
