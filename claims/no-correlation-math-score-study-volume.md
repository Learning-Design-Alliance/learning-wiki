### W. K. Black 2023

W. K. Black, Rebecca L. Matz, Mark Mills, and A. E. Evrard. (2023). Practice Makes Better: Quantifying Grade Beneﬁts of Study. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010139)

`q2 · i0` · `associational · r2`

Demographic-trend analysis (Fig. 5) in the PR physics study: mean grade correlates strongly with T (0.38±0.01) while mean study volume NQ,tot is effectively flat with T across the full student population including non-users.

> "In stark contrast, the volume of study by students (lower panel) is effectively ﬂat (insigniﬁcantly corre­lated: 0.02± 0.01); low T students study very nearly as much as their high T counterparts."

### W. K. Black 2023 (2)

W. K. Black, Rebecca L. Matz, Mark Mills, and A. E. Evrard. (2023). Practice Makes Better: Quantifying Grade Beneﬁts of Study. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010139](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.19.010139)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

KLLR fits of study volume versus GPAO within bins of T (Fig. 7) for PHYSICS 140 and 240 combined show rising study with GPAO in each T bin, with the striation revealing the anticorrelation between study volume and T at fixed GPAO.

> "In contrast to the lack of correlation between T and study, at ﬁxed GPAO there is a signiﬁcant trend of decreasing PR study volume for students with higher math scores, T."
