# Encouragement effects on knowledge are moderated by education and civic knowledge, with gains concentrated among the higher-educated and civically knowledgeable (partial support for the knowledge gap hypothesis)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` ITT encouragement effects on knowledge interact with education (monetary encouragement, child support) and with civic knowledge (verbal and monetary encouragement, child support; monetary encouragement, energy transition), consistent with the knowledge gap hypothesis. ## Evidence

### Ulloa 2026

Ulloa, Roberto; Leonard Tiedemann; Selb, Peter; Kacperski, Celina. (2026). The Knowledge Gap in a High-Choice Media Environment: Experimental Evidence from Online Search. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2605.21019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.21019)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Interaction models in Table 1 test moderation of ITT effects. The article reports "statistically significant interactions with monetary encouragement for child support" for education, and civic-knowledge interactions for child support (both treatments) and energy transition (monetary).

> "For educational attainment, we find statistically significant interactions with monetary encouragement for child support. For civic knowledge, we find significant interactions with verbal encouragement and with monetary encouragement for child support, as well as with monetary encouragement for energy transition."
