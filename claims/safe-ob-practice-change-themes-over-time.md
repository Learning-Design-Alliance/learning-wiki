## Evidence

### White Michelle C. 2019

White Michelle C., Rakotoarisoa Tsiferana, Cox Nicola H., Close Kristin L., Kotze Joan, Watrous Abigail. (2019). A Mixed-Method Design Evaluation of the SAFE Obstetric Anaesthesia Course at 4 and 12–18 Months After Training in the Republic of Congo and Madagascar. Anesthesia and Analgesia. https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0000000000004329

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Semistructured interviews at 12–18 months with 28 purposively sampled participants, analyzed by inductive thematic analysis; responses "were similar for participants from the Republic of Congo and Madagascar."

> "On thematic analysis of responses at 12–18 months, 4 areas of practice change were identified: management of obstetric hemorrhage, use of the “airway, breathing, circulation (ABC)” structured approach, management of eclampsia, and the use of spinal anesthesia."

### White Michelle C. 2019 (2)

White Michelle C., Rakotoarisoa Tsiferana, Cox Nicola H., Close Kristin L., Kotze Joan, Watrous Abigail. (2019). A Mixed-Method Design Evaluation of the SAFE Obstetric Anaesthesia Course at 4 and 12–18 Months After Training in the Republic of Congo and Madagascar. Anesthesia and Analgesia. https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0000000000004329

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Interview cohort at 4 months (46 participants); the authors attribute the fade-out of airway management and neonatal resuscitation themes to lack of opportunity to practice, since most obstetric anesthesia is spinal and midwives perform most neonatal resuscitation.

> "Neonatal resuscitation was reported by 50% of interviewees at 4 months and airway management by 25% as seen in the following comments"
