# Executive function at 48 months positively predicts mathematics ability at prekindergarten, with effects persisting after cognitive covariate controls

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r3` · `q3` · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` Executive function at 48 months is positively associated with math ability at prekindergarten (ES = .34 in demographic model; ES = .14 after cognitive covariates). [→ Blair 2015](#blair-2015)
`q3 i?` The executive function effect on math growth is reduced completely after adding cognitive covariates. [→ Blair 2015 (2)](#blair-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Blair 2015

Blair, C., Ursache, A., Greenberg, M., Vernon-Feagans, L., & The Family Life Project Investigators. (2015). Multiple Aspects of Self-Regulation Uniquely Predict Mathematics but Not Letter–Word Knowledge in the Early Elementary Grades. Developmental Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5264531/

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r3`

Multilevel growth model (Model A) in a longitudinal sample of 1,292 children from predominantly rural, low-income communities. Executive function composite at 48 months predicted prekindergarten math (WJ III Applied Problems): "B = 13.46, SE = 1.65, p < .001, ES = .34". Effect reduced to ES = .14 after cognitive covariates (Model B).

> "Executive function at 48 months was positively associated with math ability at the prekindergarten assessment (B = 13.46, SE = 1.65, p < .001, ES = .34), and with less rapid acquisition of math ability during the early elementary years (B = −1.07, SE = 0.47, p < .05, ES = .06)."

### Blair 2015 (2)

Blair, C., Ursache, A., Greenberg, M., Vernon-Feagans, L., & The Family Life Project Investigators. (2015). Multiple Aspects of Self-Regulation Uniquely Predict Mathematics but Not Letter–Word Knowledge in the Early Elementary Grades. Developmental Psychology. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5264531/

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Model B growth model adding receptive vocabulary and processing speed measured at prekindergarten. The executive function effect on math growth was "reduced completely"; no effect size printed for the null slope effect.

> "Effects on the slope were reduced completely for executive function and for directly assessed effortful control."
