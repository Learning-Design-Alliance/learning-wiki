## Evidence

### Jill S. Cannon 2006

Jill S. Cannon, Alison Jacknowitz, Gary Painter. (2006). Is Full Better than Half? Examining the Longitudinal Effects of Full-day Kindergarten Attendance. Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, Vol. 25, No. 2. https://doi.org/10.1002/pam.20174

`q3 · i0` · `associational · r3`

OLS regression on the ECLS-K baseline sample of 8,540 children (math n = 7,772; reading n = 7,407), controlling for child, household, and school characteristics and fall kindergarten scores. The article prints effect sizes of "0.145 standard deviations" for reading and "0.119 standard deviations" for math, both below d = 0.2.

> "For both reading and math achievement, attending a full-day kindergarten predicts significantly higher test scores. Specifically, the results imply that reading scores increase by 0.145 standard deviations, and math scores increase by 0.119 standard deviations."
