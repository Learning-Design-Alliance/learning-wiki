q3 · i? · review · r3

Systematic review of 30 quantitative studies (24 cross-sectional, 6 longitudinal) published February 2010 to March 2019, searched in PubMed, EMBASE and PsycINFO. The review reports "Significantly lower levels of empathy by increase in number of educational years were found in 14 out of 30 studies", with the remainder reporting higher, mixed or unchanged levels.

"Significantly lower levels of empathy by increase in number of educational years were found in 14 out of 30 studies. Of these, 12 were cross-sectional studies [21, 33, 34, 39–41, 45–48, 50, 52, 54] and two were longitudinal [52, 56]."

Related Claims¶

  • Female medical students were reported to have higher empathy scores than male students in 18 of the included studies, with no gender differences found in seven cross-sectional studies
  • Nine of 30 studies reported a propensity towards lower mean empathy scores in non-Western compared to Western countries, all applying the JSPE-S
  • Self-administered and observed empathy measures can yield opposite trajectories during clinical training, as one longitudinal study found declining self-reported but improving observed empathy
  • Only three of nine cross-sectional studies found an association between empathy scores and a preference for people-orientated specialties
  • Early clinical exposure is associated with higher empathy scores regardless of gender and academic year
