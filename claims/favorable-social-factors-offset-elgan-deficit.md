# An ELGAN with favorable maternal social factors is predicted to outscore a disadvantaged term infant in literacy by 104.1 points at grade 3, declining to 76.4 points at grade 8, and in mathematics by 33.4 points

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Predicted literacy scores of advantaged (HH) ELGANs exceeded those of disadvantaged (LL) term infants, with the advantage decreasing across grades; the mathematics advantage was 33.4 points across all grades. [→ ElHassan NO 2018](#elhassan-no-2018)

## Evidence

### ElHassan NO 2018

ElHassan NO, Bai S, Gibson N, Holland G, Robbins JM, Kaiser JR. (2018). The impact of prematurity and maternal socioeconomic status and education level on achievement-test scores up to 8th grade. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0198083

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Contrast analyses from the mixed-effect models comparing predicted scores of HH ELGANs versus LL term infants, reported in Results (Tables 3 and 4). The article reports the literacy advantage "decreased with advancing grades" while the mathematics difference of 33.4 points applied to all grades.

> "For example, an ELGAN with favorable social factors had a predicted literacy score 104.1 (P <.001), 98.2 (P <.001), and 76.4 (P <.01) points higher than an otherwise similar disadvantaged term infant at grades 3, 5, and 8, respectively. The difference in their predicted mathematic scores was 33.4 points for all grades (P <.05)."
