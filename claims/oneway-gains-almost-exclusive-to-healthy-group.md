# One-way tests showed positive pre-post changes almost exclusively in older adults without dementia, who improved in attention, visuospatial processing, visual memory and working memory but not verbal learning and language; the dementia group improved only on the easiest tasks with one negative change

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q3` · `i2` medium
> **Currency** · newest study 2019 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Healthy older adults showed significant pre-post gains on several measures, including d2 WZ-B (partial eta squared = 0.377), d2 WZ (0.205), d2 ZK (0.171), Block Design (0.15) and ROCF delayed recall (0.121), but not in verbal learning and language measures. [→ Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019](#zajac-lamparska-ludmia-2019)
`q3 i2` In the mild dementia group, positive changes were observed only in the percentage of errors in the d2 test (partial eta squared = 0.144) and the ROCF copying task (0.254), which belong to the easiest assessment tasks; a negative change was observed in BVRT correct reproductions (p = 0.031, partial eta squared = 0.166). [→ Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019 (2)](#zajac-lamparska-ludmia-2019-2)

## Evidence

### Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019

Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła, Wiłkość-Dębczyńska Monika, Wojciechowski Adam, Podhorecka Marta, Polak-Szabela Anna, Warchoł Łukasz, Kędziora-Kornatowska Kornelia, Araszkiewicz Aleksander, Izdebski Paweł. (2019). Effects of virtual reality-based cognitive training in older adults living without and with mild dementia: a pretest–posttest design pilot study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-019-4810-2

`q3 · i2` · `design · r2`

One-way repeated measures tests per cognitive measure (Table 3) in the healthy group. Printed effects include d2 WZ-B F = 42.946, p < .001, partial eta squared = 0.377; Block Design F = 12.509, p < .001, eta squared = 0.15; ROCF delayed recall F = 9.795, p = 0.003.

> "Healthy older adults also improved in visuospatial processing, visual aspects of memory and working memory, but not in verbal learning and language."

### Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła 2019 (2)

Zając-Lamparska Ludmiła, Wiłkość-Dębczyńska Monika, Wojciechowski Adam, Podhorecka Marta, Polak-Szabela Anna, Warchoł Łukasz, Kędziora-Kornatowska Kornelia, Araszkiewicz Aleksander, Izdebski Paweł. (2019). Effects of virtual reality-based cognitive training in older adults living without and with mild dementia: a pretest–posttest design pilot study. BMC Research Notes. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-019-4810-2

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r1`

One-way tests in the mild dementia group (Table 3): d2 %B F = 4.364, p = 0.047, partial eta squared = 0.144; ROCF copy F = 8.867, p = 0.006, eta squared = 0.254; BVRT correct reproductions F = 5.17, p = 0.031, eta squared = 0.166 (worsening).

> "In contrast, in the group of older adults with mild dementia positive changes were observed only in the percentage of errors in the d2 test and in the copying task of ROCF, which belong to the easiest tasks used in the assessment. In this group also a negative cognitive change was observed (in BVRT)."
