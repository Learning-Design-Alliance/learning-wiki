## Evidence

### Plata Christopher 2021

Plata Christopher, Nellessen Martin, Roth Rebecca, Ecker Hannes, Böttiger Bernd W., Löser Johannes, Wetsch Wolfgang A. (2021). Impact of video quality when evaluating video-assisted cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized, controlled simulation trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-021-00486-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Interprofessional comparison in the simulation trial across all quality stages; the study "was not powered to find differences in the evaluation of different professions". No effect size is printed.

> "Summarizing all quality stages, correct classification of the presented errors was achieved in 73.9% for paramedics and in 76.5% for emergency physicians, showing no significant interprofessional difference (p = 0.468)."

### Plata Christopher 2021 (2)

Plata Christopher, Nellessen Martin, Roth Rebecca, Ecker Hannes, Böttiger Bernd W., Löser Johannes, Wetsch Wolfgang A. (2021). Impact of video quality when evaluating video-assisted cardiopulmonary resuscitation: a randomized, controlled simulation trial. BMC Emergency Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12873-021-00486-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Separate analyses by profession in the simulation trial. For paramedics, increased chest compressions at low quality were recognized in 12.5% versus 60% at medium and high (p = 0.007); incomplete release detection also depended on quality for paramedics (p = 0.045) but not physicians (p = 0.393).

> "there was a significant influence of the video quality level on correct errors classification by paramedics (p = 0.021) but not by emergency physicians (p = 0.8)."
