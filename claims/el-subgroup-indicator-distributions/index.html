Evidence¶

Allensworth 2025¶

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Predictive power of grades and other indicators for English Learners. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive statistics (Table 1, fall 2008–2017 cohorts) on ninth-grade outcomes by EL subgroup. Former and late-arriving ELs had 33% with A-B GPAs versus 29% of never ELs; long-term ELs without IEPs had 14%.

"Long-term English Learners without IEPs had much lower GPAs than other students (14% had A-B GPA), while 22% of long-term English Learners with an IEP did."

Allensworth 2025 (2)¶

Allensworth, E.M., de la Torre, M., Franklin, K., & Xu, J. (2025). Predictive power of grades and other indicators for English Learners. Chicago, IL: University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai25-1241

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive distribution of standardized test scores given in English by subgroup; 63% of late-arriving ELs also scored low or very low. These are descriptive percentages with no inferential tests printed.

"Seventy-five percent of long-term English Learners without IEPs and 93% of long-term English Learners with IEPs scored in the low or very low range, while 30% of never English Learners and 27% of former English Learners scored in the low or very low range."

Related Claims¶

  • Long-term and late-arriving English Learners were considerably less likely to enroll in four-year colleges than their peers — related
  • Attainment likelihood thresholds differ: ELs with GPA above 2.0 had ~90% graduation rates except students with IEPs, and only active ELs needed attendance above 95% to be very likely to graduate — related
  • Among four-year college enrollees, former and late-arriving English Learners persisted at or above district-average rates while long-term English Learners with IEPs persisted at substantially lower rates — related
  • Risk thresholds on GPA and attendance differ for active English Learners: above 95% attendance or above 2.0 GPA predicted ~90% graduation for active ELs, while former and never ELs reached similar likelihood at 90% attendance — related
  • Late-arriving English Learners had cumulative GPAs similar to the district average but substantially lower SAT scores — related
  • Combining GPA or course failure rates with attendance provides the best prediction of high school graduation for most EL groups, while GPA plus test scores best predicts college degree attainment — related
