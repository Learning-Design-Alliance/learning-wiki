# A year-long Structured Read-Aloud intervention produced significantly greater overall DORA gains than traditional middle-school reading instruction

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Sixth graders receiving Structured Read-Aloud instruction showed significantly greater overall gains on DORA vocabulary and comprehension scores than control students receiving the same curriculum without read-aloud. [→ Marchessault and Larwin 2013](#marchessault-and-larwin-2013)

## Evidence

### Marchessault and Larwin 2013

Jennifer Kohart Marchessault, Karen H. Larwin. (2013). Structured Read-Aloud In Middle School: The Potential Impact On Reading Achievement. Contemporary Issues In Education Research, Volume 6, Number 2. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1073207

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental study of two randomly selected sixth-grade classrooms in the same Virginia district, with n = 70 control and n = 90 treatment students tested with DORA in fall and spring. A Repeated Measures Factorial ANOVA found "significant differences were found across the two groups" (F = 10.054, p < .001, Hotelling's Trace).

> "Multivariate analysis indicates that, overall, significant differences were found across the two groups - F= 10.054, p <.001 (Hotelling's Trace). This indicates that the intervention did have a significant impact overall on both the vocabulary and comprehension assessment scores for the students in the treatment group relative to the control group."
