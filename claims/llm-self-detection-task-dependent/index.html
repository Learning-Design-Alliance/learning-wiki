Evidence¶

Juho Leinonen 2026¶

Juho Leinonen, Paul Denny. (2026). Distinguishing Artificial from Authentic: Evaluating LLMs for Detecting LLM-Generated Content. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20446 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2607.20446

q2 · i? · associational · r1

Discussion of results from an evaluation using authentic student submissions from a 12-week introductory programming course at the University of Auckland (913 unique students) and 1200 GPT-4o-generated responses, judged by an LLM detector. The authors state detection was "most reliable for the two programming tasks and for the longer reflective responses".

"Detection was most reliable for the two programming tasks and for the longer reflective responses, where LLM-generated submissions typically received higher “LLM-likelihood” scores than real student work."

Related Claims¶

  • LLM-generated responses were substantially longer than authentic student submissions, suggesting response length as a signal the detector may exploit — related
  • For short-answer questions, real student responses received higher LLM-likelihood estimates than LLM-generated responses, a reversal of the pattern in other tasks — related
  • LLM-generated programming code was reliably detected, but inserting beginner bugs had an inconsistent effect across the two programming questions — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • EchoPrompt's AUROC rises with text length, from an average of 75.8% in the shortest 1-40 word bin to 98.9% in the 321-360 bin on DetectRL Length — a broader claim this one bears on
  • The deterministic reflection gate reliably distinguished low-effort defenses from substantive ones during validation, coaching filler answers rather than unlocking — related
  • EchoPrompt maintains cross-proxy robustness, averaging 87.06% AUROC across seven proxy models and outperforming DNA-DetectLLM by 2.70% AUROC on average — related
  • Baseline LLM reflective responses were highly detectable, and persona and typo prompts progressively reduced separability from real student responses — related
  • A graded likelihood detector prompt produced more consistent separation than a binary yes/no detector prompt — related
