## Evidence

### David Gunawan 2022

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Posterior means and standard deviations for indigenous population proportions over seven education categories (Table 2), estimated from HILDA data. The estimates "reveal positive skewed distribution for all years", with fewer people in higher and more in lower education categories.

> "They reveal positive skewed distribution for all years; there are fewer people in the higher education categories: bachelor, graduate diploma, and masters or doctorate categories and more people in the lower education categories: Year 11 or below, Year 12, and certificate."

### David Gunawan 2022 (2)

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Category-level posterior estimates show the modal category is "Year 11 or below" in every year; it was also the median category in 2001 and 2006, but "the median has moved to the Year 12 category" in 2014 and 2017.

> "In all years, the modal category is Year 11 or below. This category is also the median category in 2001 and 2006. In 2014 and 2017, the median has moved to the Year 12 category."

### David Gunawan 2022 (3)

David Gunawan, William Griffiths, Duangkamon Chotikapanich. (2022). Inequality in Education: A Comparison of Australian Indigenous and Nonindigenous Populations. Working paper, University of Wollongong. Originally published 2021. [doi:10.1515/spp-2021-0026](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2021-0026)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Between 2001 and 2006, despite an unchanged headcount index, the bottom of the indigenous distribution shifted: "the proportion in category 1 (year 11 and less) went down", with a corresponding increase in category 2 (year 12).

> "From Table 2, we observe the proportion in category 1 (year 11 and less) went down, and there was a corresponding increase in category 2 (year 12)."
