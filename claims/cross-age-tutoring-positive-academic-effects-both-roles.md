## Evidence

### Chang 2025

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Meta-analysis of 32 studies using robust variance estimation with Hedges' g. The pooled estimate was "0.34 (SE = 0.07) from 147 effect sizes across 31 studies" with significant heterogeneity (I² = 75.26).

> "The overall weighted mean effect size was 0.34 (SE = 0.07) from 147 effect sizes across 31 studies, indicating a significant positive effect of cross-age tutoring interventions on academic outcomes (95% CI [0.19, 0.48], p < 0.001)."

### Chang 2025 (2)

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Separate meta-analysis for tutees from 117 effect sizes across 28 studies. The printed effect size for tutees was g = 0.33, quoted as "0.33, (SE = 0.08, 95% CI [0.15, 0.50], p = 0.001)" with high heterogeneity (I² = 78.87).

> "The pooled random effects estimate of the effect of cross-age tutoring intervention on tutees' academic outcome was 0.33, (SE = 0.08, 95% CI [0.15, 0.50], p = 0.001) indicating that interventions positively impacted tutees."

### Chang 2025 (3)

Chang, A., Mauer, E., Wanzek, J., Kim, S., Scammacca, N., & Swanson, E. (2025). Examining the academic effects of cross-age tutoring: A meta-analysis. Educational Psychology Review, 37, 19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09997-z

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Separate meta-analysis for tutors from 30 effect sizes across 14 studies. The printed effect size for tutors was g = 0.39, quoted as "0.39 (SE = 0.07, 95% CI [0.23, 0.55], p < 0.001)" with moderate heterogeneity (I² = 36.76).

> "For tutors, 30 effect sizes were reported from 14 studies. The pooled random effects estimate was 0.39 (SE = 0.07, 95% CI [0.23, 0.55], p < 0.001), indicating a moderate benefit for tutors."
