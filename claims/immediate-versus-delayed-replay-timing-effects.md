# Immediate replay is consistently positively associated with learning outcomes, whereas delayed replay is weakly or negatively associated relative to non-replay

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Higher proportions of Immediate Replay are associated with stronger conceptual, procedural, flexibility, post-math, and state test performance compared with Non-Replay. [→ Shan Zhang 2026](#shan-zhang-2026)
`q2 i?` Higher proportions of Delayed Replay are negatively associated with flexibility, post-math, and state test performance compared with Non-Replay. [→ Shan Zhang 2026 (2)](#shan-zhang-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Shan Zhang 2026

Shan Zhang, Siddhartha Pradhan, Ji-Eun Lee, Ashish Gurung, and Anthony F. Botelho. 2026. Let Me Try Again: Examining Replay Behavior by Tracing Students' Latent Problem-Solving Pathways. LAK26: 16th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference. https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785093

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regressions decomposing replay into Immediate, Delayed, and Non-Replay proportions (reference: Non-Replay; N = 777) found Immediate Replay significantly outperformed Non-Replay "across all outcome domains," with the printed unstandardized coefficients and standard errors above.

> "higher proportions of Immediate Replay were associated with stronger conceptual (𝑏= 1.39,𝑆𝐸= 0.46,𝑝<. 01), procedural (𝑏= 1.47,𝑆𝐸= 0.41,𝑝<. 001), and flexibility scores (𝑏= 1.54,𝑆𝐸= 0.38,𝑝<. 001), as well as higher overall post-math scores (𝑏= 3.22,𝑆𝐸= 0.90,𝑝<. 001) and state test performance (𝑏= 0.65,𝑆𝐸= 0.22,𝑝<. 01)."

### Shan Zhang 2026 (2)

Shan Zhang, Siddhartha Pradhan, Ji-Eun Lee, Ashish Gurung, and Anthony F. Botelho. 2026. Let Me Try Again: Examining Replay Behavior by Tracing Students' Latent Problem-Solving Pathways. LAK26: 16th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference. https://doi.org/10.1145/3785022.3785093

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

In the same models, Delayed Replay was not beneficial and in some cases predicted lower achievement relative to Non-Replay, with significant negative coefficients for flexibility, post-math, and state test performance as printed; conceptual and procedural coefficients were not significant.

> "Delayed Replay was negatively associated with flexibility (𝑏=− 1.10,𝑆𝐸= 0.43,𝑝<. 01), post-math scores (𝑏=− 2.32,𝑆𝐸= 1.02,𝑝<. 05), and state test performance (𝑏=−0.62,𝑆𝐸=0.29,𝑝<.05)."
