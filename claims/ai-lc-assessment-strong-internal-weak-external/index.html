Evidence¶

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A. 2026¶

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A., Guidozzi Nadia, James Jonnie P., Pantling Jai, Menon Nainika, Salem Riadh, Markar Sheraz R. (2026). Objective technical skills assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy: a systematic review of manual, kinematic, and AI-based tools. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-026-13309-z

q3 · i? · review · r3

The AI-tools synthesis reports limited internal-structure evidence across the 13 AI studies: "Internal structure evidence was reported in only 5 of 13 AI studies (38.5%)", with direct skill classification models presenting the most consistent metrics. Generalisability was limited in 8/13 (61.5%) of studies, mostly trained on small single-centre datasets.

"Internal structure evidence was reported in only 5 of 13 AI studies (38.5%), direct skill classification models presenting the most consistent evidence, where performance metrics such as accuracy, area under the curve, and cross-validation results were commonly presented."

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A. 2026 (2)¶

Mainwaring Elizabeth M. A., Guidozzi Nadia, James Jonnie P., Pantling Jai, Menon Nainika, Salem Riadh, Markar Sheraz R. (2026). Objective technical skills assessment in laparoscopic cholecystectomy: a systematic review of manual, kinematic, and AI-based tools. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-026-13309-z

q3 · i? · review · r3

Across Messick's framework the review found that "There was no evaluation of the impact of AI-based assessment on surgical performance, training progression, behavioural modification, or patient safety outcomes"; reported benefits were limited to theoretical or anticipated improvements in feedback, standardisation, and decision support.

"There was no evaluation of the impact of AI-based assessment on surgical performance, training progression, behavioural modification, or patient safety outcomes such as bile duct injury."
