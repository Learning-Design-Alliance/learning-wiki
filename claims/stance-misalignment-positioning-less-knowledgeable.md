## Evidence

### Jessica R. Hoehn 2020

Jessica R. Hoehn, Julian D. Gifford, and Noah D. Finkelstein. (2020). Epistemic stances toward group work in learning physics: Interactions between epistemology and social dynamics in a collaborative problem solving context. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2005.02425](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2005.02425)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Analytic case study (discourse analysis of video and transcript) of one hour-long focus group in which four modern physics students solved QM problems. The authors report that "one student, whose behaviors are aligned with the non-dominant stance, is positioned as less knowledgeable" while her contributions drove the conversation. No effect size is reported; this is an interpretive qualitative finding.

> "as one stance dominates the other in the group sense making process, one student, whose behaviors are aligned with the non-dominant stance, is positioned as less knowledgeable"
