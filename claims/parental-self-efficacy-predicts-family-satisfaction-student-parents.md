## Evidence

### Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014

Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero. (2014). The influence of self-efficacy beliefs for student parents attending university. International Journal of Lifelong Education. http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/02601370.2014.884178

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Same 398-participant cross-sectional SEM study. The structural model showed "student parents with higher self-efficacy beliefs regarding their ability to parent reported feeling more satisfied with their family," with the model accounting for 29% of family satisfaction variance.

> "Similarly, student parents with higher self-efficacy beliefs regarding their ability to parent reported feeling more satisfied with their family."

### Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero 2014 (2)

Tricia M. van Rhijn & Donna S. Lero. (2014). The influence of self-efficacy beliefs for student parents attending university. International Journal of Lifelong Education. http://dx.doi.org/10.1080/02601370.2014.884178

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Bootstrapped mediation analysis (1000 draws) of the same survey data. The parental self-efficacy total effect on family satisfaction (b = .65) separated into a direct effect (b = .50, t = 4.75) and an indirect effect through school-family balance (b = .15, t = 3.19).

> "the total effect of parental self-efficacy (F2) on family satisfaction (F5) (b = .65) was separated into a significant direct effect (b = .50, t = 4.75) and a significant indirect effect through the mediator, school-family balance (b = .15, t = 3.19)."
