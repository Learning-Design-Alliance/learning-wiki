# Learning environment indicators only moderately discriminate between countries with high versus low ICT aspiration growth, with digital skills the strongest discriminant

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Linear discriminant analysis using autonomy, digital skills and teacher support achieves moderate out-of-sample classification of high- versus low-growth ICT aspiration countries, with substantial overlap between groups. [→ Popa 2022](#popa-2022)

## Evidence

### Popa 2022

Popa, D. M., Oprea, S.-V., & Bâra, A. (2022). Learning after COVID-19 and the ICT career aspirations: Are students entering the AI era with weaker skills? Department of Economic Informatics and Cybernetics, Bucharest University of Economic Studies. simona.oprea@csie.ase.ro. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27391 [doi:10.48550/arxiv.2605.27391](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2605.27391)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

LDA on standardized country-level PISA 2022 indicators, evaluated with stratified K-fold cross-validation; the article reports only that average accuracy "remains moderate rather than high", with no printed accuracy value or effect size.

> "The estimated coefficients show that digital skills have the strongest positive association with high ICT aspiration growth, followed by teacher support. In contrast, the contribution of autonomy is comparatively weaker, reinforcing the idea that autonomy alone is insufficient in the absence of complementary instructional support."
