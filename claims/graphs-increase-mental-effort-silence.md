# Solving problems with graphs increased mental effort, shown by a higher proportion of silence in think-aloud data

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants spoke less when problems included a graph; the proportion of silence rose from 62.3% to 66.3% in the multimedia condition. [→ Ögren 2017](#ogren-2017)

## Evidence

### Ögren 2017

Ögren, M., Nyström, M., & Jarodzka, H. (2017). There's more to the multimedia effect than meets the eye: is seeing pictures believing? Instructional Science, 45, 263–287. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-016-9397-6

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Verbal-data measure across eight problems; a multi-level regression on the proportion of silence showed a marginally significant effect of presentation condition (p = 0.07). The authors interpret silent pauses as indicators of increased mental effort.

> "the proportion of silence increases from 62.3 % (SD ¼ 7:0) to 66.3 % (SD ¼ 9:1) for participants in the multimedia condition"
