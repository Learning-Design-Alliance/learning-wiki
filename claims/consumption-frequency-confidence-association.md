# More frequent media consumption is associated with greater perceived ability to manage and critically analyze information

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Daily or almost-daily news consumers report higher capacity to manage information (48.5%) than occasional consumers (40.6%), and higher perceived ability to critically analyze media content (48.5% vs 34.4%). [→ Albardía MS 2025](#albardia-ms-2025)

## Evidence

### Albardía MS 2025

Albardía MS, Peña-Fernández S and Agirreazkuenaga I (2025) Technology, education and critical media literacy: potential, challenges, and opportunities. Front. Hum. Dyn. 7:1608911. https://doi.org/10.3389/fhumd.2025.1608911

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Cross-tabulation of survey data from 141 students relating consumption frequency to perceived competence. The article reports frequent consumers "report a higher capacity to manage the amount of information they receive (48.5%)" versus 40.6% of occasional consumers, and similarly 48.5% vs 34.4% for critical analysis ability. No statistical test is reported.

> "Those who consume information frequently (daily or almost daily) report a higher capacity to manage the amount of information they receive (48.5%) compared to those who consume information occasionally (40.6%)."
