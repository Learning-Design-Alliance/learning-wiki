## Evidence

### Li X 2025

Li X, Keown-Stoneman CD, Omand JA, Cost KT, Gallagher-Mackay K, Hove J, Janus M, Korczak DJ, Pullenayegum EM, Tsujimoto KC, Vanderloo LM, Maguire JL, Birken CS. (2025). Screen Time and Standardized Academic Achievement Tests in Elementary School. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.37092

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Prospective cohort analysis of 3322 grade 3 children in the TARGet Kids! primary care cohort, using adjusted cumulative logit proportional odds models linking parent-reported screen time (mean age 5.54 years) to EQAO achievement levels. The printed odds ratio effect size is "OR, 0.91" (95% CI, 0.86-0.96), i.e. "9% to 10% lower odds" per additional hour.

> "each additional hour of total screen time was associated with 9% to 10% lower odds of achieving a higher academic level in grade 3 reading (odds ratio [OR], 0.91; 95% CI, 0.86-0.96; P = .001)"

### Li X 2025 (2)

Li X, Keown-Stoneman CD, Omand JA, Cost KT, Gallagher-Mackay K, Hove J, Janus M, Korczak DJ, Pullenayegum EM, Tsujimoto KC, Vanderloo LM, Maguire JL, Birken CS. (2025). Screen Time and Standardized Academic Achievement Tests in Elementary School. JAMA Network Open. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.37092

`q3 · i1` · `associational · r2`

Same adjusted proportional odds analysis of grade 3 children: each additional hour of total screen time was associated with lower odds of a higher grade 3 math level, printed effect size "OR, 0.91" (95% CI, 0.86-0.96).

> "grade 3 math (OR, 0.91; 95% CI, 0.86-0.96; P = .001), and grade 6 math (OR, 0.90; 95% CI, 0.84-0.96; P = .002)"
