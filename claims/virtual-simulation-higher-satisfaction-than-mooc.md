# Students taught with the virtual simulation platform reported higher satisfaction with learning outcomes across all surveyed items than MOOC students

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` On a 10-item Likert satisfaction questionnaire, the virtual simulation group scored higher than the MOOC group on every item, with differences statistically significant. [→ Zhang Wang 2024](#zhang-wang-2024)

## Evidence

### Zhang Wang 2024

Zhang Wang, Xie Zhe, Li Jingfeng, Liu Changhuan, Wang Zheng, Xie Yadian, Liu Yuping, Li Zonghuan, Yang Xiaqing, Fang Xue, Wang Xinghuan, Wei Renxiong, Wang Xin. (2024). Investigating the impact of virtual simulation experiment and massive open online course (MOOC) on medical students' wound debridement training: a quasi-experimental study. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05991-1

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-class questionnaire (Cronbach's alpha 0.981, KMO 0.941) completed by 121 of 135 virtual simulation students and 112 of 122 MOOC students; all ten items favored "the experimental group", each with P < 0.05. Item means ranged from 4.55 to 4.72 in the experimental group. No standardized effect size is printed.

> "students in the experimental group were more satisfied with the training compared with the control group, and the difference between the two groups was statistically significant"
