# Second-order metacognitive judgments of subjective uncertainty show a small high-confidence error/correct difference, smaller than curiosity ratings show

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` When asked how surprised they would be to be wrong, participants showed slightly greater expected uncertainty for high-confidence errors than high-confidence correct answers, but the effect was small. [→ Metcalfe 2023](#metcalfe-2023)

## Evidence

### Metcalfe 2023

Metcalfe, J., Vuorre, M., Towner, E., & Eich, T. S. (2023). Curiosity: The effects of feedback and confidence on the desire to know. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, vol. 152, no. 2, pp. 464-482. https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001284

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Experiment 3, 48 online adults made a second-order judgment (100 minus expected surprise at being wrong) after each confidence rating with no feedback. "high-conﬁdence errors elicited slightly greater subjective uncertainty" (Difference = 8.02, pd > 99.9%), a smaller difference than curiosity ratings showed in Experiment 2.

> "A post hoc comparison on subjec- tive uncertainty at con ﬁdence = 90, between correct and incorrect answers indicated that high-conﬁdence errors elicited slightly greater subjective uncertainty than did high-con ﬁdence correct"
