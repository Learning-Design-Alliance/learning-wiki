# The best-fitting exam-score model included gender, FCI pretest, and self-efficacy, with the gender coefficient reversing to an advantage for women after adjustment

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (3 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2020 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all predictor combinations, no model beat gender, FCI pretest, and self-efficacy together for exam scores; test anxiety did not improve the models. [→ Jared B. Stang 2020](#jared-b-stang-2020)
`q2 i?` Adding FCI pretest and self-efficacy reversed the gender coefficient from a significant disadvantage for women to a significant advantage of 0.20 standard deviations. [→ Jared B. Stang 2020 (2)](#jared-b-stang-2020-2)
`q2 i?` Including self-efficacy explained 7% more of the variance in exam scores relative to the model with gender and FCI pretest. [→ Jared B. Stang 2020 (3)](#jared-b-stang-2020-3)

## Evidence

### Jared B. Stang 2020

Jared B. Stang, Emily Altiere, Joss Ives, and Patrick J. Dubois (2020). Exploring the contributions of self-efficacy and test anxiety to gender differences in assessments. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

AIC model-selection analysis of multiple regression models predicting standardized exam scores in the physics course: 'no other model had a lower AIC (within |∆AIC| < 2) than the model with gender, FCI pretest, and self-efﬁcacy as predictors'.

> "In considering all combinations of the predictors, including gender-self-efﬁcacy and gender-test anxiety interaction terms, no other model had a lower AIC (within |∆AIC| < 2) than the model with gender, FCI pretest, and self-efﬁcacy as predictors."

### Jared B. Stang 2020 (2)

Jared B. Stang, Emily Altiere, Joss Ives, and Patrick J. Dubois (2020). Exploring the contributions of self-efficacy and test anxiety to gender differences in assessments. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Regression comparison within the exam model-selection analysis: adding FCI pretest and self-efficacy 'caused the regression coefﬁcient for gender to change signs', to 'a statistically signiﬁcant advantage (of 0.20 standard deviations)' for women.

> "the addition of FCI pretest and self-efﬁcac y caused the regression coefﬁcient for gender to change signs , going from one that predicted a statistically signiﬁcant disadvantage for women to a statistically signiﬁcant advantage (of 0.20 standard deviations)"

### Jared B. Stang 2020 (3)

Jared B. Stang, Emily Altiere, Joss Ives, and Patrick J. Dubois (2020). Exploring the contributions of self-efficacy and test anxiety to gender differences in assessments. [doi:10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang](https://doi.org/10.1119/perc.2020.pr.stang)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Model comparison in the exam analysis: 'the inclusion of self-efﬁcacy explained 7% more of the variance in the exam data' relative to the gender-plus-FCI-pretest model.

> "Relative to the model with gender and FCI pretest, the inclusion of self-efﬁcacy explained 7% more of the variance in the exam data."
