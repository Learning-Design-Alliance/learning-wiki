## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Fourth-year medical students trained with facilitated interactive video did not differ significantly from those trained with simulation on the 3-week OSCE (median 24.00 vs 24.00, p = 0.385). [→ Telli Serkan 2026](#telli-serkan-2026)

## Evidence

### Telli Serkan 2026

Telli Serkan, Camgöz Süleyman, Yaman Merve, Arslan Seyda Ferah. (2026). Facilitated interactive video versus simulation-based training after a standardized lecture for basic life support in medical students: a randomized trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09128-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Prospective randomized controlled trial; 121 fourth-year medical students randomized 1:1 to video (n = 60) or simulation (n = 61). The primary outcome, the between-group OSCE difference at 3 weeks, was "24.00(22.00–26.00) vs. 24.00(21.00–26.00) (p = 0.385)"; effect size r = -0.079 with a CI crossing zero.

> "Betweengroup differences were small and not statistically significant: post-test 90.00 (85.00–95.00) vs. 90.00(80.00–95.00) (p = 0.748); gain 15.00(10.00–20.00) vs. 10.00(5.00–20.00) (p = 0.058); and OSCE 24.00(22.00–26.00) vs. 24.00(21.00–26.00) (p = 0.385)."
