## Evidence

### Sinha 2014

Sinha, T. (2014). "Your click decides your fate": Leveraging clickstream patterns from MOOC videos to infer students' information processing & attrition behavior. B.Tech project report, School of Computing Science and Engineering, VIT University, Chennai. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Full-course statistical analysis of IPI across student partitions (Viewers, Active, Statement, Achievement, Dropouts) defined by video viewing, homework submission and grades. The report also reports striking differences between Active versus Viewers (|z|=10.45, p<0.01).

> "An important observation in figure 8 is that IPI is clearly able to distinguish between Non-dropouts and Dropouts (|z|=9.06, p<0.01)."

### Sinha 2014 (2)

Sinha, T. (2014). "Your click decides your fate": Leveraging clickstream patterns from MOOC videos to infer students' information processing & attrition behavior. B.Tech project report, School of Computing Science and Engineering, VIT University, Chennai. [doi:10.3115/v1/w14-4102](https://doi.org/10.3115/v1/w14-4102)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Z test comparing IPI means of Statement versus No Statement students in the full course. The report cautions that "IPI alone is not a very good measure" for this partition because achieving a statement requires completing quizzes and assignments beyond video viewing.

> "we observe that students who do not achieve a statement have significantly higher IPI than the ones who get a statement (|z|=4.58, p<0.01)."
