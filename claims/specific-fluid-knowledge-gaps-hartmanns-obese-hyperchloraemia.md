## Evidence

### Johnson D 2026

Johnson D, Houdmont J, Levy N, Lobo DN. (2026). Investigating the fluid and electrolyte prescribing knowledge of Foundation Year doctors. Ann R Coll Surg Engl. https://doi.org/10.1308/rcsann.2025.0004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Percentage-correct analysis of individual knowledge items in the same survey of 190 Foundation Year doctors, reported in the Results section and Figure 1.

> "Although 70% of respondents correctly identified the component breakdown of 0.9% sodium chloride, only 42% could do the same for Hartmann's solution."

### Johnson D 2026 (2)

Johnson D, Houdmont J, Levy N, Lobo DN. (2026). Investigating the fluid and electrolyte prescribing knowledge of Foundation Year doctors. Ann R Coll Surg Engl. https://doi.org/10.1308/rcsann.2025.0004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

The lowest-scoring survey item asked respondents to prescribe 24-h maintenance fluid for an obese nil-by-mouth patient using NICE's ideal-body-weight principle; only 14% answered correctly.

> "This question was answered poorly, with only 14% answering correctly (2,000ml for a patient weighing 115kg, height 183cm, BMI 34kg/m2)."

### Johnson D 2026 (3)

Johnson D, Houdmont J, Levy N, Lobo DN. (2026). Investigating the fluid and electrolyte prescribing knowledge of Foundation Year doctors. Ann R Coll Surg Engl. https://doi.org/10.1308/rcsann.2025.0004

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Item-level result from the same survey; the discussion links this gap to the possibility that the risk is not covered in the limited undergraduate teaching time on fluids.

> "Few respondents (38%) were aware of the risk of metabolic acidosis associated with over-infusion of 0.9% sodium chloride solution"
