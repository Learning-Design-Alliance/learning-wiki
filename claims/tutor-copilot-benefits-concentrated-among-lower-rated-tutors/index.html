Evidence¶

Rose E. Wang 2025¶

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2410.03017

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Heterogeneity analysis of the RCT using exit-ticket passing rate, with tutor quality rating and tutoring experience categorized into tercile indicators interacted with treatment (Figure 2). The article prints percentage-point gains but no standardized effect size. Note the article's own typo labeling both results Figure 2a.

"In Figure 2a, lower-rated tutors experienced a 9 p.p. increase in student's passing their exit ticket (56%→ 65% student passing rate from control to treatment). In Figure 2a, lower-experienced tutors experienced a 7 p.p. increase (61%→ 68%)."

Rose E. Wang 2025 (2)¶

Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dora Demszky. (2025). Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise. arXiv:2410.03017v2. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017

q4 · i? · causal · r3

Same heterogeneity analysis (Figure 2, by tutor quality rating and experience). The article interprets the pattern as Tutor CoPilot helping less-effective tutors achieve outcomes comparable to their more-effective peers. No standardized effect size is printed for this interaction pattern.

"We saw these treatment effects diminish with increasing tutor effectiveness, but notably, students of lower-rated or less-experienced tutors in the treatment group performed at or above the level of students with higher-rated or more experienced tutors in the control group."
