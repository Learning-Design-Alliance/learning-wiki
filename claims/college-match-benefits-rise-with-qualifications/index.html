Evidence¶

Roderick 2018¶

Roderick, M., Holsapple, M., Clark, K., & Kelley-Kemple, T. (2018). From High School to the Future: Delivering on the Dream of College Graduation. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-delivering-dream-college-graduation

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Simulation 3 (n=36,609 four-year college-goers, classes of 2003-09) modeling elimination of undermatch: districtwide graduation would rise about 5 percentage points, with gains concentrated among very selective-access students (13 points per the report title).

"When considering all CPS college-goers, ensuring that no students undermatched could lead to a 5-point increase in their four-year college graduation rate."

Roderick 2018 (2)¶

Roderick, M., Holsapple, M., Clark, K., & Kelley-Kemple, T. (2018). From High School to the Future: Delivering on the Dream of College Graduation. University of Chicago Consortium on School Research. https://consortium.uchicago.edu/publications/high-school-future-delivering-dream-college-graduation

q2 · i? · associational · r2

The report finds college choice has little impact on graduation for low-qualification students: even a better choice leaves very little chance of graduating. For students with somewhat-selective access, avoiding underperforming colleges matters most, with local college graduation rates ranging from 2 to almost 50 percent.

"More than 70 percent of CPS graduates in our sample have academic qualifications so low that their probability of earning a four-year degree is less than 10 percent."
