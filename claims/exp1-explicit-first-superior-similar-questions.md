# Experiment 1: explicit instruction first was superior to problem-solving first on similar post-test questions (d = 0.56)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3` · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Year 5 students who received explicit instruction before problem-solving scored significantly higher on similar post-test questions than students who solved problems first (Cohen's d = 0.56). [→ Ashman et al. 2020](#ashman-et-al-2020)

## Evidence

### Ashman et al. 2020

Greg Ashman & Slava Kalyuga & John Sweller. (2020). Problem-solving or Explicit Instruction: Which Should Go First When Element Interactivity Is High? Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09500-5

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

Randomised controlled experiment with 64 Year 5 students randomly assigned to lecture–problem-solving (n = 34) or problem-solving–lecture (n = 30) conditions; post-test followed 6 days later. "scored significantly higher" for the explicit-instruction-first group, t(62) = 2.25, p = 0.03, Cohen's d = 0.56.

> "For the similar post-test questions, learners in the lecture–problem-solving condition, who received explicit instruction first, scored significantly higher than learners in the problem-solving–lecture condition who received problem-solving first (t(62) = 2.25, p = 0.03, Cohen's d = 0.56)."
