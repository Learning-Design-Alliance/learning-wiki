# Patient safety knowledge improved equally after the serious game and the e-module compared with a no-extra-education control group

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Both the game group and the e-module group scored significantly higher on a patient safety knowledge test than the historic control group, with large effect sizes, and did not differ from each other. [→ Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017](#dankbaar-mary-e-w-2017)

## Evidence

### Dankbaar Mary E. W. 2017

Dankbaar Mary E. W., Richters Olivier, Kalkman Cor J., Prins Gerrie, ten Cate Olle T. J., van Merrienboer Jeroen J. G., Schuit Stephanie C. E. (2017). Comparative effectiveness of a serious game and an e-module to support patient safety knowledge and awareness. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0836-5

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Randomized posttest-only comparison of fourth-year medical students assigned to a serious game (n = 32), an e-module (n = 34), or a historic control group (n = 37). Knowledge test means: e-module "M = 57.9, SD = 6.5", game "M = 60.1, SD = 6.7"; ANOVA F(2,89) = 9.89, p < 0.0001, ES = 0.78 and 1.11.

> "were significantly higher than in the control group (M = 52.6, SD = 7.1, F(2,89) = 9.89, p < 0.0001), with large effect sizes (ES = 0.78 and ES = 1.11 for the e-module and game group resp.) There were no significant differences in scores between the game and e-module group."
