Evidence¶

Bagnall 2025¶

Bagnall, C. L., Jindal-Snape, D., Banwell, E., Panayiotou, M., Mason, C., & Qualter, P. (2025). Emotional Wellbeing in the Context of Primary-Secondary School Transitions: A Concept Analysis Paper. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09990-6

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Thematic Framework Analysis of an open-ended online survey with a multi-disciplinary expert panel of 30 UK educational practitioners and researchers. Experts framed transitions-context emotional wellbeing as an affective state, quoting "Stability of wellbeing across the transition".

"Children described as having good emotional wellbeing were discussed as being emotionally stable during this time: "Stability of wellbeing across the transition" (Researcher [transitions], P21), and able to feel a broad range of balanced emotions, which are distinct from mental health complaints"

Bagnall 2025 (2)¶

Bagnall, C. L., Jindal-Snape, D., Banwell, E., Panayiotou, M., Mason, C., & Qualter, P. (2025). Emotional Wellbeing in the Context of Primary-Secondary School Transitions: A Concept Analysis Paper. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-09990-6

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Same expert survey analysis. Mechanisms included recognising that mixed emotions are normal, resilience and coping skills, expectation management, personal experience, and social support; transition provision was described as scaffolding these.

"Finally, conceptualisations of emotional wellbeing in the context of primary-secondary school transitions also included discussion of mechanisms to maintain a stable affective state"
