# Within the GL group, word-reading and spelling gains were larger during the intervention than during the follow-up period

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Word reading gain during training exceeded gain during follow-up, with a large effect size (d = 1.51). [→ Ronimus 2019](#ronimus-2019)
`q3 i?` Spelling gain during training exceeded gain during follow-up, with a large effect size (d = 1.27). [→ Ronimus 2019](#ronimus-2019)

## Evidence

### Ronimus 2019

Ronimus, M., Eklund, K., Pesu, L., & Lyytinen, H. (2019). Supporting struggling readers with digital game-based learning. Education Tech Research Dev, 67, 639–663. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09658-3

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Paired samples t tests comparing pre–post and post–follow-up gain scores within the training group (n = 17). Word reading: t(16) = 3.80, p = .002, d = 1.51; spelling: t(16) = 2.84, p = .012, d = 1.27 (Table 2). No significant difference in reading fluency (p = .441); reading comprehension was close to significant (p = .066).

> "the gain during the training period was significantly larger than during the follow-up period, and the effect size was above 0.80, which can be considered large according to Cohen (1988)"
