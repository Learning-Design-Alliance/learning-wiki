# Surgeons trained on a 3D-printed biliary-enteric anastomosis model earn higher anastomosis quality scores than surgeons trained by video observation or general suture practice

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` Operative scores (anastomosis quality) were significantly higher in the 3D model group than in the suture and video groups (P=0.001), with partial eta squared 0.684. [→ Shen Jiliang PhD 2024](#shen-jiliang-phd-2024)

## Evidence

### Shen Jiliang PhD 2024

Shen Jiliang PhD, Zhang Yaping PhD, Zhang Bin PhD, Lu Chen PhD, Cao Jiasheng PhD, Chen Mingyu MD, Zheng Bin MD, PhD, Yang Jin PhD. (2024). Simulation training of laparoscopic biliary-enteric anastomosis with a three-dimensional-printed model leads to better skill transfer: a randomized controlled trial. International Journal of Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1097/JS9.0000000000001079

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

In the same randomized controlled trial, blinded specialists rated anastomosis quality (leakage and knot-tying security on a 5-point Likert scale per side). The article reports "a significant difference in operative scores among the three groups (P=0.001)" and partial eta squared of 0.684 for operation score.

> "Average operative scores of the 3D model group, simple-suture group, and video group were 8.2±0.8, 6.8±0.5, and 5.2±1.3, respectively. There was a significant difference in operative scores among the three groups (P=0.001)."
