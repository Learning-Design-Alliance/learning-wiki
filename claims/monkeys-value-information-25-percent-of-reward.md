## Evidence

### Kidd 2015

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review describes the authors' curiosity tradeoff task (Blanchard, Hayden, & Bromberg-Martin, 2015), in which monkeys chose between gambles differing only in whether they offered immediate information about win or loss. The indifference-point titration showed "the value of information about a reward is about 25% of the value of the reward itself".

> "by determining the indifference point between informative and uninformative options, we found that the value of information about a reward is about 25% of the value of the reward itself—surprisingly high."

### Kidd 2015 (2)

Kidd, Celeste and Hayden, Benjamin Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. Neuron. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.010

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

The review reports from the same curiosity tradeoff task that "the value of information increases with the stakes": monkeys pay more for information about a high-stakes gamble than a low-stakes one. The review notes similar findings in pigeons choosing risky options with discriminative cues.

> "In addition, the value of information increases with the stakes."
