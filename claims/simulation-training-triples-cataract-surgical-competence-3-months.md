# Simulation-based cataract surgical training produces an almost 3-fold increase in objectively assessed surgical competence at 3 months compared with conventional training alone

## Subclaims
`q4 i?` Trainee ophthalmologists who received a 5-day simulation-based cataract surgical training course scored 16.6 points higher (out of 40) on the Sim-OSSCAR competency rubric at 3 months than control participants, after adjusting for baseline scores. ## Evidence

### Dean William H. 2021

Dean William H., Gichuhi Stephen, Buchan John C., Makupa William, Mukome Agrippa, Otiti-Sengeri Juliet, Arunga Simon, Mukherjee Subhashis, Kim Min J., Harrison-Williams Lloyd, MacLeod David, Cook Colin, Burton Matthew J. (2021). Intense Simulation-Based Surgical Education for Manual Small-Incision Cataract Surgery: The Ophthalmic Learning and Improvement Initiative in Cataract Surgery Randomized Clinical Trial in Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, and Zimbabwe. JAMA Ophthalmology. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2020.4718

`q4 · i?` · `causal · r3`

Multicenter investigator-masked parallel-group RCT at 5 university hospitals in Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, and Zimbabwe; 50 trainees randomized, 49 analyzed intention-to-treat. Linear regression adjusting for baseline Sim-OSSCAR score and training center as random effect showed scores "16.6 points (out of 40) higher (95% CI, 14.4-18.7; P < .001)". No standardized effect size was printed.

> "The participants in the intervention group were estimated to have scores 16.6 points (out of 40) higher (95% CI, 14.4-18.7; P < .001) at 3 months than the participants in the control group."
