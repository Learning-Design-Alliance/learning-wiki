# The effect of games on mathematics achievement does not differ significantly by implementation period

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r3` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Between-group homogeneity for implementation period was not significant (QB = 8.313, p = 0.216); the 6–10 hours subgroup showed the largest effect (1.010). [→ Sedat Turgut 2017](#sedat-turgut-2017)

## Evidence

### Sedat Turgut 2017

Sedat Turgut, Özlem Dogan Temur. (2017). The Effect of Game-Assisted Mathematics Education on Academic Achievement in Turkey: A Meta-Analysis Study. International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education. https://doi.org/10.26822/iejee.2017236115

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Subgroup analysis across seven implementation-period groups (1–5 hours ES = 0.337 up to 6–10 hours ES = 1.010), with QB = 8.313 below the critical value of 12.592 at 95% significance.

> "Accordingly, a statistically significant difference between groups in terms of implementation period is not found. Therefore, the academic achievement attained by using games in different implementation periods does not show a statistically  significant differentiation."
