# Individual biases and narratives influence how systems are investigated in continuous improvement, potentially leading projects to address symptoms rather than root causes

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · qualitative `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` System investigations in continuous improvement can be influenced by individual mental models, so leaders must understand their own biases and roles in the systems they seek to change. [→ Valdez 2020](#valdez-2020)

## Evidence

### Valdez 2020

Valdez, A., Takahashi, S., Krausen, K., Bowman, A., & Gurrola, E. (2020). Getting better at getting more equitable: Opportunities and barriers for using continuous improvement to advance educational equity. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED606089

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

In the interview findings on personal bias, a leader explained that "the narratives, that people are holding about what they think is going on in their system" influence how systems are investigated and problems are defined, potentially yielding symptom-level projects.

> "Even though continuous improvement says, 'See the system,' I think we have a diferent defnition of what it means to 'see the system.' Particularly, part of it is around the narrative, the narratives, that people are holding about what they think is going on in their system."
