## Evidence

### T. P. Reber and N. Rothen 2018

T. P. Reber and N. Rothen (2018). Educational App-Development needs to be informed by the Cognitive Neurosciences of Learning & Memory. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0039-4

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The comment states this as an established principle of the cognitive neuroscience of learning and memory for optimizing learning in traditional settings; it is asserted without a new experiment in this article.

> "One of the most efﬁcient ways to boost learning is to focus on the practice to actively retrieve (i.e., retrieval practice) rather than repeatedly, passively expose one-self to to-be-learned information."

### T. P. Reber and N. Rothen 2018 (2)

T. P. Reber and N. Rothen (2018). Educational App-Development needs to be informed by the Cognitive Neurosciences of Learning & Memory. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-018-0039-4

`q3 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

The comment reports the forward testing effect, citing Yang, Potts & Shanks's review, as evidence that retrieval practice generalizes to learning of new material, which it interprets as "learning to learn".

> "This so-called testing effect is not only operational within a given pool of learning items (“backward testing effect”) but more recently has also been shown to generalize to the acquisition of entirely new material (“forward testing effect”), suggesting a mechanism of “learning to learn”"
