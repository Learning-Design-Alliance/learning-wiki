# The evaluation used a school-level cluster-randomized quasi-experiment (N = 50) that was powered only to detect large effects (d = 0.81)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1`–`r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Fifty pre-service teachers in 10 schools were assigned at the school level by simple lottery to experimental (5 schools, n = 25) or control (5 schools, n = 25) conditions within a quasi-experimental embedded mixed-methods design. [→ Ismael I 2026](#ismael-i-2026)
`q2 i?` With N = 50, the minimum detectable effect size was Cohen's d = 0.81, so the study was adequately powered only for large effects and small-to-moderate effects may have gone undetected. [→ Ismael I 2026 (2)](#ismael-i-2026-2)

## Evidence

### Ismael I 2026

Ismael I, Luo X and Li S (2026). An AI-supported E-mentoring model to develop EFL pre-service teachers' self-efficacy and emotional intelligence. Front. Psychol. 17:1853510. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1853510

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Methodology section (§4.2) describing the study's assignment procedure: an embedded mixed-methods quasi-experiment in which randomization occurred at the school level, with all pre-service teachers in a given school in the same condition. The article reports the assignment 'using a simple lottery method' with 5 schools and n = 25 per condition.

> "the researcher randomly assigned the schools, as complete units, to the experimental condition (5 schools; n = 25 pre-service teachers) or the control condition (5 schools; n = 25 pre-service teachers) using a simple lottery method."

### Ismael I 2026 (2)

Ismael I, Luo X and Li S (2026). An AI-supported E-mentoring model to develop EFL pre-service teachers' self-efficacy and emotional intelligence. Front. Psychol. 17:1853510. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1853510

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Sensitivity analysis reported in §4.2 using G*Power 3.1 (two-tailed independent-samples t-test, α = 0.05, power = 0.80). The article states the minimum detectable effect was "Cohen's d = 0.81 (a large effect)" because sample size was determined by availability rather than an a priori power analysis.

> "the minimum detectable effect size for N = 50 was Cohen's d = 0.81 (a large effect). The study was therefore adequately powered to detect large intervention effects, whereas small-to-moderate effects may have gone undetected"
