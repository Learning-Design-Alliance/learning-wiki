# Adding items to a knowledge test can increase reliability without requiring the added items to be redundant with existing items

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Because the error variance term in alpha depends on the number of items, reliability can be improved by lengthening the test, requiring only that added items are not much less reliable than existing ones. [→ Steffen Zitzmann 2025](#steffen-zitzmann-2025)

## Evidence

### Steffen Zitzmann 2025

Steffen Zitzmann, Gabe A. Orona. (2025). Why We Might Still be Concerned About Low Cronbach's Alphas in Domain-specific Knowledge Tests. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10015-5

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Theoretical argument from the alpha formula: since error variance depends on the number of items K, reliability "can be easily improved by expanding the test's length." The authors argue this is a feature over mere interrelatedness, and that added items can also improve generalizability and content coverage.

> "Increasing reliability via the addition of items does not require that an additional item is well predictable by existing items. It only requires that this item is not much less reliable than the other items."
