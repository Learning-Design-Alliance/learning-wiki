# Not explicitly defining the system boundary leads to double-counting errors such as erroneously deriving v = √(4gh) for a falling apple

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` When the system boundary is diffuse or undefined, the same gravitational interaction may be included both as potential energy and as work, erroneously yielding v = √(4gh) instead of v = √(2gh) for a falling apple. [→ Suárez 2020](#suarez-2020)

## Evidence

### Suárez 2020

Suárez, A., Baccino, D., Martí, A. C., & Monteiro, M. (2020). Inconsistencies and errors in traditional approaches to energy in our introductory courses. Avances en la Enseñanza de la Física, 2(1), en. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2007.09480](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2007.09480)

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r3`

Analytical example in section 4: an apple of mass m falls from height h. Both the apple-only and apple-Earth system choices are valid and give v = √(2gh), but an undefined boundary double-counts the interaction and gives the erroneous result v = √(4gh).

> "When due attention is not paid to the definition of the system, when the boundary is diffuse or simply not defined, errors such as the following may occur, ΔK + ΔU = W (19) i.e., including the interaction of the system as potential energy and, at the same time, as work, which would erroneously result in v= √ 4gh."
