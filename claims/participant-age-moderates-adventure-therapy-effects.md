# Participant age is the only significant moderator of short-term adventure therapy outcomes, with larger effects for older participants (6.8% of variance)

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In meta-regression, only participant age significantly predicted short-term effect sizes, accounting for 6.8% of variance (β = .014, p = .004), with larger effects in studies of older age groups. [→ Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill 2013](#daniel-j-bowen-and-james-t-neill-2013)

## Evidence

### Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill 2013

Daniel J. Bowen and James T. Neill. (2013). A Meta-Analysis of Adventure Therapy Outcomes and Moderators. The Open Psychology Journal, 2013, 6, 28-53. http://www.daniel-bowen.com.au/meta-analysis

`q2 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Weighted generalised least squares meta-regression (random-effects, REML) with backwards elimination over sample, program and participant predictors; no standardised effect size is printed for the age predictor, so impact is null. Breakdowns showed effects of .24 (age ≤9), .37 (10-14), .50 (15-17) and .66 (18+). Sample characteristics explained 5.6%, program characteristics 11.5%, participant characteristics 27.0% of variance.

> "Only age was found to be a significant predictor, accounting for 6.8% of the variance (Q (1) = 8.28, p = .00; n = 157), indicating that larger effect sizes tended to be reported in studies with older age groups (β = .014, p = .004, n = 157, 95% CI = .004-.024)."
