# Course-related humour is positively correlated and course-unrelated humour negatively correlated with students' perceptions of instructional interestingness and intrinsic motivation

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Course-unrelated humour correlates negatively and course-related and self-defeating humour positively with perceived interestingness of instruction, with no significant correlation for aggressive humour. [→ Hannah Robinson 2024](#hannah-robinson-2024)

## Evidence

### Hannah Robinson 2024

Hannah Robinson, Sarah E. Rose, Jade M. Elliott, Romina A. Vivaldi. (2024). Teachers' Humour Use in the Classroom: A Scoping Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-024-09913-x

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative synthesis of correlational survey studies (theme four); the review reports Bieg and Dresel's (2018) correlations with interestingness of instruction and, separately, course-related humour as a predictor of intrinsic motivation.

> "Finally, a significant negative correlation was found for course-unrelated, and a positive correlation for self-defeating, and course-related humour and students' perceptions of interestingness of instruction, whereas there was no significant correlation for aggressive humour (Bieg & Dresel, 2018)."
