# The feedback intervention improved teachers' classroom practice as measured by the CLASS rubric (50th to 57th percentile) but had no statistically significant impact on practice measured by the FFT rubric

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2017 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Treatment teachers scored 4.50 versus 4.39 for control teachers on the CLASS overall scale, an improvement index of 7 percentile points; the FFT contrast was not statistically significant. [→ Garet 2017](#garet-2017)
`q3 i?` In the four CLASS districts the impact on CLASS scores was 0.31 points (18 percentile points), with no significant impact on CLASS scores in FFT districts and no impact on FFT scores in either district group. [→ Garet 2017 (2)](#garet-2017-2)

## Evidence

### Garet 2017

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Spring Year 2 video-recorded lessons (668 treatment and 793 control videos) coded with CLASS and FFT by trained observers, analyzed with three-level regression controlling for random assignment blocks and teacher background. The 0.11-point CLASS difference corresponds to "an improvement index of 7 percentile points".

> "On average, treatment teachers received a score of 4.50 on the CLASS (on the 7-point CLASS scale), compared with 4.39 for control teachers."

### Garet 2017 (2)

Garet, M.S., Wayne, A.J., Brown, S., Rickles, J., Song, M., and Manzeske, D. (2017). The Impact of Providing Performance Feedback to Teachers and Principals, Executive Summary (NCEE 2018-4000). Washington, DC: National Center for Education Evaluation and Regional Assistance, Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education. http://ies.ed.gov/ncee

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subgroup analysis by district rubric type. Because districts chose their rubric rather than being randomly assigned, the article states it "cannot draw definitive conclusions about why an impact on classroom practice was found in CLASS but not in FFT districts".

> "We found a 0.31-point impact on CLASS scores in the four CLASS districts (corresponding to an improvement index of 18 percentile points)."
