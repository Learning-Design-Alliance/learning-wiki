Evidence¶

Praveen Pathak 2026¶

Praveen Pathak, Siddharth Tiwary, Charudatt Kadolkar, Vijay Singh, David Rakestraw, Shirish Pathare, and Anwesh Mazumdar. (2026). Large-scale AI grading of handwritten physics assessments: Score agreement and Olympiad team selection outcomes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20521 doi:10.48550/arxiv.2608.20521

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Focused refinement run on QM question 3, part b (3 points, 40 responses), regrading with explicit rubric items. The article reports the refinement "increased Pearson'srfrom 0.70 to 0.82"; r here is AI–official score agreement, not an effect size of the refinement.

"For this question, the refinement reduced MAD from 1.1 to 0.4 marks, increased Pearson'srfrom 0.70 to 0.82, raised exact agreement from 15% to 45%, and raised agreement within 0.5 mark, including exact matches, from 35% to 80%."

Praveen Pathak 2026 (2)¶

Praveen Pathak, Siddharth Tiwary, Charudatt Kadolkar, Vijay Singh, David Rakestraw, Shirish Pathare, and Anwesh Mazumdar. (2026). Large-scale AI grading of handwritten physics assessments: Score agreement and Olympiad team selection outcomes. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20521

q2 · i? · causal · r1

Focused regrading of only the OE1 Q2 submissions with large Round I AI–official differences (n=72), an 8-point conceptual thermodynamics question. The article reports "Pearson'srincreased from 0.58 to 0.83"; r here is AI–official score agreement, not an effect size of the refinement.

"Within this subset, MAD fell from 38% to 17% of the maximum possible score for the part, and Pearson'srincreased from 0.58 to 0.83."
