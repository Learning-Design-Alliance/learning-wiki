# Subject similarity moderates dimensional comparison effects: strong contrast for dissimilar subjects, reduced contrast for similar verbal subjects, and near-zero effects for similar math/science subjects

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` For dissimilar subject combinations, paths to noncorresponding self-concepts were substantially negative (β = −.16, β = −.19). [→ Möller 2020](#moller-2020)
`q3 i0` For similar verbal subjects, dimensional comparison paths were reduced but still negative (β = −.09, β = −.11). [→ Möller 2020 (2)](#moller-2020-2)
`q3 i0` For similar math/science subjects, dimensional comparison paths were near zero and nonsignificant (β = −.06, β = −.03). [→ Möller 2020 (3)](#moller-2020-3)

## Evidence

### Möller 2020

Möller, J., Zitzmann, S., Helm, F., Machts, N., & Wolff, F. (2020). A Meta-Analysis of Relations Between Achievement and Self-Concept. Review of Educational Research, 90(3), 376–419. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320919354

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Moderator analysis with dissimilar combinations (one math/science, one verbal subject; k = 124 data sets) as reference group. Results supported Hypothesis 5 on dimensional comparisons for dissimilar subjects, with paths of β = −.16 and β = −.19.

> "The path analyses revealed strong positive paths from achievement to corresponding self-concepts (β = .55, β = .46), and lower but substantially negative paths from achievement to noncorresponding self-concepts (β = −.16, β = −.19)."
