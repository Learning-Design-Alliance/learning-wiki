## Evidence

### Gasciauskaite G 2024

Gasciauskaite G, Castellucci C, Malorgio A, Budowski AD, Schweiger G, Kolbe M, Fries D, Grande B, Nöthiger CB, Spahn DR, Roche TR, Tscholl DW, Akbas S. (2024). User Perceptions of Visual Clot in a High-Fidelity Simulation Study: Mixed Qualitative-Quantitative Study. JMIR Human Factors. https://doi.org/10.2196/47991

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Survey of 23 of the interviewed anesthesia personnel who rated 5 Likert-scale statements generated from interview themes; the article reports medians and IQR with Wilcoxon signed rank tests, and states "All sample medians differed statistically significantly from neutral (P<.05)". No effect size is printed.

> "All sample medians differed statistically significantly from neutral (P<.05). The number of participants in the quantitative part of the evaluation differs from the qualitative part because not all participants completed the questionnaire."

### Gasciauskaite G 2024 (2)

Gasciauskaite G, Castellucci C, Malorgio A, Budowski AD, Schweiger G, Kolbe M, Fries D, Grande B, Nöthiger CB, Spahn DR, Roche TR, Tscholl DW, Akbas S. (2024). User Perceptions of Visual Clot in a High-Fidelity Simulation Study: Mixed Qualitative-Quantitative Study. JMIR Human Factors. https://doi.org/10.2196/47991

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Word-count analysis of free verbalization interviews with 77 anesthesia personnel after high-fidelity simulation scenarios; "easier or easy (26/77, 33.8%)" was the most frequent advantage term. Frequencies are descriptive; no inferential test is reported for this analysis.

> "The most frequently used words and word combinations used to describe the advantages of Visual Clot were: easier or easy (26/77, 33.8%), interpret or interpretation (23.4%, 18/77 participants), quick or quickly (19.5%, 15/77 participants), visual, visualize, visualized, or visualization (19.5%, 15/77 participants)"
