# The original Head Start Impact Study threshold-variable method conflates low quality with missing data and discards continuous variance

## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Coding children with missing data as zero on the quality threshold variable fails to differentiate low quality from missing data and does not use the full variance of the continuous quality data. [→ Friedman-Krauss 2013](#friedman-krauss-2013)

## Evidence

### Friedman-Krauss 2013

Friedman-Krauss, A. H., Connors, M. C., & Morris, P. A. (2013). Estimating impacts of treatment random assignment on classroom quality in the Head Start Impact Study: The problem of missing data. SREE Spring 2013 Conference. https://www.sree.org/conferences/2013s/

`q1 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Methodological critique of the original HSIS analyses, which created threshold variables such as "ECERS-R/FDCRS score of 5 or higher" and coded all other children, including those with missing data, as zero. This is the authors' analytical argument, not a tested result.

> "Thus one problem with this method is that it does not differentiate low quality and missing data. Another problem is that it does not use the full variance of the continuous quality data."
