## Subclaims
`q2 i3` In a semi-experimental study of 70 fourth graders, the Jigsaw group's amended post-test mean (16.98) exceeded the control group's (13.99), with F = 18.366, significance 0.000, and a printed Eta Square effect size of 22% (η² = .218). [→ Hamadneh 2017](#hamadneh-2017)

## Evidence

### Hamadneh 2017

Dr. Qaseem Mohammad Salim Hamadneh. (2017). The Effect of Using Jigsaw Strategy in Teaching Science on the Acquisition of Scientific Concepts Among the Fourth Graders of Bani Kinana Directorate of Education. Journal of Education and Practice, Vol.8, No.5. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1133045

`q2 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Semi-experimental ANCOVA on post-test scores of 70 fourth graders (experimental and control groups of 17 males and 18 females each), with the pre-test as a companion variable. The amended arithmetic mean was (16.98) for the Jigsaw group versus (13.99) for the control group; the printed effect size was "the effect size (Eta Square) was (22%)", i.e. η² = .218 as printed in Table 5.

> "there are statistically significant differences at the level of (0.05) for the variable of the group in the post measurement as the value of (F) was (18.366) by a statistical significance of (0.000), and the differences were in favor of the experimental group, where the amended arithmetic mean was (16.98), while the amended arithmetic mean of the control group was (13.99); the effect size (Eta Square) was (22%)"
