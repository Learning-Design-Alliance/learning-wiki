Evidence¶

de Jong 2021¶

de Jong, T., Gillet, D., Rodríguez-Triana, M. J., Hovardas, T., Dikke, D., Doran, R., Dziabenko, O., Koslowsky, J., Korventausta, M., Law, E., Pedaste, M., Tasiopoulou, E., Vidal, G., & Zacharia, Z. C. (2021). Understanding teacher design practices for digital inquiry–based science learning: the case of Go-Lab. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09904-z

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Kruskal-Wallis tests on 2414 implemented ILSs showed design time and actions were significantly higher for collaborative teams. Design time averaged 433 minutes for teacher-project member teams versus 251 for single teachers. "The values for all design parameters were significantly higher in the case of collaboration, especially when teachers collaborated with project members."

"The values for all design parameters were significantly higher in the case of collaboration, especially when teachers collaborated with project members"

de Jong 2021 (2)¶

de Jong, T., Gillet, D., Rodríguez-Triana, M. J., Hovardas, T., Dikke, D., Doran, R., Dziabenko, O., Koslowsky, J., Korventausta, M., Law, E., Pedaste, M., Tasiopoulou, E., Vidal, G., & Zacharia, Z. C. (2021). Understanding teacher design practices for digital inquiry–based science learning: the case of Go-Lab. Education Tech Research Dev. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-020-09904-z

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Likelihood ratio chi-square tests showed 14.3% of teacher-project member ILSs were iteratively designed versus 7.2% for single teachers, and 49.0% were reused later versus 20.3% for single teachers. "implemented ILSs co-created by teachers and project members were more likely to have been iteratively designed, with intermediate versions."

"implemented ILSs co-created by teachers and project members were more likely to have been iteratively designed, with intermediate versions; they also had a markedly higher chance of being reused later on"
