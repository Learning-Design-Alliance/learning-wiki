## Evidence

### Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan 2018

Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan, Gallagher Anthony, O'Brien Niall, Sudir Vinod, Barrett Nick, O'Connor Raymund, Holt Francesca, Lee Peter, O'Donnell Brian, Shorten George. (2018). Proficiency-based progression training: an 'end to end' model for decreasing error applied to achievement of effective epidural analgesia during labour: a randomised control study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-020099

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Validation study of 22 eligible first-person videos (13 expert, 9 novice) scored by two blinded independent assessors trained to IRR above 0.8. ANOVA compared error scores between expert and novice groups; the quote reports "less (16) than the equivalent in the trainee group (20)".

> "The average number of errors, as measured by metrics, made by the expert group was less (16) than the equivalent in the trainee group (20) (p=0.02 based on ANOVA)."

### Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan 2018 (2)

Kallidaikurichi Srinivasan Karthikeyan, Gallagher Anthony, O'Brien Niall, Sudir Vinod, Barrett Nick, O'Connor Raymund, Holt Francesca, Lee Peter, O'Donnell Brian, Shorten George. (2018). Proficiency-based progression training: an 'end to end' model for decreasing error applied to achievement of effective epidural analgesia during labour: a randomised control study. BMJ Open. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2017-020099

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same validation phase comparing metrics, task-specific checklist and global rating scale against GRS and TSCL for concurrent validity. The quote states the metrics IRR was "the highest (0.88) among the three" and that GRS "but not TSCL" showed construct validity.

> "The GRS scores (but not TSCL) demonstrated construct validity, that is, differentiate between expert and novice performance. It must be noted that not only was the IRR (calculated as detailed earlier) of metrics the highest (0.88) among the three, it also enabled differentiation of trainees and experts."
