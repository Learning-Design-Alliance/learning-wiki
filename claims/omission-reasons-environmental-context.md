# Most reasons for omitting ID steps reflect the environmental context, and decision already made is cited most often

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q2` quasi-experiment · `i1` small

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Reasons 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 for not performing steps reflect the environmental context of the project, while Reason 4 reflects the designer's attitude. [→ Winer 1994](#winer-1994)
`q2 i?` Decision already made was the most-cited reason (115 citations), ahead of not enough time (94). [→ Winer 1994 (2)](#winer-1994-2)

## Evidence

### Winer 1994

Winer, L. R., Vázquez-Abad, J. A., & Tessmer, M. (1994). Enriching the Layers of Necessity Model. Paper presented at the Annual Meeting of AERA. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED371037

`q2 · i1`

Analysis of the six provided reasons for not performing steps in the Montreal survey; the authors judge Reason 4 (Considered unnecessary) qualitatively different because it "reflects the attitude of the Instructional Designer" rather than the environmental context.

> "Reasons 1, 2, 3, 5 & 6 all reflect elements of the environmental context in which a project is being carried out."

### Winer 1994 (2)

Winer, L. R., Vázquez-Abad, J. A., & Tessmer, M. (1994). Enriching the Layers of Necessity Model. Paper presented at the Annual Meeting of AERA. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED371037

`q2 · i1`

Citation counts for omission reasons in the Montreal survey: Decision already made (115), Not enough time (94), Considered unnecessary (92), Client won't support (92), Not enough money (48), Lack expertise (29), which was cited least often.

> "The ranking of the reasons was: Decision already made (115 citations) Not enough time (94 citations) Considered unnecessary (92 citations)"
