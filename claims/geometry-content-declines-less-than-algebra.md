## Evidence

### Bahrick 1991

Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(1), 20-33. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Comparison of regression results for the geometry analysis (946 participants) against the Algebra 1 analysis. The article offers conjectural interpretations: higher average degrees of learning in the plane geometry course and greater parsimony and integration of that content, while noting no scales exist for comparing the contents on relevant dimensions.

> "the overall decline in performance is smaller for geometry content (the retention interval variables yield a multiple correlation of .39 with Algebra 1 test scores and of. 17 with geometry test scores)."

### Bahrick 1991 (2)

Bahrick, H. P., & Hall, L. K. (1991). Lifetime maintenance of high school mathematics content. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 120(1), 20-33. https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.120.1.20

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Hierarchical regression of geometry test performance. The article attributes the lower predictability to the greater autonomy of geometry content from other mathematics courses, which yields more regression of predicted scores and less divergent retention profiles across mathematics backgrounds.

> "two differences are noteworthy: (a) The retention interval accounts for only 2% of variation in performance, (b) The final step of the hierarchical analysis yields a multiple R of .69, which is considerably lower than the validity of prediction achieved for the algebra data."
