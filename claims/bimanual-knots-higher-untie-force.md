# More force is required to untie knots tied after bimanual training than after dominant-hand training

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The median force required to untie knots was significantly higher in the bimanual group (14 N) than in the dominant-hand group (5.1 N; p = 0.001). [→ Asencio FA 2018](#asencio-fa-2018)

## Evidence

### Asencio FA 2018

Asencio FA, Ribeiro HASA, Romeo A, Wattiez A, Ribeiro PAGA. (2018). The Effect on Performance Time and Quality of the Knots after Mono or Bimanual Training of Laparoscopic Intracorporeal Knot Tying according to the Gladiator Rule Technique. Rev Bras Ginecol Obstet. https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0038-1649494

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Laboratory knot-quality testing (dynamometer, 17 N traction limit) of knots from the two training groups; the force-to-untie comparison favored the bimanual group, whose median was "very close to the breaking strength of the thread" (18 N).

> "The force required to untie the knots was significantly higher in group B, with a median of 14 N, than in group A, with a median of 5.1 N (Table 2)."
