## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Nelissen Ellen 2017

Nelissen Ellen, Ersdal Hege, Mduma Estomih, Evjen-Olsen Bjørg, Twisk Jos, Broerse Jacqueline, van Roosmalen Jos, Stekelenburg Jelle. (2017). Clinical performance and patient outcome after simulation-based training in prevention and management of postpartum haemorrhage: an educational intervention study in a low-resource setting. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1481-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Direct observational outcome data from the same before-after intervention study (3622 vs 5824 births). The article reports the proportion receiving 10 IU oxytocin increased significantly from 87.8% to 91.7% (p < .001) and that "Removal of placenta by controlled cord traction and subsequent uterine massage were more frequently performed after training".

> "Removal of placenta by controlled cord traction and subsequent uterine massage were more frequently performed after training (98.8% and 99.0% respectively) compared to before training (96.5% and 93.0% respectively)."

### Nelissen Ellen 2017 (2)

Nelissen Ellen, Ersdal Hege, Mduma Estomih, Evjen-Olsen Bjørg, Twisk Jos, Broerse Jacqueline, van Roosmalen Jos, Stekelenburg Jelle. (2017). Clinical performance and patient outcome after simulation-based training in prevention and management of postpartum haemorrhage: an educational intervention study in a low-resource setting. BMC Pregnancy and Childbirth. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-017-1481-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Observed timing of uterotonic administration in the same prospective before-after study; the article prints the increase from 40.4% to 44.3% as significant (p = 0.001 in Table 3).

> "After training, a significantly greater proportion of women received uterotonic drugs within one minute after birth (before training 40.4%, after training 44.3%)."
