# Failure Free Reading shows no discernible effects on alphabetics outcomes for beginning readers

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In a randomized controlled trial, Failure Free Reading did not have a statistically significant effect on any of four alphabetics outcomes, and the domain average effect size (0.04) was not substantively important. [→ What Works Clearinghouse 2007](#what-works-clearinghouse-2007)

## Evidence

### What Works Clearinghouse 2007

What Works Clearinghouse, U.S. Department of Education. (2007). WWC Intervention Report: Failure Free Reading. https://www.failurefreeonline.com/index_parents.php

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Findings from the Torgesen et al. (2006) randomized controlled trial of 93 third-grade students in eight Pennsylvania school units, as summarized by the WWC across four alphabetics measures (WRMT-R Word Identification and Word Attack; TOWRE Phonetic Decoding Efficiency and Sight Word Efficiency). The WWC computed a domain average effect size of 0.04, "neither statistically significant nor large enough to be considered substantively important" (threshold 0.25), with an average improvement index of +1 percentile points (range -3 to +7).

> "The authors reported that Failure Free Reading did not have a statistically significant effect on any of the four outcomes. The average effect size across the three outcomes was neither statistically significant nor large enough to be considered substantively important"
