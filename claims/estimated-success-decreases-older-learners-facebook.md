# Estimated success at helping others learn Facebook decreases significantly as the hypothetical learner's age increases from 20 to 60

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i3` large

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` University students' estimated success helping others learn Facebook significantly decreased with the learner's age (20, 40, 60), with a large main effect of age. [→ Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016](#harvey-j-ginsburg-2016)

## Evidence

### Harvey J. GINSBURG 2016

Harvey J. GINSBURG, Rebecca CAMERON, Roque V. MENDEZ & Michael WESTHOFF. (2016). Helping others use social media: age stereotypes when estimating learner's success. Psychology, Society, & Education, 8(1), 1-12. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1123456

`q3 · i3`

Repeated measures experiment with 212 undergraduate Facebook users rating six hypothetical scenarios. The ANOVA showed "age differences in estimated learner success, F(1.802,383.84) = 114.42, p< .001,partial η2 = .349". Means declined from 7.47 (age 20) to 5.07 (age 40) to 3.62 (age 60).
