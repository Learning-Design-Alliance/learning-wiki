## Evidence

### E. Tufino 2025

E. Tufino, P. Onorato and S. Oss. (2025). Exploring active learning in physics with ISLE-based modules in high school. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1088/1742-6596/2950/1/012021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/2950/1/012021)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Descriptive rubric analysis of final tests across three 11th-grade (N=56) and two 12th-grade (N=44) classrooms, using four proficiency levels. The comparison between grade groups is descriptive; no statistical test is reported.

> "The results indicate a positive trend, with the majority of students demonstrating good levels of Technical Skills, Conceptual Knowledge, and familiarity with the ISLE Process. Notably, the outcomes are slightly better for the two 12th grade classes, where a greater number of instructional hours were allocated."

### E. Tufino 2025 (2)

E. Tufino, P. Onorato and S. Oss. (2025). Exploring active learning in physics with ISLE-based modules in high school. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1088/1742-6596/2950/1/012021](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/2950/1/012021)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Post-module survey ratings from the two 12th-grade classrooms (N=44), which had roughly twice the teaching time of other modules. Descriptive percentages only; no test statistic is printed.

> "For the 12th grade classes, which had more hours available and engaged in group activities using whiteboards, 35 students rated the experience positively ('4' or '5'), 8 were neutral, and only 1 student gave a low rating ('1')."
