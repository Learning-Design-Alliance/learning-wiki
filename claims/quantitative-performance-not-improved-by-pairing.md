# Pairing a conceptual question with a quantitative question does not significantly improve quantitative performance (H1 not supported)

## Subclaims

`q2 i?` Student performance on quantitative questions was not significantly different when both questions of an IPP were given back to back than when only the quantitative question was given. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016)

`q2 i?` Contrary to hypothesis H1, quantitative-question performance was not significantly different whether both questions or only the quantitative question were given. [→ Chandralekha Singh 2016](#chandralekha-singh-2016-2)

## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part II: Effect of Some Potential Factors on Problem Solving and Transfer. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 Chi-square comparisons in Table 1 across nine introductory physics courses found no significant difference for quantitative questions given alone versus with the conceptual pair; equivalence was not tested.

> "Table 1 shows that contrary to our hypothesis H1, stu- dent performance on quantitative questions was not sig- niﬁcantly different when both quantitative and concep- tual questions were given back to back (with the quan- titative question preceding the conceptual question) than when only the corresponding quantitative question was given."

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`
