# Addressing the real/ideal gulf directly without addressing affect failed to shift Judy's view that conceptual reasoning is unimportant

## Evidence

### Ayush Gupta 2010

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2010). Understanding students' difficulties in terms of coupled epistemological and affective dynamics. 40th ASEE/IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference. [doi:10.1109/fie.2010.5673256](https://doi.org/10.1109/fie.2010.5673256)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Disconfirmatory Episode B from the same interview: with her annoyance still apparent, Judy judged physical aspects of real-world circuits 'not very important'. The authors state the two-node affect-free model cannot explain this, motivating the affect-inclusive model.

> "a model in which she finds conceptual problems unproductive because they apply only to an idealized world does not explain why she finds conceptual reasoning unproductive even when used to analyze real-world circuits."

### Ayush Gupta 2013

Ayush Gupta, Brian A. Danielak, Andrew Elby. (2013). Toward affect-inclusive models of cognitive dynamics: Coupling epistemological resources and emotions. Pre-print for arxiv.org, under review at Physical Review Special Topics – Physics Education Research. https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1333

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Episode B from the interview: asked whether knowing physical aspects matters for analyzing a real-world circuit while her annoyance was still apparent, Judy answered "Not very important." The authors read this as evidence against the affect-free model. No effect sizes are reported.

> "As discussed above, a model in which affect is just incidental cannot explain Judy’s response: if she finds conceptual problems unproductive partly because they apply only to an idealized world, then why would conceptual reasoning be “not very important” when applied to a real-world circuit?"
