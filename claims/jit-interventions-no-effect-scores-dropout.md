# Just-in-time interventions had no significant effects on course dropout rates or scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Just-in-time interventions (RFC and break) had no significant effects on dropout rates or scores in the course (all p > 0.69). [→ Ralf Teusner 2018](#ralf-teusner-2018)

## Evidence

### Ralf Teusner 2018

Ralf Teusner, Thomas Hille, Thomas Staubitz. (2018). Effects of Automated Interventions in Programming Assignments: Evidence from a Field Experiment. L@S 2018. https://doi.org/10.1145/3231644.3231650

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

A/B test comparing dropout rates (70.6% control, 71.3% break, 71.9% RFC) and scores across groups; the study found they "had no signiﬁcant effects on dropout rates or scores in the course (all p > 0.69)"; equivalence was not tested.

> "As the ﬁrst of four key ﬁndings on the just-in-time interventions, it is apparent that they had no signiﬁcant effects on dropout rates or scores in the course (all p > 0.69)."
