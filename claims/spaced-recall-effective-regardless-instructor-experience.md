# The retention benefit of spaced recall quizzes holds regardless of instructor teaching experience

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A two-way ANOVA found no evidence that instructor experience level (spanning 5 to 20 years) affected student retention performance, while the treatment effect remained significant. [→ Lindquist 2024](#lindquist-2024)

## Evidence

### Lindquist 2024

Lindquist, D. S., Sparrow, B. E., & Lindquist, J. M. (2024). Spaced recall reduces forgetting of fundamental mathematical concepts in a post high school precalculus course. Instructional Science, 52, 859-878. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-024-09680-w

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two-way ANOVA (treatment/control by instructor) on retention index in the USMAPS experiment; Table 4 reports instructor effect p = 0.302 and C vs. T p = 0.022. No effect size is printed.

> "We conducted a two-way ANOVA and confirmed that there is almost no evidence to suggest that student performance was affected by the instructor's level of teaching experience (see Table 4). This result is powerful because it shows that spaced recall in the form of recall quizzes can be effective for any instructor no matter the years of teaching experience."
