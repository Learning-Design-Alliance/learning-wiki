## Evidence

### Shioiri Toshiki 2024

Shioiri Toshiki, Nakashima Michiyo, Tsunekawa Koji. (2024). When can we identify the students at risk of failure in the national medical licensure examination in Japan using the predictive pass rate? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05948-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Single-year (2018) application of the PPR formula at GUSM, where 15 candidates with PPR ≤ 95% were selected and given guidance by the competency committee. The article reports "PPR predicted all 5 failures who were included in 15 candidates"; no effect size is printed.

> "PPR predicted all 5 failures who were included in 15 candidates. This suggested that performing risk analysis based on several variables, such as PPR, can lead to more effective intervention compared to a single variable, i.e., performance in the ME."

### Shioiri Toshiki 2024 (2)

Shioiri Toshiki, Nakashima Michiyo, Tsunekawa Koji. (2024). When can we identify the students at risk of failure in the national medical licensure examination in Japan using the predictive pass rate? BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-024-05948-4

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The authors' report of the outcome after the 2018 PPR-guided intervention, in which flagged students "were provided adequate guidance by the competency committee to overcome their shortcomings before taking the actual NMLE". No pass-rate numbers or test statistics are printed for this comparison.

> "This dramatically improved the actual pass rate for NMLE at the first attempt in 2018."
