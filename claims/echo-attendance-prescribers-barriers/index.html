Evidence¶

Puckett 2021¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Participation logs and interviews with 24 primary physicians and clinical staff from 13 clinics registered for a 12-week case-based OUD ECHO curriculum. Attendance was also higher in early program weeks; no test statistic printed.

"primary care physician (PCP) attendance to ECHO sessions was variable, with higher rates of participation among prescribers (mean/SD rate of 5.9/3.6) than non-prescribers (mean/SD rate of 2.6/3.1)"

Puckett 2021 (2)¶

Puckett, H. M., Bossaller, J. S., & Sheets, L. R. (2021). The impact of project ECHO on physician preparedness to treat opioid use disorder: a systematic review. Addiction Science & Clinical Practice, 16, 6. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00215-z

q2 · i? · review · r2

Interview data from the same 12-week ECHO study. Participants valued "the ease of access of the sessions" and interactive case presentations, but consistently cited prioritizing patient care and leadership willingness as constraints on MOUD integration.

"The most notable engagement challenge reported by participants was the burden of competing demands on time and availability."
