Evidence¶

Andrä 2020¶

Andrä, C., Mathias, B., Schwager, A., Macedonia, M., & von Kriegstein, K. (2020). Learning Foreign Language Vocabulary with Gestures and Pictures Enhances Vocabulary Memory for Several Months Post-Learning in Eight-Year-Old School Children. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09527-z

q3 · i? · causal · r2

Summary of the three-experiment program: 54, 43, and 51 eight-year-old children trained 5 consecutive days on auditorily presented English vocabulary, tested at 3 days, 2 months, and 6 months post-learning via recall and translation tests. Benefits "persisted up to 6 months after training."

"B oth gesture and picture enrichment enhanced children 's test performance compared with non-enriched learning. Bene- fits of gesture and picture enrichment per sisted up to 6 months after training and occurred for both concrete and abstract words."

Related Claims¶

  • Children's age did not moderate gesture enrichment benefits on vocabulary test performance in Experiment 1 — related
  • Children's L2 translation and free recall scores were higher for concrete than abstract nouns across experiments — related
  • Gesture-enriched learning improves 8-year-olds' L2 vocabulary translation and free recall compared with non-enriched learning, persisting up to 6 months — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Gesture enrichment specifically aided translation of abstract words in Experiment 1, but this word-type specificity did not replicate in Experiment 2 — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Gesture and picture enrichment produced similar benefits on children's L2 vocabulary learning, contrary to adult findings favoring gestures — related
