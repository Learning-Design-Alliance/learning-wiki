## Subclaims
`q2 i?` LD students taught through the traditional lecture method did not make a statistically significant gain from pre-test to post-test, though post-test means were descriptively higher. [→ Sivaram and Ramar 2014](#sivaram-and-ramar-2014)

## Evidence

### Sivaram and Ramar 2014

R.T. Sivaram, R. Ramar. (2014). Effect of CAI on Achievement of LD Students in English. li-manager's Journal of English Language Teaching, Vol. 4, No. 1, January-March. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1068387

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Pre-test/post-test analysis of 25 control-group LD students taught by lecture; Table 1 reports means of 20.6 (pre) and 23.2 (post) with a t-value of 1.62, marked "not significant at 0.05 level". Equivalence was not tested.

> "There is no significant difference in the performance of the control group LD students taught through traditional lecture method between pre-test and post-test."
