Evidence¶

Hollands 2020¶

Hollands, F., & Escueta, M. (2020). How research informs educational technology decision-making in higher education: the role of external research versus internal research. Education Tech Research Dev, 68, 163–180. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-019-09678-z

q2 · i? · qualitative · r2

Qualitative interview study finding that internal investigations varied widely in goals and rigor, with a few resulting in peer-reviewed publications but most not shared publicly. The article reports that "In 78% of our interviews, decision-makers reported conducting their own investigations of educational technology products."

Related Claims¶

  • Higher education educational technology decision-makers rarely use externally-produced, scientifically-rigorous research, which was mentioned in less than 20% of interviews — related
  • The research activities decision-makers most often counted as research were internal: staff/student/faculty interviews, surveys or focus groups (40% of interviews) and looking at student outcomes after implementation (38%) — related
  • Interview respondents identify collaborative relationships, trust in evidence and its users, clarity on research types, and supportive leadership as facilitators of evidence use — related
