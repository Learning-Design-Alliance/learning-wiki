# In interviews, American students appeared more confident, used fewer words, and stayed more focused on the task than Romanian students

## Subclaims

`q1 i?` Three American and three Romanian students were interviewed while thinking aloud; Americans were overall more confident, answered with fewer words, and were more focused ("pure activity mode" vs "mixed activity mode"). [→ C. M. Răduță 2013](#c-m-raduta-2013)

`q2 i?` Three American and three Romanian students interviewed on video showed American students more confident, using fewer words, and talking fewer times about unrelated topics. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

`q2 i?` American students answered with fewer words and appeared overall more confident than Romanian students in tape-recorded interviews. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

`q2 i?` American students went off-topic fewer times, described as pure activity mode versus mixed activity mode. [→ Cristian Raduta 2005](#cristian-raduta-2005)

## Evidence

### C. M. Răduță 2013

C. M. Răduță. (2013). Consequences the Extensive Use of Multiple-Choice Questions Might Have on Student's Reasoning Structure. Institute of Physics and Nuclear Engineering, Magurele. Originally published 2010. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1307.1050](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1307.1050)

`q1 · i?` · `qualitative · r1`

A think-aloud interview study in which three American and three Romanian students explained the solution to each part of the Table 5.1 E&M problem; results are reported in Table 5.7 as consistent with the written survey.

> "American students seem to be overall more confident on their answers than Romanian students did, answering each question usin g smaller number of words than Romanians did. Also, American students seemed to be more focused (“pure activity mode” vs. “mixed activity mode”) on the in terview than Romanian students did"

### Cristian Raduta 2005

Cristian Raduta, Gordon Aubrecht. (2005). Cultural differences in answering physics questions: Could they arise from the difference between reasoning expected in answering exam questions in those cultures?. Originally published 2002. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503124](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.physics/0503124)

`q1 · i?`

Second experiment: three American and three Romanian students were interviewed and videotaped while explaining each part of the magnetic-force problem aloud; Table 4.7 records word counts, phrase length, tone and off-topic talk. The authors state the results do not contradict the written survey.

> "As in the written surveys, American students seem to be overall more confident on their answers than Romanian students did, answering each question using smaller number of words than their omologs from Romania."
