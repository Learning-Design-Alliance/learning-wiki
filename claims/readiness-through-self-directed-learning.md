# Interns' perceived readiness for internship came from self-directed learning rather than the undergraduate curriculum

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Interns who felt well prepared attributed readiness to self-motivated, self-directed seeking of clinical experiences, as undergraduate pressures rewarded study over clinical exposure. [→ Pattinson 2025](#pattinson-2025)

## Evidence

### Pattinson 2025

Pattinson, S. R., Savelberg, H., & Atherley, A. (2025). Readiness for the Realities of Internship: An Ethnographic Study Using Legitimation Code Theory. Perspectives on Medical Education, 14(1), 628–640. https://doi.org/10.5334/pme.1802

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

From informal conversations and interviews during the ethnography. The authors report readiness "came from being self-motivated and self-directed in seeking out clinical experiences", since curricular pressure prioritised high-stakes theory assessments.

> "This readiness, however, came from being self-motivated and self-directed in seeking out clinical experiences: There was little pressure or expectation to get involved with patient care… so clinical experience came down to individual motivation and willingness and keenness to get involved."
