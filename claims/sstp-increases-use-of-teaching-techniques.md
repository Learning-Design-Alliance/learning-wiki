# The SSTP increased teachers' classroom use of social skills teaching techniques for students with and without special needs, and use persisted at follow-up

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · causal `r1` · `q2`
> **Currency** · newest study 2013 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Before training, teachers in both groups used none of the observed social skills teaching techniques; after training, experimental teachers used rewarding, prompting, and modeling for students with special needs and the same techniques at higher frequency for students without special needs. [→ Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013](#elif-sazak-pinar-2013)
`q2 i?` At follow-up, experimental teachers continued using the techniques more often than control teachers, and began using rehearsal, which they had not used before. [→ Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013 (2)](#elif-sazak-pinar-2013-2)

## Evidence

### Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013

Elif SAZAK PINAR, Bülbin SUCUOGLU. (2013). The Outcomes of a Social Skills Teaching Program for Inclusive Classroom Teachers. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 13(4), 2247-2261. https://www.edam.com.tr/kuyeb

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Classroom observations with the Teacher Behavior Observation Form, conducted before, after, and two months after the SSTP; mean frequencies of techniques are reported descriptively (no significance tests, since no teacher used any technique before training).

> "After the training however, teachers in the experiment group used the rewarding (X =4,25), prompting (X =4), and modeling (X =2,6) techniques for the student with special needs. For the students without special needs, they used the same techniques of prompting (X = 6,25 ), rewarding (X = 5,8), modeling (X = 3,8), and other prompts (X = 0,2), but at a higher frequency."

### Elif SAZAK PINAR 2013 (2)

Elif SAZAK PINAR, Bülbin SUCUOGLU. (2013). The Outcomes of a Social Skills Teaching Program for Inclusive Classroom Teachers. Educational Sciences: Theory & Practice, 13(4), 2247-2261. https://www.edam.com.tr/kuyeb

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Follow-up observation data: experimental teachers used social skills teaching techniques more often than control teachers, who used rewarding only for students without special needs (X = 0.8); the authors interpret this as the SSTP increasing technique use for all students.

> "In the follow-up stage, it was seen that teachers in the control group used the rewarding technique only for the students without special needs ( X = 0.8) while the teachers in the experiment group used social skills teaching techniques more often. This finding shows that the SSTP is effective in increasing the teachers’ use of social skill teaching techniques for all students, whether they have special needs or not."
