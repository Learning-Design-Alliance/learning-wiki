Evidence¶

Kaito Kawakami 2024¶

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Longitudinal analysis of 4175 TEDS participants correlating age-7 GPAΔ with individual achievement slopes (ages 7, 9, 12, 16). The article reports the correlation "explained 20.8% of the variance of achievement slopes," indicating movement toward genomic trajectories.

"The correlation was negative (r(4173) =−0.456, CI95%[−0.480, −0.432], p < 0.001; Fig. 2)a n d explained 20.8% of the variance of achievement slopes."

Kaito Kawakami 2024 (2)¶

Kaito Kawakami, Francesca Procopio, Kaili Rimfeld, Margherita Malanchini, Sophie von Stumm, Kathryn Asbury, Robert Plomin. (2024). Exploring the genetic prediction of academic underachievement and overachievement. npj Science of Learning. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-024-00251-9

q3 · i? · associational · r3

Using a trimmed-mean regression percentage formula on the same TEDS longitudinal sample, the authors found participants "regressed to 38.4% of their genomically predicted levels of achievement at age 16." Underachievers progressed more, overachievers less.

"We found that participants, on average, regressed to 38.4% of their genomically predicted levels of achievement at age 16. In other words, GPA Δ underachievers progressed more and GPA Δ overachievers progressed less during their school years."
