## Evidence

### Michael F. J. Fox and Taylor O. Pomfret 2025

Michael F. J. Fox and Taylor O. Pomfret. (2025). Self-Evaluated Expertise in experimental physics: a measure of students' physics self-recognition. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.07885](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.07885)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-wise pre/post comparison of item mean scores across the public E-CLASS dataset, using Cohen's d to normalise the different scales of the two measures (Fig. 6). The article reports a Spearman rank-order correlation of "0.808±0.006" between the item-level shifts; no standardized effect-size statistic with a printed symbol-value pair is printed for this correlation, so impact is left unquantified.

> "The SEE score also shows a negative shift from pre-test to post-test, and has a strong correlation with the YOU score, see Figure 6 (Spearman rank-order correlation 0.808±0.006)."

### Michael F. J. Fox and Taylor O. Pomfret 2025 (2)

Michael F. J. Fox and Taylor O. Pomfret. (2025). Self-Evaluated Expertise in experimental physics: a measure of students' physics self-recognition. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.07885](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.07885)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Item-wise analysis of the same dataset: respondents on item 17 re-evaluate their perception of how experts would respond without changing their own view. The article calls this "perhaps the strongest evidence that the SEE score practically measures a different quantity than YOU scores."

> "item 17 ("When I encounter difficulties in the lab, my first step is to ask an expert, like the instructor.") shows no change in YOU score from pre-test to post-test, while having a decrease in SEE score."
