## Evidence

### Hwan Young Jang and Hye Jeong Kim 2020

Hwan Young Jang and Hye Jeong Kim. (2020). A Meta-Analysis of the Cognitive, Affective, and Interpersonal Outcomes of Flipped Classrooms in Higher Education. Educ. Sci. 2020, 10, 115. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci10040115

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Publication bias assessment of the 43-study meta-analysis using a funnel plot of effect sizes against standard errors (Figure 3). The analysis "shows no missing studies and ﬁnds no imputations of eﬀect size for publication bias."

> "The funnel plot (Figure 3) shows the symmetry of eﬀect size distribution in the mean eﬀect size whether publication bias in the overall eﬀect size exists, providing no evidence for publication bias."

### Hwan Young Jang and Hye Jeong Kim 2020 (2)

Hwan Young Jang and Hye Jeong Kim. (2020). A Meta-Analysis of the Cognitive, Affective, and Interpersonal Outcomes of Flipped Classrooms in Higher Education. Educ. Sci. 2020, 10, 115. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci10040115

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Subgroup analysis by publication type across 218 effect sizes (Table 7): dissertations k = 29, ES = 0.61; journal articles k = 188, ES = 0.29. The difference was not significant. Data format also varied: standardized mean change difference ES = 0.47, pre–post ES = 0.38, treatment vs. control ES = 0.25.

> "Regarding publication type, the eﬀect size of dissertations (ES = 0.61, 95% CI = 0.54 to 0.68) was larger than the eﬀect size of journal articles (ES = 0.29, 95% CI = 0.26 to 0.31), but the diﬀerence was not signiﬁcant (Table 7)."
