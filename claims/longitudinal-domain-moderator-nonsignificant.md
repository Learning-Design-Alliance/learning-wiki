# Longitudinally, the ToM–achievement association does not differ significantly across academic achievement domains, and time lag does not moderate it

## Evidence

### Lecce 2026

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Moderator test over all 61 longitudinal effect sizes (21 reading, 19 math, 21 academic performance) in the early-ToM-to-later-achievement meta-analysis. The domain moderator was not significant, "F(2,58) = 2.54, p = .088"; no effect size is printed for the moderator test.

> "The test of moderator for academic achievement domain was not significant, F(2,58) = 2.54, p = .088, suggesting that the strength of the association between ToM and academic achievement was not different when comparing different academic achievement domain."

### Lecce 2026 (2)

Lecce, S., Stagnitto, S. M., Mascheretti, S., Lampis, V., & Devine, R. T. (2026). Children's Theory of Mind and Academic Achievement: a Meta-analysis on Reading Comprehension and Math Skills. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10185-w

`q4 · i2` · `quant-synthesis · r3`

Post-hoc tests in the longitudinal meta-analysis showed significant pooled correlations in each domain, each a small-to-medium printed effect size: reading comprehension r = .218, math r = .299, general academic performance r = .322, all p < .001.

> "Post-hoc tests showed that the association between ToM and academic achievement was significant for all the three academic achievement domains, rToM-ReadingComprehension = .218, 95% CI [.14, .29], p < .001 (Supplementary Material—Figure SM4); rToM-Math = .299, 95% CI [.22, .37], p < .001 (Supplementary Material—Figure S M5); rToM-AcademicPerformance = .322, 95% CI [.24, .40], p < .001 (Supplementary Material—Figure S M6)."
