# Using Eye to understand new code did not significantly improve quiz comprehension scores in a fixed time (p = 0.446)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In a two-group comprehension experiment, the Eye group scored higher on average but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.446). [→ Aman Bansal 2021](#aman-bansal-2021)

## Evidence

### Aman Bansal 2021

Aman Bansal, Preey Shah, and Sahil Shah. (2021). Eye: Program Visualizer for CS2. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1145/3408877.3439684](https://doi.org/10.1145/3408877.3439684)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Two equal-sized randomly assigned groups studied a deque-based solution new to them for 6 minutes with Eye or unrestricted code, then answered a 10-minute quiz. Table 2 reports average scores of 59.2 without Eye and 60.6 with Eye, p = 0.446.

> "We use the quiz score as a proxy for understanding. Our null hypothesis for RQ2(a) was that there would be no considerable difference in the scores of the two groups. We report the average percentage score for each group in Table 2."
