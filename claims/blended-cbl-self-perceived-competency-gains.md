# The experimental group reported significantly higher self-perceived competency in 6 of 8 CanMEDS-based domains, but not in research skills or professionalism

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Mean Likert self-ratings were significantly higher in the experimental group for clinical skills, information collection and processing, medical knowledge and lifelong learning, communication, teamwork and leadership, and health promotion (all P < .001). [→ Zhu P 2025](#zhu-p-2025)
`q3 i?` No significant differences were observed in self-ratings of research skills (P = .126) or professionalism and ethical practice (P = .854). [→ Zhu P 2025 (2)](#zhu-p-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Zhu P 2025

Zhu P, Jiang B, Lu R, Wang B, He Y, Yang K, Zhang Q, Zhao P, Huang B. (2025). A competency-based instructional design: A progressive blended case-based teaching method. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000043920

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Welch t-tests on a 5-point Likert questionnaire (Cronbach alpha 0.85) adapted from CanMEDS, administered after the intervention. Experimental means ranged from 4.2 ± 0.5 to 4.4 ± 0.5 versus 3.8 ± 0.6 to 4.1 ± 0.6 in controls; "significantly higher than the control group’s (P < .001)"; no effect sizes printed.

> "It is notable that the experimental group’s scores of students’ self-perceived competency (except research capabilities and professionalism and ethical practice) were significantly higher than the control group’s (P < .001)."

### Zhu P 2025 (2)

Zhu P, Jiang B, Lu R, Wang B, He Y, Yang K, Zhang Q, Zhao P, Huang B. (2025). A competency-based instructional design: A progressive blended case-based teaching method. Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000043920

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Same Likert questionnaire analysis: research skills (3.5 ± 0.5 vs 3.4 ± 0.5, P = .126) and professionalism and ethics (3.7 ± 0.6 vs 3.6 ± 0.5, P = .854) showed "No significant differences"; equivalence was not tested and no effect sizes were printed.

> "No significant differences were observed in students’ self-ratings for research skills (3.5 ± 0.5 vs 3.4 ± 0.5, P = .126) or professionalism and ethics (3.7 ± 0.6 vs 3.6 ± 0.5, P = .854)"
