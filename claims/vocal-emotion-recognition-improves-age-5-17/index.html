Evidence¶

Grosbras 2018¶

Grosbras, M.-H., Ross, P. D. & Belin, P. (2018). Categorical emotion recognition from voice improves during childhood and adolescence. Scientific Reports 8, 14791. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-32868-3

q3 · i? · associational · r2

Cross-sectional mixed-model logistic regression on 225 participants aged 5–17 labeling affect bursts. The article reports the age effect as highly significant (Z = 5.52, p = 3.4 × 10−8); no effect size is printed, so impact is null.

"The effect of age was highly significant (Z = 5.52, p = 3.4 × 10−8). Adding a quadratic term (age2) improved the model fit: Chi-sq(1) = 5.14, p < 0.023"

Related Claims¶

  • Adult-level vocal emotion recognition performance is reached between 14 and 15 years of age, with a non-linear trajectory of faster childhood improvement followed by a late-adolescent plateau — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Children's average vocal emotion recognition performance is significantly below the adult group's average — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Testing procedure (individual computer with headphones vs small-group loudspeaker with paper response) does not affect vocal emotion recognition performance or the developmental trajectory — related
  • Female children and adolescents recognize vocal emotions more accurately than males, with no interaction between sex and age — related
  • Confusion patterns between vocal emotions are broadly stable across development: children miscategorize anger as fear, adolescents as sadness — related
  • In CSCL written civic discussions, gaps exist between a composer's emotion labeling and others' emotion recognition — related
  • Metacognitive efficiency at Grade 1 predicts children's performance improvement 10 months later in both a linguistic and a non-linguistic task — related
