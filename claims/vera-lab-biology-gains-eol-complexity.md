## Evidence

### An 2022

An, S., Bates, R., Rugaber, S., Hammock, J., Weigel, E., & Goel, A. K. (2022). Cognitive Assistance for Inquiry-Based Modeling. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Controlled lab experiment with 15 self-selected participants (14 with paired pre/post) from a general biology introductory course. Students completed pre-test, tutorial, four-phase exercise, open question, and post-test. The article reports a significant increase in biology scores from "pre-test=71.61" to "post-test=83.33"; no effect size is printed.

> "As shown in Table 3, the data indicates a signiﬁcant increase in answering questions pertaining to biology (pre-test=71.61; post-test=83.33)."

### An 2022 (2)

An, S., Bates, R., Rugaber, S., Hammock, J., Weigel, E., & Goel, A. K. (2022). Cognitive Assistance for Inquiry-Based Modeling. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2209.02576)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Correlational analysis of the lab experiment (N=15). The article reports positive correlations between frequency of EOL use and number of hypotheses (r=0.38) and model complexity (r=0.26). The authors conjecture that EOL information about predator, prey, and competitor relationships may have led to more complex models.

> "The students who answered that they used the EOL frequently were found to come up with multiple hypotheses and build complex models (r=0.38; r=0.26)"
