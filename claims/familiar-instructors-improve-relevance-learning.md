# Familiar instructors who knew participants' clinical context were perceived to improve learning outcomes compared with unknown instructors

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants reported that instructors familiar with them and their workplace enabled personalised feedback and relevant patient cases, while unknown instructors were perceived to reduce learning outcomes. [→ Alfstad E 2025](#alfstad-e-2025)

## Evidence

### Alfstad E 2025

Alfstad E, Lockertsen V, Johannessen A-K, Werner A. (2025). The applicability of a structured learning programme focusing on improving observational competencies to strengthen patient safety: a qualitative study with professionals in primary healthcare services. BMC Health Services Research. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-025-12692-y

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r2`

Interview data from the same qualitative study; a participant contrasts a known instructor who can give direct feedback with "someone from the outside who does not know me". The article reports some participants with unknown instructors felt course relevance and learning outcomes decreased.

> "I feel secure knowing that [the instructor] is conducting this course. We know her very well, and she knows us, too, our weaknesses and strengths. She can tell me that I need to train more, unlike someone from the outside who does not know me."
