# A 10-week structured intervention including cooperative learning produced no statistically significant main effects on non-counting computation competence of grade-2 persistent counters relative to a control group at posttest

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Participants in the CL condition did not solve significantly more computation problems with non-counting strategies than control students at posttest (b = 1.45, p = .303). [→ Opitz et al. 2018](#opitz-et-al-2018)
`q3 i?` Participants in the IND condition did not solve significantly more computation problems with non-counting strategies than control students at posttest (b = -0.92, p = .665). [→ Opitz et al. 2018](#opitz-et-al-2018)

## Evidence

### Opitz et al. 2018

Moser Opitz, E., Grob, U., Wittich, C., Häsel-Weide, U., & Nührenbörger, M. (2018). Fostering the Computation Competence of Low Achievers Through Cooperative Learning in Inclusive Classrooms: A Longitudinal Study. Learning Disabilities: A Contemporary Journal, 16(1), 19-35. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1179942

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Multilevel regression analysis of 126 persistent counters in 35 classes randomized to CL, IND, or control. Model 1 posttest coefficients: CL b = 1.45, p = .303; IND b = -0.92, p = .665, showing "did not have a significant impact" relative to control.

> "Contrary to expectations, the treatment conditions CL and IND did not have a significant impact on the progress in computation relative to the control group."
