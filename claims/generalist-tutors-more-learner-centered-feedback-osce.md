# Generalist tutors use more learner-centered feedback process skills during formative OSCEs than specialist tutors

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In objective coding of audiotaped direct-observation feedback sessions, specialist tutors scored lower than generalists on almost all learner-centered feedback process items (exploring learning needs, stimulating self-assessment, involving students in problem-solving, checking understanding). [→ Junod Perron Noelle 2016](#junod-perron-noelle-2016)

## Evidence

### Junod Perron Noelle 2016

Junod Perron Noelle, Louis-Simonet Martine, Cerutti Bernard, Pfarrwaller Eva, Sommer Johanna, Nendaz Mathieu. (2016). The quality of feedback during formative OSCEs depends on the tutors' profile. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0815-x

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Objective analysis of 140 audiotaped direct-observation feedback sessions coded with a 21-item scale (interrater ICC = 0.82). Specialists (n=14 tutors) scored lower than generalists (n=21) on process items such as "The tutor explored students' learning needs" (adjusted p < 0.001) and "The tutor checked students' understanding" (adjusted p < 0.001).

> "Specialists scored lower in almost all items of the feedback process and addressed communication or professionalism issues less often."
