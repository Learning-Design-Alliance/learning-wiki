# Secondary suspension risk declined and the racial gap narrowed slightly since 2009-10, while elementary rates did not decline and some districts saw large increases

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The Black secondary student suspension risk declined 6 percentage points since 2009-10, versus -5.4 for Latinx and -2.5 for White students, while elementary rates rose by less than half a percentage point. [→ Losen 2020](#losen-2020)
`q2 i?` Despite national declines, some large districts saw large increases in secondary suspension risk, e.g., Richmond City, GA (17 percentage points) and Trenton, NJ (15). [→ Losen 2020 (2)](#losen-2020-2)

Trend analysis of unduplicated student suspension rates (risk) from CRDC collections since 2009-10, quoting the printed percentage-point declines by race at the secondary level. Descriptive; no inferential test.

### Losen 2020 (2)

Losen, D. J., & Martinez, P. (2020). Lost opportunities: How disparate school discipline continues to drive differences in the opportunity to learn. Learning Policy Institute; Center for Civil Rights Remedies at the Civil Rights Project, UCLA. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED608537

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

District trend analysis identifying the largest increases in secondary out-of-school suspension risk from 2011-12 to 2015-16, led by Richmond City, GA at 17 percentage points. The report also notes declines were not offset by increases in in-school suspensions.

> "Secondary students in the following large districts experienced the greatest overall increase in their risk for out-of-school suspension: Richmond City, GA: 17 percentage points"
