# Static self-explanation prompts in the Geometry Cognitive Tutor produced greater gains on Reason and Not Enough Info test items than problem-solving practice alone

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Students who explained their steps with the tutor showed greater gains on Reason items and Not Enough Info transfer items than students who only solved problems. [→ Koedinger 2009](#koedinger-2009)

## Evidence

### Koedinger 2009

Koedinger, K. R., Aleven, V., Roll, I., & Baker, R. (2009). In vivo experiments on whether supporting metacognition in intelligent tutoring systems yields robust learning. In *Handbook of Metacognition in Education*. Routledge. [doi:10.4324/9780203876428-29](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203876428-29)

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

In vivo classroom experiment comparing an Explanation Condition of the Geometry Cognitive Tutor against the standard Problem Solving Condition, with pre- and post-tests on three item types. The explanation group showed "greater gains with respect to Not Enough Info items and with Reason items"; no effect size is printed.

> "We found that students who had explained their steps during their work with the tutor showed greater gains with respect to Not Enough Info items and with Reason items than the students who solved problems without explaining their steps"
