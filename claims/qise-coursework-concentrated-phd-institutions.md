## Evidence

### A.R. Piña 2025

A.R. Piña, Shams El-Adawy, Mike Verostek, H. J. Lewandowski, Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Investigating Opportunities for Growth and Increased Diversity in Quantum Information Science and Engineering Education in the U.S. based on an Analysis of the Current Educational Landscape. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.00104](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.00104)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Table 1 reports courses per institution by Carnegie classification among ABET-accredited institutions: PhD-granting institutions average "almost three quantum-related engineering courses" (2.74) and 0.56 QISE courses, versus 0.17 and 0 at two-year and 0.12 and 0.01 at four-year institutions.

> "On average, PhD-granting institutions offer almost three quantum-related engineering courses, and about half of PhD-granting institutions have an engineering course covering QISE topics."

### A.R. Piña 2025 (2)

A.R. Piña, Shams El-Adawy, Mike Verostek, H. J. Lewandowski, Benjamin M. Zwickl. (2025). Investigating Opportunities for Growth and Increased Diversity in Quantum Information Science and Engineering Education in the U.S. based on an Analysis of the Current Educational Landscape. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2505.00104](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.00104)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Because two-year and four-year institutions typically have 0 or 1 quantum-related courses, the authors report that "roughly 17% of two-year colleges and 12% of four-year colleges contain a quantum-related engineering course" with QISE courses nearly completely absent.

> "roughly 17% of two-year colleges and 12% of four-year colleges contain a quantum-related engineering course, and QISE-related courses are nearly completely absent within engineering"
