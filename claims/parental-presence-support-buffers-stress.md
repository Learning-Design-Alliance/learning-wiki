# Parental presence and support buffer children's neurobiological stress responses, mitigating adversity effects when support is high

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · review `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In animal models, parental presence inhibits neurobiological threat response systems, with rodent pups and infant primates showing reduced glucocorticoid release and decreased amygdala activation in the mother's presence, though buffering fails under abusive maternal rearing. [→ Karen E. Smith and Seth D. Pollak 2020](#karen-e-smith-and-seth-d-pollak-2020)
`q2 i?` In humans, parental presence dampens cortisol and amygdala reactivity to stress, parent voice speeds cortisol recovery, and adolescents in poverty show altered prefrontal connectivity only when parent support is low, with family and friend social support reducing psychopathology risk in maltreated children. [→ Karen E. Smith and Seth D. Pollak 2020 (2)](#karen-e-smith-and-seth-d-pollak-2020-2)
