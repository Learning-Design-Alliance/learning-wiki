# No statistically significant difference in effective teaching perceptions between Facebook and Adobe Connect groups

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` There is no statistically significant difference between the academic success posttest scores of pre-service teacher groups on the whole sub-dimensions of the Effective Teaching Scale. [→ Arabacioglu 2014](#arabacioglu-2014)

## Evidence

### Arabacioglu 2014

Arabacioglu, T. & Akar-Vural, R. (2014). Using Facebook as a LMS?. TOJET: The Turkish Online Journal of Educational Technology, 13(2). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1022959

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quasi-experimental static group comparison with 42 pre-service teachers (21 per group) taught via Facebook or Adobe Connect for 15 weeks. MANOVA on Effective Teaching Scale factor scores showed the group effect was not significant (λ=.91, F(6,35)=.55, p>.05, η2=.08).

> "The results of MANOVA, conducted on the factor scores of effective teaching scale of pre-service teachers, show that the effect of group variable [ λ=.91, F(6,35)=.55, p>.05, η2=.08] on the scale in general is not significant."
