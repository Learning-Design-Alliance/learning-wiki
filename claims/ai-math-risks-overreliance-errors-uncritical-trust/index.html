Omirzakova, F. N., & Tileubay, S. (2026). Artificial intelligence in mathematics education: A PRISMA-based systematic literature review (2021-2025). European Journal of STEM Education, 11(1), Article 43. https://doi.org/10.20897/ejsteme/19221

q2 · i? · quant-synthesis · r2

Review synthesis on uncritical trust in AI outputs. The authors conclude that "uncritical trust in AI -generated information is unjustified" and that active oversight by educators remains essential.

"Evidence shows that learners often pay little attention to verifying the accuracy of AI -provided solutions [S15], allowing misconceptions to take root in the learning process."

  • The review found AI was effective for improving basic problem-solving but required teacher mediation to handle more complex mathematical tasks — related
  • A systematic review of 14 studies (8,305 participants) found AI-based tools enhanced comprehension, engagement, problem-solving, and critical thinking by personalizing learning experiences — related
  • Overreliance on AI is the most prevalent pedagogical limitation (27 studies, 50%), followed by reduced critical thinking (25 studies, 46%) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • Unreliable AI-generated output is the most frequently reported technical limitation of AI-supported undergraduate EFL instruction, appearing in 22 of 54 studies (41%) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
