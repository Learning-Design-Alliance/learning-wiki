Survey averages for three HE questions displayed by gender (Figure 3). The scores 'agree within error', but the article observes a trend with 'female respondents answering more positively, on average, to all questions'.

### Robson 2020 (2)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Survey responses on space-sector employment by gender (Figure 5). The article reports that 'of our alumni in the space sector, women make up 46%', contrasting this with the UK STEM sector figure it cites.

> "These figures show that, of our alumni in the space sector, women make up 46%. This contrasts, for example, with the UK STEM sector which is 22%  women"

### Robson 2020 (3)

Robson, D., Lau, H., O'Brien, Á., Williams, L., Sutlieff, B., Thiemann, H., McCaul, L., Weaver, G., Dickens, T. (2020). Assessing the Impact of Space School UK. 3rd Symposium on Space Educational Activities, University of Leicester. Originally published 2019. [doi:10.29311/2020.35](https://doi.org/10.29311/2020.35)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Self-reported skill gains by gender from the alumni survey. The article reports that 'female respondents reported that S SUK increased their skills in teamwork and problem solving more than male respondents', while men cited other skill categories more often.

> "Furthermore, female respondents reported that S SUK increased their skills in teamwork and problem solving  more than male respondents, whilst male respondents cited increased skills in communication,  organization, practical and technical skills over female respondents."
