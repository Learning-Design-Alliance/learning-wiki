## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2023). Helping students become proficient problem solvers Part II: An example from waves. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Quantitative analysis of quiz and midterm work among students who wrote down v = λf (Table 4). The article notes plugging in a numerical value for v is not necessarily unproductive because it can reduce cognitive load.

> "less than half of the students (48% in the quiz and 36% in the midterm exam) were able to eliminate the undesired quantities from the two equations and solve for the target variable without resorting to pluggin g in numerical information about wave speed that was not explicitly given"

### Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries 2023 (2)

Chandralekha Singh and Alexandru Maries. (2023). Helping students become proficient problem solvers Part II: An example from waves. Originally published 2016. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2304.06188)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Students were given an isomorphic purely mathematical system of two equations with a constant C. The article cautions these percentages may not be directly comparable to the physics-context percentages because students there could plug in a value for v.

> "64% of students at the beginning of the first semester algebra -based course are able to solve this system correctly and 89% of students at the beginning of their second semester course are able to solve this system correctly"
