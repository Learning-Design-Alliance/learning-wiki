# Upper-division E&M students who activate sub-atomic resources about internal atomic structure when explaining polarization are very likely to reach dielectric breakdown

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Across all three testing situations, students using sub-atomic resources almost always activated breakdown, while super-atomic responses on unseen tests were not likely to reach breakdown. [→ Savannah L. Mitchem 2016](#savannah-l-mitchem-2016)

## Evidence

### Savannah L. Mitchem 2016

Savannah L. Mitchem, Dina Zohrabi Alaee, and Eleanor C. Sayre. (2016). Student understanding of electric and magnetic fields in materials. [doi:10.1119/1.4991376](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4991376)

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

Analysis of 50 usable written exam responses from two cohorts (2014 and 2015) in an upper-division E&M course, coded with an emergent scheme (IRR 82% before, 100% after discussion). Table I shows sub-atomic responses mostly reaching breakdown (e.g., 7 of 8 sub-atomic at Mid-Semester 2015) versus super-atomic responses mostly reaching saturation. The authors state students considering "the internal structure of the atom during polarization" are "very likely to arrive at the correct answer, dielectric breakdown."

> "if a student considers the internal structure of the atom during polarization, they are very likely to arrive at the correct answer, dielectric breakdown. This is true for all three testing situations we evaluated."
