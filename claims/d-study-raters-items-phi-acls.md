## Evidence

### Lertsakulbunlue 2025

Lertsakulbunlue, S., Thammasoon, K., & Kantiwong, A. (2025). Reliability and validity of simulation-based Electrocardiogram assessment rubrics for cardiac life support skills among medical students using generalizability theory. Medical Education Online, 30(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2479962

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

D-study projecting Phi-coefficients for the crossed p×i×r design across rater-by-item combinations. Phi ranged "from 0.47 to 0.67 for one rater" up to "0.66 to 0.82 for three raters", supporting the conclusion that "at least three raters are required".

> "The table displays the phi-coefficient, ranging from 0.47 to 0.67 for one rater, 0.60 to 0.78 for two raters, and 0.66 to 0.82 for three raters, indicating that at least three raters are required to achieve a reliable assessment."

### Lertsakulbunlue 2025 (2)

Lertsakulbunlue, S., Thammasoon, K., & Kantiwong, A. (2025). Reliability and validity of simulation-based Electrocardiogram assessment rubrics for cardiac life support skills among medical students using generalizability theory. Medical Education Online, 30(1). https://doi.org/10.1080/10872981.2025.2479962

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

D-study for the two-facet nested r:(p×i) design, which matches the course's rater assignment. It showed "a combination of 10 items and 2 raters is sufficient", with Phi = 0.83, and that three raters needed "only 8 items" (Phi = 0.83).

> "The results illustrate that a combination of 10 items and 2 raters is sufficient to achieve a reliable evaluation, with a Phi-coefficient of 0.83. While for three raters, only 8 items are needed (Phi-coefficient = 0.83)."
