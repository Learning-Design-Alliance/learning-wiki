## Evidence

### Arezoo Shirazi and Amir H. Behzadan 2015

Arezoo Shirazi and Amir H. Behzadan. (2015). Content Delivery Using Augmented Reality to Enhance Students' Performance in a Building Design and Assembly Project. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076141

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Statistical comparison of NASA TLX subscales between the two treatments using a p-value test against the null hypothesis that each subscale was statistically equal: the test group "felt more frustrated (p-value = 0.044)" and "believed that they performed relatively better (p-value = 0.081)". No effect sizes are printed.

> "Results of the p-value analysis revealed that although students in the test group felt more frustrated (p-value = 0.044), they believed that they performed relatively better (p-value = 0.081)."

### Arezoo Shirazi and Amir H. Behzadan 2015 (2)

Arezoo Shirazi and Amir H. Behzadan. (2015). Content Delivery Using Augmented Reality to Enhance Students' Performance in a Building Design and Assembly Project. Advances in Engineering Education. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1076141

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

NASA TLX final score results (Figure 9): the average workload score achieved by both groups was "almost the same". The authors interpret the test group's workload as divided between the main task and learning the application, implying less workload on the assembly task itself.

> "according to the NASA TLX final assessment results, as shown in Figure 9, the average workload score achieved by both groups are almost the same."
