# Intraindividual and interindividual correlations between achievement goals and emotions are statistically independent yet empirically convergent, with goals and emotions less correlated within persons

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Level 1 (within-person) and Level 2 (between-person) correlations are statistically independent, and some differ substantially, e.g., performance-approach and performance-avoidance goals were strongly correlated between persons but only moderately within persons. [→ Goetz 2016](#goetz-2016)

## Evidence

### Goetz 2016

Goetz, T., Sticca, F., Pekrun, R., Murayama, K., & Elliot, A. J. (2016). Intraindividual relations between achievement goals and discrete achievement emotions: An experience sampling approach. Learning and Instruction, 41, 115-125. https://dx.doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2015.10.007

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Correlational analysis of the two-level state data (1409 assessments, 120 students) comparing Level 1 and Level 2 correlations (Tables 3 and 4). The article reports the printed r values and notes anxiety-shame correlations also differed (r ¼ .51 within vs. rs ¼ .80 and .94 between).

> "performance-approach and performance-avoidance goals were clearly separated at the intraindividual level (r ¼ .48), in contrast to their strong correlation at the interindividual level ( rs ¼ .86 and .90 for trait goals and aggregated state goals, respectively)."
