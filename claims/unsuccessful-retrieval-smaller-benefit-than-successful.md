# Unsuccessful retrieval attempts confer a smaller benefit to future performance than successful retrievals, whose advantage grows with item difficulty

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` The model showed a benefit from prior incorrect trials (estimate 1.47) smaller than prior correct trials (estimate 1.82), but interactions with format and depth made successful practice much more impactful, e.g., more than twice as impactful in the Applied SA condition with one correct and one incorrect prior trial. [→ Maass 2016](#maass-2016)

## Evidence

### Maass 2016

Maass, J.K. and Pavlik Jr., P.I. (2016). Modeling the Influence of Format and Depth during Effortful Retrieval Practice. Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Educational Data Mining. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED592715

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

PFA-based logistic regression of practice data (5,696 observations, 178 participants): Count of Prior Correct estimate 1.82 vs Count of Prior Incorrect 1.47. In the Applied SA condition with one prior correct and one prior incorrect trial, "successful practices is more than twice as impactful on future performance as previous unsuccessful practices".

> "However, the current model also indicates a benefit from unsuccessful retrieval, although to a smaller degree."
