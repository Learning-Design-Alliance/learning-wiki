# Collaborative annotation of whole slide images significantly improves quiz performance over individual annotation for senior medical students, with a large effect size

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Senior medicine students scored significantly higher on quizzes after collaborative annotation than after individual annotation (Cohen's d = 1.37). [→ Sahota Michael 2016](#sahota-michael-2016)

## Evidence

### Sahota Michael 2016

Sahota Michael, Leung Betty, Dowdell Stephanie, Velan Gary M. (2016). Learning pathology using collaborative vs. individual annotation of whole slide images: a mixed methods trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-016-0831-x

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r1`

Cross-over trial with senior Year 6 Medicine students (n = 12) in a Pathology selective course; paired t-test comparing quiz items tied to each annotation condition. Collaborative mean was 77.78% versus individual 57.58%, with "a large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.37)".

> "Amongst the senior (Phase 3 Medicine) cohort, there was a statistically significant difference in favour of collaborative annotation over individual annotation, with a large effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.37)"
