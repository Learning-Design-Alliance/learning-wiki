# Self-directed learning by video did not produce a statistically significant difference in total OSATS score between surgery resident groups

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r2` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2021 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Residents given an expert video for self-directed study did not significantly outperform controls on total OSATS score at the second filmed anastomosis (p = 0.28). [→ Chartrand G 2021](#chartrand-g-2021)

## Evidence

### Chartrand G 2021

Chartrand G, Soucisse M, Dubé P, Trépanier J-S, Drolet P, Sideris L. (2021). Self-directed learning by video as a means to improve technical skills in surgery residents: a randomized controlled trial. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-021-02524-y

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Single-blinded randomized controlled trial; 22 general surgery residents (11 per group) filmed performing a pig-bowel anastomosis, re-filmed about 3 weeks later. Mean total OSATS at the second procedure was "control: 27.1 (3.9) vs. SDL-V: 29.6 (3.4), p = 0.28" — no significant difference; equivalence was not tested.

> "There was no difference between groups regarding the total OSATS score during the initial (control: 23.6 (4.5) vs. SDL-V: 23.9 (4.5) p = 0.9) or the second filmed anastomosis procedure (control: 27.1 (3.9) vs. SDL-V: 29.6 (3.4), p = 0.28)"
