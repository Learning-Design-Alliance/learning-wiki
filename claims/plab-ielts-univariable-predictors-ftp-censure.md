## Evidence

### Tiffin Paul A. 2017

Tiffin Paul A., Paton Lewis W., Mwandigha Lazaro M., McLachlan John C., Illing Jan. (2017). Predicting fitness to practise events in international medical graduates who registered as UK doctors via the Professional and Linguistic Assessments Board (PLAB) system: a national cohort study. BMC Medicine. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-017-0829-1

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r3`

Observational survival analysis linking GMC fitness to practise, PLAB and IELTS data for 27,330 international medical graduates, of whom 210 were censured. The abstract-reported univariable hazard ratios are printed with 95% confidence intervals for each predictor, e.g. "HR, 0.94; 0.91 to 0.97" for PLAB part 2.

> "The significant univariable educational predictors of eventual censure (versus no censures or referrals) were lower PLAB part 1 (hazard ratio [HR], 0.99; 95% confidence interval, 0.98 to 1.00) and part 2 scores (HR, 0.94; 0.91 to 0.97) at first sitting, multiple attempts at both parts of the PLAB, lower IELTS reading (HR, 0.79; 0.65 to 0.94) and listening scores (HR, 0.76; 0.62 to 0.93) and higher IELTS speaking scores (HR, 1.28; 1.04 to 1.57)."
