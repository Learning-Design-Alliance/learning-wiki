# Teachers reported increased use of culturally and linguistically relevant instruction over the year, while teacher collaboration remained unchanged

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2`–`r3` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Average self-reported CLRI use rose from 3.14 to 3.44 over the year, while collaboration stayed at 3.14 (N = 20). [→ Patrick Rich 2025](#patrick-rich-2025)
`q2 i?` Teachers with a local coach showed higher growth in CLRI (0.20 vs. -0.19), but these differences were not statistically significant at the 5 percent level. [→ Patrick Rich 2025 (2)](#patrick-rich-2025-2)

## Evidence

### Patrick Rich 2025

Patrick Rich, Audrey Altieri, Rachel Garrett. (2025). A pilot study of Write to Succeed: An intervention to enhance writing instruction in New Mexico. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest (REL 2025-013). https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/pilot-study-write-succeed-intervention-enhancing-writing-instruction-new-mexico

`q2 · i?` · `design · r3`

Teacher surveys at three time points (N = 20) show CLRI averages of 3.14, 3.15, and 3.44 and collaboration averages of 3.28, 3.30, and 3.14 on a 1-4 agreement scale; analysis used calculated averages.

> "Teachers increased their use of CLRI practices over the course of the year. Teacher collaboration overall remained unchanged."

### Patrick Rich 2025 (2)

Patrick Rich, Audrey Altieri, Rachel Garrett. (2025). A pilot study of Write to Succeed: An intervention to enhance writing instruction in New Mexico. Regional Educational Laboratory Southwest (REL 2025-013). https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/pilot-study-write-succeed-intervention-enhancing-writing-instruction-new-mexico

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Survey-based subgroup comparison (N = 14 local coach, N = 6 no local coach) shows average CLRI gains of 0.20 versus -0.19 and collaboration gains of 0.08 versus -0.08; the report states the differences are not statistically significant.

> "Teachers with a local coach had higher growth particularly for CLRI. For collaboration, teachers who had a local coach had slightly higher growth in scores, whereas those who did not have a local coach had a decrease in collaboration scores over the course of the year. These differences are not statistically significant at the 5 percent level."
