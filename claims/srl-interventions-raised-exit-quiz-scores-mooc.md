# Both SRL intervention modes produced higher course exit quiz scores than control in a programming MOOC

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` One-way ANOVA showed exit quiz scores differed across conditions; both intervention groups scored about 3.7 points higher than the control group. [→ Psathas 2023](#psathas-2023)

## Evidence

### Psathas 2023

Psathas, G., Tegos, S., Demetriadis, S. N., & Tsiatsos, T. (2023). Exploring the impact of chat-based collaborative activities and SRL-focused interventions on students’ self-regulation profiles, participation in collaborative activities, retention, and learning in MOOCs. Intern. J. Comput.-Support. Collab. Learn 18:329–351. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11412-023-09394-0

`q3 · i2` · `causal · r2`

One-way ANOVA on the 20-item course exit quiz across control (M = 8.46, SD = 7.666), general (M = 11.46, SD = 6.864), and personalized (M = 11.71, SD = 6.129) groups. Partial η² = 0.071; Tukey post hoc confirmed both intervention groups (mean increases 3.74 and 3.72) exceeded control (p < 0.01).

> "The score in the course exit quiz was statistically significantly different between the groups that received different interventions., F (2, 357) = 13.650, p < 0.01, partial η 2 = 0.071."
