## Subclaims
`q3 i0` First-year nursing students taught vital signs via a 3D animation-based game simulation scored the same on academic achievement as students taught by traditional demonstration (means 17.86 vs 17.80, p = 0.932, d = 0.02). [→ Tanrıkulu F 2025](#tanrkulu-f-2025)

## Evidence

### Tanrıkulu F 2025

Tanrıkulu F, Gündoğdu H, Erol F, Dikmen Y. (2025). A comparison of 3D game-based simulation versus traditional methods in vital signs education. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07980-4

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

Randomized controlled experiment with 73 first-year nursing students (intervention n = 37, control n = 36) using the 26-item Academic Achievement Test of Vital Signs Measurement Skills; Table 2 reports d = 0.02, t = 0.085, p = 0.932.

> "the mean academic achievement test scores of the intervention and control groups were found to be 17.86 and 17.80, respectively. As a result of the independent sample t-test, there was no statistically significant difference between these two groups (p > 0.05)."
