## Subclaims
`q1 i?` Current AI systems cannot reliably exercise the situated professional judgment required for valid evaluative feedback, and the normative commitment to human professional judgment should hold regardless of technical progress. [→ Venetsanos 2026](#venetsanos-2026)

## Evidence

### Venetsanos 2026

Venetsanos, D. T. (2026). A Tripartite Feedback Framework for AI-Assisted Assessment of Complex Reports in Higher Education. AI Educ. 2026, 2, 26. https://doi.org/10.3390/aieduc2030026

`q1 · i?` · `theoretical · r2`

Theoretical argument in Section 4.2 of this conceptual paper, citing empirical studies that find AI feedback limited in depth, disciplinary specificity, and pedagogical appropriateness, tending toward generic rather than targeted responses. The article adds that the normative commitment "should remain constant regardless of technical progress".

> "Given current LLM architectures and near-term deployment constraints in high-stakes assessment, high-level feedback should remain a human responsibility for the foreseeable future."
