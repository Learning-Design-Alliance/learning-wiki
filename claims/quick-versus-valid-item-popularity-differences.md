## Evidence

### Ene 2019

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Normal-approximation comparison of item popularities between quick and valid responses in quartile 1 on WebAssign data; five randomized-option questions (Q10, Q12, Q13, Q18, Q27) and seven fixed-option questions differed significantly.

> "We calculated with 95% confidence that twelve BEMA questions on WebAssign were answered with different probabilities of success by the two time-categories of quartile 1."

### Ene 2019 (2)

Ene, E., & Webb, R. C. (2019). A tale of two test setups: The effect of randomizing a popular conceptual survey in electricity and magnetism. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1908.09221)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Null comparison for four hard questions with randomized options; the authors suggest that when the key lands at an early position, quick guessing and slow naive thought yield nearly equal chances of a correct answer.

> "For four BEMA-questions (Q7, Q8, Q9 and Q11) with the options presented in randomized order on WebAssign, the quick and the valid popularities had comparable values at the 95% confidence level."
