Evidence¶

Lemke 2023¶

Lemke, M., Murphy, D., Soo Ping Chow, A., Spencer, H., & Zhang, A. (2023). Comparing early literacy screening assessment benchmarks in Massachusetts. WestEd. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED638508

q2 · i? · associational · r2

Descriptive review (Table 1) of risk-level documentation for the state-approved screeners (DIBELS 8th Edition, mCLASS, FastBridge aReading, i-Ready, Lexia RAPID, MAP Growth, Star Reading), showing levels from two to five per assessment, set by percentile bands or grade-level labels; the authors note 'These levels differ across assessments in how they were determined and in what they represent.'

"Most of the approved screening assessments, however, do not simply identify risk using a single cut score, instead providing several benchmarks or risk levels (e.g., low risk, some risk, high risk; see Table 1)."

Related Claims¶

  • Screening assessments vary in design, administration, and risk definitions such that there is no truly common definition of risk across assessments — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Equipercentile linking shows grade 3 screening benchmark cut scores identifying at-risk students cluster in the MCAS Partially Meeting Expectations performance level, with variation among assessments — related
  • Screening assessment benchmarks indicating significant risk shift up and down on the MCAS scale within and across grades, so students with the same skills may be classified differently at different times — related
  • Grade 3 screener benchmark scores marking any level of risk map to MCAS ELA scores from mid Partially Meeting Expectations to the beginning of Meeting Expectations — related
  • In the scanned textbooks, statistics terms such as cluster and outlier are defined vaguely before any examples, and more formal criteria are never developed — related
  • The evidence base does not yet provide a menu of program options for helping students at risk of dropping out, and program developers need a way to identify approaches while stronger scientific evidence is developed — related
