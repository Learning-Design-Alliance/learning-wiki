Evidence¶

Breit et al. 2025¶

Breit, M., Schneider, M., & Preckel, F. (2025). Mathematics achievement and learner characteristics: A systematic review of meta-analyses. Learning and Individual Differences, 118, 102621. doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2024.102621

q3 · systematic review of meta-analyses · i3 · large effect, r = .43 vs .12 · n=30 meta-analyses, 66 correlations from 13,853 effect sizes (~4.66 million participants) · review · r3

The review compiles zero-order correlations from published meta-analyses (each with at least ten effect sizes, the largest one kept where several covered a variable), found by a systematic PsycINFO search in July 2024. It pools nothing further and controls for nothing: many of these characteristics correlate with one another, so a correlation here says how strongly the characteristic goes with mathematics achievement, not that it causes it. Mathematics self-concept r = .43 and verbal self-concept .12, both from Möller et al. (2009), school and university samples. Publication bias was not examined for these estimates. Mathematics self-concept had the largest correlation of all 18 personality variables.

Discussion¶

The authors read the pattern through the internal/external frame of reference model: students compare their mathematics achievement with other students' and with their own verbal achievement, so verbal self-concept stays nearly unrelated to mathematics achievement even though verbal and mathematics achievement correlate strongly. They describe a reciprocal relationship between self-concept and achievement, and cite an earlier meta-analysis (O'Mara et al. 2006) in which domain-specific self-concept interventions were effective; that evidence is not part of this review.
