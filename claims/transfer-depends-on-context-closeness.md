## Evidence

### Chandralekha Singh 2016

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part II: Effect of Some Potential Factors on Problem Solving and Transfer. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 projectile-motion pair judged to require the farthest transfer: question (7) asks time to maximum height, question (8) compares flight times of three projectiles. Interviews found students unsure whether the horizontal component of motion mattered.

> "Consistent with hypothesis H3, student performance on question (8) did not improve signiﬁcantly when it was given together with question (7)."

### Chandralekha Singh 2016 (2)

Chandralekha Singh. (2016). Assessing Student Expertise in Introductory Physics with Isomorphic Problems, Part II: Effect of Some Potential Factors on Problem Solving and Transfer. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1602.07678)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment 1 Batman/boat versus rain/cart momentum pair: Table 1 shows significant improvement on question (10) when paired with question (9), and many students transferred the relevant knowledge between the somewhat different contexts.

> "students’ performance on question (10) improved signiﬁcantly when it was given with question (9) rather than alone despite the fact that the contexts were somewhat different; in particular, in one case a person is falling vertically and in the other case rain is falling vertically."
