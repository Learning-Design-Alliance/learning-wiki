## Evidence

### Deutschmann Michael W 2013

Deutschmann Michael W, Yunker Warren K, Cho John J, Andreassen Meri, Beveridge Shari, Bosch John Douglas. (2013). Use of a Low-fidelity simulator to improve trans-nasal fibre-optic flexible laryngoscopy in the clinical setting: a randomized, single-blinded, prospective study. Journal of Otolaryngology - Head & Neck Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/1916-0216-42-35

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Subjective 10-point scale ratings collected during both endoscopies in the randomized trial, from learners and the blinded senior author. The article reports statistically significant improvements with no effect size printed.

> "Qualitatively, learners felt like they were more comfortable during the second endoscopy compared to the first (4.2 ± 1.9 vs. 5.7 ± 2.0, p < 0.01; Table 3). Similarly, the senior author felt that the learners were better, and more comfortable, at manipulating the scope during the second TFFL (3.4 ± 2.5 vs. 5.6 ± 2.4, p < 0.01; 3.3 ± 2.4 vs. 5.6 ± 2.4, p < 0.01; Table 3)."

### Deutschmann Michael W 2013 (2)

Deutschmann Michael W, Yunker Warren K, Cho John J, Andreassen Meri, Beveridge Shari, Bosch John Douglas. (2013). Use of a Low-fidelity simulator to improve trans-nasal fibre-optic flexible laryngoscopy in the clinical setting: a randomized, single-blinded, prospective study. Journal of Otolaryngology - Head & Neck Surgery. https://doi.org/10.1186/1916-0216-42-35

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Patient-rated and investigator-rated patient comfort measures in the same trial showed no significant change between the first and second endoscopy; no effect size is printed and equivalence was not tested.

> "However, patient comfort, graded by the senior author, did not vary between the first and second endoscopy (6.4 ± 2.4 vs. 7.1 ± 2.1, p = 0.09; Table 3). Similarly, from the patients’ perspective, there was no difference in comfort or willingness to repeat the examination (3.4 vs. 3.0, p = 0.38; 1.7 vs. 1.5, p = 0.79; Table 3)."
