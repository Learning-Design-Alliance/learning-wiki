# Ethical acceptability of GenAI use varied with disciplinary norms: assistance was more acceptable for supporting or procedural tasks than for core intellectual tasks

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Participants' ethical reasoning was shaped by how a task was positioned within disciplinary practice: GenAI assistance was more acceptable for supporting or procedural tasks and more problematic when it displaced activities central to scholarly formation. ## Evidence

### Wei Dai and Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan 2026

Wei Dai and Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan. (2026). Shaping responsible GenAI use in research through AI literacy-oriented guidelines: insights from postgraduate students. International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-026-00609-6

`q2 · i?` · `qualitative · r3`

RQ2 theme on protecting learning and critical thinking: the analysis reports that what counted as a core intellectual task varied with disciplinary norms; one participant treated AI-assisted writing as unacceptable while another accepted AI-assisted coding as a supporting skill in their discipline.

> "GenAI assistance was more acceptable when applied to supporting or procedural tasks, but more problematic when it displaced activities understood as central to scholarly formation or intellectual contribution."
