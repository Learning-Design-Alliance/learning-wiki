# Longer time on task in the RL condition occurred across all pretest groups, so time on task is unlikely to be the primary reason for the low performers' benefit

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Subjects spent longer on the RL narrative task than control, consistently across all three pretest groups, yet only the low pretest group showed benefit. [→ Sherry Ruan 2024](#sherry-ruan-2024)

## Evidence

### Sherry Ruan 2024

Sherry Ruan, Allen Nie, William Steenbergen, Jiayu He, JQ Zhang, Meng Guo, Yao Liu, Kyle Dang Nguyen, Catherine Y Wang, Rui Ying, James A Landay, Emma Brunskill. (2024). Reinforcement Learning Tutor Better Supported Lower Performers in a Math Task. Originally published 2023. [doi:10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-023-06423-9)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Observational time-on-task analysis within the experiments (Figure 5; students exceeding 90 minutes excluded, 8 individuals). The article reports the time difference was largely similar across all three pretest groups, so it deems time on task unlikely to be the primary reason for the low-pretest benefit; this is the authors' reasoning, not a tested mediation.

> "Indeed, on average, subjects spend longer on the RL narrative condition task than in the control condition."
