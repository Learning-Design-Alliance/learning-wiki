## Subclaims
`q3 i?` For visually salient abnormalities, experts and semi-experts performed better than naïve participants, with no difference between experts and semi-experts. [→ Bertram Raymond 2013](#bertram-raymond-2013)

## Evidence

### Bertram Raymond 2013

Bertram Raymond, Helle Laura, Kaakinen Johanna K., Svedström Erkki. (2013). The Effect of Expertise on Eye Movement Behaviour in Medical Image Perception. PLoS ONE. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066169

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Same experiment, ABN task: the group effect on overall accuracy was significant, with experts (74%) and semi-experts (78%) outperforming naïve participants (50%); the difference was driven by naïve participants failing to observe abnormalities rather than false alarms (F<1).

> "With respect to ABN detection, a significant group effect emerged for overall accuracy F(2, 35) = 22.54, p<.01. In line with Hypothesis 2, experts (E) and semi-experts (SE) performed better than naïve participants (N) did (both ps<.001)."
