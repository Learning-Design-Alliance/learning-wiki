## Evidence

### Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting 2021

Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting. 2021. Together we learn better: leveraging communities of practice for MOOC learners. In Asian CHI Symposium 2021, May 8–13, 2021, Yokohama, Japan. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3429360.3468176

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Field study of 56 participants recruited from Facebook MOOC communities across four course spaces over 6 weeks; usage was analyzed from platform logs of posts, likes, meetings and artifacts. No new course spaces were created by participants during the study.

> "independent rapid communities comprising 286 threads in total and consuming 2.4 hours per member on average over the 6 week study. 60% joined the platform via links posted in the social media such as Facebook"

### Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting 2021 (2)

Dilrukshi Gamage and Mark E Whiting. 2021. Together we learn better: leveraging communities of practice for MOOC learners. In Asian CHI Symposium 2021, May 8–13, 2021, Yokohama, Japan. ACM, New York, NY, USA. https://doi.org/10.1145/3429360.3468176

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Log analysis of field-study communities: thread counts per community show highly uneven engagement across the five rapid communities, with the creative, feedback-oriented course communities most active and programming communities least active.

> "The Creative Problem solving MOOC community had the most threads, representing 44% of all discussion during the study. Community 1 in the Design thinking had 27%, and Community 2 there had 15%. Introduction to java had 12%, and C++ only received 2%."
