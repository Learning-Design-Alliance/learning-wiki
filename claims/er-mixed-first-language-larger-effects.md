# Extensive reading effects are larger when sample members have different first languages

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` First language was a significant moderator: interventions in samples with mixed language backgrounds showed larger effects (d = 1.03) than same-language samples (d = 0.33). [→ Sangers 2025](#sangers-2025)

## Evidence

### Sangers 2025

Sangers, N. L., van der Sande, L., Welie, C., Dobber, M., & van Steensel, R. (2025). Learning a Language Through Reading: A Meta-analysis of Studies on the Effects of Extensive Reading on Second and Foreign Language Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10068-6

`q3 · i3` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Sample-characteristics moderator analysis over all effect measures combined (Table 2). The article reports "interventions in which students had mixed language backgrounds showed larger effects"; Table 2 shows d = 1.03 for different-language samples versus d = 0.33 for same-language samples (Q = 6.19*).

> "First language was a significant moderator of intervention effects: interventions in which students had mixed language backgrounds showed larger effects."
