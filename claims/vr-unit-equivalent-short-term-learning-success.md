# VR and conventional teaching produce equivalent short-term learning success in a quasi-randomized field experiment

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The experimental (VR) and control (regular) groups showed no statistically significant difference in learning success between Test 1 and Test 2 (difference 0.08 points, statistical significance 58.24%). [→ Thomas Keller 2019](#thomas-keller-2019)

## Evidence

### Thomas Keller 2019

Thomas Keller, Fabian Hagen and Elke Brucker-Kley. (2019). A FIELD STUDY ABOUT THE IMPACT OF A VR LEARNING UNIT. 16th International Conference on Cognition and Exploratory Learning in Digital Age (CELDA 2019). https://doi.org/10.33965/celda2019_201911L038

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Field experiment with four secondary-school classes (quasi-randomized by class distribution), 67 subjects in the final analysis. The experimental group gained 1.38 points (19.14%) and the control group 1.30 points (18.49%) between Test 1 and Test 2; "the two teaching methods do not differ."

> "If the average learning outcomes of the subjects in the experimental and control groups are compared, the difference between Test 1 and Test 2 is 0.08 points. The statistical significance is 58.24%, which means that the two teaching methods do not differ."
