## Evidence

### Sprague S. 2013

Sprague S., Kaloty R., Madden K., Dosanjh S., Mathews D.J., Bhandari M. (2013). Perceptions of Intimate Partner Violence: a cross sectional survey of surgical residents and medical students. Journal of Injury and Violence Research. https://doi.org/10.5249/jivr.v5i1.147

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Descriptive results from the survey (Table 2) report the four most-endorsed barriers to IPV assessment among the 200 trainees, led by "lack of time (82.9%)".

> "Key barriers to IPV assessment perceived by respondents included lack of time (82.9%), lack of knowledge of what to ask (66.8%), lack of knowledge of community resources (69.9%), and personal discomfort (52.8%)"

### Sprague S. 2013 (2)

Sprague S., Kaloty R., Madden K., Dosanjh S., Mathews D.J., Bhandari M. (2013). Perceptions of Intimate Partner Violence: a cross sectional survey of surgical residents and medical students. Journal of Injury and Violence Research. https://doi.org/10.5249/jivr.v5i1.147

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Chi-squared comparisons between medical students and surgical residents (Table 2) show students significantly more likely to report each barrier, e.g. "personal discomfort (62.1% vs. 36.2%, p=0.003)".

> "Medical students were significantly more likely to have issues with lack of time (89.5% vs. 71.0%, p=0.004), lack of knowledge of what to say (73.4% vs. 55.1%, p=0.010), lack of knowledge of what to do if a patient is abused (72.5% vs. 44.9%, p<0.001), and personal discomfort (62.1% vs. 36.2%, p=0.003) when compared to surgical residents"
