# Mastery learning improves students' attitudes toward the instruction and toward the subject

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)

Effects of mastery learning programs on student attitudes.

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Kulik et al. 1990

Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs: A Meta-Analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299. [doi:10.3102/00346543060002265](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)

`q4 · meta-analysis (18 and 14 studies of attitudes)` · `i2 · medium effect, ES = 0.63 toward instruction, 0.40 toward subject` · `n=18 studies` · `quant-synthesis · r?`

Eighteen evaluations measured students' ratings of the instructional method: 16 favoured the mastery class, with a mean effect of 0.63 SD. Fourteen measured attitudes toward the subject being taught: 12 favoured the mastery class, with a mean effect of 0.40 SD.

> "Overall, the average effect size in the 18 studies was 0.63, t(17) = 4.50, p < .001."

## Discussion

Attitude scales were self-report and the studies were mostly college PSI courses, so this says students like the method, not that it raises motivation more broadly. It sits alongside a small cost in instructional time: in six of eight studies mastery students needed more, a median 4% more.
