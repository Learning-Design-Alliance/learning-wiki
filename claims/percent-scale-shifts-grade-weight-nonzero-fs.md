# Instructors using the percent scale shifted roughly 14% of exam-item grade weight into non-zero F grades, an effect not explained by student GPA

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Percent-scale instructors were considerably more likely to judge individual exam solutions as non-zero F's, shifting about 14% of exam-item grade weight down from higher grades. [→ Webb 2020](#webb-2020)
`q3 i?` Regressions controlling for entering GPA found 8.6% ± 0.1% (CLASP A) and 18.0% ± 0.2% (CLASP B) extra grade weight in non-zero F's under percent scales, both significant. [→ Webb 2020 (2)](#webb-2020-2)

## Evidence

### Webb 2020

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Fractions of grades given to individual exam answers across all 96 courses (Fig. 4). Zeros were similar across scales; the shifted weight came mostly from C/D range grades, since A's were more common and A+B fractions were about equal (56.4% vs 56.9%).

> "This amounts to shifting about 14% of the entire exam item grade weight from higher grades under 4-point grading down to non-zero F’s under percent grading."

### Webb 2020 (2)

Webb, D. J., Paul, C. A., & Chessey, M. K. (2020). Relative impacts of diﬀerent grade-scales on student success in introductory physics. arXiv. [doi:10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevphyseducres.16.020114)

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Linear regression of student-level fraction of non-zero F's on entering GPA and grade scale (N = 11,804 CLASP A, adjusted R2 = 0.30; N = 9,546 CLASP B, adjusted R2 = 0.46). Controlling for GPA changed the shifted weight little, so the extra F's are likely not due to student differences.

> "the percent scales had 8 .6% ± 0.1% of extra grade weight in non-zero F’s compared to 4-point scales, t = 61,P < 0.001 for the variable GrScale"
