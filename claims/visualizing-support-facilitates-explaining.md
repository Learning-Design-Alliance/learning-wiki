## Evidence

### Fiorella 2023

Fiorella, L. (2023). Making Sense of Generative Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09769-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r3`

Narrative review attributing this to Ainsworth and Loizou (2003): college students studied a circulatory-system lesson as text or diagrams, all prompted to self-explain. The review reports diagram explainers outperformed text explainers; no effect size printed.

> "Students who self-explained diagrams generated more inferences in their explanations and performed better on a subsequent comprehension test than students who explained text only."

### Fiorella 2023 (2)

Fiorella, L. (2023). Making Sense of Generative Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09769-7

`q2 · i?` · `review · r2`

Narrative review attributing this to Bobek and Tversky (2016): college students learning tire-pump and bonding texts wrote self-explanations or drew. The review reports the drawing advantage across both physical-system and abstract-comparison materials; no effect size printed.

> "Learners who created drawings exhibited higher comprehension test performance than learners who wrote verbal summaries"

### Fiorella 2023 (3)

Fiorella, L. (2023). Making Sense of Generative Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09769-7

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r?`

Narrative review attributing this to Fiorella and Kuhlmann (2020): college students taught a respiratory-system text on video via explaining, drawing, or drawing while explaining, tested one week later. The review notes drawing-only and explaining-only groups 'did not significantly differ'.

> "Students who created drawings while explaining generated more elaborative inferences in their oral explanations than students who only orally explained, and this resulted in better subsequent comprehension and transfer test performance."
