# A three-dimensional model of kanji abilities (reading, writing, semantic comprehension) fits Level 2 Kanken data better than two- or one-dimensional models in both 2006 and 2016

## Evidence

### Otsuka 2020

Otsuka, S., & Murai, T. (2020). The multidimensionality of Japanese kanji abilities. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59852-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Confirmatory factor analysis of the 2006 Level 2 Kanken public-site dataset compared three-, two- and unidimensional models with maximum likelihood estimation. The article reports the "RMSEA estimate for the three-dimensional model (0.047)" against 0.070 for both alternatives, and a lower AIC for the three-dimensional model.

> "CFAs with Level 2 data from 2006 showed that the RMSEA estimate for the three-dimensional model (0.047) was lower than those for the two- (0.070) and unidimensional models (0.070), and tests for closeness of fit indicated significance only for the three-dimensional one"

### Otsuka 2020 (2)

Otsuka, S., & Murai, T. (2020). The multidimensionality of Japanese kanji abilities. Scientific Reports. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-59852-0

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

The same CFA model comparison in the 2016 Level 2 public-site dataset replicated the ordering, with the article reporting that "the lowest AIC value for the three dimensional model was replicated with the 2016 data" and good CFI, TLI and SRMR values for all models.

> "the RMSEA for the three-dimensional model with the 2016 data (0.055) was also lower than those for the two- (0.063) and unidimensional ones (0.062), though all of them were not significant on statistical tests (all p close ≤ 0.05)"
