## Evidence

### Thomas C. Toppino and William DiGeorge 1984

Thomas C. Toppino and William DiGeorge. (1984). The spacing effect in free recall emerges with development. Memory & Cognition, 12(2), 118-122. https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=The+spacing+effect+in+free+recall+emerges+with+development+Memory+%26+Cognition+1984

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

A theoretical analysis in the general discussion estimated the independent-encoding maximum from the once-presented recall proportion (P = .139, giving 2P - P² = .259) and found it "reliably lower than the obtained level of recall (.333)" (t = 2.31, p < .05), questioning encoding variability accounts.

> "the estimated probability of re­calling one or both of two independent events was .259, which was reliably lower than the obtained level of recall (.333) for repeated items [t(17) = 2.31, p < .05]"
