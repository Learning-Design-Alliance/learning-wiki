## Evidence

### Meaders Clara L. 2025

Meaders Clara L., Mendez Lilyan, Aguilar Alfonso Godínez, Rivera Angelita T., Vasquez Izabella, Mueller Liam O., Owens Melinda T. (2025). An Asynchronous Chemistry-in-biology Intervention Improves Student Content Knowledge and Performance in Introductory Biology. CBE—Life Sciences Education 24(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.24-05-0151

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Contingency analysis of module usage (submission of practice problems) across three intervention quarters found 435/1241 (35%) of students completed at least one module, with 52% of no-prior-chemistry students using modules versus 30% of college-chemistry students. No effect size was printed.

> "Prior chemistry coursework was significantly associated with module usage, with a greater proportion of students who reported having less chemistry experience reporting using the modules relative to their classmates (X2 (3, N = 1241) = 16.9, p < 0.001)"
