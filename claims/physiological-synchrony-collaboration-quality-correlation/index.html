Evidence¶

Juliene Madureira Ferreira 2021¶

Juliene Madureira Ferreira. (2021). What If We Look at the Body? An Embodied Perspective of Collaborative Learning. Educational Psychology Review. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09607-8

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Schneider et al. (2020), studies measuring electrodermal activity from students' wrists during small-group collaboration. The review itself labels the results as still limited.

"have been able to show initial positive correlations between physiological synchrony and collaboration quality, task performance, and learning gains, by computing the number of cycles between low and high synchronization (Schneider et al., 2020)"

Related Claims¶

  • The editorial reports that a mixed methods study of 42 participant pairs found partly contradictory and partly complementary results on physiological synchrony and collaborative learning — related
  • Increased physiological arousal and higher physiological synchrony relate to more monitoring with negative valence — related
  • Higher physiological synchrony relates to less monitoring with positive valence — related
  • Real collaborative groups show higher physiological synchrony than randomly matched false groups on %REC, %DET, and ADL — related
  • Neither physiological arousal nor physiological synchrony predicts equality of participation in monitoring interactions — related
  • During-simulation physiological synchrony is positively correlated with team leaders' CRM performance scores (preliminary, 9 teams) — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
