## Evidence

### Sackstein S 2015

Sackstein S, Spark L & Jenkins A. (2015). Are e-books effective tools for learning? Reading speed and comprehension: iPad vs. paper. South African Journal of Education, 35(4), Art. #1202. https://doi.org/10.15700/saje.v35n4a1202

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

MANOVA testing treatment order effects on reading speed showed a significant effect for School B (Hotelling's Trace = .273, p < 0.05). iPad reading was faster regardless of which day it was administered.

> "The results reveal that the treatment for School B had a statistically significant effect on reading speed (Hotelling's Trace = .273, p < 0.05). The students who moved from paper on Day 1 to iPad on Day 2 read faster , and the students who moved from iPad on Day 1 to paper on Day 2 read slower; thus regardless of the day, the iPad reading speed was faster."
