# Frequency of performing blood transfusions is associated with knowledge score, while gender, qualification, ward, experience and training are not

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Nurses who performed 1–4 transfusions in the past 6 months had a 4.13% significantly higher knowledge score (p = 0.005); no significant differences were found for gender, educational level, ward type, working experience or previous training exposure. [→ Mohd Noor Noor Haslina 2021](#mohd-noor-noor-haslina-2021)
`q2 i?` Higher transfusion experience in the last 6 months was significantly associated with better knowledge (0 vs. 1–4 times, p = 0.020; 1–4 vs. >5 times, p = 0.035). [→ Mohd Noor Noor Haslina 2021 (2)](#mohd-noor-noor-haslina-2021-2)

## Evidence

### Mohd Noor Noor Haslina 2021

Mohd Noor Noor Haslina, Saad Noor Hafiza, Khan Mohammad, Hassan Mohd Nazri, Ramli Marini, Bahar Rosnah, Mohamed Yusoff Shafini, Iberahim Salfarina, Wan Ab Rahman Wan Suriana, Zulkafli Zefarina, Islam Md Asiful. (2021). Blood Transfusion Knowledge among Nurses in Malaysia: A University Hospital Experience. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182111194

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Simple and multiple linear regression on the 200-nurse survey data (Table 3); the article reports a crude coefficient of 4.13 (95% CI 1.25, 7.01), p = 0.005 for performing 1–4 transfusions versus none.

> "From both simple and multiple linear regression models, we observed that nurses who performed blood transfusions between 1 to 4 times (within the past 6 months) had a 4.13% significantly higher (p = 0.005) blood transfusion knowledge score (Table 3)."

### Mohd Noor Noor Haslina 2021 (2)

Mohd Noor Noor Haslina, Saad Noor Hafiza, Khan Mohammad, Hassan Mohd Nazri, Ramli Marini, Bahar Rosnah, Mohamed Yusoff Shafini, Iberahim Salfarina, Wan Ab Rahman Wan Suriana, Zulkafli Zefarina, Islam Md Asiful. (2021). Blood Transfusion Knowledge among Nurses in Malaysia: A University Hospital Experience. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph182111194

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Association analysis section of the 200-nurse survey; the article reports pairwise comparisons of transfusion frequency reaching significance at p = 0.020 and p = 0.035, with no effect size printed.

> "Interestingly, higher blood transfusion experience in the last 6 months was significantly associated with better knowledge (0 times vs. 1–4 times, p = 0.020 and 1–4 times vs. >5 times, p = 0.035)."
