# Group differences in activation are more extensive when behavioral performance is equated across groups, indicating they are not a consequence of worse performance

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 studies · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` When typical readers' Fast > Slow contrast was compared to dyslexic readers' Medium > Slow contrast under matched in-scanner accuracy, typical readers showed greater activation in almost all brain regions engaged by the task. [→ Christodoulou JA 2014](#christodoulou-ja-2014)
`q3 i?` Dyslexic readers showed greater activation than typical readers in left anterior cingulate regions under the performance-equated comparison. [→ Christodoulou JA 2014 (2)](#christodoulou-ja-2014-2)

## Evidence

### Christodoulou JA 2014

Christodoulou JA, Del Tufo SN, Lymberis J, Saxler PK, Ghosh SS, et al. (2014) Brain Bases of Reading Fluency in Typical Reading and Impaired Fluency in Dyslexia. PLoS ONE 9(7): e100552. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100552

`q3 · i?`

A performance-equated whole-brain comparison matched typical readers' Fast > Slow contrast against dyslexic readers' Medium > Slow contrast, since accuracy did not differ significantly between these conditions. Typical readers showed greater activation in almost all engaged regions.

> "Typical readers (Fast . Slow) and readers with dyslexia (Medium . Slow) were compared based on matched in-scanner performance (Table 4). Typical readers showed significantly greater activation in almost all brain regions engaged by the task"

### Christodoulou JA 2014 (2)

Christodoulou JA, Del Tufo SN, Lymberis J, Saxler PK, Ghosh SS, et al. (2014) Brain Bases of Reading Fluency in Typical Reading and Impaired Fluency in Dyslexia. PLoS ONE 9(7): e100552. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100552

`q3 · i?`

In the same performance-equated comparison, the dyslexic group showed greater activation than typical readers in left anterior cingulate regions, the only direction of group difference reported for this contrast.

> "Readers with dyslexia showed greater activations compared to typical readers in left anterior cingulate regions."
