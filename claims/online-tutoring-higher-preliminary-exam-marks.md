# Students who attended online tutoring scored higher on the preliminary multiple-choice exam test than all students, with p < 0.001 in all three exam sessions

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` In the June, July and September 2020 exam sessions, tutored students' average marks on the 15-question preliminary quiz were about 0.8 points higher than those of all students, and T-tests gave p-values below 0.001 in every session. [→ Matteo Luca Ruggiero and Lorenzo Galante 2020](#matteo-luca-ruggiero-and-lorenzo-galante-2020)

## Evidence

### Matteo Luca Ruggiero and Lorenzo Galante 2020

Matteo Luca Ruggiero and Lorenzo Galante. (2020). Online Tutoring in Introductory Physics Courses: a Lockdown Experience. arXiv. [doi:10.48550/arXiv.2011.13315](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.13315)

`q2 · i?` · `design · r2`

Comparison of preliminary-test marks between tutored students (e.g. 742 quiz attempts in June, average 10.02) and all students (3522 attempts, average 9.31), analysed with T-tests; the article reports "the resulting p-value is smaller than 0.001" in all sessions. No standardised effect size is printed.

> "In all cases (June, July and September sessions) the resulting p-value is smaller than 0.001, meaning that we have a conﬁdence level bigger that 99.9% to reject the null hypothesis."
