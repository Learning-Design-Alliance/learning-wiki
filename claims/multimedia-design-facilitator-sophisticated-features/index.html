Evidence¶

Bower K 2026¶

Bower K, Scrivener K, Larkins S, Seaton C, Carlisle K. (2026). Technology-based teaching to support health students’ clinical skills in stroke recovery: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08709-7

q2 · i? · review · r3

Content-analysis finding from the review; multimedia learning was the most frequently reported technology, featuring in 39 of 48 studies, with videos used to enhance visual learning and modelling.

"Multimedia design was frequently stated as a facilitator of learning. Videos were used widely to enhance visual learning by, for example, observing authentic clinical scenarios [45, 47, 60], enabling ‘passive skill building’ or modelling [50]."

Bower K 2026 (2)¶

Bower K, Scrivener K, Larkins S, Seaton C, Carlisle K. (2026). Technology-based teaching to support health students’ clinical skills in stroke recovery: a scoping review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08709-7

q2 · i? · causal · r?

The review reports, citing one included study, that "e-modules with more sophisticated visual and interactive features led to stronger learning outcomes" than less sophisticated modules; no effect size is printed in the review.

"One study demonstrated that e-modules with more sophisticated visual and interactive features led to stronger learning outcomes when compared to modules with less sophisticated features [62]."
