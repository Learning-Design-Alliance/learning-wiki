# Students' self-rated confidence in their physics answers rose from pre-test to post-test in both the VR and slideshow conditions, with no between-condition difference (one pre-post study)

## Evidence

### Battipede 2025

Battipede, E., Giangualano, A., Boffi, P., Clerici, M., Calvi, A., Cassenti, L., Cialini, R., Van Den Weghe, T. L. A., Addimando, L., Lanzi, P. L., Gallace, A. (2025). Physics Playground: Insights from a Qualitative-Quantitative Study about VR-Based Learning. Originally published 2024. [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-88881-6_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-88881-6_8)

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Confidence (0-100 per question) measured in the 44-participant experiment. Paired t-tests showed "significant differences in confidence between pre-test and post-test" across all, topic-related, and unrelated questions; no between-condition differences were found.

> "Paired samples Student's t -tests showed significant differences in confi- dence between pre -test and post -test with: all questions ( t(43) = −14.39, p  < .001), topic-related questions ( t(43) = −13.30, p < .001), and unrelated questions ( t(43) = −4.56, p < .001)."
