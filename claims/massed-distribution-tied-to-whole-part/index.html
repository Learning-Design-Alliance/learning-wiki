Evidence¶

Pechstein 1921¶

Pechstein, L. A. (1921). Massed vs. Distributed Effort in Learning. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070137

q2 · i? · causal · r1

The author's fifth conclusion, framing the paper's central thesis that the distribution-of-effort question cannot be separated from the whole-versus-part question in difficult motor learning.

"The hard problem becomes easy if it is learned under massed conditions by the part method and in no other way; it remains hard if it is learned as a whole under massed or distributed conditions, or even in parts under distributed conditions."

Related Claims¶

  • The longer and more difficult the motor problem, the more advisable it is to break it into units and learn both the units and their connections under massed conditions — related
  • Short maze units learned under massed conditions connect into a serial whole with great economy, whereas units learned under distributed conditions connect only with extreme difficulty — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
  • For short maze problems, massed learning effort is more economical than distributed effort in trials, time and errors, for both rats and human adults — related
  • For rats, whole and part maze learning show no difference in trials, but the whole method saves time and errors — related
  • Distributed practice aids digit memorization, but massed practice is preferred for new and short mazes, indicating a boundary condition on the spacing effect — related
