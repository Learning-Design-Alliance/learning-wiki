# Major downward difficulty adaptation increases situational interest in game-based math learning, while major upward adaptation decreases it and minor adaptation has no effect

## Subclaims
`q3 i1` A major decrease in task difficulty significantly enhanced situational interest (Cohen's d = 0.19 for level -3). [→ Koskinen 2023](#koskinen-2023)
`q3 i?` Minor adaptations of difficulty did not significantly affect situational interest. [→ Koskinen 2023 (2)](#koskinen-2023-2)

## Evidence

### Koskinen 2023

Koskinen, A., McMullen, J., Hannula-Sormunen, M., Ninaus, M., & Kiili, K. (2023). The strength and direction of the difficulty adaptation affect situational interest in game-based learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104694

`q3 · i1` · `causal · r2`

Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA of in-game situational interest ratings across nine content-matched basic/adapted level pairs showed "significant interaction effect of the level of adaptation and difficulty condition", with a small printed effect size η2p = .02.

> "there was a significant interaction effect of the level of adaptation and difficulty condition, F (5, 1428) = 6.73, p < .001, η2p = .02"

### Koskinen 2023 (2)

Koskinen, A., McMullen, J., Hannula-Sormunen, M., Ninaus, M., & Kiili, K. (2023). The strength and direction of the difficulty adaptation affect situational interest in game-based learning. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104694

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Paired-samples t-tests across the six adaptation levels found "significant differences in situational interest" only for major adaptations; minor adaptations showed no significant differences and no effect size indicating more than a trivial change.

> "there were significant differences in situational interest between the difficulty conditions when participants received major adaptations of difficulty, but not for minor adaptations of difficulty"
