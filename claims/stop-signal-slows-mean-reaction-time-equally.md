# Go-trial response times are slower during the stop-signal task than the no-tone task, with no diagnostic group difference

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · `q3` peer-reviewed experiment · `i2` medium

## Subclaims
`q3 i2` A main effect of task on MRT (BF10 = 8.27 x 10^12; d = 0.76) showed slower responding during the stop-signal task; groups were equivalent (BF01 = 3.43; d = 0.17). [→ Fosco 2019](#fosco-2019)

## Evidence

### Fosco 2019

Fosco, W. D., Kofler, M. J., Alderson, R. M., Tarle, S. J., Raiker, J. S., & Sarver, D. E. (2019). Inhibitory Control and Information Processing in ADHD: Comparing the Dual Task and Performance Adjustment Hypotheses. Journal of Abnormal Child Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-018-0504-9

`q3 · i2`

Tier 1 Bayesian mixed ANOVA on mean reaction time in 144 children aged 8-13. "children responded more slowly during the stop-signal than the no-tone task" (d = 0.76), replicating prior work; the group x task interaction was inconclusive (BF10 = 1.94).

> "a main effect of task was observed (BF10 = 8.27 × 1012; d = 0.76), such that children responded more slowly during the stop-signal than the no-tone task, as expected. The ADHD and Non-ADHD groups were equivalent in terms of response speed (BF01 = 3.43; d = 0.17)."
