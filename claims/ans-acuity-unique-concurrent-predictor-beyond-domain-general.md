# ANS acuity is a unique concurrent predictor of preschool math ability beyond expressive vocabulary, non-numerical attention RT, and memory span

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · associational `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Accuracy on the ANS task uniquely predicted concurrent TEMA-3 scores controlling for vocabulary, K-CPT RT, and memory span (partial r2 = 0.18, p < 0.001). [→ Libertus Melissa E. 2013](#libertus-melissa-e-2013)
`q3 i?` RT on the ANS task remained a significant predictor even controlling for RT on the non-numerical attention task (partial r2 = 0.05, p = 0.01). [→ Libertus Melissa E. 2013](#libertus-melissa-e-2013)

## Evidence

### Libertus Melissa E. 2013

Libertus Melissa E., Feigenson Lisa, Halberda Justin. (2013). Is Approximate Number Precision a Stable Predictor of Math Ability? Learning and Individual Differences. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lindif.2013.02.001

`q3 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Concurrent linear regressions at Time 2 (Table 5) predicting TEMA-3 from ANS accuracy or Weber fraction w, ANS RT, expressive vocabulary, K-CPT RT, and memory span while controlling for age; models captured significant variance (model 1: R2 = 0.33, F(5,117) = 11.63, p < 0.001), with accuracy contributing partial r2 = 0.18 and ANS-RT partial r2 = 0.05, p = 0.01.

> "both estimates of ANS acuity in each model (i.e., accuracy and RT, or w and RT) contributed uniquely to the relationship with math ability when controlling for all other variables"
