# Instructor demographics (teaching experience, gender, age) show mostly weak associations with student descriptor ratings, the strongest being a moderate positive correlation between teaching experience and initial enthusiasm ratings

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` Teaching experience correlated moderately with initial enthusiasm ratings (r = 0.414), with weak associations for other descriptors (e.g., confident r = 0.269, nervous r = −0.224); gender and age showed only weak associations with a few descriptors at initial and final surveys. ## Evidence

### Kendall 2013

Kendall, K. D., & Schussler, E. E. (2013). Evolving Impressions: Undergraduate Perceptions of Graduate Teaching Assistants and Faculty Members over a Semester. CBE—Life Sciences Education 12(1). https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-07-0110

`q3 · i3` · `associational · r1`

Spearman rank correlations on pooled initial-survey data (GTAs and faculty together) linked instructor demographics to descriptor ratings, with a "moderately positive association ... for the rating of enthusiastic (r = 0.414, p = 0.001)" and weak associations for confident, engaging, relaxed, and strict; gender and age correlations were similarly weak.

> "Weak positive associations were identified between university teaching experience and initial student ratings for confident (r = 0.269, p = 0.000), engaging (r = 0.154, p = 0.001), relaxed (r = 0.145, p = 0.001), and strict (r = 0.095, p = 0.031), while a moderately positive association was identified for the rating of enthusiastic (r = 0.414, p = 0.001)."
