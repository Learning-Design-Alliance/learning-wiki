# Teacher autonomy support did not produce significantly higher post-test scores than the controlling style in a single simulated 20-minute teaching session

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · causal `r1` · `q3`
> **Currency** · newest study 2025 · not yet checked against its sources · not reviewed by a person

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` A two-factor repeated-measures ANOVA on test scores found no significant group effect and no significant group × time interaction between the autonomy-support and control conditions. [→ Wang Guoxia 2025](#wang-guoxia-2025)

## Evidence

### Wang Guoxia 2025

Wang Guoxia, Zhang Qin, Wang Lumeng, Liu Yi. (2025). The impact of teachers’ autonomy support on students’ academic performance from the perspective of hyperscanning: the mediating role of autonomy need satisfaction. Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience. https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsaf115

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Two-factor repeated-measures ANOVA on pre-test and post-test scores (eight single-choice items, maximum 8 points) across the two teacher-style groups. The article reports "no significant effect for group (F(1,40) = 2.38, P = .131)" and no interaction; the main effect of time of test was significant.

> "However, there was no significant effect for group (F(1,40) = 2.38, P = .131) or the group × time interaction (F(1,40) = 1.46, P = .233)."
