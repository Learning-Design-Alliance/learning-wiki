Evidence¶

Daneshfar Majid; Moonaghi Hossein Karimi 2025¶

Daneshfar Majid; Moonaghi Hossein Karimi. (2025). The impact of clinical simulation on bridging the theory–practice gap in nursing education: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07790-8

q2 · i? · review · r2

Systematic review of 15 studies (2010–2025) on clinical simulation in nursing education; the review reports "twelve reported significant improvements in nursing students’ clinical decision-making, judgment, or self-confidence". No meta-analysis was possible due to heterogeneity.

"Of the fifteen included studies, twelve reported significant improvements in nursing students’ clinical decision-making, judgment, or self-confidence, involving over 1,100 participants."

Related Claims¶

  • Simulation type–outcome alignment: high-fidelity formats favour judgment and decision-making, VR and games favour empathy and reflection — related
  • In an RCT of ICU nurses, simulation-based education produced within-group gains in knowledge, confidence and competence but no between-group differences at 6 weeks — related
  • AI-enhanced simulation in high-acuity nursing training improves clinical confidence and emergency preparedness — related
  • Simulation-based education compared to patient-based clinical teaching alone shows a large pooled advantage in objectively assessed outcomes in undergraduate nursing students — related
  • The effect of high-fidelity simulation on practising nurses' self-confidence is inconclusive, varying by study design — related
  • Very high majorities of students rated high-fidelity simulation as enhancing learning, clinical reasoning, decision-making, and value — related
  • Simulation's impact on student reflection remains inconclusive due to limited evidence — related
  • Simulation training in critical care during COVID-19 enhanced knowledge and confidence more than theoretical and clinical training alone — related
  • The review's authors interpret structured pre-briefing as supporting nursing students' metacognition, clinical judgment, and meaningful simulation learning — a narrower finding that bears on this claim
