# Teacher-assisted consolidation (CALT) produced significantly higher long-term retention than computer-aided consolidation (CALC) among computer-aided learners.

## Evidence

### Conradty 2016

Conradty, C. & Bogner, F. X. (2016). Hypertext or Textbook: Effects on Motivation and Gain in Knowledge. Education Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030029

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Comparison of the two computer-aided learning conditions (CALT n=114, CALC n=51) on the six-week retention test in the same quasi-experimental study. The article reports "significantly higher knowledge scores" for CALT with Z = −2.23, p = 0.026.

> "In respect to long-term learning, students of CALT gained significantly higher knowledge scores than students of CALC (MWU: Z = −2.23, p = 0.026)."

### Conradty 2016 (2)

Conradty, C. & Bogner, F. X. (2016). Hypertext or Textbook: Effects on Motivation and Gain in Knowledge. Education Sciences. https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci6030029

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Oblivion rate (K3–K2 difference) compared across treatments; the CALT group showed the lowest knowledge loss. Table 3 reports the pairwise CALT–CALC oblivion contrast as MWU: Z = −3.963, p < 0.000.

> "There is a significant treatment effect with lowest oblivion rates found with CALT (Table 3; Figure 4)."
