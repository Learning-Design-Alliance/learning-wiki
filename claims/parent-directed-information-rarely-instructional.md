# 79% of literacy apps include adult-directed information, but few offer performance feedback (17%) or detail on educational content (14%)

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · associational `r1` · `q2`

## Subclaims
`q2 i?` Most apps (79%) contained adult-directed information, usually in a parent section (76%), most often basic usage instructions (40%) or privacy/security information (38%). [→ Vaala 2015](#vaala-2015)
`q2 i?` Detailed information about educational content was rare overall (14%) but most common in Expert-awarded apps (27%) versus Top 50 Paid (9%) and Free (10%). [→ Vaala 2015 (2)](#vaala-2015-2)

## Evidence

### Vaala 2015

Vaala, S., Ly, A., & Levine, M.H. (2015). Getting a read on the app stores: A market scan and analysis of children's literacy apps. New York, NY: The Joan Ganz Cooney Center at Sesame Workshop. https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/publication/getting-a-read-on-the-app-stores-a-market-scan-and-analysis-of-childrens-literacy-apps/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Content coding of parent-directed information in the 183-app scan; the report adds that 40% gave usage instructions and 38% privacy/security information. No effect size printed.

> "We found that most of the apps (79%) contained some adult-directed information within the app’s content. Overwhelmingly, this adult-directed information was contained in a specific section of the app (e.g., “Parent section”; 76% of apps)."

### Vaala 2015 (2)

Vaala, S., Ly, A., & Levine, M.H. (2015). Getting a read on the app stores: A market scan and analysis of children's literacy apps. New York, NY: The Joan Ganz Cooney Center at Sesame Workshop. https://joanganzcooneycenter.org/publication/getting-a-read-on-the-app-stores-a-market-scan-and-analysis-of-childrens-literacy-apps/

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r1`

Subsample comparison of educational-content detail within the parent-information coding, contrasting Awarded (27%), Top 50 Paid (9%), and Top 50 Free (10%) apps. No effect size printed.

> "Notably, detailed information about the educational content, such as what the app’s teaching strategies entailed or why certain skills were important, were most commonly encountered in Expert-awarded apps (27%) compared to Top 50 Paid (9%) or Top 50 Free apps (10%)."
