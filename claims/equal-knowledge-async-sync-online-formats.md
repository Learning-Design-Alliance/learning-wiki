## Evidence

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Post-training multiple-choice test comparison between the two randomized groups; means were nearly identical ("MLP = 12.69 ± 4.17; MW = 12.66 ± 4.01") and the difference was not statistically significant; equivalence was not tested.

> "When comparing the asynchronous and synchronous study groups, we found no significant differences in knowledge status after training according to the multiple-choice test (MLP = 12.69 ± 4.17; MW = 12.66 ± 4.01, p =.449, p_adj > 0.999)"

### Zsifkovits Monika 2025 (2)

Zsifkovits Monika, Amplatz Lukas, Triebner Nina, Utz Janine, Kornhuber Johannes, Spitzer Philipp. (2025). Randomized controlled trial of asynchronous vs. synchronous online teaching formats: equal knowledge after training, greater acceptance and lower intrinsic motivation through asynchronous online learning. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-07481-4

`q3 · i0` · `causal · r2`

ANCOVA controlling for years of education confirmed the results "did not differ significantly", with a printed effect size of η2 = 0.000. The authors note equivalence was not the tested hypothesis; only non-significance was reported.

> "After adjusting for the paramedics' duration of education (highest educational qualification, in years) via ANCOVA, the multiple-choice results did not differ significantly (F (1,1041) = 0.398, p =.528, p_adj > 0.999, η2 = 0.000)."
