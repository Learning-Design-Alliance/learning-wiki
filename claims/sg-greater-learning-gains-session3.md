# By session 3, SG-tutor students achieved significantly greater pre-to-post learning gains than PR-tutor students despite lower pre-test scores

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` A significant time-by-condition interaction in session 3 showed SG students gained more from pre- to post-test than PR students (β = 0.72, p = 0.01), while SG students had significantly lower pre-test scores (β = −0.52, p = 0.02). [→ Jérôme Brender 2026](#jerome-brender-2026)

## Evidence

### Jérôme Brender 2026

Jérôme Brender, Laila El-Hamamsy, Kim Uittenhove, Aitor Perez, Patrick Jermann, Francesco Mondada, and Engin Bumbacher. (2026). Reflective Dialogue or Prompt Refinement? Effects of Tutor Scaffolding on Students' Independent LLM Use for Programming. arXiv preprint. [doi:10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-29763-1_37)

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Repeated-measures mixed-effects model of test scores (130 observations, 65 students) with time, condition, and their interaction; the interaction was significant (β = 0.72, p = 0.01) and the model's R² marginal was 0.041 / conditional 0.230. Sessions S1 and S2 showed no significant effects.

> "While students in the SG condition had significantly lower pre-test scores (main effect of condition:β=−0.52,p= 0.02), they showed significantly greater gains from pre- to post-test than students in the PR condition (interaction effect:β= 0.72,p= 0.01)"
