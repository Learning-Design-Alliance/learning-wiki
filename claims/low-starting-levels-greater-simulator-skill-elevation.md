## Subclaims
`q2 i?` The two weakest simulator performers raised their skill levels 31.6% and 29.2% during training, versus 23.3% and 21.1% for the two best performers, yet remained below them at the end. [→ Jokinen E 2019](#jokinen-e-2019)

## Evidence

### Jokinen E 2019

Jokinen E, Mikkola TS, Härkki P. (2019). Effect of structural training on surgical outcomes of residents' first operative laparoscopy: a randomized controlled trial. Surgical Endoscopy. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00464-018-06657-y

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r2`

Visual analysis of simulator data for the two best (B1, B2) and two weakest (W1, W2) participants across 32 chosen parameters; the article calls this a tendency, not a tested difference. At the end the best performers were still higher (35.7% and 34.2%) than the weakest (32.6% and 25.4%).

> "We found a tendency that the best performed participants elevated their skill levels 23.3% and 21.1%, while the skill levels were elevated 31.6% and 29.2% among the weakest performed participants (Fig. 4)."
