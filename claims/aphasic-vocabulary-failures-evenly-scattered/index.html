Evidence¶

Wells 1917¶

Wells, F. L. (1917). Aphasic performance in the Terman vocabulary test. Journal of Educational Psychology, 8(8), 483–487. doi:10.1037/h0073107

q1 · i? · qualitative · r2

Case observation on the Stanford series, March 13-14, of a 72-year-old man with arteriosclerotic psychosis (IQ .57). The examiner noted that "the words missed were scattered fairly evenly throughout the list" rather than clustered at the difficult end as in normal performance.

"the words missed were scattered fairly evenly throughout the list, instead of being, as normally, heaped up towards the more difficult end."

Related Claims¶

  • Cued combination yields much better vocabulary performance than definition in an aphasic patient — related
  • On retest, performance on previously failed words shifts between methods, with combination again advantaged
  • Definition is quicker overall on the Terman list, but combination disposes of easier words faster
  • Scattering is considerably greater on the Stanford-Binet than on the earlier Binet-Simon scales, so conclusions about scattering differ according to the scale used
