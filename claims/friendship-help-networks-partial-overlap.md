## Evidence

### van Rijsewijk 2019

van Rijsewijk, L. G. M., Snijders, T. A. B., Dijkstra, J. K., Steglich, C., & Veenstra, R. (2019). The Interplay Between Adolescents' Friendships and the Exchange of Help: A Longitudinal Multiplex Social Network Study. Journal of Research on Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12501

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Descriptive network statistics (Table 2) across three waves: friendship density about 25% versus help density about 12%, friendship reciprocity about 65% versus help about 45%. The article states "friendship networks were on average twice as dense as help networks."

> "Thus, friendship networks were on average twice as dense as help networks, suggesting that there are pairs of individuals who are friends, but not helpers."

### van Rijsewijk 2019 (2)

van Rijsewijk, L. G. M., Snijders, T. A. B., Dijkstra, J. K., Steglich, C., & Veenstra, R. (2019). The Interplay Between Adolescents' Friendships and the Exchange of Help: A Longitudinal Multiplex Social Network Study. Journal of Research on Adolescence. https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12501

`q2 · i?` · `associational · r2`

Classroom-level scatterplot (Figure 3) of friendship network density against help network density, with reciprocity coded by color and shape. The article reports "hardly any association between the densities of the two networks" and no clear association between reciprocity rates.

> "Figure 3 demonstrates that there is hardly any association between the densities of the two networks; if many students are friends in a classroom, this does not imply that many students in this classroom help each other, and vice versa."
