Karpicke 2017¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Kang et al. (2007), who crossed initial and final formats with 2500 word articles: students did much better initially on multiple-choice "(86% vs. 54% correct in their Experiment 1)" and also on the final test.

"Students did much better on initial multiple- choice tests than they did on initial short-answer items (86% vs. 54% correct in their Experiment 1)."

Karpicke 2017 (2)¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

In Kang et al.'s second experiment, as the chapter reports, students studied correct answers as feedback, and "students who took initial short-answer tests performed better than did students who took initial multiple-choice tests".

"In this experiment, students who took initial short-answer tests performed better than did students who took initial multiple-choice tests."

Karpicke 2017 (3)¶

Karpicke, J. D. (2017). Retrieval-Based Learning: A Decade of Progress. Learning and Memory: A Comprehensive Reference, 2nd edition, Volume 2. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-12-809324-5.21055-9

q2 · i? · review · r3

The chapter reports Little et al. (2012), who found multiple-choice better without feedback, and "when students received feedback, there was a slight advantage of initial short-answer testing".

"However, when students received feedback, there was a slight advantage of initial short-answer testing over initial multiple-choice testing."

  • Providing feedback after initial multiple-choice tests cut lure intrusions on final short-answer tests roughly in half, with immediate and delayed feedback equally effective — related
  • Taking initial multiple-choice tests without feedback can lead students to later produce the incorrect lure answers they selected, even when an overall retrieval practice benefit occurs — related
  • Initial retrieval conditions that provide less cue support, such as free recall rather than recognition or fewer letter cues, tend to produce better retention despite lower initial success — reports the opposite
  • Short-answer (production) and multiple-choice (recognition) retrieval practice produce no statistically significant difference in undergraduates' reading comprehension on a final MCQ test — related
  • Evidence on whether initial short-answer questions produce more learning than initial multiple-choice questions is mixed, with recent studies finding little or no difference — related
  • Prior meta-analyses of feedback (Li 2010; Kang & Han 2015; Van der Kleij et al. 2015) reported positive effects but none was devoted entirely to feedback in CALL — related
  • Multiple choice practice items produce significantly higher performance during retrieval practice than short answer items, with applied short answer the most difficult condition — related
