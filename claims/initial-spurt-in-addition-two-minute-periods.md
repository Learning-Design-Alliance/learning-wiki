## Subclaims
`q3 i?` In continuous addition work, aggregate output in the first two-minute period exceeds later two-minute periods, with a much greater drop from the first to the second period than from the second to the fifth, which the author attributes to initial spurt. [→ Chapman 1914](#chapman-1914)

## Evidence

### Chapman 1914

Chapman, J. C. (1914). A Study of Initial Spurt in the Case of Addition. The Journal of Educational Psychology. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070019

`q3 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Experiment with 46 male college students adding columns of ten one-place numbers over ten ten-minute periods, each divided into five two-minute periods marked by a spoken signal. Scores indexed to the first period were 100, 92.5, 89.4, 88.0, and 87.1, showing "a much greater drop in efficiency from the first to the second period".

> "From this table, it will be seen that there is a much greater drop in efficiency from the first to the second period, than from the second to the fifth period, which seems to indicate the presence of a factor which can only be called initial spurt."
