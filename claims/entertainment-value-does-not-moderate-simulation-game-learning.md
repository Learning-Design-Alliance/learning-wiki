# Entertainment value of simulation games did not moderate learning relative to comparison instruction

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study · quant-synthesis `r2` · `q3`

## Subclaims
`q3 i?` Trainees learned the same amount from high-entertainment (d = .26) and low-entertainment (d = .38) simulation games relative to comparison groups, failing to support the entertainment hypothesis. [→ Traci Sitzmann 2011](#traci-sitzmann-2011)

## Evidence

### Traci Sitzmann 2011

Traci Sitzmann. (2011). A Meta-Analytic Examination of the Instructional Effectiveness of Computer-Based Simulation Games. Personnel Psychology, 64, 489-528. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/17446570

`q3 · i?` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Meta-analytic moderator analysis comparing nine high-entertainment samples (N = 809) with 51 low-entertainment samples (N = 3,216); the QB statistic was not significant for entertainment value, and the article reports trainees "learned the same amount" across categories.

> "Relative to the comparison group, trainees learned the same amount from simulation games that had high ( d = .26) or low (d = .38) entertainment value, failing to support Hypothesis 5."
