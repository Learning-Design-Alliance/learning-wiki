# A strategic resource-use exam playbook intervention raised final course grades in introductory statistics across two randomized cohorts, with a dosage effect for completing it twice

> **Claim** · [All claims](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 study (2 entries) · design `r2` · `q4` · `i1`–`i3`

## Subclaims
`q4 i1` Treatment students outperformed control students on final course grades in Study 1 (d = 0.33) and Study 2 (d = 0.37). [→ Peters 2022](#peters-2022)
`q4 i3` A treatment dosage effect was found between students who took the pre-exam intervention twice and those who took it only once (d = 0.81). [→ Peters 2022 (2)](#peters-2022-2)

## Evidence

### Peters 2022

Peters, V., Pakhira, D., White, L., Fennelly-Atkinson, R., & Means, B. (2022). Designing gateway statistics and chemistry courses for today's students: Case studies of postsecondary course innovations. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/162

`q4 · i1` · `design · r2`

Two randomized controlled trials of the self-administered Strategic Resource Use intervention in college introductory statistics, with students randomly assigned to treatment (N = 179) or control (N = 182); the analysis found treatment students "performed better on final course grades than all students in the control condition" with d = 0.33 and d = 0.37.

> "The analysis found that students in the treatment condition performed better on final course grades than all students in the control condition in Study 1 (d = 0.33) and Study 2 (d = 0.37)."

### Peters 2022 (2)

Peters, V., Pakhira, D., White, L., Fennelly-Atkinson, R., & Means, B. (2022). Designing gateway statistics and chemistry courses for today's students: Case studies of postsecondary course innovations. Digital Promise. https://doi.org/10.51388/20.500.12265/162

`q4 · i3` · `design · r2`

Dosage analysis within the same two RCTs comparing students who completed both pre-exam surveys with those who completed one; the article reports "a treatment dosage effect" with d = 0.81.

> "Finally, a treatment dosage effect was found between students who took the pre-exam intervention twice (full intervention) and those who took the intervention only once (d = 0.81)."
