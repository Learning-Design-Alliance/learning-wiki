# Reading text from screens has a small, significant negative effect on reading performance compared to paper (g = .25)

## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Clinton 2019

Virginia Clinton. (2019). Reading from paper compared to screens: A systematic review and metaanalysis. Journal of Research in Reading. https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.12269

`q3 · i1` · `quant-synthesis · r2`

Random effects meta-analysis of 33 randomized experiments (k = 33) comparing reading performance from paper and screens; the pooled Hedges' g was .25 with 95% CI [.37, .12], described as a "small, but significant negative effect". One-study-removed analyses showed no outliers, and Egger's test of the intercept (β = 1.29, p = .20) suggested publication bias was unlikely.

> "Based on the random effects model, reading text from screens had a small, but significant negative effect on performance scores compared to reading from paper, g = .25, k = 33, SE = .06, 95% CI = [ .37, .12], p < .001"
