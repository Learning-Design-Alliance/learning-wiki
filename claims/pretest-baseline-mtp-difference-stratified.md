Exploring effects of a booster workshop on progression and retention of resuscitation skills of residents when added to regular low-dose simulation. `q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Baseline Mann-Whitney comparison of the 89 residents reported in Results (Table 3). The article reports the pre-test medical task performance difference with p = 0.003 and no effect size.

> "There are no statistically significant differences for most outcome measures at the pre-test between the two groups except for medical task performance score which is higher in regular low-dose training group (p = 0.003)."

### Chen Hui-Wen 2025 (2)

Chen Hui-Wen, Tsai Hung-Wei, Chen Yi-Chun, Chiu Yu-Jui, Kang Enoch Yi-No, Hsu Yi-Ting, Issenberg S. Barry, Wu Jen-Chieh. (2025). Exploring effects of a booster workshop on progression and retention of resuscitation skills of residents when added to regular low-dose simulation. BMC Medical Education. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-025-06705-x

`q2 · i?` · `causal · r1`

Stratified subgroup analysis reported in Results, with detailed data in Supplementary Materials 1 to 4; the article reports no statistically significant subgroup differences and no effect size.

> "Upon analyzing the subgroups within the educational system, results revealed that there are no statistically significant differences observed within the cohorts of 6-year or 7-year medical education system students across both groups (Supplementary Material 1 to 4 )."
