# Among students not provided with diagrams, self-construction of free-body diagrams strongly and positively correlated with problem-solving performance and prior Mechanics achievement

## Subclaims
`q3 i3` The number of problems for which control-group students created free-body diagrams correlated significantly with their five-problem set score (r=0.7) and with their earlier Mechanics exam achievement (r=0.72). [→ Vanes Mešić 2017](#vanes-mesic-2017)

## Evidence

### Vanes Mešić 2017

Vanes Mešić, Sabaheta Mahmutović, Elvedin Hasović, Nataša Erceg. (2017). Free-Body Diagrams and Problem Solving in Mechanics: An Example of The Effectiveness of Self-Constructed Representations. European J of Physics Education, Volume 7 Issue 3. https://portal.issn.org/resource/ISSN/1309-7202

`q3 · i3` · `causal · r2`

Correlational analysis within the 18-student control group: 13 of 18 students created FBDs for all five problems, averaging 4.2 of 5 problems; creation frequency correlated with problem-set score "(r=0.7, p= 0.001)" and with prior exam achievement (r=0.72, p=0.001).

> "Correlational analyses showed that there was a statistically significant correlation between number of problems for which  free-body diagrams have been created and score on the five -problem set  (r=0.7, p= 0.001), as well as between number of problems for which free-body diagrams have been created and achievement on the Mechanics exam that had been conducted one week earlier (r=0.72, p=0.001)."
