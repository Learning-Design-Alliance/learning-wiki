## Subclaims

## Evidence

### Tully 2017

Tully, L. A., Collins, D. A. J., Piotrowska, P. J., Mairet, K. S., Hawes, D. J., Moul, C., Lenroot, R. K., Frick, P. J., Anderson, V. A., Kimonis, E. R., & Dadds, M. R. (2017). Examining Practitioner Competencies, Organizational Support and Barriers to Engaging Fathers in Parenting Interventions. Child Psychiatry and Human Development. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10578-017-0733-0

`q2 · i2` · `associational · r2`

Logistic regression on cross-sectional online survey data from practitioners delivering parenting interventions (N = 203 analyzed). Training in father engagement was the only unique significant predictor; "2.25 times more likely to be in the high versus low competence category" (OR = 2.25, 95% CI 1.13–4.47).

> "The overall model was statistically significant, χ2 (4, N = 203) = 12.92, p < .05. However, the only independent variable to make a unique statistically significant contribution to the model was participation in training in father engagement. That is, practitioners who had received training in father engagement were 2.25 times more likely to be in the high versus low competence category"
