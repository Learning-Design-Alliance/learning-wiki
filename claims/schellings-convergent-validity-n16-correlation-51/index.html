Evidence¶

Marcel V. J. Veenman 2011¶

Marcel V. J. Veenman. (2011). Alternative assessment of strategy use with self-report instruments: a discussion. Metacognition and Learning. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11409-011-9080-x

q2 · i? · theoretical · r3

Discussion of a convergent-validity validation study comparing a task-specific questionnaire with thinking-aloud protocols of text studying. The article also notes reliabilities for Schellings' subscales were remarkably low and comprehension was not assessed, leaving external validity indeterminate.

"Schellings (this issue) found a non-significant correlation of .51 between questionnaire and thinking-aloud scores. Her conclusions, however, should be formulated with modesty for two reasons. First, results were based on N=16"

  • Most subscale reliabilities of the task-specific questionnaire were moderate to low (three of four subscales with 16 students, all four with 190), despite acceptable full-instrument reliability — related
  • A task-specific questionnaire built item-by-item from a think-aloud coding taxonomy correlated r=.51 with think-aloud protocols, higher than the -.07 to .42 regularly reported — possibly the same claim (merge candidate)
  • Self-report factors correlate weakly with metacognitive behavior while within-factor subcomponent correlations are larger, per studies reviewed for the meta-analysis — related
  • Self-report subscales do not reliably assess distinct metacognitive subcomponents, with structures of two to nine components found across studies — related
  • In the case study, students answering the questionnaire while thinking aloud showed misunderstanding of items, general rather than task-specific reference perspectives, middle-category 'neutral' answering, and probable social desirability — related
  • Task-based and questionnaire-based EF measures show consistently low correlations and may assess partially distinct phenomena — related
  • Cromley and Azevedo's prompted strategy-use measure correlated .23–.74 with other measures and accounted for about 30% of comprehension variance on average — related
  • The strength of the relationship between metacognitive measures and academic achievement depends on the operationalisation, with think-aloud protocols correlating highly and self-report questionnaires weakly — related
