Evidence¶

Paul 2025¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing the LearnEval platform study, that calibration raised the peer–instructor grade correlation "from 0.19 to 0.72". Correlations are printed but no standardized effect size; impact is null.

"LearnEval increased the correlation between peer and instructor grades from 0.19 to 0.72 after calibration [49], while TRUPEQA consistently achieved lower root mean square error than simple averaging or Gibbs sampling methods with calibration [80]."

Paul 2025 (2)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r3

The review attributes to TRUPEQA (Swagrader) consistently lower RMSE than simple averaging or Gibbs sampling under calibration. RMSE comparisons are printed without a standardized effect size.

"TRUPEQA consistently achieved lower root mean square error than simple averaging or Gibbs sampling methods with calibration [80]."

Paul 2025 (3)¶

Paul, U., Mantravadi, A., Shah, J., Shah, S., Mylavarapu, S. V., Rashid, M. P., & Gehringer, E. (2025). Scaling Success: A Systematic Review of Peer Grading Strategies for Accuracy, Efficiency, and Learning in Contemporary Education. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11677

q2 · i? · review · r2

The review reports, citing Fang et al. (RankWithTA), that calibration with "just 10 TA-graded submissions" improved grading in a population of 1,000 students. No effect size is printed.

"Fang et al. [47] showed that with just 10 TA-graded submissions, improvements were achieved in a population of 1,000 students."
